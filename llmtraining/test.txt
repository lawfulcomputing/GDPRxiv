Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD)
Resolution PS/00123/2024
Date: 14 March 2024
Parties
Controller (primary addressee): LÍNEA DIRECTA ASEGURADORA, S.A., COMPAÑÍA DE SEGUROS Y REASEGUROS (Tax ID: A80871031)
Related entity (joint involvement indicated): ACME ANALYTICS IBERIA, S.L.
Complainant: John Doe
Cross-border cooperation: Conducted under Article 60 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) (lead authority: AEPD; concerned authorities: CNIL (France), BfDI (Germany)).
I. Background and Procedural History
On 10 May 2023, the AEPD opened preliminary inquiries following a complaint regarding telemarketing, profiling, and late breach notification.
On 12 June 2023, the AEPD notified the Controller of the initiation of sanctioning proceedings and issued a Statement of Objections.
The matter concerns the General Data Protection Regulation (also referred to as Regulation (EU) 2016/679, RGPD, DSGVO). References to national instruments (e.g., Ley 39/2015) appear only for procedural aspects and do not alter the material legal basis under the GDPR.
Cooperation among supervisory authorities occurred in accordance with Article 60 GDPR.
II. Findings of Fact
2.1 Legal basis and transparency
a) Between May and July 2023, the Controller relied on Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest) for telemarketing and profiling without a documented Legitimate Interest Assessment (LIA).
b) Information provided to data subjects was incomplete and untimely, contrary to Articles 12–13 GDPR (expand to 12, 13).
c) For certain processing operations allegedly based on consent, the Controller could not produce valid consent records as required by Art. 6(1)(a).
2.2 Records of processing
The Controller maintained records that were incomplete with respect to processing purposes and categories of recipients, contrary to Article 30 GDPR.
2.3 Personal data breach notification
A personal data breach discovered on 1 March 2023 was notified to the AEPD on 15 April 2023 (45 days later), exceeding the time limit in Art. 33(1) GDPR.
2.4 Corrective powers considered
The Authority considered measures under Art. 58(2)(b) GDPR, including orders to bring processing into compliance (normalize to base 58).
2.5 Important disambiguation (NOT GDPR—exclude these occurrences)
“Article 20 of the LOPDGDD (Spain) permits certain credit information processing …” → Not GDPR; exclude 20 in this clause.
“Pursuant to § 25 TTDSG, consent is required for storing information on user devices …” → Not GDPR; exclude 25 here.
“Under Royal Decree 1720/2007, Article 5 defines security measures …” → Not GDPR; exclude 5 in that clause.
“Directive 2002/58/EC (ePrivacy), Article 5(3) …” → Not GDPR; exclude 5 in that clause.
III. Legal Basis
3.1 GDPR Principles and Legal Grounds
Articles 5, 6, 7, 9 GDPR are implicated via Article 83(5)(a) (sanction catalog).
Transparency duties under Articles 12–13.
Records of processing under Article 30.
Breach notification obligations under Article 33(1).
Corrective powers per Article 58(2).
Cross-border cooperation per Article 60.
3.2 Normalization of forms
References such as 6.1, 6(1)(f), 58.2, 58(2)(b) shall be reduced to base 6 and 58 for the purpose of article enumeration. Ranges such as 12–13 shall be expanded to 12, 13.
IV. Assessment
4.1 Legal basis (Art. 6)
The Controller failed to demonstrate a balanced legitimate interest and provided inadequate opt-out mechanisms. For consent-based processing, evidence of valid consent was missing.
4.2 Transparency (Arts. 12–13)
Privacy information omitted categories of data and retention periods and was not presented at or before the time of collection.
4.3 Records of processing (Art. 30)
Records lacked several mandatory fields, notably categories of recipients and data retention periods.
4.4 Breach notification (Art. 33)
Notification occurred 45 days after discovery; no justification was provided for exceeding the 72-hour window.
4.5 Corrective powers (Art. 58)
Given the persistent nature of the infringements, orders under Art. 58(2) are appropriate in addition to a pecuniary sanction.
V. Sanctions and Amount
5.1 Proposed amounts (Statement of Objections)
€100,000 for infringement of Art. 6(1) (classified under Art. 83(5)(a)).
€200,000 for infringement of Art. 28 (processor obligations; Art. 83(4)(a)).
5.2 Final operative sanction
After analysis of aggravating/mitigating factors and considering cooperation, the AEPD consolidates the infringements and imposes a single fine of €240,000 (two hundred forty thousand euros).
Note: Earlier drafts referenced €300,000 (sum of heads). The final payable amount is €240,000 following consolidation and reductions.
VI. Operative Part (Decision)
The Spanish Data Protection Agency RESOLVES to:
IMPOSE on LÍNEA DIRECTA ASEGURADORA, S.A., COMPAÑÍA DE SEGUROS Y REASEGUROS an administrative fine of €240,000 for infringements of the GDPR identified in Sections II–IV.
ISSUE A REPRIMAND for deficiencies in transparency (Articles 12–13 GDPR).
ORDER CORRECTIVE MEASURES under Article 58(2) GDPR, requiring the Controller to:
Implement a valid legal basis for telemarketing or cease the processing;
Update privacy information to fully comply with Arts. 12–13;
Complete records of processing (Art. 30);
Establish procedures to ensure breach notifications comply with Art. 33(1) within 72 hours.
COMPLIANCE PERIOD: Within 30 calendar days of notification of this Resolution, the Controller shall demonstrate compliance to the AEPD.
VII. Remedies
This Resolution may be appealed before the Contentious-Administrative Chamber of the Audiencia Nacional within two months from the day following its notification, without prejudice to any other remedy deemed appropriate.
VIII. Publication
In accordance with Article 50 of the LOPDGDD, an anonymized version of this Resolution will be published on the AEPD’s website.
IX. Notification
This Resolution shall be notified to the parties in accordance with applicable procedural rules.
