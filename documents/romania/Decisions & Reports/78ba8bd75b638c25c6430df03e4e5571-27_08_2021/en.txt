**27.08.2021**  
**EDPB Study**  

The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) has recently issued a document titled "Overview of resources made available to data protection authorities by Member States and regarding enforcement actions by data protection authorities." This study was prepared at the request of the Committee on Civil Liberties, Justice and Home Affairs (LIBE) of the European Parliament and includes certain statistics regarding the financial and human resources made available by EU Member States and the European Economic Area (EEA) to national supervisory authorities, as well as information regarding the related supervisory and control activities. 

Furthermore, the document emphasizes that the protection of personal data is a fundamental right in the EU, which requires robust oversight, that can only exist if supervisory authorities are adequately equipped with personnel and resources to monitor compliance with the GDPR. Moreover, the Committee highlights that, in order to keep pace with the rapid developments in technology and digitalization, supervision should be carried out consistently, which is why supervisory authorities must have the means and expertise necessary to do so. 

At the same time, the Committee points out that due to the cooperation and consistency mechanism established in the GDPR (One-Stop-Shop), supervisory authorities have become interdependent, and the effective implementation of this mechanism requires, among other things, that all supervisory authorities, as well as the EDPB, are adequately equipped, as the lack of resources in a competent national supervisory authority to manage cross-border cases can have repercussions on the respect for the rights of citizens across the EU. 

Additionally, the document notes that, besides handling complaints and conducting investigations, the GDPR provides supervisory authorities with a multitude of other tasks and tools for promoting and monitoring compliance, which equally contribute to the protection of the fundamental right to data protection in the EU. These include, among others, data protection impact assessments, evaluation of codes of conduct, issuance of certification and accreditation tools, assessment of Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs), and, of course, contributing to the EDPB's activities to ensure the consistent application and interpretation of the GDPR throughout the Union. In this regard, the Committee emphasizes that adequate resourcing of supervisory authorities is of significant importance for all these tasks and tools.
