The restricted formation first notes that both the data protection policy, accessible via the websites, and the company's processing activity register, provide that the legal basis for the transmission of prospect data for electronic, telephone, or postal marketing purposes is consent, which the company confirms. The restricted formation recalls that the specific consent required by the provisions of Article 6 of the GDPR can only result from the express consent of the user, given knowingly after adequate information about the use that will be made of their personal data. It is important in this regard to ensure that the individuals concerned have given unequivocal, specific, free, and informed consent when their personal data is collected through contest entry forms. 

The restricted formation notes in this regard that the work conducted on the practices implemented concerning cookies regarding consent collection banners can usefully serve to assess more generally the conditions for obtaining free, unequivocal, specific, and informed consent, and serve as a reference in terms of commercial prospecting when it is based on the collection of consent. Furthermore, regarding the same conditions of consent, the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) specified, in its decision Planet49 GmbH: "Article 7(a) of Directive 95 provides that the consent of the data subject can make such processing lawful provided that this consent is 'unequivocally' given by the data subject. However, only an active behavior on the part of this person to express their consent is capable of fulfilling this requirement" (CJEU, Grand Chamber, October 1, 2019, Planet49 GmbH, C-673/17, ECLI:EU:C:2019:801, §54). Therefore, it should be considered that in the absence of unequivocal consent, consent must be regarded as lacking, which renders the processing unlawful due to the lack of a legal basis. More specifically regarding the methods of obtaining consent, the CJEU states that "the manifestation of will referred to in Article 2(h) of the directive...
95/46 must, in particular, be "specific," in the sense that it must precisely relate to the processing of the concerned data and cannot be inferred from a manifestation of will having a distinct object. In this case, contrary to what Planet49 has argued, the fact that a user activates the participation button for the promotional game organized by this company cannot suffice to consider that the user has validly given their consent to the placement of cookies (Idem, §§ 58-59). 

32. Furthermore, the Council of State has held that "free, specific, informed, and unambiguous consent can only be an express consent of the user, given with full knowledge of the facts and after adequate information about the use that will be made of their personal data." (CE, 10th and 9th chambers combined, June 19, 2020, Google LLC, no. 430810, pt. 21). 

33. The restricted formation also notes, by way of example, that the guidelines 5/2020 on consent, adopted on May 4, 2020, by the "Article 29" working group (which has become the European Data Protection Board, hereinafter "EDPB"), specify that the free nature of consent "implies a real choice and control for the concerned individuals. Generally, the GDPR states that if the concerned individual is not genuinely able to exercise a choice, feels compelled to consent, or will suffer significant negative consequences if they do not give their consent, the consent is not valid […] In general terms, any pressure or inappropriate influence exerted on the concerned individual (which can manifest in various ways) preventing them from exercising their will will render the consent invalid." 

34. By way of illustration and comparison, in its deliberation no. 2020-092 of September 17, 2020, adopting a recommendation proposing practical modalities for compliance in the case of the use of "cookies and other trackers," the Commission thus recommends that the concerned organizations ensure "that users fully understand the options available to them, notably through the chosen design and the information provided (§ 10) […] In order not to mislead users, the Commission recommends that data controllers ensure that the interfaces for collecting choices do not incorporate potentially misleading design practices that may lead users to believe that their consent is mandatory or that visually emphasize one choice over another. It is recommended to use buttons and a font of the same size, offering the same ease of reading, and highlighted in the same way" (§ 34). It adds that it is important "to ensure that the information accompanying each actionable item allowing for the expression of consent or refusal is easily understandable and does not require significant concentration or interpretation efforts from the user. Thus, it is particularly recommended to ensure that it is not drafted in such a way that a quick or inattentive reading could lead to the belief that the selected option produces the opposite of what users thought they were choosing." (§ 23). Otherwise, the unambiguous nature of the consent would not be characterized.

2) Regarding the transmission of prospect data to partners. The rapporteur notes that the company TAGADAMEDIA transmits prospect data to partners for direct marketing purposes via postal or electronic means. The rapporteur notes that the personal data protection policy, accessible via the company's website, specifies that the legal basis for the transmission of data to TAGADAMEDIA's partners for "postal or telemarketing prospecting" is consent. 

53. The rapporteur observes that the lack of consent to the transmission of one's data does indeed allow the user to prevent the transmission of their email address. However, the rapporteur points out that, despite the absence of consent, the postal address or phone number is transmitted to partners for prospecting purposes via postal mail or telephone. 

54. The rapporteur therefore considers that the company has engaged in unfair processing of the data of prospects who did not consent to their transmission to partners, which constitutes unfair processing, contrary to Article 5, paragraph 1, a) of the GDPR. 

55. In defense, the company claims that it transmits data to its partners, on the one hand, for commercial prospecting purposes and, on the other hand, for the execution of technical operations and qualification, the latter purpose falling under legitimate interest. The transmission of database exports is done via a "registration design" specific to each partner. These registration designs may or may not include a column related to the status of "opt-in prospect." If the column is present, all data is transmitted, and the column must frame the use of prospect data (if it is indicated "opt-in = 0," the partner must not prospect on the transmitted data but will only carry out technical and qualification operations). If there is no column, the email address and phone number of the prospect are not transmitted, and the partner will only receive the postal details for technical and qualification operations. 

56. The company argues that the use of postal data when prospects have refused the transmission of their data is not the result of a widespread practice and that it discovered the existence of this practice at the time of the inspection. It adds that if some partners have acted in this manner, this situation does not concern all partners, does not reflect an intentional act on the part of TAGADAMEDIA, and does not provide any economic advantage to the company. 

57. Finally, the company specifies that it no longer transmits to its partners, since February 1, 2023, the data of prospects who have not given their consent ("non-opt-in"). 

58. The restricted panel notes that the personal data protection policy of the company states that: "In the event of your express acceptance, your data may also be used by Tagadamedia and/or its partners for: • Sending you commercial and promotional offers by email, including political or union-related content, by email, SMS/MMS, postal mail, or telemarketing • Sending you newsletters • Conducting statistical evaluation of site traffic • Performing segmentation and statistical cross-referencing. The legal basis for processing is consent." 

59. The restricted panel considers that the purposes identified by the company as relating to the execution of technical operations and qualification, namely postal normalization, deduplication, telephone enrichment,
etc., participate in the execution of the commercial prospecting operations of its partners. These processes are carried out based on data transmitted by the company TAGADAMEDIA, within the framework of contracts whose purpose is, notably with the company […], "the technical integration of the TAGADAMEDIA SAS database at […] and its commercialization for rental by […]" (Article 1 of the contract). 

60. The restricted panel notes that the data of individuals who have not consented to their transmission to partners are fully or partially transmitted to partner companies for technical operations and qualification purposes and are used, in some cases, for prospecting purposes. 

61. The restricted panel considers that as soon as a prospect does not consent to the transmission of their data to the company's partners, the company is not authorized to transmit them, including for the purpose of carrying out technical operations and qualification. 

62. Moreover, the restricted panel notes that the company's privacy policy does not specify that the data of users who have refused the transmission of their data to partners for commercial prospecting purposes are nonetheless transmitted to these partners for other purposes and based on another legal basis. 

63. The restricted panel observes that the company only became aware of the use of postal data by its partners for commercial prospecting purposes, while prospects had refused the transmission of their data, at the time of the inspection in 2022. 

64. It believes that to transmit this data to its partners, and insofar as it has chosen the legal basis of consent for processing related to prospecting by post or by phone, the company should have obtained the consent of the individuals concerned, which is not the case here. 

65. The restricted panel concludes, therefore, that a breach of Article 6 of the GDPR has occurred. It notes, however, that the company has not transmitted "non-opt-in" prospect data to its partners since February 1, 2023. 

B. On the breach of the obligation to implement a record of processing activities 

66. Article 30 of the Regulation states that the data controller, regardless of the number of its employees, is obliged to maintain a record of processing activities, as long as the processing of personal data is not occasional. In this case, the record of processing activities must include all the information listed in 1. of Article 30 of the GDPR, namely: the name and contact details of the data controller, its representative, and the data protection officer, the purposes of the processing, the categories of data subjects and the categories of personal data, any data transfers, and, where possible, the time limits for data erasure and a description of the technical and organizational measures implemented to ensure an appropriate level of data security relative to the risk. 

67. The rapporteur noted that the company shares a record of processing activities with the company […]. However, this record does not specify which of the companies acts as the data controller. 

68. In defense, the company first points out that it has indeed established a record of processing activities and that it is a simplified model of a record proposed by the CNIL. It adds that this record does include all the mentions required by Article 30 of the GDPR. 

69. It further adds that it is relatively rare for the CNIL to sanction organizations based on Article 30 of the GDPR and that decisions targeting this breach would concern more serious facts (total absence of a record, failure to comply after a formal notice, etc.). 

70. The restricted panel notes that the company TAGADAMEDIA implements a record of processing activities, maintained jointly with the company […], which it acquired, for processing related to human resources or prospecting activities. 

71. The restricted panel notes that said record does not distinguish which company acts as the data controller for the activity in question. Indeed, it is not specified for human resources or prospecting activities whether it is TAGADAMEDIA or the company […] that acts as the data controller. However, TAGADAMEDIA should have, given the number of data processed and its activity, ensured that its record of processing activities is exhaustive, precise, and up to date. 

72. It follows from the above that the breach of Article 30 of the GDPR is established. 

73. The restricted panel notes that the company has updated its record of processing activities, which is now compliant with the requirements of the GDPR.

Regarding the obligation to have a legal basis for the processing carried out, the restricted panel emphasizes first that the ecosystem of data resale from partner to partner requires particularly strong guarantees regarding the quality and validity of the consent obtained by the initial data collector, which partners rely on for commercial prospecting purposes. It recalls that the absence of valid consent collection deprives the transmission of prospect data to partners of a legal basis, which then appears illicit. It considers that the requirements must be particularly strengthened regarding the modalities of obtaining consent from users of the websites published by the company, whose purpose is to offer prospects of gains, these individuals not necessarily being aware of the scope of their agreement in the context of their registration. FOR THESE REASONS The restricted formation of the CNIL, after deliberation, decides to: • impose an administrative fine on the company TAGADAMEDIA in the amount of seventy-five thousand (75,000 €) for breaches of Articles 6 and 30 of the GDPR; • issue an injunction against the company TAGADAMEDIA to implement on the sites it operates a data collection form for prospects that allows for the collection of free, specific, informed, and unambiguous consent regarding the transmission of their personal data to partners for prospecting purposes; • attach to the injunction a penalty of one thousand (1,000) euros per day of delay after a period of one month following the notification of this deliberation, with proof of compliance to be submitted to the restricted formation within this timeframe; • make public, on the CNIL website and on the Légifrance website, its deliberation, which will no longer allow for the specific identification of the company after a period of two years from its publication.
