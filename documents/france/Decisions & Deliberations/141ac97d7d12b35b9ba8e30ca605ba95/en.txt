II- Reasons for the Decision 

A- On the quality of the data controller of SAF LOGISTICS 

14. The data controller is defined, in accordance with Article 4, point 7, of the GDPR, as "the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing." 

15. The processing subject to this procedure
**Concerning the collection of data through a form sent by the company to employees wishing to apply for a position in China in order to feed what is referred to as the "internal talent pool" by SAF LOGISTICS.**

16. The rapporteur considers that, in this case, while it is undisputed that the disputed form was not developed by SAF LOGISTICS but by its Chinese parent company, Sinotrans Hongfeng Shanghai Limited, this does not exclude that SAF LOGISTICS must be regarded as the data controller for the following reasons.

17. First, she recalls that, following a request from an employee wishing to work in China, the company approached its parent company to obtain the form in order to transmit it to other employees for the first time on July 30, 2020. The rapporteur notes in this regard that if SAF LOGISTICS had not taken the initiative to request the form from the parent company for the purpose of disseminating it to its employees, the processing in question would not have occurred.

18. The rapporteur then considers that the company processes part of the data provided by its employees through the disputed form for another purpose determined by it, which appears specific to its needs, namely to enable it to contact the relatives of employees in case of problems.

19. In defense, the company argues that its role in the dissemination of the form is not sufficient to confer upon it the status of data controller. It indicated during the session of the restricted formation that it had not used the data from the form for its own purposes since the form was established by the parent company as part of the recruitment of employees in China, and that it had not used the data from the form to inform the relatives of employees in case of emergency.

20. In its observations, the company asserts that it limited itself to disseminating the form to about twenty employees, which does not imply that it had a determining role or influence in the determination of the purposes and means of the processing. It thus claims, in its response observations, that "it acted as a mere 'mailbox' for those wishing to apply to the Parent Company" and that it therefore did not define either the purposes or the means of the processing.

21. During the session before the restricted formation, the company ultimately indicated that it was the employee wishing to work in China who had himself disseminated the form to his colleagues.

22. First, the restricted formation recalls that the data controller is the person who determines the purposes of the processing implemented, that is to say, the expected or sought result, and the means of this processing, that is to say, the way to achieve this result.

23. For clarification, in its guidelines on the concepts of data controller and processor, which were adopted on July 7, 2021, the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) specifies the definition of the data controller in these terms: "The determination of the purposes and means involves deciding respectively the 'why' and the 'how' of the processing: for a particular processing operation, the data controller is the actor who has determined the reason for which the processing takes place (i.e., 'for what purposes' or 'why') and how this objective will be achieved (i.e., what means must be implemented to achieve the objective). A natural or legal person who exercises this influence over the processing of personal data thus participates in the determination of the purposes and means of the processing in question, in accordance with the definition set out in Article 4, paragraph 7, of the GDPR. The data controller must decide both the purposes and the means of the processing, as indicated above. Therefore, the data controller cannot limit itself to determining...**
only the purpose. It must also make decisions regarding the means of processing. Conversely, the party acting as a subcontractor can never determine the purpose of the processing. 

24. It follows from both the GDPR and the interpretation provided by the EDPB that the entity that defines the purposes for which personal data processing is implemented, as well as the practical modalities of this processing, must be qualified as the data controller. The restricted panel considers that the notion of data controller must be subject to a concrete assessment taking into account all elements that allow attributing this status to an entity. 

25. Secondly, the restricted panel notes that it appears from the elements communicated to the CNIL that it is the company SAF LOGISTICS that requested the parent company in China to send the form to disseminate it to its employees. It is therefore not the parent company that ordered SAF LOGISTICS to transmit the form to the employees, but rather the latter that made this decision as the data controller. In its observations in response dated April 11, 2023, the company specifies in this regard: "The sending of this form followed a request from a Chinese employee who wished to apply in China and know the application process. SAF contacted the parent company in China about this" and that it had "sent a form to employees in the event that some of them would be willing to work for the parent company in China." It thus appears that the company, in wanting to identify employees potentially interested in relocating to China and not only the employee who made the initial request, defined the "why" of the processing. Then, by obtaining the form from the parent company and disseminating it to about twenty people, the company defined the means of processing, in other words, the "how."

26. Regarding the argument presented in the meeting by the company that it was the employee wishing to go to China who transmitted the form to his colleagues, the restricted panel notes that aside from the fact that this argument contradicts the company's written submissions, it appears from the file that this form was disseminated to employees by the company via WeChat messaging.

27. The restricted panel therefore considers, in light of these elements, that the company SAF LOGISTICS must be qualified as the data controller related to the collection of data provided through the form.

B - On the competence of the CNIL

28. Article 3-1 of the GDPR states that "this regulation applies to the processing of personal data carried out in the context of the activities of an establishment of a data controller or a processor on the territory of the Union, whether the processing takes place in the Union or not."

29. Article 55-1 of the GDPR states that "each supervisory authority is competent to exercise the missions and powers conferred on it in accordance with this regulation on the territory of the Member State to which it belongs."

30. Article 8-2 of the French Data Protection Act specifies that "the National Commission for Information Technology and Civil Liberties (...) ensures that the processing of personal data is carried out in accordance with the provisions of this law and other provisions relating to the protection of personal data provided by legislative and regulatory texts, the law of the European Union, and France's international commitments."

31. The company considers that insofar as it is not the data controller related to the form, the CNIL is not competent to impose a corrective measure against it.

32. The restricted panel recalls that the CNIL is competent to ensure, on French territory, that the processing to which the provisions of the GDPR apply is carried out in compliance with the applicable regulations.
RGPD or the law of January 6, 1978, as amended, which apply must be implemented in accordance with the provisions of these texts. 33. In this case, the restricted panel notes that, since the company SAF LOGISTICS, which is responsible for the processing related to the form, is established in the territory of the European Union, specifically in France, the criteria for the territorial competence of the CNIL are met. 34. The CNIL is therefore competent, based on Article 3-1) of the RGPD, to address the processing of personal data carried out in the context of the form submitted to its employees by the company SAF LOGISTICS. C- On the failure to ensure the adequacy, relevance, and non-excessiveness of the data in accordance with Article 5, paragraph 1, c) of the RGPD. 35. Article 5, paragraph 1, c) of the RGPD provides that personal data must be "adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (data minimization)." When data is collected on the basis of legitimate interest, this collection must also not disproportionately affect privacy, in light of the objectives pursued by the company. 36. The rapporteur considers that the company has failed to adhere to the principle of minimization regarding the data collected by SAF LOGISTICS for the purpose related to contacting the relatives of employees in case of emergency, as certain data does not appear relevant to achieve this objective. 37. In defense, the company argues that since it is not responsible for the processing related to the disputed form, and that the information provided in the form was intended for the parent company to contact the relatives of employees in case of emergency, it cannot be held accountable for a failure to adhere to the principle of minimization. The company then indicates that it decided to cease the communication of the form to employees following the receipt of the first letters from the CNIL. 38. The restricted panel recalls that the assessment of compliance with the principle of data minimization relies on the strict adequacy between the data collected and the reasons for their collection. 39. It considers that while the purpose of being able to contact an employee's relatives in case of emergency is legitimate, it remains that by transmitting the form to employees, the company was responsible for collecting their data and transmitting it to the parent company. In this case, the company collects information related to the employees' entourage (siblings, ascendants, descendants) such as name, first name, date and place of birth, gender, relationship, phone number, employer, position, and marital status, thereby infringing on the privacy of the employees. 40. In this regard, the restricted panel first observes that it is not necessary to collect so much information if the objective of this collection is simply to have the contact details of a person to reach out to. 41. Next, it notes that the form proposes to provide information regarding several family members. However, the restricted panel observes that it is not necessary for the company to collect data concerning so many different individuals to achieve this purpose. 42. Finally, the restricted panel considers that the significant number and variety of personal data collected is not justified in light of the purpose of contacting relatives in case of emergency and that it would be sufficient to provide the name, first name, relationship, and phone number of the employee's relative to contact in case of emergency. 43. E- On the breach of the prohibition on collecting or processing data related to offenses, convictions, and security measures under Article 10 of the GDPR. 56. The provisions of Article 10 of the GDPR state that "the processing of personal data related to criminal convictions and offenses or related security measures based on Article 6, paragraph 1, may only be carried out under the control of the public authority, or if the processing is authorized by Union law or by the law of a Member State that provides appropriate safeguards for the rights and freedoms of the data subjects." 57. Article 777-3 of the Code of Criminal Procedure states that "No file or processing of personal data held by any person or by a state service not dependent on the Ministry of Justice may mention, except in the cases and under the conditions provided by law, judgments or convictions." 58. The rapporteur notes that it follows from the findings made by the CNIL delegation during the on-site inspection on October 21, 2021, that extracts from the criminal record, specifically bulletin No. 3, were found in the individual files of employees whose employment contracts are currently in execution. 59. The rapporteur considers that for employees subject to a clearance procedure, it is the "police and gendarmerie services" that consult the criminal record and not the employer, and that for employees who are not subject to a clearance procedure, the employer can only consult the criminal record. 60. In defense, the company argues in its observations that not all employees are subject to the described clearance procedure, and that consequently, the company could consult extracts from bulletin No. 3 given its "air freight activity, receipt of goods, handling, and employees handling high-value items." Furthermore, the company does not contest the
conservation of extracts from bulletin no. 3 but specifies that their retention was not systematic. 61. The restricted panel notes that regarding employees subject to the authorization procedure, Article L. 6342-3 of the Transport Code provides that the "national police and gendarmerie services" consult the criminal record of employees as part of an administrative investigation before issuing the necessary authorization. Article L. 6342-3 of the Transport Code states that "[t]he issuance of this authorization is preceded by an administrative investigation which may involve consultation of bulletin no. 2 of the criminal record and automated processing of personal data managed by the national police and gendarmerie services in accordance with the provisions of Article 31 of Law No. 78-17 of January 6, 1978, relating to data processing, files, and freedoms, with the exception of identification files." Thus, the employer is not required to carry out these verifications when they are ensured by an authorized authority. 62. The restricted panel then considers that concerning employees who are not subject to the authorization procedure, while the company could consult the extracts of employees' criminal records, it was not authorized to retain them. 63. It follows from the above that by consulting and retaining extracts of its employees' criminal records outside the cases provided for by law, the company failed to meet its obligations under Article 10 of the GDPR. B. On publicity 

89. To justify this request for publicity, the rapporteur invokes, in particular, the seriousness of the alleged breaches, as they relate to key principles of the GDPR. The company contests the rapporteur's proposal to make this decision public. It considers that in view of the risk to the economic viability of the company and its good faith, the publicity of the sanction measure would be disproportionate. 

90. The restricted formation considers that the publicity of this decision is justified in light of the seriousness of the breaches in question and the categories of data collected by the company. 

91. Finally, the measure is proportionate since the decision will no longer specifically identify the company after a period of two years from its publication. 

FOR THESE REASONS 

The restricted formation of the CNIL, after deliberation, decides to: 
• impose an administrative fine of two hundred thousand euros (€200,000) against the company SAF LOGISTICS for the breaches constituted under Articles 5-1-c), 9, 10, and 31 of Regulation (EU) No. 2016/679 of April 27, 2016, on the protection of personal data; 
• make public, on the CNIL website and on the Légifrance website, its deliberation, which will no longer specifically identify the company after a period of two years from its publication.
