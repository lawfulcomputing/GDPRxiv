**B. On the breach of the obligation to limit the duration of data retention**  
19. Under Article 5-1, e) of the GDPR, personal data must be "kept in a form which permits identification of the data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed."  
20. The rapporteur noted that during the on-site inspection, the company indicated that it had defined a data retention policy that varied according to the type of users of the website www.pap.fr. Thus, regarding customer data (individuals using the paid services of the site), the rapporteur noted that the company had defined a retention period of ten years from the date of order acceptance. She observed that the systematic and indiscriminate retention of all account data for ten years did not appear justified in light of the legal obligation arising from the Consumer Code and that the database extraction provided included data related to transactions below 120 euros, the retention of which did not appear justified. Regarding user data (individuals using the free services of the site), the rapporteur noted that while the company had defined a retention period of five years from the date of the last connection to the account, it emerged from the on-site inspection that the company had retained 2,394,538 records for more than five years and less than ten years, and 737,563 records for more than ten years.  
21. In defense, regarding the data of customers using paid advertisements, the company specified during the investigation that it retained the advertisement and the email address for ten years for the purpose of complying with legal obligations arising from Articles L.213-1, D.213-1, and D.213-2 of the Consumer Code, combating fraud, and due to the specificities related to real estate activity. It detailed the two paid plans offered to customers: either a contract with no commitment for 59 euros per month, which is considered a unique contract of indefinite duration, or a three-month contract subscribed for 135 euros. For contracts without commitment, estimating that it could not determine in advance the duration of the contract, the company specified that it retained all data related to these contracts for a period of ten years, regardless of the final amount. It further considers that the financial stakes of the advertisements and sales contracts concluded subsequently for amounts well above 120 euros should be taken into account. It also indicated that the retention period for birth years was reduced to twenty-five months and that for data related to an inactive account to three years of inactivity.  
22. Regarding user data, the company specified during the investigation that it retained only the email address and the associated account for a period of five years for litigation purposes and combating fraud, and that it had deleted data retained beyond this five-year period.  
23. The restricted formation recalls that it is the responsibility of the data controller to define and implement a data retention period that does not exceed what is necessary in light of the purpose for which the data are processed.  
24. Regarding relevant durations, by way of illustration, the restricted formation notes that in its framework relating to the processing of personal data implemented for the management of commercial activities, the CNIL specifies that data necessary for the execution of a contract are retained during the contractual relationship and that compliance with a legal obligation incumbent on the organization may, in particular, justify a longer retention period. In the absence of this, retention...
must rely on another legal basis provided for in Article 6 of the GDPR. 25. Under Article L. 213-1 of the Consumer Code: "When the contract is concluded electronically and concerns an amount equal to or greater than an amount set by decree, the professional contractor ensures the preservation of the written record that confirms it for a period determined by the same decree and guarantees access to it at any time to its co-contractor if requested." 26. Article D. 213-1 of the same code provides that "[t]he amount mentioned in Article L. 213-1 is set at 120 euros" and Article D. 213-2 states that "[t]he period mentioned in Article L. 213-1 is set at ten years from the conclusion of the contract when the delivery of the good or the execution of the service is immediate. In the contrary case, the period runs from the conclusion of the contract until the date of delivery of the good or the execution of the service and for a duration of ten years from that date." 27. In this case, the restricted formation first notes that, regarding the preservation of customer data, the ten-year retention period from the date of acceptance of the order defined by the company is justified by its legal obligations resulting from the aforementioned Consumer Code for contracts exceeding 120 euros. Thus, the restricted formation considers that, for the three-month contracts offered by the company at a price of 135 euros, the retention of data for a period of ten years is fully justified. 28. On the other hand, the restricted formation notes that, for contracts without commitment at a price of 59 euros per month, the company retains by default the data related to these contracts as soon as they are concluded, even if the total amount paid by the user is less than 120 euros. However, in the case where a customer has only used the company's paid services for one or two months, that is, for an amount less than 120 euros, the data retention period provided for by the Consumer Code would not apply. The restricted formation notes that Article D.213-1 of the Consumer Code expressly states "when the contract (...) concerns an amount equal to or greater than." The only amount to be considered is therefore that of the contract concluded between the company PAP and the customer, especially since the company PAP is a third party to the sales contract concluded between the selling customer and the buyer. Consequently, the restricted formation considers that the retention of customer data in the situation just described is not made mandatory by compliance with the Consumer Code, contrary to what the company indicates. 29. In any case, the restricted formation notes that an extraction of 100 lines corresponding to customer accounts that placed orders more than five years ago shows that 69 of them concerned orders for an amount less than 120 euros. Therefore, the restricted formation considers that the company has retained data from accounts not covered by Article D. 213-1 of the Consumer Code for excessive durations. 30. Next, the restricted formation notes, regarding the retention of user data, that the company has defined a retention period of five years that begins to run from the date of the last connection to the user account. The restricted formation considers that the explanations provided by the company during the investigation justify the retention period for litigation purposes and for combating fraud. 31. Nevertheless, the restricted formation observes that it emerges from the on-site inspection that the company had retained 2,394,538 lines corresponding to user accounts for more than five years from the date of the last connection and less than ten years, and 737,563 lines corresponding to user accounts for more than ten years from the date.

Article 13 of the GDPR lists the information that must be provided to the data subject when personal data is collected directly from them. This information includes, in particular, the identity of the data controller and their contact details, the purposes of the processing carried out, its legal basis, the recipients or categories of recipients of the data, and the fact that the data controller intends to carry out a transfer of data to a third country. The article also imposes on the data controller, when necessary to ensure "fair and transparent processing" of personal data in this case, to inform individuals about the retention period of the data, the existence of the various rights that individuals have, the existence of the right to withdraw consent at any time, and the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority.

35. However, the rapporteur notes that the company has, since the inspections, engaged in a compliance process, without this affecting the breaches for the past. 36. Regarding the legal bases applicable to the processing, the company mentions a presentation awkwardness. Regarding the mention of recipients or categories of recipients, it considers that it was not required to provide the identity of all data recipients. 37. Aside from this case, no information was provided regarding the other recipients or categories of recipients.

E. On the breaches of the obligation to ensure data security 51. Under Article 32 of the GDPR, "1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing as well as the risks, the likelihood and severity of which vary, to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, including among other things, as necessary: a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data; b) the means to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services; c) the means to restore the availability of personal data and access to it in a timely manner in the event of a physical or technical incident; d) a procedure for regularly testing, analyzing, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure processing security." 1. On passwords and confidential references 52. The rapporteur notes that, on the one hand, during the online inspection, the delegation found that when creating a user account on the company's website, passwords of a unique character (one digit or one letter) were accepted and that no access restriction in the event of authentication failure was implemented. Furthermore, the rapporteur notes that, during the checks, each password was stored both in clear text and hashed with the Bcrypt hashing algorithm. 53. On the other hand, the rapporteur notes that, during the on-site inspection, the delegation was informed that when submitting an advertisement without having an account, the user was provided in clear text with a confidential reference consisting of ten alphanumeric characters, of which the first seven were public as they corresponded to the characters of the reference of the advertisement submitted on the site. This confidential reference could not be modified by the advertiser. This single reference allowed the user to directly access the advertisement and the associated space on the site after entering it in the corresponding field. Moreover, this confidential reference, which resembles a password, was stored in clear text in the database. 54. In defense, the company does not substantially contest the breaches but states that it has taken corrective actions. First of all, it announces that it has adapted its password policy by requiring passwords of a length of eight characters composed of at least one uppercase letter, one lowercase letter, one digit, and one special character. It recalls that now passwords are hashed with the Bcrypt algorithm and that passwords stored in clear text have been deleted. Next, the company specifies that it no longer communicates confidential references to users requiring the creation of an account on the site and has implemented the blocking of the proprietary space after ten unsuccessful login attempts. 55. First and foremost, the restricted panel recalls that it follows from the provisions of Article 32 of the GDPR that the data controller is required to ensure that
the automated processing of data that it implements is sufficiently secure. The sufficiency of the security measures is assessed, on the one hand, in light of the characteristics of the processing and the risks it entails, and on the other hand, taking into account the state of knowledge and the cost of the measures. 

56. The restricted formation first considers that overly permissive password complexity rules, which allow the use of insufficiently robust passwords, can lead to attacks by unauthorized third parties, such as "brute force" or "dictionary" attacks, which consist of systematically and successively testing numerous passwords and thus lead to a compromise of the associated accounts and the personal data they contain. 

57. It notes, in this regard, that the necessity of a strong password is recommended both by the National Cybersecurity Agency (ANSSI) and by the Commission in its deliberation No. 2017-012 of January 19, 2017, adopting a recommendation regarding passwords, a requirement confirmed in its deliberation No. 2022-100 of July 21, 2022. 

58. By way of illustration, the restricted formation recalls that the Commission considers in its deliberation No. 2017-012 of January 19, 2017 – which is certainly not imperative but provides relevant insight into the measures to be taken regarding security – that, to ensure a sufficient level of security and confidentiality, in the event that authentication relies solely on an identifier and a password, the latter must consist of at least twelve characters including uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters. 

59. Failing that, the Commission also considers that a sufficient level of security and confidentiality can be ensured by authentication based on a password of a minimum length of eight characters, composed of three different categories of characters but accompanied by a complementary measure such as, for example, account access throttling after several failures (temporary suspension of access with duration increasing with each attempt), the implementation of a mechanism to guard against automated and intensive submission of attempts (e.g., "captcha"), and/or the blocking of the account after several unsuccessful authentication attempts (a maximum of ten). 

60. The restricted formation emphasizes that it has adopted financial sanctions where the characterization of a breach of Article 32 of the GDPR results from insufficient measures to ensure the security of the processed data. Deliberations No. SAN-2019-006 of June 13, 2019, No. SAN-2019-007 of July 18, 2019, and No. SAN-2022-018 of September 8, 2022, specifically target the insufficient robustness of passwords. 

61. The restricted formation notes that in this case, on the one hand, the passwords of users of the website www.pap.fr had to be, at the time of the checks, composed of a single character and lacked any complementary security measure. It emerges from the company's observations that the required passwords are now of a length of 8 characters composed of at least one uppercase letter, one lowercase letter, one number, and one special character without any access restriction being provided. On the other hand, the confidential references - comparable to passwords in the sense of the definition in deliberation No. 2022-100 of July 21, 2022, according to which the term password refers to any knowledge factor, namely any set of revocable information, known only to the person concerned and allowing or contributing to their authentication - consisted of ten alphanumeric characters, of which the first seven were public, as they corresponded to the reference of the announcement on the site, with only the last three characters.
privileged. Furthermore, these references were transmitted in clear text and could not be modified by the user, thus constituting a lasting means of authentication. 62. The restricted panel considers that such constructions do not ensure data security and prevent unauthorized third parties from accessing it. 63. Regarding the passwords required at the time of account creation, it recalls, as highlighted by the rapporteur, that the company was processing, at the time of the on-site inspection, data associated with nearly five million user accounts such as name, first name, email address. Thus, these passwords, associated with their identifiers, allow access to all personal data contained in their accounts at www.pap.fr. They were not robust enough, given the personal data at stake and the state of the art. 64. Regarding the confidential reference, the restricted panel considers that the use of this single reference consisting of ten alphanumeric characters did not meet the sufficient complexity criterion. Indeed, it appears that the first part of this reference, the first seven alphanumeric characters corresponding to the advertisement reference, must be considered a public identifier. As for the second part of the reference, composed of the last three alphanumeric characters, which resembles a password, it does not meet the robustness criteria as described earlier. 65. Furthermore, as highlighted by the rapporteur, this confidential reference allows access to the personal data present in the proprietary space associated with the person who published the advertisement, to modify them and also to modify the advertisement. Moreover, access to this space allows access to the exchanges that occurred between the owner and the interested parties regarding the advertisement, during which numerous personal information may be transmitted (family, professional, financial situations). 66. Thus, authentication based on the use, on the one hand, of a password, which is short and lacking additional security measures in the past, currently still insufficiently robust in the absence of additional security measures, and, on the other hand, of a non-modifiable confidential reference, transmitted in clear text and without sufficient complexity, can lead to attacks by unauthorized third parties and thus to a compromise of user accounts and the "proprietary space" and the numerous personal data they contain. 67. Consequently, the restricted panel considers that the password and confidential reference policy deployed was and remains insufficiently robust to guarantee the security of the processed data, which disregards Article 32 of the GDPR. 68. Secondly, the restricted panel recalls that securely storing passwords constitutes a basic precaution in terms of personal data protection. As early as 2013, ANSSI alerted and reminded good practices regarding password storage, indicating that they must "be stored in a transformed form by a one-way cryptographic function (hashing function) that is slow to compute such as PBKDF2" and that "the transformation of passwords must involve a random salt to prevent an attack by precomputed tables" (ANSSI, "CERTA-2013-ACT-046 News Bulletin," November 15, 2013, https://www.cert.ssi.gouv.fr/actualite/CERTA-2013-ACT-046/). 69. Similarly, in its deliberation No. 2017-012 of January 19, 2017, CNIL already indicated that it "recommends [that the password] be transformed using a non-reversible and secure cryptographic function (i.e., using a publicly recognized strong algorithm whose software implementation is free of known vulnerabilities),
integrating the use of a salt or a key. Indeed, non-robust hash functions have known vulnerabilities that do not guarantee the integrity and confidentiality of passwords in the event of a brute force attack following the compromise of the servers hosting them. 70. In this case, the restricted panel notes that the clear storage, on the one hand, of user passwords, associated with their identifiers and email addresses, and, on the other hand, of confidential references, associated with a personal space, does not guarantee their security. This method of storage implies that anyone with access to the company's customer database – whether it be an information systems administrator of the company or an attacker in the event of a compromise – can consult, collect, modify, or even sell them. 71. Under these conditions, the restricted panel considers that the methods of storing passwords and confidential references did not, at the time of the findings, guarantee the security and confidentiality of the personal data of account holders, which violates Article 32 of the GDPR. 72. Consequently, the restricted panel considers that the aforementioned facts, uncontested by the company, constitute breaches of the obligations under Article 32 of the GDPR. It acknowledges that since the inspections, the company has partially remedied the identified breaches by implementing a password policy that presents an adequate level of security and by encrypting all passwords. 

2. On the retention of data in active database 73. The rapporteur notes that during the inspections, the delegation was informed that all data related to inactive customers was retained for ten years and that data related to inactive user accounts was retained for five years in the active database without any intermediate archiving taking place. 74. In defense, the company contests the existence of the breach. First, it argues that this retention of data in the active database for customers and users who have become inactive is justified by a purpose of combating fraud that requires daily controls. Next, it specifies that only individuals with a legitimate interest in processing the data due to their roles can access personal data, namely customer service advisors, employees from the IT departments, and members of management. In this regard, it adds that these employees are subject to a confidentiality clause and a confidentiality commitment and that each of them has a personal password. 75. The restricted panel recalls that to ensure data security, it is necessary to sort through this data when it is no longer necessary for the purpose for which it was collected. Thus, they must be deleted or subjected to intermediate archiving, which consists notably of physical or logical separation. 76. In the present case, the restricted panel notes that it emerges from the explanations provided by the company that while the purpose of combating fraud may justify data retention, the methods of retaining data in the active database as defined by the company do not ensure data security. On the one hand, the restricted panel notes the significant number of categories of employees who have access to the database, as both customer service advisors, IT department employees, and management members are authorized to access the database. On the other hand, it observes the absence of sorting among the retained data, whereas it appears that the retention of data such as that of the announcement and the billing address is not...
necessary to the objective pursued of combating fraud. The company indeed confirmed during the session that the data used to identify fraudsters was the email address. 77. Consequently, the restricted panel considers that the breach is established. F. On the breach of the obligation to inform and the right to object to commercial prospecting by email for similar products or services. 78. Article L. 34-5 of the Postal and Electronic Communications Code (CPCE) provides that "Direct prospecting by means of automated electronic communication systems [...], a fax machine or emails using the contact details of a natural person [...] who has not previously expressed their consent to receive direct prospecting by this means is prohibited. Direct prospecting is defined as the sending of any message intended to promote, directly or indirectly, goods, services, or the image of a person selling goods or providing services. For the application of this article, calls and messages aimed at encouraging the user or subscriber to call a premium-rate number or to send a premium-rate text message also fall under direct prospecting. However, direct prospecting by email is permitted if the recipient's contact details have been collected from them, in compliance with the provisions of Law No. 78-17 of January 6, 1978 relating to data processing, files, and freedoms, during a sale or service provision, if the direct prospecting concerns similar products or services provided by the same natural or legal person, and if the recipient is expressly and unambiguously offered the possibility to object, at no cost other than those related to the transmission of the refusal, and in a straightforward manner, to the use of their contact details at the time they are collected and each time a prospecting email is sent to them in case they have not initially refused such exploitation." 79.
