R E A S O N I N G  
A. Submissions of the Parties and Procedural History  
1. The complainant stated in his complaint received by the Data Protection Authority on December 1, 2020, that the respondent had not responded to his written (via email) and justified request for erasure dated October 3, 2020, nor had he received a rejection of the request. He had received unsolicited job offers via SMS from the respondent, whereby the respondent had stated by phone that it had obtained his contact details from the Public Employment Service (AMS).  
2. The respondent, requested by the Data Protection Authority to provide a statement (procedural order dated December 7, 2020, GZ: 2020-0.800.849), initially did not respond.
3. Following the urgency of a statement (procedural order dated March 25, 2021, reference number: 2021-0.213.417, duly delivered to the respondent as a registered letter on April 6, 2021), the respondent submitted the following statement in writing on April 12, 2021: The appellant was registered as seeking employment at the AMS at the time of the first contact. Since the data was received "from the AMS as the official platform for job placement," the respondent does not consider it unlawful to present a job offer to the appellant. The respondent did not address the question of the appellant's right to deletion in their statement.

4. After hearing from the parties regarding the findings of the investigation (procedural order dated April 15, 2021, reference number: 2021-0.267.486), the appellant submitted the following statement on April 19, 2021: The respondent's perspective on handling his data was known to him from direct contacts. However, he is not concerned with the legality of the collection of his data, but solely with the question of enforcing his right to deletion. He had suspended all his job advertisements via the AMS online portal after he no longer sought support from the AMS; however, he had received SMS messages with job offers from the respondent despite multiple requests for deletion, the last one being on April 16, 2021.

5. After hearing from the parties regarding the appellant's statement of April 19, 2021, and requesting the respondent to respond again to the appellant's submissions, including a reference to the accountability obligation under Article 5(2) GDPR (procedural order dated April 23, 2021, reference number: 2021-0.291.660), the respondent did not provide any further statement.

Based on the submissions of the parties, it is evident that the subject of the proceedings is whether the respondent has violated the appellant's right to deletion by refusing to comply with a deletion request dated October 3, 2020.

D. From a legal perspective, it follows that:  
D. 1. Summary:  
14. The complaint has been found to be justified, as the respondent has not lawfully processed the complainant's data from the outset, having violated the principle of transparency in accordance with Art. 5 Para. 1 lit. a and Art. 14 Para. 1 and 2 GDPR when collecting the data, which is why they are obliged to delete it in accordance with Art. 17 Para. 1 lit. d GDPR. The respondent has failed to demonstrate the legality of their data processing despite being requested to do so by the data protection authority.  
D.2. Objection of the respondent:  
15. The respondent has only countered the complaint (in the very brief statement of April 12, 2021) by claiming that they received the complainant's data from the AMS as the "official platform for job placement." Thus, they imply that they are allowed to process the complainant's data for the purposes of exercising their business, thus based on overriding legitimate interests in accordance with Art. 6 Para. 1 lit. f GDPR.  
D.3. Legal basis for data processing, principles, and justification reasons:  
16. Detached from the complaint case, such legitimate interests could be considered as legal bases for processing here.  

17. However, the respondent overlooks that as the data protection controller, they need not only a justification reason in accordance with Art. 6 GDPR to ensure the legality of their data processing but must also adhere to the principles for processing personal data according to Art. 5 Para. 1 GDPR and demonstrate compliance with them in the event of a dispute in accordance with Para. 2 of the same legal text to the data protection authority (accountability). The respondent was explicitly informed of this in the procedural order of April 23, 2021, GZ: 2021-0.291.660. Such proof has not been provided.  
18. Accountability is a central element of the GDPR that has been newly introduced with the increased self-responsibility of the controller in relation to the Data Protection Directive. It arises from Art. 5 Para. 2 and Art. 24 Para. 1 GDPR and states that the controller must not only comply with the provisions of the GDPR but must also be able to demonstrate this. Thus, the burden of proof for compliance with the GDPR lies with the controller. This particularly entails a comprehensive documentation obligation (Hötzendorfer/Tschohl/Kastelitz in Knyrim, DatKomm Art 5 GDPR (as of May 7, 2020, rdb.at), Rz 58).  
D.4. Principle of transparency and information obligation:  
19. According to Art. 5 Para. 1 lit. a GDPR, personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject ("Lawfulness, Fairness, Transparency").  
20. Any processing of personal data should be lawful and fair. For natural persons, transparency should exist in such a way that they are aware of which personal data concerning them is being collected, used, accessed, or otherwise processed, and to what extent the personal data is processed and will be processed in the future (GDPR, Recital 39, sentences 1 and 2, emphasis by the data protection authority).  
21. The implementation of the principle of transparency is particularly served by the information obligation according to Art. 14 GDPR, which applies when the data to be processed is not collected from the data subject themselves (i.e., not with their involvement, especially through questioning, or by written or online notification by the data subject themselves).  
22. The respondent has taken the complainant's data from a job advertisement created by the complainant themselves in the eJob-Room of the AMS, having viewed and copied it there; however, this occurred without the involvement of the complainant and thus without the associated knowledge of the data collection and the information that the controller is required to provide in this context. The exception according to Art. 14 Para. 5 lit. a GDPR does not apply here.  
23. According to Art. 14 Para. 3 lit. b GDPR, the respondent should have provided the complainant with the information relevant under Art. 14 Para. 1 and 2 GDPR at the latest at the time of the first SMS to the complainant.
24. By failing to do so, it has violated the principle of transparency according to Art. 5 para. 1 lit. a GDPR and has thus unlawfully processed the complainant's data from the very beginning.  
D.5. Regarding the right to deletion:  
25. According to Art. 17 para. 1 lit. d GDPR, the data subject has the right to request the immediate deletion of personal data concerning them from the controller, and the controller is obliged to delete personal data immediately, provided that the personal data has been unlawfully processed.  
26. Further possible grounds for deletion (in particular a potentially justified objection by the complainant according to Art. 17 para. 1 lit. c GDPR) do not need to be addressed further.  
D.6. Conclusions:  
27. The respondent would therefore have been obliged to delete the complainant's data immediately upon receipt of their deletion request on October 3, 2020, but at the latest within the one-month period according to Art. 12 para. 3 GDPR. Since it has failed to do so, it has violated the complainant's right to deletion according to Art. 17 GDPR. This was to be established (point of decision 1).  
28. To enforce the right to deletion, the respondent was further to be instructed according to Art. 58 para. 2 lit. c GDPR to comply with the complainant's deletion request (point of decision 2).
