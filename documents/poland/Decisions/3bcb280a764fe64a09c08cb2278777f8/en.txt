In this factual state, after reviewing all the evidence collected in the case, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office considered the following: 

According to Article 34 of the Act of May 10, 2018 on the Protection of Personal Data (Journal of Laws of 2019, item 1781), hereinafter referred to as the Act of May 10, 2018, the President of the UODO is the competent authority in matters of data protection and the supervisory authority within the meaning of Regulation 2016/679. Pursuant to Article 57(1)(a) and (h) of Regulation 2016/679, without prejudice to other tasks specified under this regulation, each supervisory authority in its territory monitors and enforces the application of this regulation and conducts proceedings regarding violations of this regulation, including based on information received from another supervisory authority or another public authority. 

I. Legal basis for the processing of personal data. Article 5 of Regulation 2016/679 specifies the principles regarding the processing of personal data that must be respected by all administrators, i.e., entities that independently or jointly with others determine the purposes and means of processing personal data. According to Article 5(1)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner for the data subject (“lawfulness, fairness, and transparency”). Compliance with the aforementioned principle is necessary for the proper implementation of the accountability principle resulting from Article 5(2) of Regulation 2016/679. 

According to Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679, the processing of personal data is lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met: a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes; b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract; c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject; d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or another natural person; e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller; f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular where the data subject is a child. 

It should be noted that the provision of Article 6(1)(f) of Regulation 2016/679 does not apply to the processing of personal data by public authorities in the course of performing their tasks. Article 9(1) of Regulation 2016/679 prohibits the processing of personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, as well as the processing of genetic data, biometric data for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person, or data concerning health, sexuality, or sexual orientation of that person. Recital 35 of Regulation 2016/679 specifies the concept of personal data concerning health, stating that “Personal data concerning health includes all data about the health status of the data subject, revealing...
information about the past, present, or future state of the physical or mental health of the person to whom the data pertains.”

In this case, it is necessary to first analyze the legal bases indicated by the Administrator that authorize him to implement additional video monitoring in 2 rooms of the Department (…). According to the information provided in the letter dated August 17, 2023, the legal norm justifying the implementation of the aforementioned monitoring is Article 23a paragraph 1 point 2 of the Act of April 15, 2011 on medical activity (Journal of Laws of 2024, item 799) in connection with § 29 of the Regulation of the Minister of Health of March 26, 2019, on detailed requirements that should be met by the premises and equipment of entities performing medical activities (Journal of Laws of 2022, item 402).

Article 23a paragraph 1 point 2 of the aforementioned Act (in the wording applicable at the time of the violations of Regulation 2016/679 described in this decision) indicates that the manager of the entity performing medical activities may specify in the organizational regulations the method of observing the premises where health services are provided and where patients stay, in particular, bedrooms, sanitary-hygienic rooms, changing rooms, and locker rooms, if required by separate regulations – using devices that enable image recording (monitoring).

According to the Administrator's position, the separate regulations authorizing him to introduce temporary video monitoring in 2 rooms of the Department (…) under Article 23a paragraph 1 point 2 of the Act on medical activity are the legal norms contained in § 29 of the Regulation of the Minister of Health of March 26, 2019, on detailed requirements that should be met by the premises and equipment of entities performing medical activities. 

According to the aforementioned provision, the installation of devices enabling the observation of patients in bedrooms is permitted if it is necessary for their treatment process and to ensure their safety. A key element in this provision is the emphasis on the necessity of introducing monitoring as an obligatory condition. 

Therefore, according to the accepted assumptions, the Administrator should possess evidence, e.g., appropriate analyses, that would unequivocally indicate that only the introduction of temporary video monitoring in 2 rooms of the Department (…) would contribute to reducing the number of patient illnesses and the effective process of their treatment. It should be emphasized that only appropriate justification supported by adequate evidence would fulfill the legality condition for the introduction of image recording in accordance with the aforementioned provision. 

At this point, it should be noted that the Administrator did not possess any evidence indicating that the use of video monitoring is necessary in the treatment process of patients (e.g., to cause a decrease in their illnesses) or necessary to ensure their safety, as confirmed in the letter dated February 12, 2024, quoted: “(…) The Administrator did not conduct an analysis confirming the necessity of introducing temporary monitoring in the bedrooms of the Department.” 

At the same time, in the correspondence conducted, the Administrator informed that he had taken actions aimed at detecting improper and unintended behaviors causing infections in newborns in the Department; however, this did not relieve the Facility of the obligation to prove the absolute necessity of implementing temporary video monitoring in the aforementioned area. Moreover, based on the Report provided by the Administrator (…), there is no possibility to identify which factors actually influenced the reduced number of patient infections (from 25 to 18) – particularly considering the increased use of pharmacology in the relevant department, as mentioned in the Analysis (…), quoted: (…), as well as the loss or theft of memory cards from additional monitoring devices and lack of access to the recorded image.

In addition to unequivocally proving the necessity of implementing video monitoring, medical facilities are also obliged to introduce appropriate changes to the organizational regulations quoted: “in accordance with Article 23a of the Act on medical activity, the principles of monitoring should be specified in the content of the organizational regulations, thus it will be necessary to amend it if the facility expands the existing monitoring”[2]. The Patient Rights Ombudsman also expressed a similar view in a guide issued for managers of medical facilities titled “Video Monitoring in Medical Entities,” informing that quoted: “(…) The manager of the medical entity, at the moment of implementing video monitoring in the medical entity, is obliged to introduce into the organizational regulations the method of its conduct. The obligation imposed by the legislator on the manager of the entity highlights the need to protect the rights of individuals – patients and employees of the medical entity – from unjustified use of data collected as a result of video monitoring. In this perspective, the decision to implement monitoring belongs to the manager of the medical entity, who, based on an analysis of adverse events, has knowledge adequate to assess whether video monitoring will support the staff's work in ensuring patient safety.”

When asked by the President of the Personal Data Protection Office in a letter dated January 31, 2024, whether he had made appropriate changes to the organizational regulations (in connection with the introduction of additional monitoring devices), the Administrator incorrectly stated that quoted: “The changes to the Regulations (…) regarding video monitoring were introduced by a resolution of the Management Board (…)”. This did not constitute fulfillment by the Medical Center of the above obligation due to the fact that temporary video monitoring in 2 rooms of the Department (…)
functioned from July 1 to July 23, 2023, and therefore could not be included in the resolution of the Management Board cited by the Administrator (…). Consequently, the Facility did not meet the requirements related to the introduction of additional monitoring in this regard. 

Confirmation of the Administrator's passivity regarding the analyzed video monitoring is also the fact that he did not update Annex No. (…) to the Regulations of the (…) Facility, amended by the resolution of the Management Board (…), thereby failing to indicate new areas covered by video monitoring. At the same time, it should be noted that according to Chapter (…) of Annex No. 1 to the Resolution of the Management Board (…) of company X, dated (…), cited as (…) “(…)”. In the correspondence conducted by the Administrator with the supervisory authority, in a letter dated March 11, 2024, he directly admitted to irregularities in the aforementioned regard, declaring that cited as (…) “Annex No. (…) to the Regulations of the (…) Hospital, i.e., the list of buildings and rooms covered by video monitoring was not updated before the implementation of additional monitoring in the Hospital Ward.” 

In light of the above, due to the demonstrated irregularities in the introduction of ad hoc video monitoring in 2 rooms of the Ward (…), the Medical Center cannot rely on Article 23a, paragraph 1, point 2 of the Act on Medical Activity as a legal basis for its actions, as the mandatory conditions for recognizing the subject monitoring as legal have not been met (i.e., the lack of adequate regulations in the organizational regulations and the failure to demonstrate the necessity of implementing video monitoring). 

At the same time, it should also be emphasized that the analysis of the provisions of the Act on Medical Activity (in the wording in force at the time of the violations of Regulation 2016/679 described in this decision), as well as the provisions of other legal acts relating to this matter, clearly indicates the existence of an obligation on the part of medical facilities to respect the intimacy and dignity of patients in connection with the provision of medical services, which obligation the Administrator failed to uphold towards his patients by installing ad hoc video monitoring. 

Consequently, although the obligation in this regard was explicitly formulated in the provisions of the Act on Medical Activity only as a result of its amendment, which came into force on September 6, 2023, it cannot be considered that earlier (and thus also during the operation of ad hoc video monitoring in the aforementioned rooms) the Medical Center was exempt from taking them into account when providing services (which will be discussed further in this decision). 

In this matter, the Provincial Administrative Court in Warsaw also expressed its opinion in the judgment of September 13, 2019, case file VII SA/Wa 1545/19, LEX No. 2728947, in which one of the parties to the administrative proceedings was the Patients' Rights Ombudsman, cited as “The Court in the panel reviewing this case fully shares the position of the authority, which is based on doctrine, that the intimacy of the patient, i.e., closeness, should relate to all feelings and actions associated with the provision of health care services. Such understood intimacy consists of: caring for the patient's well-being, respect for the patient, understanding their situation, exchanging intimate information, considering the patient as a subject of the highest value. Intimacy relates both to the patient themselves - to the sphere of their exclusively personal, confidential life, as well as to relationships with other people, based on warmth and closeness. Violation of intimacy is associated with a sense of shame. The sphere of an individual's intimacy should be subject to special protection, according to which only the individual can dispose of this sensitive sphere of feelings.” 

With the aim of a detailed legal analysis regarding the introduction of ad hoc video monitoring by the Medical Center, it is necessary to refer to Annex No. 1 IV titled “Delivery Room” point 4 of the Regulation of the Minister of Health of March 26, 2019, on detailed requirements that should be met by the premises and equipment of entities performing medical activity, which directly relates to the possibility of applying video monitoring in premises designated for mothers and newborns. According to this provision, the delivery room should contain at least one room designated for mothers and the newborn in the first hours of life, after complicated births, in which direct observation or observation using cameras equipped with auto-start functions is ensured, particularly the possibility of observing the face. This provision may serve as a legal basis for such a form of observation only in situations where the health condition of newborns, due to birth complications, is unstable and may threaten their life or health. 

In the case of the implementation of video monitoring by the Administrator in 2 rooms of the Ward (…) (from July 1 to July 23, 2023), this legal basis cannot be applied, as according to the “Final Position of the Party in the Matter” provided in the letter dated August 30, 2024, in the premises where ad hoc video monitoring was installed, there were cited: “children who were transferred from sections 1 and 2 and no longer require intensive therapy. The children in these rooms are undergoing rehabilitation, learning to be fed and cared for by their parents” – thus confirming that the implementation of monitoring was not aimed at observing newborns whose life or health was at risk. 

In a letter dated August 17, 2023, the Administrator also indicated that the legal basis justifying the implementation of ad hoc video monitoring in 2 rooms of the Ward (…), in addition to Article 23a, paragraph 1, point 2 of the Act on Medical Activity, is also Article 6, paragraph 1, letter f) of Regulation 2016/679 and Article 222
**Labor Code Act.**

As indicated above, in the case of medical facilities, the rules for implementing video surveillance (and processing personal data related to it) are governed by the provisions of the Act on Medical Activity. An administrator who does not comply with the principles of its implementation arising from these provisions (as is the case with the Medical Center) cannot invoke other grounds for processing personal data, including the ground specified in Article 6(1)(f) of Regulation 2016/679, to justify its application. It should also be noted that due to the applied video surveillance in 2 rooms of the Department (…), health data were also processed, and these can only be processed after meeting one of the grounds specified in Article 9(2) of Regulation 2016/679. For obvious reasons, the ground for processing such data mentioned in Article 6(1)(f) of Regulation 2016/679 cannot be recognized as valid.

It should be pointed out that the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 established fundamental principles for processing personal data, including the principle of legality, formulated in Article 5(1)(a) of Regulation 2016/679. The principle of legality, "also referred to as the legality of data processing, means the requirement to comply with the norms established in legal provisions. The principle of lawful processing of data has a broad scope, not only concerning the provisions of the commented regulation but also regarding provisions contained in other normative acts. (…) Among the provisions concerning data processing, particular importance is given to the requirements relating to the legality of processing (also referred to as the so-called grounds for the admissibility of data processing or the grounds for the legality of data processing), specified in the provisions of Articles 6, 9, and 10 of the commented regulation. These provisions indicate the cases when data processing is legally permissible (to put it simply: when it is lawful to process personal data)" (P. Fajgielski, Commentary on Regulation No. 2016/679 on the protection of natural persons in relation to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation), [in:] General Data Protection Regulation. Personal Data Protection Act. Commentary).

It should therefore be emphasized that the use of the ground expressed in Article 6(1)(f) of Regulation 2016/679 requires an in-depth analysis of the specific factual situation. First of all, it is necessary to assess whether there is a legitimate interest on the part of the administrator or a third party. According to the position of W. Chomiczewski expressed in the commentary on Regulation 2016/679 titled "Legitimate Interest as a Ground for Legalizing Data Processing – Concept" (LEX/el. 2018), "For the realization of the objective resulting from such an understood interest of the administrator, the processing must be necessary. This means a reasonable need for achieving this objective. It should be assessed both in relation to processing in general and to the processing of specific categories of personal data. In the next step, it should be assessed whether the planned realization of the objective resulting from the legitimate interest of the administrator or a third party may infringe upon the interests, fundamental rights, or freedoms of the data subject that require the protection of personal data. Processing based on Article 6(1)(f) of the General Regulation will not be permissible if, after the above assessment, it turns out that the interests, fundamental rights, and freedoms of the data subject that require the protection of personal data are superior to the legitimate interests of the administrator or a third party."

Taking the above into account and considering the previous findings regarding the analyzed case, it should be deemed unfounded for the Administrator to cite Article 6(1)(f) of Regulation 2016/679 as the legal basis authorizing the implementation of additional video surveillance in the form of 2 monitoring devices in 2 rooms of the Department (…). The Administrator's reference to Article 222 of the Labor Code as the legal basis authorizing the introduction of temporary monitoring in 2 rooms of the Department (…) should also be considered misguided. According to the content of the aforementioned provision, the use of monitoring in the workplace is permitted if it is necessary to ensure the safety of employees or the protection of property or the control of production or the confidentiality of information whose disclosure could expose the employer to harm.

II. Information Obligation. The President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO) also found that the Administrator violated Article 13(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679, which pertains to the information obligation towards individuals whose personal data are being processed. According to Article 13(1) of Regulation 2016/679, the administrator is obliged to provide individuals from whom personal data has been collected with the following information: their identity and contact details and, where applicable, the identity and contact details of their representative, where applicable - the contact details of the data protection officer, the purposes of processing personal data, and the legal basis for processing, if the processing is based on Article 6(1)(f) – legitimate interests pursued by the administrator or by a third party, information about the recipients of personal data or categories of recipients, if any, where applicable - information about the intention to transfer personal data to a third country or an international organization and about the determination or lack of determination by the Commission of an adequate level of protection or in the case of transfer referred to in Articles 46, 47, or 49(1) second paragraph, a mention of appropriate or suitable safeguards and the possibility of obtaining a copy of the data or the place where the data will be made available. 

Furthermore, according to Article 13(2) of Regulation 2016/679, in addition to the information specified in paragraph 1 of the aforementioned article, the administrator is obliged to provide individuals from whom personal data has been collected with information necessary to ensure the fairness and transparency of processing: the period for which the personal data will be stored, and where that is not possible, the criteria used to determine that period, information about the right to request from the administrator access to personal data concerning the data subject, their rectification, erasure, or restriction of processing or the right to object to processing, as well as the right to data portability, if the processing is based on Article 6(1)(a) or Article 9(2)(a) – information about the right to withdraw consent at any time without affecting the lawfulness of processing based on consent before its withdrawal, information about the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, information on whether the provision of personal data is a statutory or contractual requirement or a requirement necessary to enter into a contract and whether the data subject is obliged to provide the data and what the possible consequences of not providing the data are.

**III. Principles of Data Processing Security**

Article 5(1)(f) of Regulation 2016/679 indicates that personal data must be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures ("confidentiality and integrity"). According to Article 5(2) of Regulation 2016/679, the administrator is responsible for compliance with the provisions of paragraph 1 and must be able to demonstrate compliance ("accountability").

The specifics of the confidentiality principle mentioned above are further detailed in the provisions of this regulation, including Article 24(1) of Regulation 2016/679, which states that taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons of varying likelihood and severity, the administrator implements appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that processing is performed in accordance with Regulation 2016/679 and to be able to demonstrate this. These measures are reviewed and updated as necessary. As stated in Article 24(1) of Regulation 2016/679, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons of varying likelihood and severity are factors that the administrator is obliged to consider in the process of building a data protection system, particularly in light of the other obligations specified in Article 25(1), Article 32(1), or Article 32(2) of Regulation 2016/679.

The indicated provisions specify the confidentiality principle defined in Article 5(1)(f) of Regulation 2016/679, and adherence to this principle is necessary for the proper implementation of the accountability principle arising from Article 5(2) of Regulation 2016/679. According to Article 25(1) of Regulation 2016/679, taking into account the state of technical knowledge, the cost of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons of varying likelihood and severity arising from processing, the administrator—both when determining the methods of processing and during the processing itself—implements appropriate technical and organizational measures, such as pseudonymization, designed to effectively implement data protection principles, such as data minimization, and to provide the necessary safeguards for processing to meet the requirements of this regulation and protect the rights of data subjects.

From the content of Article 32(1) of Regulation 2016/679, it follows that the administrator is obliged to apply technical and organizational measures corresponding to the risk of infringement of the rights and freedoms of natural persons of varying likelihood.
**Occurrences and Severity of Threats.** The provision specifies that when deciding on technical and organizational measures, one must take into account the state of technical knowledge, the cost of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing, as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons with varying probabilities of occurrence and severity. From the cited provision, it follows that determining appropriate technical and organizational measures is a two-step process. 

1. **Risk Assessment.** First and foremost, it is essential to determine the level of risk associated with the processing of personal data, taking into account the criteria indicated in Article 32(1) of Regulation 2016/679. Subsequently, it is necessary to establish what technical and organizational measures will be appropriate to ensure a level of security commensurate with that risk. These determinations, where appropriate, should include measures such as pseudonymization and encryption of personal data, the ability to ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services on an ongoing basis, the ability to quickly restore the availability of personal data and access to it in the event of a physical or technical incident, as well as regular testing, measuring, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures intended to ensure the security of processing. According to Article 32(2) of Regulation 2016/679, when assessing whether the level of security is appropriate, the controller takes into account, in particular, the risks associated with processing, especially those arising from accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or unauthorized access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.

2. **Scope of Personal Data Processing.** Considering, in particular, the scope of personal data processed by the Controller, contained on lost or stolen memory cards that were placed in video surveillance devices (i.e., images and health-related data), in order to properly fulfill the obligations imposed by the aforementioned provisions of Regulation 2016/679, the Controller was required to take actions ensuring an appropriate level of data protection by implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of the processed personal data. The nature and type of these actions should result from the conducted risk analysis, in which vulnerabilities related to the utilized resources should be identified, along with the threats arising from them, and then appropriate security measures should be determined. An incorrect assessment of the level of risk prevents the application of appropriate security measures for a given resource and increases the likelihood of its occurrence. The consequence of the above was the loss of control over the personal data of individuals whose data was on the lost or stolen memory cards, which contained recordings from video surveillance installed in 2 rooms of the Department (…). 

It should also be emphasized that due to the nature and specificity of the Department (…), the patients included children (newborns). This is significant because at the time of the loss or theft of the memory cards containing images of newborn patients, one cannot exclude the possibility of unauthorized individuals publicizing the obtained images on the internet. According to the ongoing educational campaign and the published guide titled "The Image of a Child on the Internet," the President of the UODO refers to the risks associated with the mentioned sharing of images of children, such as cyberbullying or the use of content involving them for criminal purposes.

It should be noted that Regulation 2016/679 introduced an approach in which risk management constitutes the foundation of actions related to the protection of personal data. Risk management is a continuous process that requires the data controller not only to ensure compliance with the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 through a one-time implementation of organizational and technical security measures but also to ensure the continuity of monitoring the level of threats and to ensure accountability regarding the level and adequacy of the implemented safeguards. Therefore, it becomes necessary to demonstrate to the supervisory authority that the solutions implemented to ensure the security of personal data are adequate to the level of risk, as well as take into account the nature of the organization and the mechanisms used for processing personal data. The Controller must therefore conduct a detailed analysis of the data processing processes and assess the risk, and then apply such measures and procedures that will be adequate to the estimated risk. 

The consequence of such an approach is the necessity of independently selecting safeguards based on a threat analysis. Specific measures and procedures regarding security are not indicated to the Controllers. Conducting a detailed analysis of the data processing processes and assessing the risk is an obligation of the Controller, who should then, based on such analysis, apply such measures and procedures that will be adequate to the estimated risk. 

Therefore, it is the appropriately conducted risk assessment that provides the controller with the ability to determine and implement technical and organizational measures that will eliminate or at least significantly reduce the established level of risk of materializing identified threats to the processed personal data. The risk assessment conducted by the controller should be documented and justified by the factual state existing at the time of its conduct. The main factors constituting a proper assessment, which...
should be taken into account during the analysis, namely the characteristics of the processing processes, assets, vulnerabilities, threats, and current security measures. It should be noted that significant factors in assessing risk also include the scope and nature of the personal data processed by the data controller, as these will determine any potential negative consequences for the individual at the time of a breach of their personal data protection.

The risk analysis provided by the Administrator in a letter dated March 11, 2024, in the form of a document titled "Analysis (...)" (date of preparation not specified) raises many doubts. The risk analysis conducted by the Medical Center was improperly prepared, as it did not detail the identified vulnerabilities, and the threats associated with the processing of personal data in connection with the conducted video monitoring were not correctly and exhaustively identified. Consequently, the aforementioned risk analysis did not specify the security measures aimed at mitigating the risks associated with the video monitoring conducted by the Facility.

Analyzing the submitted document, it should be stated that among the provisions contained in the risk analysis, the Administrator did not indicate the possibility of an event involving the loss or theft of external data carriers (i.e., memory cards). Therefore, it must be assumed that the Medical Center inadequately conducted the aforementioned analysis, thus failing to consider the relevant risks associated with the video monitoring conducted at the Facility. The Administrator thus neglected to conduct a risk analysis for the situation that led to the reported breach of personal data protection submitted to the President of the UODO, which should be considered non-compliant with the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 cited above. Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679 states that if the administrator or processor intentionally or unintentionally violates several provisions of this regulation within the same or related processing operations, the total amount of the administrative monetary penalty shall not exceed the amount of the penalty for the most serious violation. 

In connection with the finding of multiple violations of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 in this case (Article 6(1), Article 9(1), Article 13(1) and (2), Article 24(1), Article 25(1), and Article 32(1) and (2), as well as Article 5(1)(a) and (f) and Article 5(2)), the President of the UODO is entitled to apply one or more corrective measures specified in Article 58(2) of Regulation 2016/679. In determining the specific sanctions for the violations identified in this case, the President of the UODO utilized the guidance contained in the EROD Guidelines 04/2022 on calculating administrative monetary penalties under the GDPR (version 2.1) adopted on May 24, 2023, hereinafter referred to as the "Guidelines 04/2022". The first step in the methodology adopted there for calculating administrative monetary penalties is "to determine the processing operations in the case and assess the application of Article 83(3) of the GDPR" (see paragraph 17 of the Guidelines 04/2022). Expanding on this guidance in paragraph 24 of its guidelines, EROD recommends first determining: a) whether the circumstances indicate a single behavior or multiple behaviors subject to penalty; b) in the case of a single behavior, whether this behavior constitutes a single violation or multiple violations; c) in the case of a single behavior that constitutes multiple violations, whether attributing one violation excludes attributing another violation, or whether they should be attributed concurrently. 

The interpretation of the term "single behavior" is contained in paragraph 28 of the Guidelines 04/2022, referring to Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679, which speaks of "the same or related processing operations." According to it, "[t]he term 'related' refers to the principle that a single behavior may consist of several parts that are carried out as a result of a single act of will and are contextually (in particular regarding the identity of the data subject, the purpose, and the nature of the processing), spatially, and temporally so closely related that from an objective point of view they can be considered a single coherent behavior."

Due to the absence of any aggravating or mitigating circumstances in the case, the President of the UODO did not make any adjustments to the established penalty, leaving it at the level of EUR 300,000.00 (equivalent to PLN 1,309,590.00).
5. The President of the UODO stated that the amount of the administrative monetary penalty determined in the manner presented above does not exceed—according to Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679—the legally defined maximum penalty amount provided for the most serious violation (see Chapter 6 of Guidelines 04/2022). As indicated above, the most serious violation in this case is the violation of Article 5(1)(f) and Article 5(2), subject to an administrative monetary penalty of up to EUR 20,000,000, and in the case of an enterprise—up to 4% of its total annual worldwide turnover from the previous financial year, with the higher amount applying. The President of the UODO determined that the "dynamic maximum amount" for this violation and for this perpetrator of the violation expressed as a percentage (4%) of its turnover would amount to (…) EUR, therefore, the "static maximum amount" of EUR 20,000,000 should be applied in this case as the higher amount for the examined violation. The indicated amount of EUR 300,000.00 obviously does not exceed the amount of EUR 20,000,000.
6. Despite the fact that the amount determined in accordance with the above
the penalty amount does not exceed the legally defined maximum penalty. The President of the UODO (Personal Data Protection Office) determined that it requires further adjustment due to the principle of proportionality mentioned in Article 83(1) of Regulation 2016/679 as one of the three directives for penalty assessment (see Chapter 7 of Guidelines 04/2022). Undoubtedly, a monetary penalty equivalent to 300,000.00 EUR would be an effective penalty (due to its severity, it would achieve its repressive goal of punishing unlawful behavior) and deterrent (effectively discouraging both the Medical Center and other administrators from committing future violations of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679). However, such a penalty would be, in the opinion of the President of the UODO, a disproportionate penalty due to its excessive severity. The principle of proportionality requires, among other things, that the measures adopted by the supervisory authority do not exceed what is appropriate and necessary to achieve legitimate objectives (see points 137 and 139 of Guidelines 04/2022). In other words: "A sanction is proportional if it does not exceed the threshold of severity determined by taking into account the circumstances of the specific case" (P. Litwiński (ed.), Regulation of the European Parliament and Council (EU) 2016/679 of April 27, 2016 [...]; Commentary on Article 83 [in] P. Litwiński (ed.) General Data Protection Regulation. Personal Data Protection Act. Selected sectoral provisions. Commentary). Therefore, considering the proportionality of the penalty, the President of the UODO further reduced the penalty amount – to 35% of the amount obtained after taking into account the aggravating circumstances (see point 4 above), that is, to the amount of 105,000.00 EUR (equivalent to 458,356.50 PLN). In his opinion, such determination of the final amount of the imposed penalty will not affect its effectiveness and deterrent nature. This amount constitutes a threshold, above which any further increase in the penalty amount will not be associated with an increase in its effectiveness and deterrent nature. On the other hand, a greater reduction in the penalty amount could occur at the expense of its effectiveness and deterrent nature, as well as consistent application and enforcement of Regulation 2016/679 and the principle of equal treatment of entities in the EU and EEA internal market.

In the opinion of the President of the UODO, the administrative monetary penalties imposed in the total amount of 1,145,891.25 PLN (in words: one million one hundred forty-five thousand eight hundred ninety-one zlotys and 25/100) for the violations of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 identified in this decision fulfill, in the established circumstances of this case, the functions referred to in Article 83(1) of Regulation 2016/679, i.e., they will be effective, proportional, and deterrent in this individual case. According to the President of the UODO, the administrative monetary penalties imposed on the Medical Center will be effective, as they will lead to a situation in which the Facility will comply with the applicable legal provisions and will permanently cease violating the provisions on personal data protection by using video surveillance in the facility (i.e., in patient rooms) without legal basis. The effectiveness of the administrative monetary penalties is therefore equivalent to the guarantee that the Administrator, from the moment of the conclusion of this proceeding, will approach the requirements set by the personal data protection regulations with the utmost diligence.

The administrative monetary penalties imposed are also proportional to the identified violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679, especially regarding its severity, impact, the number of affected individuals, and the high risk of negative consequences that these individuals face in connection with this violation. The amount of the penalties has been set at a level that, on the one hand, constitutes an adequate response from the supervisory authority to the degree of violation of the administrator's obligations, while on the other hand, does not create a situation in which the necessity of payment would lead to negative consequences in the form of a significant deterioration of the Administrator's financial situation. In the opinion of the President of the UODO, the Medical Center should and is able to bear the consequences of its negligence in the area of data protection, thus the imposition of administrative monetary penalties is fully justified.

In the opinion of the President of the UODO, the administrative monetary penalties imposed meet, in the established circumstances of this case, the criteria referred to in Article 83(1) of Regulation 2016/679 due to the seriousness of the identified violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 in the context of the fundamental purpose of Regulation 2016/679 – the protection of the fundamental rights and freedoms of individuals, in particular the right to personal data protection.

Referring to the amount of the administrative monetary penalties imposed on the Administrator, the President of the UODO concluded that they are proportional to the financial situation of the Administrator and will not constitute an excessive burden for him. The financial report submitted by the Administrator on April 2, 2024, indicates that the net sales revenue as of the end of the 2023 financial year amounted to (…) PLN, which means that the administrative monetary penalties in this case, imposed in total, constitute approximately (…) % of the aforementioned amount. At the same time, it is worth emphasizing that the total amount of imposed penalties is only 1.31% of the maximum penalty amount that the President of the UODO could – in accordance with Article 83(5) of Regulation 2016/679 – impose on the Administrator for the identified violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 in this case (i.e., 20,000,000 euros).

In the opinion of the President of the Personal Data Protection Office, the administrative monetary penalties will serve a repressive function in these specific circumstances, as they will constitute a response to the violation by the Medical Center of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679.
and preventive, as they will contribute to preventing future violations by the Administrator of obligations arising from the provisions on the protection of personal data.

In the opinion of the President of the UODO, the administrative monetary penalties applied meet, in the established circumstances of this case, the criteria referred to in Article 83(1) of Regulation 2016/679, due to the seriousness of the identified violations in the context of the fundamental requirements and principles of Regulation 2016/679 – especially the principle of lawfulness, fairness, and transparency expressed in Article 5(1)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, as well as the principle of confidentiality and integrity referred to in Article 5(1)(f) of Regulation 2016/679.

Pursuant to the provisions of Article 103 of the Act of May 10, 2018 on the protection of personal data (Journal of Laws of 2019, item 1781), the amounts expressed in euros referred to in Article 83 of Regulation 2016/679 are calculated in zlotys according to the average euro exchange rate announced by the National Bank of Poland in the exchange rate table on January 28 of each year, and in the event that the National Bank of Poland does not announce the average euro exchange rate on January 28 of a given year – according to the average euro exchange rate announced in the nearest exchange rate table of the National Bank of Poland after that date.

Having the above in mind, the President of the Office for Personal Data Protection, based on Article 83(4)(a) and Article 83(5)(a) in connection with Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679 and in connection with Article 103 of the Act of May 10, 2018 on the protection of personal data, for the violation of:
- Article 6(1), Article 9(1), Article 13(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679, which resulted in a violation of Article 5(1)(a), and consequently also Article 5(2) of Regulation 2016/679, imposed on the Medical Center – applying the average euro exchange rate from January 29, 2024 (1 EUR = 4.3653 PLN) – an administrative monetary penalty in the amount of 687,534.75 PLN (which is equivalent to 157,500.00 EUR),
- Article 25(1) and Article 32(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679, which resulted in a violation of Article 5(1)(f), and consequently also Article 5(2) of Regulation 2016/679, imposed on the Medical Center – applying the average euro exchange rate from January 29, 2024 (1 EUR = 4.3653 PLN) – an administrative monetary penalty in the amount of 458,356.50 PLN (which is equivalent to 105,000.00 EUR), i.e. in the total amount of 1,145,891.25 PLN (which is equivalent to 262,500.00 EUR).

The purpose of the imposed administrative monetary penalties is to ensure compliance by X. based in K. with the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 in the future, and consequently to conduct data processing processes in accordance with applicable legal provisions.

In this factual and legal state, the President of the Office for Personal Data Protection decided as stated in the operative part.
