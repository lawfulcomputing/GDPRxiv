I. Legal basis for the processing of personal data.

16. Article 5 of Regulation 2016/679 specifies the principles regarding the processing of personal data that must be respected by all administrators, i.e., entities that independently or jointly with others determine the purposes and means of processing personal data. According to Article 5(1)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner for the data subject ("lawfulness, fairness, and transparency"). Compliance with the aforementioned principle is necessary for the proper implementation of the accountability principle arising from Article 5(2) of Regulation 2016/679.

17. According to Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679, the processing of personal data is lawful only if – and to the extent that – at least one of the following conditions is met: a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes; b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract; c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject; d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or another natural person; e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller; f) processing is necessary for the purposes of legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except...
situations in which the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject, requiring the protection of personal data, take precedence over these interests, particularly when the data subject is a child. It should be noted that the provision of Article 6(1)(f) of Regulation 2016/679 does not apply to the processing of personal data by public authorities in the course of performing their tasks.

18. The catalogue of legal grounds for the processing of personal data listed in Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679 is closed. Each of the grounds legitimizing the processing of personal data is autonomous and independent. They are also equal to each other, which means that for the legality of the data processing process, it is sufficient to meet one of them.

19. According to Article 6(3) of Regulation 2016/679, the basis for processing referred to in Article 6(1)(c) and (e) must be specified: a) in Union law; or b) in the law of the Member State to which the controller is subject. Furthermore, this provision indicates that the purpose of processing must be specified in this legal basis or, in the case of processing referred to in Article 6(1)(e) – it must be necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller. The legal basis may contain specific provisions adapting the application of the provisions of this regulation, including: general conditions for lawful processing by the controller; the type of data subject to processing; the persons whose data are concerned; entities to whom personal data may be disclosed; the purposes for which they may be disclosed; limitations of purpose; retention periods; and processing operations and procedures, including measures ensuring compliance with the law and the reliability of processing, including in other specific situations related to processing, referred to in Chapter IX. Union law or the law of the Member State must serve the realization of a purpose in the public interest and be proportional to the designated, legally justified purpose.

20. In this case, the supervisory authority is obliged to analyze the legal bases resulting from the laws indicated in the Decision of the PRM of April 16, 2020, and in the Company's Application of April 20, 2020, for the electronic transfer of data from the PESEL register, taking into account the binding nature of the authority by the judgments indicated in points 11 and 12 of this decision, resulting from the principle of binding effect of a final court judgment expressed in Article 170 of the Administrative Procedure Code. According to the judgment of the Provincial Administrative Court in Gdańsk of March 27, 2024 (case reference I SA/Gd 1109/23), "The essence of the binding force of a final court judgment expressed in Article 170 of the Administrative Procedure Code is that state authorities and courts must take into account the existence and content of a final court judgment and the overall legal consequences arising from it. Although the authority of res judicata encompasses the operative part of the judgment, considering that the essence of judicial review is expressed in legal assessment, which is articulated in the reasoning, the scope of the authority of res judicata in the sense of Article 171 of the Administrative Procedure Code is indicated by the motives. In a situation where there is a binding final court judgment and the factual findings that underlie it, it is inadmissible in another case with a different subject matter to make findings and legal assessments that are inconsistent with the matter that has been finally adjudicated. The ruling contained in the final judgment creates a legal state as it results from it. Courts adjudicating between the same parties in another dispute must assume that a given legal issue is shaped as accepted in the final, earlier judgment (cf. the judgment of the NSA of October 28, 2022, case reference III FSK 778/22; the cited judgments are available in the Central Database of Administrative Court Judgments at orzeczenia.nsa.gov.pl, hereinafter referred to as "CBOSA"). In the jurisprudence of the Supreme Administrative Court, there is also the view that although the binding effect of a final judgment only binds in the given case, it may relate to other proceedings to the extent that a specific legal issue resolved in that judgment is significant for the resolution in another case as a preliminary issue or as a further element shaping the process of applying the law by the court (see judgments of the NSA of May 31, 2016, case reference II OSK 2295/14, of November 24, 2020, case reference II FSK 1014/19, CBOSA)." Furthermore, in the judgment of November 14, 2023, case reference III OSK 2623/21, the Supreme Administrative Court indicated that "The allegation of violation of Article 170 of the Administrative Procedure Code is completely unfounded, as the President of the UODO was bound, in accordance with the content of this provision, by the judgments issued in the case of the disclosure of public information, in the form of attachments to the applications of candidates for members of the National Council of the Judiciary, including lists of judges supporting the applications, by the judgments: WSA in Warsaw of August 29, 2018, case reference II SA/Wa 484/18 and NSA of June 28, 2019, case reference I OSK 4282/18. It should be recalled that according to Article 170 of the Administrative Procedure Code, "a final judgment binds not only the parties and the court that issued it but also other courts and other state authorities, and in cases provided for by law, also other persons." It is noted that "the ratio legis of Article 170 of the Administrative Procedure Code is that it guarantees the coherence and logic of the actions of state authorities, preventing the functioning in legal circulation of decisions that are irreconcilable within the entire system of exercising power (judgment of the NSA of May 19, 1999, IV SA 2543/98). The view of the doctrine should be shared that "although the binding effect of a final judgment only binds in the given case, it may relate to other proceedings to the extent that in that judgment."
the legal issue in question has been resolved, which is significant for the resolution of another case as a preliminary issue, or as a further element shaping the process of applying the law by the court" (B. Dauter (in:) A. Kabat, M. Niezgódka-Medek, B. Dauter, Law on Proceedings Before Administrative Courts. Commentary, 2nd ed., LEX/el. 2021, art. 170). Article 170 of the Administrative Procedure Code (p.p.s.a.) has an organizing character and means that in a case where a final ruling of an administrative court has been issued, the entities affected by that ruling are obliged to recognize that the legal issue constituting the content of the court's decision is shaped as stated therein. It is therefore inadmissible for any entity other than those strictly specified in sections VII and VIIa of the p.p.s.a. to challenge or change the content of the ruling. The binding force of a final ruling of an administrative court is defined by the subject matter boundaries of the case being examined, which "according to the rules governing administrative court proceedings are determined by the actual scope of the decision contained in that judgment" (T. Woś (in:) H. Knysiak-Sudyka, M. Romańska, T. Woś, Law on Proceedings Before Administrative Courts. Commentary, 6th ed., Warsaw 2016, art. 171), and thus the scope of the ruling concerning the assessment of the legality of the action or inaction of a public administration body defines the subject matter boundaries of the case. On the other hand, the subject matter boundaries of the binding scope of the ruling of the administrative court are defined by Article 170 of the p.p.s.a. as "the essence of the binding force of a final ruling of the court as specified in the commented provision is expressed in that state authorities must take into account the existence and content of the final ruling of the court and the totality of legal effects resulting from it" (Law on Proceedings Before Administrative Courts. Commentary, ed. R. Hauser, M. Wierzbowski, Legalis 2021, art. 170, thesis 1). There is therefore no doubt that both the courts and public administration bodies are obliged to execute and respect the final judgment of the administrative court. Furthermore, in the judgment of November 7, 2019, file no. II SA/Lu 392/19, the Provincial Administrative Court in Lublin indicated that "Article 153 of the p.p.s.a. expresses the principle of the binding nature of the court and the authority whose action or inaction was the subject of the appeal, to the legal assessment expressed in the court's ruling. The legal assessment referred to in this provision may concern both the interpretation of the law itself and the lack of clarification in the controlled administrative proceedings of significant circumstances of the factual state. In turn, Article 170 of the p.p.s.a. provides that a final ruling binds not only the parties and the court that issued it but also other courts and other state authorities, and in cases provided for by law, also other persons. The binding nature of the legal assessment referred to in Article 153 of the p.p.s.a. and Article 170 of the p.p.s.a. means that such a ruling has significant importance for administrative and administrative court proceedings conducted after its issuance, concerning related subject matters."

26. Expanding on the last of the above statements, it should be emphasized that in the Guidelines 07/2020 of the European Data Protection Board (hereinafter: EDPB), it is indicated that "The definition of administration may arise from legal provisions or an analysis of the elements of the factual state or circumstances of the case." In light of the above, it should be emphasized that the rules for accessing personal data processed within the PESEL register and the purposes for which such access may occur are detailed in legal provisions, including the Population Registration Act. These provisions clearly indicate that it is the minister responsible for digitization as a public administration authority (and not the person currently holding that position) who ensures the maintenance and development of the PESEL register in order to fulfill the tasks specified in the Act (Article 6(2) of the Population Registration Act). Therefore, the indicated provisions determine that the status of the administrator is held by the Minister of Digitization (the authority), and it is on him that the obligations arising from the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 rest. Consequently, it cannot be said in such a case that the determination of the entity having the status of an administrator is decided by the actual actions of the person holding the ministerial position, since it is the legal provision that grants a specific entity (the administrator) the rights to process personal data. Regardless of the above, it should be emphasized that the analysis of the factual state in this case clearly indicates that the administrator – primarily due to the legal and factual access to the PESEL register – is, in the examined aspect, the Minister of Digitization (the authority). Thus, even if the detailed legal provisions mentioned above did not exist, the answer to the question of who the administrator is would be the same.

27. According to the definition contained in Article 4(7) of Regulation 2016/679, the administrator means a natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body that alone or jointly with others determines the purposes and means of processing personal data; if the purposes and means of such processing are determined by Union law or the law of a member state, then they may also be determined by Union law or the law of a member state.

28. Furthermore, Guidelines 07/2020 indicate that “Sometimes enterprises and public authorities designate a specific person responsible for carrying out activities related to processing. Even if a specific natural person is appointed to ensure compliance with data protection regulations, that person will not be the administrator but will act on behalf of the legal entity (enterprise or public authority), which will bear ultimate responsibility in the event of a breach of regulations due to acting in the capacity of the administrator.”

29. Adopting a different position would necessitate recognizing that each time the person holding the position of a public administration body (in this case, the Minister of Digital Affairs) changes, there would be a change in the data administrator. Such an approach is, however, not only contrary to the applicable regulations, as indicated above, but also unacceptable from the perspective of the ability to fulfill the legal obligations of the administrator, given that then the responsibility for fulfilling the obligations of the administrator arising from Regulation 2016/679 would not rest with the public administration body but with its head.

30. The head of the public administration body who has committed a breach of the law, including data protection regulations, may, however, incur criminal liability, e.g., in connection with exceeding their authority (including Article 107(1) of the Personal Data Protection Act). Additionally, pursuant to the provisions contained in Chapter I of Title 5 of the Labor Code, they may incur financial liability for the damage caused, not exceeding the amount of three months' salary due to them on the day the damage was caused (Article 119 of the Labor Code), unless the damage was caused intentionally. In that case, they are obliged to repair it in full (Article 122 of the Labor Code), which in practice allows for the enforcement of the amount corresponding to the damage incurred by the employer due to the intentional actions of the responsible person (employee) against the administrator in such a case (regressive liability). However, this decision is related to administrative liability borne by the data administrator (the Minister of Digital Affairs and the Company) for the breach of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679. As indicated, the data administrator has legal means that allow them to seek legal and financial liability – particularly in civil regressive proceedings, or by holding the person responsible for the breach of data protection regulations criminally liable for their violation. The President of the Personal Data Protection Office is of the opinion that to ensure effective protection of personal data, effective mechanisms for seeking accountability for their breach by administrators are necessary, including in relation to the employees of the administrator, including those employed in the structure of the administrator by appointment as heads of the body. However, this responsibility should be pursued through the appropriate procedures mentioned above.

II. Declaration of nullity of the PRM Decision of April 16, 2020, and ineffectiveness of the actions of the Minister of April 22, 2020, consisting of providing the Company with personal data from the PESEL register.

II.A Declaration of nullity of the PRM Decision of April 16, 2020.

31. The Administrative Court in the justification of the judgment of September 15, 2020 (case file VII SA/Wa 992/20) indicated in particular: - “(...) the only highest electoral body of the Republic of Poland authorized to take any actions related to general elections on the date of issuance of the contested decision was the National Electoral Commission [Państwowa Komisja Wyborcza – note by the author], and not the Prime Minister. III. Assessment of the President of the UODO.
35. As previously indicated, the enumeration of the conditions for the admissibility of processing personal data, contained in Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679, is exhaustive (it is a closed catalog), and each of them has an equal, autonomous, and independent character. Therefore, for the legality of the data processing process (any action constituting their processing), it is necessary and sufficient to meet one of them.

36. At the same time, considering the constitutional rule of the rule of law (Article 7 of the Constitution of the Republic of Poland), it is reasonable to assume that the legality of the analyzed actions of the Minister should be verified primarily in the context of meeting the conditions for the admissibility of data processing specified in Article 6(1)(c) of Regulation 2016/679. Given that the assessed data processing process by the Company was intended to serve the implementation of specific tasks in the public sphere, which the Company was to be obliged to undertake by the Decision of the Prime Minister of April 16, 2020, it also requires verification primarily from the perspective of meeting the requirements specified in the aforementioned provision.

37. The decisive factors for assessing the legality of the analyzed actions of the Minister and the Company in the context of data protection regulations are also the rulings contained in the aforementioned court judgments, i.e., in the judgment of the Administrative Court of September 15, 2020 (case file VII SA/Wa 992/20) declaring the invalidity of the Decision of the Prime Minister of April 16, 2020, upheld by the ruling of the Supreme Administrative Court of June 28, 2024 (case file III OSK 4524/21) and in the judgment of the Administrative Court of February 26, 2021 (case file IV SA/Wa 1817/20) declaring the ineffectiveness of the Minister's actions of April 22, 2020, consisting of making available to the Company, from the PESEL register, personal data concerning living Polish citizens who reached adulthood on May 10, 2020, and whose country of residence is Poland, as issued in violation of the legal provisions in force at the time of its execution, upheld by the ruling of the Supreme Administrative Court of March 13, 2024 (case file II OSK 1630/21). The legal assessment expressed in the aforementioned judgments of the administrative courts obviously also affects the assessment of the Company's actions (which were a consequence and implementation of the Decision of the Prime Minister of April 16, 2020) and results in the necessity to recognize that the analyzed processing of personal data by the Minister (consisting of making them available to the Company), as well as the processing of this data by the Company (in a manner consisting of obtaining it from the Minister and subjecting it to further processing for purposes related to the organization of the general elections for the President of the Republic of Poland held in 2020, which process ultimately concluded with the removal of this data) did not find support in Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679.

38. Considering that the cited judgments declared the invalidity of the decision of the Prime Minister of April 16, 2020, imposing obligations on the Company in the area of carrying out necessary actions aimed at preparing for the conduct of elections by mail and the ineffectiveness of the actions of the Minister of Digital Affairs aimed at ensuring the Company the possibility of fulfilling these obligations - i.e., making personal data from the PESEL register available to the Company, as an action violating legal provisions, it should be recognized that the assessed data processing actions (their provision by the Minister to the Company and the Company's acquisition and further processing for the purposes of organizing the elections) did not find support in Article 6(1)(c) of Regulation 2016/679. The findings and legal assessments indicated in the above judgments of the administrative courts obligate us to accept that since at the time of the execution of the aforementioned data processing actions there was no competency norm on the part of the Company authorizing it to act in the area of preparing for the elections for the President of the Republic of Poland, the provision of the subject data by the Minister to the Company and their acquisition and further processing by the Company for the purposes related to the preparation of these elections cannot be considered as processing actions carried out to fulfill the obligations resting on the aforementioned administrators, nor from the perspective of the realization of these obligations as necessary.
39. For the reasons stated above, being bound by the positions expressed in the aforementioned judgments of the administrative courts and the comprehensive analysis of the legal status applicable at the time of issuing the PRM Decision of April 16, 2020, and at the time of the transfer of personal data by the Minister to the Company (their acquisition by the Company from the Minister), and thus fully sharing them, the supervisory authority determined that in this case, the condition from Article 6(1)(c) of Regulation 2016/679, allowing for the provision of personal data by the Minister to the Company and their acquisition and processing by the Company from April 22, 2020, to May 15-22, 2020, for the purpose of carrying out tasks related to the organization of general elections for the President of the Republic of Poland scheduled for 2020, was not met.

40. For the same reason, the analyzed processing activities (the provision of data from the PESEL register by the Minister and their acquisition and further processing by the Company) cannot be assessed as legal under the conditions specified in Article 6(1)(e) of Regulation 2016/679. In both cases regulated by this provision – i.e., both in situations where the processing of data is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest and when it is necessary for the exercise of public authority entrusted to the administrator – the task and the exercise of public authority must be specified in the regulations referred to in Article 6(3) of Regulation 2016/679. Meanwhile, in the case under consideration – in light of the cited judgments of the administrative courts – such regulations did not exist. It is secondary in this situation that basing processing on Article 6(1)(e) of Regulation 2016/679 is associated with granting individuals whose data is to be processed additional, specific rights regulated in Article 21 of Regulation 2016/679. According to this provision, the data subject has the right to object, for reasons related to their particular situation, to the processing of personal data concerning them. The objection results in the administrator no longer being able to process that personal data unless they demonstrate the existence of compelling legitimate grounds for the processing that override the interests, rights, and freedoms of the data subject, or grounds for establishing, exercising, or defending legal claims. In the case under consideration, it is evident that the actions of the Minister and the Company were not associated – even in assumptions – with the intention of examining the legitimacy and respect for any position of the data subjects regarding their processing, expressed in the form of an objection.

41. For the sake of order, it should be indicated that the assessed processing activities also did not have a basis in Article 6(1)(d) of Regulation 2016/679 (the necessity of processing data to protect the vital interests of the data subject or another natural person). Even if one were to attempt to recognize that the actions of the Minister and the Company were intended to serve the realization of the vital interests of the data subjects (undefined precise purposes regarding the protection of their life and health), in the absence of the indicated administrators having competency regulations regarding the performance of activities related to the organization of elections, and at the same time the lack of specific competencies concerning health protection, it is evident that the aforementioned condition could not be met in this case. Respecting the independent, autonomous nature of the conditions for the permissibility of data processing cannot lead to conclusions that would contradict the constitutional principle of legality in the actions of public authority. This authority cannot, therefore, exceed the scope of the competencies defined by law, referring to needs related to the protection of the vital interests of the data subjects. On one hand, it seems reasonable to assume that the overall organization of the state – in its public sphere – should serve the broadly understood vital interests of citizens. On the other hand, undoubtedly certain state structures, as well as certain entities in the private sector, are dedicated to achieving goals in the area of life and health protection; however, neither the Minister nor the Company belong to them. Finally, the organization of elections, even in a special period – due to the pandemic situation – does not directly serve the protection of life or health. One cannot deny the need and justification for discussions regarding potential, adequate modifications in the area of the electoral procedure in situations where the election date fell during a pandemic; however, the primary goal of the actions of the Minister and the Company was the preparation of elections, not actions aimed at protecting the vital interests of the data subjects in the area of their health. For this reason, it is impossible to consider the criterion of necessity of the analyzed data processing process (their provision by the Minister and acquisition by the Company) for the realization of the vital interests of the data subjects (in the area of protecting their life and health) as met. According to the position presented in the literature on the subject, the concept of necessity of data processing from the perspective of protecting the vital interests of data subjects is to be understood: “(...) in a similar way as under the provision of Article 6(1)(b). This means that the processing of data for the protection of the vital interests of the data subject or another natural person must be, reasonably assessing, necessary (...) there must be a direct link between the protection of the vital interest and the need to process personal data.” (D. Lubasz, W. Chomiczewski [in:] GDPR. General Data Protection Regulation. Commentary, ed. E. Bielak-Jomaa, Warsaw 2018, Article 6). It is also emphasized that: “In order to base data processing on the discussed premise, it should be necessary to protect the vital interests of the person, i.e.
**Processing of Data**

The non-processing of data could lead to a threat or violation of the vital interests of that person. The determination of whether data processing is necessary should be made individually, in a specific case, taking into account the actual circumstances of data processing (P. Fajgielski [in:] Commentary on Regulation No. 2016/679 on the protection of natural persons in relation to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation) [in:] General Data Protection Regulation. Personal Data Protection Act. Commentary, 2nd ed., Warsaw 2022, art. 6).

42. The absence of the remaining conditions for the admissibility of data processing in the examined case does not require further justification. The analyzed data processing process was to be carried out within the framework of exercising public authority. Undoubtedly, the processing of data justified by the necessity of preparing for elections did not involve the acceptance of the possibility for data subjects to make individual decisions regarding the admissibility of such data processing actions or to determine their conditions. It is therefore evident that the discussed data processing activities were not carried out under the conditions specified in art. 6 para. 1 lit. a) (based on the consent of the data subjects) or art. 6 para. 1 lit. b) of Regulation 2016/679 (as necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party, or to take actions at the request of the data subjects prior to entering into a contract).

This premise cannot even theoretically be analyzed as the basis for the examined data processing process to the extent that it was carried out by the Minister (provision of personal data to the Company) – due to the regulation contained in art. 6 para. 1 in fine.

44. The determination that the analyzed process of personal data processing by the Minister and the Company was carried out despite the lack of a legal basis from art. 6 para. 1 of Regulation 2016/679 is associated with the finding that the indicated administrators also violated art. 5 para. 1 lit. a) of the aforementioned legal act – breaking the principle of legality, fairness, and transparency expressed therein.

45. Under the conditions in which unauthorized processing occurred by the Minister and the Company of a particularly extensive set of personal data from the PESEL register, the need for which was justified by referring to the ongoing pandemic and the necessity to protect the most essential goods from the perspective of citizens – namely, their life and health (as this was the justification for actions aimed at preparing for the conduct of elections by mail), the weight and significance of data quality rules, including the aforementioned rules and the necessity of respecting them, seem particularly significant. As indicated in the literature on the subject: “The obligation of fairness in data processing means that the administrator should not (…) exploit the difficult situation, limitations, or coercive position of the data subject, take advantage of their stronger position by imposing burdensome conditions for data processing, and should respect the will and interests as well as the legitimate expectations of the person whose data is being processed” (P. Personal Data Protection Act. Commentary, 2nd ed., Warsaw 2022, art. 5).

47. In analyzing the subject matter of this case, it is crucial to emphasize and clearly articulate that the fact of unlawful disclosure of personal data from the PESEL register and its processing by the Company endangered the proper realization of the rights granted to citizens under the Constitution of the Republic of Poland of April 2, 1997 (Journal of Laws No. 78, item 483, as amended). This is particularly important, as according to art. 47 of the Constitution of the Republic of Poland, everyone has the right to legal protection of their private life, family, honor, and good name, as well as to decide about their life.
personal. It should be emphasized that the right to privacy is one of the fundamental human rights, and its protection constitutes an essential element of the value system. At the same time, the consequences of violations of unauthorized access to information can be pursued under civil, criminal, and administrative law. The Appellate Court in Warsaw also addressed this matter in its judgment of November 19, 2019, case file VI ACa 397/18, indicating that "The right to privacy, in light of constitutional regulations, is primarily viewed through the lens of individual autonomy derived from the dignified concept of the human person, which, according to Article 30 of the Constitution of the Republic of Poland, is the source of all rights and freedoms. The right to protection of private life, which is the subject of protection under Article 47 of the Constitution of the Republic of Poland, means the individual's right to decide about their personal life. An individual, as a subject endowed with autonomy of will, has the right to independently define the area of their privacy, particularly to delineate the boundaries of access to their personal life and information about it for others. The right to privacy in the aspect of the individual's informational autonomy means the entitlement to decide which information concerning them will be available to third parties." In correlation with the aforementioned provision, Article 51 of the Constitution of the Republic of Poland also applies, according to which every citizen has the right to the protection of personal data. According to the Appellate Court in Warsaw (judgment of August 23, 2018, case file VI ACa 1927/16), "The right to the protection of personal data is an emanation of the right to informational autonomy, allowing the individual to control, to a certain extent, the manner and scope of the information concerning them that is made available. In the event of its violation, depending on the specific circumstances of the case, there may be a violation of certain personal goods, subject to a separate regime, protected by the provisions of the Constitution, as well as particularly within the framework of the regulations of Articles 23 and 24 of the Civil Code."

48. It is worth quoting the statements of the Supreme Administrative Court (NSA), which, when considering the issue of the ineffectiveness of the Minister's action, directly referred to constitutional principles in its judgment of March 13, 2024: "The protection of citizens' personal data, including their data contained in the public electronic population register (PESEL), which collects basic data identifying the identity and legal status of natural persons, has strong foundations in constitutional and EU law. Jurisprudence and doctrine emphasize that there is an inseparable connection between the right of individuals whose data is processed to the protection of that data and the right to privacy, a value protected under Article 47 of the Constitution of the Republic of Poland. In relations with public authorities, this provision should be treated as a principle, from which exceptions are permissible only when they meet the requirements specified in Article 31(3) of the Constitution (see M. Wild [in:] Constitution of the Republic of Poland Commentary, ed. M. Safjan and L. Bosek, Volume I, C.H. Beck Publishing, Warsaw 2016, p. 1174). According to Article 51(2) of the Constitution, public authorities may not acquire, collect, or disclose other information about citizens than what is necessary in a democratic rule of law. The Constitutional Tribunal emphasizes the importance of the right to privacy, recognizing that this right—like other rights and freedoms of the individual—is not absolute and may be subject to limitations. However, it is necessary that these limitations be formulated in a manner that meets constitutional requirements (see the ruling of the Constitutional Tribunal of June 24, 1997, case file K 21/96, OTK 1997/2/23). This means that the limitation of the right to privacy manifested in the disclosure by the administrator of information about citizens (personal data) can occur only when another clearly defined norm, principle, or constitutional value supports it, and the degree of this limitation remains in appropriate proportion to the significance of the interest that the limitation is intended to serve."

49. In summarizing the arguments analyzed above, it is important to emphasize that both the Minister and the Company, by unlawfully disclosing and processing personal data from the PESEL register, processed personal data in violation of applicable legal provisions, thus breaching the provisions, i.e., Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679, and consequently violating the principle of legality expressed in Article 5(1)(a) of Regulation 2016/679.

The provisions obligate the authority conducting the administrative proceedings to consider the party's request for the introduction of evidence only if the subject of the evidence is a fact relevant to the case (Article 78 § 1 of the k.p.a.). The public administration authority may disregard a request (§ 1) that was not made during the evidence-taking process or during the hearing if that request concerns facts already established by other evidence, unless they are significant for the case (Article 78 § 2 of the k.p.a.).
61. To put it differently: the party's request for the introduction of evidence must be considered by the public administration authority only when it determines that the subject of the evidence is a fact relevant to the case. The doctrine holds that the assessment of whether the subject of the evidence is a fact relevant to the case or not belongs to the authority, not to the party. This means that the authority conducting the proceedings is not bound by the evidentiary requests of the party. The authority is, however, bound in this regard by the provisions of substantive law that constitute the basis for the decision[9]. Therefore, it will be up to the authority to decide whether to admit a given piece of evidence or not. There is no doubt that the authority should first consider whether the fact for which the party has introduced the evidence requires proof. According to the judgment of the Supreme Administrative Court (NSA) of August 4, 2017 (case file I OSK
1607/16, LEX no. 2345355), in every proceeding, the authority is obliged to gather evidence that is significant for resolving the matter at hand and to make the necessary factual findings based on this evidence. However, the determination of what factual findings are necessary for resolving the matter is governed by correctly interpreted provisions of substantive law, not by the subjective belief of the party. No authority conducting the proceedings is obliged to present all evidence requested by the party. The evidence indicated by the party may only be disregarded if the disputed facts significant for resolving the matter have not been clarified. This means that only the failure to establish circumstances relevant to the case can be considered a violation of procedural rules that could affect the content of the factual findings, and consequently, the content of the ruling concluding the proceedings in the given matter.

62. The entitlement of the party provided for in Article 78 of the Administrative Procedure Code (k.p.a.) is subject to limitations due to the purposefulness and speed of the proceedings. The authority may disregard a request to present evidence if it aims to prolong the case (judgment of the Supreme Administrative Court (NSA) of November 29, 2016, case file II GSK 3322/15, LEX no. 2230883).

75. According to Article 89 of the Code of Administrative Procedure, the public administration authority shall conduct, ex officio or at the request of a party, a hearing during the proceedings, in any case when it ensures the acceleration or simplification of the proceedings or when required by law (§1). The authority should conduct a hearing when there is a need to reconcile the interests of the parties and when it is necessary to clarify the matter with the participation of witnesses or experts or by means of inspections (§ 2). Analyzing first the prerequisites from paragraph 2 of the aforementioned article, it should be noted that the need to reconcile the interests of the parties arises when "at least two parties with conflicting interests are involved in the case, which the authority should reconcile (E. Iserzon [in:] Commentary, 1970, p. 182; cf. judgment of the NSA in Warsaw of December 19, 1983, I SA 806/83, LEX no. 1689118)" (A. Wróbel [in:] M. Jaśkowska, M. Wilbrandt-Gotowicz, A. Wróbel, Updated Commentary to the Code of Administrative Procedure, LEX/el. 2025, Article 89). This is not the situation we are dealing with in this case. Moreover (as already explained above), the President of the UODO found no grounds to consider the taking of evidence from the questioning of witnesses or experts as justified. In the analyzed situation, therefore, the prerequisites from paragraph 2 of the aforementioned provision do not exist. The President of the UODO took the position that in this case, the prerequisites from Article 89 § 1 of the Code of Administrative Procedure do not exist either. As indicated in the doctrine: "The public administration authority is obliged to conduct a hearing when it ensures the acceleration or simplification of the proceedings. The assessment of whether conducting a hearing will contribute to the acceleration or simplification of the proceedings belongs to the authority conducting it" (A. Wróbel [in:] M. Jaśkowska, M. Wilbrandt-Gotowicz, A. Wróbel, Updated Commentary to the Code of Administrative Procedure, LEX/el. 2025, Article 89). In the opinion of the President of the UODO, conducting a hearing in the analyzed case...
the situation (in which, as indicated, there is no justification for conducting evidence from witnesses or experts) would be unnecessary. It should be noted that "The party's request for a hearing is not binding on the authority. The failure to conduct a hearing despite the party's request should be assessed in terms of the efficiency of the proceedings. The obligation to conduct the proceedings in a quick and thorough manner, using the simplest means possible, is contained in Article 12 § 2" (P. M. Przybysz [in:] Administrative Procedure Code. Updated Commentary, LEX/el. 2024, Article 89). In order to fully explain the reasons for not considering the analyzed request of the Company, the President of the UODO additionally refers to the content of the judgment of the NSA of December 1, 2020, case file II OSK 3866/19 (LEX no. 3121822), which stated that: "The request for a hearing in administrative proceedings does not bind the authority. It is also not a form of implementing the principle of explaining to the parties the grounds for resolving the case as indicated in Article 11 of the Administrative Procedure Code. The hearing is intended to serve the authority in establishing the factual state of the case as fully as possible, especially when the participation of witnesses, experts, or the conduct of inspections is required. Its purpose is therefore not to explain to the parties the grounds on which the authority acts, nor to debate its position. The explanation of the reasons for a specific resolution of the case is primarily served by the opportunity to familiarize oneself with the collected evidence and the justification of the decision."

80. For clarity, it should be reiterated here that the supervisory authority is bound by the final judgments indicated in points 11 and 12 of this decision and to recall the position expressed in the doctrine, according to which: “Binding pertains to both law (binding by the disposition contained in the judgment of a specific legal norm derived by the court from general norms contained in legal provisions) and factual findings. The legal assessment of a binding nature must concern the proper application of a specific provision or its correct interpretation in relation to a strictly defined resolution made in a specific case. Regarding factual findings, the binding limits the proof of specific facts established in the preliminary ruling. The ratio legis of Article 170 of the Administrative Procedure Code is that it guarantees the consistency and logic of the actions of state authorities, preventing the functioning in legal circulation of resolutions that are irreconcilable within the entire system of exercising power (judgment of the NSA of May 19, 1999, IV SA 2543/98, LEX No. 48643). (…) A final ruling of the administrative court may be – in relation to the fate of the challenged act – negative or positive. A negative ruling contained in a judgment granting the complaint has a retroactive effect concerning that act. As a result, in the proceedings before the administrative authorities, a return to the situation that existed before the issuance of the act occurs. A subsequent ruling of the court thus has a constitutive character and operates in this respect ex tunc – from the moment of issuing the decision affected by the defect (judgment of the WSA in Warsaw of August 23, 2017, II SA/Wa 1317/16, LEX No. 2366752)” (B. Dauter [in:] A. Kabat, M. Niezgódka-Medek, B. Dauter, Law on Proceedings Before Administrative Courts. Commentary, 9th ed., Warsaw 2024, Article 170).

81. In light of the above, considering the Company’s conclusions would not only be unjustified but would also lead to unnecessary prolongation of the proceedings. It should be emphasized that “The purpose of the regulation contained in Article 78 § 2 is to prevent a party from prolonging the proceedings by submitting unnecessary evidence requests, i.e., concerning circumstances that have already been undisputedly established. This concerns both circumstances established through evidence and facts that are commonly known as well as facts known to the authority ex officio (see judgment of the NSA of February 19, 2002, V SA 1918/01, LEX No. 685539). The administrative authority is therefore entitled to assess the necessity of conducting evidence and will not violate the provisions of administrative proceedings if it refuses to conduct evidence, citing the unnecessary nature of its conduct in light of the commented provision (cf. judgment of the NSA of March 17, 1986, III SA 1160/85, ONSA 1986, No. 1, item 19). The authority assesses, guided by the norm of substantive law, which facts are significant for the case, whether they require proof, and what evidence is needed to prove these facts (cf. judgments of the NSA of December 15, 1995, SA/Lu 507/95, LEX No. 27107 and SA/Lu 508/95, LEX No. 1691402)” (P. M. Przybysz [in:] Administrative Procedure Code. Updated Commentary, LEX/el. 2024, Article 78). A similar position was expressed by A. Wróbel [in:] M. Jaśkowska, M. Wilbrandt-Gotowicz, A. Wróbel, Updated Commentary to the Administrative Procedure Code (LEX/el. 2025, Article 78), citing relevant case law: “The authority may not consider a request for evidence if it aims to prolong the case (judgment of the NSA in Gdańsk of October 2, 1998, I SA/Gd 1863/96, LEX No. 37600; cf. judgment of the NSA of December 8, 2017, I OSK 1214/17, LEX No. 2456039, in which it was stated that the party's right to submit a request for evidence is subject to limitations, which in terms of purposefulness and necessity to ensure the speed of proceedings, the authority should consider each time, especially when there are insufficient arguments to challenge the previous findings).”

82. At this point, it is also worth recalling the position of A. Wróbel [in:] M. Jaśkowska, M. Wilbrandt-Gotowicz, A. Wróbel, Updated Commentary to the Administrative Procedure Code (LEX/el. 2025, Article 78), which pointed out that “In the judgment of the NSA in Warsaw of March 17, 1986, III SA 1160/85, ONSA 1986/1, item 19, it was accepted that: ‘The refusal by the appellate authority to conduct evidence to clarify circumstances already undisputedly clarified by the first-instance authority does not constitute a violation of the provisions of administrative proceedings that affected the outcome of the case, and thus does not justify the acceptance of the complaint by the Supreme Administrative Court and the annulment of the challenged decision.’”

83. In order to respond to the Company’s statements regarding Articles 7, 77 § 1, and Article 80 of the Administrative Procedure Code, it should be noted that the principle of objective truth regulated in Article 7 of the Administrative Procedure Code requires the authority conducting the proceedings to gather ex officio or at the request of a party and to consider all evidence in a manner that allows for the establishment of the factual state of the case in accordance with reality. Of course, this concerns the clarification of factual circumstances to the extent necessary for the resolution of the case. This principle is a fundamental rule of any modern procedural regulation. It should be remembered that the correct establishment of the factual state of the case is a necessary condition – although still insufficient – for its proper resolution. Article 77 § 1 of the Administrative Procedure Code, on the other hand, imposes the obligation to gather...
and consideration of the full body of evidence in the case, which takes place on two levels. Firstly, it involves conducting evidentiary proceedings regarding all circumstances that constitute legal facts, and thus those that are associated with specific legal consequences in light of the provisions of applicable law. Secondly, the collected evidence should be fully reflected in the factual justification of the decision. As for the obligation to assess whether a given circumstance has been proven based on the entirety of the evidence (Article 80 of the Administrative Procedure Code), it cannot be claimed that a circumstance has been proven if there is evidence in the evidence material, recorded in the case files, confirming it, while at the same time there is also evidence that contradicts that circumstance, unless this contradiction can be convincingly explained. It is worth noting that for the effectiveness of the objection of violation of the principle of free assessment of evidence, it is not sufficient to state the invalidity of the factual findings made, referring to a factual state that, in the opinion of the person raising such an objection, corresponds to reality. It is necessary to indicate the reasons disqualifying the authority's proceedings in this regard. In particular, the person raising such an objection should indicate what criteria of assessment the authority violated when assessing specific evidence, deeming them lacking in credibility and evidentiary power or unjustly attributing them credibility (see: judgment of the Supreme Administrative Court of August 7, 2018, case file I OSK 2051/16). In the opinion of the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO) – considering the above argumentation – it would be unfounded to allege that he violated the principles expressed in Articles 7, 77 § 1, and 80 of the Administrative Procedure Code. The supervisory authority conducted the proceedings guided by the principles arising from these provisions, including extensive reasoning supporting this in the decision.

84. In summary, in the present proceedings, the President of UODO did not take into account the evidentiary motions of the Company due to the fact that sufficient evidence had been gathered from the perspective of resolving the matter through an administrative decision, which was complete, logical, and internally consistent. From the perspective of the legal assessment of the actions of the Minister and the Company, the judgments described in points 11 and 12 of this decision are of key importance. Due to the binding nature of the legal assessment of the actions of the Minister and the Company contained therein – deemed unauthorized – the supervisory authority is not obliged to conduct any clarifying activities that would serve to assess the legality of these actions. Regardless of the number and nature of the evidence presented regarding the above circumstances, the authority could not assess the actions of the Minister and the Company differently than they were assessed by the Provincial Administrative Court (WSA) and the Supreme Administrative Court (NSA) in the aforementioned judgments. Such evidentiary activities should therefore be assessed as illusory and unnecessarily prolonging the proceedings, which obviously undermines the principle of efficiency in conducting it. The authority must assess the validity of evidentiary motions solely based on the real need for their conduct in the context of the necessity to gather information essential for the possibility of resolving the case. These activities cannot be motivated solely by the suggestion of the party that the refusal to consider the evidentiary motions will be regarded as a violation of the guarantees of active participation in the proceedings or the presentation of a complete position in the matter.

V. Administrative monetary penalties.

96. The administrative proceedings conducted by the President of UODO serve to control the compliance of data processing with personal data protection regulations and are aimed at issuing an administrative decision to apply the corrective powers specified in Article 58(2) of Regulation 2016/679.

97. In light of the above, as well as the violations of personal data protection regulations established in these proceedings, the President of UODO – exercising the power specified in Article 58(2)(i) of Regulation 2016/679, according to which each supervisory authority has the power to impose, in addition to or instead of the measures referred to in Article 58(2)(a)-(h) and (j) of this Regulation, an administrative monetary penalty specified in Article 83(5)(a) – determined that the conditions justifying the imposition of an administrative monetary penalty on both the Minister and the Company have materialized in the case at hand.

98. According to Article 83(5)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, violations of the provisions concerning the fundamental principles of processing, including the conditions for consent, referred to in Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9, are subject to an administrative monetary penalty of up to 20,000,000 EUR, and in the case of an enterprise – up to 4% of its total annual worldwide turnover from the previous financial year, with the higher amount applying.

99. Furthermore, according to Article 102(1)(1) of the Act on Personal Data Protection, the President of UODO may impose, by way of a decision, administrative monetary penalties of up to 100,000 PLN on entities in the public finance sector referred to in Article 9(1-12 and 14) of the Act of August 27, 2009, on public finances (Journal of Laws of 2024, item 1530, as amended). This limit will undoubtedly apply in this case to the Minister, as a public authority, and more specifically – to a government administration authority. The administrative monetary penalties referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2 are imposed by the President of the Office based on and under the conditions specified in Article 83 of Regulation 2016/679 (Article 102(3) of the Act on Personal Data Protection).

V.A. Behaviors subject to administrative monetary penalties and the application of Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679.

100. According to the wording of Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679, if the administrator or processor intentionally violates...
unintentionally within the same or related processing operations several provisions of this regulation, the total amount of the administrative monetary penalty does not exceed the amount of the penalty for the most serious infringement.

101. The term "processing operations" referred to in Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679 has been explained in Article 4(2) of Regulation 2016/679, where "processing" is defined as "an operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether automated or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure or destruction." Therefore, a processing operation will consist of any action to which data is subjected until its deletion, loss, or destruction.

102. Guidelines for identifying the "most serious infringement" are contained in the EDPB Guidelines 04/2022 on calculating administrative monetary penalties under the GDPR (version 2.1) adopted on May 24, 2023, hereinafter referred to as "Guidelines 04/2022." According to the position expressed therein, "the formulation ‘the amount of the penalty for the most serious infringement’ refers to the statutory maximum amounts of monetary penalties" specified in Article 83(4)–(6) of Regulation 2016/679 (see point 43 of Guidelines 04/2022). Thus, the most serious infringement in an individual case will be that for which the EU legislator has provided a higher threshold for the maximum administrative monetary penalty.

103. Given that both administrators involved in this case committed an infringement of Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679, resulting in a simultaneous infringement of Article 5(1)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, the President of the UODO was obliged to consider the regulation mentioned in the preceding paragraph to determine whether the circumstances of this case warrant the supervisory authority's use of only one or several remedial measures provided for in Article 58(2) of Regulation 2016/679 – more precisely, whether the authority should impose only one administrative monetary penalty on each of the administrators as a response to all the infringements committed by them, or separate and independent penalties for each of those infringements considered separately.

104. In determining specific sanctions for the behaviors identified in this case that violate the provisions of Regulation 2016/679, the President of the UODO took into account that the infringements attributed to both the Minister and the Company were committed within the framework of the same processing operations. The action of the Minister in providing the Company with personal data of voters from the PESEL register, as well as the action of the Company in obtaining and processing that data in connection with the actions taken to prepare for the conduct of the Presidential elections in the Republic of Poland in 2020 by mail, were indeed one-off actions undertaken as a result of a single decision by the administrator and aimed at achieving one predetermined goal. At the same time, these actions concerned the same set of data. Both described processing operations (i.e., the provision of data on one side and their processing on the other) were not based on legal provisions, as both administrators did not have any of the valid grounds for processing listed exhaustively in Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679 at the time of data transfer. The failure to meet even one condition of compliance with the law for the processing of personal data directly resulted in a violation of the norm expressed in Article 5(1)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, according to which personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject. It should be noted that the cited Article 5(1)(a) of Regulation 2016/679 establishes a general principle of personal data processing, which sets the framework for the entire data protection system. Compliance with the law in processing, understood as a superior concept, has been specified in the other provisions of Regulation 2016/679, which establish specific obligations – addressed to administrators and processors – and correlate with them the rights of data subjects. Such a provision that undoubtedly specifies this is also Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679. In this context, it should be clarified that the finding of a violation of the provisions defining the fundamental, general principles of processing (in this case – Article 5(1)(a)) does not preclude attributing to the administrator a violation of specific provisions that constitute a specification of those principles (in this case – Article 6(1)) and imposing a financial sanction for it.

105. Therefore, having established that in the analyzed factual state both administrators violated more than one provision of Regulation 2016/679 (Article 6(1), and consequently also Article 5(1)(a)) within the same processing operations, and that none of the identified infringements excludes – in the opinion of the President of the UODO – the possibility of attributing the second of them to the administrators, in this proceeding the responsibility of both the Minister and the Company should be shaped "in parallel" in relation to all the infringements committed by the aforementioned. In such a situation, the provision of Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679 will apply.

106. In summary, in this case, the administrative monetary penalty was imposed on the Company for the infringement of Article 5(1)(a) and Article...
6 ust. 1 of Regulation 2016/679. With respect to both of these violations, Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679 was applied, noting that both violations are subject to (in abstracto) the same penalty as stipulated in Article 83(5)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, which can amount to up to 20,000,000 EUR or up to 4% of annual turnover – thus, these violations should be assigned the same level of seriousness. Consequently, it is not possible to impose a penalty for the aforementioned violations that exceeds the maximum penalty for one of them, i.e., the amount of (…) EUR – due to the necessity of adopting what is referred to as the "dynamic maximum penalty" concerning the Company (see point 146 of the reasoning).

107. Conversely, the administrative fine imposed on the Minister was applied for the violation of Article 5(1)(a) and Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679, based on Article 83(5)(a) of Regulation 2016/679. This penalty, assessed collectively for the violation of both of the above provisions – in accordance with the wording of Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679 – cannot exceed the amount of the penalty for the most serious violation identified in this case. Given that both violations attributed to the Minister are categorized under Article 83(5)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, which provides for the same maximum threshold for administrative fines, these violations should also be assigned the same level of seriousness. Consequently, the penalty imposed on the Minister cannot exceed the limit set for one of these violations, i.e., the amount of 100,000 PLN, established in Article 102(1)(1) of the Act on the Protection of Personal Data concerning public authorities and other public entities.

V.B. Justification for the Imposition and Determination of the Amount of the Administrative Fine Imposed on the Minister

Conditions for the Penalty Assessment – Application of Article 83(2) of Regulation 2016/679

108. In deciding to impose an administrative fine on the Minister, the President of the UODO – in accordance with the provisions of Article 83(2)(a) - k) of Regulation 2016/679 – took into account the following circumstances of the case, which necessitate the application of this type of sanction in the present case and which weigh heavily on the assessment of the imposed administrative fine.

109. The nature, gravity, and duration of the violation, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the processing, the number of affected individuals whose data are concerned, and the extent of the damage suffered by them (Article 83(2)(a) of Regulation 2016/679). The violations attributed to the Minister carry a very high weight and an exceptionally serious nature, as they undermine the fundamental principles of personal data processing, which are essential within the entire data protection system, defining its framework. In this context, the nature of the processing carried out by the central government administration in the performance of its public tasks should also be assessed as aggravating. The Minister, being the entity responsible for maintaining and developing the PESEL register – which constitutes the central and most important state database of personal data of individuals – should not only possess the highest knowledge of the law but also guarantee respect for the rights and freedoms of citizens (including those relating to the processing of their personal data contained in the register). Meanwhile, the aforementioned made a unilateral and authoritative decision to provide the Company with the personal data of all adults as of May 10, 2020, who, at the time of data generation, had registered Polish citizenship in the PESEL register, were listed as living individuals, and for whom Poland was indicated as the country of residence, despite the fact that his actions had no legal basis at the time of this disclosure – of which the Minister was aware (as explained extensively in point 110 of the reasoning).

The President of the UODO also considers the scope of the processing and the significant number of individuals affected by the violation as an aggravating circumstance. By fulfilling the Company's request from April 20, 2020, for the transmission of data from the PESEL register, the Minister provided information about nearly 30 million individuals holding Polish citizenship, which, in general terms, constituted almost 80% of the country's population. In light of the above information, indicating the Minister's processing of personal data on a massive scale – as although the scope of this processing was solely national, it concerned the vast majority of Polish society – the violations attributed to him should be assessed as systemic, which significantly impacts their weight.

Despite the lack of evidence indicating that the individuals whose data were concerned suffered material damages as a result of the identified violations, the President of the UODO took into account, as an aggravating circumstance affecting the assessment of the imposed fine, the possibility of non-material damages occurring on the part of the data subjects, such as particularly the fear or uncertainty arising from the inability to control their personal data – given the unlawful disclosure of their data to the Company. The correctness of the assessment made by the supervisory authority is supported by the content of the judgment of the Court of Justice of the EU of December 14, 2023, in the case of Natsionalna agentsia za prihodite (C-340/21, EU:C:2023:986), in which the Court emphasized that “[a]rt. 82(1) of the GDPR should be interpreted in such a way that the fear of potential misuse of personal data by third parties, which a data subject experiences following a breach of this regulation, may in itself constitute ‘non-material damage’ within the meaning of that provision.” Not without significance in this context is the fact that the risk of non-material damages potentially affected all (approximately) 30 million individuals whose data were found.
on the DVD provided to the Company – and certainly, this risk materialized in relation to those individuals who lodged complaints with the supervisory authority regarding irregularities in the processing of their personal data by the Minister and the Company, in connection with the organization of the Presidential elections of the Republic of Poland, which were to take place on May 10, 2020.

Due to the specificity of the violation consisting of the one-time provision of personal data to the Company, which occurred on April 22, 2020, the President of the UODO could not assess, within the framework of the analyzed criterion for the dimension of the penalty, the impact that the duration of the violation had on this criterion. Given this position, this circumstance should be considered neutral, meaning it had neither an aggravating nor a mitigating effect on the amount of the imposed administrative monetary penalty.

However, regardless of the above, taking into account the nature and seriousness of the identified violations, as well as the number of affected individuals whose data are concerned and the level of identified non-material damages, the criterion specified in Article 83(2)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, considered in a comprehensive manner, should be assessed as decidedly aggravating.

110. The intentional nature of the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 by the Minister (Article 83(2)(b) of Regulation 2016/679) It should be emphasized that in analyzing this criterion, the President of the UODO was also bound, in accordance with Article 170 of the Administrative Procedure Code, by the findings of the Administrative Court and the Supreme Administrative Court contained in the judgments mentioned in points 11 and 12 of this decision. Furthermore, he took into account the guidelines contained in Guidelines 04/2022, according to which intent "includes both knowledge and purposeful action, in connection with the characteristics of the prohibited act" (see point 55 of Guidelines 04/2022). In light of the above, the supervisory authority concluded that the Minister provided the Company with personal data of voters from the PESEL register, despite being aware of the illegality of such action.

It should be noted, in accordance with the position of the administrative courts expressed in the aforementioned judgments, that according to the provisions of the Electoral Code, in force at the time of the analyzed provision, the permanent highest electoral authority responsible for conducting elections and referendums was the National Electoral Commission (Article 157 § 1). The aforementioned was serviced by the National Electoral Office, whose tasks included ensuring organizational, administrative, financial, and technical conditions related to the organization and conduct of elections and referendums within the scope defined in the code and other laws (Article 187 § 1 and 2).

Additionally, the provisions of the Electoral Code – the application of which during the state of epidemic threat or epidemic, in conducting general elections for the President of the Republic of Poland in 2020, was, however, abolished as of April 18, 2020, by virtue of Article 102(4) of the Act on Special Principles for Conducting Elections – authorized only: electoral commissioners, election officials, as well as the Chief Sanitary Inspector and the Sanitary Inspection (Article 53b of the Electoral Code) to carry out activities aimed at preparing for the conduct of these elections by postal voting. The possibility of voting by mail was reserved for a narrow group of voters, namely voters with disabilities, voters in mandatory quarantine, isolation, or home isolation, and voters who would turn 60 years old at the latest on the day of voting (Article 53a of the Electoral Code). Although on April 6, 2020, the Sejm of the Republic of Poland passed a law on special principles for conducting general elections for the President of the Republic of Poland scheduled for 2020, providing for their conduct exclusively by postal voting concerning all voters with active voting rights (as mentioned in point 31 of the justification), this law came into force only on May 9, 2020, and thus after the date of the Minister's provision of data. According to the President of the UODO, it is undisputed that the Minister – being a member of the Council of Ministers – was aware of the ongoing legislative work on the mentioned legal act. This argument is further strengthened by the fact that the person who, at the time of the identified violations of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679, was the head of a public authority recognized in this proceeding as the data controller (i.e., the then Minister of Digital Affairs, (...)), was serving as a member of the Sejm of the Republic of Poland of the IX term and participated in the voting on the draft of the aforementioned law, giving it his support. The Minister's knowledge of the legislative changes aimed at excluding the aforementioned provisions of the Electoral Code from application leaves no doubt that the Minister was aware that at the time of providing the Company with the personal data of voters from the PESEL register, the Company was not an entity authorized by law to undertake and carry out the necessary actions aimed at preparing for the conduct of the general elections for the President of the Republic of Poland in 2020. Moreover, the Minister was aware that no applicable statutory provision at that time allowed for the conduct of these elections exclusively by postal voting. 

From the above, the President of the UODO concludes that the violations attributed to the Minister are intentional, which significantly affects the aggravating nature of the imposed administrative monetary penalty.

116. Actions taken to minimize the damage suffered by the data subjects (Article 83(2)(c) of Regulation 2016/679) In the context of this criterion, the purpose of the administrator's action is significant, namely to minimize the damage suffered by the data subjects. This assessment is not altered by the fact that the Minister requested the Company to submit a declaration regarding the deletion of the personal data provided to it, the consequence of which was the cessation of processing these data by the Company. 117. The degree of responsibility considering the technical and organizational measures implemented by him under Articles 25 and 32 of Regulation 2016/679 (Article 83(2)(d) of Regulation 2016/679) Due to the nature of the violations of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 established in this case (disclosure of personal data from the PESEL register, despite the lack of a legal basis) – which violations in essence are not related to the technical and organizational measures applied by the administrator, but solely to the assessment of the correctness of the interpretation of the provisions legalizing this processing made by the Minister – it should be accepted that the criterion indicated in Article 83(2)(d) of Regulation 2016/679 has no aggravating or mitigating effect on the amount of the imposed administrative monetary penalty. It is not significant in assessing the Minister's violation of the provisions of Article 5(1)(a) and Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679.

118. The degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority in order to remedy the violation and mitigate its potential negative effects (Article 83(2)(f) of Regulation 2016/679) The President of the UODO wishes to emphasize that the behavior of the Minister subject to sanction in this case, which was inherently a one-time and irreversible action, fundamentally excluded the possibility of remedying the violations attributed to him. Furthermore, at the time of initiating this administrative proceeding, the personal data disclosed by the Minister were not being processed by the Company, which had deleted them without prior action or intervention from the supervisory authority. Consequently, at this stage, there is no possibility to assess the degree of cooperation of the Minister with the President of the UODO in order to remedy the violation and mitigate its potential negative effects. The factual state presented in this manner cannot constitute an aggravating or mitigating circumstance when imposing sanctions.

119. The manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the violation, in particular, whether and to what extent the administrator or processor reported the violation (Article 83(2)(h) of Regulation 2016/679) The supervisory authority became aware of the violation by the Minister of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 as a result of complaints received from individuals, also obtaining information from publicly available media. Given that the reported unlawful behavior of the administrator does not constitute a "personal data breach" within the meaning of Article 4(12) of Regulation 2016/679 – i.e., a security breach leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or unauthorized access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed – the Minister was not obliged to report it to the supervisory authority in the manner provided for in Article 33 of the discussed legal act. It should be noted that the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 do not impose a similar obligation on administrators in the event of a violation that does not result in a disruption of the security of the processed personal data.

**121.** The application of approved codes of conduct under Article 40 or approved certification mechanisms under Article 42 (Article 83(2)(j) of Regulation 2016/679) – As of the date of this decision, the Minister did not apply any approved codes of conduct or approved certification mechanisms mentioned in the provisions of Regulation 2016/679. However, their adoption, implementation, and application are not – as stipulated by the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 – mandatory for administrators, which means that the circumstance of their non-application cannot be interpreted against the Minister in this case. On the other hand, the circumstance of adopting and applying such instruments could be taken into account in favor of the Minister as means guaranteeing a higher than standard level of protection for personal data processing; however, such a circumstance did not occur in this case.

**122.** Financial benefits obtained directly or indirectly in connection with the violation or losses avoided (Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679) – During the proceedings, no impact of the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 on the Minister's achievement of financial benefits or avoidance of losses was established. Therefore, there are no grounds for treating this circumstance as burdensome. The establishment of measurable financial benefits resulting from the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 should be assessed very negatively. The Minister's failure to achieve such benefits, as a natural state independent of the violation and its consequences, is a circumstance that, by its very nature, cannot be mitigating. This interpretation is confirmed by the very wording of Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679, which requires the supervisory authority to pay due attention to the benefits "obtained" – that occurred on the part of the entity committing the violation.

**Establishing the amount of the fine using Guidelines 04/2022**

**123.** In determining the amount of the administrative fine imposed on the Minister in this case, the President of the UODO applied, in a limited manner, the methodology adopted by the EROD in Guidelines 04/2022. The limited scope of application of these guidelines for calculating fines imposed on authorities and public entities results from the inability to adopt the turnover (revenue) of such an entity as a measure of its size allowing for the calibration of the fine amount, so that it is effective, proportional, and deterrent. As indicated by the EROD in point 10 of its guidelines, if under national law supervisory authorities have the power to impose administrative fines on authorities and public entities, these guidelines apply to their calculation, except for Chapter 4.3 of this document ("Turnover of the enterprise for the purpose of imposing an effective, deterrent, and proportional monetary penalty"). Where national law provides for statutory maximum amounts of fines for authorities and public entities (other than those resulting from Articles 83(4)-(6) of Regulation 2016/679), Chapter 6 of the guidelines ("Legally defined maximum amount of the monetary penalty and liability of enterprises") will not apply. Therefore, taking the above into account, the President of the UODO made the calculation of the fine imposed on the Minister as presented below.

**124.** As the legally defined maximum amount of the fine that can be imposed on the Minister, the President of the UODO adopted – in accordance with Article 102(1)(1) of the u.o.d.o. – the amount of 100,000 PLN, which applies regardless of which provision of Regulation 2016/679 the violation concerns.

**125.** The President of the UODO categorized the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 established in this case (see Chapter 4.1 of Guidelines 04/2022). Violations of both Article 5 and Article 6 of Regulation 2016/679 belong – according to Article 83(5)(a) of Regulation 2016/679 – to the category of violations subject to a higher penalty of the two provided for in this legal act (with a maximum amount of up to 20,000,000 EUR or up to 4% of the enterprise's turnover from the previous financial year). They are therefore in abstracto "the most serious" of the violations provided for by Regulation 2016/679; they are more serious than the second group of violations subject to administrative monetary penalties (indicated in Article 83(4) of Regulation 2016/679).

**126.** The President of the UODO assessed the violation established in this case as a violation of high severity (see Chapter 4.2 of Guidelines 04/2022). In this assessment, the factors among those listed in Article 83(2) of Regulation 2016/679 that pertain to the subject matter of the violation (constituting the "seriousness" of the violation) were taken into account, namely: the nature, gravity, and duration of the violation (Article 83(2)(a) of Regulation 2016/679), the intentional or unintentional nature of the violation (Article 83(2)(b) of Regulation 2016/679).

A detailed assessment of these circumstances has been presented above (see paragraphs 109, 110, and 112 of the reasoning). At this point, it should only be noted that considering their cumulative impact on the assessment of the breach established in this case leads to the conclusion that the level of its seriousness (understood in accordance with Guidelines 04/2022) is high. These circumstances, which may have an aggravating or mitigating effect on the assessment of the breach, relate – as assumed by Guidelines 04/2022 – to the subjective side of the breach (to the entity itself that committed the breach and to its behavior before, during, and after the breach occurred) and possibly to other circumstances that may be relevant to its assessment. One factor, on the other hand, had a minor mitigating effect on the amount of the fine – the voluntary action taken by the Minister to mitigate the negative effects of the breach (Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679). The remaining factors from Article 83(2) (letters c, d, f, h, i, and j of Regulation 2016/679) – as indicated above – had neither a mitigating nor an aggravating effect on the assessment of the breach and consequently on the amount of the fine. Therefore, due to the existence of additional circumstances affecting the assessment of the breach, and thus the amount of the fine, the President of the UODO deemed it appropriate to further adjust the amount of the fine determined based on the assessment of the seriousness of the breach (see paragraph 126 of the reasoning). In the opinion of the President of the UODO, the appropriate adjustment to the total impact on the amount of the fine of all three aforementioned factors, significant for the assessment of the breach, will be a further increase in its amount – up to 100,000 PLN, that is to the legally defined maximum amount of the fine that can be imposed on the Minister.

128. According to Article 83(1) of Regulation 2016/679, each supervisory authority ensures that administrative fines imposed for breaches of this regulation are effective, proportionate, and dissuasive in each individual case. Moreover, Guidelines 04/2022 indicate that the last step in calculating the fine according to the methodology presented there should be to analyze whether the final amount of the calculated fine meets these requirements and to appropriately increase or decrease the fine. In conducting such an analysis in this case, the President of the UODO found that an administrative fine lower than 100,000 PLN, imposed in these specific, individual circumstances of the case, would not meet any of the aforementioned requirements. In his opinion, such a fine would not be, firstly, proportional to the very high seriousness of the breach and the overall assessment of the circumstances in which it was committed (see paragraphs 126 and 127 of the reasoning). It would also not be proportional to the size of the administrator, which is the managing body of a key ministry with substantial material and human resources and – importantly – responsible for the security of the largest databases of personal data in the country. Secondly, the fine at the aforementioned level would not be effective and dissuasive; in the opinion of the President of the UODO, it would not achieve the intended goals, namely, to realistically punish the Minister for his unlawful behavior and to deter both him and other administrators from committing the same or similar breaches in the future. It should be emphasized that the effectiveness and dissuasive nature of the monetary fine depend on its severity and real impact on the penalized entity. A fine lower than 100,000 PLN would not, in the opinion of the President of the UODO, be a realistically burdensome penalty for an entity such as the Minister of Digital Affairs. In light of the above, the President of the UODO stated that it would be unacceptable – due to the effectiveness, proportionality, and dissuasive nature of the imposed fine – to impose a fine on the Minister lower than 100,000 PLN, that is lower than the maximum amount of the fine provided for in Article 102(1)(1) of the Act on the Protection of Personal Data. The President of the UODO is also aware that a fine of 100,000 PLN will not be a fine that is realistically (in terms of financial impact) excessively burdensome for the Minister. However, imposing the fine at the maximum possible amount should be a clear expression of the President of the UODO's decisively negative assessment of the breach committed by the Minister (the authority). It is also worth emphasizing that the amount of the imposed fine results solely from the significant (in
in relation to the general principles of imposing administrative monetary penalties provided for by the provisions of Regulation 2016/679) the limitation of the penalty threat provided for public entities in Article 102(1) of the Act on the Protection of Personal Data. In the event of a monetary penalty being imposed on the Minister within the general threat defined in Article 83(5)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, this penalty – due to the very high severity and highly reprehensible nature of the violation – would have to be imposed in an amount many times exceeding 100,000 PLN. Under general principles, the maximum penalty threat for violations of the principles concerning the processing of personal data specified in Article 5 of Regulation 2016/679 and Article 6 of Regulation 2016/679, in accordance with Article 83(5)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, would amount to 20,000,000 EUR.

V.C. Justification for the imposition and determination of the amount of the administrative monetary penalty imposed on the Company

Conditions for imposing the penalty – application of Article 83(2) of Regulation 2016/679

129. In deciding to impose an administrative monetary penalty on the Company, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO) – in accordance with the provisions of Article 83(2)(a) - (k) of Regulation 2016/679 – took into account the following circumstances of the case, which necessitate the application of this type of sanction in the present case and which have a burdening effect on the amount of the imposed administrative monetary penalty.

130. The nature, gravity, and duration of the violation, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the specific processing, the number of affected individuals whose data are concerned, and the extent of the damage suffered by them (Article 83(2)(a) of Regulation 2016/679). The violations attributed to the Company have a very high weight and an exceptionally serious nature, as they undermine the fundamental principles of personal data processing, which are fundamental within the entire data protection system, defining its framework. It should be noted that Poczta Polska S.A., being a state-owned company and at the same time a designated operator for providing universal services for the years 2016-2025 (see point 6 of the justification), should – in connection with the performance of its public tasks – demonstrate the utmost diligence and respect for applicable legal provisions, including those concerning the protection of personal data. Additionally, as an entity designated by the Decision of the Prime Minister dated April 16, 2020, to undertake and carry out the necessary actions aimed at preparing for the conduct of the Presidential elections in the Republic of Poland in 2020 by postal voting, the Company should have conducted a thorough analysis of the legality of such action, as well as the process of processing these data, even before applying to the Minister of Digital Affairs with a request dated April 20, 2020, for the provision of personal data of voters from the PESEL register. This obligation, resting on every data controller under the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 (and the Company should undoubtedly be considered as such in the established factual state), was in no way diminished by the state of epidemic that was ongoing in the country at that time. It should be noted that the right to legal protection of private life, as well as the right to the protection of personal data – as rights guaranteed constitutionally – were not subject to any limitations in the analyzed context. It should be added in passing that according to the Constitution of the Republic of Poland, even during a state of war and emergency, the law defining the scope of limitations on freedoms and rights of individuals and citizens cannot restrict the right to privacy (see Article 233(1) of the Constitution of the Republic of Poland). The above necessitates a significant burdening assessment of the nature of the violations attributed to the Company.

The President of UODO also considers the scope of processing and the significant number of individuals affected by the violation as an aggravating circumstance. In order to implement the Decision of the Prime Minister dated April 16, 2020, the Company unlawfully obtained from the PESEL register the personal data of all adults as of May 10, 2020, who at the time of data generation (i.e., April 22, 2020) had registered Polish citizenship in the PESEL register, were listed as living persons, and for whom Poland was indicated as the country of residence. Thus, the Company processed – without a valid legal basis – information about approximately 30 million individuals holding Polish citizenship, which in general terms constituted almost 80% of the country's population. Although the scope of the analyzed processing was purely national (i.e., did not extend beyond state borders), its enormous scale, expressed in the processing of personal data of the vast majority of Polish society, necessitates assessing the attribution of violations to the Company as systemic, which significantly increases their weight.

As already indicated in the part of the justification of this decision dedicated to the reasons for imposing and determining the amount of the administrative monetary penalty imposed on the Minister of Digital Affairs (see point 109 of the justification), the supervisory authority did not find that the individuals whose data were concerned suffered material damages in connection with the identified violations. Nevertheless, the mere processing of personal data by the Company, despite the lack of a valid legal basis for it, caused the data subjects the possibility of incurring non-material damages, especially concerns and uncertainty arising from the inability to exercise control over their personal data. In particular, individuals whose data the Company processed in connection with the preparation for the conduct of the Presidential elections in the Republic of Poland in 2020 had no knowledge of how, and whether in accordance with legal provisions, this processing was taking place. The significance of the analyzed premise is heightened by the circumstance that the risk of incurring non-material damages potentially concerned all (approximately) 30 million individuals.
whose personal data was on the DVD provided to the Company. Furthermore, this risk materialized in relation to those individuals who filed complaints with the supervisory authority regarding irregularities in the processing of their personal data by the Minister and the Company, in connection with the organization of the Presidential elections in the Republic of Poland, which were to take place on May 10, 2020. The relatively short duration of the violations, i.e., from the moment of obtaining the contested data from the PESEL register on April 22, 2020, to the complete deletion of that data by the Company on May 22, 2020, should be considered in its favor. Nevertheless, taking into account the nature and seriousness of the identified deficiencies, as well as the number of affected individuals whose data is concerned and the level of non-material damages they suffered, the criterion specified in Article 83(2)(a) of Regulation 2016/679 – assessed in a comprehensive manner – should be regarded as aggravating.

131. The intentional nature of the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 by the Company (Article 83(2)(b) of Regulation 2016/679) should be emphasized. In analyzing this criterion, the President of the UODO was also bound by the findings of the WSA and NSA contained in the judgments mentioned in points 11 and 12 of this decision, in accordance with Article 170 of the Administrative Procedure Code. In light of the above, considering the established factual findings, the President of the UODO concluded that the Company made the decision to submit a request to the Minister of Digital Affairs on April 20, 2020, for the provision of its personal data from the PESEL register, and subsequently processed that data, despite being aware of the risk of unlawfulness of those actions – which it accepted. It should be noted, in accordance with the position of the administrative courts expressed in the aforementioned judgments, that at the time the Company submitted its request for the transfer of voter data from the PESEL register, the tasks related to ensuring organizational, administrative, financial, and technical conditions associated with the organization and conduct of elections were the exclusive competence of the National Electoral Office. Although the provisions of the Act of April 6, 2020, on specific principles for conducting general elections for the President of the Republic of Poland scheduled for 2020 provided for the transfer of these tasks to the competence of the designated operator (i.e., the Company), it should be emphasized that this regulation came into force only on May 9, 2020 – which means that by obtaining voter data from the PESEL register, the Company acted outside the law. Additionally, Article 102(4) of the Act on Special Principles for Conducting Elections suspended the application of the provisions of the Electoral Code regarding, among other things, postal voting, as referred to in Article 53a of that Code, during the period of the state of epidemic threat or epidemic, in the context of conducting general elections for the President of the Republic of Poland in 2020. Thus, it should be noted that from April 18, 2020 (i.e., from the moment the provisions of Article 102 of the Act on Special Principles came into force) to May 9, 2020 (i.e., until the moment the Act on Specific Principles for Conducting General Elections for the President of the Republic of Poland scheduled for 2020 came into force), there were no statutory provisions enabling voting in these elections by postal means. The aforementioned circumstances were known to the Company. Firstly, the issue of the legality of conducting elections in the manner described above, as raising justified doubts, was commented on widely in the public space, making it impossible to assume that the Company – as an entity directly involved in the organization of these elections – was not interested in the development of current events. In particular, the Company, being the recipient of the PRM Decision of April 16, 2020, was to bear the first economic burden associated with the implementation of the task entrusted to it. The doubts of the Company regarding its lack of competence in the aforementioned context are evidenced by the fact that the Company commissioned the preparation of three legal opinions concerning the subject of the tasks and obligations as well as the responsibilities of the Members of the Company’s Management Board in connection with the implementation of the directive contained in the PRM decision of April 16, 2020 (to which the supervisory authority already referred in point 89 of the justification). In this context, the content of the legal opinion dated April 23, 2020, prepared in the Legal and Ownership Supervision Office of PP S.A., is particularly significant for the assessment of this criterion, in which it was indicated that the Members of the Management Board had an obligation to act diligently in a situation where the Act on Specific Principles for Conducting Elections had not yet been passed. Consequently, it was recommended, among other things, to conduct an analysis of the decision (...) regarding the justification for its appeal, particularly in the context of its compliance with the law. Given the aforementioned doubts expressed by the Company – undisputed in light of the fact that the Company chose to seek legal advice to resolve them – it is impossible to accept that the unlawful acquisition of voter data from the PESEL register was the result of an unintentional error by the Company regarding the legality of this processing.

All arguments previously raised by the supervisory authority will indeed apply in relation to the violations attributed to the Company.

134. In determining the amount of the administrative fine, the President of the UODO took into account, for the benefit of the Company, within the framework of the premise concerning any other aggravating or mitigating factors applicable to the circumstances of the case (Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679), the fact of the relatively quick removal of the state of violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 by the Company. As established, the Company commenced on May 15, 2020, actions related to the irreversible deletion of all personal data provided to it by the Minister of Digital Affairs—which process it completed on May 22, 2020. The restoration of its lawful state thus occurred shortly after the cessation of the purpose that, in the Company's assessment, justified the acquisition of voters' personal data from the PESEL register (i.e., after the completion of the Company's preparations for the general elections of the President of the Republic of Poland scheduled for May 10, 2020). The deletion of data by the Company was an action entirely independent of the intervention of the supervisory authority, which argues for a reduction in the amount of the administrative fine imposed on the Company. However, the reduction made takes into account...
the circumstances surrounding the actions taken by the Company were not entirely spontaneous, but were related to the prior intervention of the Minister, who formally obligated the Company to submit a statement regarding the removal of the personal data provided to it and any copies thereof. The mentioned request for the Company to cease processing personal data obtained from the PESEL register—although it does not change the overall assessment of the analyzed premise, which undoubtedly should be classified as mitigating—suggests that it should be attributed less significance than it would have had if the Company had proceeded to remove the data on its own initiative.

135. Other circumstances indicated below, referred to in Article 83(2) of Regulation 2016/679, after assessing their impact on the identified violation in this case, were deemed neutral by the President of UODO, meaning they had neither an aggravating nor a mitigating effect on the amount of the imposed administrative monetary penalty.

136. Actions taken to minimize the damage suffered by the data subjects (Article 83(2)(c) of Regulation 2016/679) In the context of this premise, the purpose of the administrator's action is significant, namely to minimize the damage suffered by the data subjects. The President of UODO did not note any such actions taken by the Company in this case, which is why he assessed this premise as neutral. This assessment is not altered by the fact that the Company ultimately, i.e., after the cessation of the declared purpose for which it processed the personal data of voters obtained from the PESEL register, permanently deleted this data and all copies thereof. While the deletion of data may have alleviated the state of concern on the part of the data subjects, related to the loss of control over their personal data, the Company did not undertake any other actions that would directly aim to minimize the aforementioned damages. In light of the above, there are no grounds for evaluating the analyzed premise in any way other than as resulting from this decision.

137. The degree of responsibility considering the technical and organizational measures implemented by it under Articles 25 and 32 of Regulation 2016/679 (Article 83(2)(d) of Regulation 2016/679) Due to the nature of the violations of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 identified in this case (processing personal data from the PESEL register, despite the lack of a legal basis)—which violations are essentially not related to the technical and organizational measures applied by the administrator, but solely to the assessment of the correctness of the interpretation of the provisions legalizing the processing carried out by the Company—it should be accepted that the premise indicated in Article 83(2)(d) of Regulation 2016/679 has no aggravating or mitigating effect on the amount of the imposed administrative monetary penalty. It has no significance in assessing the Company's violation of the provisions of Article 5(1)(a) and Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679.

138. The degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority to remedy the violation and mitigate its potential negative effects (Article 83(2)(f) of Regulation 2016/679) The President of UODO indicates that at the time of initiating this administrative proceeding, the personal data obtained from the PESEL register was no longer processed by the Company. Consequently, at this stage, there is no possibility to assess the degree of the Company's cooperation with the President of UODO to remedy the violation and mitigate its potential negative effects. The fact that the Company voluntarily remedied the violation was, however, taken into account in its favor in the assessment of the premise specified in Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679.

139. The manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the violation, in particular, whether and to what extent the administrator or processor reported the violation (Article 83(2)(h) of Regulation 2016/679) The supervisory authority became aware of the violation by the Company of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 as a result of numerous complaints received from individuals and information from publicly available media. Given that the reported unlawful behavior of the administrator does not constitute a "personal data breach" within the meaning of Article 4(12) of Regulation 2016/679—i.e., a security breach leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or unauthorized access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed—the Company was not obliged to report it to the supervisory authority in the manner provided for in Article 33 of the discussed legal act. It should be noted that the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 do not impose a similar obligation on administrators in the event of a breach that does not result in a disruption of the security of the processed personal data, which could affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of that data. Thus, the manner in which the authority became aware of the violation should be assessed as a neutral fact, having no significance for the resolution of this decision.

140. If measures referred to in Article 58(2) have previously been applied to the administrator in the same matter—compliance with those measures (Article 83(2)(i) of Regulation 2016/679) Prior to issuing this decision, the President of UODO did not apply any of the measures listed in Article 58(2) of Regulation 2016/679 to the Company in the matter under consideration, which is why the Company had no obligation to take any actions related to their implementation, and such actions, subject to the assessment of the supervisory authority, could have had an aggravating or mitigating effect on the assessment.
**Determined Violation.**  
141. The application of approved codes of conduct under Article 40 or approved certification mechanisms under Article 42 (Article 83(2)(j) of Regulation 2016/679) The Company, as of the date of the decision, did not apply approved codes of conduct or approved certification mechanisms referred to in the provisions of Regulation 2016/679. However, their adoption, implementation, and application are not – as stipulated by the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 – mandatory for controllers, which means that the circumstance of their non-application cannot be considered detrimental to the Company in this case. On the other hand, the circumstance of adopting and applying such instruments could be taken into account in favor of the Company as measures guaranteeing a higher than standard level of protection for personal data processing; however, such a circumstance did not occur in this case.  
142. Financial benefits obtained directly or indirectly in connection with the violation or avoided losses (Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679) During the proceedings, no impact of the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 on the Company achieving financial benefits or avoiding losses was established. Therefore, there are no grounds to treat this circumstance as aggravating. The determination of measurable financial benefits resulting from the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 should be assessed very negatively. The Company’s failure to achieve such benefits, as a natural state independent of the violation and its effects, is a circumstance that, by its very nature, cannot be mitigating. This interpretation is confirmed by the very wording of Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679, which requires the supervisory authority to pay due attention to the benefits "obtained" – that is, those that occurred on the part of the entity committing the violation.

In accordance with the guidance presented in this document, the President of the UODO conducted the calculation process for the fine as outlined below.

145. The President of the UODO categorized the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 established in this case (see Chapter 4.1 of Guidelines 04/2022). Violations of Articles 5 and 6 of Regulation 2016/679 belong – according to Article 83(5)(a) of Regulation 2016/679 – to the category of violations subject to a higher penalty of the two dimensions of penalties provided for in Regulation 2016/679 (with a maximum amount of up to 20,000,000 EUR or up to 4% of the enterprise's turnover from the previous financial year). They are therefore in abstracto the "most serious" of the violations provided for in Regulation 2016/679; they are more serious than the second group of violations subject to administrative fines (indicated in Article 83(4) of Regulation 2016/679).

146. The President of the UODO determined the legally defined maximum amount of the fine that could be imposed on the Company in this case (for the violation of Article 6(1) and Article 5(1)(a) of Regulation 2016/679 assessed in this decision) – see Chapter 6.1 of Guidelines 04/2022. The value of 4% of the Company's turnover for the previous financial year is (…) PLN (equivalent to (…) euros). Since this amount is higher than the static maximum amount (20,000,000 euros), the President of the UODO is obliged to accept it as the maximum that the administrative fine imposed on the Company cannot exceed.  
147. The President of the UODO assessed the violation established in this case as a violation of high severity (see Chapter 4.2 of Guidelines 04/2022). In this assessment, those factors among those listed in Article 83(2) of Regulation 2016/679 that pertain to the subject of the violation (constituting the "seriousness" of the violation) were taken into account, namely: the nature, gravity, and duration of the violation (Article 83(2)(a) of Regulation 2016/679), the intentional or unintentional nature of the violation (Article 83(2)(b) of Regulation 2016/679), and the categories of personal data to which the violation pertained (Article 83(2)(g) of Regulation 2016/679). A detailed assessment of these circumstances has been presented above (see points 130, 131, and 133 of the justification). At this point, it should only be noted that considering their cumulative impact on the assessment of the violation established in this case taken as a whole leads to the conclusion that the level of its seriousness (understood in accordance with Guidelines 04/2022)
is high. The consequence of this is the adoption – as the starting amount for calculating the penalty – of a value ranging from 20% to 100% of the maximum penalty that can be imposed on the Company (see point 60, third indent of the Guidelines 04/2022), that is – considering the dynamic maximum amount established for the Company (see point 146 of the justification) – from the amount (…) PLN ((…) euros) to the amount (…) PLN ((…) euros). The President of the UODO considered the amount (…) PLN, equivalent to (…) euros ((…) % of the legally defined maximum penalty for the Company), to be adequate and justified by the circumstances of this case.

In accordance with the EROD's guidance presented in point 66, third indent of the Guidelines 04/2022 (referring to enterprises with an annual turnover of (…)), the President of the UODO did not find it justified to take advantage of the possibility of reducing the starting amount based on the assessment of the high seriousness of the violation, a possibility that these guidelines (in Chapter 4.3) provide for smaller enterprises with less economic power. The EROD indicates therein that in the case of (…).

149. The President of the UODO assessed the impact of other circumstances (besides those considered above in the assessment of the seriousness of the violation) indicated in Article 83(2) of Regulation 2016/679 (see Chapter 5 of the Guidelines 04/2022). These circumstances, which may have an aggravating or mitigating effect on the assessment of the violation, relate – as the Guidelines 04/2022 assume – to the subjective side of the violation, that is, to the entity itself that committed the violation and to its behavior before the violation, during it, and after it occurred, as well as possibly to other circumstances relevant to the case. A detailed assessment and justification of the impact of each of these premises on the assessment of the violation have been presented above (see points 132 and 134-142 of the justification). At this point, it should only be noted that two of them had – in the opinion of the President of the UODO – an impact on the amount of the imposed penalty. The President of the UODO considered that an aggravating circumstance for the Company (albeit to a small extent) is the previous violations of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 identified by the President of the UODO (Article 83(2)(e) of Regulation 2016/679). On the other hand, a mitigating circumstance for the amount of the imposed penalty (and this – in the opinion of the President of the UODO – significantly) is the fact of the relatively quick removal of the violation by the Company (Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679). The remaining premises from Article 83(2) (letters c), d), f), h), i), and j) of Regulation 2016/679) – as indicated above – had no mitigating or aggravating effect on the assessment of the violation and consequently on the amount of the penalty. Therefore, due to the occurrence of additional circumstances affecting the assessment of the violation, the President of the UODO deemed it justified to further adjust the amount of the penalty determined based on the assessment of the seriousness of the violation (see point 147 of the justification). In the opinion of the President of the UODO, the appropriate further reduction of the penalty amount due to the combined impact of both of these premises on the assessment of the violation is by (…) % – to the amount of (…) PLN, equivalent to (…) euros. Such a significant reduction in the amount of the penalty at this stage of its calculation is primarily the result of the President of the UODO taking into account the fact of the relatively quick removal of the violation by the Company. This action by the Company removed one of the objectives that the administrative monetary penalty is intended to serve – the necessity to restore compliance with the law.

150. Although the amount of the penalty determined in accordance with the above principles does not exceed the legally defined maximum penalty, the President of the UODO considered that it requires additional correction due to the principle of proportionality mentioned in Article 83(1) of Regulation 2016/679 as one of the three directives for determining the penalty (see Chapter 7 of the Guidelines 04/2022). Undoubtedly, a monetary penalty of (…) PLN would be an effective penalty (due to its severity, it would achieve its repressive goal, which is to punish for unlawful behavior) and deterrent (allowing effectively to discourage both the Company and other administrators from committing such serious violations of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 in the future). However, a penalty of this amount would be – in the opinion of the President of the UODO – a disproportionate penalty due to its excessive severity. According to the Guidelines 2016/679, monetary penalties: first, “should not be disproportionate to the objectives they serve” (point 138), second, “should be proportionate to the violation assessed as a whole, taking into account especially the severity of the violation” (point 138), and third, should be proportionate “to the size of the enterprise to which the entity that committed the violation belongs” (point 139). Additionally, however, the principle of proportionality “requires that the measures taken by the supervisory authority do not exceed what is appropriate and necessary to achieve legitimate objectives,” and “where there is a possibility of choosing from a greater number of appropriate solutions, the least burdensome should be applied, and the inconveniences resulting from this should not be excessive in relation to the intended objectives” (points 137 and 139). In other words, as is accepted in Polish doctrine, “[s]anction is proportional if it does not exceed the threshold of severity determined by taking into account the circumstances of the specific case”[15]. 

It should be noted that consideration for the least possible severity of the applied sanction – the last of the above-mentioned aspects of the principle of proportionality (and only this aspect) – argues in this case for reducing the amount of the penalty imposed on the Company. Weighing the impact on the amount of the penalty of the principle of proportionality (in all its aspects), while also simultaneously maintaining the effectiveness and deterrent nature of the penalty, that is, all the requirements indicated by the legislator.

According to the financial report for 2023, submitted by the Company in the attachment to the letter dated February 6, 2025, it is evident that in 2022, the Company recorded a net loss of approximately PLN 5.7 million, while in 2023, the net loss amounted to approximately PLN 621 million. For 2024, the Company did not present a financial report to the President of UODO; as indicated by the Company, the financial data is currently under review by an auditor. However, from publicly available information presented by the Secretary of State in the Ministry of State Assets (thus, in the ministry overseeing the ownership supervision of the Company), it follows that in the first half of 2024, the Company's loss amounted to approximately PLN 315 million, while for the entire year of 2024 – approximately PLN 230 million. These figures undoubtedly indicate the poor financial situation of the Company. Such high losses of the Company are, among other things – as emphasized by a representative of the Ministry of State Assets – "the result not only of changes in the postal shipment market but also of years of neglect and poor management, which have led to the misalignment of the company's model with the current challenges of the postal market." Therefore, their cause largely lies with the Company; they are not the result of extraordinary events occurring in the external environment of the Company. However, it should also be noted that the losses incurred by the Company also arise from reasons independent of the Company – they are related to the obligation to provide universal postal services, which are not subject to free market conditions. Their provision, including the determination of their price and the maintenance of necessary infrastructure, is strictly regulated by law and is subject to oversight by the Office of Electronic Communications, in order to ensure their availability, continuity, and affordability. The President of UODO is also aware that the delay in refinancing by the State Treasury of the net cost of the obligation to provide universal services incurred by the Company in the years 2021-2022, resulting from the necessity of notifying it (as public aid) to the European Commission and from the delay in the issuance of a decision by this body regarding the compliance of the refinancing of this cost with EU rules on granting public aid, had an impact on the Company's financial results during the discussed period. Ultimately, in 2024, the Company received an initial payment for financing the net cost of the obligation to provide universal services for the years 2021-2022 in the amount of approximately PLN 749 million, and the European Commission issued a decision in November of that year confirming the compliance of public aid consisting of financing this cost by the Polish state in the years 2021-2025 with EU law. This decision undoubtedly helped stabilize the Company's financial situation. Based on it, a total of approximately PLN 3.7 billion in financing from the State Treasury will be paid (PLN 749 million in 2024) or is to be paid to the Company in the coming years, including approximately PLN 963 million in 2025 for financing the net cost incurred for public services in 2023.

Analyzing the Company's financial situation in the context of assessing the severity of the penalty and its effects on the functioning of the Company, the President of UODO notes the current attempts to improve the Company's financial condition, expressed in the implementation of remedial actions within the Company, including: the adoption by the Company's management in August 2024 of the so-called Transformation Plan, the termination of the Company’s Collective Labor Agreement effective February 28, 2025, the implementation of a Voluntary Departure Program for the Company's employees adopted by the Company's management resolution on January 7, 2025. These actions aim, among other things, to reduce the costs of operations conducted by the Company, including the reduction of costs ("optimization") of employment. Only within the framework of this last program, a reduction of approximately 8,500 positions is anticipated. All these actions of the Company, undoubtedly necessary and justified by its financial situation, may impact the Company’s ability to fulfill its public tasks; they may be associated with the risk of lowering the quality of the universal postal services provided by it or disrupt the process of implementing the e-delivery system. The President of UODO is aware that imposing a penalty on the Company in this situation would be excessively burdensome and would be associated with the materialization of this risk, which would result in negative consequences for both individual citizens and entire areas of state functioning (public administration, justice system, defense, etc.). A penalty that does not take into account the circumstances presented above could also – by disrupting the Company’s remedial processes – threaten its very existence, which – given the size of the Company – would be associated with negative consequences on a large scale in the social sphere.

The depletion of the Company's financial resources by this amount during the remedial process (which in itself requires the investment of additional funds, including to finance the Voluntary Departure Program) could, in the opinion of the President of UODO, lead to the materialization of the aforementioned risks for the fulfillment of public tasks performed by the Company. On the other hand, a greater reduction in the penalty amount could occur at the cost of its effectiveness, deterrent nature, and proportionality to the seriousness of the violation, as well as at the expense of the consistent application and enforcement of Regulation 2016/679 by European supervisory authorities and in violation of the principle of equal treatment of entities in the EU and EEA internal market. The President of UODO stated that the amount of the administrative monetary penalty established in the manner presented above does not exceed – in accordance with Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679 – the legally defined maximum penalty amount provided for the most serious violation (see Chapter 6 of Guidelines 04/2022). As indicated above, the “dynamic maximum amount” applicable in this case concerning the Company is the amount (…) PLN (equivalent to (…) euros) – see point 146 of the justification. The amount of the penalty determined in the manner presented in the above points for the violations of both provisions of Regulation 2016/679 – 27,124,816 PLN (equivalent to 6,444,174 euros) – obviously does not exceed the “dynamic maximum amount” defined for each of the considered violations separately (“does not exceed the penalty for the most serious violation” – in accordance with the literal wording of Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679).

**V.D. Summary**

152. Taking into account the established factual circumstances and legal conditions, the President of UODO – for the violations of Article 5(1)(a) and Article 6(1) of Regulation 2016/679 attributed in this proceeding to both administrators – imposed administrative monetary penalties on them. The penalty of 100,000 PLN applied to the Minister of Digital Affairs was imposed on him based on Article 83(5)(a) in connection with Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679 and Article 102(1)(1) and (3) of the Act on the Protection of Personal Data. On the other hand, the administrative monetary penalty in the amount of 27,124,816 PLN (equivalent to 6,444,174 euros) imposed on Poczta Polska S.A. was imposed on the Company based on Article 83(5)(a) in connection with Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679.

153. In the opinion of the President of UODO, the aforementioned financial sanctions fulfill the punitive functions defined in Article 83(1) of Regulation 2016/679 and represent a possibly adequate, and above all, fair response of the supervisory authority to the identified violations of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679. The imposition of administrative monetary penalties in the amounts determined by the supervisory authority is necessary and justified by the nature, severity, and other circumstances of the case that the supervisory authority considered as aggravating in the context of the violations attributed to both the Minister and the Company. At the same time, the President of UODO notes that the application of any other corrective measure provided for in Article 58(2) of Regulation 2016/679 against the aforementioned administrators, particularly merely issuing a reprimand (Article 58(2)(b) of Regulation 2016/679), would not be proportional to the identified irregularities in the personal data processing process and would not guarantee that these administrators would not commit further negligence in the future.
