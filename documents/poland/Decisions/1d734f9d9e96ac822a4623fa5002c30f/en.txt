**Legal Justification**

After reviewing all the evidence collected in the case, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office has considered the following.

As of May 25, 2018, the provisions of the Act of May 10, 2018, on the protection of personal data (Journal of Laws of 2019, item 1781), hereinafter referred to as "u.o.d.o.", have come into effect. According to Article 160, paragraphs 1-3 of the u.o.d.o., proceedings conducted by the General Inspector for Personal Data Protection, initiated and not concluded before the entry into force of this Act, are conducted by the President of the Personal Data Protection Office based on the Act of August 29, 1997, on the protection of personal data (Journal of Laws of 2016, item 922, as amended), in accordance with the principles specified in the Act of June 14, 1960, the Code of Administrative Procedure (Journal of Laws of 2021, item 735, as amended). At the same time, actions taken in proceedings initiated and not concluded before the entry into force of the provisions of the u.o.d.o. remain effective.

As of May 25, 2018, the Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council (EU) 2016/679 of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (OJ EU L 119 of 04.05.2016, p. 1 and OJ EU L 127 of 23.05.2018, p. 2), hereinafter referred to as "Regulation 2016/679", is also applicable. Pursuant to Article 57, paragraph 1 of Regulation 2016/679, without prejudice to other tasks specified under this regulation, each supervisory authority on its territory monitors and enforces the application of this regulation (lit. a) and conducts proceedings regarding the application of this regulation, including based on information received from another supervisory authority or another public authority (lit. h).

The President of the Personal Data Protection Office, when issuing an administrative decision, is obliged to resolve based on the factual state existing at the time of issuing that decision. As doctrine states, "the public administration authority assesses the factual state of the case according to the moment of issuing the administrative decision. This rule also applies to the assessment of the legal state of the case, which means that the public administration authority issues an administrative decision based on the legal provisions in force at the time of its issuance (...). The resolution in administrative proceedings consists of applying the applicable law to the established factual state of the administrative case. In this way, the public administration authority realizes the purpose of administrative proceedings, which is to actualize the applicable legal norm in the area of administrative-legal relations when such relations require it" (Commentary on the Act of June 14, 1960, the Code of Administrative Procedure, M. Jaśkowska, A. Wróbel, Lex, el/2012). The Supreme Administrative Court – in its judgment of May 7, 2008, in case file I OSK 761/07 stated that: "when examining the legality of the processing of personal data, the GIODO is obliged to determine whether, at the date of issuing the resolution in the matter, the data of a specific entity are being processed and whether this is done in accordance with the law."

According to Article 105 § 1 of the Act of June 14, 1960, the Code of Administrative Procedure (Journal of Laws of 2021, item 735, as amended), hereinafter referred to as "k.p.a.", when the proceedings have become entirely or partially moot for any reason, the public administration authority issues a decision to discontinue the proceedings accordingly in whole or in part. In the judgment of the Provincial Administrative Court in Warsaw dated April 6, 2018 (VII SA/Wa 1463/17, with which the President of UODO agrees), the Court stated that "(...) according to Article 61 § 1 of the k.p.a., administrative proceedings are initiated at the request of a party or ex officio. According to the content of the mentioned provision, the request for the initiation of administrative proceedings defines the subject of that proceeding; in case of doubts: the specification of the request is the responsibility of the party, not the evaluative sphere of the administrative authority. Therefore, in the situation of initiating proceedings on the basis of a complaint (at the request of a party), the type of case and its subject is determined by the content of the submitted request; the initiated administrative proceedings at the request do not mean that the administrative authority has discretion, furthermore - this authority is not authorized to change this application. In the established case law of administrative courts, based on the above-mentioned provisions, it is accepted that in the situation of initiating proceedings at the request of a party, the content of the submitted request determines the type of case that is the subject of the proceedings, and also designates the relevant norm of substantive or procedural law that will be relevant for determining the subjective and objective scope of the proceedings. The administrative authority is bound by this request, as only and exclusively the party submitting the application defines the subject of its request and disposes of it. In other words, this request (application) of the party defines the boundaries of the administrative case to be resolved in the given administrative proceedings. In the decision of March 4, 2014...
Case No. II OW 157/13, Supreme Administrative Court

It was established that in the case of initiating proceedings at the request of a party, only and exclusively that party defines the subject of its request, and in the event of doubts regarding the scope or subject of the request, its specification is the responsibility of the party, not the evaluative sphere of the administrative authority. 

Referring the above-mentioned provisions and case law to the factual state established in this case, it should be stated that the Complainant, in the content of the complaint, accused the Company of violating the principles of personal data protection by conducting persistent and, in her opinion, unlawful debt collection activities, and made the restoration of a lawful state by ceasing the aforementioned actions of the Company the subject of her request. Therefore, the decisive significance for the resolution that was made in this case is the fact that at present, the Company does not process the personal data of the Complainant for the purpose of conducting debt collection activities. In the absence of the processing of the Complainant's personal data by the Company for the purpose described in the complaint, it is not possible to address the request of the Complainant presented in the content of the complaint, and the President of the Personal Data Protection Office is obliged to recognize this proceeding as moot and consequently to terminate it based on Article 105 § 1 of the Administrative Procedure Code.

The wording of the indicated provision leaves no doubt that in the event of establishing the mootness of the proceedings, the authority conducting the proceedings is obliged to terminate it. At the same time, the literature on the subject indicates that the mootness of administrative proceedings, as stated in Article 105 § 1 of the Administrative Procedure Code, means that one of the elements of the material legal relationship is missing, and therefore it is not possible to issue a decision resolving the matter by determining its essence. The premise for terminating the proceedings may exist even before the initiation of the proceedings, which will only be revealed in the ongoing proceedings, and it may also arise during the course of the proceedings, thus in a case already pending before the administrative authority (B. Adamiak, J. Borkowski "Administrative Procedure Code. Commentary," 14th Edition, C.H. Beck Publishing, Warsaw 2016, p. 491). The same position was taken by the Provincial Administrative Court in Krakow in the judgment of February 27, 2008, in the case with reference No. III SA/Kr 762/2007, in which it stated that "the proceedings become moot when one of the elements of the material legal relationship is missing, which results in the inability to resolve the matter by determining its essence."

In this factual and legal state, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office decided as stated in the operative part of this decision.
