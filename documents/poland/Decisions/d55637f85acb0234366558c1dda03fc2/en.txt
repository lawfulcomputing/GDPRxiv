I. States the violation by J. (…) in M. “(…)” S.A. in liquidation based in M. at (…) of the provisions of Article 24 paragraph 1 and Article 32 paragraphs 1 and 2 of Regulation 2016/679, consisting of:

1) failure to conduct a risk analysis for the processing of personal data in connection with the activities involving the editing, preparation, creation, or publication of press materials;
2) non-compliance with the provisions of the “Personal Data Security Policy in J. (…) S.A.” and the “Instructions for Managing Information Systems Used for Processing Personal Data in J. (…) S.A.”;
3) failure to implement data security measures ensuring the ability to continuously guarantee the confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services due to:
   a) lack of clear and transparent rules for handling press materials containing personal data, regulating the obligation to verify these materials before publication regarding the personal data contained therein that could identify natural persons, the publication of which may violate legal provisions or the rights and freedoms of natural persons;
   b) lack of encryption of media containing personal data used outside the processing area;
4) failure to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure regular testing, measuring, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures intended to ensure the security of personal data.

II. Orders J. (…) in M. “(…)” S.A. in liquidation based in M. to adjust the processing operations to the provisions of Regulation 2016/679, within 60 days from the date of delivery of this decision, by:

1) implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure the ability to continuously guarantee confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services, after conducting a risk analysis, taking into account the state of technical knowledge, cost of implementation, scope, context, and purposes of processing, as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons, in connection with:
   a) editing, preparing, creating, and publishing press materials, to define and introduce rules for verifying press materials regarding the personal data contained therein, the publication of which may violate legal provisions or the rights and freedoms of natural persons;
   b) using media containing data for their processing;

5. According to Article 2(1) of the Act, the provisions of Articles 5-9 do not apply to activities involving the editing, preparing, creating, or publishing press materials within the meaning of the Act of January 26, 1984 – Press Law (Journal of Laws of 2018, item 1914), as well as to statements made in the context of literary or artistic activities.

It should be clarified here that despite the exclusion of the application of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 indicated in the aforementioned Article 2 paragraph 1 of the Act to activities involving the editing, preparation, creation, or publication of press materials, which is an expression of the legislator's fulfillment of the obligation arising from Article 85 of Regulation 2016/679, the remaining provisions of this regulation apply in this area. Such exclusion does not, therefore, apply to, among others, the obligations of the controller imposed by Article 24 paragraph 1 and Article 32 paragraphs 1 and 2 of Regulation 2016/679.

6. Article 24 paragraph 1 of Regulation 2016/679 indicates that the controller, taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons with varying probabilities and severity, implements appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that processing is performed in accordance with this regulation and to be able to demonstrate it. These measures are reviewed and updated as necessary.

7. According to Article 32 paragraph 1 of Regulation 2016/679, taking into account the state of technical knowledge, the cost of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons with varying probabilities of occurrence and severity, the controller and the processor implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to that risk, including, where appropriate:
a) pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data in a timely manner in the event of a physical or technical incident;
d) regular testing, measuring, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.
According to paragraph 2 of this article, when assessing whether the level of security is appropriate, the controller takes into account, in particular, the risks associated with processing, especially those resulting from accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or unauthorized access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.

8. It should be indicated, therefore, that the legal basis for the protection of personal data introduced by Regulation 2016/679 includes the obligation to ensure the security of processed data, as specified, among others, in Article 32 paragraph 1 of Regulation 2016/679. This provision introduces a risk-based approach, simultaneously indicating the criteria based on which the controller should select appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security corresponding to that risk. In addition to the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons, the state of technical knowledge, the cost of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing should also be taken into account. Regulation 2016/679 introduced an approach in which risk management is the foundation of activities related to the protection of personal data and is characterized as a continuous process.

9. The determination of appropriate technical and organizational measures is a two-step process. Firstly, it is essential to determine the level of risk associated with the processing of personal data, taking into account the criteria indicated in Article 32 paragraph 1 of Regulation 2016/679, and then to select such technical and organizational measures that will ensure an appropriate level of security in relation to that risk. In appropriate cases, measures such as pseudonymization and encryption of personal data, the ability to ensure ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services, the ability to quickly restore the availability of personal data and access to it in the event of a physical or technical incident, as well as regular testing, measuring, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing should be implemented.

10. The entity that processes personal data is obliged not only to ensure compliance with the guidelines of Regulation 2016/679 through a one-time implementation of organizational and technical security measures but also to ensure the continuity of monitoring the level of threats and to ensure accountability regarding the level and adequacy of the implemented safeguards. This means that it becomes necessary to demonstrate to the supervisory authority that the solutions implemented to ensure the security of personal data are adequate to the level of risk, as well as taking into account the nature of the given organization and the mechanisms used for processing personal data. The controller must conduct a detailed analysis of the data processing processes and assess the risk, and then apply such measures and procedures that will be adequate to the estimated risk. The consequence of such an orientation is the abandonment of lists of requirements regarding security imposed by...
the legislator, for the purpose of independently selecting safeguards based on a threat analysis. Administrators are not provided with specific means and procedures regarding security. The Provincial Administrative Court in Warsaw, in the justification of its judgment of August 26, 2020, case no. II SA/Wa 2826/19, indicated that “(...) This provision [Article 32 of Regulation 2016/679] does not require the data controller to implement any technical and organizational measures that are to constitute means of personal data protection, but requires the implementation of adequate measures. Such adequacy should be assessed in terms of the manner and purpose for which personal data is processed, but also the risk associated with the processing of that personal data, which may vary in severity. The adopted measures must be effective; in specific cases, some measures will need to mitigate low risk, while others must mitigate high risk. It is important that all measures (as well as each one individually) are adequate and proportional to the level of risk (...) technical and organizational activities are the responsibility of the personal data controller, but they cannot be selected in a completely free and voluntary manner, without taking into account the level of risk and the nature of the protected personal data (...)”.

11. In light of the above, it should be noted that the risk analysis conducted by the data controller should be documented and justified primarily based on the determination of the factual state existing at the time of its conduct. It is necessary to take into account, in particular, the characteristics of the processes occurring, assets, vulnerabilities, threats, and existing safeguards within the framework of the ongoing personal data processing activities. It is also essential not to overlook the scope and nature of the personal data processed during the activities carried out by the data controller, as the potential negative consequences for the individual in the event of a breach of their personal data protection will depend precisely on the scope and nature of the disclosed data.

12. For the risk analysis to be conducted properly, threats that may arise in the data processing processes should be defined for each asset. The term asset is used to indicate everything that constitutes value for the data controller. Certain assets will have a higher value than others, and they should also be assessed and secured from this perspective. The interconnections of the existing assets are also very important; for example, the confidentiality of assets (personal data) will depend on the type and manner of processing that data. Determining the value of assets is necessary to assess the consequences of a potential incident (breach of personal data protection). It is obvious that a wide range of personal data or the processing of personal data referred to in Article 9(1) or Article 10 of Regulation 2016/679 may cause (in the event of a breach of personal data protection) far-reaching negative consequences for the individuals concerned, and should therefore be assessed as high-value assets, and consequently, the level of their protection should be correspondingly high.

13. The determination of the safeguards applied is necessary, among other things, to avoid duplication. It is also imperative to verify the effectiveness of these safeguards, as the existence of an untested safeguard may first eliminate its value, and second, may create a false sense of security and may result in the omission (failure to detect) a critical vulnerability, which, if exploited, could lead to negative consequences, including in particular a breach of personal data protection. According to the ISO/IEC 27000 standard, a vulnerability is defined as a weakness or gap in security that, if exploited by a given threat, can disrupt operations and may also lead to incidents or breaches of personal data protection. Identifying threats involves determining what threats may arise and from what direction (cause).

14. One method of conducting a risk analysis is, for example, defining the level of risk as the product of the probability and impact of a given incident occurring. The necessity of conducting a risk analysis is also emphasized in case law. In this regard, the Administrative Court in Warsaw stated, among other things, in its judgment of May 13, 2021, case no. II SA/Wa 2129/20, that “The data controller should therefore conduct a risk analysis and assess what threats it is facing.” This court similarly expressed itself, among others, in its judgment of October 5, 2023, case no. II SA/Wa 502/23.

During the conducted inspection, it was established that within the framework of the adopted organizational measures aimed at ensuring the security of data processing, in order to fulfill the obligations arising from Regulation 2016/679, the Company, by Management Board Resolution No. (…) of (…) December 2018 of the President of the Management Board J. (…) S.A. regarding personal data protection in J. (…) S.A., introduced the “Personal Data Security Policy in J. (…) S.A.” as Annex No. 1 to this resolution and the “Instruction for Managing IT Systems Used for Processing Personal Data in J. (…) S.A.” as Annex No. 2 to the mentioned resolution. The introduced “Personal Data Security Policy in J. (…) S.A.” in § 5(4) indicates that the basis for the designed and maintained personal data security system is a risk analysis conducted.
temporarily to new and changing business processes (data processing activities) that may pose a real threat to the privacy of individuals whose data is processed in the Company; a regular risk assessment (at specified intervals) is conducted in relation to previously analyzed processes in order to update the implemented system solutions and applied safeguards. In § 5 item 6 of the aforementioned document, it is indicated that the risk analysis process is conducted in the manner and scope specified by a separate instruction that is not part of this Policy, within a period not exceeding two years from the last risk assessment or in the event of a change in the scope and principles of personal data processing within a specific data processing activity.

16. Nevertheless, the conducted control activities revealed that the Company, as the data controller, did not carry out such a risk analysis for the operations of personal data processing related to the activity of editing, preparing, creating, or publishing press materials. Such a risk analysis was also not presented to the supervisory authority in response to the notification of the initiation of administrative proceedings. The provisions of the "Instruction for Managing IT Systems Used for Processing Personal Data in J. The implementation of safeguards should occur through the encryption of media containing personal data. However, during the control activities, it was found that (…), smartphones and disks in laptops are not encrypted. The above leads to the conclusion that the Company, despite the introduction, or rather only the development of the "Personal Data Security Policy in J. (…) S.A." and the "Instruction for Managing IT Systems Used for Processing Personal Data in J. (…) S.A.", has not fully implemented them, as the provisions contained therein are not adhered to by the Company. The deficiencies in this regard indicate a violation of the obligations of the controller arising from Article 24(1) and Article 32(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679.

17. It should be noted that the aforementioned Article 24(1) of Regulation 2016/679 includes the obligation for the controller to demonstrate that processing is carried out in accordance with Regulation 2016/679, and thus to show that it has fulfilled the obligation to implement "appropriate technical and organizational measures," taking into account the concept of "risk" as significant for each of the criteria that the controller must consider when assessing the appropriateness of such measures, namely: the nature, scope, context, and purpose of processing, as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons with varying probabilities and severity of the threat. Furthermore, the cited legal norm requires that the measures used by the controller be reviewed and updated as necessary. Article 32(1) of Regulation 2016/679 elaborates on and specifies the aforementioned general obligations of the controller to ensure the security of processing, indicating the basic technical and organizational measures that the data controller is obliged to implement to ensure an appropriate level of protection. Moreover, when assessing the appropriateness of the level of security of the adopted measures, Article 32(2) of Regulation 2016/679 requires that the controller take into account the risks associated with processing, in particular those arising from accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or unauthorized access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.

18. Deficiencies in conducting a risk assessment constitute a violation of the aforementioned requirements of Regulation 2016/679 and indicate that the Controller did not identify the threats that may arise in connection with the Company's activities related to editing, preparing, creating, or publishing press materials, nor did it identify the risks for data subjects that may result from this. This, in turn, translates into the inability to assess what technical and organizational measures would be appropriate to the threats arising from data processing. Consequently, without conducting a risk analysis, the Company cannot effectively identify and manage threats, which may lead to violations of the rights or freedoms of individuals whose data is processed. It is unable to demonstrate that the technical and organizational measures it employs to ensure the security of processed data, corresponding to the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of those individuals when processing their personal data, are appropriately selected.

19. It should be pointed out that the Press Law in Article 13(2) establishes a prohibition on publishing in the press the image and other personal data of individuals against whom preparatory or judicial proceedings are being conducted, as well as the image and other personal data of witnesses, victims, and injured parties, unless these individuals consent to it. The concept of consent as a condition for the lawful publication of information obtained from individuals providing it is also referenced in Article 14 of the Press Law, which stipulates that the publication or dissemination in any other way of information recorded through audio and visual recordings requires the consent of the individuals providing the information (section 1). In the further part of this provision...
provides that information and data concerning the private sphere of life may not be published without the consent of the interested party, unless it is directly related to the public activity of that person (sec. 6). The prohibition on publication thus extends to all information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, as defined in Article 4, point 1 of Regulation 2016/679. According to this provision, "personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, identification number, location data, online identifier, or one or more specific factors characteristic of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person. Compliance with the aforementioned prohibition on publishing personal data under data protection regulations imposes on the data controller the obligation to implement appropriate security measures that ensure the ability to continuously guarantee confidentiality, as well as integrity, availability, and resilience of the systems and services for processing data used in activities related to the editing, preparation, creation, or publication of press materials.

20. During the inspection, it was established that the verification of press materials containing personal data, the publication of which may be subject to restrictions arising from press law, does not take place based on any procedure for handling such materials, as such a procedure or instruction has not been adopted by the Company as the data controller. The Administrator has not defined principles in this regard in any other way either. Although the Company has documentation related to the protection of personal data, regulating, among other things, the obligations of employees when processing personal data, the basic principles of data protection, methods of securing data, or the obligation to maintain confidentiality, i.e., "Data Protection Security Policy at J. (...) S.A.", which employees are familiarized with upon hiring (and the provisions of which, as previously mentioned, are not implemented by the Administrator), the scope of the obligations of employees engaged in the processing of personal data, a declaration of familiarization with the regulations on personal data protection, and the ethical principles of journalism, it cannot be inferred from this documentation any principles regarding the verification of press material prior to its publication, regulating issues such as, for example, the identification of the person or persons responsible within the Company for adhering to the prohibition on publishing data, the method of documenting and verifying consent for publication of data, the method of anonymizing data, etc. The verification of materials is based solely on the organizational structure of the editorial department and the hierarchical subordination of journalists (editors) working in a given editorial office.

21. In the opinion of the President of the Office, the lack of a transparent procedure for verifying press materials intended for publication in terms of their compliance with the law and whether they do not violate the rights or freedoms of natural persons before their publication signifies complete discretion in this regard and a lack of control over whether in each case the publication of press materials occurred after conducting the aforementioned verification. The absence of encryption of devices and media used for processing personal data transmitted outside the processing area, as well as the lack of adopted principles for verifying press materials prior to their publication regarding the personal data contained therein, confirms that the data security measures functioning within the Company do not ensure the ability to continuously guarantee confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of the processing systems and services referred to in Article 32, sec. 1, lit. b) of Regulation 2016/679.

S.A." developed in 2018 and the "Instruction for Managing Information Systems Used for Processing Personal Data at J. (...) Such actions had not been undertaken by the Administrator, even though such an obligation was specified in § 15 of the Policy, and additionally supervised by the data protection officer based on § 18 of the adopted "Data Protection Security Policy at J. (...) S.A." The described requirement for reviewing the aforementioned documentation fits into the obligation of the Administrator to conduct regular testing, measuring, and assessing the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of personal data processing, as required by Article 32, sec. 1, lit. d) of Regulation 2016/679. The Administrator is obliged to verify both
**Assessment of the Effectiveness of Security Measures**

1. The effectiveness of the security measures applied must be assessed at every stage of processing. The complexity of this verification should be evaluated in terms of adequacy to risks and proportionality concerning the state of technical knowledge, implementation costs, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing. Testing, measuring, and evaluating, in order to fulfill the requirement arising from Article 32(1)(d) of Regulation 2016/679, must be conducted regularly, consciously planned, organized, and documented within specified time frames, regardless of changes in the organization and the course of data processing processes.

2. New risks or threats may materialize or be revealed spontaneously, entirely independently of the administrator, and this fact should also be taken into account both when building the personal data protection system and during its implementation. This, in turn, defines the necessity of conducting regular verification of the entire personal data protection system in terms of both adequacy and effectiveness of the implemented organizational and technical solutions. The assessment of the probability of a given event occurring should not be based solely on the frequency of events in a given organization, as the fact that a particular event has not occurred in the past does not mean that it cannot occur in the future. The necessity for the Administrator to fulfill the discussed obligation was also pointed out by the Provincial Administrative Court in Warsaw (hereinafter referred to as "WSA"), which in its judgment of October 21, 2021, case file II SA/Wa 272/21, indicated that “(...) the President of the UODO was also correct in pointing out in the justification of the challenged decision that the lack of regulations in the procedures adopted by the Company ensuring regular testing, measuring, and evaluating the effectiveness of the applied technical and organizational measures aimed at ensuring data processing security contributed to the occurrence of a personal data breach. The authority also rightly points out that it was demonstrated in the proceedings that the Company did not conduct tests aimed at verifying the security of the application (...) and the WebAPI of the system (...), concerning the vulnerabilities of the IT system related to the occurrence of the personal data breach (...) conducting tests only in the event of an emerging threat, without introducing a procedure that would define a schedule of actions ensuring regular testing, measuring, and evaluating the effectiveness of the implemented measures is insufficient. The Company, as evidenced by the collected material, despite the adopted solutions, was unable to detect vulnerabilities due to the lack of regular tests of the system implemented by the Company (...) Therefore, it is impossible in this context to challenge the assessment of the President of the UODO that regular testing, measuring, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures aimed at ensuring the security of processing is a fundamental obligation of every administrator and processor arising from Article 32(1)(d) of the GDPR (...)”. Similarly, in the judgment of June 6, 2023, case file II SA/Wa 1939/22, the WSA indicated that “(...) the obligation of regular testing of technical and organizational measures, securing the processing of personal data, to ensure a level of security corresponding to the risk, in the understanding of Article 32(1) introductory sentence of the GDPR, arises directly from the wording of point (d) of the indicated Article 32(1) (...)”. The WSA expressed a similar opinion in its judgment of June 21, 2023, case file II SA/Wa 150/23.

3. The necessity for ongoing verification of the effectiveness of the security measures provided is particularly important in the case of processing personal data outside the area of the Administrator's organization. In this context, it should be emphasized that the proper implementation by the Administrator of the obligation arising from Article 32(1)(d) of Regulation 2016/679 would allow for the detection that the requirement specified in point 13 of the "Instruction for Managing IT Systems Used for Processing Personal Data in J. (...) SA" regarding the encryption of media used for processing personal data transferred outside the processing area is not being practically fulfilled.

4. In summary, the irregularities indicated above consequently constitute a violation by the Administrator of the obligations arising from Article 24(1) and Article 32(1) and (2) of Regulation 2026/679.

**Assessment of Circumstances Referred to in Article 83(2) of Regulation 2016/679**

28. In deciding to impose an administrative fine, the President of the Office – in accordance with Article 83(2)(a)–(k) of Regulation 2016/679 – took into account the circumstances of the case presented below (see points 29–32 of the justification of the decision), which necessitate the application of this type of sanction in the present case and affect the severity of the imposed administrative fine.

29. The nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the processing, the number of affected individuals whose data is concerned, and the extent of the damage suffered by them [Article 83(2)(a) of Regulation 2016/679]. The infringement of the provisions of Articles 32(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679 by the Company in connection with the processing of personal data within the framework of its activities involving the editing, preparing, creating, or publishing of press materials, without implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures based on a conducted risk analysis, has significant gravity, due to the nature of the activities it performs, and thus the potential scope of data processing. It should be noted that the Company's activities are not limited solely to conducting (…) of a regional nature. The website operated by the Company, www.(…).pl, has a reach greater than local. Such a nature of processing is associated with significantly greater risks for the individuals whose data is concerned and means that the infringement of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 is serious and significant. The press information generated by the Company can reach an unlimited number of recipients, which consequently creates a higher risk associated with the processing of personal data in the event of a security breach of that data. The lack of a procedure for verifying press materials before their publication, in terms of the personal data contained therein, entails the risk of unauthorized disclosure and use of that data against the will of the individuals whose data is concerned. Furthermore, due to the nature of the activities and the scope of data processing, in the event of an infringement, its removal and mitigation of any negative effects may be impossible to achieve. It thus has a continuous and prolonged nature, thereby posing more serious threats in the area of privacy violations for individuals whose data and information concerning them may have been published by the Company during this time, and whose identification may occur under various circumstances even in the future.

30. The unintentional nature of the infringement [Article 83(2)(b) of Regulation 2016/679]. According to the Guidelines 04/2022 on calculating administrative fines under the GDPR (hereinafter referred to as Guidelines 04/2022), “[i]n principle, ‘intent’ encompasses both knowledge and deliberate action, in relation to the characteristics of the prohibited act, while ‘negligence’ means the absence of intent to cause an infringement, despite the failure of the administrator/processor to fulfill the due diligence required by law.” The evidence indicates that the Company was aware of its obligations to implement appropriate security measures for data processing in relation to the risk of infringement of rights or freedoms, as directly evidenced by the content presented during the document control (see point

Such behavior of the Company indicates a lack of sufficient knowledge about the risks arising from the processing of personal data and the consequences of these risks for the individuals concerned, primarily due to the lack of a risk assessment. Potential risks include, among others, data loss, unauthorized access to data, and their modification. According to the supervisory authority, the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 results from the Company's negligence and should be assessed as an unintentional violation.

31. Degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority in order to rectify the violation and mitigate its potential negative effects [Article 83(2)(f) of Regulation 2016/679]. In relation to the identified violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 indicated in the notification of the initiation of administrative proceedings, the Company did not take any actions aimed at improving the state of protection of the data processed by it. As indicated by the European Data Protection Board (hereinafter referred to as "EDPB") in Guidelines 04/2022 (p. 31), "the ordinary obligation of cooperation is mandatory and should therefore be regarded as a neutral (not mitigating) circumstance. However, if cooperation with the supervisory authority resulted in limiting or avoiding negative consequences for the rights of individuals, which consequences could otherwise occur, the supervisory authority may consider this as a mitigating circumstance within the meaning of Article 83(2)(f) of the GDPR, resulting in a reduction of the monetary penalty. This may be the case, for example, when the controller or processor responded in a specific manner to the requests of the supervisory authority during the investigation phase in this particular case, which consequently significantly limited the impact on the rights of the data subjects." The lack of response from the Company to the requests of the authority, in this case regarding the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 identified in the notification of the initiation of administrative proceedings (see point 3 of the reasoning of the decision), cannot therefore be regarded as neutral, let alone as mitigating in the context of the imposition of an administrative monetary penalty.

32. Categories of personal data affected by the violation [Article 83(2)(g) of Regulation 2016/679]. Given that the subject of the Company's activity is press activity involving the editing, preparation, creation, or publication of press materials, which may include any information about an identified or identifiable natural person, it should be assumed that the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 indicated in this decision concerns all data processed by the Administrator, including special categories of data specified in Article 9(1) and Article 10 of Regulation 2016/679. The nature of the activities performed by the Administrator indicates that it may lead to the publication of any information held by the Company that may allow for the identification of a specific person, including data that is subject to special protection under Regulation 2016/679. In this context, it is worth recalling the EDPB Guidelines 04/2022, which state: "Regarding the requirement to consider the categories of personal data affected by the violation (Article 83(2)(g) of the GDPR), the GDPR explicitly indicates the types of data that are subject to special protection, and thus a more stringent response when imposing monetary penalties. This applies at least to the types of data covered by Articles 9 and 10 of the GDPR and data not covered by the scope of these articles, the dissemination of which immediately causes harm or discomfort to the data subject (e.g., location data, private communication data, national identification numbers, or financial data such as transaction statements or credit card numbers). Generally speaking, the more categories of such data are affected by the violation or the more sensitive the data is, the greater weight the supervisory authority may attribute to this factor. The quantity of data concerning each individual also matters, as the larger the amount of data concerning each individual, the greater the scale of the violation of the right to privacy and data protection."
