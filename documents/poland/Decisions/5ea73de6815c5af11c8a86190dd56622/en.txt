**Legal Assessment**  
Referring the above provisions to the established factual state in this case, it should first be emphasized that the burden of proof for the execution of the decision regarding the processing of personal data based on the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 rests on the data controller. This norm directly arises from Article 5(2) of Regulation 2016/679, which establishes the principle of accountability, according to which the controller – being responsible for compliance with the principles of personal data processing formulated in Article 5(1) of Regulation 2016/679 (including, among others, the principle of legality, according to which data processing must be carried out "lawfully") – is obliged to demonstrate compliance with these principles. The application of the principle of accountability in the proceedings conducted by the supervisory authority aimed at determining the execution of the decision means that the controller is obliged to prove before the President of UODO the fulfillment of the obligation indicated in the decision, which will be equivalent to restoring the processes of personal data processing to a state compliant with the law. Such an interpretation of the principle of accountability is confirmed in the doctrine of data protection law, according to which: "the statement that the controller should be able to demonstrate compliance with the principles can be interpreted as imposing on the controller the burden of proof regarding compliance with the data processing principles. In the event of a dispute with the data subject or with the supervisory authority, the controller should be able to present evidence that they comply with the principles. Such evidence may primarily consist of documents related to the processing and protection of data" (see: P. Fajgielski [in:] Commentary on Regulation No. 2016/679 on the protection of natural persons in relation to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation) [in:] General Data Protection Regulation. Personal Data Protection Act. Commentary, Warsaw 2018).  
Thus, in this case, it is the Company – as the data controller and at the same time the addressee of the decision of the President of UODO dated December 15, 2022, ref. ZSPR.440.1621.2018 and the decision of the President of UODO...
November 29, 2022, ref. ZSPR.440.1530.2019 – was obliged to provide the President of the UODO with all evidence and information necessary for the supervisory authority to determine that it had complied with the requests of Mr. T. B. and Mr. M. N. regarding the removal of their personal data from the Company’s databases, which would lead to the conclusion that the processing of personal data by the Company is currently in accordance with the provisions of Regulation 2016/679. Meanwhile, the Company did not take any actions to prove the execution of the orders resulting from the aforementioned administrative decisions – despite written calls directed by the supervisory authority preceding the initiation of these proceedings concerning the imposition of an administrative monetary penalty. It should be noted that the Company did not raise any doubts before the President of the UODO regarding the manner of fulfilling the obligations imposed on it, nor did it signal any potential difficulties (technical or organizational) related to their implementation. The circumstances cited lead to the conclusion that the Company did not execute (or, in accordance with the terminology used by the EU legislator in Article 83(6) of Regulation 2016/679 – "did not comply with") the orders of both aforementioned administrative decisions of the President of the UODO. In light of the above findings, the President of the UODO determined that in this case, there were grounds justifying the imposition of an administrative monetary penalty on the Company, in accordance with the wording of Article 83(6) of Regulation 2016/679.

**Grounds and Principles for Imposing Administrative Monetary Penalties**  
Pursuant to Article 83(2) of Regulation 2016/679, administrative monetary penalties are imposed depending on the circumstances of each individual case. Attention is drawn to a number of circumstances listed in points a) to k) of the aforementioned provision. In deciding to impose an administrative monetary penalty on the Company in this case and determining its amount, the President of the UODO took into account the following circumstances, indicated below, which weigh negatively on the assessment of the established violation.

The nature, gravity, and duration of the violation, the number of affected individuals (Article 83(2)(a) of Regulation 2016/679). The violation committed by the Company in this case, consisting of non-compliance with the orders of the administrative decisions of the President of the UODO, which is the competent authority for personal data protection and the supervisory authority within the meaning of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679, should be considered as significantly undermining the personal data protection system. On the one hand, the Company’s omissions indicate its overall dismissive attitude towards the obligations established by the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 concerning data controllers. On the other hand, the Company’s failure to delete the personal data of two identified natural persons, named in the issued orders – resulting in further unlawful processing of their personal data – violates the rights of these individuals. Despite the objections expressed by the individuals concerned regarding the processing of their personal data by the Company, as well as the clear directive from the supervisory authority regarding the obligation to cease such processing, the Company continues to retain the disputed data in its resources. This allows the Company to further use this data for purposes related to its business activities (e.g., for sending unsolicited spam via SMS or email to individuals or making unwanted marketing phone calls). The continued processing of personal data by the Company also poses a risk of sharing this data with other entities with which the Company cooperates. This entails a risk for the data subjects directly affected by the violation established in these proceedings, namely the loss of control over their personal data. At the same time, in the event of further use of this data by the Company, the individuals concerned will be forced to endure unwanted attempts at contact from the Company, which undoubtedly involves the inconvenience of such actions. For this reason, in the assessment of the President of the UODO, the violation committed by the Company should be attributed significant weight and reprehensible character. This weight is further increased by the fact that the state of violation has persisted continuously since January 4, 2023 (in the context of the order from the decision dated December 15, 2022, ref. ZSPR.440.1621.2018) and since December 16, 2022 (in the context of the order from the decision dated November 29, 2022, ref. ZSPR.440.1530.2019), i.e., from the date of recognition of the aforementioned final decisions of the President of the UODO as delivered to the Company.

**Unintentional Nature of the Violation (Article 83(2)(b) of Regulation 2016/679)**. Given that none of the letters sent by the President of the UODO to the Company were actually received by it (which applies to both the administrative decisions of the President of the UODO, requests for explanations and evidence of their execution, as well as written reminders sent to the Company), there are no grounds to consider that the actions of the Company subject to sanction in this case were intentional. However, due to the finding that the violation committed by the Company is a consequence of gross and prolonged negligence by the Company of its fundamental obligation – which is to ensure such an organization of the receipt of correspondence that the course of official correspondence proceeds continuously and without interruption – this circumstance should be assessed negatively and regarded as aggravating in the context of determining both the justification for imposing and the amount of the imposed monetary penalty. At the same time, the supervisory authority indicates that the obligation to comply with the principles of personal data protection (including the aforementioned principle of accountability) rests on the Company by virtue of the law itself. Therefore, the circumstance of the lack of receipt by...
provided any evidence of applying approved codes of conduct or certification mechanisms as outlined in Article 83(2)(j) of Regulation 2016/679. Therefore, this circumstance does not contribute to mitigating the assessment of the established violation.

**Conclusion**

In light of the above considerations, the President of the UODO finds that the circumstances surrounding the case do not warrant a reduction in the amount of the administrative fine imposed on the Company. The Company’s lack of cooperation, disregard for the obligations imposed by the President of the UODO, and the nature of the violation all support the decision to impose the maximum administrative fine permissible under the law.

Benefits gained directly or indirectly in connection with the violation or avoided losses (Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679). The President of the UODO did not find that the Company, in connection with non-compliance with the orders issued by the supervisory authority, gained any financial benefits or avoided such losses. Therefore, there are no grounds for treating this circumstance as burdensome for the Company. The determination of the existence of measurable financial benefits resulting from the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 should be assessed very negatively. On the other hand, the lack of such benefits for the Company, as a natural state resulting from the assumption that an entrepreneur conducting business activities gains financial benefits (profits) on the condition of complying with applicable legal requirements, is a circumstance that, by its very nature, cannot be mitigating for it. This is confirmed by the very wording of Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679, which requires the supervisory authority to pay due attention to the benefits "gained" – those that occurred on the part of the entity committing the violation.

Other aggravating or mitigating factors (Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679). The President of the UODO, comprehensively considering the case, did not record any circumstances other than those described above that could influence the assessment of the violation and the amount of the imposed administrative monetary penalty. 

The remaining circumstances listed in Article 83(2) of Regulation 2016/679 could not be taken into account by the President of the UODO, as they do not apply in this case. These are:
- actions taken by the controller to minimize the damage suffered by the data subjects (Article 83(2)(c) of Regulation 2016/679) – due to the fact that no damage materialized on the part of individuals (only the risk of its occurrence), there is no obligation or possibility for the Company to take any actions aimed at minimizing them;
- the degree of responsibility of the controller, taking into account the technical and organizational measures implemented by them under Articles 25 and 32 of Regulation 2016/679 (Article 83(2)(d) of Regulation 2016/679) – due to the fact that the violation in its very essence is not related to the technical and organizational measures implemented by the Company to ensure the protection of personal data and the security of their processing.

According to the wording of Article 83(1) of Regulation 2016/679, the administrative monetary penalty imposed by the supervisory authority should be effective, proportionate, and deterrent in each individual case. In defining the aforementioned principles of imposing administrative monetary penalties, reference should be made to the views of the doctrine of law on data protection. "A sanction is effective if it allows achieving the goal for which it was introduced. A sanction is proportional if it does not exceed the threshold of inconvenience determined by taking into account the circumstances of the specific case. A sanction is deterrent if it fulfills the considerations of individual and general prevention, in other words, it constitutes a clear signal of disapproval of the violation for society, as well as for the addressee of the sanction" (P. Litwiński (ed.), Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council (EU) 2016/679 of April 27, 2016 […]; Commentary on Article 83 in: P. Litwiński (ed.) General Data Protection Regulation. Data Protection Act. Selected Sectoral Provisions. Commentary). 

The principles of imposing administrative monetary penalties as defined require reference to the size, financial capabilities, and market position of the penalized entity, and as a measure of these attributes of the penalized entity, in the case of a business, Regulation 2016/679 allows for the adoption of the total annual global turnover from the previous financial year (Article 83(5) of Regulation 2016/679). 

Supplementing this principle, Article 101a(2) of the Personal Data Protection Act states that in the absence of such data, the President of the UODO determines the basis for calculating the administrative monetary penalty based on estimates regarding the size of the entity, the specifics of the business it conducts, or generally available financial data concerning the entity. 

Referring to the economic potential of the penalized entity is necessary because, among other things, a penalty that is disproportionately low in relation to the financial capabilities of the perpetrator of the violation (a penalty that is practically "unnoticeable" for that entity) will not be effective and deterrent for that entity; on the other hand, a penalty that is too burdensome (a penalty whose payment threatens the existence of the entity) will not be a proportional penalty. 

In the opinion of the President of the UODO, the penalty imposed on the Company in this proceeding corresponds to the principles indicated in Article 83(1) of Regulation 2016/679. Its financial severity will discipline the Company to comply with data protection principles.
**Personal Data and Compliance with Obligations Arising from Administrative Decisions of the President of UODO**

In a disciplinary dimension, the penalty imposed on the Company will therefore be effective, as it will achieve its purpose. The penalty imposed by this decision is also, in the opinion of the President of UODO, proportional to the severity of the identified violation and to the Company's ability to bear it without significant detriment to its ongoing activities. This penalty will also serve a deterrent function; it will be a clear signal to the Company, which is obliged under the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 to comply with the orders issued by the supervisory authority pursuant to Article 58(2) of the aforementioned legal act, that neglecting this obligation constitutes a violation of significant weight and, as such, will be subject to financial sanctions.

Taking into account the provisions of Article 103 of the Personal Data Protection Act, the President of UODO imposed on the Company – using the average euro exchange rate from January 30, 2023 (where 1 EUR = 4.7160 PLN) – an administrative monetary penalty in the amount of 23,580 PLN (which is equivalent to 5,000 euros).
