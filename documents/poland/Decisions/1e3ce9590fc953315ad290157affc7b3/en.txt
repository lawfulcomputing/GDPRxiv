Warsaw, December 18, 2024

Decision

DKN.5112.14.2022  
Based on Article 104 § 1 of the Act of June 14, 1960, the Code of Administrative Procedure (Journal of Laws of 2024, item 572) and Article 7 paragraphs 1 and 2, Article 60, Article 90, Article 101, and Article 103 of the Act of May 10, 2018, on the Protection of Personal Data (Journal of Laws of 2019, item 1781) and Article 57 paragraph 1 letters a) and h), Article 58 paragraph 2 letter i), Article 83 paragraphs 1–3 and Article 83 paragraph 4 letter a) in connection with Article 30 paragraph 1, Article 35 paragraphs 1 and 7, and Article 38 paragraph 3 of the Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council (EU) 2016/679 of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation) (OJ EU L 119 of 05.04.2016, p. 1, as amended), hereinafter referred to as the "Regulation 2016/679", after conducting administrative proceedings initiated ex officio regarding the violation of personal data protection regulations by C. S.A. based in D. at ul. (…), the President of the Personal Data Protection Office1) finds that C. S.A. based in D. at ul. (…) violated Article 38 paragraph 3 of Regulation 2016/679 by failing to ensure that the data protection officer was directly subordinate to the highest management of the controller and did not receive instructions regarding the performance of their tasks, imposes on C. S.A. based in D. at ul. (…),

an administrative fine in the amount of 261,918.00 PLN (in words: two hundred sixty-one thousand nine hundred eighteen zlotys);  
2) finding that C. S.A. based in D. at ul. (…) violated the provisions:  
a) Article 30 paragraph 1 of Regulation 2016/679, consisting of failing to take profiling into account in the description of processing operations contained in the processing activities register maintained by C. S.A.;  
b) Article 35 paragraphs 1 and 7 of Regulation 2016/679, consisting of failing to conduct an assessment of the impact on the protection of personal data regarding processing operations involving profiling of data, imposes on C. S.A. based in D. at ul. (…), an administrative fine in the amount of 314,302.00 PLN (in words: three hundred fourteen thousand three hundred two zlotys).

The President of the Personal Data Protection Office, hereinafter referred to as the "President of UODO", based on Article 78 paragraph 1, Article 79 paragraph 1 point 1, and Article 84 paragraph 1 points 1-4 of the Act of May 10, 2018, on the Protection of Personal Data (Journal of Laws of 2019, item 1781), hereinafter referred to as the "Act", in connection with Article 57 paragraph 1 letters a) and h) and Article 58 paragraph 1 letters b) and e) of Regulation 2016/679, in order to control compliance of data processing with personal data protection regulations, conducted control activities at C. S.A. based in D. at ul. (…), hereinafter referred to as "C. S.A." (case reference: DKN (…) ).  
The President of UODO conducted control activities at C. S.A. from (…) to (…) . The scope of the control included the processing of personal data of clients and potential clients of C. S.A. in connection with their profiling, by determining, among other things:  
1) The scope and type of processed personal data.  
2) The purpose and duration of personal data processing.  
3) Whether C. S.A. has developed procedures regarding profiling.  
4) Whether C. II. In this factual state, after reviewing all the evidence gathered in the case, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office considered the following:

II.1. Violation of Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679. According to Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679, the administrator and the processor ensure that the data protection officer does not receive instructions regarding the performance of these tasks. He is not dismissed or penalized by the administrator or the processor for fulfilling his tasks. The data protection officer is directly accountable to the highest management of the administrator or the processor.

During the inspection activities, it was established that C. S.A., contrary to the provisions of Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679, did not implement organizational measures to ensure that the IOD was directly accountable to the highest management of the administrator and did not receive instructions regarding the performance of his tasks. The gathered evidence clearly indicates that in the internal documentation, it included organizational solutions that do not meet the requirements specified in the aforementioned provision of Regulation 2016/679 (employment contract dated September 14, 2017, along with an annex, organizational regulations before the amendment after the inspection by the President of UODO).
Thus, considering the fact that the data protection officer was employed on the day of the inspection in the Department (…) of C. S.A. as a specialist in (…) and was executing orders from his direct superior, i.e., the Director of the aforementioned Department, who is not a member of the "senior management of the administrator," it must be stated that C. S.A. violated Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679. The violation of the aforementioned provision also arises from the fact that C. S.A., as the administrator, did not ensure, in light of the factual state established above (described in points 5-9), that the data protection officer did not receive instructions regarding the performance of these tasks. It must be recognized that the full subordination of the data protection officer to the Director of the Department (…) excludes the possibility of ensuring that the officer does not receive instructions regarding the performance of his tasks, especially since the department in which he worked was substantively responsible for issues that also fall within the scope of the data protection officer's duties, namely primarily for information security at C. S.A.

Taking the above into account, it must be stated that, from a logical point of view, it is not possible for the data protection officer, working in the organizational unit of C. S.A. responsible for information security, and thus personal data, not to receive orders from the head of that unit, also in the substantive area covering issues for which that officer is responsible. For this reason, the explanations provided by C. S.A. regarding the alleged factual subordination of the data protection officer directly to a member of the management board of C. S.A., supported by individual emails exchanged between these individuals, are unconvincing.

It should be noted that by Resolution No. (…) / (…) of the Management Board of C. S.A., a deputy data protection officer was appointed, employed on the day of the inspection as the Director of the Department (…) of C. S.A. and simultaneously being the direct superior of SJ, the data protection officer of C. S.A., employed in the aforementioned department as a specialist in (…). The situation described above should also be assessed as a violation of the provisions of Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679, as it is difficult to accept that the data protection officer of C. S.A., in a situation where a person who is also his direct superior has been appointed as his deputy, does not receive instructions regarding the performance of tasks from the Director of the Department (…).

In light of the above, once again, the explanations provided by C. S.A. in this regard, contained in the letter dated August 4, 2023, addressed to the President of the UODO, in which C. S.A. referred, among other things, to the attached electronic correspondence indicating, in its opinion, the actual independence of the data protection officer, despite his formal employment in the organization of C. S.A. as subordinate to the Director of the Department (…), should be considered unreliable. Referring to these explanations, it must be stated that the aforementioned correspondence cannot be regarded as evidence of the data protection officer being directly subordinate to the senior management of C. S.A., since the content of all documents, starting from the employment contracts concluded with C. S.A. by SJ, who held the position of data protection officer on the day of the inspection, and KN, employed as the Director of the Department (…), and ending with the internal procedures of C. S.A. and its organizational chart, indicates the subordination of the data protection officer to a person who is not a member of the management board of C. S.A.

It must therefore be stated that the explanations provided by C. S.A. in this regard do not bring anything new to the case and do not constitute an argument against the accusation made by the President of the UODO regarding the data protection officer's lack of subordination to the senior management of C. S.A.

It should be emphasized that the appointment of SJ to perform the function of the data protection officer under the aforementioned factual circumstances, and primarily due to the fact that he was employed in the Department (…) and made subordinate to the director of that unit, contradicts the written post-inspection explanations of C. S.A. regarding the "actual" independence of the data protection officer and subordination solely to the management of C. S.A., as well as the content of § (…) of the Policy (…) in force at C. S.A. on the day of the inspection, according to which SJ, as the data protection officer, was to be subordinate in the organization of C. S.A. directly to the member of the management board responsible for the area of personal data protection. This means that the Policy (…), in light of the circumstances indicated above and the manner of employing the data protection officer in C. S.A., was not implemented in this regard, which means that C. S.A. did not meet the requirements of Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679.

C. S.A. argues in its explanations that SJ was subordinate to the Director of the Department (…) only "organizationally," but in performing the duties of the data protection officer, he was "actually" subordinate solely to a member of the management board of C. S.A. However, the explanations provided by C. S.A. cannot be considered reliable and substantive. The regulation adopted by C. S.A. in § (…) of the Policy (…) cannot merely be a declaration that is not supported by the actual state of affairs established during the inspection. Therefore, if the aforementioned regulation adopts the principle that the data protection officer is directly subordinate to a member of the management board responsible for the area of data protection, then consistent actions must be implemented to confirm this declaration. The circumstances of SJ's employment by C. S.A., i.e., his employment in the Department (…) as subordinate to the director of that unit and the appointment of the latter to perform the function of deputy SJ as the data protection officer, as well as the substantive scope of tasks performed by the Department (…), contradict the provision of § (…) of the Policy (…) in force at C. S.A., according to which the data protection officer is directly subordinate to a member of the management board.

In other words, it cannot happen, as in the present case, that the administrator, i.e., C. S.A., introduces certain organizational measures, such as the policy (…), in which it declares the independence of the data protection officer in performing his duties, including his subordination solely and directly to the senior management (i.e., in this case, the management board).
C. S.A.), and at the same time, it actually applies organizational and legal solutions, such as employment contracts along with the specification of duties therein, which indicate that they are in contradiction with the provisions of the policy, and consequently with the content of Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679. It should be recalled that in this case, in contradiction with the requirements of § (…) of the Policy (…) and Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679 is the content of the employment contract concluded with SJ on September 14, 2017, under which he was first employed in the position of (…), then, by virtue of annex no. (…) dated March 25, 2020, in the position of specialist in (…), and subsequently – as of the date of the inspection – in the Department of (…) C. S.A. The content of the job description attached to the aforementioned employment contract for the position of specialist in (…) in the Department of (…) C. S.A. indicates that the person acting as the DPO SJ, simultaneously employed as a specialist in (…) in the Team for (…)(later Department of (…)), "reports to" the Director of the Department (…), to whom he is subordinate. The above designation indicates that the aforementioned person was obliged to provide the Director of the Department (…) with information about the tasks performed by him, including those related to the performance of his function as a data protection officer. From the entirety of the duties performed by SJ and the Director of the Department (…) as of the date of the inspection and the tasks of the aforementioned department, it follows unequivocally that his director was also responsible for the sphere of data processing processes occurring in C. S.A. This is related to the fact that the subordinate to the Director of the Department (…) SJ, performing the function of DPO, necessarily had to be subordinate to him also in the scope of performing that function. Such a situation completely excludes the fulfillment of the requirement of the DPO being subordinate to the highest management of the administrator, in accordance with Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679. 

It should also be noted that C. S.A., appointing SJ to perform the function of DPO and simultaneously employing him in the position of specialist in (…) in the Department (…) as a subordinate of the director of this unit and appointing the aforementioned director to perform the function of SJ's deputy as DPO, did not comply with the provisions of the policy (…), which it was obliged to follow under Article 24(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679, according to which, taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons of varying likelihood and severity of the threat, the administrator implements appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that processing is performed in accordance with this regulation and to be able to demonstrate it. These measures are reviewed and updated as necessary (paragraph 1). If it is proportionate in relation to the processing activities, the measures referred to in paragraph 1 include the implementation by the administrator of appropriate data protection policies (paragraph 2). 

Considering all the circumstances of the case mentioned above, and above all the formal organization of SJ's work (including when performing DPO duties), the substantive scope of his duties as DPO and specialist in (…) in the Department (…) and the scope of tasks of the Department (…) C. S.A., and also referring finally to life experience in similar situations, it must be stated that the explanations of C. S.A., in which it argues that in fact SJ in the scope of performing DPO duties was directly and exclusively subordinate to a member of the management board, are unconvincing and do not find support in the established factual state. In this situation, the provisions of the employment contract concluded with C. S.A. by the person performing the function of DPO SJ as of the date of the inspection were in contradiction with the content of § (…) of the Policy (…) and Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679, causing that he was both formally and factually, directly subordinate to a person not belonging to the highest management of C. S.A. (the Director of the Department (…) ).

In connection with the above, C. S.A. cannot argue that the above-described state is in accordance with the requirements of Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679 and § (…) of the Policy (…), because, according to C. S.A., it is a "formal" solution, not a factual one. The President of the UODO cannot therefore accept C. S.A.'s explanation as credible, that the evidence of the DPO being subordinate to the highest management of C. S.A. as the administrator is the declaration of the latter that, although formally, inter alia, based on the employment contract, SJ was subordinate to the Director of the Department (…), in fact, C. S.A. "did not fully utilize" the formal provisions of this contract and de facto SJ as DPO was directly and exclusively subordinate to the management of C. S.A. The contradiction between the provisions of the employment contract and the provision of Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679 and § (…) of the Policy (…) of C. S.A. determines that the President of the UODO must conclude that C. S.A. did not ensure that the DPO was directly subordinate to the highest management of C. S.A. as the administrator. The subordination of C. S.A. to the Director of the Department (…), i.e., an internal unit of C. S.A., whose tasks also included activities related to the sphere of information security in C. S.A., and thus the protection of personal data, is also equivalent to the fact that SJ, contrary to the requirement of Article 38(3) of Regulation 2016/679, received instructions regarding the performance of DPO tasks from the Director of the Department (…). The aforementioned subordination, by the nature of the employment relationship, in which the employee is obliged to follow the orders of the superior, determines that SJ received instructions that are also significant for the manner in which he performed the function of DPO in C. S.A. To confirm the above reasoning, reference should be made to the content of the Guidelines of the Article 29 Working Party on Data Protection regarding data protection officers ("DPO"), amended and adopted on April 5, 2017. According to the content of the aforementioned document, "The administrator and the processor are responsible for complying with data protection regulations and must be able to demonstrate compliance."
In the event that the administrator or the processor makes a decision that is inconsistent with the provisions of the GDPR and the recommendations of the DPO (i.e., the Data Protection Officer – note from the President of the UODO), the DPO should have the opportunity to clearly present their separate opinion to the highest management and decision-makers. Article 38(3) states that <The Data Protection Officer reports directly to the highest management of the administrator or the processor.> This direct reporting ensures that the highest management (e.g., members of the board) is informed about the advice and recommendations of the DPO in the context of the DPO's task of informing and advising the administrator or the processor. Another example of direct reporting is the preparation of an annual report for the highest management regarding the activities conducted by the DPO.

II.2. Violation of Article 30(1) and Article 35(1) and (7) of Regulation 2016/679.

1. According to Article 30(1) of Regulation 2016/679, each administrator and, where applicable, the representative of the administrator shall maintain a record of processing activities for which they are responsible. This record shall include all of the following information: a) the name and contact details of the administrator and any co-administrators, as well as, where applicable, the representative of the administrator and the Data Protection Officer; b) the purposes of the processing; c) a description of the categories of data subjects and the categories of personal data; d) the categories of recipients to whom the personal data have been or will be disclosed, including recipients in third countries or international organizations; e) where applicable, the transfer of personal data to a third country or an international organization, including the name of that third country or international organization, and in the case of transfers referred to in Article 49(1) second paragraph, documentation of the appropriate safeguards; f) if possible, the planned time limits for erasure of the different categories of data; g) if possible, a general description of the technical and organizational security measures referred to in Article 32(1).

In light of the above, the violation by C. S.A. of Article 30(1) of Regulation 2016/679 arises from the fact established during the inspection and proceedings that C. S.A. does maintain the aforementioned record; however, it does not include in the description of the processes (activities) the processing of data related to their profiling. This fact was confirmed by C. S.A. in the explanations provided during the inspection, indicating that the record of processing activities records products, not activities or processes on personal data, while profiling is an element of providing a credit product, which is included in the record of processing activities. Similar explanations were provided by C. S.A. in the letter dated August 4, 2023, addressed to the President of the UODO following the inspection.

However, it should be noted that profiling, as defined in Article 4(4) of Regulation 2016/679, is a specific form of automated processing of data, as a result of which personal data – in the case of C. S.A. – are used to assess the economic situation of individuals, i.e., its clients. It should be noted that the record of processing activities is a document that should transparently, specifically, and exhaustively describe the data processing processes occurring at the administrator. The lack of the aforementioned characteristics in the record of processing activities renders it ineffective, and even useless, considering that its content is intended to serve, among other things, the supervisory authority, in the event of its inspection, to obtain information regarding the processing processes actually occurring at the controlled administrator without leaving any doubt.

Therefore, it should be indicated that C. S.A. should have included profiling in the record of processing activities it maintains as a specific form and, at the same time, an essential stage of processing customer data in the broadly understood process of granting them loans. The description of the processing activities related to profiling should be included in the record of processing activities due to the specificity of profiling in data processing, i.e., the use of automated technical devices (means) and the creation of new information about individuals, including those whose disclosure could lead to negative consequences for them (such as stigmatization, discrimination related to the potential availability of information about the financial status, i.e., the creditworthiness of C. S.A. clients to unauthorized persons). This, in turn, is of significant importance due to the potential risk of violating the rights or freedoms of data subjects.

For the reasons indicated above, the clear demonstration in the content of the record of processing activities of the activities involving the profiling of C. S.A. clients' data serves data security and is necessary to ensure the transparency and specificity of this document. The aforementioned view is also confirmed in the literature on the subject. As A. Mednis points out, "It should therefore be accepted that activities are distinguished by the purposes of processing. At the same time, according to the guidelines of the GIODO, in cases of significant differences in the other criteria listed in Article 30(1) of the GDPR, activities serving the same purpose but differing in terms of the categories of data subjects, categories of data, and methods of processing should be distinguished. This is particularly important in the case of profiling and making automated decisions. The exceptional nature of both actions and their specification in the GDPR means that they should be distinguished in the record."

In this situation, the lack of distinction by C. S.A. of profiling in the content of the record of processing activities constitutes a violation of Article 30(1).

According to Article 35(1) of Regulation 2016/679, if a specific type of processing—particularly using new technologies—due to its nature, scope, context, and purposes is likely to result in a high risk to the rights or freedoms of natural persons, the controller shall, prior to processing, carry out an assessment of the impact of the planned processing operations on the protection of personal data. For similar data processing operations involving a similar high risk, a single assessment may be conducted. Furthermore, according to Article 35(7) of Regulation 2016/679, the aforementioned assessment shall include at least: a) a systematic description of the planned processing operations and the purposes of the processing, including, where applicable, the legitimate interests pursued by the controller; b) an assessment of whether the processing operations are necessary and proportionate to the purposes; c) an assessment of the risks to the rights or freedoms of data subjects referred to in paragraph 1; and d) the measures planned to address the risks, including safeguards and security measures and mechanisms to ensure the protection of personal data and to demonstrate compliance with this Regulation, taking into account the rights and legitimate interests of data subjects and other persons concerned.

As established during the inspection, C. S.A. did not carry out an assessment of the impact on the protection of personal data concerning the data processing activities in the form of profiling. Considering the above, it should be stated that the lack of an assessment of the impact on the protection of data related to the profiling of customer data by C. S.A. constitutes a violation of Article 35(1) and (7) of Regulation 2016/679. Furthermore, the assessment of the planned processing operations of personal data by C. S.A., submitted during the inspection, does not meet the requirement contained in Article 35(7)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, i.e., it does not include in the systematic description of the planned processing operation in the form of profiling data for the purpose of determining the creditworthiness of C. S.A. customers.

It should be noted that profiling as a form of data processing indicated in Article 4(4) of Regulation 2016/679, particularly in the case of entities such as C. S.A. and the circumstances of data processing, should be clearly included in the content of the impact assessment due to the aforementioned specificity of profiling in data processing and the associated risks, i.e., the use of automated technical devices (means) and the creation of new information about natural persons, including information whose disclosure could lead to negative consequences for them (stigma, discrimination related to the potential availability of information about the financial status, i.e., the creditworthiness of C. S.A. customers).

Therefore, due to the threats associated with data processing through profiling and the risk of violation of the rights or freedoms of data subjects, a clear demonstration of profiling within the risk analysis and the content of the impact assessment for processing is essential to ensure the security of that data. Although, as evidenced by the evidence collected during the inspection, C. S.A. does not make automated decisions regarding the granting of credit, which, in light of the wording of Article 35(3)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, which expands the provisions of Article 35(1), would explicitly require it to prepare an impact assessment regarding profiling activities, it should be noted that the catalog of cases indicated in the aforementioned provision, in which the controller is obliged to carry out an assessment of the impact on the protection of data, is not closed, which means that this provision does not exclude that the performance of the subject assessment is also mandatory in other cases.

The aforementioned issue is also addressed on page 8 of the Guidelines of the Article 29 Working Party (WP251rev.01) regarding automated decision-making in individual cases and profiling for the purposes of Regulation 2016/679, adopted on October 3, 2017, last amended and adopted on February 6, 2018, by stating that “Decisions that are not solely automated may also involve profiling. For example, before granting a mortgage loan, a bank may take into account the credit scoring of the borrower, while before making any decision regarding a natural person, there is still human intervention.”

Moreover, on page 29 of the aforementioned Guidelines, the Article 29 Working Party states that “Article 35(3)(a) refers to assessments that include profiling and decisions ‘based’ on automated processing, and not ‘exclusively’ on automated processing. In the opinion of the Article 29 Working Party, Article 35(3)(a) will apply in cases of decision-making, including profiling, which have legal effects or similarly significantly affect the data subject, which is not fully based on automated processing, as well as decision-making based on automated processing as specified in Article 22(1). (…) The controller can still foresee a ‘model’ of decision-making based on profiling significantly increasing the level of human intervention, so that the model is no longer a fully automated decision-making process, although the processing may still pose risks to the fundamental rights and freedoms of natural persons. In such a case, the controller must ensure that it is able to address these risks and meet the requirements described in Chapter III of these Guidelines.”

The first step in the methodology for calculating administrative fines adopted there is "to determine the processing operations in the case and assess the application of Article 83(3) of the GDPR" (see paragraph 17 of the Guidelines 04/2022).
