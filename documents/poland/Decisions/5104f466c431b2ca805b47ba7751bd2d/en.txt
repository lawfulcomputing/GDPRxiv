According to Article 4(1) of RODO, "personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person. 

27. Therefore, the argument presented by the Commander regarding the absence of personal data in the materials shared during the press conference is not sufficient to exempt the processing of any information from the application of RODO. The definition of personal data is broad and encompasses various types of information that could potentially identify individuals, even if such identification is not immediately apparent.

42. The provisions regulating the issue of disclosing information to the press are contained in the Press Law. It should be pointed out at this point that in this case, the possibility of applying Article 5 of the Press Law to the actions of the Commander should be excluded, according to which (1) everyone, in accordance with the principle of freedom of speech and the right to criticism, may provide information to the press; (2) no one may be exposed to detriment or accusation due to providing information to the press if they acted within the limits permitted by law. As previously indicated, the Commander, as a public authority, is not a beneficiary of the freedom of expression and dissemination of information specified in Article 54(1) of the Constitution of the Republic of Poland. Furthermore, the right to criticism mentioned in Article 5(1) of the Press Law should pertain to citizens, not to public authorities. This follows from Article 1 of the Press Law, according to which the press realizes the right of citizens, among others, to social control and criticism. The manner in which state authorities respond to press criticism is regulated in Article 6(2) of the Press Law, which states that state authorities, state enterprises, and other state organizational units, as well as cooperative organizations, are obliged to respond to the press criticism conveyed to them without undue delay, no later than within a month. As indicated by legal doctrine regarding Article 6(2) of the Press Law, it is stated: “By 'conveyance' one should understand not just the publication of publicly available press material, but any form of delivery of the criticized entity the content contained in the press material along with the communication of the expectation of obtaining a response (see J. Sobczak, Press Law, p. 303; E. Ferenc-Szydełko, Press Law, p. 71; M. Brzozowska-Pasieka, Press Law, p. 122; A. Chajewska, in: K. Orlik (ed.), Press Law. Court Proceedings in Press Matters, Warsaw 2017, p. 156). (...) The response should be documented in writing and communicated by any means of communication.” (B. Kosmus, G. Kuczyński (eds.), Press Law. Commentary. 3rd ed., Warsaw 2018).

43. Consequently, this provision does not cover the case of providing a response at a press conference, on the initiative of the authority, to critical material published in the press. This means that it is not a provision that would result in the occurrence of the premise specified in Article 9(2)(g) of the GDPR in this case.

The above provision states that processing is lawful only if – and to the extent that – at least one of the following conditions is met: (a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes; (b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract; (c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject; (d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or another natural person; (e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller; (f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular where the data subject is a child. (…) – stating a violation of Article 13 and Article 31(1)(5) of the Personal Data Protection Act of December 14, 2018, the President of UODO ordered the Commander to restore compliance with the law by removing the personal data of an individual.

The number of previously mentioned violations indicates that the aforementioned data controller has problems with fulfilling the obligations arising from data protection regulations. This includes, among other things, ensuring that the provision of personal data, to which the GDPR regulations apply, occurs when there is a legal basis for carrying out such an operation on personal data. Violations in this regard were found in two of the aforementioned previous decisions of the President of UODO. This issue is also related to the administrative fine imposed by this decision. In light of the above, it should be acknowledged that there are grounds for treating the premise in Article 83(2)(e) of the GDPR as aggravating.

58. Categories of personal data to which the violation pertained [Article 83(2)(g) GDPR]. The personal data of the Complainant disclosed in violation of the GDPR (without legal basis) during the press conference on (...) includes information about the breakdown of the Complainant's relationship, the locality of their residence, the type of residential building, the legal title to the premises, the fact of their treatment (...), that they suffer from (...), the place of the procedure (...), and information about their emotional state – in the scope of information about (...) and statements “(...)” and “(...)”. Among them, the information about the fact of treatment (...) of the Complainant, that they suffer from (...), the place of the procedure (...) and information about their emotional state – in the scope of information about (...) and statements “(...)” and “(...)”, constitutes data concerning health, which is subject to special protection under Article 9(1) of the GDPR. This imposes on the controllers of this data the obligation to treat this information with particular care, also due to the possible negative consequences for the individuals to whom this data pertains, in the event of its disclosure to unauthorized persons, including discrimination or loss of good reputation.

In this context, it is necessary to refer to the EDPB Guidelines 04/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines under the GDPR, which state: “As for the requirement to take into account the categories of personal data to which the violation pertains (Article 83(2)(g) GDPR), the GDPR explicitly indicates the types of data that are subject to special protection, and thus a more stringent response when imposing fines. This applies at least to the types of data covered by Articles 9 and 10 of the GDPR, as well as data not covered by the scope of these articles, the dissemination of which immediately causes harm or discomfort to the individual to whom the data pertains (e.g., data concerning location, data concerning private communication, national identification numbers, or financial data, such as...
transaction summaries or credit card numbers). Generally speaking, the greater the number of such categories of data involved in the breach, or the more sensitive the data, the more weight the supervisory authority may assign to this factor. The amount of data concerning each individual whose data is affected is also significant, as the scale of the breach of the right to privacy and personal data protection increases with the amount of data concerning each individual whose data is affected.

59. In determining the amount of the administrative monetary penalty, the President of the UODO found no grounds to consider mitigating circumstances that would affect the final penalty. All the criteria listed in Article 83(2)(a-j) of the GDPR, in the authority's assessment, constitute either aggravating factors or are merely neutral. Additionally, applying the criterion mentioned in Article 83(2)(k) of the GDPR (which requires consideration of any other aggravating or mitigating factors applicable to the circumstances of the case), no mitigating circumstances were found, only neutral ones (as noted below in point 67).

60. Other circumstances indicated below, referred to in Article 83(2) of the GDPR, after assessing their impact on the identified breach in this case, were deemed neutral by the President of the UODO, meaning they had neither an aggravating nor a mitigating effect on the amount of the imposed administrative monetary penalty.

61. The degree of responsibility of the controller, taking into account the technical and organizational measures implemented by them under Articles 25 and 32 [Article 83(2)(d) of the GDPR]. The breach assessed in this proceeding (the breach of the provisions of Article 6(1) and Article 9(1) of the GDPR) is not related to the technical and organizational measures applied by the Commander.

62. The degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority to remedy the breach and mitigate its potential negative effects [Article 83(2)(f) of the GDPR]. During the proceedings in this case, the Commander responded to the supervisory authority's requests. However, the fact of responding to the authority's requests cannot be treated by the President of the UODO as a mitigating factor, but rather as an expression of the fulfillment of the legal obligations resting on the personal data controller. As emphasized in the EROD Guidelines 04/2022 on calculating administrative monetary penalties under the GDPR, quote: "It should therefore be recognized that the ordinary obligation to cooperate is mandatory and, as such, should be regarded as a neutral circumstance (and not a mitigating one)."

63. The manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the breach [Article 83(2)(h) of the GDPR]. The President of the UODO was informed of the occurrence of the personal data breach, i.e., the disclosure of the Complainant's personal data during a press conference, by the Complainant's representative, and not by the Commander. However, the President of the UODO accepts that this criterion will not be treated as an aggravating circumstance. The President of the UODO learned of the breach from the complaint filed in this case, and the EROD in the Guidelines 04/2022 on calculating administrative monetary penalties under the GDPR indicates that "In cases where the supervisory authority became aware of the breach, for example, as a result of a complaint or during proceedings, this element should generally also be considered a neutral circumstance."

64. Compliance with previously applied measures in the same case referred to in Article 58(2) of the GDPR [Article 83(2)(i) of the GDPR]. Prior to issuing this decision, the President of the UODO did not apply any of the measures listed in Article 58(2) of the GDPR against the Commander in the case under consideration, which is why the Commander had no obligation to take any actions related to their application, actions that, if assessed by the President of the UODO, could have had an aggravating or mitigating effect on the assessment of the identified breach.

65. The application of approved codes of conduct under Article 40 of the GDPR or approved certification mechanisms under Article 42 of the GDPR [Article 83(2)(j) of the GDPR]. The Commander does not apply the instruments referred to in Articles 40 and 42 of the GDPR. However, their adoption, implementation, and application are not mandatory for controllers and processors, as stipulated by the GDPR, which means that the circumstance of their non-application cannot be considered detrimental to the Commander in this case. Conversely, the circumstance of adopting and applying such instruments as means guaranteeing a higher than standard level of protection for processed personal data could be taken into account in favor of the Commander.

66. Financial benefits directly or indirectly achieved in connection with the breach or losses avoided [Article 83(2)(k) of the GDPR]. The President of the UODO found no evidence that the Commander derived any financial benefits or avoided such losses in connection with the breach. Therefore, there are no grounds to treat this circumstance as aggravating for the Commander. The establishment of measurable financial benefits resulting from the breach of GDPR provisions would need to be assessed very negatively. However, the absence of such benefits for the Commander, as a natural state independent of the breach and its consequences, is a circumstance that, by its very nature, cannot be mitigating for the Commander. This is confirmed by the very wording of Article 83(2)(k) of the GDPR, which requires the supervisory authority to pay due attention to the benefits "achieved" – those that occurred on the part of the entity committing the breach.

67. Other aggravating or mitigating factors applicable to the circumstances of the case [Article 83(2)(k) of the GDPR]. The President of the UODO, comprehensively considering the case, did not...
noted other than the circumstances described above that may affect the assessment of the violation and the amount of the imposed administrative monetary penalty.

68. Taking into account all the circumstances discussed above, the President of UODO concluded that imposing an administrative monetary penalty on the Commander is necessary and justified by the weight, nature, and scope of the violations of the provisions of the GDPR attributed to the Commander. It should be stated that applying any other corrective measure provided for in Article 58(2) of the GDPR, particularly merely issuing a reprimand (Article 58(2)(b) of the GDPR), would not be proportional to the identified irregularities in the processing of personal data and would not guarantee that the Commander would not commit further negligence in the future.

69. Regarding the amount of the administrative monetary penalty imposed on the Commander, it should be noted that given the fact that the Commander, as a central government administration body, is an entity in the public finance sector, as referred to in Article 9(1) of the Act of August 27, 2009 on Public Finances (Journal of Laws of 2024, item 1530, as amended), Article 102(1)(1) of the Personal Data Protection Act of May 10, 2018, applies, which limits the amount (to 100,000 PLN) of the administrative monetary penalty that can be imposed on an entity in the public finance sector.

70. Justifying the imposition of an administrative monetary penalty on the Commander in the amount of 75,000 PLN, it should be indicated that, in the opinion of the President of UODO, a lower penalty would not fulfill its deterrent function, which is explicitly mentioned in Article 83(1) of the GDPR (and as indicated below, the penalty imposed in this specific case also has a preventive character). In the opinion of the President of UODO, the administrative monetary penalty applied fulfills, in the established circumstances of this case, the functions referred to in Article 83(1) of the GDPR, i.e., it will be effective, proportional, and deterrent in this individual case.

71. According to the President of UODO, the administrative monetary penalty imposed on the Commander will be effective, as it will lead to a situation in which the Commander will properly protect personal data from being disclosed without a legal basis. The effectiveness of this penalty is thus equivalent to the guarantee that the Commander, from the moment this proceeding is concluded, will diligently approach the requirements set by the provisions on the protection of personal data.

72. The administrative monetary penalty applied is also proportional to the identified violation of the GDPR provisions, especially regarding its weight, the negative impact on the person affected by the violation of personal data protection, and the high risk of negative consequences that they may incur. In the opinion of the President of UODO, the administrative monetary penalty imposed on the Commander does not constitute an excessive burden for him, given the statutory limitation on its amount in the case of entities in the public finance sector. In particular, its payment will not affect the Commander's ability to fulfill his statutory duties. According to the President of UODO, the Commander should and is able to bear the consequences of his negligence in the area of data protection, hence the imposition of an administrative monetary penalty in the amount of 75,000 PLN (in words: seventy-five thousand zlotys) is fully justified.

73. In the opinion of the President of UODO, the administrative monetary penalty will fulfill an educational function in these specific circumstances, but also a preventive one. It will indicate both to the Commander and to other data administrators the responsibility of state authorities for unlawful actions taken by them using their power and the possibilities that this power provides.

74. Therefore, the administrative monetary penalty applied fulfills, in the established circumstances of this case, the criteria referred to in Article 83(1) of the GDPR, due to the weight of the identified violations in the context of the compliance of personal data processing with the law.

75. At this point, it should be noted that a holder of a public authority who has committed a violation of the law, including the provisions on the protection of personal data, may incur potential criminal liability, e.g., in connection with exceeding their authority (Article 231 of the Penal Code, Article 107(1) and (2) of the Personal Data Protection Act of May 10, 2018). Moreover, Article 2 of the Act of May 7, 1999 on the financial liability of officers of the Police, Border Guard, Marshal's Guard, Customs and Tax Service, State Protection Service, State Fire Service, Prison Service, Internal Security Agency, Intelligence Agency, Military Counterintelligence Service, Military Intelligence Service, and Central Anti-Corruption Bureau (Journal of Laws of 2018, item 2349) states that an officer who, due to non-performance or improper performance of official duties through their fault, causes damage to property referred to in Article 1(1), bears financial liability within the limits of the actual loss and only for the normal consequences of action or inaction, even if the damage resulting from it occurred after the termination of the official relationship. The property referred to in Article 1(1) of the aforementioned Act is the property of the State Treasury that is at the disposal of the authority or organizational units subordinate to, subordinated to, or supervised by ministers responsible for internal affairs, public finances, the Minister of National Defense, the Marshal of the Sejm, the Minister of Justice, the Head of the Internal Security Agency, the Head of the Intelligence Agency, the Head of the Military Counterintelligence Service, the Head of the Military Intelligence Service, and the Head of the Central Anti-Corruption Bureau. It should be emphasized that the decision in question is related to...
with administrative liability borne by the Commander (the authority) – the administrator of personal data – for violations of the provisions of the GDPR. The Commander, however, has the option to apply legal measures that allow him to seek legal and financial accountability – in particular, in civil proceedings for recourse, as well as by holding the person responsible for violating the provisions on the protection of personal data criminally liable for their infringement. The President of the UODO holds the position that to ensure effective protection of personal data, effective mechanisms for seeking accountability for their violation by administrators are necessary, including in relation to employees or officers of the administrator.

76. Referring to the request contained in the complaint cited as “removal of personal data processed unlawfully,” it should be noted that the subject of these proceedings is the disclosure of personal data during a press conference on (...) date. This disclosure of data is an irreversible process; therefore – despite the finding that the disclosure occurred without legal basis – it is not possible to restore the legal state prior to this disclosure, particularly by ordering the removal of that personal data. The President of the UODO, in the event of a finding of a violation of the provisions on the protection of personal data, may exercise the corrective powers granted under Article 58(2) of the GDPR. In this case, although the President of the UODO found a violation of the provisions on the protection of personal data, there is no possibility of restoring the legal state by ordering the Commander to remove the disclosed personal data. This is a consequence of the fact that the established violation of the provisions on the protection of personal data is of a past and irreversible nature.

77. Referring to the request contained in the complaint cited as “taking action to inform the administrators processing this personal data that the person to whom the data pertains requests that these administrators remove all links to this data, copies of this personal data, or their replicas,” it should be indicated that the obligation to take the aforementioned actions requires the person to whom the data pertains to exercise their right to data deletion. This arises from Article 17(2) of the GDPR, which states that if the administrator has made personal data public, and under paragraph 1 has an obligation to delete that personal data, then – taking into account the available technology and the cost of implementation – they take reasonable actions, including technical measures, to inform the administrators processing this personal data that the person to whom the data pertains requests that these administrators remove all links to this data, copies of this personal data, or their replicas. Article 17(1) of the GDPR, in turn, provides that the person to whom the data pertains has the right to request the administrator to delete their personal data without delay, and the administrator has the obligation to delete the personal data without undue delay if one of the circumstances indicated in letters a-f of this editorial unit of the GDPR exists.

78. According to the provisions of Article 58(2)(g) of the GDPR, the President of the UODO may order the removal of deficiencies by ordering the administrator to fulfill the obligation specified in Article 17(2) of the GDPR, i.e., to take actions to inform other administrators processing this personal data that the person to whom the data pertains requests that these administrators remove all links to this data, copies of this personal data, or their replicas. This provision states that each supervisory authority has the corrective power to order, under Articles 16, 17, and 18, the rectification or deletion of personal data or the restriction of their processing and to order, under Article 17(2) and Article 19, notification of these actions to recipients to whom the personal data has been disclosed. However, such an order can only be issued after a prior assessment of the behavior of the administrator of this data, who previously received the request from the person to whom the data pertains. The obligation specified in Article 17(2) of the GDPR pertains to the transmission of information about the requests of the person to whom the data pertains. It should be noted that the fulfillment of the obligation referred to in Article 17(2) of the GDPR does not require the person to whom the data pertains to submit a separate request to the administrator to inform other administrators processing this personal data that the person to whom the data pertains requests that these administrators remove all links to this data, copies of this personal data, or their replicas. Recital 66 of the GDPR preamble indicates that this obligation strengthens the right of the person to whom the data pertains under Article 17(1) of the GDPR. This recital explains that to strengthen the right to “be forgotten” on the Internet, the right to data deletion should be extended by obliging the administrator who made this personal data public to inform the administrators who process such personal data about the removal of all links to this data, copies of this personal data, or their replicas. In fulfilling this obligation, the administrator should take reasonable actions considering the available technologies and means available to them, including available technical means, to inform the administrators who process personal data about the request of the person to whom the data pertains. The fact that the fulfillment of the obligation referred to in Article 17(2) of the GDPR does not require the person to whom the data pertains to direct any request to the administrator other than the request for the deletion of personal data is also indicated by the judgment of the Court of Justice of the European Union of October 27, 2022, in case C-129/21, which stated that “Article 17(2) of Regulation 2016/679 should be interpreted as not precluding a national supervisory authority from ordering a provider to publicly...
available subscriber lists and directory services, to which a request was made to cease the publication of data concerning a specific individual, so that it takes "reasonable actions," within the meaning of this provision, to inform search engine providers of this request for data removal." Furthermore, such an interpretation of Article 17(2) of the GDPR is systematically consistent with the literal wording of Article 19, first sentence, of the GDPR, which does not require a separate request from the data subject for the controller to be obliged to inform recipients to whom personal data has been disclosed about the deletion of data carried out as a result of the request under Article 17(1) of the GDPR. This provision states that the controller informs about the rectification or deletion of personal data or the restriction of processing that has been carried out in accordance with Article 16, Article 17(1), and Article 18, each recipient to whom personal data has been disclosed, unless this proves impossible or would require a disproportionate effort.

79. The role of the authority in this specific case is therefore to examine whether the personal data controller, to whom the data subject made a specific request for the deletion of personal data, properly fulfilled the obligation related to that request, as referred to in Article 17(2) of the GDPR. The controller cannot presume the existence of a request for the deletion of personal data. Such a request should exist, and thus be clearly and explicitly formulated and communicated to the data controller. The lack of a request for the deletion of personal data directed to the data controller excludes the possibility of the President of the UODO ordering the controller to fulfill the obligation specified in Article 17(2) of the GDPR. For this decision, the fundamental importance lies not in the request presented by the Complainant to the President of the UODO, but in the request of the Complainant to the Commander – the administrator of her personal data.

80. From the gathered evidence in the case, it follows that the Complainant did not approach the Commander with a request for the deletion of her personal data, i.e., with the request referred to in Article 17(1) of the GDPR. At the same time, it should be noted that specifying the request for the deletion of personal data in the complaint to the President of the UODO does not validate the lack of a request directed by the Complainant to the personal data controller. Regarding the request that this part of the justification of this decision concerns, the authority therefore has no basis to issue any of the orders specified in Article 58(2) of the GDPR, especially the one resulting from Article 58(2)(g) of the GDPR, an order under Article 17(2) of the GDPR to take actions to inform the controllers processing this personal data that the data subject requests that these controllers delete all links to this data, copies of this personal data, or their replicas. The authority is only empowered to issue such a decision when the data subject has submitted a request to the controller, exercising the right granted under Article 17(1) of the GDPR. In this case, it has been established that the Complainant did not submit a request for the deletion of her personal data to the Commander.

81. Regarding the allegation raised in the complaint of a violation of Article 32(1), Article 33(1), and Article 34(1) of the GDPR, it should be pointed out that issues of the Commander’s compliance with these provisions cannot be the basis for issuing a decision in the individual case of the Complainant.

82. First of all, Article 32(1) of the GDPR is a provision imposing on the data controller the obligation to implement appropriate safeguards in the process of processing personal data. It provides that, taking into account the state of technical knowledge, the cost of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons of varying likelihood and severity, the controller and the processor implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk. A complaint to the President of the UODO cannot serve to control the technical and organizational measures applied at the request of the data subject, but should serve to restore a lawful state in the event of a violation of that person's rights. The Provincial Administrative Court in Warsaw also stated in its judgment of January 7, 2016 (case file II SA/Wa 1238/15) that "The Court shares the authority's position that matters concerning the security of personal data (...) can only be the subject of proceedings initiated ex officio by the General Inspector."

83. The obligation specified in Article 34(1) of the GDPR to notify the data subject of a personal data breach that may result in a high risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons is related to the obligation arising from Article 33(1) of the GDPR for the controller to report the breach to the supervisory authority. The notification of the data subjects, in accordance with Article 34(1) of the GDPR, is carried out by the controller unless there are circumstances that exclude such an obligation. Therefore, the controller is obliged to notify the data subject of the breach, provided that the breach may result in a high risk of infringement of the rights and freedoms of natural persons and one of the circumstances specified in Article 34(3) of the GDPR, which exempts the controller from this notification obligation, has not occurred simultaneously. Only the supervisory authority is entitled to assess the controller's actions in this regard, which, after examining the circumstances related to the reported breach, may require the controller to notify the data subject or determine that one of the circumstances specified in Article 34(3) of the GDPR has been met. Also, only the supervisory authority is entitled to assess whether the controller was obliged to report the personal data breach to the supervisory authority. Thus, the supervisory authority verifies ex officio the actions taken by the controller.
In connection with this, the party to the proceedings regarding the notification of a personal data breach or the notification of such a breach may solely be the administrator, and the Complainant does not have the right to request that the administrator inform the President of the UODO or the Complainant about the personal data breach. It should be emphasized at this point that the catalog of rights that can be effectively enforced by the data subject in the proceedings conducted on their complaint by the President of the UODO is defined in Chapter III of the GDPR, in Articles 12-22.

84. At the same time, it should be noted that the allegation of the Commander providing information about (...) to the Complainant has not been confirmed. The evidence shows that in the recording replayed at the conference, the question “(...)” is asked. However, the content of the aforementioned question does not reveal information about whether the Complainant had (...). The answer to this question in the replayed recording has been drowned out, and the beginning of the next statement (from the duty officer of the Municipal Police Headquarters in Ź.) that may relate to this issue is unintelligible and has not been included in the transcription of the recording displayed on the screen behind the Commander (the printout with the screenshot of the transcription of this statement attached to the official note from July 3, 2024, constitutes page 70 verte of the case file). In the content of the complaint, the representative of the Complainant sees the provision of information about (...) to the Complainant as inferred from the operator's statement during the conversation recorded in the first of the recordings replayed at the Commander's press conference. However, in the opinion of the President of the UODO, due to the quality of the replayed recording, it is not possible to determine what the formulation “(...)” in the operator's statement refers to, in particular, whether it is information about previous (...) of the Complainant, or whether its meaning is, for example, to provide the reporting party with guidance to inform the Police about any (...) or whether it constitutes information about a connection with the organizational unit of the Police responsible for the matter (...). The middle part of this statement, which may contain the context of the formulation “(...)”, is indeed unintelligible, as evidenced by the content of the official note from July 3, 2024, prepared after viewing the recording of the Commander's conference (page 61 verte of the case file) and the screenshot attached to this note (page 67 of the case file) with the transcription of the statement displayed during the conference. Therefore, it cannot be concluded that information about (...) of the Complainant was provided at the Commander's press conference. Thus, it cannot be stated that this process of personal data processing occurred, and therefore, in this regard, the current proceedings are moot and, consequently, subject to dismissal based on Article 105 § 1 of the Administrative Procedure Code (Kpa). According to the aforementioned provision, when the proceedings have become moot in whole or in part for any reason, the public administration authority issues a decision to dismiss the proceedings accordingly in whole or in part. The wording of the cited regulation leaves no doubt that in the event of establishing the mootness of the proceedings, the authority conducting the proceedings is obliged to dismiss it. As indicated by the Provincial Administrative Court in Warsaw, quote: “mootness of administrative proceedings means the absence of an object of the proceedings. This object is a specific case in which the public administration authority is empowered and simultaneously obliged to resolve based on the provisions of substantive law regarding the rights or obligations of an individual entity” (judgment of October 5, 2017, case reference VI SA/Wa 1093/17). The establishment by the public administration authority of the existence of the premise referred to in Article 105 § 1 of the Kpa obliges it, as emphasized in doctrine and case law, to dismiss the proceedings, as there are no grounds for resolving the matter on the merits, and further conducting the proceedings in such a case would indicate its defectiveness, which would have a significant impact on the outcome of the case.

85. In this case, sharing the position expressed in the aforementioned court judgment, it should be stated that the premise of mootness of the proceedings and dismissal of the proceedings – based on Article 105 § 1 of the Kpa – exists in the scope covering the provision of information about (...) of the Complainant at the Commander's press conference. The conducted proceedings did not demonstrate that the data processing process contested by the Complainant occurred in this regard, thus there is no subject matter of the proceedings in this respect. In this factual and legal situation, the President of the UODO has resolved as stated in the operative part of this administrative decision.
