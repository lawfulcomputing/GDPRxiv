Decision

Based on Article 104 § 1 of the Act of June 14, 1960, the Code of Administrative Procedure (Journal of Laws of 2023, item 775, as amended) in connection with Article 7 § 1, Article 60, Article 101, Article 101a § 2, and Article 103 of the Act of May 10, 2018, on the Protection of Personal Data (Journal of Laws of 2019, item 1781), hereinafter referred to as "uodo", as well as Article 57 § 1 letters a) and h), Article 58 § 2 letter i), Article 83 § 1-3, Article 83 § 4 letter a) and Article 83 § 5 letter a) in connection with Article 5 § 1 letter f), Article 5 § 2, Article 24 § 1, Article 25 § 1, Article 32 § 1 and 2, as well as Article 33 § 1 and Article 34 § 1 and 2 of the Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council (EU) 2016/679 of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons in relation to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation) (OJ EU L 119 of 4.05.2016, p. 1, OJ EU L 127 of 23.05.2018, p. 2, and OJ EU L 74 of 4.03.2021, p. 35), hereinafter referred to as "Regulation 2016/679", after conducting administrative proceedings initiated ex officio regarding the violation of personal data protection regulations by P. Sp. z o.o. based in W. at ul. (…), the President of the Personal Data Protection Office, finds that P. Sp. z o.o. based in W. at ul. (…) violated the provisions of Article 5 § 1 letter f), Article 5 § 2, Article 24 § 1, Article 25 § 1, and Article 32 § 1 and 2, as well as Article 33 § 1 and Article 34 § 1 and 2 of Regulation 2016/679, consisting of: 
1) failure to implement: 
   a) appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of data processing in information systems and the protection of the rights of data subjects, 
   b) appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure regular testing, measuring, and assessing the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures intended to ensure the security of personal data processed in information systems, particularly regarding vulnerabilities, errors, and their possible consequences for these systems, as well as actions taken to minimize the risk of their occurrence, which violations result in a breach of the principle of integrity (Article 5 § 1 letter f of Regulation 2016/679) and accountability (Article 5 § 2 of Regulation 2016/679), 
2) failure to notify the President of the Personal Data Protection Office of the personal data breach without undue delay, and no later than 72 hours after becoming aware of the breach, and failure to inform without undue delay the data subjects about the personal data breach that poses a high risk to the rights or freedoms of natural persons, imposes on P. Sp. z o.o. based in W. at ul. (…), for the violation of the provisions of Article 5 § 1 letter f), Article 5 § 2, Article 25 § 1, Article 32 § 1 and 2, Article 33 § 1, and Article 34 § 1 and 2 of Regulation 2016/679, an administrative fine in the amount of 47,160 PLN (forty-seven thousand one hundred sixty zlotys).

On (…) July 2020, the Personal Data Protection Office received information from a third party indicating the loss of concession documentation maintained electronically by P. Sp. z o.o. based in W. (hereinafter also referred to as the "Company" or "Administrator"), which documentation should contain personal data of security employees (employed under an employment contract or individuals performing tasks under civil law contracts), as well as natural persons who are parties to civil law contracts for security services concluded by the Company. 
In connection with the above, on (…) July 2020, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office, hereinafter also referred to as the "President of UODO" or "supervisory authority", based on Article 58 § 1 letters a) and e) of Regulation 2016/679, requested the Company to provide information on whether an analysis of the incident had been conducted regarding the risk of violation of the rights or freedoms of natural persons necessary to assess whether there had been a breach of data protection requiring notification of the President of UODO and the individuals affected by the breach. In the letter, the supervisory authority pointed out to the Company the content of Article 33 § 1 and 3 of Regulation 2016/679 and the manner in which it could report the breach. From the explanations provided by the Administrator in response to this and subsequent requests from the supervisory authority (addressed to him before the initiation of administrative proceedings in this matter), it follows, among other things, that: 
1) As a result of a ransomware attack conducted for the purpose of obtaining financial gain, personal data located on three servers were encrypted, with this data concerning all employees of the Company and individuals providing services to the Company under civil law contracts. 
2) As a result of the aforementioned incident, the Administrator lost access to the data of the aforementioned individuals belonging to the following categories: surnames, first names, dates of birth, bank account numbers, addresses of residence or stay, PESEL identification numbers, email addresses, salary data, phone numbers, and identity card numbers.

In the process of analyzing (...) logs from network devices and system events, no signs of data exfiltration were observed.” No report was prepared from the audit of the IT system conducted by the Company's employees, which (as stated by the Company) showed that there was no unauthorized access to the data processed therein. In this factual state, after reviewing all the evidence gathered in the case, the President of the UODO considered the following: 

According to Article 34 paragraphs 1 and 2 of the Personal Data Protection Act, the President of the Office for Personal Data Protection is the competent authority in matters of personal data protection and the supervisory authority within the meaning of Regulation 2016/679. Pursuant to Article 57 paragraph 1 letters a) and h) of Regulation 2016/679, without prejudice to other tasks specified under this regulation, each supervisory authority in its territory monitors and enforces the application of the aforementioned regulation, as well as conducts proceedings regarding the application of this regulation, including based on information received from another supervisory authority or another public body. 

Article 5 of Regulation 2016/679 formulates the principles regarding the processing of personal data that must be respected by all administrators, i.e., entities that independently or jointly with others determine the purposes and means of processing personal data. Article 5 paragraph 2 of Regulation 2016/679 provides that the Administrator is responsible for compliance with the provisions of paragraph 1 and must be able to demonstrate compliance ("accountability"). According to Article 5 paragraph 1 letter f) of Regulation 2016/679, personal data must be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures ("integrity and confidentiality"). The specifics of this principle are further detailed in the provisions of the regulation. 

According to Article 24 paragraph 1 of Regulation 2016/679, taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons with varying probabilities and severity, the administrator implements appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that processing is performed in accordance with this regulation and to be able to demonstrate compliance. These measures are reviewed and updated as necessary. 

Pursuant to Article 25 paragraph 1 of Regulation 2016/679, taking into account the state of technical knowledge, the cost of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons with varying probabilities of occurrence and severity arising from processing, the administrator - both when determining the means of processing and during the processing itself - implements appropriate technical and organizational measures, such as pseudonymization, designed to effectively implement data protection principles, such as data minimization, and to provide the processing with necessary safeguards to meet the requirements of this regulation and protect the rights of data subjects. 

From the content of Article 32 paragraph 1 of Regulation 2016/679, it follows that the administrator is obliged to apply technical and organizational measures corresponding to the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons with varying probabilities of occurrence and severity. The provision specifies that when deciding on technical and organizational measures, one must consider the state of technical knowledge, the cost of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing, as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons with varying probabilities of occurrence and severity. From the cited provision, it follows that determining appropriate technical and organizational measures is a two-step process. 

In the first instance, it is essential to determine the level of risk associated with the processing of personal data, taking into account the criteria indicated in Article 32 of Regulation 2016/679, and then it is necessary to establish what technical and organizational measures will be appropriate to ensure a level of security corresponding to that risk. These determinations, where appropriate, in accordance with letters a), b), c), and d) of this article, should include measures such as pseudonymization and encryption of personal data, the ability to continuously ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience.
systems and processing services, the ability to quickly restore the availability of personal data and access to it in the event of a physical or technical incident, as well as regular testing, measuring, and assessing the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing. According to Article 32(2) of Regulation 2016/679, when assessing whether the level of security is appropriate, the controller shall take into account, in particular, the risks associated with processing, especially those arising from accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or unauthorized access to personal data that are transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.

As indicated in Article 24(1) of Regulation 2016/679, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing, as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons with varying likelihood and severity, are factors that the controller is obliged to consider in the process of building a data protection system, also from the perspective of other obligations specified in Article 25(1), Article 32(1), or Article 32(2) of Regulation 2016/679.

Taking into account the scope of personal data processed by the Company (including first names, last names, dates of birth, bank account numbers, residential or temporary addresses, PESEL identification numbers, email addresses, salary data, phone numbers, personal identification numbers), as well as the categories of individuals whose data are processed (employees of the Company and individuals providing services to the Company under civil law contracts), in order to properly fulfill the obligations imposed by the aforementioned provisions of the regulation, the Company was required to take actions to ensure an adequate level of data protection by implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures, including the use of appropriate technical safeguards, actions aimed at optimal configuration of the operating systems used, and regular measuring and assessing the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing in the form of security tests concerning the IT infrastructure. The nature and type of these actions should result from a conducted risk analysis, in which vulnerabilities related to the utilized resources should be identified, as well as the threats arising from them, and then appropriate security measures should be determined.

Meanwhile, the collected evidence did not contain anything that would indicate that the Company had properly conducted a risk analysis for the process of processing personal data in electronic form (no responses were obtained to the requests for clarification sent to the Company). Failure to assess the level of risk (or incorrect assessment) prevents the application of appropriate security measures for a given resource and increases the likelihood of an infringement (similar effects may arise from a mismatch of security measures to the conclusions drawn from a proper risk analysis).

Regardless of the circumstances related to the risk analysis, it should be stated that the Company did not apply appropriate security measures. The lack of appropriate technical safeguards constitutes a vulnerability, and consequently creates a high threat in the form of reduced system resilience, among other things, to the action of malicious software. As a result, in the analyzed case, the risk materialized: there was a breach of the security of the Company's IT system used by it to process personal data, and subsequently, the data contained in this system were encrypted. The action of the encrypting malware was not stopped by the security mechanisms of the operating systems. The infection occurred on (…) May 2020 (on Saturday), and its scale was verified during periodic checks of the operational correctness of servers and computer workstations conducted by employees.

Not focusing on the lack of information regarding the risk analysis conducted by the Company, in demonstrating the justification for this decision, it is necessary first to systematize the information about what organizational and technical measures were implemented by the Company. Due to the fact that information was not always provided by the Company adequately to the questions asked (which in some cases is significant), in this decision, the Company's explanations will most often be quoted along with the content of the questions to which they were provided. The dates of letters sent by the Company to the local Office will be consistently referred to in the further part of this justification according to the date of their receipt, as the dates indicated in the content of the sent letters are only identical to the dates of their dispatch on two occasions (e.g., in the case of a letter with a receipt date of (…) July 2021, the date indicated in the header is (…) June 2021, and the dispatch date is (…) July 2021).

Thus, in response to the supervisory authority's request dated (…) March 2021 for clarification on who, when, how, and using what tools conducted testing, measuring, and assessing the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processed personal data in the IT systems covered by the breach (and what measures these actions concerned), particularly regarding vulnerabilities, errors, and their possible consequences for these systems, as well as actions taken to minimize the risk of their occurrence (while also requesting information on whether as a result of these actions the Company made any changes to the measures it applied - if so, what and when),
the Company measures and actions taken to ensure the security of personal data are insufficient. The lack of a comprehensive security audit, which the Company has repeatedly postponed, raises significant concerns regarding its ability to adequately protect personal data. The assertions made by the Company regarding its preparedness to ensure an appropriate level of security for processed personal data are deemed unfounded, especially in light of the ongoing difficulties in conducting such an audit. 

The Company has indicated in its correspondence that it plans to conduct a security audit with the involvement of an external professional entity and to implement all post-audit recommendations. However, the repeated delays and the reasons provided for not conducting the audit, including financial constraints and the potential impact on the Company’s operational capabilities, suggest a lack of commitment to addressing the security vulnerabilities that may exist.

In conclusion, the Company’s current security measures, while they may include some technical and organizational safeguards, do not appear to be sufficient to mitigate the risks associated with personal data processing. The absence of a timely and thorough security audit further exacerbates these concerns, indicating that the Company may not be in compliance with applicable data protection regulations.
The technical and organizational measures of the Company were not adequate to the existing risk. They should have been selected with consideration of the state of technical knowledge, the cost of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purpose of processing, as well as the risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons. Meanwhile, the circumstances of the occurrence of a breach of personal data protection in the case at hand, as well as the manner in which it was responded to and how its effects were countered, indicate their inadequacy. The Administrator processed personal data of many entities and of a wide scope, which should have been taken into account as a factor indicating the necessity of applying more advanced security measures than those in the present case. Furthermore, the process of their recovery lasted unreasonably long, and the supervisory authority, due to the Company not receiving correspondence, is unable to determine whether it was completed in full.

In summary: due to the lack of application by the Administrator of other security measures than those indicated above aimed at minimizing the risk of infringement of personal data protection, it must be stated that the Company did not implement appropriate technical and organizational measures during the processing of this data, so that the processing took place in accordance with Regulation 2016/679 and in order to provide the necessary safeguards for the processing, which it was obliged to do in accordance with Article 24(1) and Article 25(1) of Regulation 2016/679. This also indicates the Company’s failure to fulfill the obligations arising from Article 32(1)(b) of Regulation 2016/679 (it did not apply technical and organizational measures ensuring a degree of security appropriate to the risk associated with the processing of personal data by ensuring the ability to continuously ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services). The Company did not demonstrate that a proper assessment was conducted to determine whether the aforementioned level of security is appropriate, taking into account the risk associated with the processing of personal data (the obligation to conduct such an assessment arises from Article 32(2) of Regulation 2016/679), which is also significant in the context of assessing the Administrator's compliance with the obligation provided for in Article 5(2) of Regulation 2016/679 (the accountability principle).

At this point, it should be emphasized what the Provincial Administrative Court in Warsaw indicated in its judgment of August 26, 2020, case no. II SA/Wa 2826/19: “(...) technical-organizational activities lie within the competence of the personal data administrator, but they cannot be selected in a completely free and voluntary manner, without taking into account the degree of risk and the nature of the protected personal data.” The technical and organizational measures indicated by the Administrator proved to be inadequate, which led to a situation where the systems were more susceptible to cyberattacks, including ransomware attacks that block access to data and aim to demand a ransom for their recovery.

It should be noted that Regulation 2016/679 introduced an approach in which risk management is the foundation of actions related to the protection of personal data and is characterized as a continuous process. Entities that process personal data are obliged not only to ensure that such processing complies with the guidelines of the aforementioned regulation through a one-time implementation of organizational and technical security measures but also to ensure the continuity of monitoring the level of threats and accountability regarding the level and adequacy of the implemented safeguards. This means that it becomes necessary to demonstrate before the supervisory authority that the solutions introduced to ensure the security of personal data processing are adequate to the level of risk, as well as take into account the nature of the organization and the mechanisms used for processing personal data. The consequence of such an orientation is the abandonment of lists of requirements regarding security imposed by the legislator in favor of independently selecting safeguards based on a threat analysis. Administrators are not provided with specific measures and procedures regarding security. The administrator must independently conduct a detailed analysis of the data processing processes and assess the risk, and then apply such measures and procedures that will be adequate to the estimated risk.

From the analysis of the incident, it follows that the methodology adopted by the Company for internal testing was unable to demonstrate a reliable assessment of the security status of the IT systems indicating all vulnerabilities and resistances to attempts to breach security due to unauthorized actions by third parties and malicious software. Therefore, the assessment of the security status proved insufficient in terms of applying appropriate technical and organizational safeguards.

**Consequences of Data Breach Notification Obligations**

This results in the Company, as the administrator of such data, being obligated to report the personal data breach to the supervisory authority, in accordance with Article 33(1) of Regulation 2016/679, which must include the information specified in Article 33(3) of Regulation 2016/679. It is worth emphasizing that the Guidelines indicate that: “(...) when assessing the risk that may arise from a breach, the administrator should take into account the severity of the potential impact on the rights and freedoms of natural persons and the likelihood of its occurrence. Of course, the risk increases when the consequences of the breach are more serious, as well as when the likelihood of their occurrence increases. In case of any doubts, the administrator should report the breach, even if such caution may seem excessive.”

It is particularly important to note that the possible consequences of the incident do not have to materialize - the content of Article 33(1) of Regulation 2016/679 states that the mere occurrence of a personal data breach, which is associated with a risk to the rights or freedoms of natural persons, implies the obligation to report the breach to the appropriate supervisory authority. The correctness of this approach has been confirmed multiple times in the justifications issued by the Provincial Administrative Court in Warsaw (see: judgment of September 22, 2021, in case no. II SA/Wa 791/21, judgment of July 1, 2022, in case no. II SA/Wa 4143/21, or judgment of January 21, 2022, in case no. II SA/Wa 1353/21).

It should also be emphasized that the Company, when processing personal data in electronic form, should be aware of the risks associated, for example, with ransomware attacks. The existence of these risks, in the absence of actions by the data administrator aimed at minimizing them through the implementation of appropriate organizational and technical measures, directly leads to the emergence of a risk of violation of the rights or freedoms of natural persons whose data is processed in this manner. Therefore, in this case, it is also concerning that the Company, despite the occurrence of a personal data breach, did not implement changes in the actions taken to ensure the security of personal data indicated in the explanations dated (...) April 2021. This is evidenced by the fact that the same measures were referenced by the Administrator in response to a question posed in a letter sent to the Company on (...) June 2021, in which the supervisory authority called upon the Administrator to provide explanations regarding the security measures implemented to minimize the risk of a recurrence of the breach. Furthermore, the Company explained in the same letter that no changes were made to the applicable measures at the Administrator’s (...) and (...). Changes were only indicated in the explanations submitted to this Office on (...) December 2020, which state that: “(...) after the incident, professional software was installed - (...)”.

In a situation where, as a result of a personal data breach, there is a risk of violation of the rights or freedoms of natural persons, the administrator is obliged to implement all appropriate technical and organizational measures to immediately identify the personal data breach and promptly inform the supervisory authority. The administrator should fulfill this obligation as quickly as possible. Recital 85 of the preamble to Regulation 2016/679 explains: “In the absence of an appropriate and timely response, a personal data breach may result in physical harm, material or non-material damage to natural persons, such as loss of control over their personal data or restriction of rights, discrimination, identity theft or fraud, financial loss, unauthorized reversal of pseudonymization, damage to reputation, breach of confidentiality of personal data protected by professional secrecy, or any other significant economic or social damage. Therefore, immediately after identifying a personal data breach, the administrator should report it to the supervisory authority without undue delay, if feasible, no later than 72 hours after the breach is identified, unless the administrator can demonstrate in accordance with the accountability principle that it is unlikely that the breach could result in a risk to the rights or freedoms of natural persons. If it is not possible to report within 72 hours, the report should be accompanied by an explanation of the reasons for the delay, and information may be provided gradually, without further undue delay.”

Consequently, it should be stated that the Company did not report the personal data breach to the supervisory authority within the time frame specified in Article 33(1) of Regulation 2016/679, which constitutes a violation of this provision by the Company. 

According to Article 34(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679, in a situation where, as a result of a personal data breach, there is a high risk of violation of the rights or freedoms of natural persons, the administrator is obliged to notify the affected individuals of such a breach without undue delay. The notification should describe the nature of the personal data breach in clear and plain language (e.g., presenting the circumstances of the breach along with a description of the categories of data that were breached) and include at least the information and measures referred to in Article 33(3)(b), (c), and (d) of Regulation 2016/679. 

The administrator notifies individuals about the breach of their data individually, unless this would require a disproportionate effort. In such a case, the administrator issues a public communication or uses a similar measure to inform the affected individuals in an equally effective manner (Article 34(3)(c) of Regulation 2016/679).
As previously indicated, the Administrator assumed that as a result of the ransomware attack, there was no breach of personal data protection. Based on this - erroneous in the assessment of the supervisory authority - assumption, it was not evaluated whether there was a high risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons, which would require notifying the individuals concerned about the breach of their personal data protection.

The fact that part of the encrypted data was subsequently recovered with the help of authorities indicates a loss of availability for at least several months. Seeking assistance from authorities to recover lost data casts doubt on the truthfulness of the Company's explanations regarding the possession of all lost data in paper form.

In the explanations provided by the Company, it is also difficult to find justification for the assertion that the incident did not result in a breach of the confidentiality of personal data (including, among others, names, surnames, and PESEL identification numbers) contained in the systems subject to the ransomware attack. Considering the above, as well as the scope of the breached personal data and the duration of their unavailability due to the encryption of backup copies and the lack of the possibility of quickly restoring the encrypted data, it must be concluded that there was a high risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons.

Consequently, the Company was obliged to notify the individuals concerned about the breach of their personal data protection, in accordance with Article 34(1) of Regulation 2016/679, providing them with the information specified in Article 34(2) of Regulation 2016/679.

In light of the above, it should be concluded that the Administrator incorrectly stated that there was no breach of personal data protection, and consequently did not recognize that this incident was associated with a high risk of infringement of the rights or freedoms of natural persons (hence there was no notification of the breach to the individuals concerned). Therefore, it should be concluded that the administrator violated Articles 34(1) and 34(2) of Regulation 2016/679.

In connection with the above findings made in the case, it should be stated not only that the Company (by not applying technical and organizational measures ensuring the security of processed data, resulting in a breach of personal data protection) violated Article 5(1)(f) of Regulation 2016/679, reflected in the form of obligations specified in Articles 24(1), 25(1), 32(1), and 32(2) of Regulation 2016/679, but also failed to fulfill the obligations arising from Article 5(2) of this Regulation.

The fact of the Administrator's violation of the accountability principle is also evidenced by its terse and inconsistent explanations and the consistent failure to provide evidence of their truthfulness. The above is confirmed by the ruling of the Administrative Court in Warsaw dated February 10, 2021, case reference II SA/Wa 2378/20: "The principle of accountability is therefore based on the legal responsibility of the administrator for properly fulfilling obligations and imposes on them the obligation to demonstrate, both to the supervisory authority and to the data subject, evidence of compliance with all data processing principles." Similarly, the issue of the accountability principle is interpreted in the ruling dated August 26, 2020, by the Administrative Court in Warsaw, case reference II SA/Wa 2826/19: "Considering the entirety of the norms of Regulation 2016/679, it should be emphasized that the administrator has considerable freedom regarding the security measures applied; however, they are also responsible for violations of data protection regulations. The principle of accountability directly implies that it is the data administrator who should demonstrate, and thus prove, that they comply with the provisions specified in Article 5(1) of Regulation 2016/679."

Furthermore, it should be concluded that there were no justified grounds for the Company to assume the lack of necessity to fulfill the obligations arising from Articles 33(1) and 34(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679.

In light of the above findings, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office, exercising the authority granted to them under Article 58(2)(i) of Regulation 2016/679, according to which each supervisory authority has the power to impose, in addition to or instead of other corrective measures provided for in Article 58(2)(a)-(h) and (j) of this Regulation, an administrative fine under Article 83(4)(a) and (5)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, taking into account the circumstances established in the proceedings, stated that there were grounds justifying the imposition of an administrative fine on the Administrator.

According to Article 83(4)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, violations of the provisions concerning the obligations of the administrator and the processor, referred to in Articles 8, 11, 25-39, and 42 and 43, are subject to an administrative fine of up to EUR 10,000,000, and in the case of an enterprise - up to 2% of its total annual worldwide turnover from the previous financial year, with the higher amount applying.

According to Article 83(5)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, violations of the provisions concerning the fundamental principles of processing, including the conditions for consent, referred to in Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9, are subject to an administrative fine of up to EUR 20,000,000, and in the case of an enterprise - up to 4% of its total annual worldwide turnover from the previous financial year, with the higher amount applying.

Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679 states that if the administrator or processor intentionally or unintentionally violates within the same or related operations...
processing several provisions of this regulation, the total amount of the administrative monetary penalty does not exceed the amount of the penalty for the most serious violation. 

In this case, the administrative monetary penalty imposed on the Company was for violating Article 25(1), Article 32(1) and (2), Article 33(1), and Article 34(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679 based on the aforementioned Article 83(4)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, while for the violation of Article 5(1)(f) and Article 5(2) of Regulation 2016/679 - based on Article 83(5)(a) of that regulation. At the same time, the penalty amounting to the equivalent of EUR 10,000 imposed on the Company for the violation of all the above provisions - in accordance with Article 83(3) of Regulation 2016/679 - does not exceed the amount of the penalty for the most serious violation identified in this case, namely the violation of Article 5(1)(f) and Article 5(2) of Regulation 2016/679, which, according to Article 83(5)(a) of Regulation 2016/679, is subject to an administrative monetary penalty of up to EUR 20,000,000, or in the case of an enterprise - up to 4% of its total annual worldwide turnover from the previous financial year.

1. The nature, gravity, and duration of the violation, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the processing, the number of affected individuals whose data is concerned, and the extent of the damage suffered by them (Article 83(2)(a) of Regulation 2016/679). In determining the penalty, it was significant that the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679, which impose obligations on the controller regarding the application of appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of the processed personal data, primarily affected the availability of data of a special category of data subjects. Considering the aspect of the purpose of processing, it should be noted that the processing of employees' personal data requires particular diligence, and there is an imbalance between the Company (employer or principal) and the data subjects (employees or contractors). The violation of the aforementioned provisions identified in this case is of significant weight and serious nature also because it may lead to material or non-material damages for the individual whose data has been violated, and the probability of their occurrence cannot be excluded. Furthermore, the risk arising from the broad scope of data covered by the violation should be taken into account. It should be emphasized that it is unknown whether, apart from the violation of data availability (even if temporary, but causing the individuals concerned to potentially suffer material damage), there was also a breach of the confidentiality of that data, which also constitutes non-material damage (harm). The data subject may at least feel fear of losing control over their personal data, identity theft, or identity fraud, or finally, financial loss. It is also significant in this context that the individuals whose data were subject to the violation were not properly informed about it (i.e., about its circumstances, consequences, or risks associated with it), and therefore cannot counteract potential damages. It should be assumed that the violation of the provisions of Article 5(1)(f), Article 5(2), Article 25(1), and Article 32(1) and (2) began even before the occurrence of the personal data breach (i.e., before the day of (...) May 2020). Meanwhile, the violation of the obligations arising from Article 33(1) and Article 34(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679 has been ongoing since the moment the Controller was required to fulfill its informational obligations arising from these provisions. Considering this premise, it should also be noted that the personal data breach concerned data of approximately 30 individuals.

2. The intentional or unintentional nature of the violation (Article 83(2)(b) of Regulation 2016/679). According to the Guidelines of the Article 29 Working Party on the application and determination of administrative monetary penalties for the purposes of Regulation No. 2016/679 WP253 (adopted on October 3, 2017, approved by EROD on May 25, 2018), intent "includes both knowledge and deliberate action, in relation to the characteristics of the prohibited act." The Company made a conscious decision, among other things, not to notify the President of the UODO about the violation, as well as the individuals whose data were concerned. There is no doubt that the Company, processing personal data of individuals working or contracting for it, must have knowledge regarding personal data protection, including knowledge of the consequences of identifying a personal data breach resulting in a high risk of violating the rights or freedoms of natural persons. Being aware of this, the Controller nevertheless made a decision, among other things, to refrain from reporting the breach to the President of the UODO and notifying the individuals whose data were concerned, despite the fact that the President of the UODO had initially informed the Company about the obligations imposed on the controller in connection with the data protection breach. Ultimately, the very initiation by the President of the UODO of this proceeding regarding, among other things, the obligation to report the personal data breach to the supervisory authority and to notify the individuals whose data...
relating to, should at least raise doubts for the Administrator regarding the correctness of the position he has adopted. However, the supervisory authority did not find that the violation of other provisions (i.e., in particular, Article 25(1) and Article 32(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679) had a deliberate character in this case—it rather resulted from negligence on the part of the Company, which does not change the fact that the criterion from Article 83(2)(b) of Regulation 2016/679 should be considered in this case as generally aggravating.

3. The degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority in order to remedy the violation and mitigate its potential negative effects (Article 83(2)(f) of Regulation 2016/679). The evidence gathered in this case does not indicate anything that would demonstrate the actual involvement of the Company in remedial actions aimed at eliminating the violation and mitigating its potential negative effects, apart from declared attempts to recover data availability with the help of the relevant authorities (and it is unclear whether these actions were fully effective). During the proceedings (within the framework of cooperation with the authority), the Company did not undertake practically any actions aimed at implementing appropriate technical and organizational safeguards, new procedures for regular testing and examination of already implemented measures, etc. The aforementioned evidence also does not indicate that the Administrator conducted an analysis of the impact of the personal data breach on the rights or freedoms of the affected individuals, nor did he perform an audit, which he declared to conduct after the breach. Meanwhile, attempts to recover data availability with the help of the relevant authorities are merely an attempt to fulfill the "ordinary" obligation resting on the Administrator.

4. Categories of personal data affected by the breach (Article 83(2)(g) of Regulation 2016/679). The personal data to which the Company lost (even temporarily) access (and to which unknown and unauthorized third parties could potentially gain access) do not belong to the special categories of personal data referred to in Article 9 of Regulation 2016/679. However, their broad scope (i.e., names, surnames, dates of birth, bank account numbers, residential or temporary addresses, PESEL identification numbers, email addresses, salary data, phone numbers, identity card numbers) is associated with a high risk of violation of the rights or freedoms of the individuals affected by the breach. It should be emphasized that in particular, the fact that the breach involved the PESEL identification number (in conjunction with the name and surname) can realistically and negatively affect the protection of the rights or freedoms of individuals. The PESEL identification number, which is an eleven-digit numerical symbol that uniquely identifies an individual, containing among other things the date of birth and gender designation, and thus closely linked to the private sphere of the individual and also subject, as a national identification number, to special protection under Article 87 of Regulation 2016/679, is data of a special nature and requires such special protection.

5. The manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the breach (Article 83(2)(h) of Regulation 2016/679). The President of the UODO learned about the breach of personal data protection regulations that are the subject of these proceedings from a third party. In this case, it is particularly important to emphasize that the supervisory authority was not informed about the occurrence of the personal data breach in accordance with the procedure provided for such situations as specified in Article 33 of Regulation 2016/679.

6. Other aggravating or mitigating factors (Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679). In considering this criterion, the President of the UODO deemed it appropriate to take into account that the Company did not provide comprehensive explanations in response to the supervisory authority's requests—responses to the questions posed were often terse or inconsistent. The timeliness and manner of their provision (e.g., repeatedly sending documents not signed by persons authorized to represent the Company) are detrimental to the Administrator when assessing his cooperation with the President of the UODO. 

In determining the amount of the administrative monetary penalty, the President of the UODO found no grounds to consider mitigating circumstances that would affect the final amount of the penalty. All the criteria listed in Article 83(2)(a) - (j) of Regulation 2016/679, in the opinion of the supervisory authority, constitute either aggravating criteria or are merely neutral. Also, applying the criterion mentioned in Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679 (which requires consideration of all other aggravating or mitigating factors applicable to the circumstances of the case), no mitigating circumstances were found, only neutral ones (as noted below in point 6 regarding financial benefits gained directly or indirectly in connection with the breach or avoided losses) and aggravating ones (indicated in point 6 above). 

The fact that the President of the UODO applied a sanction in the form of an administrative monetary penalty against the Company in this case, as well as its amount, was not influenced by other circumstances indicated in Article 83(2) of Regulation 2016/679, namely: 

1. Actions taken to minimize the damage suffered by the individuals whose data are concerned (Article 83(2)(c) of Regulation 2016/679). In this decision, it was indicated as an aggravating circumstance that the individuals whose data are concerned may continue to suffer property damage, and the occurrence of the data breach may lead to non-material damage (harm). In the gathered...
**1.** There is no evidence material indicating the occurrence of material damages. Considering this premise, it should be noted in passing that the Company did not take appropriate actions to minimize the non-material damages suffered by the individuals to whom this data pertains.

**2.** The degree of responsibility of the administrator, taking into account the implemented technical and organizational measures (Article 83(2)(d) of Regulation 2016/679). The Guidelines adopted on October 3, 2017, by the Article 29 Working Party regarding the application and determination of administrative fines for the purposes of Regulation No. 2016/679 indicate that when considering this premise, "the supervisory authority must answer the question of to what extent the administrator 'did everything that could be expected,' considering the nature, purposes, or scope of processing and in light of the obligations imposed on them by the regulation." The President of the UODO stated in this case that the Company violated the provisions of Article 25(1) and Article 32(1) and (2) of Regulation 2016/679. In his opinion, the administrator bears a high degree of responsibility for the failure to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures that would have prevented the violation of personal data protection. It is evident that in the context of the nature, purpose, and scope of personal data processing, the Administrator did not "do everything that could be expected of them"; thus, they failed to fulfill the obligations imposed on them by Articles 25 and 32 of Regulation 2016/679. In this case, this circumstance constitutes the essence of the violation itself; it is not merely a factor influencing - mitigating or aggravating - its assessment. For this reason, the lack of appropriate technical and organizational measures referred to in Articles 25 and 32 of Regulation 2016/679 cannot be recognized by the President of the UODO in this case as a circumstance that could additionally influence a stricter assessment of the violation and the imposition of an administrative fine on the Administrator.

**3.** Relevant previous violations of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 (Article 83(2)(e) of Regulation 2016/679). The President of the UODO did not find relevant previous violations of Regulation 2016/679 by the Company; therefore, there are no grounds to treat this circumstance as aggravating. However, it is the duty of every administrator to comply with the law (including provisions concerning the protection of personal data), which means that the lack of previous violations of the aforementioned legal act cannot be considered a mitigating circumstance when imposing sanctions.

**4.** Compliance with previously applied measures in the same case, referred to in Article 58(2) of Regulation 2016/679 (Article 83(2)(i) of Regulation 2016/679). Prior to issuing this decision, the President of the UODO did not apply any measures listed in Article 58(2) of Regulation 2016/679 against the Company in the case under consideration, which means that the Administrator had no obligation to take any actions related to their implementation, and such actions, assessed by the President of the UODO, could have had an aggravating or mitigating impact on the assessment of the established violation.

**5.** The application of approved codes of conduct under Article 40 of Regulation 2016/679 or approved certification mechanisms under Article 42 of Regulation 2016/679 (Article 83(2)(j) of Regulation 2016/679). The Company does not apply approved codes of conduct or approved certification mechanisms referred to in the provisions of Regulation 2016/679. However, their adoption, implementation, and application are not mandatory for administrators and processors, as stipulated by the provisions of Regulation 2016/679; therefore, the circumstance of their non-application cannot be considered against the Administrator in this case. On the other hand, the circumstance of adopting and applying such instruments as measures guaranteeing a higher than standard level of protection for processed personal data could be taken into account in favor of the Administrator.

**6.** Financial benefits obtained directly or indirectly in connection with the violation or losses avoided (Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679). The President of the UODO did not find that the Administrator obtained any financial benefits or avoided such losses in connection with the violation. Therefore, there are no grounds to treat this circumstance as aggravating for the Administrator. The existence of measurable financial benefits resulting from the violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 would need to be assessed very negatively. Conversely, the Administrator's failure to achieve such benefits, as a natural state independent of the violation and its consequences, is a circumstance that, by its very nature, cannot be mitigating for the Administrator. This is confirmed by the wording of Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679, which requires the supervisory authority to pay due attention to the benefits "obtained" - that is, those that occurred on the part of the entity committing the violation. The President of the UODO, comprehensively considering the case, did not note any circumstances other than those described above that could influence the assessment of the violation and the amount of the imposed administrative fine, which should be taken into account in the context of the premise from Article 83(2)(k) of Regulation 2016/679.

In the opinion of the President of the UODO, the applied administrative fine fulfills, in the established circumstances of this case, the functions referred to in Article 83(1) of Regulation 2016/679, i.e., it is effective, proportional, and deterrent in this individual case.
It should be emphasized that the penalty will be effective if its imposition leads to the Company, which is obliged to process personal data in accordance with the requirements not only arising from Regulation 2016/679 but also other provisions (mentioned in this justification), fulfilling its obligations regarding data protection in the future, particularly in terms of reporting personal data breaches to the President of the UODO and notifying the individuals affected by the breach.

In the opinion of the President of the Personal Data Protection Office, the administrative fine will serve a repressive function, as it will constitute a response to the Company's violation of the provisions of Regulation 2016/679. It will also serve a preventive function; in the opinion of the President of the UODO, it will indicate to both the Company and other data controllers the reprehensibility of neglecting the obligations of controllers related to the occurrence of personal data breaches, which aim to prevent their negative and often severe consequences for the individuals affected by the breach, as well as to eliminate these consequences or at least mitigate them.

Pursuant to the provisions of Article 103 of the UODO, the equivalent amounts expressed in euros, referred to in Article 83 of Regulation 2016/679, are calculated in zlotys according to the average euro exchange rate announced by the National Bank of Poland in the exchange rate table on January 28 of each year, and in the event that the National Bank of Poland does not announce the average euro exchange rate on January 28 of a given year - according to the average euro exchange rate announced in the nearest exchange rate table of the National Bank of Poland after that date.

In the opinion of the President of the UODO, the imposed monetary penalty of 47,160 PLN (forty-seven thousand one hundred sixty zlotys), which is equivalent to 10,000 euros (average euro exchange rate from January 30, 2023 - 4.7160 PLN), meets the conditions referred to in Article 83(1) of Regulation 2016/679 due to the severity of the established violation in the context of the fundamental objective of Regulation 2016/679 - the protection of the fundamental rights and freedoms of natural persons, particularly the right to the protection of personal data.

The collected evidence does not contain data regarding the total annual global turnover of the Company from the previous financial year. However, the Company provided information regarding its net sales revenue and equivalent for the period from (…) April 2019 to (…) March 2020 (which amounted to 6,948,467.84 PLN) and for the preceding financial year (8,827,593.26 PLN). These data indicate relatively high turnover during the examined period and the fact of its decrease - they could, among other things, serve to estimate the basis for determining the amount of the administrative monetary penalty in accordance with the provisions of Article 101a(2) of Regulation 2016/679.

Referring to the amount of the administrative monetary penalty imposed on the Company, the President of the UODO considered that it is proportional to the violations that occurred - it is worth emphasizing that the amount of the imposed penalty of 47,160 PLN is only: - 0.1% of the maximum penalty that the President of the UODO could impose on the Company for the violations established in this case - applying the static maximum penalty in accordance with Article 83(4) of Regulation 2016/679 (i.e. 10,000,000 euros) - 0.05% of the maximum penalty that the President of the UODO could impose on the Company - applying the static maximum penalty in accordance with Article 83(5) of Regulation 2016/679 (i.e. 20,000,000 euros).

In this factual and legal state, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office ruled as in the operative part.
