**Criminal Responsibility**  
Minors over 14 years of age are also liable* for the crimes defined in the Penal Code such as harassment, threats, or the dissemination or forwarding of images that severely undermine a person's privacy, even if obtained with their permission, applicable in cases of sexting, cyberbullying, or cyber harassment. The measures in these cases depend on their circumstances: developmental progress, background, etc., and typically involve community service or socio-educational tasks, which may escalate to supervised freedom and even deprivation of liberty (internment in centers or weekend confinement).

**Disciplinary Responsibility in the Educational Field**  
These behaviors give rise to disciplinary responsibility when they occur in educational centers (harassment, intimidation, humiliation, serious offenses, discrimination, or violence against other students or teachers conducted through the Internet). Corrective measures can be imposed, ranging from verbal warnings or written reprimands to suspension of the right to attend the center or expulsion of the student.  

**Administrative Responsibility**  
The publication without consent of sensitive information about a person (images, audio, videos, or information of a sexual or violent nature that allows identification) through the Internet constitutes a violation of data protection regulations which, without prejudice to reporting it to the Priority Channel of the Agency to request the urgent removal of such content, may result in a financial penalty for the person who published or contributed to the dissemination. The financial penalty for violation of data protection regulations imposed on minors is jointly borne by their parents or guardians. An example of a sanctioning procedure in which the fine imposed must be paid by the parents as it concerns a minor.  
It is an infringement both to obtain data illegally (images, videos, audio, or other content) and for anyone who, without the consent of the affected person, has disseminated or published it on the Internet.  

**Civil Responsibility**  
The material and moral damages caused to third parties by minors as a result of these behaviors (both criminal and non-criminal) give rise to patrimonial civil responsibility, which is borne by the parents or guardians.
