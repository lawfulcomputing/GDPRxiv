**Family Digital Plan**

1. Turn off electronic devices that no one is using.

2. Avoid using two or more devices at the same time.

3. Set time limits for screen use: For children aged 0 to 2 years, screen use should be avoided, as there is no safe amount of time. For ages 3 to 5 years, less than one hour per day, and from age 5 onwards, less than two hours of digital leisure time per day.

4. Encourage physical exercise as a family, as it is known that the less physical activity is done, the more time is spent on screens. Avoid screen mounts for strollers, cars, etc. This increases screen time and prevents them from being aware of their surroundings and interacting with it. Look for alternatives such as sleeping, playing, listening to music, or commenting on the landscape.

5. Use devices with proper posture, take breaks every 30 minutes, and do short walks and stretches.

6. Avoid prolonged exposure to backlit screens and the resulting visual fatigue. We should follow the 20/20/20 rule: every 20 minutes of screen exposure, blink 20 times and focus on a distant object for 20 seconds.

7. Plan daily routines and activities without screens according to age, including disconnection times: games, crafts, painting, reading, etc.

8. Establish screen-free zones such as the bedroom or bathroom. We can choose a place to leave devices when not in use, a "device parking" area.

9. Reduce their use in play areas and during meals.

10. Avoid screen use 1-2 hours before bedtime.

11. Practice mindfulness without distractions when studying, working, or spending time with family. During these moments, devices should be silenced or turned off and kept out of the room.

12. Encourage creative use of technology: create a family photo album or videos, search for information together about something that piques our curiosity, etc.

13. Choose age-appropriate content for children and ensure it comes from reliable sources.

14. Be present and aware of the content. Review it before the child accesses it to decide if it is appropriate or not. Avoid using "nanny devices" and be with them when they use screens.

15. Foster critical thinking in children.

16. Work on digital empathy: being able to put oneself in another's shoes and understand that behind the screens, there are people.

17. Give them our old devices instead of gifting them a new one, as if we give a new device to the child, they will understand that it is theirs and will find it harder to accept parental supervision.

18. Before giving a device, carry out the Family Digital Plan and establish clear limits on time, spaces, disconnection time, and ask for permission to purchase or install applications, among others.

19. Conduct regular reviews of devices with the children. If we do it with them, we do not violate their privacy and help them detect risks early and find solutions.
