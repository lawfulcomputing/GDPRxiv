**Plan for the Arrival of the Mobile Phone**  
When giving a mobile phone to our children, we provide them with the opportunity to access a wide variety of information, relationships, and content. However, there are also risks that they must be aware of. Before giving it to them, assess their level of maturity and explain these risks to them.  

**Be Careful with Data on Social Media**  
Discuss the dangers that may be encountered and how to manage their data. Set up their social media profile together.  

**Know Who They Are Talking To**  
What contacts do they have on their social media? Who do they follow? Take an interest in these matters while respecting their privacy. Teach them not to share personal information or photographs with strangers, as this can lead to situations of cyberbullying, blackmail, or grooming.  

**Be Open to Help**  
Teach them that they can tell you about any situation that has made them feel uncomfortable. Being available to help in a timely manner can prevent the situation from becoming more serious. The AEPD has a Priority Channel for reporting unauthorized publication of sensitive, sexual, or violent content on the internet and requesting its urgent removal.  

**Ensure a Space for Disconnection**  
Share with them the need to have technology-free moments for the whole family that allow access to other types of experiences that benefit their development.  

**Supervise and Set Limits**  
Make sure that the mobile phone does not take away time from engaging in other key activities for their development (sleeping, studying, playing without electronic devices, etc.).  

**Take an Interest in Their Video Games**  
Offer them games appropriate for their age and maturity. Accompanying them in their digital life also means supervising what they play, how much time they dedicate to it, and how they behave.  

**Stimulate Their Critical Thinking**  
Explain the possible consequences of what is sent or published. Before posting on social media or sending photos, videos, or audio in which other people appear, they must ensure that the other person agrees.  

**You Are Responsible for Your Children**  
Parents are civilly liable for the damages caused by their children. They are also jointly liable for fines imposed in matters of data protection concerning minors.  

**Observe How They Feel in Their Digital Life**  
Pay attention to their emotions, especially changes in mood or inappropriate reactions after periods of intensive use. Identify any situation that is affecting their physical and mental well-being at an early stage.
