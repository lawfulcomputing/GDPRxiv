I  
Competence  
In accordance with the provisions of Articles 58.2 and 60 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, regarding the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data (GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 and 68.2 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.

II  
Preliminary Issues  
In this case, the claimant reports the improper inclusion of their personal data in solvency files, as the debt is not yet certain or enforceable since it is being contested in an ongoing judicial process.  
The claimant asserts that the responding entity includes their personal data in credit information systems, in relation to a debt that is contested and has been admitted for processing in the Court of First Instance number 4 of Barcelona, on March 10, 2023, and submits a report from ASNEF regarding the inclusion of their data at the request of the responding party, dated August 27, 2023.  
The responding party claims that the reported facts are a security incident caused by a technical error (the misconfiguration of the Claimant's customer file button).

Article 6.1 of the GDPR  
The GDPR in its Article 4.11 defines the consent of the data subject as “any freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes by which they signify agreement, by a statement or by a clear affirmative action, to the processing of personal data relating to them.”  
Regarding the lawfulness of the processing of personal data, Article 6.1 of the GDPR establishes the following:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;

c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject;  
d) the processing is necessary to protect vital interests of the data subject or another natural person;  
e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller;  
f) the processing is necessary for the purposes of legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, provided that such interests do not override the interests or the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require the protection of personal data, particularly when the data subject is a child.  
The provisions of item f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the exercise of their functions.

“1. Unless proven otherwise, the processing of personal data relating to the non-fulfillment of monetary, financial, or credit obligations by common credit information systems shall be presumed lawful when the following requirements are met:

IV  
Classification of the infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR  
The inclusion of the personal data of the complaining party in solvency files, despite the fact that the debt is not yet certain or enforceable because it is being contested in an ongoing judicial process, constitutes an infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR.  
However, the responding party claims that it is not a credit institution, so the aggravating factor of Article 76.2 b) of the LOPDGDD does not apply. Subsequently, the responding party, in response to the initiation agreement, further states that it has already deregistered the complainant, which implies that it has the necessary technical and organizational security measures, asserting that the facts in question are the result of a technical error (the misconfiguration of the complainant's client file button), and that there is no improper data processing involved.  
In this regard, it should first be noted that Article 76.2 b) of the LOPDGDD considers the link between the infringer's activity and the processing of personal data as an aggravating factor, which does not only apply to credit institutions but also to entities that provide financial services to users, as their activity involves the processing of personal data, which is why this aggravating factor is applicable.  
Regarding the consideration that it is a technical error, this is not an argument that exonerates the responding party from liability, nor does it grant legitimacy in the processing of personal data, as this has been an unlawful processing of data, since the personal data of the complaining party has been improperly registered in the ASNEF solvency file, despite the fact that the debt subject to registration is not certain as it is being legally contested.  
For all these reasons, the reported facts constitute an infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, as the inclusion of personal data in solvency files for a debt pending judicial resolution constitutes processing of personal data without evidence that the responding party has carried out the necessary balancing to determine the prevalence of its legitimate interest over the interests, rights, and freedoms of the complaining party, as this processing is not covered by the presumption of lawfulness provided for in Article 20 of the LOPDGDD since the required debt subject to inclusion in the solvency files is still not certain or enforceable as it is in an ongoing judicial process.

Termination of the procedure  
Article 85 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), under the heading "Termination in sanctioning procedures" provides as follows:  
“1. Once a sanctioning procedure has been initiated, if the offender acknowledges their responsibility, the procedure may be resolved with the imposition of the appropriate sanction.  
2. When the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature or when a pecuniary sanction and another non-pecuniary sanction can be imposed but the impropriety of the second has been justified, voluntary payment by the presumed responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except regarding the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for the damages caused by the commission of the infringement.  
3. In both cases, when the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature, the competent authority to resolve the procedure will apply reductions of at least 20% on the amount of the proposed sanction, which are cumulative. The aforementioned reductions must be determined in the notification of initiation of the procedure and their effectiveness will be conditioned on the withdrawal or waiver of any action or appeal in the administrative route against the sanction. The percentage of reduction provided for in this section may be increased by regulation.”
