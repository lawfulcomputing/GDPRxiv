Sixth.- PRINCIPLE OF PRESUMPTION OF INNOCENCE.- The claiming entity wishes to emphasize the full validity of the principle of presumption of innocence in the administrative sanctioning field, including procedures related to the protection of personal data.  
This principle, enshrined in Article 24.2 of the Spanish Constitution and repeatedly recognized by the Constitutional Court, constitutes a fundamental right that protects any person or entity from administrative sanctions without clear and conclusive evidence of their responsibility. In the context of administrative sanctioning, the principle of presumption of innocence implies that:

1. Burden of proof: The burden of proving the commission of an infringement lies exclusively with the sanctioning authority. It must provide sufficient and valid evidence that conclusively demonstrates the existence of the facts constituting the infringement and the responsibility of the sanctioned entity.  
2. Absence of obligation to prove one's own innocence: The alleged infringer is not obliged to demonstrate their innocence, as this burden falls on the party alleging the commission of the infringement.  
3. Resolution in case of doubt: In the presence of reasonable doubts regarding the materiality of the facts or the authorship of the same, an acquittal must prevail, in accordance with this principle. These elements have been reiterated by the jurisprudence of the Constitutional Court and by the Administrative Litigation Chamber of the Supreme Court, as well as by the National Court in numerous resolutions. Within the framework of the GDPR and the LOPDGDD, the principle of presumption of innocence takes on special relevance.  
The Guidelines of the EDPB and the sanctioning regime of the LOPDGDD require that any sanctioning decision be based on clear, sufficient, and verifiable evidence that leaves no reasonable doubt about the infringement.  
In the present case, the arguments and evidence provided by the sanctioning authority have not conclusively demonstrated that the entity has failed to comply with the requirements for obtaining and demonstrating the consent of the claimant, as required by Article 7 of the GDPR.  
In particular:  
1. The evidence presented by the entity (log records, verification certificates, and form model) is compatible with data processing in accordance with the regulations and has not been duly assessed as a whole by the proposed resolution.  
2. The proposed resolution is based on a strict interpretation of the formal requirements of proof, without considering the entirety of the evidence provided by the entity and without assessing the absence of specific harms.  
3. The absence of clear and conclusive incriminating elements demonstrating that the entity failed to comply with the legal requirements for obtaining consent makes the principle of presumption of innocence applicable, obliging any doubts to be resolved in favor of the claiming entity.  
The National Court, in its Judgment of February 22, 2022 (rec. 26/2020), emphasized that the principle of presumption of innocence requires the existence of clear incriminating evidence, and not mere statements or indications, to issue a sanctioning resolution.  
In the same vein, the jurisprudence establishes that the mere insufficiency of the evidence of charge must lead to an acquittal. Likewise, the First Section of the Constitutional Court, in its Judgment 76/1990, of 25...

Prohibition of unsolicited or expressly authorized commercial communications  
The LSSI prohibits unsolicited or expressly authorized commercial communications, based on a concept of commercial communication that is qualified as a service of the information society and is defined in its Annex as: “f) any form of communication aimed at the promotion, directly or indirectly, of the image or of the goods or services of a company, organization, or person engaged in a commercial, industrial, artisanal, or professional activity.”

105. Recital 42 states that: “When the processing is carried out with the consent of the data subject, the data controller must be able to demonstrate that the data subject has given consent to the processing operation.”

IV  
Classification of the infringement committed by sending commercial communications without the necessary legitimacy.  
The fact that the defendant sends unsolicited or expressly unauthorized advertising emails to the claimant constitutes a violation of the provisions established in Article 21 of the LSSI, which states the following:

2. The provisions of the previous section shall not apply when there is a prior contractual relationship, provided that the provider has lawfully obtained the contact details of the recipient and uses them to send commercial communications regarding products or services of their own company that are similar to those that were initially the subject of the contract with the customer. V  
Sanction  
The aforementioned infringement is classified as "minor" in article 38.4.d) of said regulation, which qualifies as such, “The sending of commercial communications by email or other equivalent electronic communication means when the requirements established in article 21 are not met and do not constitute a serious infringement.”  
According to the provisions of article 39.1.c) of the LSSI, minor infringements may be sanctioned with a fine of up to €30,000.  
On the other hand, article 40 of the LSSI establishes the criteria for the graduation of the amount of the sanctions:  
“The amount of the fines imposed will be graduated according to the following criteria: a) The existence of intent. b) The period of time during which the infringement has been committed. c) The recurrence of committing infringements of the same nature, when this has been declared by a firm resolution. d) The nature and amount of the damages caused. e) The benefits obtained from the infringement. f) The volume of business affected by the committed infringement. g) Adherence to a code of conduct or an applicable advertising self-regulation system regarding the committed infringement, which complies with the provisions of article 18 or in the eighth final provision and which has been favorably reported by the competent authority or authorities.  
Based on the evidence obtained, it is considered appropriate to graduate the sanction to be imposed in accordance with section c) of the aforementioned article 40 LSSI. In this case, we can cite files PS/161/2021 and PS/425/2021 where the respondent is sanctioned for carrying out commercial communications without having been previously requested or expressly authorized.  
According to these criteria, it is deemed appropriate to impose a sanction of €10,000 (ten thousand euros) for the infringement of article 21.1 of the LSSI.
