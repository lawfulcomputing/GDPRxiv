FIRST.- Vodafone has not infringed Article 48.1(b) of the LGT nor Article 21 of the GDPR in relation to the complaint filed by Mr. B.B.B.  
I. The infringement attributed by the Agency in the Initiation Agreement  
The sanction that the Agency intends to impose on Vodafone arises from the alleged infringement of Article 48.1(b) of the LGT, which is typified in Article 21 of the GDPR, as well as in Article 23.4 of the LOPDGDD and, as a minor infringement, in Article 78.1 of the LGT.  
The Agency understands that, by indicating that EXPANDIA was the owner of the number from which the call subject to Mr. B.B.B.'s complaint was made, ***PHONE.4, and by confirming that they made that call as a sub-contractor of Optime, representative of LOWI, and that EXPANDIA, with the apparent authorization of LOWI, had made a call for commercial purposes in its name to the complainant's number, ***PHONE.1, despite the fact that he had opposed receiving such calls.  
II. Vodafone has not violated the right of opposition exercised by the complainant to receive commercial communications.  
EXPANDIA, in its allegations submitted to the AEPD, states that it made the call on September 6, 2022, and provides the Collaboration Agreement between EXPANDIA and Optime, which indicates that Optime is a company dedicated to the marketing of LOWI products and services. However, EXPANDIA was not authorized to make commercial calls on behalf of Vodafone, as stated in the Agency Agreement signed between Vodafone and Optime, which was not requested from my client. In this regard, it is relevant to highlight that, as of the date of the call, Vodafone and Optime had a valid contract regulating their relationship, which expressly prohibits, under no circumstances, the possibility of conducting telemarketing or tele-scheduling activities by Optime or its collaborators.  
Below, a screenshot of the referred clause is provided, with the text underlined to highlight the mentioned obligation.  
Optime, not being authorized to carry out tele-scheduling activities, is not part of the call routing system that ensures the filtering of the complainant's number against the official advertising exclusion files and Vodafone's internal files. As a result of the situation presented, Vodafone could not know that the number ***PHONE.4 was being used by Optime to conduct commercial campaigns on behalf of Vodafone, as it is not an authorized number nor is it enabled to carry out any type of telephone activity, as stated in the contract, so that activity cannot be considered to have been carried out on behalf of Vodafone.

SECOND.- EXPANDIA violated the guidelines set by Vodafone by engaging in telemarketing activities. By acting on their own behalf, determining the purposes and means for making the call, EXPANDIA becomes responsible for the processing. Optime has not worked with EXPANDIA due to malpractice since October 2022.  
As detailed in the first statement of the present allegations, EXPANDIA, by making the call to the Claimant's number, violated Vodafone's guidelines by being unable to engage in telemarketing on behalf of LOWI under any circumstances. That is to say, EXPANDIA independently determined the purposes and means for carrying out that call, relying on a telemarketing or tele-scheduling activity that was not authorized by Vodafone, thus becoming responsible for the processing of the call and violating data protection regulations by infringing the right not to receive commercial communications.  
This is stipulated in Article 28.10 of the GDPR: "Without prejudice to the provisions of Articles 82, 83, and 84, if a processor infringes this Regulation by determining the purposes and means of the processing, they shall be considered responsible for the processing in relation to that processing."  
Consequently, Vodafone did not violate the provisions of Article 48.1b) of the LGT, nor Article 21 of the GDPR, nor Article 23.4 of the LOPDGDD, and cannot be held accountable for the lack of diligence of EXPANDIA, which acted without following the stipulations of the contract signed with my client.

According to the provisions of Article 114.1.b) of the LGTEL and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to resolve this procedure.
