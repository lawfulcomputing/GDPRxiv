From the actions carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On March 6, 2023, a complaint was filed with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against ECOSCOOTING DELIVERY, S.L. with NIF B65874893 (hereinafter, the complained party). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The complainant states that they were the recipient of two packages consolidated into one, the delivery management of which was the responsibility of the complained party, and indicates that they were notified on March 3, 2023. They assert that it was delivered to a concierge of their Community of Owners, although there is no concierge in their building, so they state that they asked neighbors in the building, none of whom are recorded as having received the package, leading them to understand that it was delivered to a third party, with their personal data without their consent, exposing their personal data inappropriately.  
The complainant states that they have been subject to a violation of personal data —including phone number, address, and email— as well as the loss of purchased products, as a result of inadequate service provided by the complained party as a transport company.  
They indicate that they were waiting for the receipt of two unified orders that were to be delivered by the mentioned company and that they received a delivery notification to their email, indicating that the packages had been delivered to a "concierge of the building." They indicate that this statement immediately raised doubts, given that there is no concierge staff in the building in question.  
They assert that, upon arriving home and checking the mailboxes, they did not find the packages, so they proceeded to ask the neighbors in the building, who confirmed that they had not received said products. However, a neighbor from their apartment reported that a delivery person attempted to make a delivery, but ultimately hung up the intercom, leaving the delivery incomplete and without providing further information about the whereabouts of the packages.  
The complainant maintains that this situation has generated two main issues:

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the functions conferred to each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR) and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve these investigative proceedings.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  

II  
Preliminary Issues  
In the present case, according to the provisions of Article 4.1 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, since the respondent party carries out, among other processes, the collection and use of the following personal data of individuals, such as: name and surname, postal address, and phone number.  
Likewise, the respondent party carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR: “data controller” or “controller”: the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing.  

III  
Principles Relating to Processing  
Letter f) of Article 5.1 of the GDPR advocates:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
(...)  
f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, by applying appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).”  
The aforementioned principle of confidentiality entails that personal data must be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing, loss, destruction, or accidental damage, by applying appropriate technical and organizational measures.  
For its part, the breach of this principle of confidentiality occurs when personal data are exposed, processed, or accessed in an unauthorized manner, due to inadequacy or lack of technical or organizational measures of any kind. Consequently, for a breach of this principle to exist, it must be established that there has indeed been an unauthorized exposure, processing, or transfer of personal data. Therefore, mere suspicion is not sufficient; there must be concrete evidence that the data were unlawfully processed. Conversely, if it cannot be proven that the personal data have been unlawfully processed, it cannot be concluded that there has been a breach of the principle of confidentiality.  
In the present case, it has not been established that the package was delivered to any third party or unauthorized person. The respondent party provided a delivery receipt at the claimant's address, in which the claimant is listed as the recipient, although the signature or ID number of the “received” does not match theirs. Consequently, the delivery receipt does not establish that the delivery was made to a third party.  
According to the respondent party, in this case, the delivery person did not act diligently and marked the package as delivered when it was still in storage. Therefore, it asserts that the data were never provided to third parties.  
While this fact is not proven, neither is the contrary; that is, that an unauthorized third party received the package and, consequently, had access to the personal data of the claimant.

In this regard, in order to initiate a sanctioning procedure, there must be indications or evidence that motivate it, taking into account the principle of the presumption of innocence that governs the administrative sanctioning procedure. Therefore, if such evidence does not exist, as is the case here, it is not possible to initiate a sanctioning procedure.  
Administrative Sanctioning Law, due to its specificity, applies, with some nuances but without exceptions, the guiding principles of criminal law, resulting in the clear full applicability of the principle of the presumption of innocence. Not in vain, Article 53.2 b) of the LPACAP recognizes the right of presumed responsible parties to "the presumption of non-existence of administrative responsibility until proven otherwise."  
Well, the Constitutional Court, in Judgment 76/1990, considers that the right to the presumption of innocence implies that "any insufficiency in the result of the evidence presented, freely assessed by the sanctioning body, must result in an acquittal."  
In short, the application of the principle of the presumption of innocence prevents attributing an administrative infringement when there has not been obtained and verified the existence of incriminating evidence supporting the facts that motivate this attribution, as is the case here.  
Regarding the compensation request made by the claiming party, it is appropriate to indicate that this authority does not have the competence to determine the existence of such compensation nor, if applicable, the amount or scope of it.  
It is true that Article 82.1 of the GDPR grants individuals who have suffered damages as a result of an infringement of the aforementioned Regulation the right to receive compensation for such damages from the responsible party or the processor:  
"Any person who has suffered material or immaterial damages as a result of an infringement of this Regulation shall have the right to receive compensation from the responsible party or the processor for the damages suffered."  
However, this right must be exercised, if applicable, by filing a judicial action before the competent judicial bodies of the corresponding jurisdiction, which will depend, among other aspects, on the nature of the responsible party or processor that has been responsible for such infringement. In this regard, the sixth paragraph of the aforementioned Article 82 and the second paragraph of Article 79 of the GDPR state:
