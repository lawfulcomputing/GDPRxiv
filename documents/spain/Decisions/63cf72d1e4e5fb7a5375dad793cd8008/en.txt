RESOLUTION FOR ARCHIVING OF PROCEEDINGS  
From the proceedings carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: The Spanish Agency for Data Protection became aware through a complaint filed with registration number REGAGE23e00036326867 and REGAGE23e00043167280, that the Autonomous Community of the Region of Murcia, through the Ministry of Education, Vocational Training, and Employment, has signed a Collaboration Agreement with a commercial company for the use and dissemination of the platform www.mihuelladigital.es, with the aim of informing and raising awareness about digital privacy and data protection, in order to combat and prevent situations of cyberbullying in publicly funded centers.  
The aforementioned Agreement contemplates the processing of different types of data and various interested parties by the company in charge, without providing information on how and under what conditions access and subsequent processing of the personal data of students, especially minors, will occur.  
According to the reported facts, in accordance with the cited Agreement, the commercial company in charge of processing will handle the following types of personal data: name and surname, ID number, email address, family book, images for facial recognition service, user accounts, and internet profiles, as well as "other documentation" not detailed, corresponding to parents, students, and the management team of the educational centers. However, the Agreement does not indicate how the commercial company will be allowed to access the personal data of students, nor what the subsequent processing will be.  
In the Register of Processing Activities of the educational centers and on the general website of the Ministry, there is no record of any processing that could frame this activity, nor, in particular, any activity that contemplates the processing of biometric personal data of students, also lacking references to the security measures applicable by the Ministry to protect the fundamental right to data protection of the students of the schools that will be part of this initiative.  

SECOND: Considering the relevance of the information, it was necessary for this Agency to gain an in-depth understanding of the described facts and the processing carried out in order to determine the consequences that could arise for the rights and freedoms of individuals.  
The need to assess the implications regarding personal data protection required the opening of an investigation to verify these aspects and, if applicable, the proper observance of the regulations concerning certain facts that could violate data protection legislation.

III  
Record of Processing Activities  
Article 30 of the GDPR, concerning the record of processing activities, states:  
"1. Each controller and, where applicable, their representative, shall maintain a record of processing activities carried out under their responsibility. This record shall contain all the information indicated below:  
a) the name and contact details of the controller and, where applicable, the joint controller, the representative of the controller, and the data protection officer;  
b) the purposes of the processing;  
c) a description of the categories of data subjects and the categories of personal data;  
d) the categories of recipients to whom the personal data have been or will be disclosed, including recipients in third countries or international organizations;  
e) where applicable, transfers of personal data to a third country or an international organization, including the identification of that third country or international organization and, in the case of transfers referred to in Article 49, paragraph 1, second sentence, the documentation of adequate safeguards;  
f) where possible, the envisaged time limits for erasure of the different categories of data;  
g) where possible, a general description of the technical and organizational measures referred to in Article 32, paragraph 1."

4. The supervisory authority shall establish and publish a list of the types of processing operations that require a data protection impact assessment in accordance with paragraph 1. The supervisory authority shall communicate these lists to the Committee referred to in Article 68.  
5. The supervisory authority may also establish and publish a list of the types of processing that do not require data protection impact assessments. The supervisory authority shall communicate these lists to the Committee.  
6. Before adopting the lists referred to in paragraphs 4 and 5, the competent supervisory authority shall apply the consistency mechanism provided for in Article 63 if those lists include processing activities related to the offering of goods or services to data subjects or the monitoring of their behavior in several Member States, or processing activities that may substantially affect the free movement of personal data within the Union.  
7. The assessment shall include at least:  
a) a systematic description of the envisaged processing operations and the purposes of the processing, including, where appropriate, the legitimate interest pursued by the data controller;  
b) an evaluation of the necessity and proportionality of the processing operations in relation to their purpose;  
c) an assessment of the risks to the rights and freedoms of data subjects referred to in paragraph 1, and  
d) the measures envisaged to address the risks, including safeguards, security measures, and mechanisms that ensure the protection of personal data, and to demonstrate compliance with this Regulation, taking into account the rights and legitimate interests of the data subjects and other affected persons.

9. Where appropriate, the controller shall seek the opinion of the data subjects or their representatives regarding the envisaged processing, without prejudice to the protection of public or commercial interests or the security of the processing operations.

Conclusion  
As stated in the Report issued by the Data Protection Delegate for the Educational Centers of the Ministry of Education, Vocational Training and Employment of the Region of Murcia, dated June 28, 2023:  
“The General Directorate of Attention to Diversity, Innovation, and Vocational Training did not provide, before the start of the procedure, the activity record of processing, which is mandatory when there is data processing, for which the Ministry of Education, Vocational Training and Employment could be charged with a violation of Article 30 of the GDPR, included within the typology framed in Article 83.4(a) of the GDPR. Violation classified as a serious offense according to Article 73(n) of the LOPDGDD.”  
“It is noted that the mandatory DPIA was not provided before the start of the procedure when the processing involves the use of biometric data, for which the Ministry of Education, Vocational Training and Employment could be charged with a violation of Article 35 of the GDPR, included within the typology framed in Article 83.4(a) of the GDPR. Violation classified as a serious offense according to Article 73(t) of the LOPDGDD.”  
“The processing of biometric data under Article 9.2(g) requires that it be provided for in a European or national law, and in the latter case, such law must, according to the cited constitutional doctrine and as provided in Article 9.2 of the LOPDGDD, have the rank of law. This law must also specify the essential public interest that justifies the restriction of the right to personal data protection and under what circumstances it may be limited, establishing the precise rules that make the imposition of such limitation and its consequences foreseeable for the data subject, without the generic invocation of a public interest being sufficient for these purposes.”  
“(...) considering what has been raised in this case, it is deemed insufficient to allow the use of facial recognition techniques for the purpose of constructing the digital fingerprint of students, as it does not meet the previously mentioned requirements.”

It is necessary for a regulation with the rank of law to be approved that specifically justifies to what extent and under what circumstances the identification of students through the use of biometrics would respond to an essential public interest.  
Therefore, there is currently no legal norm, as indicated, that enables such processing of biometric data under Article 9.2.g) of the GDPR, since neither Article 2 nor Article 111 bis of Organic Law 2/2006, of May 3, on Education, cited, meet the requirements established legally and jurisprudentially.  
“(...) we consider that the possibility of obtaining free consent from students that would allow the use of facial recognition techniques for the processing of their biometric data for the use of the “My Digital Fingerprint” platform would require that they be offered the possibility of using said platform in a comparable situation where the processing of their facial recognition was not necessary, that is, by offering other alternatives that do not require the processing of their biometric data; otherwise, the consent could not be considered freely given.  
It is therefore the responsibility of the Ministry of Education, Vocational Training, and Employment, by virtue of its competencies in the field of education and as the entity responsible for the intended processing, to determine whether in the implementation of the agreement, therefore, of the “My Digital Fingerprint” platform, equality between students who consent to the processing of their biometric data and those who do not is ensured. Only in this way could the consent legitimize such processing.”  
However, the investigative actions carried out have confirmed that the 2022-2023 school year has concluded and the project referred to in the agreement has not been executed, as the agreement was deemed extinguished without coming into effect.  
Therefore, based on the information provided in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found that substantiates the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
All of this is without prejudice to any possible subsequent actions that this Agency may carry out, applying the investigative and corrective powers it holds.
