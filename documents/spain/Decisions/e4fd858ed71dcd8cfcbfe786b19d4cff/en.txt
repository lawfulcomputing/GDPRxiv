BACKGROUND  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on August 18, 2023. The complaint is directed against AYUNTAMIENTO DE JEREZ DE LOS CABALLEROS with NIF P0607000G (hereinafter, the complained party or the City Council).  
The reasons on which the complaint is based are as follows:  
On 03/08/22, the complainant submitted a complaint to the City Council of Jerez de los Caballeros for an alleged infringement of the GDPR and the LOPDGDD. Their full name and two surnames appeared, along with their Tax Identification Number (NIF), more than 20 times in the minutes of the Plenary session held on 03/08/21, having submitted allegations to various public exposure files. The City Council of Jerez de los Caballeros, like any other public entity, is obliged to comply with Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data -GDPR-, and with Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights -LOPDGDD- concerning the processing of personal data they carry out, understanding personal data as “any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.”  
An identifiable natural person is considered to be one whose identity can be determined, directly or indirectly, in particular by means of an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or several specific elements of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that person.  
Furthermore, “processing” must be understood as “any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of making available, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.”  
The City Council of Jerez de los Caballeros would have processed the personal data of the claimant, specifically, the name and surname and the Tax Identification Number (NIF).  
The City Council carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the entity that determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR:  
“‘data controller’ or ‘controller’: the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be provided for by the law of the Union or of the Member States.”

For its part, the Second Additional Provision of the LOPDGDD, relating to "Data protection and transparency and access to public information," establishes that active publicity and access to public information regulated by Title I of Law 19/2013, of December 9, on transparency, access to public information, and good governance, as well as the obligations of active publicity established by regional legislation, shall be subject, when the information contains personal data, to the provisions of articles 5.3 and 15 of Law 19/2013, in Regulation (EU) 2016/679, and in this organic law.  
Therefore, the public interest in access to information is not unlimited, finding a limit precisely in the guarantee of the rights of the interested parties whose personal data may be affected.  
The LTAIBG itself establishes the system for the protection of personal data, stating the following in its article 15:  
“1. If the requested information contains personal data revealing ideology, union affiliation, religion, or beliefs, access may only be authorized if there is express and written consent from the affected party, unless the affected party has made the data publicly available prior to the request for access. If the information includes personal data referring to racial origin, health, or sexual life, includes genetic or biometric data, or contains data relating to the commission of criminal or administrative offenses that do not involve public reprimand of the offender, access may only be authorized if there is express consent from the affected party or if it is covered by a norm with the force of law.  
2. 3. When the requested information does not contain specially protected data, the body to which the request is directed will grant access after a sufficiently reasoned assessment of the public interest in disclosing the information and the rights of the affected parties whose data appears in the requested information, particularly their fundamental right to the protection of personal data. The process of applying these regulations comprises the following stages or successive phases:  
First. Assess whether the requested information or information subject to active publicity contains personal data or not (understood as defined in Article 4.1 of the GDPR).  
Second. If affirmative, assess whether the data reveals ideology, union affiliation, religion, or beliefs (in which case access may only be authorized if there is express and written consent from the affected party, unless the affected party has publicly disclosed the data prior to the request for access) or refers to racial origin, health, and sexual life, includes genetic or biometric data, or contains data related to the commission of criminal or administrative offenses that do not involve public reprimand of the offender (in which case access may only be authorized if there is express consent from the affected party or if it is protected by a law).  
Third. If the personal data contained in the information are not data mentioned in the previous section, assess whether they are exclusively merely identifying data related to the organization, functioning, or public activity of the corresponding body or entity. If affirmative, the information will be published or made available in general, unless in the specific case the protection of personal data and other constitutionally protected rights prevails over the public interest in disclosure.  
Fourth. If the personal data are not merely identifying and related to the organization, functioning, or public activity of the body or are not exclusively so, carry out the balancing required by Article 15.3 of the Law.
