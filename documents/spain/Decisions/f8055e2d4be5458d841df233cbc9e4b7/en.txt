LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
The Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve the present recourse in accordance with the provisions of Article 123 of the LPACAP and Article 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD).  

II  
Response to the allegations presented

Regarding the insolvency situation alleged by the appellant, it should be noted that:  
"According to the doctrine of the European Court of Human Rights ("ECHR"), the principle of proportionality in the sanctioning field is also incorporated in Article 1 of the First Additional Protocol to the Convention for the Protection of Human Rights and Fundamental Freedoms ("ECHR"), relating to the protection of property. Thus, the ECHR has considered, in its judgments of July 9, 2009 (Case Moon v. France), February 26, 2009 (Case Grifhorst v. France), January 11, 2007 (Case Mamidakis v. Greece), October 9, 2018 (Case Gyrlyan v. Russia), and January 31, 2017 (Case Boljevic v. Croatia), that in the sanctioning field, respect for the principle of proportionality recognized in Article 1 of the Protocol requires assessing the breach committed, the severity of the offense, and the fair balance between the demands of the general interest and the protection of fundamental rights. Likewise, it considers that there must be a reasonable proportionality relationship between the means employed and the ends pursued (ECHR judgment of May 14, 2013 - Case N.KM v. Hungary, paragraph 65). The ECHR also considers that, in the sanctioning field, the principle of proportionality requires, on the one hand, that the imposed sanction reflects the severity of the infringement and, on the other, that when determining the sanction and setting the amount of the fine, the individual circumstances of the specific case are taken into account (in particular, ECHR judgment of July 9, 2009 - Moon v. France, paragraph 51).  
For these purposes, this party understands that the criteria emanating from the ECHR should serve as an interpretative element regarding the principle of proportionality in sanctioning matters, in accordance with the provisions of Article 10.2 of the Spanish Constitution."  
However, in the present case, this Agency wishes to point out that there is no evidence nor has there ever been evidence that the insolvency circumstances alleged by the appellant are true. Thus, considering the circumstances listed in Article 83.2 of the GDPR and 76.2 of the LOPDGDD, it is deemed proportional to impose an administrative fine of €10,000, without the new assertion made by the appellant undermining this decision as it is not supported by any evidence.  
Therefore, the present allegations are dismissed.  
III  
Conclusion  
Article 118.1 of the LPACAP establishes that: "When new facts or documents not included in the original file must be taken into account, they will be made available to the interested parties so that, within a period of no less than ten days and no more than fifteen, they may formulate allegations and present the documents and justifications they deem appropriate.

Facts, documents, or allegations from the appellant will not be taken into account in the resolution of the appeals when they could have been submitted during the allegations phase but were not. The practice of evidence cannot be requested when its lack of execution in the procedure in which the contested resolution was issued is attributable to the interested party."  
In the procedure PS/00177/2023, whose resolution is contested in this appeal, the appellant only made allegations regarding the proposed resolution. In these, they expressed the need to publish on their social media the documents containing personal data of the complainant in order to justify their complaint of racism against them, considering this Agency as "complicit" in the racist behavior of the complainant.  
In the aforementioned resolution, this Agency responded to these statements indicating that "at no time has the principle of impartiality established in our legal system been violated in the present sanctioning procedure; since the sanctioning power exercised has been limited to objectively analyzing the facts reported by the complainant, as well as the evidence provided for that purpose.  
Thus, as indicated in the Established Facts, it has been confirmed that the respondent published —and continues to publish— on their Facebook social media profile (A.A.A.) documents containing identifying and health-related personal data of the complainant without respecting what is established in Articles 9 and 6.1 of the GDPR. And not only that, but also the personal data of the image."  
Furthermore, this Agency reiterates what was stated in the contested resolution regarding the measures, specifically, the obligation of the appellant to remove the publication subject to the complaint from the respondent's Facebook social media profile (A.A.A.).  
Therefore, from the above, it is evident that, in the present appeal for reconsideration, the appellant has not provided new facts that would allow for a reconsideration of the validity of the contested resolution.  
IV  
Late Resolution  
Due to reasons related to the functioning of the administrative body, therefore not attributable to the appellant, as of this date, the mandatory pronouncement of this Agency regarding the present appeal has not been issued.  
According to the provisions of Article 24 of the LPACAP, the effect of administrative silence in the procedures for challenging acts and provisions is dismissive.  
Nevertheless, and despite the time elapsed, the Administration is obliged to issue an express resolution and notify it in all procedures regardless of their initiation form, as provided in Article 21.1 of the aforementioned LPACAP.
