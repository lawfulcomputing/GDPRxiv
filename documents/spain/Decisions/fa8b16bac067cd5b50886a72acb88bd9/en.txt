LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence and procedure  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Preliminary issues  
The name, surname, and phone number of individuals are considered personal data, the processing of which is subject to the regime provided for in the GDPR, as well as its implementing provisions, in accordance with the provisions of Article 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, which states the following:  
"Article 4 Definitions  
For the purposes of this Regulation, the following shall be understood as:  
1) 'personal data': any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('the data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific elements of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person;  
2) 'processing': any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use,..."

Although it is a disputed fact the source from which access to such personal data was obtained (through the SALAMANCA TARJETA ACTIVA system, as stated by the complainant, or because the complainant provided the data directly to the establishment, as indicated by the responding party), as well as the purpose and content of such messages (personal content according to the complainant, and professional content according to the responding party), it is certain that the sending of these two messages to the complainant involved the execution of two operations of processing the complainant's phone number (and therefore, their name and surname), which fit the definition of personal data processing contained in Article 4.2 of the GDPR: collection/extraction to obtain the phone number, as well as its use to send the messages.  
The execution of the operations of personal data processing referred to is subject to compliance with certain requirements established in data protection regulations, the breach of which could result in an administrative infringement classified under the GDPR, the sanctioning power of which is the responsibility of this Agency. Therefore, another preliminary issue to determine is who would be considered responsible for the processing, and thus, the presumed subject of administrative liability arising in case of non-compliance, in accordance with the provisions of Article 70.1 a) of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the protection of personal data and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPPDD):  
“Article 70. Responsible parties.  
1.  
Those subject to the sanctioning regime established in Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and in this organic law are: a) The controllers of the processing. b) The processors of the processing. c) The representatives of the controllers or processors not established in the territory of the European Union. d) The certification entities. e) The accredited entities supervising the codes of conduct. (…)”  
In this case, it is considered that the responsible party for carrying out the aforementioned processing operations was the employee of the fruit shop and (...) of B.B.B., against whom this procedure should be directed, since they were the one who determined the purposes and means of the processing by accessing the data and sending the messages to the complainant. All of this, in accordance with the provisions of Article 4.7 of the GDPR, which states the following:  
7) “'data controller' or 'controller': the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; if the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the controller or the specific criteria for their appointment may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.”

The line of this number was not registered in the name of the fruit shop, nor in the name of its owner, as would have been expected if the messages had been sent in the name or on behalf of the establishment.  
- The claimant also states that the profile picture of the WhatsApp account from which the messages were sent corresponded to that of “B.B.B.”, which only confirms the previous evidence, which by itself is already decisive.  
- It has been confirmed that B.B.B. is an employee of the fruit shop and (...) of its owner, C.C.C., who has stated this in his two responses to the requests made to the establishment during the investigation phase, providing the employment contract signed with him. - The content of the messages themselves does not imply any professional purpose that forms part of the commercial activity of the fruit shop, as neither the word “order” appears nor is there any indication that it is prepared or pending collection, with its content being clearly personal, from which an apparent intention to contact the claimant for personal purposes and not for contractual purposes is deduced, addressing her in a personal tone without any reference to her order.  
In conclusion, from this evidence, it is deduced that when B.B.B. accessed the claimant's phone number and sent her the messages referred to in this file, he was not acting as an employee of the fruit shop in the performance of his duties, nor in compliance with the instructions given by the owner of the fruit shop regarding the possible vegan orders that were pending collection as he states, so he must be considered personally responsible for the processing referred to in this file.  
Consequently, it is established that B.B.B. would have incurred a breach of personal data protection regulations by processing the claimant's data, without a basis for legitimacy, which allows for administrative liability to be demanded from him for the alleged breaches committed, in accordance with the provisions of Article 70.1 a) of LOPPDD.
