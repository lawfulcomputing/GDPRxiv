RESOLUTION OF FILE OF ACTIONS  
From the actions carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following:  

FACTS  
FIRST: On September 21, 2021, a complaint was filed with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against BESTIAL EVENTIS, S.L. with NIF B35551977 (hereinafter, the complained party).  
The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
It is stated in the complaint that the entity BESTIAL EVENTIS organizes one of the most important obstacle race events in the Canary Islands, called BESTIALRACE, with the participation of hundreds of people, and that on the registration page it requests information on whether the participant has a COVID-19 Vaccination Certificate and, if not, requires the participant to present a PCR or Antigen test in order to participate in the race, understanding that such requests are contrary to data protection regulations. A screenshot is provided showing the question posed to the runners who wish to participate.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the complaint was forwarded to the complained party/ALIAS, for them to analyze it and inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
The forwarding, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was acknowledged on October 21, 2021, as stated in the certificate in the file.  
On November 17, 2021, this Agency received a response letter indicating the following:  
“The legal basis for the processing is CONSENT, based on Article 9.2.i of the DATA PROTECTION REGULATION, which establishes:  
2. The provision in paragraph 1 shall not apply when one of the following circumstances occurs:  
i) the processing is necessary for reasons of public interest in the field of public health, such as the protection against serious cross-border threats to health, or to ensure high standards of quality and safety of health care and of medicines or medical products, based on Union or Member State law that establishes appropriate and specific measures to protect the rights and freedoms of the data subject, in particular professional secrecy.  
The purpose is the registration for the race, specifically in this section, to ensure public health.  
The registration forms, as well as the information they contain, are only processed by Bestial Events, whose only employee is me, A.A.A., so I am the only one who has access to it.  
Regarding the complaint, the format in the form was changed, and it is established in a first layer “you must present a vaccination certificate or PCR/antigens” and a tab is opened that states “I have been informed of the above” with a selection box.  
To provide context, since there was widespread confusion and a tangled regulatory framework that changed day by day, we will explain the chronology:  
On August 1, 2021, registration for the race opened, with Gran Canaria on the brink of PHASE 4, declared by the Government of the Canary Islands, which is the maximum alert that could be declared. At that time, a vaccination certificate was required to enter the islands and, even, attempts were made to establish this requirement for entry into bars and restaurants. For the planning of the race, a COVID protocol was developed appropriate to the circumstances we are experiencing, that is, the pandemic situation.  
The registration, as has been customary, was managed with the TRACKING SPORT platform. To collect the data, the minimum necessary data was assessed. It should be emphasized that the vaccination section is merely informative, that is, it is only to verify that you know you must present the vaccination certificate and that, if you do not have it, you would need to present a PCR or antigen test 72 hours in advance.  
On September 14, 2021, the Supreme Court, Administrative Litigation Chamber, Section Four, in its ruling 1.112/2021, which is attached under document number 3, establishes the appropriateness of the adopted measure, not perceiving any limitation when the mere exhibition of the document is established, that is, showing or displaying the documentation. Certainly, we do not create a file nor do we process that data.  
When a serious and imminent danger to people's lives and the protection of public health arises, it is logical to believe that it is in our hands to do everything possible to try to prevent the spread of the virus, always attending to the

The application of the aforementioned measure was suspended by the High Court of Justice of the Canary Islands and ultimately became ineffective by virtue of the Government Agreement of September 2, 2021, which nullified the Government Agreement of June 19, 2020, which established the preventive measures to address the health crisis caused by COVID-19, once Phase III of the plan for the transition to a new normality was surpassed, following the end of the validity of the measures specific to the state of alarm and its successive updates, published by Resolution of the General Secretariat of the Presidency of the Government on September 6, 2021 (BOC No. 183, of 06.09.2021).

Conclusion  
Article 27 of Law 40/2015, of October 1, on the Legal Regime of the Public Sector, enshrines the principle of legality in the field of the sanctioning power of the Administration, as it prescribes that only violations of the legal order provided as such by a law constitute administrative infractions.  
According to the doctrine of the Constitutional Court, the principles inspiring criminal law apply, with certain nuances, to administrative sanctioning law, given that both are manifestations of the punitive order of the State, as reflected in the Constitution itself in its article 25, which imposes the necessity of the normative predetermination of infringing behaviors and the corresponding sanctions, allowing for the prediction with a sufficient degree of certainty (lex certa) of those behaviors and knowing what to expect regarding the associated liability and the eventual sanction, with a precise correlation between.

the unlawful act and the action attributed, through a correct and adequate subsumption of the proven facts into the applicable sanctioning type.  
In accordance with the principles of sanctioning administrative law, it is necessary for a series of elements to be present in order to understand that an administrative infringement has occurred. First, for administrative liability to exist, it is necessary that the person being accused has carried out an action or omission. If this conduct, whether active or omitted, is regulated in the administrative norms as an infringement, then it will be granted such nature. Furthermore, the principle of responsibility requires that the alleged responsible party has carried out the conduct in a culpable or fraudulent manner, with the first case being one in which the actions are conscious and voluntary, and the culpable being those that are involuntary but result in a breach of the norm. Another essential element of administrative infringements is the effect of time, as only those behaviors that are typified as infringements at the time of their commission can be punished.  
In the present case analyzed, even though the identity of the responsible party and the supposedly unlawful act has been established, there is no evidence that the latter has occurred affecting the claimant, as no treatment of their personal data has been carried out.  
Therefore, based on what has been indicated in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found that supports the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, as it has not been confirmed that any data processing of the claimant has taken place in any of the areas affected by the measures set forth in the Resolution of July 23, 2021, which provides for the publication of the Agreement approving the update of the preventive measures established by the Government Agreement of June 19, 2020, to address the health crisis caused by COVID-19, once Phase III of the Plan for the transition to a new normality has been completed, ending the validity of the measures specific to the state of alarm.
