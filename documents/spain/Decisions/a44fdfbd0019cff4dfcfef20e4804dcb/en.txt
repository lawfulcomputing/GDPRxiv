SANCTIONING PROCEDURE RESOLUTION  
From the procedure conducted by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST: On 03/07/2023, a complaint was filed with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection regarding a possible infringement attributable to the entity AGENCIA ESTATAL DE METEOROLOGIA, with NIF Q2801668A (hereinafter, the complained party or AEMET).  
The facts brought to the attention of this authority:  
On 09/03/2023, the complainant sent an email to the data protection officer of the complained party to verify whether the documentation requested by the General Affairs and Internal Regime Service to apply for the parking card complies with the provisions of data protection regulations.  
According to the complainant, the officer issued a report concluding that there is no justification supporting the request for documents made, considering the purpose pursued.  
The complainant adds that, on 22/06/2023, they requested a vehicle change on their parking card and were again asked for documentation without an express resolution and without justifying the purposes of data processing or the minimization of documentation.  
Along with the complaint, the complainant provides the form that must be submitted to request the parking card, through which personal data of the applicant is collected, including ID number, phone/extension, first name, last name, administrative unit, license plate, and make/model of the vehicle. According to this form, the applicant must attach the following documentation: a photocopy of the valid driver's license, a photocopy of the vehicle's registration certificate, a photocopy of the Family Book or original authorization if the vehicle is not registered in the applicant's name.  
Additionally, the following documentation is included:  
- A copy of the email dated 09/03/2023, mentioned in the complaint, sent by the complainant to the address “dpd@aemet.es”, in which they inquire whether collecting the aforementioned documentation complies with the principles of data minimization and purpose limitation.  
- An email dated 30/06/2023, addressed to the complainant from the address “buzónasuntosgenerales@aemet.es”, which includes the following text: “Request received to modify your parking card at the Central Headquarters, this service, in order to initiate the necessary procedures, in accordance with the established procedure, informs you that you must provide the following documentation for the new vehicle license plate…:  
  - authorization document for its use from the vehicle owner, if it is not in your name  
  - documentation of the vehicle's driving license.”  
- A copy of two emails, both dated 03/07/2023, sent by the complainant to the address “dpd@aemet.es”, in which they again raise the relevance of collecting those documents and request an express response to their inquiry.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Preliminary Issues  
Article 4.1) of the GDPR defines "personal data" as: "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('the data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person."

indirectly, in particular by means of an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or several elements specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that person.”  
Article 4.2) of the GDPR defines “processing” as: “any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure or destruction.”  
Article 4.7) of the GDPR defines the “data controller” or “controller” as: “the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its appointment may be provided for by the law of the Union or of the Member States.” In turn, Article 4.8) of the GDPR defines the “data processor” or “processor” as the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that processes personal data on behalf of the data controller.  
In the present case, according to the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, since AEMET carries out, among other processing activities, the collection and storage of personal data of natural persons related to ID number, phone/extension, name, surname, administrative unit, registration, and make/model of the vehicle; as well as those contained in the following documents: valid driving license, vehicle registration certificate, and Family Book.  
AEMET carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the entity that determines the purposes and means of such activity, pursuant to Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
III  
Unfulfilled obligation: infringement of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR  
In the present case, it is evident that the entity AEMET, for the issuance of a parking card to its employees at the Central Headquarters, in addition to the completion of the application form provided for this purpose, requires interested parties to provide a photocopy of the valid driving license, a photocopy of the vehicle or vehicles’ registration certificate, a photocopy of the Family Book, or original authorization if the vehicle is not registered in the name of the applicant.  
The use of the personal data contained in these documents has not been justified by the entity AEMET. Considering the intended purpose and the use to which they are destined, it is concluded that such personal data are not necessary, adequate, or relevant, so that their processing constitutes an infringement of the provisions of Article 5.1.c) (Principles relating to processing) of the GDPR, which establishes the following:

“1. Personal data shall be:

c) adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (“data minimization”).”  
Article 5 of the GDPR refers to the general principles for data processing. In its paragraph c), it refers to the principle of data minimization, indicating that data must be “adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed.”  
If we examine the definition, we can deduce that only the personal data that will be processed can be collected, that is, those that are strictly necessary for the processing; that they may only be used for the purpose that motivated their collection, and not for any other objective.  
It is worth mentioning the considerations regarding the principle of data minimization made by the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) in Guidelines 4/2019, “on Article 25, Data Protection by Design and by Default,” version 2.0, dated 20/10/2020, adopted in compliance with the function entrusted to it by Article 70 of the GDPR to ensure its consistent application.  
In paragraph 74 of these guidelines, it is established that:  
“Firstly, data controllers must determine whether they even need to process personal data for their relevant purposes. The controller must verify whether the relevant purposes can be achieved by processing less personal data, or by using less detailed or aggregated personal data, or without having to process personal data at all. This verification must take place before any processing, but it can also be carried out at any time during the processing cycle.” (Paragraph 74).  
And in paragraph 76, these Guidelines 4/2019 indicate that “Essential elements by design and by default regarding data minimization” include, among others, the following:  
“. Data avoidance: All processing of personal data will be avoided when possible to fulfill the relevant purpose.  
. Limitation: The amount of personal data collected will be limited to what is strictly necessary for the intended purpose.  
. Access limitation: Data processing will be configured in such a way as to minimize the number of people who need to access personal data to perform their functions, and access will be limited accordingly.  
. Relevance: Personal data must be relevant to the processing in question, and the data controller must be able to demonstrate this relevance.  
. Necessity: Each category of personal data will be necessary for the specified purposes and will only be processed if it is not possible to achieve the purpose by other means.”

IV  
Classification and qualification of the infringement  
The infringement attributed to the respondent is classified under Article 83.5 a) of the GDPR, which considers that the infringement of “the basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9” is subject to sanction.  
The LOPDGDD in its Article 71, Infringements, states that: “Acts and behaviors referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those that are contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements.”  
And in its Article 72, it considers for the purposes of prescription that: “Infringements considered very serious:  
1. According to what is established in Article 83.5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, those infringements that constitute a substantial violation of the articles mentioned therein are considered very serious and will prescribe in three years, particularly the following:  
(...)  
a) The processing of personal data violating the principles and guarantees established in Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.  
(...)”

VI  
Classification and qualification of the infringement of Article 13 of the GDPR  
The accused party has failed to comply with the stipulations of Article 13 of the GDPR.  
Article 83.5(b) provides that “the infringements of the following provisions shall be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount: a) (…)  
b) the rights of the interested parties under Articles 12 to 22;” (…)  
For the purposes of the limitation period for infringements, Article 72.1(h) of the LOPDGDD classifies as very serious “The omission of the duty to inform the affected party about the processing of their personal data in accordance with the provisions of Articles 13 and 14 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and 12 of this Organic Law.”  
The limitation period for very serious infringements provided for in the LOPDGDD is three years.

VIII  
Corrective Measures  
Once the violations are confirmed, the aforementioned article 77 of the LOPDGDD provides that the resolution issued may establish the measures that the infringing entity must adopt to cease the infringing conduct, correct the effects of the violation that has been committed, and carry out the necessary adjustments, in this case, to the requirements set out in articles 5.1.c) and 13 of the GDPR, also having to provide the AEPD with evidence of compliance with what is required.  
Thus, the responsible entity may be required to adjust its actions to the personal data protection regulations, to the extent expressed in the previous Legal Foundations.  
In this act, the presumed violation committed and the facts that could lead to this possible breach of data protection regulations are established, from which it is clearly inferred what measures are to be adopted, without prejudice to the fact that the type of procedures, mechanisms, or specific instruments for implementing them corresponds to the sanctioned party, as it is the data processor who fully knows its organization and must decide, based on proactive responsibility and a risk-based approach, how to comply with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.
