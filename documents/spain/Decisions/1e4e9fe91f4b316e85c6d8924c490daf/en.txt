RESOLUTION OF SANCTIONING PROCEDURE  
From the procedure instructed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST:  THE GROUPING OF BODIES OF THE ADMINISTRATION OF PENITENTIARY INSTITUTIONS (ACAIP) (hereinafter the claimant party 1), THE ASSOCIATION OF PENITENTIARY WORKERS "YOUR ABANDONMENT CAN KILL ME" (hereinafter the claimant party 2), and THE PROFESSIONAL ASSOCIATION OF PRISON OFFICERS (hereinafter, the claimant party 3) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on 14/09/2021, 19/09/2021, and 21/09/2021 respectively. The complaint is directed against the GENERAL SECRETARIAT OF PENITENTIARY INSTITUTIONS with NIF S2813060G (hereinafter, SGIP). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
They report the leakage of images captured by the video surveillance system of the Villena penitentiary center (Alicante), in which the aggression of some officials against a prisoner is observed. According to their statements, the images apparently only prompted a confidential internal investigation to determine possible disciplinary responsibilities, although the press has reported the events and disseminated the video. Along with the notification, links to the news containing the reported events are provided, where the video with the images can be viewed.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the complaint was forwarded to the SGIP for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements established in data protection regulations.  
The transfer, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was recorded on 06/10/2021 as evidenced by the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  

THIRD: On December 14, 2021, in accordance with Article 65 of the LOPDGDD, the complaints submitted by the claimant parties were admitted for processing.  

FOURTH: The General Subdirectorate for Data Inspection proceeded to carry out preliminary investigative actions to clarify the facts in question, by virtue of the functions assigned to control authorities in Article 57.1 and the powers granted in Article 58.1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and in accordance with the provisions of Title VII, Chapter I, Section two, of the LOPDGDD, being aware of the following details:

CONCLUSIONS  
Preliminary Information 2021/122 was opened to investigate the events that occurred on 16/8/21 at the Center and was sent to the Penitentiary Surveillance Court and the Guard Court of Villena.  
Due to the subsequent dissemination of the images of this incident, an investigation was initiated which opened Preliminary Information 131/2021 to determine the responsibility and circumstances that took place in the dissemination of the images, resulting in a breach of the principle of confidentiality and secrecy that governs the conduct duty of public function.  
Regarding the determination of authorship in the dissemination of the images, it has not been possible to establish it, nor where the breach of the confidentiality principle in their processing may have occurred.  
The respondent claims that they are covered for the processing of the images by Article 11 of Organic Law 7/2021 and Instructions 5/2006 and 6/2007 on communication and clarification of serious regulatory facts, and the duty to report to judicial authorities in accordance with the provisions of Article 262 of the Criminal Procedure Law.  
They provide information about the CCTV system, supported by two instructions:  
- Instruction 3/2015 on video surveillance in penitentiary establishments dated May 18, 2015, which was in force at the time of the claimed events and the claim; and  
- Instruction 4/2022 regulating the processing of personal data obtained through the recording of images and sounds by the existing video surveillance systems in various penitentiary establishments dated July 28, 2022, along with the Guide for the preparation of the Protocol for recording, storage, and processing of images obtained through the video surveillance systems of penitentiary establishments, dated July 28, 2022, in development of Instruction No. 4/2022.  
That is to say, the 2022 instruction and its action protocol detailing security measures and access are dated after the claimed events and the claim, taking effect 15 days after their receipt in the centers, and a period of 3 months was given to the Management Board to adapt the procedures.  
The first instruction refers to the LOPD/RLOPD and these last two documents refer to Organic Law 3/2018, Organic Law 7/2021, and Regulation (EU) 2016/679.  
It provides the status of the judicial procedure as of June: Preliminary Proceedings 2021/450 Court of First Instance and Instruction No. 1 of Villena.  
FOURTH: On November 29, 2022, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection agreed to initiate sanctioning proceedings against the respondent for the alleged infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.4 of the GDPR.

II  
In response to the allegations presented by the accused entity, the following should be noted:  
-The SGIP alleges that any decision made in the disciplinary field must be governed by the principle of the presumption of innocence.  
In this regard, this Agency expresses its total and absolute agreement, indicating that all sanctioning procedures are conducted according to the provisions established in Articles 64 and following of the LOPDGDD, scrupulously respecting the established legislation.  

The presumption of innocence, a fundamental right of citizens according to Article 24.2 of the Constitution and Article 6.2 of the European Convention on Human Rights, is expressly included in our legal system for sanctioning administrative procedures in Article 53.2.b) of Law 39/15, where among the rights of the interested party in the sanctioning administrative procedure, they shall have the right  
“To the presumption of non-existence of administrative responsibility until proven otherwise.”  

As established by the STS 28.04.2016 (RC 677/2014): “It should be noted that the right to the presumption of innocence, which applies without exception in the field of sanctioning administrative procedures, as referred to by the Constitutional Court in ruling 66/2007, of March 27, implies that ‘no sanction may be imposed that does not have a basis in prior lawful evidentiary activity,’ and also entails the recognition of the right to a due sanctioning administrative procedure with all guarantees, which respects the principle of contradiction and in which the alleged responsible party has the opportunity to defend their own positions, prohibiting the initiation of sanctioning files when the non-existence of rational indications of a committed offense is clearly or manifestly appreciable.”

"conduct that constitutes an infringement, or in which the unlawfulness or culpability is absent."  
- The SGIP argues that, in addition to the fact that the images were known to the Penitentiary Administration (Alicante I Penitentiary Center and General Subdirectorate of Analysis and Inspection), there were two judicial bodies to which the aforementioned images were sent by legal imperative (Guardia Instruction Court and Penitentiary Surveillance Court), and that they cannot be held responsible for the use and treatment granted by them.  
In this regard, this Agency indicates that, in this specific sanctioning procedure, an infringement is not being attributed for the mere leaking of images, but for lacking appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security adequate to the risk of the treatment, as required by Article 32.1 of the GDPR.  
- The SGIP claims that the necessity for broad access profiles originates from the organization and work needs of the General Subdirectorate of Analysis and Inspection, and that regarding the lack of traceability, it is indeed possible to know which person accesses which images - that is, access to the shared folder leaves a trace - the impossibility of identification referred to was regarding the specific person who made the aforementioned images public.  
In this regard, this Agency refers to the Report issued by the General Subdirectorate of Analysis and Inspection, which is part of the file, in which point 2 states verbatim (the underlining corresponds to the AEPD):  
"Once the images were brought to the attention of the Inspection Unit, they were processed, in compliance with their legally established investigation functions by the Guardia Inspection, the Instructor of Preliminary Information 2021/122, and where the rest of the Inspectors of the Unit could also have accessed them, without it being possible to prove such an extreme."  
Therefore, it does not refer, as alleged in the claims, to the impossibility of determining the specific person who made the images public, but rather that it has not even been possible to prove which persons accessed the subsequently leaked images.  
It should also be noted that, when this Agency requested information about the records of users who had access to the system (logs), the response was limited to indicating: "The logs of the file server are kept for a specific time, and at this moment, the logs from those dates are not available."

Article 32 "Security of processing" of the GDPR states:

"1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a consequence of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data only processes such data on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law."  
In the present case, although it is true that it has not been possible to determine at what moment or by which body that had access to the images the leak occurred, it is evident that the SGIP did not have appropriate measures in place to ensure a level of security adequate to the risk.  
From the instruction carried out in this procedure, it is concluded that the SGIP has failed to comply with the provisions of Article 32 of the GDPR.

VI  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 58.2 d) of the GDPR, each supervisory authority may “order the controller or processor to ensure that the processing operations comply with the provisions of this Regulation, when appropriate, in a specified manner and within a specified time frame…”.  
Therefore, the party being addressed must proceed, within 6 months from the receipt of this resolution, to adopt the necessary measures to ensure that records of access to personal data are maintained, as well as the granting of profiles to officials so that each can only access the information necessary for the performance of their duties.
