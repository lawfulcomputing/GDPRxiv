Regulatory Framework  
- On January 30, 2020, the Director-General of the World Health Organization (WHO) declared a public health emergency of international concern due to the global outbreak of the coronavirus of severe acute respiratory syndrome type 2 (SARS-CoV-2), which causes the disease known as coronavirus 2019 (COVID-19). On March 11, 2020, the WHO published an assessment classifying COVID-19 as a pandemic.  
According to the International Health Regulations, an event of this nature constitutes a risk to the public health of States due to the international spread of a disease. For these circumstances, the Regulation provides for health measures, temporary or permanent, that States may apply to individuals, luggage, cargo, containers, means of transport, goods, and/or packages, in response to a public health emergency of international importance.  
Among the measures to be applied to individuals are medical examinations, prophylactic measures, or other recognized health measures that prevent or control the spread of the disease, including isolation, quarantine, or subjecting the traveler to public health observation.  
The evolution of the pandemic and the emergence of new variants of special concern have required the adoption of different measures to ensure greater control over international traveler traffic.  
- Since the beginning of the implementation of health controls, through the Resolution of June 29, 2020, of the General Directorate of Public Health, Quality, and Innovation, regarding health controls to be carried out at points of entry to Spain, subsequently replaced by the Resolution of July 24, 2020, of the General Directorate of Public Health, Quality, and Innovation, regarding health controls to be carried out at points of entry to Spain, the epidemiological situation regarding the COVID-19 pandemic has been changing, not only in Spain but globally.  
In the same way, the mechanisms for health surveillance and control that countries have been implementing at points of entry have also evolved in order to control the importation of cases from passengers coming from at-risk countries.  
The updated list of at-risk countries or areas is reviewed periodically, and its update is published on the website of the Ministry of Health and on the SpTH website: https://www.spth.gob.es. The update of the reviews of the list of countries or areas becomes effective seven days after its publication, with the aim of allowing transport companies, travel agencies, and tour operators to adjust the informational measures for passengers.  
In Spain, health controls for international passengers at points of entry have been regulated through various Resolutions of the General Directorate of Public Health, published on the Government of Spain's website and referenced in the COVID-19 Border Regulatory Diligence, with more than one hundred regulatory references.

Validation of health documentation  
The verification of health documentation allows AIR EUROPA to ensure that the passenger meets the requirements of the destination country.  
For the validation of the health documentation uploaded by the passenger, AIR EUROPA has contracted AMADEUS IT GROUP, S.A. (hereinafter, Amadeus) as the data processor, which provides, among other things, the “SAFE TRAVEL” tool, which allows for the automatic management of the validation of the documentation. In turn, Amadeus has MICROSOFT OPERATIONS IRELAND LIMITED (hereinafter, Microsoft) as its sub-processor.

requires the service known as MICROSOFT AZURE (cloud hosted in the EEA, data extraction, automatic verification).  
The validation process begins with the automatic verification that the files uploaded by passengers, the documentation uploaded in PDF or image format, do not contain malware. Next, the necessary information (first and last name, type of test, date of the test, test result) is extracted from the image with the support of the tool “Azure Computer Vision,” a technology for optical character recognition (OCR). This technology belongs to the subcontractor Microsoft.  
With the processing of the extracted data, verification is carried out to determine whether the passenger meets the requirements to travel. If the passenger meets the requirements, an indicator of "authorized to travel" is added to the reservation, or in other words, SKYCLEARED, and this designation is included in the tool “ALTEA DCS.” This tool, also contracted to Amadeus, functions to manage check-in (acceptance of the passenger and their luggage and assignment of a seat on the aircraft) and boarding (process of verifying the passenger's identity and managing access to the aircraft in the most efficient manner possible).  
AIR EUROPA has access to the data stored in “ALTEA DCS,” and if the boarding pass does not include the designation SKYCLEARED, at the time of boarding, the ground assistance staff requests the passenger to show the health documentation, in paper or digital format, to verify at that moment that they meet the requirements of the destination country.  
Data retention period  
The health documentation uploaded in PDF or image format during online check-in through the tool “SAFE TRAVEL,” embedded in the AIR EUROPA website, is deleted after 48 hours.  
Previously, the tool performs data extraction (first and last name, type of test, date of the test, test result) to automatically validate compliance with health requirements. This process is carried out with “Azure Computer Vision,” and the extracted data is deleted after 48 hours.  
Passengers who meet the requirements have the designation SKYCLEARED (“authorized to travel”) entered on their boarding pass. This designation is stored until the flight departure date and subsequently for seven days in case there are incidents (overbooking, flight cancellation, delay, denial of boarding, change of destination, etc.) to manage them appropriately.

d) the measures envisaged to address the risks, including safeguards, security measures, and mechanisms to ensure the protection of personal data, and to demonstrate compliance with this Regulation, taking into account the rights and legitimate interests of the data subjects and other affected persons.  
8. Compliance with the approved codes of conduct referred to in Article 40 by the respective controllers or processors shall be duly taken into account when assessing the impact of the processing operations carried out by those controllers or processors, particularly for the purposes of the data protection impact assessment.  
9. Where appropriate, the controller shall seek the views of the data subjects or their representatives regarding the intended processing, without prejudice to the protection of public or commercial interests or the security of the processing operations.  
10. When the processing based on Article 6(1)(c) or (e) has its legal basis in Union law or in the law of the Member State applicable to the data controller, such law regulates the specific processing operation or set of operations in question, and a data protection impact assessment has already been carried out as part of a general impact assessment in the context of the adoption of that legal basis, paragraphs 1 to 7 shall not apply unless Member States consider it necessary to carry out such an assessment prior to the processing activities.  
11. If necessary, the controller shall examine whether the processing is in compliance with the data protection impact assessment, at least when there is a change in the risk posed by the processing operations.

Conclusion  
In the complaint filed against AIR EUROPA, this Agency is informed that the entity has enabled a space on its website for users to include health information related to COVID-19 vaccination, without having any legal authorization or being constituted as a health authority, as well as not adequately informing about the processing and management of such data.  
After the actions taken, it has been verified that AIR EUROPA, to implement the more than 300 different versions of health requirements that passengers had to provide in the destination countries since the onset of COVID-19, contracted the services of AMADEUS IT GROUP S.A., specifically the “SAFE TRAVEL” tool to automate the verification of passengers' health documentation.  
It provides the data protection impact assessment related to the processing carried out by “SAFE TRAVEL.” The DPIA includes the assessment of necessity and proportionality of the processing, the identification of threats, the inherent risk of each of them, the measures defined to mitigate it, and the level of residual risk.  
The final result is an acceptable risk, and the security measures implemented by the controller, processor, and sub-processor are adequate.  
The use of the “SAFE TRAVEL” tool is optional; the passenger is not obligated to provide their health documentation, and they always have the alternative to present it when requested at the time of boarding the plane.  
In the information clause provided to passengers prior to uploading health documentation, it states:  
"The use of this automated service is voluntary and optional. Every passenger has the option to present their documents at the airport if they do not wish to use this method."  
This information clause must be accepted to continue with the process of uploading health documentation; otherwise, the online check-in process will continue without verifying compliance with health requirements, which will imply that passengers must present their health documentation at the time of boarding.

Article 27 of Law 40/2015, of October 1, on the Legal Regime of the Public Sector, enshrines the principle of typicity in the field of the sanctioning power of the Administration, as it prescribes that only violations of the legal order that are provided as such by a law constitute administrative infractions.  
According to the doctrine of the Constitutional Court, the guiding principles of criminal law apply, with certain nuances, to sanctioning administrative law, given that both are manifestations of the punitive order of the State, as reflected in the Constitution itself in its article 25, which imposes the necessity of normative predetermination of infringing conduct and the corresponding sanctions, allowing for sufficient certainty (lex certa) in predicting such conduct and knowing what to expect regarding the associated liability and potential sanction, with a precise correlation between the illicit act and the action attributed, through a correct and adequate subsumption of the proven facts in the applied sanctioning type.  
According to the principles of sanctioning administrative law, a series of elements must concur to understand that an administrative infraction has occurred. First, for administrative liability to exist, it is necessary that the person being charged has performed an action or omission. If this conduct, whether active or omissive, is regulated in administrative norms as an infraction, then it will be granted such nature. Likewise, the principle of responsibility requires that the alleged responsible party has acted culpably or willfully, with the first case being when the actions are conscious and voluntary, and the culpable case being those that are involuntary but result in a breach of the norm. Another essential element of administrative infractions is the effect of time, as only those behaviors that are classified as infractions at the time they are committed can be punished.  
In the present case, there is no evidence that any action different from that explained by the responding party has occurred.  
Therefore, based on the indications in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found that substantiates the existence of an infraction within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.
