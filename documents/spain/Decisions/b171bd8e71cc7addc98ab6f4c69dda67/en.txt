LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
According to and pursuant to the provisions of Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve these actions.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, by the general rules on administrative procedures in a subsidiary manner."  
II  
Preliminary Issues  
In this case, the file contains various emails sent by C.C.C. on August 3, 2023, and on September 6 and 11, and by D.D.D. on August 3, 11, 17, 23, 28, August 31, and September 1, indicating various email accounts as recipients, including that of the complaining party. In these emails, the functionality of blind carbon copy was not used, so the addresses of the recipients were visible to all.

III  
Conclusion  
Recital 18 of the GDPR states the following: This Regulation does not apply to the processing of personal data by a natural person in the course of an activity that is exclusively personal or domestic and, therefore, has no connection with a professional or commercial activity. Personal or domestic activities may include correspondence and the keeping of an address book, or activity on social networks and online activity carried out in the context of the aforementioned activities. However, this Regulation applies to controllers or processors who provide the means to process personal data related to such personal or domestic activities.  
In line with the above, Article 2.2 c) of the GDPR provides the following: This Regulation does not apply to the processing of personal data: c) carried out by a natural person in the exercise of activities that are exclusively personal or domestic;  
According to the Judgment of the Supreme Court, Third Chamber, of the Administrative Litigation, Section 3, Judgment 815/2020 of June 18, 2020, Rec. 1074/2019, there are two legal requirements for the exclusion clause to come into play: that the data processing is done by an individual and that it is done within the framework of an exclusively personal or domestic activity.

Due to its specialty, the principles inspiring the criminal order apply to it, with some nuances but without exceptions, resulting in the clear full effectiveness of the principle of presumption of innocence. Not in vain, Article 53.2 b) of the LPACAP recognizes the right of the presumed responsible parties to "the presumption of non-existence of administrative responsibility until proven otherwise." Indeed, the Constitutional Court, in Judgment 76/1990, considers that the right to the presumption of innocence entails "that the sanction is based on acts or incriminating evidence of the reproached conduct; that the burden of proof corresponds to the accuser, without anyone being obliged to prove their own innocence; and that any insufficiency in the result of the evidence presented, freely assessed by the sanctioning body, must translate into an acquittal ruling." In short, the application of the principle of presumption of innocence prevents attributing an administrative infringement when there has not been obtained and verified the existence of incriminating evidence supporting the facts that motivate this attribution.  
Consequently, since it has not been proven that the communications were not made within the framework of an exclusively private and domestic activity and that the email addresses used were not previously known by the recipients, it can be concluded that no facts have occurred that fall within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.
