FACTS  
FIRST: On October 18, 2022, the Spanish Agency for Data Protection received Diligences No. ***DILIGENCIAS.1 from the Local Police of the Villa de la Orotava Town Hall, with NIF B38466512 (hereinafter, the LOCAL POLICE).  
The Local Police of Villa de la Orotava provided Diligences No. ***DILIGENCIAS.1, dated October 4, 2022, in which it is stated that, on September 26, 2022, abandoned documentation was found in containers located in public areas, which were accessible to any passerby, including documents linked to the reported entity containing a plurality of personal data.  
In the report of said diligences, it is noted that agents XXXXX and XXXXX, performing functions typical of the body and patrolling on ***CALLE.1, observed three containers for waste collection with open lids, completely filled with documents in paper format.  
The interior of the containers is visible from the street and sidewalk, with the documents easily readable and accessible to third parties.  
After the ocular inspection of the interior of the containers, it is observed that most of the documents contain the company FONTANORTE, S.L., with NIF B38466512, hereinafter FONTANORTE, as a supplier or client, and Mr. A.A.A., with DNI ***NIF.1, appears in different documents as the representative of the company.  
Given the indications of a possible infringement of L.O. 3/2018 on Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights, the three containers are located at the facilities of the Environmental Services of the Hon. Town Hall of the Villa de la Orotava, corresponding to the company PreZero, at the disposal of FONTANORTE for their processing.  
It is noteworthy that the intervened paper documents contain data and information about individuals, including: Certificates of installation and supply and evacuation of water, safety plans, contracts, invoices, employee vacation reports, photocopies of DNI, photocopies of Caja Canarias cards, gas installation certificates, employment contracts… among others.  
SECOND: The agents of the Local Police of Orotava notified Mr. A.A.A., as the joint administrator and representative of FONTANORTE, of the location of the three containers, urging him to process and destroy the personal data in accordance with current legislation.

In the present case, we proceed to examine the proceedings No. ***DILIGENCIAS.1 of the LOCAL POLICE, in which it is reported that agents 12511 and 36990, performing functions typical of the force and patrolling along Calle ***CALLE.1, observed three waste collection containers with open lids, completely filled with paper documents containing data and information about individuals, including: installation and supply certificates, wastewater evacuation certificates, safety plans, contracts, invoices, vacation reports of a worker, photocopy of ID, photocopy of Caja Canarias booklet, gas installation certificates, employment contracts… among others.  
The interior of the containers is visible from the street and sidewalk, with the documents being easily readable and accessible to third parties.  
After the ocular inspection of the interior of the containers, it was observed that the majority of the documents list the company FONTANORTE as a supplier or client, and Mr. AA.A., with ID ***NIF.1, appears in various documents as the representative of the company.  
It is, therefore, pertinent to analyze whether the processing of personal data carried out is in accordance with the provisions established in the GDPR.  
For the purposes of the GDPR, the data controller is defined in Article 4.7 as:  
“the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; if the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the data controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.”  
Article 4.2 of the GDPR also defines:  
“‘processing’: any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination, or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.”  
Translating these considerations to the specific case under examination, the company FONTANORTE, and as its representative, Mr. A.A.A., with ID ***NIF.1, carries out this activity in his capacity as data controller, as he is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines “personal data security breaches” (hereinafter security breach) as “all breaches of security that lead to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or unauthorized communication or access to such data.”  
From the actions taken, it has been confirmed that the breach had as its entry vector the destruction by FONTANORTE of documentation containing data.

III  
Article 32 of the GDPR  
Regarding the application of data protection regulations to the proposed case, it must be taken into account that the GDPR, in its Article 32, requires data controllers to adopt the corresponding necessary security measures that ensure that the processing is in accordance with current regulations, as well as to guarantee that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data can only process it following the instructions of the controller.  
Article 32 of the GDPR establishes the following regarding the security of processing:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the varying probability and severity of risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
 a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
 b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
 c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
 d) a process for regularly verifying, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
 
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a consequence of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.”

On the other hand, Recital (74) of the GDPR states that: “The responsibility of the data controller for any processing of personal data carried out by themselves or on their behalf must be established. In particular, the controller must be obliged to implement appropriate and effective measures and must be able to demonstrate compliance of the processing activities with this Regulation, including the effectiveness of the measures. Such measures must take into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.”  
Defining and establishing control and security measures is a fundamental task that must be carried out in accordance with the particularities of the processing activities and based on the risks involved.  
From the actions taken, it has been established that the breach had as its entry vector the destruction by FONTANORTE of documentation containing personal data using inadequate methods for this purpose. Documentation such as installation and supply certificates, wastewater evacuation certificates, security plans, contracts, invoices, employee vacation reports, photocopies of ID cards, photocopies of Caja Canarias cards, gas installation certificates, or employment contracts, which include personal data such as name and surname, ID number, postal address, account numbers… among others.  
In this case, the breach of the technical and organizational measures that the reported entity had for the phase of processing the destruction of paper data has been evidenced, which in a case like the one analyzed must comply with minimum standards that guarantee the effectiveness of carrying it out, given the large volume of documentation containing personal data, making it essential to take them to a destruction point.  
The GDPR also requires not only the design of the necessary technical and organizational means but also their proper implementation and appropriate use, so that they will also be liable for the lack of diligence in their use, understood as reasonable diligence considering the circumstances of the case, encompassing the assessment of the impact of an incident on personal data, regardless of whether the processing is carried out automatically or manually, or whether the incidents are accidental, both human and associated with natural events.  
Incident management is a process that, to a greater or lesser degree of maturity, must already be part of the culture of data controllers and processors. This incident management must be updated, if it is not already, and incorporate the procedures to respond to the obligations arising from the GDPR.  
In the face of the risk of not adequately and properly destroying paper documents containing personal data, the lack of these measures is observed. They must also be subject to monitoring in their compliance and effectiveness, as well as when the circumstances of the processing change, taking into account and considering, where applicable, any incident that may occur due to the data, the format, or medium of its processing.

In accordance with the evidence available in this initiation agreement of the sanctioning procedure, and without prejudice to the outcome of the instruction, it is considered that the known facts could constitute an infringement attributable to the reported entity for violation of Article 32 of the GDPR.  
IV  
Classification of the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR  
If confirmed, the aforementioned infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR could imply the commission of the infringements classified in Article 83.4 of the GDPR, which under the heading "General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines" states:  
"Violations of the following provisions shall be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 10,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to a maximum of 2% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the obligations of the controller and the processor under Articles 8, 11, 25 to 39, 42, and 43; (…)”  
In this regard, the LOPDGDD, in its Article 71 "Infringements," establishes that "Acts and behaviors referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those that are contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements."  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations, Article 73 "Infringements considered serious" of the LOPDGDD indicates:  
"Based on what is established in Article 83.4 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following are considered serious and shall be subject to a two-year statute of limitations: infringements that constitute a substantial violation of the articles mentioned therein and, in particular, the following:  
g) The breach, as a result of a lack of due diligence, of the technical and organizational measures that should have been implemented in accordance with the requirements of Article 32.1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679."  
V  
Sanction for the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR  
For the purpose of deciding on the imposition of an administrative fine and its amount, in accordance with the evidence available at this moment in the initiation agreement of the sanctioning procedure, and without prejudice to the outcome of the instruction, it is appropriate to grade the sanction to be imposed according to the criteria established by Article 83.2 of the GDPR.

II  
Termination of the procedure  
Article 85 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), under the heading "Termination in sanctioning procedures" provides as follows:  
“1. Once a sanctioning procedure has been initiated, if the offender acknowledges their responsibility, the procedure may be resolved with the imposition of the appropriate sanction.  
2. When the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature or when it is possible to impose a pecuniary sanction and another non-pecuniary sanction but the impropriety of the latter has been justified, voluntary payment by the presumed responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except in relation to the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for the damages caused by the commission of the infringement.  
3. In both cases, when the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature, the competent authority to resolve the procedure will apply reductions of at least 20% on the amount of the proposed sanction, which are cumulative. The aforementioned reductions must be specified in the notification of initiation of the procedure and their effectiveness will be conditioned on the withdrawal or renunciation of any action or administrative appeal against the sanction. The percentage of reduction provided for in this section may be increased by regulation.”
