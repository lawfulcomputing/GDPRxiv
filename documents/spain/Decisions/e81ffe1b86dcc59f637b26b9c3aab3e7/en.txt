BACKGROUND  
FIRST: On January 27, 2022, a complaint was received by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection (hereinafter, AEPD), submitted by A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant).  
The complaint is directed against the CANARY ISLANDS HEALTH SERVICE with NIF Q8555011I (hereinafter, the respondent).  
The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The complainant states that there have been unauthorized accesses to her medical history and that her diagnosis has been disclosed to third parties.  
She also states that the website of the Ministry of Health of the Government of the Canary Islands (https://www.gobiernodecanarias.org/sanidad/) uses cookies without notifying users or having a cookie policy and without requesting explicit consent for their use. They also do not have a Privacy Policy.  
Date on which the reported events took place: November 2, 2021.  
Relevant documentation provided by the complainant:  
- Response issued by the CANARY ISLANDS HEALTH SERVICE regarding accesses to the Medical History, which includes a List of Accesses made by Primary Care from 10/5/21 to 12/9/2021 and a List of Accesses made by Specialized Care at the General Hospital of Fuerteventura from 10/6/2021 to 12/10/2021.  
In this document, the complainant states that the accesses marked in color are not associated with any clinical process or consultation.  

SECOND: In accordance with the preliminary mechanism for the admission of complaints submitted to the AEPD, as provided in Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), which consists of forwarding them to the data protection delegates designated by the data controllers or processors, or to them when they have not designated any, and for the purpose indicated in the aforementioned article, the complaint was forwarded to the CANARY ISLANDS HEALTH SERVICE (hereinafter, the respondent) for analysis and response within one month, which was verified by a document received by this Agency on May 6, 2022.  
In response to the transfer and request for information, the respondent stated that it had forwarded the complaint to the Security Office (ODS) of the Electromedical and Information Services Area (ASEI) and to the Management of Health Services of Fuerteventura.  
The ASEI stated that it had audited the accesses marked by the complainant, requesting justification from the individuals who accessed such records. The Management of Health Services of Fuerteventura stated that, upon reviewing the files held by this management center, there was no documentation related to the reference file.  
On April 25, the General Secretariat of the respondent sent the results of the audit conducted by the ASEI and informed the AEPD that a letter had been sent to all managements in the following terms:  
"The Spanish Agency for Data Protection has sent, in a short period of time, several complaints regarding allegedly unauthorized accesses to the medical history of patients by personnel of the center.  
Instruction No. 4/10 of this Directorate, regarding the actions of personnel of the Canary Islands Health Service who, in the course of their work, handle personal data, makes it clear that in the service-providing bodies, the head of the healthcare center will determine which units will adopt, on behalf of the data controller, the necessary measures for the personnel of each unit to understand, in a comprehensible manner, the security rules of the files that affect the development of their functions (fifth section).  
In this regard, it is important that personnel accessing the medical history are aware of the disciplinary and even criminal responsibilities they may incur if, despite the warning already present in the application, they access a patient's medical history for unjustified reasons.  
Similarly, they are reminded that, upon any indication of unauthorized accesses, the corresponding management must take the necessary measures to clarify any administrative or criminal responsibilities that may arise."  
The respondent also added that it was deemed appropriate to develop a protocol for processing requests received in which the interested party requests information about who accessed their medical history.

III  
The processing of data from medical records is regulated by Law 41/2002, of November 14, which is the basic regulation governing patient autonomy and rights and obligations regarding information and clinical documentation.  
Its Article 3 states:  
"Medical record: the set of documents that contain data, assessments, and information of any kind regarding the situation and clinical evolution of a patient throughout the care process."  
In Article 16, the uses of the medical record are established:  
"1. The medical record is an instrument primarily intended to guarantee adequate care for the patient. The healthcare professionals at the center who perform the diagnosis or treatment of the patient have access to the medical record as a fundamental instrument for their proper care.  
2. Each center shall establish methods that enable access to the medical record of each patient by the professionals assisting them at all times."  
IV  
The principles relating to the processing of personal data are regulated in Article 5 of the GDPR, which establishes that "personal data shall be:  
"a) processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject ('lawfulness, fairness, and transparency');

c) adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed ('data minimization');

In the present case, a complaint is presented regarding unauthorized access to the medical history and the disclosure to third parties of the diagnosis of the complainant.  
In relation to the unauthorized accesses, the entity has provided:  
- Report issued by the Security Office (ODS) of the Area of Electromedical Services and Information (ASEI) regarding the audit conducted on the accesses made by Primary Care and Specialized Care at the General Hospital of Fuerteventura, which reveals that the accesses were made by ten professionals, of whom two accessed the history to inquire about the health status of the complainant as they identified her on the emergency list since she is a professional in the Area of Anesthesia and Resuscitation (FEA).  
- There is no record that the complainant has made a complaint to the ODS.  

In relation to the security of the treatments, the entity has provided:  
- Security Policy, whose approval resolution was published in the Official Bulletin of the Canary Islands on February 13, 2014.  
- Instruction 04/2010 from the Director of the CANARY ISLANDS HEALTH SERVICE, regarding the actions of personnel who, by virtue of their job performance, process personal data.  
- Security document.  
- Decree 178/2005, of July 26, which approves the Regulation governing the medical history in hospitals and healthcare establishments and establishes the content, retention, and purging of its documents.  
- Regulations for logical access control in accordance with the National Security Scheme.  
- Risk Analysis in which the treatments related to the Medical History defined as Critical are listed and indicate that the security measures provided in the National Security Scheme are applied.  

As indicated in the legal basis III, from the reading of Article 16 of Law 41/2002, of November 14, which regulates the autonomy of the patient and rights and obligations regarding information and clinical documentation, it is clearly inferred that, while the medical history is the instrument for providing healthcare to the patient, what must be duly guaranteed is also the fact that access to the medical history can only occur by the professionals who assist the patient, not generally, but specifically for the diagnosis or treatment of the patient.  
Despite the technical and organizational measures implemented, access to a patient's medical history by third parties has not been prevented, which indicates the absence of measures that ensure adequate security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, through the application of appropriate technical or organizational measures.  

Regarding the principle of data protection by design, the GDPR requires in its Article 25:  
"1. Taking into account the state of the art, the cost of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity posed by the processing to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller shall implement, both at the time of determining the means of processing and at the time of the processing itself, appropriate technical and organizational measures, such as pseudonymization, designed to effectively apply the principles of data protection, such as data minimization, and integrate the necessary safeguards into the processing, in order to comply with the requirements of this Regulation and protect the rights of data subjects."  

Therefore, it is considered that such facts constitute a breach of confidentiality, thereby violating Article 5.1 f) of the GDPR, which governs the principle of integrity and confidentiality, as unauthorized accesses to the medical history have occurred, compromising the confidentiality of the health data it contains by allowing access by third parties who were not authorized to do so.

(hereinafter, the complainant), in which he states that there have been unauthorized accesses to his medical history by a worker of the Extremadura Health Service (hereinafter SES), with a professional category of nurse. VI  
The violation of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR entails the commission of the infringements typified in Article 83.5 of the GDPR, which under the heading “General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines” states:  
“Violations of the following provisions shall be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to a maximum of 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9; (…)”  
For the purposes of the limitation period, Article 72.1 a) of the LOPDGDD states that “based on what is established in Article 83.5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following infringements are considered very serious and shall be subject to a three-year limitation period: those that involve a substantial violation of the articles mentioned therein and, in particular, the following:  
a) The processing of personal data violating the principles and guarantees established in Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.”  

VII  
On the other hand, the security in the processing of personal data is regulated in Article 32 of the GDPR, which establishes the following:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the varying probability and severity risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures.”

measures to ensure an adequate level of security commensurate with the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regular verification, evaluation, and assessment of the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. Recital 75 of the GDPR lists a series of factors or situations associated with risks to the guarantees of the rights and freedoms of data subjects:  
“The risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, of varying severity and probability, may arise from the processing of data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial harm, particularly in cases where the processing could lead to issues of discrimination, identity theft or fraud, financial losses, damage to reputation, loss of confidentiality of data subject to professional secrecy, unauthorized reversal of pseudonymization, or any other significant economic or social harm; in cases where data subjects are deprived of their rights and freedoms or prevented from exercising control over their personal data; in cases where the personal data processed reveal racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religion or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, and the processing of genetic data, data concerning health, or data concerning a person's sex life, or criminal convictions and related security measures; in cases where personal aspects are evaluated, in particular the analysis or prediction of aspects related to work performance, economic situation, health, personal preferences or interests, reliability or behavior, situation or movements, in order to create or...

The violation of Article 32 of the GDPR constitutes the commission of the infringements specified in Article 83.4 of the GDPR, which under the heading “General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines” provides:  
“Violations of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 10,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to up to 2% of the total annual global turnover of the preceding financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the obligations of the controller and the processor under Articles 8, 11, 25 to 39, 42, and 43; (…)”  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations, Article 73.g) of the LOPDGDD, under the heading “Serious infringements” provides:  
“Based on Article 83.4 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following infringements shall be considered serious and shall be subject to a two-year limitation period:  
g) The breach, as a result of a lack of due diligence, of the technical and organizational measures that should have been implemented in accordance with the requirements of Article 32.1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.”  
VIII  
In this case, this Agency has established that the security measures of the entity being complained about are inadequate, which constitutes a violation by the entity of the provisions of Article 32 of the GDPR.  
The failure to adopt measures to ensure the principle of confidentiality means that it cannot be considered that there are measures that provide an adequate level of protection against existing risks; this is because the established Security Policy is based on a resolution dated February 13, 2014, an Instruction from 2010 issued by the Director of the CANARY ISLANDS HEALTH SERVICE, and Decree 178/2005, of July 26, which approves the Regulation governing the clinical history in hospital centers and establishments and establishes the content, preservation, and purging of its documents, all of which are norms predating the current regulations on data protection, whose core is based on the GDPR 2016/679, in force since May 25, 2018.  
Therefore, by failing to adopt the necessary security measures to ensure the protection of the personal data of the patients of this health service, it is considered that Article 32 of the GDPR has been violated.

IX  
In conclusion, it should be noted that according to the evidence available, it is considered that the entity being complained about has processed personal data of the complainant, their clinical history and diagnosis, allowing access without adopting the appropriate technical or organizational measures, which implies a violation of Article 5.1 f) of the GDPR, nor have the security measures required by the regulations on personal data protection been adopted, leading to a violation of Article 32 of the GDPR.  
In light of these facts, this Agency considers that the entity being complained about has infringed Articles 5.1 f) and 32 of the GDPR, by violating the principle of integrity and confidentiality, as well as failing to adopt the necessary security measures to ensure the protection of the personal data of the patients of this health service.  
Therefore, this procedure concludes with the imposition of two sanctions for these facts: one for the violation of Article 5.1.f) GDPR, and another for Article 32 GDPR.
