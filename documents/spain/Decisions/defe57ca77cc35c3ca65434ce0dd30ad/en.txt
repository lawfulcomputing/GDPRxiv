BACKGROUND  
FIRST: On 29/09/2021, a complaint was received by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection from claimant 1, and on 18/10/2021 from claimant 2, whose identities are listed in the GENERAL ANNEX. The complaints are directed against the REAL FEDERACIÓN ESPAÑOLA DE BALONMANO with NIF Q2878006B (hereinafter, the respondent). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
Claimant 1 states that the respondent “is requiring players to present a Covid-19 vaccination certificate or, if applicable, a negative antigen test, contrary to what has been established by the TSJ of the Canary Islands, Andalusia, and Galicia, as it constitutes a violation of the right to privacy (art. 18 of the CE) and a violation of the LOPD.”  
Claimant 1 provides a copy of the “HEALTH PROTOCOL REGULAR COMPETITION - REAL FEDERACIÓN ESPAÑOLA DE BALONMANO,” which, according to its introduction, is an updated continuation of the one prepared for the 20/21 season, titled: “Return to Activity,” which begins with “Once the 20-21 season has concluded…”  
In it, measures such as the use of masks, social distancing, hand washing, etc., are established.

The monitoring of the athlete will imply, based on health data, the prescription for the repetition of some of these tests or others that may be determined.

Sentiment and authorization. Under no circumstances, contrary to what is indicated by this Agency (Legal Basis II, page 16), does "affiliation" require the provision of any type of medical, health, or personal data from each individual.  
The application for "Federal License" occurs in each sports season by those "affiliated" individuals who wish to participate in official state-level handball competitions, registered in one of the teams that hold the sports rights in each of the different competitions organized by the respondent. In the process of obtaining the license, each athlete must provide, always through their private area, the documentation required based on their team's affiliation to a specific competition category. Among the documentation required from each athlete is the certification of having passed the medical examination and being declared "fit" for the practice of handball (a document that does not include any type of personal medical or health data, but is limited to qualifying the individual as "fit" or "unfit"). Furthermore, during the 2021/22 season, and as a consequence of the COVID-19 pandemic, athletes are required to provide proof of vaccination or, alternatively, the accreditation of a negative COVID-19 diagnostic test. Both medical documents are necessary for each athlete to obtain the Federal License, contrary to what is stated by this Agency in the final paragraphs of page 15 of the Resolution, in the context of what is presented in Legal Basis Second.  
The RFEBM, contrary to what this Agency has stated, does not believe that it has dealt with "any type of health-related data for the future."  
These affiliation management data are specific and concrete, collected and reported for a specific purpose, which has allowed that, at the moment when health circumstances have evolved, such data are not required from any participant in state competitions. On another note, it is also necessary to clarify that, contrary to what seems to be indicated in the second paragraph of page 15 of the Resolution, the requirement to present the vaccination certificate with a complete COVID-19 vaccination schedule occurs in the official state-level sports competitions organized by the respondent, at the beginning of the 2021/22 sports season, "that is, from September 1, 2022, nine months after the first COVID-19 vaccine was administered in Spain." (It may be that the respondent refers to 1/09/2021, given that the date of the first COVID vaccine was December 27, 2020; likewise, the 21/22 season usually refers to its start date as the first date.)  
Finally, it should be noted that, in addition to the legal norms already cited in previous provisions, "the actions of the R.F.E.BM. are supported by the provisions expressly stated in Article 3 of Organic Law 3/1986 of April 14, on special measures in public health, which has been developed and executed by the regulatory provisions of the Autonomous Communities expressly approved, within the scope of their respective constitutional competencies, in relation to the requirement of health documentation and the practice of competitive sports."

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Regarding the allegations of nullity of the procedure, due to the initiation agreement containing the reference for documentary incorporation, for evidentiary purposes, not for the practice of evidence that has not taken place, it must be understood that such documentation is part of the file and its citation, although it does not seem necessary since it is part of it, is merely clarifying, without causing any confusion between the phases, which in any case the respondent does not explain in more detail.  
Regarding the sanction that is considered to arise from the literal text of the dispositive part, without prejudice to the fact that the expression "is imposed" is a factual error, and this can be clearly seen because it is linked to the reference to Article 64.2.b) of the LPCCAP—referring to the initiation agreement—adding "all this, without prejudice to what results from the instruction," deducing that no sanction has been imposed, much less without a procedure. Therefore, these allegations cannot be upheld.  
III  
Regarding the data collected by the respondent through vaccination certificates and antigen tests uploaded to the platform that manages them, they would fall under the concept of data processing, defined in Article 4.2 of the GDPR as:  
“any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction;”  
Article 6.1 of the GDPR establishes the circumstances that allow for the lawful processing of personal data.  
1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provisions of paragraph f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the exercise of their functions.  
In this case, data of affiliated athletes who are going to participate in the competition is being processed, which, moreover, due to the unforeseen circumstances of COVID-19, declared a global pandemic by the WHO on March 11, 2020, aims to reduce its spread upon the return of competitions. This "return to official competitions of a state and non-professional nature, season 20/21," was approved in the form of a protocol by the CSD on September 14, 2020, noting that it was before the date when the first vaccine was administered in Spain, which dates from December 27, 2020, and therefore this measure could not be referred to in such protocol. In this case, as indicated by claimant 2, at the beginning of the 21/22 season, information was provided regarding the protocol established by their Federation, and data collection began, exercising the right to withdraw the measures on September 22, 2021.  
The GDPR defines health data as: “… personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, which reveal information about their health status;” (Article 4.15 GDPR).  
In this case, the purpose of the affiliation regarding the annual license for competition must be distinguished, along with the necessary data that have specificities in their legal regime, from the purpose of processing that health data, which in the 21/22 season is collected for a specific purpose, in this case to prevent contagion during the development of competitions.
