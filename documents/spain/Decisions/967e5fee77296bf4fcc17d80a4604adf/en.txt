Considering the complaint filed on April 12, 2024, with this Agency by A.A.A., against CONFORAMA ESPAÑA, S.A., for not having duly attended to their right of access.  
After carrying out the procedural actions provided for in Title VIII of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the following FACTS have been established:  

FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) exercised the right of access against CONFORAMA ESPAÑA, S.A. with NIF A79103222 (hereinafter, the respondent) and their request, as stated by the complainant, did not receive the legally established response.  
The complainant states that they experienced a problem with the incorrect issuance of an invoice for their purchase of an ergonomic chair made on March 3, 2024, through the respondent's website. They assert that during the purchase, they specified that the billing details should be those of their partner; however, when picking up the order on March 5, 2024, at the Rivas (Madrid) warehouse, they were given an invoice with a mix of their personal data and that of their partner.  
They indicate that after becoming aware of these facts, they requested a correction at the store, where they were told to send an email to manage the change. When requesting this change, the store asked for copies of the ID cards of both individuals and an authorization, something that the complainant considers disproportionate since the correct data was already in their user profile.  
They maintain that on March 7, 2024, they contacted the Data Protection Officer (DPO) of the respondent requesting explanations regarding the mix of personal data and demanding the correction of the invoice. However, they claim to have received no response, despite the one-month period required by the regulations having elapsed.  
They conclude by stating that the lack of attention to their request, as well as the inability to obtain the correct invoice for their quarterly VAT declaration as a self-employed individual, have been the reasons that motivated the filing of this complaint with the present authority.  
The complainant provides various documents related to the complaint filed with this Agency and regarding the exercise of the right exercised:  
- Screenshots of their user profile on the Conforama website where the correct data would appear.  
- A copy of the issued invoice with the allegedly incorrect mixed data.  
- Emails exchanged with the Rivas store and with the DPO, among which stands out the request for rectification made on March 7, 2024, sent to the address atencion.clientes@conforama.es, which is listed as the contact for the Data Protection Officer of the respondent in the privacy policy on their website.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of the LOPDGDD, which has provided a mechanism prior to the admission of complaints filed with the AEPD, consisting of forwarding them to the Data Protection Officers designated by the controllers or processors of the data, for the purposes provided for in Article 37 of the aforementioned regulation, or to them when they have not designated them, the complaint was forwarded to the respondent entity for analysis and to respond to the complainant and this Agency within one month.  
Despite being duly notified of the aforementioned transfer, no response was received from the respondent.  

THIRD: The result of the transfer procedure indicated in the previous fact did not allow understanding the complainant's claims to be satisfied. Consequently, on July 12, 2024, for the purposes provided for in Article 64.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection agreed to admit the complaint for processing and informed the parties that the maximum period to resolve the present procedure, which is understood to have commenced with the aforementioned admission agreement, will be six months.  
The mentioned agreement granted the respondent entity a hearing period, so that within ten working days, they could present any allegations they deemed appropriate.  
On July 22, 2024, a response from the respondent was registered, which merely indicated that they were attaching the canceled invoice and its rectification, without providing any document that proved they had responded to the request made by the complainant and, consequently, attended to the exercise of the requested right.  

FOURTH: The allegations presented by the respondent were examined and are subject to transfer to the complainant, so that, within fifteen working days, they could formulate any allegations they consider appropriate, and no allegations were received from the respondent within the aforementioned period.

THIRD: The rights of individuals regarding the protection of personal data are regulated in Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR and Articles 13 to 18 of the LOPDGDD. The rights of access, rectification, deletion, opposition, the right to restrict processing, and the right to data portability are contemplated. The formal aspects related to the exercise of these rights are established in Articles 12 of the GDPR and 12 of the LOPDGDD.  
Additionally, consideration is given to what is expressed in Recitals 59 and following of the GDPR.  
In accordance with the provisions of these regulations, the data controller must establish formulas and mechanisms to facilitate the exercise of rights by the data subject, which shall be free of charge (without prejudice to the provisions of Articles 12.5 and 15.3 of the GDPR), and is obliged to respond to requests made no later than one month, unless it can demonstrate that it is unable to identify the data subject, and to express its reasons in case it does not intend to comply with such request. The burden of proof regarding compliance with the obligation to respond to the request for the exercise of rights made by the affected individual lies with the data controller.  
The communication directed to the data subject regarding their request must be expressed in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible manner, using clear and simple language.  
Regarding the right of access to personal data, according to the provisions of Article 13 of the LOPDGDD, when the exercise of the right refers to a large amount of data, the controller may request the affected individual to specify the "data or processing activities to which the request refers." The right shall be deemed granted if the controller provides remote access to the data, considering the request fulfilled (although the data subject may request information regarding the aspects provided in Article 15 of the GDPR).  
The exercise of this right may be considered repetitive if it is exercised more than once within a period of six months, unless there is a legitimate reason for doing so. On the other hand, the request will be considered excessive when the affected individual chooses a means other than the one offered that incurs a disproportionate cost, which must be borne by the affected individual.  
FOURTH: In accordance with the provisions of Article 15 of the GDPR and Article 13 of the LOPDGDD, "the data subject has the right to obtain from the data controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning them is being processed and, if so, the right of access to the personal data."  
Like the other rights of the data subject, the right of access is a personal right. It allows the citizen to obtain information about the processing of their data, the possibility of obtaining a copy of the data.

The possibility of obtaining a copy of the personal data being processed shall not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others.  
For its part, the right to rectification, regulated in Article 16 of the GDPR, allows any person to request the correction of inaccurate or incomplete personal data being processed by a data controller. This right aims to ensure that the data accurately reflects the situation of the data subject, in compliance with the accuracy principle established in the regulation. To this end, the data controller must proceed to rectify the data without undue delay.  
In the case analyzed here, as evidenced by the documents provided in the complaint, the complainant exercised the rights of access and rectification in accordance with Articles 15 and 16 of the GDPR. However, the respondent did not address these requests within the time frame established by the regulation.  
Subsequent to the admission of the complaint, the respondent, through a written submission dated 22/07/2024, attached the canceled invoice and a supposed rectification of the complainant's data, but did not provide any documentation proving that a formal response had been given to the request submitted by the complainant.  
In this regard, it is worth noting that Article 12 of the GDPR requires the data controller to provide a clear, complete, and formal response to the data subject when exercising their rights. Such a response must comply with the principle of transparency, be understandable, and be issued within a maximum period of one month, unless there are duly justified exceptional circumstances. Likewise, the controller must detail the measures taken, the execution timelines, and, if the request is not complied with, explain the reasons in a substantiated manner.  
Consequently, the failure to comply with the aforementioned obligation cannot be considered remedied solely by executing the requested action, such as rectifying inaccurate personal data. The lack of formal communication, regardless of whether such data has been rectified or not, prevents the obligation from being deemed fulfilled. The response is not a mere formality but a key element to ensure the protection of the rights of the data subject and transparency in the processing of personal data.  
Therefore, in the present case, the request cannot be considered addressed, as the controller has not provided a formal and detailed response to the complainant, thus failing to comply with the obligations of Article 12 of the GDPR.

In view of the cited provisions and others of general application, the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection RESOLVES:
