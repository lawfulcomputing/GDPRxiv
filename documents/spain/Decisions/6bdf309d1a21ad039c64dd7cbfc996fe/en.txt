**BACKGROUND**  
**FIRST:** Mr. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the claimant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on October 10, 2022. The complaint is directed against VODAFONE ESPAÑA, S.A.U. with NIF A80907397 (hereinafter, the respondent or Vodafone). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The claimant states that Vodafone provided third parties with duplicates of their SIM cards for two mobile phone lines, and that these actions took place on April 19 and 20 and June 3 of 2022, through requests made by telephone, even going so far as to change the ownership of both phone lines.  
They add that, after the issuance of the aforementioned duplicates, these third parties used the information contained in their mobile phone to access their bank data, making numerous fraudulent transfers from their account, resulting in economic harm.  
They point out that despite having informed the respondent of the facts, indicating that duplicates of their SIM card should not be issued by telephone, but only in person, Vodafone did not take any measures in this regard, and further illicit copies were made subsequently.  
They provide the following relevant documentation:  
- Complaints filed with the National Police on April 20, June 7, and June 16 of 2022.  
- Communications sent via email by their lawyer to the respondent and the responses received, from June 14 to June 28 of the same month and year 2022.

The forwarding, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was acknowledged on December 19, 2022, as evidenced by the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.

In accordance with the provisions of Article 83.2.k) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following may also be taken into account: [...] c) The benefits obtained as a result of the commission of the infringement.” Both provisions mention as a factor that may be considered in the assessment of the sanction the “benefits” obtained, but not the “absence” of these, which is what Vodafone claims.  
Furthermore, according to Article 83.1 of the GDPR, the imposition of fines is governed by the following principles: they must be individualized for each particular case, be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. The admission that the absence of benefits operates as a mitigating factor is contrary to the spirit of Article 83.1 of the GDPR and to the principles governing the determination of the amount of the fine. If, as a result of the commission of a GDPR infringement, it is classified as a mitigating factor that no benefits have existed, the dissuasive purpose fulfilled through the sanction is partially nullified. Accepting Vodafone's thesis in a case like the one at hand would imply introducing an artificial reduction in the sanction that should truly be imposed; that which results from considering the circumstances of Article 83.2 GDPR that must indeed be assessed.
