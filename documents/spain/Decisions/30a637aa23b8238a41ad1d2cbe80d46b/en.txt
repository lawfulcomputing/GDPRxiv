Obligation breached  
Article 6.1 of the GDPR establishes the circumstances under which the processing of personal data may be considered lawful, as follows:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person.  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;

The inquiry raises the question of whether industrial property agents can use the data of individuals published in the Official Bulletin of Industrial Property (BOPI) to contact them to carry out "promotional practices of their activity," that is, to offer their services to those individuals.  
The report highlights: “The statement of the inquiry, as it is posed, reveals that it refers to individuals with whom there has been no prior client relationship, since (…) what is intended is the promotion of their activities to attract new clients.”  
The cited report analyzes the possible application of legitimate interest (Article 6.1 f) of the GDPR) as a basis for legitimizing the processing. Among other aspects, it highlights:  
“(…). In the GDPR, for the existence of legitimate interest as a legal basis for processing, it is not required (i) that the data appears in publicly accessible sources, (ii) nor is it established for the legitimacy of this cause that the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject are 'not violated', but rather a balancing must be carried out to determine the prevalence between the alleged legitimate interest and the 'interests, or the rights and fundamental freedoms' of the data subject requiring the protection of personal data (Art. 6.1 f) GDPR).  
This highlights the standard that must be followed in determining such prevalence. Firstly, the protection regime of the data subject in the GDPR against the data controller who intends to use their personal data based on a legitimate interest is more intense in the GDPR than in the LOPD, since in the latter, to allow legitimate interest, it was sufficient that their rights or freedoms were not 'violated', whereas in the regime of the GDPR, it is sufficient that their interests, rights, or freedoms prevail over that legitimate interest. Note that the word interests is added, as opposed to the violation of the necessary rights or freedoms previously in the LOPD. And that no violation is necessary, but it is sufficient that their interests, rights, or freedoms are affected, even if slightly, as long as that interest prevails over the alleged legitimate interest.” (the emphasis is from the AEPD).

Next, the report focuses on the origin of the publication of the personal data of the interested parties in the BOPI, concluding that the publication of their data in the aforementioned Bulletin does not depend on their will, but is imposed by legal mandate:  
“It seems necessary at this moment to determine what the origin of the publication of the personal data of the interested parties in the official bulletin of industrial property is, and this can only be the establishment of a legal obligation determined by industrial property regulations so that other interested parties in the granting or, denial, etc. of the industrial property right can oppose, if it suits their rights. This is evident, for example, from Article 18.3 of Law 17/2001, of December 7, on Trademarks, which requires that upon receiving the trademark application, the Spanish Patent and Trademark Office shall proceed to publish it in the official bulletin of industrial property, and must necessarily include the name and address of the applicant and the name and address of the representative, if any. Subsequently, Article 19 of the same law establishes that once the trademark application has been published, any person who considers themselves harmed may oppose the registration of the same. Ultimately, this is a restriction on the fundamental right to the protection of personal data of the applicant, and, where applicable, of the representative, justified by the fact that third parties interested in the trademark may oppose its registration. However, this publication of personal data does not depend on the will of the trademark applicant or the representative, but is imposed by law, which cannot be irrelevant when determining the possibility of further uses of such information.” (the underlining is by the AEPD).  
The report of the Legal Office of the AEPD concludes that the interest or fundamental right to the protection of data of the interested party prevails over the legitimate interest:  
“This determines that, in the specific case submitted for consultation, and without the need to establish at this moment a general theory that may be understood as applicable to other cases of publicity in bulletins, as it will be necessary to consider the specific case, the restriction or limitation on the personal data of the interested party is imposed by law for a legitimate purpose. Therefore, this agency considers that in the present case, against the legitimate interest claimed by the consultant to want to use the personal data published in the BOPI to promote their activities as industrial property agents, the interest or fundamental right to the protection of the personal data of the interested party must prevail, who has not voluntarily made such data public in the BOPI, but whose publication is imposed by law for the purposes established therein, which are to allow third parties to oppose the application or registration of the trademark.” (the underlining is by the AEPD).

The report from the Legal Cabinet of the AEPD also highlights that personal data would be used for a purpose different from that for which it was initially collected, which is not foreseeable for the data subject:  
“Indeed, the use of personal data whose publication is mandatory by law in the official bulletins of industrial property would be used for a purpose different from that for which the data was initially collected. Therefore, to determine whether the processing for this new purpose is compatible, the provisions of Article 6.4 of the GDPR must be taken into account, interpreting it in accordance with Recital (47) of the GDPR, which establishes the existence of a relevant and appropriate relationship between the data subject and the controller for such a purpose, as in situations where the data subject is a client of the controller. Well, as has been commented at the beginning, there is no relationship in the present case between the controller and the data subject whose data has been published in the BOPI, so the latter cannot reasonably foresee, at the time in the context of the collection of personal data, that processing for such a purpose may occur, or in other words, as stated in Recital 47, the interests and fundamental rights of the data subject should prevail over the interests of the data controller when the data subject does not reasonably expect further processing to take place. And in the present case, given that, as has been repeatedly expressed, the processing of personal data consisting of publication in the official bulletins of industrial property is mandatory by law, it is independent of the will or consent of the data subject, which cannot be denied, it can be understood that their rights and freedoms and interests prevail over the legitimate interest of the controller. Consequently, in this case, there would be no prevailing legitimate interest of the controller over the rights of the data subject that would legitimize the intended processing of personal data.” (the underlining is from the AEPD).  
Finally, the report from the Legal Cabinet of the AEPD clarifies that with the GDPR, one cannot speak of a legal concept of a source of public access as existed in Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13, on the Protection of Personal Data (LOPD):

Article 14.1 f) of the GDPR only mentions this concept to establish the obligation of the data controller to provide the data subject with information on whether their personal data comes from sources of public access, but without defining these. The data contained in the BOPI, whether they originate from a public access source or not, does not change the regime or the basis of legitimacy for the processing of such personal data, which would be as stated up to this point. Therefore, as mentioned, it is considered that the legitimate interest of the data controller would not prevail in this case over the interest, right, or fundamental freedom to the protection of the personal data of the data subject. In the case examined in this file, a series of relevant elements are observed:  
- CINVENTO has processed personal data of the complainant in a document dated July 11, 2022, which it calls "informative postcard."  
- The aforementioned letter is a communication in which CINVENTO presents to the complainant the services that the company offers, trying to attract them as a client.  
- CINVENTO acknowledges that the data appearing in the document of July 11, 2022, comes from the Official Bulletin of Industrial Property (BOPI).  
- The personal data was published in the BOPI as a result of a legal mandate: As reflected in the report from the Legal Department of the AEPD, there is a legal mandate that provides for the publication of such personal data in the BOPI. That is to say, the publication of the personal data of the complainant in the BOPI occurred because the law required it, not because the complainant voluntarily decided to make such personal data public or accessible to the general public.  
- The personal data has been used (processed) deviating from the legally intended purpose for its publication in the BOPI: The purpose for which the complainant's personal data was published in the BOPI was to allow other interested parties in the granting, concession, or denial, etc., of the industrial property right to oppose, if they deemed it appropriate. However, CINVENTO has processed such data for a different purpose (sending an informative postcard to the complainant offering its services).  
- The processing was not foreseeable for the complainant: Under these circumstances, it cannot be accepted, as an exempting cause of liability, the fact that the complainant did not exercise prior or subsequently the right to the deletion of their personal data, since CINVENTO should not have processed such personal data.

When the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature or when a pecuniary sanction can be imposed alongside a non-pecuniary sanction but the latter has been justified as inappropriate, voluntary payment by the presumed responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except regarding the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for damages caused by the commission of the infringement.  
3. In both cases, when the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature, the competent authority to resolve the procedure shall apply reductions of at least 20% on the amount of the proposed sanction, which shall be cumulative. The aforementioned reductions must be specified in the notification of initiation of the procedure, and their effectiveness will be conditioned upon the withdrawal or renunciation of any action or administrative appeal against the sanction. The percentage of reduction provided in this section may be increased by regulation.”
