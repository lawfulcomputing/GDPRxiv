“52. As previously noted, the GDPR does not impose any requirements on data subjects regarding the form of the request for access to personal data. According to paragraph 55 of Guide 01/2022, the data controller is not obliged to respond to a request sent to the email address of its employees who cannot participate in the processing of requests related to the rights of data subjects, such as drivers or cleaning staff. “SIXTH.- ON THE INADMISSIBILITY OF OBJECTIVE LIABILITY”  
EDREAMS specifies in this allegation that, since in the present case there has not been a security breach, a factual assumption referred to in the Supreme Court ruling no. 188/2022, it does not understand the criterion of direct reproachability to be applicable to the present case as it concerns a case of negligence, and not a security breach, which is the assumption covered in the aforementioned ruling.  
In response to this allegation, it should be noted that the Supreme Court ruling no. 188/2022 is brought up in relation to the liability of EDREAMS for the negligent actions of its employees, without which, according to the doctrine established by that ruling, the data controller can excuse themselves based on their diligent actions separately from the actions of their employees, whether it is a security breach or the lack of attention to a right. Thus, that ruling establishes the requirements for holding legal entities liable for acts committed by their employees based not on objective liability as EDREAMS refers to, but on “the direct reproachability derived from the legal interest protected by the norm that is infringed and the necessity for that protection to be truly effective.”  
For further clarity, in the Supreme Court ruling no. 188/2022, the ruling of February 15, 2021 (rec. 1916/2020) is cited as a precedent, in which the Second Legal Ground establishes:  
“As we saw in the third precedent, the aforementioned admission order of September 28, 2020 declares that the issue raised in the appeal that presents an objective cassational interest for the formation of jurisprudence consists of specifying the responsibility of a public Administration (in this case, the City Council of San Sebastián) in relation to the infringements of the Data Protection Law that may be committed by the officials and public employees thereof, and whether such responsibility can or cannot be attributed to the Administration, regardless of the identification of the official or employee who materially committed the infringement” (the underlining is ours).
