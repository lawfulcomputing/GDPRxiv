BACKGROUND  
FIRST: Mr. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the claimant), on September 19, 2022, filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection. The complaint is directed against the CITY COUNCIL OF LOS LLANOS DE ARIDANE, with NIF P3802400F (hereinafter, the respondent).  
The reasons on which the complaint is based are as follows:  
On May 13, 2022, a Resolution was issued by the respondent, from the Mayor-President of the City Council of Los Llanos de Aridane, “approving the Public Employment Offer for the year 2022 (stabilization),” subsequently rectified in the announcement “of rectification of the Public Employment Offer for the year 2022 (stabilization),” published in the BOP, number 64, on May 30, 2022.  
This resolution motivated the filing of a contentious-administrative appeal by the claimant on July 14, 2022.  
The respondent, in compliance with Article 49 of Law 29/1998, of July 13, regulating the Contentious-Administrative Jurisdiction, notified the resolution by which the referral of the file to the Court was agreed, to all those appearing as interested parties in it, summoning them to appear as defendants within a period of nine days.  
The claimant considers that the aforementioned notification violates data protection regulations, as it revealed the identity of the person who filed the appeal. In this regard, it indicates that excessive data was included, as only the number of the Court and Procedure should have been provided.  
Along with the complaint, a copy of the notification issued on September 12, 2022, by the respondent is attached, through which the letter from the Contentious-Administrative Court No. X of Santa Cruz de Tenerife is included, in which the referral of the administrative file for the approval of the stabilization plan of the City Council is ordered, along with the transfer of the claim.  
SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), this complaint was forwarded to the respondent for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month.

II  
Preliminary Issues  
The Town Hall of Los Llanos de Aridane provides a series of public services, for which it processes personal data of its employees and citizens.  
It carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR: “data controller” or “controller”: the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; if the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its appointment may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.  
Article 4, paragraph 2 of the GDPR defines “processing” as “any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure or destruction.”  
The transfer of the claim to all interested parties in the procedure, including the details of the claimant, is considered a processing of personal data, in accordance with the aforementioned provision of the General Data Protection Regulation.  
For this processing to be compliant with data protection regulations, it must be based on one of the legitimizing bases set out in Article 6.1 GDPR.  
These legitimizing bases are as follows:  
- Consent.  
- Performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or application at the request of the data subject of pre-contractual measures.  
- Compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject.  
- Protection of vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person.  
- Performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller.  
- Pursuit of legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party.  
In the context of Local Administration, the legal basis that legitimizes processing is generally the performance of a task in the public interest or the exercise of public powers, as well as compliance with a legal obligation.

In both cases, there must be a regulatory provision with the rank of law that justifies the processing of the data.  
Article 49 of Law 29/1998, of July 13, regulating the Contentious-Administrative Jurisdiction establishes in its section 1:  
“1. The resolution agreeing to send the file shall be notified within five days following its adoption to all those appearing as interested parties, summoning them to appear as defendants within a period of nine days. The notification shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of the law regulating the common administrative procedure.  
In appeals against decisions adopted by the administrative bodies responsible for resolving special appeals and claims in matters of contracting referred to in the Public Sector Contracts legislation, those persons, other than the appellant, who have appeared in the administrative appeal shall be summoned as defendants, so that they may appear as defendants within a period of nine days.”  
For its part, in accordance with Article 4.1 of the LPACAP, the following are considered interested parties in the administrative procedure:  
“a) Those who promote it as holders of individual or collective legitimate rights or interests.  
b) Those who, without having initiated the procedure, have rights that may be affected by the decision made therein.  
c) Those whose legitimate individual or collective interests may be affected by the resolution and who appear in the procedure as long as there is no final resolution.”  
It must be analyzed whether, even being an interested party, the individuals summoned in the contentious-administrative procedure need to know the full ID number and address of the claimant to assert their rights. That said, it is important that the delivery of information complies with the principles set out in the GDPR regarding the processing of personal data. Specifically, the following can be highlighted:  
- Principle of data minimization: Personal data shall be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed.

Unfulfilled obligation. Data minimization.  
The GDPR establishes in Article 5 the principles relating to the processing of personal data that must be considered:

c) adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’)  
This article enshrines the principle of data minimization in the processing of personal data.  

In the specific case being examined, compliance with the principle of minimization would require identifying the specific data that must be communicated according to the purpose, as well as limiting it to what is strictly necessary for its proper communication. Therefore, in relation to the minimization and necessity of the processing, there are less harmful alternatives for the protection of the data of the claiming party. The delivery of the claim should have been carried out in compliance with the principles set out in the GDPR, specifically, the principle of data minimization, not providing more personal information than is strictly necessary.  
That is to say, for the City Council to comply with the obligation it has to summon the interested parties in the contentious procedure, it was not necessary for them to know the full ID number and address of the claimant.  
Therefore, by virtue of the principle of minimization, it is considered that access by the interested parties in the procedure to the full ID number and address of the claimant as stated in the copy of the claim is contrary to this principle, given that they do not need to know that information, making the claim of the claiming party plausible. In other words, unless the knowledge by the interested parties in the procedure of such data is necessary to assert their rights, its delivery is considered disproportionate, violating the indicated principle, and the full ID number and address of the claimant in the claim should have been anonymized.  
In accordance with the evidence available in this sanctioning procedure, it is considered that the known facts constitute an infringement, attributable to the summoned party, for violation of Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR, classified in Article 83.5.a) of the same legal text.

“Without prejudice to the corrective powers of the supervisory authorities under article 58, paragraph 2, each Member State may establish rules on whether and to what extent administrative fines may be imposed on authorities and public bodies established in that Member State.”

VI  
Imposition of measures  
The text of the resolution establishes what the infringements committed have been and the facts that have led to the violation of data protection regulations, from which it is clearly inferred what measures are to be adopted, without prejudice to the fact that the type of procedures, mechanisms, or specific instruments for implementing them corresponds to the sanctioned party, as it is the controller who fully knows its organization and must decide, based on proactive responsibility and a risk-based approach, how to comply with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.  
It is warned that failure to comply with the order to adopt measures imposed by this agency in the sanctioning resolution may be considered an administrative infringement in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, classified as an infringement in its articles 83.5 and 83.6, which may motivate such conduct to open a further sanctioning administrative procedure.
