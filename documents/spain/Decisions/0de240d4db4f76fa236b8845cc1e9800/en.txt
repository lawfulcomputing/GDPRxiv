Following the procedural actions provided for in Title VIII of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the following FACTS have been established:  

FIRST: Through a burofax received on February 1, 2023, Mr. B.B.B. (hereinafter, the representative) on behalf of Mr. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the claimant) requested CENTRO DENTAL ALFAZ DEL PI, S.L. (hereinafter, the respondent) access to a complete copy of his medical history and that it be sent to his email address. This request was also signed by the claimant himself.  
The legal representative of the respondent, in a letter dated February 14, 2023, communicated that he had not provided the necessary power of representation, and that the requested documentation was available to the claimant at his office.  
By email dated February 17, 2023, sent to the address listed in the aforementioned communication dated February 14, 2023, ***EMAIL.1, the legal representative of the claimant sent a copy of the power of representation.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of the LOPDGDD, the aforementioned claim was forwarded to the respondent for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements established in data protection regulations.  
The respondent indicated that, after receiving a burofax requesting the medical history of the claimant, to which the necessary power of representation was not attached nor a copy of the patient's ID, they responded, via burofax, “(…) noting the lack of such documents and in which, following the provisions of Law 41/2002, of November 14, the basic regulatory law of patient autonomy and rights and obligations in terms of information and clinical documentation, the procedure and requirements for obtaining the requested medical history were communicated.”

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, and 64.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Preliminary Issues  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 55 of the GDPR, the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to perform the functions assigned to it in Article 57, including the enforcement of the Regulation and promoting awareness among controllers and processors about their obligations, as well as addressing complaints filed by an interested party and investigating, as appropriate, the reasons for them.  
Correspondingly, Article 31 of the GDPR establishes the obligation of controllers and processors to cooperate with the supervisory authority that requests it in the performance of its functions. In cases where they have designated a data protection officer, Article 39 of the GDPR assigns this officer the function of cooperating with that authority.

IV  
Right of Access  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 15 of the GDPR and Article 13 of the LOPDGDD, "the interested party has the right to obtain from the data controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning them is being processed and, if so, the right of access to the personal data."  
Regarding the right of access to personal data, according to the provisions of Article 13 of the LOPDGDD, when the exercise of the right refers to a large amount of data, the controller may request the affected party to specify the "data or processing activities to which the request refers." The right will be deemed granted if the controller provides remote access to the data, considering the request fulfilled (although the interested party may request the information referred to in the aspects provided in Article 15 of the GDPR).  
The exercise of this right may be considered repetitive if it is exercised more than once within a period of six months, unless there is a legitimate reason for doing so. On the other hand, the request will be considered excessive when the affected party chooses a means other than the one offered that entails a disproportionate cost, which must be borne by the affected party.  
Like the other rights of the interested party, the right of access is a personal right. It allows the citizen to obtain information about the processing of their data, the possibility of obtaining a copy of the personal data concerning them and being processed, as well as information, in particular, about the purposes of the processing, the categories of personal data concerned, the recipients or categories of recipients to whom the data may be communicated, the expected retention period or criteria for retention, the possibility of exercising other rights, the right to lodge a complaint with the supervisory authority, the information available about the source of the data (if it has not been obtained directly from the data subject), the existence of automated decision-making, including profiling, and information about transfers of personal data to a third country or to an international organization. The possibility of obtaining a copy of the personal data being processed shall not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others.

Patient Autonomy and Rights and Obligations Regarding Information and Clinical Documentation (hereinafter LAP), the literal text expresses:  
"1. The patient has the right to access, with the reservations indicated in section 3 of this article, the documentation of the clinical history and to obtain a copy of the data contained therein. Healthcare centers will regulate the procedure that guarantees the observance of these rights.  
2. The patient's right to access the clinical history may also be exercised by duly accredited representation.  
3. The patient's right to access the documentation of the clinical history cannot be exercised to the detriment of the right of third parties to the confidentiality of the data contained therein collected in the therapeutic interest of the patient, nor to the detriment of the rights of the professionals involved in its preparation, who may oppose the right of access based on the confidentiality of their subjective notes.  
4. Healthcare centers and individual practitioners will only facilitate access to the clinical history of deceased patients to individuals linked to them, for family reasons or otherwise, unless the deceased expressly prohibited it and this is evidenced. In any case, access by a third party to the clinical history motivated by a risk to their health will be limited to the relevant data. Information affecting the privacy of the deceased or the subjective notes of the professionals, or that harms third parties, will not be provided."  
In this regard, it is important to highlight Article 15 of the LPA, which outlines the minimum content of the clinical history:

Regarding the personal nature of the rights of individuals in matters of personal data protection and the powers of attorney for third parties to exercise them on behalf of others, reference is made to the Judgment of the National Court of May 20, 2010, Case Number: 106/2009, which states that  
“FIFTH.-  
(...) Thus, the action of the Diana clinic, requiring an express power of attorney for the exercise of the specific right that the complainant intended to exercise, is in accordance with the personal nature of such rights and with what is established in Instruction 1/1998 of the Data Protection Agency, which is mandatory compliance, and which in its First Rule, section 3, establishes that the exercise of rights must be carried out by means of a request addressed to the data controller that will contain the name, surname of the interested party and a photocopy of the interested party's ID card "and, in cases where it is exceptionally admitted, of the person who represents them as well as a document proving such representation," adding in section 4 "In the event that the request does not meet the specific requirements in section three, the data controller must request the rectification of the same."  
However, the complainant did not grant such a specific and concrete power of attorney as it is not a general power for litigation, and therefore, the action of the Diana clinic cannot be deemed formalistic but rather respectful of the rights of the affected party, who was required to rectify such lack of individual and specific power of attorney, thus making the unequivocal will of the complainant to exercise such right indisputable. Therefore, the clinic did not hinder the exercise of the rights of access, rectification, or cancellation of the appellant, and the Agency's decision on this matter is in accordance with the law. (…)”  
In the present procedure, from the examination of the documentation provided, it has been established that the claiming party, through a voluntary representative, submitted a request for access to their medical history to the responding party, and that the latter, in light of the doubts raised by the representation document presented, requested that it be rectified or, failing that, that the claiming party personally come to collect the requested medical history, after identification.  
Consequently, given that the right of access to the medical history is a personal right, given the reasonable doubts of the data controller regarding the veracity of the representation document and that it offered alternative means to obtain a copy of the requested medical history, it cannot be considered that the responding party acted in a formalistic manner, but rather in a manner respectful of the rights of the applicant. Therefore, it is appropriate to dismiss the claim that led to this procedure.
