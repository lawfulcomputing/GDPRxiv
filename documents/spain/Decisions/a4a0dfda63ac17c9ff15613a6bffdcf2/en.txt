SANCTIONING PROCEDURE RESOLUTION  
From the procedure instructed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST: Mr. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on June 27, 2022. The complaint is directed against the GENERAL DIRECTORATE OF THE CATASTRO with NIF S2826053G (hereinafter, the CATASTRO). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The complainant states that on the official website of the electronic headquarters of the CATASTRO, in the section -generate certifications-, when entering any DNI, fields were auto-filled with the person's name, surname, and address, all without the need to register on the electronic headquarters. These facts were made available to the competent administration (General Directorate of the Catastro) on June 26, 2022, and at the time of filing this complaint, the incident persisted. Furthermore, the complainant has indications that this situation has been occurring for more than 6 months. He claims that the URL of the form was accessible through the Google search engine.  
On July 17, 2022, this Agency received a new document from the complainant providing a screenshot taken with a mobile phone showing a form linked to the electronic headquarters of the catastro and an example of a query made for a DNI number, with the name and surname fields filled in. The screenshot was taken on June 28, 2022, and is provided as evidence of the facts reported in the complaint submitted by this same party on June 27, 2022.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the complaint was forwarded to the CATASTRO for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
The forwarding, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was accepted on July 12, 2022, as recorded in the certification in the file.  
On July 29, 2022, this Agency received a response letter indicating that “1. Detailed and chronological description of the events that occurred.  
On June 26, an incident was received in the electronic headquarters mailbox notifying a possible vulnerability in the section -generate certifications- of the electronic headquarters of the catastro.  
Following the incident, the referred section was reviewed, and no anomaly was found, so more information was requested from the user. On the 28th, the user was asked about the steps taken in the referred access, and the user responded that same day detailing them.  
At that moment, an investigation into the behavior was initiated, and a first correction was made, eliminating direct access to the problematic page on July 1. This was subsequently communicated to the sender of the incident on July 6.  
2. Detailed specification of the causes that made the incident possible.  
One of the options offered in the electronic headquarters is the generation of certifications with a cadastral accreditation fee. This service is only accessible to registered users, whose identification is carried out on the registered access page and is verified on each and every page. This specific page has an option to auto-fill the name and surname fields when the applicant's NIF is entered, to facilitate the completion of the form by the registered user.  
The incident occurred because in the Google search engine, when searching for “generation certifications catastro,” the third link that appeared was a direct URL to the following page:  
***URL.1  
When accessing that link, although no protected data was displayed (as it was checked whether the user had permission to view it), the empty form was visible, and if a valid NIF was entered in the NIF field, that part of the page refreshed, showing the name and surname associated with that NIF.  
This was not detected in previous tests because the verification consisted of ensuring that no data was displayed to users who were not authorized, but it was not checked that the fields of the form were not filled in either.  
3. Number of people affected by the violation of personal data security.  
The function of auto-filling the name and surname from the NIF is offered after prior authentication and does not generate a request to the system.  
Since no protected data stored in the Catastro (ownership of properties or their cadastral value) was offered, there is no record in the database of such accesses.  
To estimate the scope of the problem with that limitation, the queries made since the implementation of this functionality (year 2016) were reviewed in the accessed tables, and 575 queries were found for which there is no record of prior authentication. This is not a high number for that period.  
4. Category of personal data involved.  
In this regard, it should be noted that after entering the NIF, the name and surname fields are auto-filled, but no specific protected data from the Catastro (ownership of properties or their cadastral value) is provided.  
5. Possible consequences for the affected individuals.

As soon as the problem was detected, direct access to that page was removed.

In this regard, it is considered that the incident may have affected, in a very residual manner, only the confidentiality, by providing information that should not have been offered (name, surname, and address of the person to whom the NIF corresponded).  
However, in no case did it affect the integrity because the existing data in the Cadastre was not altered as it was a Query function. Nor did it affect availability, as the service of the Electronic Headquarters of the Cadastre was not interrupted temporarily or permanently by the incident, as can be inferred from the provisions on pages 9 and 13 of your document.  
In response to this allegation, this Spanish Agency for Data Protection must remind that Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines “personal data security breaches” (hereinafter security breach) as “all breaches of security that result in the destruction, loss, or accidental or unlawful alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.”  
Consequently, in the present case, there is a personal data security breach under the circumstances indicated above, categorized as a breach of confidentiality, as a form was exposed on the public internet that should only have been accessed after user identification at the electronic headquarters.  
However, regardless of this, when the initiation agreement refers to confidentiality, integrity, and availability, it refers to the systems and services of the processing, as well as the purpose of the processing.  
Article 32 of the GDPR imposes on the controllers of personal data processing the obligation to determine and establish appropriate technical and organizational security measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, based on the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the variable probability and severity risks for the rights and freedoms of individuals.  
That is to say, the objective of such technical and organizational security measures must be to guarantee the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the personal data subject to processing.  
For all the above reasons, the allegation was dismissed.

Article 32 of the GDPR  
Article 32 “Security of processing” of the GDPR states:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which shall include, among others:

a) pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regular verification, evaluation, and assessment of the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the adequacy of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, especially as a result of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as a factor to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this article.  
4. The data controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data may only process such data on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law.  
In this case, at the time of the security breach, there is no record that the CATASTRO had reasonable security measures in place based on the estimated potential risks.  
The exposure on the public internet of a form that was only to be accessed after user identification in the electronic headquarters does not meet the minimum reasonable security measures. This form allowed for the automatic completion of the fields for name, surname, and address of the person to whom this NIF referred when the NIF field was filled with a valid value.  
Consequently, the exposure of this form does not guarantee the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the processing systems and services nor the purpose of the processing.  
It has been established in the file that the improper exposure of the form on the internet was not detected in the system's prior tests; this was due to the fact that the tests conducted consisted of verifying that no cadastral data was displayed to unauthenticated users in the headquarters, but it was not checked that the fields of the form were not automatically filled in, evidencing that the tests conducted for the implementation of the functionality were not sufficient or adequate.  
Following the knowledge of the security breach, the following measures were adopted:  
- Users are required to be identified in the electronic headquarters and have “management” permissions enabled to be able to display the form.  
- For all pages that have similar characteristics to the form, functionality is implemented to check that if the user attempting to access does not have adequate permissions, the page is not displayed, indicating that their session has expired and thus forcing them to authenticate with the correct permissions.  
- A metadata provided by Google is added to all pages to request the deletion of the cache of the search engine for any requested URL.

Article 33 “Notification of a personal data breach to the supervisory authority” of the GDPR states:  
“1. In the event of a personal data breach, the data controller shall notify the competent supervisory authority in accordance with Article 55 without undue delay and, where feasible, no later than 72 hours after becoming aware of it, unless it is unlikely that the personal data breach will result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. If the notification to the supervisory authority is not made within 72 hours, it shall be accompanied by reasons for the delay.  
2. The processor shall notify the controller without undue delay of any personal data breaches of which it becomes aware.  
3. The notification referred to in paragraph 1 shall, at a minimum:  
a) describe the nature of the personal data breach, including, where possible, the categories and approximate number of data subjects affected, and the categories and approximate number of personal data records affected;  
b) communicate the name and contact details of the data protection officer or another contact point where more information can be obtained;  
c) describe the potential consequences of the personal data breach;  
d) describe the measures taken or proposed by the controller to remedy the personal data breach, including, where appropriate, the measures taken to mitigate the possible adverse effects.  
4. If it is not possible to provide the information simultaneously, and to the extent that it is not possible, the information shall be provided in phases without undue delay.  
5. The controller shall document any personal data breaches, including the facts relating to the breach, its effects, and the remedial measures taken. This documentation shall enable the supervisory authority to verify compliance with the provisions of this Article.”  
In this case, the CATASTRO did not notify the security breach within the timeframe established by the GDPR for this purpose, with the information set out in Article 33 of the GDPR; as it considered that there was no risk to the rights and freedoms of the affected individuals, taking into account the volume of data leaked (575 queries), the type of data (name, surname, and address), and the time the form has been in production (since 2016).  
However, during the course of this investigation, the AEPD tool “Breach Advisor” was used with the following input data, resulting in the “Obligation to notify the security breach”:  
- The incident has affected confidentiality.  
- The incident occurred due to a failure, absence, or breach of security measures.  
- There is a likelihood that the breach poses some risk to the rights and freedoms of individuals as there has been a loss of control over the personal data of the affected individuals in the 575 detected queries.  
Regarding the affected individuals, the CATASTRO states that after reviewing the internal logs, 575 queries have been detected since the form was activated in 2016. Access was made to data such as name, surname, and postal address, but the inquirer also knew the NIF of the data subjects; therefore, there was indeed a risk to the rights of individuals as there was a risk of identity theft. On the other hand, the inquirers were not logged in, so it is unknown who accessed the data, as well as the particular situation of the data subjects whose data was accessed.  
Moreover, depending on the situation of the data subjects, this loss of confidentiality could have generated greater risks to the rights and freedoms of individuals.
