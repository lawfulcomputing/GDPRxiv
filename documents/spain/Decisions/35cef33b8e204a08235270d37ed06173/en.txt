This is a copy from the CENDOJ of the judgment of the Supreme Court dated 21/12/2023, Administrative Litigation Chamber, appeal for cassation 7041/2022, against the resolution of 14/12/2020 by the Director of the AEPD regarding the filing of a complaint related to the exercise of the right to erasure of links in the GOOGLE search engine, which “refer to news published in various media and blogs, in relation to the summons of the appellant (…) to testify (…)”. Furthermore, the judgment nominatively designates the Attorney and the Lawyer of the party filing the cassation appeal. 
The National Court dismissed the appeal filed by the complainant, confirming the filing in the judgment of Section 1 of the Administrative Litigation Chamber dated 24/06/2022, appeal 202/2021, against which the complainant filed a cassation appeal. 
The summary of the case is included in the legal basis V: 
“The background of the present cassation appeal, which we have previously summarized, consists of the fact that the appellant requested the entity 'Google Spain S.L.' to remove from the search engine nine links that he considered harmed his reputation and good name. These are pieces of information published on different websites by various media outlets that report on the summons of the appellant (…) to testify (...), in his capacity as (...) of a company that was contracted by the Popular Party, which was then under investigation. The information also included the fact that the appellant's wife held a high position in the structure of the aforementioned political party. Additionally, the links refer to information about the appellant's professional change as he moves to develop his professional activity in the recycling sector.” 
In the doctrine established in the Supreme Court judgment, it is indicated: 
“The right to information and the public interest in truthful information regarding the testimony of the appellant as (...) of a service company in a significant criminal operation affecting a national political party and his subsequent change of professional sector prevails over the right to be forgotten claimed, as there is a current public interest in accessing the manner and details of the investigation of serious criminal acts that have a manifest impact on public opinion, and as the position as a witness in a criminal case does not contain a pejorative content or a negative or harmful connotation for the interested party, in addition to the concurrent interest of the markets in knowing the professional profiles of those who engage in business activities.” 
The judgment determines that “the cassation appeal filed is not admissible.”

III  
Regarding the information provided on the AEPD website: https://www.aepd.es/areas-de-actuacion/internet-y-redes-sociales/eliminar-fotos-y-videos-de-internet, it states “last modification 7/09/2023”, indicating that: “The dissemination of images or videos published on different internet services without legitimate grounds to process this data of yours, especially on social networks, is a matter frequently raised before the Agency. The General Data Protection Regulation recognizes individuals' exercise of the right to erasure.”  
In the following paragraph, it establishes the conditional that the AEPD will safeguard “your right to erasure”, “if…” after you have contacted the person responsible by a means that allows for verification, and that person responsible for data processing has not responded within the established timeframe or if you believe that the response has not been adequate, so you may file a complaint in this regard.”  
It is implied that this information refers to the case of exercising the right to erasure, which as such is a power of the claimant; outside of this situation of exercising such a right, it is not logically specified that a complaint can also be filed, but the regulation expressly provides for it. The appellant believes that it is deduced from this information that it is a mandatory requirement, resulting in their opinion, that it is obligatory to first request the responsible party to erase the data, and, furthermore, that technological operators have tools for erasure, so they must also be addressed.  
That is to say, it reiterates in another way what occupied a long space in the response to the allegation in the file. It would be, as a result of the thesis defended by the appellant, the legal obligation for the claimant to first and necessarily submit to the data processing responsible a written request for the removal of the content, as a mandatory exercise of the right to erasure, in this case, of a video and some documents below the video, as well as the same documents located at an address of the respondent, as an imperative scheme.  
According to the appellant's thesis, this would imply as a consequence a sort of prior route that would condition the corrective power of the supervisory authority, deducing that, by default, all treatments would be legitimate. Given that the objectives of the GDPR and the LOPDGDD are to guarantee a high level of protection for individuals while eliminating obstacles to the circulation of personal data, the underlying reasoning cannot be accepted, and for the response to this allegation, we refer to the reasoning contained in the LEGAL GROUNDS IV of the contested resolution.

IV  
In relation to the statements concerning the processing carried out based on the exercise of freedom of expression and information, and the allegation of 11/03/2024, providing a copy of the Supreme Court's cassation ruling of 21/12/2023, regarding the conflict between the right to data protection (right to be forgotten, suppression of Article 17 of the GDPR) due to the removal of nine links from the GOOGLE search engine that refer to various media outlets, against the right to information, it should be clarified that in the resolution now being contested, it is made clear that it is possible to inform about the doctrine established by the judge while respecting the availability of the data by its owner, according to the assessment of the principles of necessity and proportionality of the processing in relation to the purposes to be carried out. It is made clear that, in this case, the physical medium of the videoconference used by the appellant to express his ideas or thoughts and inform, originally, comes from a procedural act between the parties, regarding the right to honor, of a private nature, and has been used outside the reasonable expectations of the data owner in the video channel, along with documentation that was presented in that process, which also appeared on the appellant's website.  
This aims to emphasize that the case analyzed here with the ruling provided does not have similar essential base elements for comparison. V  
Regarding the application of the "Guidelines 4/2022 on the calculation of fines under the GDPR," adopted on 24/05/2023, it should be indicated that they are adopted to harmonize the methodology used by the supervisory authorities when calculating the amount of the fine, and that they complement those adopted on 3/10/2017 "on the application and setting of administrative fines for the purposes of the GDPR," which focus on the circumstances under which a fine is imposed.  
The approval of such Guidelines by the European Data Protection Committee, an entity created under the GDPR to act independently, with, among other functions, ensuring the coherent application of the GDPR (art 70), empowers it to issue such Guidelines, as mentioned in its article 70.1.k).  
For the application of the Guidelines 4/2022 invoked in the present case, at least two elements must be taken into account:  
The first is that, in the present case, the infringement being appealed appears related to the exposure of a video of a procedural act held on ***DATE.1, with the video obtained by the respondent on 20/07/2021, video and documents that contained personal data that remained exposed upon issuing the resolution. The aforementioned Guidelines were adopted on 24/05/2023, so it would involve applying such interpretative criteria that would retroactively address events occurring before their adoption. “Scope” determines that data controllers will not be held liable when they are natural persons and do not act as businesses, as is the case here.  
Regarding the concept of damages, considering paragraph 146, first, third, and sixth sentences, of the GDPR:  
“The controller or the processor must compensate for any damages that a person may suffer as a result of processing in violation of this Regulation. As a consequence of a violation of the GDPR, damages can be both material and immaterial. In any case, if the appellant did not suffer any personal harm, this is not necessary to infringe Article 5.1.b) which was declared violated and which constitutes an objectively evident interference in the interests of the person, and the temporary deprivation of the ability to dispose of their own data due to publication on YOU TUBE for such a long period is sufficient to objectively derive harm. In any case, there is no evidence of unequal treatment in similar cases where a violation of data protection regulations is claimed through video exposure on a platform such as the one mentioned, with the amount duly justified.  
Regarding the adjustment of the amount of the violation, (…) it must combine the deterrent nature with the proportional and effective nature that fines have for compliance with data protection regulations, relating it to the nature, severity, and consequences of the violation. In this case, given the long duration of the (…) alleged, it would allow for proportionality to apply a reduction in the amount, although the appellant must take extreme precautions in data processing to avoid continuing to openly identify what has been prohibited, whether through direct identification or making it identifiable, along with compliance with the principles of the GDPR, among which transparency in information about personal data is paramount.  
Considering the circumstances noted by the claimant, a reduction of the amount of the sanction to a total of 5,000 euros is deemed proportional.
