From the procedure instructed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following:  
BACKGROUND  
FIRST: Ms. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on May 11, 2022. The complaint is directed against Vodafone España, S.A.U. with NIF A80907397, previously Vodafone Enabler España (LOWI) (hereinafter, the defendant party or Vodafone). The reasons on which the complaint is based are as follows:  
The complainant states that, on February 28, 2022, Vodafone provided a third party with duplicates of her two SIM cards, without having conducted any verification in this regard, leaving the complainant without service on her two devices.  
She adds that this third party modified the access data to her customer area and that, using the information contained in her mobile phone, accessed her bank account, carrying out fraudulent operations.  
Subsequently, she contacted the defendant party by phone requesting new duplicates of her two SIM cards and also the blocking of the lines, and, on the other hand, Vodafone confirmed to her that the address of her contact email had been modified.  
She indicates that she filed a complaint with the defendant entity without receiving a response.  
Relevant documentation provided by the complainant:  
- Email dated March 7, 2022, sent by the complainant from the address ***USUARIO.1@gmail.com to the address (...)@lowi.es of the defendant entity informing about the activation of two fraudulent duplicates of the cards associated with the numbers ***TARJETA.1 and ***TARJETA.2 and subsequently money was withdrawn from her bank account. In this email, she also informs that she has requested by phone the sending of new duplicates and the blocking of the lines until their receipt. She highlights that she has been informed that the email address has been modified.  
- Complaint filed with the National Police dated March 1, 2022, regarding the aforementioned events.

This is further confirmed in the same Guidelines of the EDPB regarding the application and imposition of administrative fines for the purposes of Regulation 2016/679, adopted on October 3, 2017, in which it is asserted that “That said, it would not be appropriate to additionally take into account the cooperation required by law; for example, in any case, the entity is required to allow the supervisory authority access to its premises to conduct audits or inspections.”  
Therefore, we can conclude that what is required or mandatory compliance by law for the data controller cannot be understood as “cooperation,” as occurred in this case.
