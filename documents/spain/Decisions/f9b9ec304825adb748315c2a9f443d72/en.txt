From the proceedings carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On 06/09/2022, a written submission was received by this Agency from A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant), in which a complaint is made against CAIXABANK, S.A. with NIF A08663619 (hereinafter, CAIXABANK), for a possible breach of the provisions of personal data protection regulations.  
The reasons on which the complaint is based are as follows:  
“[…]  
FIRST.- The undersigned entered into a mortgage loan with Caixabank in May 2006. Since the deed contains abusive clauses, two ordinary judicial proceedings have been filed against the bank, which are pending a final judgment. The borrower has been depositing amounts in the Court corresponding to the installments that have accrued on account of the loan since 2016. Of the two lawsuits, the first one dating back to 2016 is pending judgment in the Supreme Court. The second was filed last April and there is still no admission order.  
Caixabank, S.A. filed an enforcement proceeding against the debtor, disregarding the deposits made, which is pending an appeal in the Barcelona Court of Appeal.  
SECOND.- Caixabank, S.A. has transferred the credit and all personal data of the undersigned and her parents to the Irish fund, Promotora Yellowstone Designated Activity Company, without her consent, in violation of Article 20.2.b) of the Data Protection Act, as the debt is neither liquid, nor due, nor enforceable, as both the settlement of the loan and its amount are pending the decision of the courts.  
[…]”

The transfer, which was carried out in accordance with the regulations established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was recorded on 10/10/2022 as evidenced by the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  
On 11/10/2022, this Agency received a response letter in which the following is stated:  
“[…]  
1. The decision made regarding this claim. According to the provisions in the claim submitted by the client, he considers that CaixaBank has communicated his personal data and that of his parents to a third entity without his consent as a result of the assignment of the credit of which they were holders. In this regard, as indicated in the letter sent to the client on August 9, 2022, according to the provisions of Article 347 of the Commercial Code, the client's consent is not necessary for the assignment of the credit:  
“Article 347 Non-negotiable commercial credits or bearer credits may be transferred by the creditor without the need for the debtor's consent, it being sufficient to notify the debtor of the transfer. The debtor shall be obliged to the new creditor by virtue of the notification, and from the moment it takes place, no payment shall be deemed legitimate except that made to this creditor.”  
Thus, the only requirement for the assignment of the credit is the notification of such transfer. The notification was made through the aforementioned letter dated August 9, 2022, which the claimant himself submits to the procedure as Annex number 2.  
Therefore, it is concluded that the communication of the credit, as well as the communication of the debtors' data, is legitimate and lawful, so no specific decision is required within the framework of this claim.  
2. Report on the causes that have motivated the incident that originated the claim.  
The origin of the claim is the lack of knowledge on the part of the claimant of the regulations that empower CaixaBank to assign its credit without his consent. Therefore, since this is not an incident, it is not appropriate to prepare a report regarding the causes that have originated it.  
3. Report on the measures adopted to prevent similar incidents from occurring, implementation dates, and controls carried out to verify their effectiveness.  
In the same vein as the previous point, in this case, the reason for the claim is the lack of knowledge of the regulations that empower CaixaBank to assign the credit to a third entity without the consent of its holder. Therefore, since this is a lawful data processing that has been carried out correctly, the adoption of specific measures is not warranted.”

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence and applicable regulations  
In accordance with the functions conferred on each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f) and h) of the GDPR and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to resolve these investigative proceedings.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Legitimacy of the processing of personal data  
The principles governing the processing are enumerated in Article 5 of the GDPR. In this regard, paragraph 1 letter a) states: “Personal data shall be:  
a) Processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject (lawfulness, fairness, and transparency);

The principle of lawfulness is primarily regulated in Article 6 of the GDPR. The circumstances that allow for the lawful processing of personal data are enumerated in Article 6.1 of the GDPR:  
1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
   a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
   b) the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or for the implementation of pre-contractual measures taken at the request of the data subject;  
   c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject;  
   d) the processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
   e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller;  
   f) the processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in point f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.”  
In this regard, Recital 40 of the GDPR states that “For processing to be lawful, personal data must be processed with the consent of the data subject or on another legitimate basis laid down by law, whether in this Regulation or by virtue of other Union or Member State law to which this Regulation relates, including the necessity of complying with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject or the necessity of performing a contract to which the data subject is party or for the taking of steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract.”  
III  
Conclusion  
On 06/09/2022, a complaint was received by this Agency against CAIXABANK for having transferred personal data of the complainant and their parents to the entity PROMONTORIA YELLOWSTONE DESIGNATED ACTIVITY COMPANY, without their consent, as a result of the sale of the credit of which they were holders.  
The documentation provided in the file records the existence of a contractual relationship between the complainant and CAIXABANK by virtue of the Public Notarial Deed of granting an open credit with mortgage guarantee signed before the Notary of the Illustrious Notarial College of Catalonia, Mr. E.E.E., on 23/05/2006.  
Subsequently, CAIXABANK transferred the credit referred to in the previous paragraph to PROMONTORIA YELLOWSTONE, after signing a credit portfolio transfer contract, formalized on 27/07/2022. As a result of this transfer, and according to the notarized testimony of the Notary of the Illustrious College of Notaries of Madrid, Mr. D.D.D., the data communicated regarding the complainant and their parents were as follows:  
- Loan number: ***LOAN.1.  
- Holder: A.A.A., ***NIF.1.  
- Holder: B.B.B., ***NIF.2.  
- Holder: C.C.C., ***NIF.3.  
- Property Registry: ***PROPERTY.1.  
In this regard, CAIXABANK has informed that the transfer of the credit and, consequently, of the personal data, occurred based on the provisions of the Commercial Code, which in its Article 347 only requires that this action be communicated to the debtors, in this case, to the complainant and their parents. Thus, as evidenced in the file, on 09/08/2022, CAIXABANK along with PROMONTORIA YELLOWSTONE communicated to the affected parties the transfer made and its consequences, especially regarding the protection of personal data.  
The GDPR requires that the processing of personal data be lawful, which must be based on one of the legal bases listed in its Article 6.1. In light of the documentation in the file, it seems evident that the processing of personal data carried out by the entity PROMONTORIA YELLOWSTONE is covered by the circumstance detailed in point b) of the aforementioned provision. The transfer of the mentioned credit entails the subrogation of PROMONTORIA YELLOWSTONE in the rights and obligations of CAIXABANK towards the complainant; thus, the processing of the personal data of the complainant and their parents is necessary for the execution of the mortgage loan.  
Therefore, based on the information provided in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found to support the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, as the processing of personal data without a legitimate basis has not been substantiated.
