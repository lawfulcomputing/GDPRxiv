FACTS  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on September 27, 2023. The complaint is directed against LOS NIÑOS DE MONTESSORI, S.L. with NIF B88155734 (hereinafter, the respondent).  
In the received document, the responsible party for the website https://www.montessoritrescantos.com/ is reported for lacking a privacy policy when potentially collecting personal data through a contact form, and for installing non-exempt cookies without informing or obtaining the consent of users.  
Along with the notification, two screenshots are provided showing the contact page without a legal privacy notice and the main page of the cited website without a cookie notice.  

SECOND: On November 22, 2023, it was noted that the respondent entity offers educational immersion services in the English language, and that in the contact section of the reported website https://www.montessoritrescantos.com/contacto, there is a form to be filled out requiring personal data (name, surname, phone number, and email) to be able to contact, make inquiries, and/or reserve a spot, without including a legal notice or section regarding the privacy policy of the responsible entity. It is also verified that the main page does not have a banner or link regarding cookie notice through which users of the browser can provide their consent, and on the same date, a report on cookies is added to the file, which detects that the web address uses a total of 18 cookies.  

THIRD: According to the report collected from the AXESOR tool, the entity LOS NIÑOS DE MONTESSORI, S.L. is a microenterprise established in 2018, with a business volume of €313,772 in 2022.  

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence and applicable regulations  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  

II  
Preliminary issues  
Without prejudice to the liability that may arise from failing to comply with the obligations regarding the installation of cookies established by Law 34/2002 of July 11, on information society services and electronic commerce (hereinafter, LSSI) - which, if applicable, should be subject to independent processing - this procedure focuses on determining whether it is appropriate to attribute liability for the alleged non-compliance regarding the lack of a privacy policy on the page.  
The GDPR aims to guarantee the right to the protection of personal data of individuals. Article 4.1 of the GDPR defines "personal data" as: "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('the data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, a...

identifier online or one or several elements specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, psychological, economic, cultural, or social identity of that person;”.  
Article 4.2 of the GDPR defines “processing” as “any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure or destruction.”  
Finally, Article 4.7 of the GDPR considers the “data controller” to be: “the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; if the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the data controller or the specific criteria for its appointment may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.”  
Therefore, in accordance with the above definitions, it is evident that the website https://www.montessoritrescantos.com processes personal data of those individuals who fill out the contact form made available to interested parties both to contact the entity and to reserve a place in the English immersion school it manages, as well as to make an inquiry, since the following personal data is requested: name, email, and phone number. And in turn, there is no doubt that the controller of said website performs at least one operation considered as processing, which is the collection or recording of said data, determining the purposes and means thereof.  
There is no doubt, therefore, that the entity being claimed is subject to compliance with the current data protection regulations, which implies, among other things, that it is obliged to provide to the interested parties who fill out the form, at the moment prior to sending the information, the information required in the provisions set out below, which is commonly referred to as the entity's “privacy policy.”  
For all the above reasons, and without prejudice to what may be deduced from the instruction, LOS NIÑOS DE MONTESORI, S.L is the presumed responsible party to whom this sanctioning procedure must be addressed in accordance with the provisions of Article 70.1 a) of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the protection of personal data and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPPDD):

III  
Transparency and duty of information in the processing of personal data.  
The processing of any personal data must be governed by the principles outlined in Article 5 of the GDPR, among which the so-called Principle of Transparency provided in Article 5.1.a) can be highlighted, which states: “Personal data shall be:  
a) Processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject (‘lawfulness, fairness, and transparency’).”  
The development of the principle of transparency is specified in a series of information duties that the data controller must provide to each data subject, which are primarily regulated in Articles 12 to 14 of the GDPR. In this regard, Article 12 of the GDPR establishes that “1. The data controller shall take appropriate measures to provide the data subject with all the information referred to in Articles 13 and 14” and contains various rules regarding the “form” in which the information that is mandatory must and can be provided. The content of the information regarding the processing of data that must be provided to the data subject is regulated in the GDPR in Articles 13 and 14, which distinguish two scenarios: that the data is collected from the data subject (Article 13 GDPR) or obtained from another source (Article 14 GDPR).  
In particular, the obligation to provide the information contained in Article 13 of the GDPR regarding “Information to be provided when personal data is obtained from the data subject” is applicable to the present case, which establishes:  
“1. When personal data relating to a data subject is obtained from the data subject, the data controller shall provide the data subject with all the information indicated below at the time the data is obtained:  
a) The identity and contact details of the controller and, where applicable, of its representative;  
b) the contact details of the data protection officer, where applicable;  
c) the purposes of the processing for which the personal data are intended and the legal basis for the processing;  
d) when the processing is based on Article 6(1)(f), the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or a third party;  
e) the recipients or categories of recipients of the personal data, where applicable;  
f) where applicable, the intention of the controller to transfer personal data to a third country or international organization and the existence or absence of an adequacy decision by the Commission, or, in the case of transfers referred to in Articles 46 or 47 or Article 49,

In this regard, Recital 60 of the GDPR adds that "The principles of fair and transparent processing require that the data subject be informed of the existence of the processing operation and its purposes. The data controller must provide the data subject with as much supplementary information as necessary to ensure fair and transparent processing, taking into account the specific circumstances and context in which the personal data are processed. The data subject must also be informed of the existence of profiling and the consequences of such profiling. If the personal data are obtained from the data subjects, they must also be informed whether they are obliged to provide the data and of the consequences of failing to do so."  
In light of the documentation in the administrative file, it is evident that the website https://www.montessoritrescantos.com processes personal data of those who fill out the contact form made available to interested parties but does not provide any information regarding the processing of such personal data, as it lacks a privacy policy section that informs about the content established in the aforementioned Article 13 of the GDPR.  
Consequently, in accordance with the evidence available at this stage of the initiation agreement of the present sanctioning procedure, and without prejudice to what may result from the instruction, it is considered that this has incurred a violation of Article 13 of the GDPR that has been ongoing since August 23, 2019 (the date of the action taken during the first sanctioning procedure initiated for the same reason).  
IV  
Classification of the violation of Article 13 of the GDPR  
If confirmed, the aforementioned violation of Article 13 of the GDPR could constitute the commission of the violation classified in Article 83(5)(b) of the GDPR, which under the heading "General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines" states:  
"Violations of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an undertaking, an amount equivalent to 4% of the total annual global turnover of the preceding financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) The rights of data subjects under Articles 12 to 22;"  
For the mere purposes of prescription, Article 72(1)(h) of the LOPDGDD classifies as very serious "The omission of the duty to inform the affected party about the processing of their personal data in accordance with the provisions of Articles 13 and 14 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and 12 of this Organic Law." The limitation period for very serious violations provided for in Organic Law 3/2018 is three years.

VI  
Adoption of corrective measures.  
If the infringement is confirmed, it may be agreed to impose on the controller the adoption of appropriate measures to adjust its actions to the regulations mentioned in this act, in accordance with the provisions of the aforementioned Article 58.2 d) of the GDPR, according to which each supervisory authority may “order the controller or processor to ensure that the processing operations comply with the provisions of this Regulation, where appropriate, in a specific manner and within a specified period…”.  

The imposition of this measure is compatible with the sanction consisting of an administrative fine, as provided in Article 83.2 of the GDPR.  
In such a case, in the resolution adopted, this Agency may require the entity being claimed against to demonstrate within 1 month to this body that it has proceeded to introduce on its website (www.montessoritrescantos.com) a banner or link to the “privacy policy,” where all aspects regulated in Article 13 of the GDPR are informed to those interested in completing the contact form on its website.  
It is warned that failure to comply with the possible order to adopt measures imposed by this body in the sanctioning resolution may be considered an administrative infringement in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, classified as an infringement in its Articles 83.5 and 83.6, which may motivate such conduct to open a further administrative sanctioning procedure.
