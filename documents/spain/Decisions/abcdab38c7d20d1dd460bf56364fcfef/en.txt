LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  

II  
An image is a personal data  
The physical image of a person, according to Article 4.1 of the GDPR, is personal data and its protection is therefore subject to this Regulation. Article 4.2 of the GDPR defines the concept of "processing" of personal data.  
Images generated by a system of cameras or video cameras are personal data, so their processing is subject to data protection regulations.  
It is therefore pertinent to analyze whether the processing of personal data (image of individuals) carried out through the reported video surveillance system complies with the provisions of the GDPR.  

III  
In this case, the complaint dated 07/11/22 is examined, through which the alleged irregularity committed by an owner of the property "who has installed a video surveillance system without authorization for it" is reported.  
The cameras installed on the exterior of the homes, aside from the implications they may have within the framework of the current Horizontal Property Law (LPH), may have repercussions on data protection regulation, as there is an undetermined processing of third-party data affecting common areas.  
The installation of these must be communicated to the entire community of neighbors, included in the corresponding agenda item, and the presence of these must be authorized by the entire community of owners.  
Without the corresponding authorization, the presence of these lacks a legitimizing basis for the processing of data under the current data protection regulations, such that such conduct would infringe the applicable regulations.  
The legitimizing bases are a list of situations or specific assumptions established by law, in which it is possible to process personal data.  
Article 6, section 1, letter e) of the GDPR states:  
“e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
Once authorized, the presence of the cameras must be duly signposted, without prejudice to the obligation of the installation responsible to comply with the other legally required obligations (e.g., informing that it is a video-surveilled area, etc.).  
For the installation of surveillance services in a community of owners, the votes in favor of 3/5 of the total owners are needed, which must also represent at least 3/5 of the participation quotas, without prejudice to what may be stated in the Statutes of the community.  
The purpose of these types of devices is to protect the home and its belongings, with some owners considering that their presence outside serves as a deterrent against possible thefts, although they do not take into account the other affected rights or the inconveniences for the other co-owners who feel intimidated by their mere presence or the implications of being placed on a common wall.  
The person responsible for the installation of any type of "device" that proceeds to obtain images of third parties (data) must be able to demonstrate convincingly that it strictly complies with the requirements set by the Legislator, which must be analyzed by this agency in the context of the specific case.  
Consequently, the first thing to do before starting to process personal data is to identify the legitimizing basis because, if it does not exist, the processing is unlawful regardless of whether the other regulatory requirements are met.  
The lack of the aforementioned authorization leads to the commission of the described infringement, as it lacks the <legitimizing basis> that allows it to install a device for capturing images (personal data), given that, as we recall, the camera installed externally affects an area owned by the Community of owners, hence it is also considered that there are less invasive measures, such as placing it on a common wall.

IV  
In accordance with the evidence available in this sanctioning procedure, it is considered that the respondent has a video surveillance camera system that affects the rights of third parties without justified cause, as they are disproportionately oriented towards community areas without being duly authorized by the collective of property owners of the building.  
Likewise, conducting a presumed assessment of the rights at stake, it is noted that cameras inside the home are also effective in protecting against potential burglaries, preventing intimidation and disturbance caused by their presence to the neighbors, who feel their personal freedom affected as it impacts community areas.  
The known facts are therefore constitutive of an infringement, attributable to the respondent, for violating the content of Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR.  
Article 72, section 1, letter a) establishes a statute of limitations of three years for “infringements that constitute a substantial violation of the articles mentioned therein and, in particular, the following: a) The processing of personal data in violation of the principles and guarantees established in Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.”  
V  
From the initial evidence provided, the presence of an informative sign indicating that it is a video-surveillance area has not been confirmed, reminding of the necessity of such a sign if the cameras are located outside the building.  
Article 22, section 4 of the LOPDGDD states: “The information duty provided for in Article 12 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 shall be deemed fulfilled by placing an informative device in a sufficiently visible location identifying, at least, the existence of the processing, the identity of the controller, and the possibility of exercising the rights provided for in Articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679. An access code or internet address to this information may also be included in the informative device.”  
The aforementioned facts imply an infringement of the content of Article 13 of the GDPR, due to the lack of informative sign(s) with an effective address to which one could direct inquiries or indicate, if applicable, the main responsible party for the processing of the data.  
Article 13 of the GDPR “Information to be provided when personal data are obtained from the data subject”  
1. When personal data relating to a data subject are obtained from that data subject, the data controller shall provide the data subject with all the information indicated below at the time of obtaining the data: a) the identity and contact details of the controller and, where applicable, of its representative; b) the contact details of the data protection officer, where applicable; c) the purposes of the processing for which the personal data are intended and the legal basis for the processing (...).  
Article 72, section 1 of the LOPDGDD (LO 3/2018, December 5) regarding the statute of limitations for very serious infringements “shall prescribe after three years” and in particular the following:  
h) The omission of the duty to inform the affected party about the processing of their personal data in accordance with the provisions of Articles 13 and 14 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and Article 12 of this organic law.  
The duty to inform the affected parties as provided in Articles 12 and 13 of the GDPR, and Article 22 of the LOPDGDD, must be fulfilled as already indicated.  
VI  
Article 83.5 of the GDPR states the following: “Infringements of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an undertaking, an amount equivalent to up to 4% of the total annual global turnover of the preceding financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) The basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9 (...).  
b) The rights of the data subjects under Articles 12 to 22 (...).  
In motivating the sanction, the lack of initial allegations in response to the request from this body is taken into account, although the evidence provided confirms the presence of the device(s) with poor orientation, as well as the absence of informative sign(s), which denotes serious negligence in the described conduct, as well as failure to comply with the requirements of the governing bodies of the building; therefore, a sanction of €600 (300€ + 300€) is agreed upon for the infringement of Articles 6 and 13 of the GDPR, a sanction situated at the lower end of the scale for such behaviors.  
VII  
The text of the resolution establishes what the committed infringements have been and the facts that have led to the violation of data protection regulations, from which it is clearly inferred what measures are to be adopted, without prejudice to the type of procedures, mechanisms, or specific instruments for implementing them being the responsibility of the sanctioned party, as it is the data controller who fully knows their organization and must decide, based on proactive responsibility and a risk-based approach, how to comply with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.  
It is warned that failure to comply with the order to adopt measures imposed by this body in the sanctioning resolution may be considered an administrative infringement in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, classified as an infringement in its article.
