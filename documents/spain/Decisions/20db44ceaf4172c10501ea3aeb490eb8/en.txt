LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  
II  
Preliminary issue  
Article 58 of the GDPR, Powers, states:  
“2. Each supervisory authority shall have all of the following corrective powers:  
“(…)  
d) to order the controller or processor to bring processing operations into compliance with the provisions of this Regulation, when appropriate, in a specific manner and within a specified time;  
(…)  
i) to impose an administrative fine in accordance with Article 83, in addition to or instead of the measures mentioned in this paragraph, depending on the circumstances of each particular case;  
(…)”  
III  
Obligation breached: Article 5.1.d) GDPR  
The GDPR addresses in its Article 5 the principles that must govern the processing of personal data and mentions among them the principle of accuracy, stating that:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
(…)  
d) accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; every reasonable step must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate with respect to the purposes for which they are processed are erased or rectified without delay (‘accuracy’);  
(…)”  
What is stated in the aforementioned regulation must be related to Article 5, Accuracy of data, of the new Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), which establishes in its section 1 that:  
“1. In accordance with Article 5.1.d) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the data shall be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date.  
2. a) have been obtained by the controller directly from the affected party.  
b) have been obtained by the controller from a mediator or intermediary in cases where the applicable regulations in the sector to which the controller belongs established the possibility of intervention by a mediator or intermediary who collects the data on their own behalf for transmission to the controller. 1. The respondent has reiterated their disagreement with the proposed sanction, arguing that the inaccuracy of the personal data obtained directly from the affected party cannot be attributed to the data controller, as the person who contracted was the one who provided the CUPS number, stating that it had been provided by the distributor and that the distributor did not reject the switching despite being the only one with the data of the supply point, including the ID of the holder.  
However, such a statement cannot be accepted, since even assuming that another person contracted the service on behalf of the claimant, a representation that has not been substantiated, the responsibility for the events falls on the respondent for incurring in a serious lack of diligence in the processing carried out.  
It is reiterated to the respondent that, as the incoming marketer and agent of their client, they requested a Change of Marketer from the distributor without modifications to the access contract (format C1), which was rejected by the distributor, raising objections to the aforementioned request on the grounds that the NIF did not match the one existing in the contract that was in force.  
Therefore, it is not true what the respondent claims when they state that the distributor did not reject the switching despite being the only one with the data of the supply point, including the ID of its holder; in this case, the ID of the claimant was included in the change request, and due to the discrepancy with the existing data, the distributor expressed their objection by rejecting the request.

The respondent has alleged the inadequate grading of the sanction imposed in the proposal, given the mitigating circumstances of responsibility that have not been taken into account to reduce the sanction imposed by the AEPD. VI  
Qualification and Analysis  
1. From the documentation in the file, it is evident that the respondent has violated Article 5.1.d), principle of accuracy, in relation to Article 5 of the LOPGDD, Accuracy of data, since, after the request for a change of marketer was rejected, the respondent did not carry out any activity to verify the accuracy of the data or confirm it with the interested party before requesting the change of ownership.  
In relation to Article 5.1.d) and its update (which would be the manifestation of the principle of accuracy that could be understood as breached), it requires that “reasonable measures be taken to ensure that inaccurate personal data are erased or rectified without delay with respect to the purposes for which they are processed.”  
In this sense, the Audiencia Nacional stated in its ruling of 27/02/2008, Appeal 210/2007, “… The principle of truthfulness or accuracy is of great relevance, as it is not only necessary that the data be collected for processing according to a series of criteria (principle of proportionality) and that...
13/22  
they are used for purposes compatible with those that motivated the collection (principle of purpose), but it also requires that those who collect and process personal data guarantee and protect that the information being processed is not inaccurate and is kept up to date.  
The failure or violation of the principle of accuracy can have significant consequences for the affected party…>>. In relation to the aforementioned article and its update (which would be the manifestation of the principle of accuracy that could be understood as violated), it requires that “reasonable measures are adopted to ensure that inaccurate personal data is erased or rectified without delay concerning the purposes for which it is processed.”  
That is to say, Article 5.1.d) does not impose disproportionate measures to update the data, but rather reasonable ones, taking into account the available means and the purpose for which the data is used. This is also expressed in Recital 39 of the GDPR.  
2. It should be noted that the customer or consumer wishing to receive electrical energy at their home needs to be the holder of two differentiated contracts concerning their supply point (CUPS):  
a) The supply contract, which is signed between a consumer and an electricity marketing company.  
b) The access contract to the distribution network that the consumer signs with the distribution company, with the intermediation as a representative of the marketing company with which they have contracted the purchase of electrical energy.  
When a customer wishes to contract electricity for a supply point or make any contractual modification, they approach a marketing company, which, on their behalf and as their representative, contracts access to the distribution network.  
Any contractual modification requested by a marketer from a distributor is carried out through digital requests complying with the exchange formats between agents established by the CNMC, under the Resolution of 17/12/2019, which approves new formats for the information exchange files between distributors and marketers and modifies the Resolution of 20/12/2016.  
In a case such as the one being analyzed, where a change of marketer was requested with a simultaneous change of holder, especially after the first request for a change of marketer was rejected due to a change of marketer without modifications to the access contract, it requires that the entity not only comply with the specific regulations of the electricity sector but also carry out a minimum due diligence to ensure that the documentation and the person attempting to contract is who they claim to be and to effectively manage the requested change.
