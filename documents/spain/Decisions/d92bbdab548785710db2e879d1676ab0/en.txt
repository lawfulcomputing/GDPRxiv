From the proceedings carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On October 27, 2022, a complaint was filed with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against the AYUNTAMIENTO DE MADRID with NIF P2807900B (hereinafter, the complained party, Ayuntamiento de Madrid, or the Ayuntamiento). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The complainant states that they were reported by the Ayuntamiento de Madrid for committing two traffic violations on the M30 communication route (Calle 30). Upon being notified of the complaint, they observed that their image had been processed by an automated image capture system linked to a speed measurement system (radar).  
According to the complainant, the radar that captured their image does not comply with the parameters established in the Regulation for the development and execution of Organic Law 4/1997, of August 4, which regulates the use of video cameras by the Security Forces and Corps in public places. In this regard, they allege that the sole additional provision of the aforementioned Regulation establishes the authorization by the competent Public Administration for the installation and use of the devices.  
The radar was installed by the General Directorate of Traffic when the road was within its scope of competencies. In 2004, the competence was transferred to the Ayuntamiento de Madrid.  
The complainant requested from the complained party and the General Directorate of Traffic the resolution that enables the installation of the device. This resolution has not been provided to the complainant; for this reason, they conclude that there are reasonable indications that the complained Administration has processed their personal data through an automated image capture system linked to a speed radar, violating the obligation to have prior administrative authorization.  
Furthermore, they conclude that the complained party has violated Articles 5 and 6 of the GDPR.  
Along with their complaint letter dated October 27, 2022, they provide, in addition to the two traffic violation reports from the Ayuntamiento de Madrid, the following documents:

II  
Legitimacy of the processing  
Article 6 of the GDPR, which regulates the legality of processing, establishes the following:  
"1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or for the implementation at the request of the data subject of pre-contractual measures;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in paragraph f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks."

4. When processing for a purpose other than that for which the personal data were collected is not based on the consent of the data subject or on Union law or the law of the Member States that constitutes a necessary and proportionate measure in a democratic society to safeguard the objectives set out in Article 23, paragraph 1, the data controller, in order to determine whether the processing for another purpose is compatible with the purpose for which the personal data were initially collected, shall take into account, among other things:  
a) any relationship between the purposes for which the personal data were collected and the purposes of the intended further processing;  
b) the context in which the personal data were collected, in particular regarding the relationship between the data subjects and the data controller;  
c) the nature of the personal data, in particular when special categories of personal data are processed, in accordance with Article 9, or personal data related to criminal convictions and offenses, in accordance with Article 10;  
d) the possible consequences for the data subjects of the intended further processing;  
e) the existence of appropriate safeguards, which may include encryption or pseudonymization."  
III  
Conclusion  
The Eighth Additional Provision of Organic Law 4/1997, of August 4, regulating the use of video cameras by the Security Forces and Corps in public places, establishes:  
"The installation and use of video cameras and any other means of capturing and reproducing images for the control, regulation, surveillance, and discipline of traffic shall be carried out by the authority responsible for traffic regulation for the purposes provided in the articulated text of the Law on Traffic, Circulation of Motor Vehicles, and Road Safety, approved by Royal Legislative Decree 339/1990, of March 2, and other specific regulations in the matter, and subject to the provisions of Organic Laws 5/1992, of October 29, on the Regulation of Automated Processing of Personal Data, and 1/1982, of May 5, on Civil Protection of the Right to Honor, Personal and Family Privacy, and One's Own Image, within the framework of the principles of use of the same provided in this Law."  
Likewise, the sole additional provision of Royal Decree 596/1999, of April 16, approving the Regulation for the development and execution of Organic Law 4/1997, of August 4, regulating the use of video cameras by the Security Forces and Corps in public places, provides:  
"Applicable regime for video cameras for the surveillance, control, and discipline of traffic"  
1. The installation and use of video cameras and any other means of capturing and reproducing images for the control, regulation, surveillance, and discipline of traffic on public roads shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of the eighth additional provision of Organic Law 4/1997 and this provision.  
2. It shall be the responsibility of the public administrations with competence for traffic regulation to authorize the installation and use of the devices referred to in the previous paragraph.

For its part, the Sustainable Mobility Ordinance of the City Council of Madrid (hereinafter, OMS) establishes the following:  
The third additional provision of the OMS, relating to the Protection of personal data, provides:  
“Protection of personal data.  
Within the framework of this ordinance, special protection shall be given to personal data contained in the information that the City Council of Madrid and its linked or dependent public bodies obtain and use, ensuring, in all cases, the rights inherent to the protection of personal data, for which security measures will be established to prevent any personal traceability not covered by the purpose or consent.  
In general, compliance with Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council and Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and guarantee of digital rights shall be observed, especially regarding the principles of data protection, legitimization of processing, rights of affected individuals, data protection by design and by default, data processors, security breaches, and security measures in accordance with the National Security Scheme (ENS) resulting from the corresponding risk analyses and, where applicable, impact assessments.”  
Article 13, which regulates automated means of traffic management, states:  
“Article 13. Automated means of traffic management.  
1. The City Council of Madrid shall employ technological means both to collect real-time information on pedestrian traffic and the circulation of all types of vehicles and to manage urban mobility in a safer, more efficient, sustainable, and intelligent manner.  
2. The Mobility Management Center (CGM) implements the technological control of information panels, cameras, loops, sensors, and other technical elements and functionalities that allow for the development of science and technology.”  
Article 15, relating to technical means of monitoring traffic and parking, establishes:  
“Article 15. Technical means of monitoring traffic and parking.  
1. The City Council of Madrid may employ automated technical means for monitoring traffic and parking, as well as for the automated reporting of violations committed using, among others, the following technical means defined in Annex VI (…)  
2. The images, and where applicable videos, captured by these means shall be used as evidence in the automated or personal reporting of violations.  
3. The location of fixed means shall be indicated through signage and on the municipal website. Mobile means, by their very nature, do not require signage, without prejudice to the possibility of using variable message panels or other means to inform about areas where these controls occur frequently.”  
Consequently, no evidence has been found to substantiate the existence of an infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR or any other infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
All of this is without prejudice to any subsequent actions that this Agency may carry out, exercising the investigative and corrective powers it holds.
