BACKGROUND  
FIRST:  
On March 18, 2022, the Division of Technological Innovation of this Spanish Agency for Data Protection (hereinafter AEPD or the Agency) was notified of a security breach of personal data reported by I-DE REDES ELÉCTRICAS INTELIGENTES, S.A.U. with NIF A95075578 (hereinafter, I-DE) as the data controller, in which it informs this Agency of the following:  
On the afternoon of March 15, 2022, an attack against the management website for connections (GEA) of I-DE was detected. (…). At this moment, no impact on personal data is identified. The next day, March 16, a brute force attack directed against the same target (GEA) as the incident the previous day is detected. Measures are taken to repel it. On March 17, GEA is reopened, the activity log is analyzed, and it is concluded that there has been an extraction of personal data. It is indicated that the number of affected individuals is 4.5 million customers of this company.  

SECOND:  
On March 29, 2022, I-DE submitted a new notification expanding the information regarding the security breach reported on the 18th of the same month, indicating that, after a forensic analysis of the incident, the number of its customers whose data has been affected is 1.35 million and that it is also likely that there are affected data from customers of other companies in the Iberdrola group, as the attacker could potentially have surpassed the security conditions of the information exclusive to I-DE, accessing information ranges of other companies, which has already been communicated to the Company's Systems management for a detailed analysis of other impacts on other companies or businesses of the Iberdrola group.  
They also indicate that the exact date of the start of the breach is March 7, 2022, and inform that the breach has not yet been communicated to the affected individuals and that, at the latest, they will be informed by March 31, 2022.

Regulatory Framework  
- The regulatory framework of the electricity sector, Law 54/1997, of November 27, on the Electricity Sector, imposes an obligation of total separation between regulated activities, such as distribution, and liberalized activities, such as commercialization.  
- The right of consumers of electrical energy to access and connect to the electricity transport and distribution networks in Spanish territory is specifically included in Law 24/2013, of December 26, on the Electricity Sector.  
The distribution companies and the commercialization companies are two distinct entities within the Electricity Sector. In this sense, Law 24/2013, of December 26, on the Electricity Sector defines them as different subjects.  
- According to the regulation of the electricity sector, the consumer, in order to receive electrical energy at their home, needs to be the holder of two differentiated contracts related to their supply point (CUPS):  
   On one hand, the energy purchase contract, “supply contract,” which is signed between a consumer and an electricity commercialization company.  
  Although it is also possible for the consumer to purchase electricity directly in the market, without the need for a commercialization company, this is not typical for individual consumers but rather for large electricity-consuming companies, as indicated by I-DE, IBERCLI, and CURENERGÍA in their response.  
   On the other hand, the network access or distribution or transport contract, “ATR contract,” which the consumer signs with the intermediation as a representative of the commercialization company with which they have contracted the purchase of electrical energy.  
  Although it can also be signed directly with the network owner, this is not typical for individual consumers but rather for large electricity-consuming companies, as indicated by I-DE, IBERCLI, and CURENERGÍA in their response.  
- When a customer wishes to contract electricity at a supply point or make any contractual modification, that customer approaches a commercialization company, which, on behalf of the customer and as their representative, contracts the ATR contract, the network access contract.  
Any contractual modification requested by a commercialization company from a distributor is carried out through digital XML requests, complying with the exchange formats between agents established by the National Commission of Markets and Competition (CNMC), by virtue of the Resolution of December 20, 2016, which approves the formats of the information exchange files between electricity and natural gas distributors and marketers, and the Resolution of December 17, 2019, which approves new formats of the information exchange files between distributors and marketers and modifies the Resolution of December 20, 2016.  
Taking the above into account:  
- I-DE, the electricity distributor of the Iberdrola group, states that it can only access the data of its customers, that is, the users of the electricity service whose supply point is within the network managed by it as a distributor, and not those managed by other distribution companies.

ELEVENTH: On May 24, 2023, IBERDROLA submitted a written request for the accumulation of the present file with EXP202205206, as well as the suspension of the deadline for the submission of allegations until this request is resolved, stating the following:  
IBERDROLA understands that the facts that serve as the basis for the exercise of the sanctioning power that the Agency seeks to exercise are or have a single basis that affects the two sanctioning files that have been opened as differentiated, which is why it requests the accumulation of both sanctioning procedures, understanding that there is a necessary connectivity between them, that is, it is the same situation that may result in the responsibility of both. IBERDROLA therefore understands that the terms of such responsibility, total, partial, as an author, as a collaborator, or any other that may arise from the criminal references can only be appreciated if the procedure is analyzed as a whole.  
IBERDROLA argues that the lack of accumulation in this case could imply a double imputation to two entities of the same facts, which, moreover, belong to the same business group, specifically the Iberdrola group of which IBERDROLA is the parent company.  
If both files are not accumulated, IBERDROLA states that it would prevent clarifying the degree of responsibility of each of them, as the facts would be analyzed separately and without assessing the alleged simultaneous action, in terms of responsibility, of the two entities against which both procedures are directed. In this way, a double imputation of the same facts to both entities could be occurring without assessing whether it is shared or whether the separate sanctioning reproach against both should not be subject to reduction as a consequence of that alleged concurrence of responsibility. This limits, in the terms established in the constitutional jurisprudence that is reproduced below, the right to defense of IBERDROLA, as it cannot analyze the concurrent circumstances in the case in a unified manner due to the fragmentation caused by the opening of two differentiated procedures.  

IBERDROLA understands that the prerequisites established in Article 57 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP) that justify the accumulation of the procedures, as well as the individualization of the relevance of its application to the present case, are met:  
A) Existence of "intimate connection" or "substantial identity".  
IBERDROLA points out that, in the present case, on March 18, 2022, a breach of personal data security occurred, initially reported by I-DE. It is this same security breach that determines the opening of the present

proceeding in which responsibility is attributed to IBERDROLA, as well as that of the proceeding which is intended to be accumulated with the present one, opened to clarify the responsibility of I-DE.  
IBERDROLA indicates that the connectivity, in this case, derives from the fact that it concerns the attribution of responsibility to two legal entities, but for the same fact: it is the Agency itself that makes it clear that it is a single security breach and that the subjective responsibilities of IBERDROLA, in the present proceeding, and of I-DE in the proceeding whose accumulation is requested, will be based on the same.  
Therefore, IBERDROLA concludes that, given the existence of unique facts for which responsibility is attributed to both it and I-DE, it is evident that a joint assessment of the same is necessary to determine whether there is joint or separate responsibility of both entities, as well as whether the responsibility would be under different titles in each case.  
B) That the same body is responsible for the processing and resolution of the proceeding.  
IBERDROLA points out that, along with the previous requirement, the LPACAP imposes respect for the general principle of competence of the body that must issue the resolution, a requirement that is met in the present case, since the Law assigns the competence for the processing of both proceedings to a single sanctioning body, so that with the accumulation, that competence is neither lost nor blurred as a result of the potential existence of different instructing bodies.  
In IBERDROLA's opinion, the essential effect of the accumulation of files is that all issues to be resolved must be examined in a single proceeding and decided in a single final act that jointly assesses the responsibilities of all those involved.  
IBERDROLA notes that the scheme just analyzed has, without a doubt, special characteristics in the sanctioning field due to its own structure and the value judgment that it entails.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
According to the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Preliminary Issues  
In this case, according to the provisions of Article 4.1 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, given that IBERDROLA carries out, among other processing activities, the collection, storage, consultation, use, deletion, etc., of personal data of individuals, such as: name, surname, ID number, postal address, telephone number, email address, banking data, data related to electricity supply and consumption, bank account, etc.  
IBERDROLA carries out this activity in its capacity as data processor, in accordance with Article 4.8 of the GDPR, since it processes this personal data on behalf of other companies belonging to the Iberdrola Group to which it provides, among other services, Support and Maintenance of IT Infrastructures and Application Development (SWF). Specifically, in the case at hand, it provides these services as a data processor, among others, for I-DE, CURENERGÍA, and IBERCLI.

www.aepd.es  
28001 – Madrid

Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines "personal data security breaches" (hereinafter referred to as security breach) as "all breaches of security that result in the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or unauthorized communication or access to such data."  
In this case, there is a personal data security breach under the circumstances mentioned above, categorized as a confidentiality breach, due to a cyber attack on a web application of one of the companies in the Iberdrola Group, which resulted in unauthorized access by a third party to personal data of two other companies in the same Group that were stored in the shared database managed and administered by IBERDROLA. Therefore, the security breach suffered has affected personal data processed by IBERDROLA in its capacity as data processor.  

III  
On the request for accumulation and the suspension of the deadline for submitting allegations  
Regarding the request for the accumulation of this file and EXP202205206 made by IBERDROLA, it should be noted that Article 57 of the LPACAP establishes:  

"The administrative body that initiates or processes a procedure, regardless of the manner of its initiation, may, ex officio or at the request of a party, accumulate it with others that have substantial identity or intimate connection, provided that it is the same body that must process and resolve the procedure.  
No appeal shall proceed against the accumulation agreement."  
(the underlining is ours)  
Therefore, it is a possibility available to the Administration, which is not obliged to proceed with the accumulation if requested. However, this does not prevent the subsequent motivation of the reasons why it has been deemed appropriate to process both sanctioning procedures separately.  
Thus, although the two sanctioning files, one directed against I-DE and the other against IBERDROLA, S.A., stem from the same security incident (the attack on the GEA application, a web application of I-DE), it has resulted in two different and distinct personal data breaches, as reflected in the Background Facts of this proposal, especially in Background Fact Eight, where the information gathered during the preliminary investigation phase carried out by this AEPD is detailed.  
Thus, on one hand, the attack occurred through a web application of I-DE, exploiting a vulnerability that allowed access to the I-DE database and affected the confidentiality of 1,350,000 I-DE customers. Therefore, the sanctioning procedure related to EXP202205206 is directed exclusively...

sedeagpd.gob.es

a I-DE as the data controller of its clients' personal data and as a consequence of an existing vulnerability in one of its web applications.  
On the other hand, the cyberattack not only affected personal data of I-DE, but also, by accessing I-DE's database, which was hosted on a system where databases of other companies in the same group coexist, the attacker, taking advantage of vulnerabilities in the databases, managed to access the databases of two other companies, IBERCLI and CURENERGÍA, affecting the confidentiality of personal data of clients of these two latter companies. These different databases of different companies are hosted or operated on a system maintained and supported exclusively by IBERDROLA, which, consequently, is responsible for processing all of them, that is, of I-DE, IBERCLI, and CURENERGÍA.  
This fact has led to the initiation of the present sanctioning procedure against IBERDROLA, but for its responsibility as the processor of IBERCLI and CURENERGÍA and exclusively for the breach of personal data that has only affected the personal data of the clients of these two marketing companies, and only considering the responsibility that IBERDROLA may have regarding the configuration of the database it manages concerning these two affected companies.  
In this regard, the impact on personal data of clients hosted in I-DE's databases cannot be part of this sanctioning procedure directed exclusively at IBERDROLA, since I-DE is not responsible for the personal data of affected clients belonging to other companies, nor for the possible failure to adopt appropriate measures to protect the confidentiality of the personal data of other companies hosted in a database managed by IBERDROLA as the processor.  
Therefore, the management of the databases carried out by IBERDROLA regarding those third companies must be analyzed independently, without I-DE being held accountable for possible breaches of data protection regulations in that management.  
Thus, since the sanctioning procedures are directed at different subjects (two different companies), the personal data of clients of different companies are affected, and I-DE has nothing to do with the data of external clients, being processed for vulnerabilities or breaches regarding different systems (in one a web application of I-DE, in this one, a database managed by IBERDROLA), etc., it is for this reason that this AEPD has not considered accumulating the two files, but rather processing the two sanctioning procedures separately, as the responsibility attributed to each is clearly separated, as well as dealing with different personal data breaches affecting personal data processed by different controllers.  
Furthermore, this does not leave IBERDROLA defenseless since at all times it is aware of the facts attributed to it, the infringement that they imply, the responsibility it has incurred, as well as that it has had and has the opportunity to make allegations and present any documentation it deems appropriate in defense of its interests as permitted by the applicable legislation.

Finally, regarding the request for suspension of the deadline to submit allegations to the Initiation Agreement until a decision is made on the accumulation of the two proceedings, it is stated that such a possibility does not exist either in the applicable data protection regulations (GDPR and LOPDGDD) or in the LPACAP. On the contrary, this latter law establishes the obligation that the procedures to be completed by the interested parties are mandatory:  
“Article 73. Compliance with procedures.  
1. The procedures that must be completed by the interested parties must be carried out within ten days from the day following the notification of the corresponding act, unless a different deadline is set in the corresponding regulation.”  
Therefore, the request for suspension is not appropriate, as this possibility does not legally exist, nor has it had any effect, and consequently, the deadline for submitting allegations has not been suspended.

In this system, what is decisive in the GDPR is not the fines. The corrective powers of the supervisory authorities provided for in Article 58.2 of the GDPR combined with the provisions of Article 83 of the GDPR show the prevalence of corrective measures over fines.

IBERDROLA again indicates that the principle of non bis in idem has been violated in the imposition of the two infringements, as it understands that the AEPD is not judging the violation of article 5.1.f) of the GDPR for a reason different from that derived from what it considers to be inadequate security of personal data, but solely and exclusively for that reason.

“(...) According to the legislation and jurisprudence presented, the principle of non bis in idem prevents punishing the same individual twice for the same act based on the same grounds, understood as the same legal interest protected by the sanctioning norms in question. Indeed, when there is a triple identity of subject, act, and grounds, the accumulation of sanctions creates a penalty that is foreign to the proportionality assessment carried out by the legislator and materializes the imposition of a sanction not legally provided for, which also violates the principle of proportionality.  
However, for "bis in idem" to be applicable, there must be a triple identity among the compared terms: objective (same facts), subjective (against the same subjects), and causal (for the same grounds or reason for punishment):  
a) The subjective identity implies that the affected subject must be the same, regardless of the nature or judicial or administrative authority that judges and irrespective of who the accuser or specific body that has resolved the matter is, whether judged alone or in conjunction with others affected.  
b) The factual identity implies that the judged facts must be the same, and excludes cases of real concurrence of infractions where there is not a single unlawful act but several.  
c) The identity of grounds or causal identity implies that the sanctioning measures cannot coexist if they respond to the same nature, that is, if they share the same teleological foundation, which occurs between penal and administrative sanctions, but not between punitive and merely coercive measures.”  
Taking into account what has been previously stated, in the sanctioning procedure, the principle of non bis in idem has not been violated, since, although broadly understood, the facts are detected as a consequence of a personal data breach, the infringement of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR is specified in a clear loss of confidentiality that affected certain clients, while the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR is reduced to the deficiency of the security measures (only security) detected, which are present independently of the personal data breach. In fact, if these security measures that IBERDROLA had in place had been detected by the AEPD without the loss of confidentiality occurring, it would only have been sanctioned under Article 32 of the GDPR.  
As we have indicated, Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR sanctions a loss of confidentiality and availability, while Article 32 of the GDPR addresses the deficiency of the security measures implemented by the data controller. Deficient security measures, we add, that infringe the GDPR, regardless of whether or not a personal data breach has occurred.  
Article 32 of the GDPR is violated regardless of whether or not a personal data breach occurs. That is to say, it is infringed by not having appropriate measures to ensure adequate security in the processing of data without it being necessary or essential for a personal data security breach to occur, which, in its case, may affect either the confidentiality of the data, or only the availability, or only the integrity, or some or all of them.

Another matter is that the deficiency in security measures is manifested, in the specific case, during a violation of personal data security (breach of confidentiality in this case), as has occurred in the present case.  
  
On the other hand, Article 5.1(f) of the GDPR is violated when there is a loss of confidentiality or integrity of personal data, which may or may not occur due to the absence or deficiency of security measures. This principle merely determines the means through which the maintenance of confidentiality, integrity, or availability can be achieved when it explicitly states "through the application of appropriate technical and organizational measures," which are not strictly security measures. It is also emphasized again that Article 5.1(f) of the GDPR is one of the principles related to processing. The principles related to processing are, on one hand, the starting point and the closing clause of the legal framework for data protection, constituting true guiding rules of the system with an intense expansive force; on the other hand, having a high level of specificity, they are mandatory norms that can be infringed.  
The breach of confidentiality attributed to I-DE is due to non-compliance with the obligation imposed in Article 5.1(f) to process data in such a way as to ensure an adequate level of security, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing, through the application of appropriate technical or organizational measures.

• The principle of proportionality is fully regulated in Article 83 of the RGPD.  
• There is no legal gap.

• In the procedures processed by the AEPD, for the administrative procedures processed, the subsidiary application of the general rules on administrative procedures is provided.  
• In the procedures processed by the AEPD, for the administrative procedures processed and not in relation to the principles of the sanctioning procedure, the LOPDGDD does not establish a subsidiary application of the LRJSP.

VI  
Integrity and Confidentiality  
Article 5.1.f) "Principles relating to processing" of the GDPR establishes:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
(...)  
f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, by means of the application of appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).”  
The principle of integrity and confidentiality of data requires a guarantee of security in the application of technical or organizational measures that prevent the alteration of personal data, its loss, unauthorized or unlawful processing or access. The existence of this fundamental right is not possible if confidentiality, integrity, and availability are not guaranteed.  
Hence, the integrity and confidentiality of personal data are considered essential to prevent data subjects from suffering negative effects. Therefore, they must be processed in a manner that ensures adequate integrity and confidentiality of personal data, especially to prevent unauthorized access, processing, or use of such data.  
Ultimately, both the data controller and the processor have the obligation to integrate the necessary guarantees in the processing, with the aim of, under the principle of proactive responsibility, complying with and being able to demonstrate compliance, while respecting the fundamental right to data protection.  
In this regard, it should be noted that the confidentiality of personal data is regulated in Article 5 of the GDPR and is therefore one of the principles relating to processing. The principles relating to processing are, on one hand, the starting point and the closing clause of the legal framework for data protection, constituting true guiding rules of the system with intense expansive force; on the other hand, having a high level of specificity, they are mandatory norms that can be infringed.  
Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR establishes a clear obligation of compliance consisting of preventing unauthorized or unlawful processing by implementing appropriate security measures. Therefore, one must be in a position to guarantee the confidentiality of personal data to prevent a third party from accessing data that does not belong to them, as it is mandatory to process personal data in accordance with the GDPR and LOPDGDD. For this reason, it is an activity where the diligence provided by

Article 32 does not establish static security measures; it is up to controllers and processors to determine the necessary security measures to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal data. Therefore, the same data processing may involve different security measures depending on the specific circumstances in which such data processing takes place.  
In line with these provisions, Recital 75 of the GDPR states: The risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, of varying severity and probability, may arise from data processing that could cause physical, material, or immaterial harm, particularly in cases where processing could lead to issues of discrimination, identity theft or fraud, financial losses, damage to reputation, loss of confidentiality of data subject to professional secrecy, unauthorized reversal of pseudonymization, or any other significant economic or social harm; in cases where data subjects are deprived of their rights and freedoms or prevented from exercising control over their personal data; in cases where the personal data processed reveal ethnic or racial origin, political opinions, religion or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, and the processing of genetic data, data concerning health, or data about sexual life, or criminal convictions and related security measures; in cases where personal aspects are evaluated, in particular the analysis or prediction of aspects related to work performance, economic situation, health, preferences or personal interests, reliability or behavior, situation or movements, in order to create or use personal profiles; in cases where personal data of vulnerable individuals, particularly children, are processed; or in cases where the processing involves a large amount of personal data and affects a large number of data subjects. (the underlining is ours)  
Furthermore, Recital 83 of the GDPR states: In order to maintain security and prevent processing from infringing the provisions of this Regulation, the controller or processor must assess the inherent risks of the processing and implement measures to mitigate them, such as encryption. These measures must ensure an adequate level of security, including confidentiality, taking into account the state of the art and the cost of their implementation in relation to the risks and the nature of the personal data that must be protected. When assessing the risk in relation to data security, the risks arising from the processing of personal data must be considered, such as the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data, particularly susceptible to causing physical, material, or immaterial harm. (the underlining is ours)  
In this regard, it should be emphasized that Article 28.3.c) of the GDPR assigns to the processor the obligation to take all necessary measures in accordance with Article 32.

The security of data requires the application of appropriate technical or organizational measures in the processing of personal data to protect such data against unauthorized or unlawful access, use, modification, dissemination, loss, destruction, or accidental damage. In this sense, security measures are key to guaranteeing the fundamental right to data protection. The existence of the fundamental right to the protection of personal data is not possible if confidentiality, integrity, and availability of such data cannot be guaranteed. It should not be forgotten that, in accordance with Article 32.1 of the cited GDPR, the technical and organizational measures to be applied to ensure an adequate level of security relative to the risk must take into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the variable likelihood and severity of risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.  
Therefore, IBERDROLA, when assessing risks and determining the appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an adequate level of security relative to the risk for the rights and freedoms of natural persons in the data processing it carries out as a data processor, is obliged to consider the specific activity that constitutes its business, which involves processing personal data continuously and on a large scale (numerous data to be collected, processed, stored…); the type of data processed: identifying, contact, those related to the supply and consumption of electricity, bank accounts, etc.; the context: existence of web applications on the internet, that is, in a non-isolated environment, which entails risks arising from the very interconnectivity that the network implies, which must be addressed in a specialized manner; existence of a common database shared by several companies with requirements for separation (physical or logical).  
Therefore, due to the activity in which it is engaged, IBERDROLA is obliged to carry out a highly specialized risk analysis and implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an adequate level of security relative to the risk of its activity for the rights and freedoms of individuals.  
In this case, as previously noted, due to the cyberattack suffered by a web application of one of the companies in the group, there was, in addition to unauthorized access to the personal data processed by that company, unauthorized access to personal data of clients from two other different companies and an unlawful exfiltration of the same, as the logical separation existing in the database where personal data of various companies in the Group are housed and managed by IBERDROLA was circumvented or breached.

Classification of the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR  
The aforementioned infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR constitutes the commission of the infringements classified in Article 83.4 of the GDPR, which under the heading "General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines" states:  
“Violations of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 10,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to 2% of the total annual global turnover of the preceding financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the obligations of the controller and the processor under Articles 8, 11, 25 to 39, 42, and 43; (…)”  
In this regard, the LOPDGDD, in its Article 71 "Infringements," establishes that “Acts and behaviors referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those that are contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements.”

Sanction for the violation of Article 32 of the GDPR  
For the purpose of deciding on the imposition of an administrative fine and its amount, in accordance with the evidence available, it is appropriate to assess the sanction to be imposed, according to the following criteria established by Article 83.2 of the GDPR:  
As aggravating factors:  
- Article 83.2.a) GDPR: Nature, severity, and duration of the infringement.  
  - It is considered that the nature of the infringement is serious as it has resulted in a loss of confidentiality and, therefore, of irretrievable control and disposition over personal data.  
  - Number of affected parties: The number of affected individuals is very high, as it amounts to over one and a half million people (1,607,550).  
  - Level of damages suffered: High. Numerous personal data were stolen (…) and from a very considerable number of clients (1,607,550), thus losing all control over them, which poses a high risk of fraudulent use (identity theft, fraud, financial losses, etc.), thereby undermining the fundamental right to the protection of personal data which, as indicated by the Constitutional Court in the previously mentioned ruling, aims to guarantee individuals control and disposition over their personal data, regarding its use and destination, with the purpose of preventing its illicit and harmful trafficking to the dignity and rights of the affected party.  
- Article 83.2.b) GDPR. There is evidence of negligence on the part of IBERDROLA in complying with and observing the technical and organizational measures necessary to ensure the security required for the protection of personal data, specifically to guarantee the confidentiality of such data. In this regard, it should be noted that IBERDROLA is a large company that, as a processor, carries out large-scale processing, affecting numerous individuals (from the declarations made by the three companies affected by the breach, it processes data of at least 21 million people), for which a higher level of diligence and appropriate security measures are required to ensure the confidentiality of the personal data it processes.  
It is worth recalling, in this sense, the ruling of the National Court of 17/10/2007 (rec. 63/2006), which regarding entities whose activity involves the continuous processing of customer data, indicates that “…the Supreme Court has understood that there is negligence whenever a legal duty of care is neglected, that is, when the offender does not behave with the required diligence. And in assessing the degree of diligence, the professionalism of the subject must be particularly weighed, and there is no doubt that, in the case now examined, when the activity of the appellant involves constant and abundant handling of personal data, it must be insisted on the rigor and exquisite care to comply with the legal provisions in this regard.”  
As mitigating factors:  
- Article 83.2.c) GDPR. Measures taken by the controller or processor to mitigate the damages suffered by the affected parties: Positive. As it was IBERDROLA that, upon detecting performance and availability issues on the website, confirmed that it was a breach and proceeded to block the attacker's IP and to notify I-DE of the breach, which may have prevented a much more serious impact. It also managed the breach jointly with I-DE.  
Furthermore, it is considered appropriate to assess the sanction to be imposed according to the following criteria established in section 2 of Article 76 “Sanctions and corrective measures” of the LOPDGDD:  
As aggravating factors:  
- Article 76.2.b) LOPDGDD. Linkage of the infringer's activity with the processing of personal data: The development of the business activity carried out by IBERDROLA involves continuous and large-scale processing of personal data. Therefore, it is a large company accustomed to processing personal data.  
The balance of the circumstances contemplated in Article 83.2 of the GDPR and Article 76.2 of the LOPDGDD, regarding the infringement committed by violating the provisions of Article 32 of the GDPR, allows for the imposition of a sanction of €1,000,000 (one million euros).
