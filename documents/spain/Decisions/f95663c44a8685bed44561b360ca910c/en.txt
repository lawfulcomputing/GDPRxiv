RESOLUTION OF RECONSIDERATION APPEAL  
Examined the reconsideration appeal filed by DIGI SPAIN TELECOM, S.L. (hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated June 2, 2023, and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On June 2, 2023, a resolution was issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202204881, by virtue of which DIGI SPAIN TELECOM, S.L., with NIF B84919760, was imposed a fine of 70,000 euros (seventy thousand euros) for an infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR.  
This resolution, which was notified to the appellant on June 5, 2023, was issued following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (LOPDGDD), and subsidiarily under Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), regarding the processing of sanctioning procedures.  

SECOND: As proven facts of the aforementioned sanctioning procedure, PS/00636/2022, the following was recorded:  
FIRST. - The complainant filed a complaint with this Agency on April 3, 2022, stating that on April 1, 2021, their mobile phone lost service, receiving several emails from two banking entities notifying them of attempted access to their account and the execution of several transfers via Bizum.  
SECOND. - DIGI certifies that the duplicate was made on April 1, 2021, at 14:52 hrs. at a point of sale for the distribution of DIGI products and services, and that the duplicate could only be made by the line holder and only in person at a distributor. The customer must present the original identity document, with photocopies not being valid, and the distributor must verify the line number and identity document data, which must match those recorded for the customer in the systems of the respondent.  
THIRD. - It is recorded that DIGI, on April 12, 2021, established measures concerning the point of sale for the distribution of DIGI products and services involved, as well as reminding them of their contractual obligations regarding the identification of DIGI customers. Likewise, they proceeded to limit access to the application to prevent possible unauthorized access.  
FOURTH. - The complainant's mobile line was allegedly active with fraudulent activity from 14:52 hrs. to 19:22 hrs. on April 1, 2022.  

THIRD: The appellant submitted a reconsideration appeal to this Spanish Agency for Data Protection on July 5, 2023.  
As the basis for the appeal, the appellant reiterates the arguments presented throughout the procedure and considers that the proven facts allow for the conclusion that DIGI had a procedure in place, which was scrupulously followed, and that allowed it to carry out, in accordance with its obligations, a lawful data processing.  
On the other hand, DIGI states that it makes every effort to identify and mitigate attempts at fraud, and cannot be imposed an absolute responsibility for its detection; in the face of a third party who has the complainant's telephone, identifying, and banking data, who complies with the protocol, even identifying themselves in person.  
Furthermore, it points out that the AEPD is based solely on the outcome; there is a possibility that even following the protocol and having requested a copy of the ID, it could have been false, so it cannot be concluded as the AEPD does, that if this operation had been carried out correctly, the duplicate should have been denied.  
Additionally, DIGI argues that the AEPD unequivocally imposes an objective responsibility on it, in which, regardless of the diligence and measures taken, the entity's culpability is declared.  

Regarding the lack of proportionality of the proposed sanction and that, following the appropriate procedures, a resolution is issued indicating the filing of procedure EXP202204881.  

Subsidiarily, in the event that the AEPD rules against the legal reasoning put forth by DIGI, it requests that the AEPD conclude the procedure by means of a warning and, as a last resort, moderate or adjust the sanction stated in the resolution notified to DIGI.

However, the manner of attributing responsibility to legal entities does not correspond to the forms of culpability that are attributable to human conduct. Thus, in the case of infringements committed by legal entities, although the element of culpability must be present, it necessarily applies differently than it does with natural persons.  
According to STC 246/1991 " (...) this distinct construction of the attribution of the authorship of the infringement to the legal person arises from the very nature of the legal fiction to which these subjects respond. They lack the volitional element in the strict sense, but not the capacity to infringe the norms to which they are subject.  
Capacity for infringement and, therefore, direct reproachability that derives from the legal good protected by the norm that is infringed and the necessity for such protection to be truly effective and the risk that, consequently, the legal person must assume who is subject to compliance with that norm" (in this sense, STS of November 24, 2011, Rec 258/2009).  
To the above, it should be added, following the ruling of January 23, 1998, partially transcribed in the SSTS of October 9, 2009, Rec 5285/2005, and October 23, 2010, Rec 1067/2006, that "although the culpability of the conduct must also be subject to proof, it should be considered in relation to the assumption of the corresponding burden, that ordinarily the volitional and cognitive elements necessary to appreciate it form part of the proven typical conduct, and that their exclusion requires evidence of the absence of such elements, or in its normative aspect, that due diligence has been exercised by the one claiming its non-existence; it is not enough, in short, for exculpation against typically unlawful behavior to invoke the absence of fault."

In the present case, it is established that on April 1, 2021, Digi processed the issuance of duplicates of the SIM card for the line ***TELEFONO.1, belonging to the claimant.  
However, it should be noted that SIM Swapping is a fraud that allows identity theft by hijacking the phone number through obtaining a duplicate of the SIM card.

The result was that the respondent issued the SIM card to a third party who was not the owner of the line.

DIGI, in its capacity as an operator, must be more demanding when providing a duplicate of a SIM card. Identity verifications must be exhaustive to avoid identity theft problems.

Therefore, after analyzing the allegations made in the present optional appeal for reconsideration, it is verified that no new legal arguments have been presented that would allow for a reconsideration of the meaning of the sanctioning resolution issued on June 2, 2023.  
III  
Conclusion  
Consequently, in the present appeal for reconsideration, the appellant has not provided new facts or legal arguments that would allow for a reconsideration of the validity of the contested resolution.
