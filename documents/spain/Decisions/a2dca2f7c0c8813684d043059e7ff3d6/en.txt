The reported facts materialize in the access by third parties not involved to a document submitted by the claiming party to the responding party, through the Electronic Registry, violating the regulations regarding data protection. The document in question was subsequently disseminated through an instant messaging application.  
The security of personal data is regulated in Articles 32, 33, and 34 of the GDPR.  
Article 32 of the GDPR, "Security of processing," establishes the following:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the varying likelihood and severity of risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which shall include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data in a timely manner in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a result of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data only processes such data on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law.”  
The GDPR defines personal data breaches as “all breaches of security that result in the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or unauthorized communication or access to such data.”  
It should be noted that the GDPR does not establish a list of security measures that are applicable according to the data being processed, but rather establishes that the controller and the processor shall implement technical and organizational measures that are appropriate to the risk posed by the processing, taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, and the risks of likelihood and severity to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.

Likewise, the security measures must be adequate and proportionate to the detected risk, indicating that the determination of technical and organizational measures should be made taking into account: pseudonymization and encryption, the ability to ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience, the ability to restore availability and access to data after an incident, verification process (which is not an audit), evaluation, and assessment of the effectiveness of the measures.  
In any case, when assessing the adequacy of the level of security, particular attention shall be paid to the risks presented by the processing of data, such as the destruction, loss, or accidental or unlawful alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial harm.  
In this same regard, Recital 83 of the GDPR states that:  
“(83) In order to maintain security and prevent processing from infringing the provisions of this Regulation, the controller or the processor must assess the inherent risks of the processing and apply measures to mitigate them, such as encryption. These measures must ensure an adequate level of security, including confidentiality, taking into account the state of the art and the cost of their implementation in relation to the risks and the nature of the personal data that must be protected. When assessing the risk in relation to data security, the risks arising from the processing of personal data must be taken into account, such as the destruction, loss, or accidental or unlawful alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data, particularly susceptible to causing physical, material, or immaterial harm.”  
In this case, the documentation in the file provides sufficient evidence of a breach of Article 32.1 of the GDPR by the respondent party, caused by a lack of security measures that allowed access by its employees to all documents submitted in the Electronic Registry of the entity, including those unrelated to the functions assigned to them. Access to the documents obviously entailed access to the personal information contained therein.  
In this regard, from the information provided by the respondent entity itself, which is included in a report on the incident prepared by its Information Systems Service, it follows that the Entry Register of documents of this entity could be accessed without restriction by a large number of people, even if the matter to which the document in question corresponded was not related to specific actions or functions assigned to such persons. It is even stated that there were access permissions to the information of that Register associated with persons who had changed their workplace or functions.

The CITY COUNCIL OF GIJÓN even claims that the measures adopted to limit generalized access to the common module of the Entry Register have not affected the performance of the usual tasks of the positions under investigation, which further confirms that the protocols implemented by this entity at the time the analyzed events took place were not adequate to guarantee data security and that such protocols were not justified by the performance of the functions assigned to the individuals who had permissions to access the aforementioned Register.  
It is also noted that the document of the responding party accessed improperly, which informed about a possible infringement of the disciplinary regime of the Local Police, in addition to the factual circumstances that, in the opinion of the claiming party, determine the possible disciplinary infringement, included personal data relating to the name and surname of the possible infringer and the data of the representative of the claiming party relating to name, surname, ID number, and position held in the organization of the claiming party.  
These facts demonstrate that the responding entity did not have the appropriate technical and organizational measures to guarantee the security and confidentiality of the data, especially those aimed at preventing access to the information by unauthorized third parties. This allowed several individuals within the organization to access the document submitted by the claiming party on 20/01/2021 in the Entry Register of the responding party, even though the functions assigned to these individuals were not related to the subject matter of the document in question.  
In the Resolution of the Mayor's Office, dated 30/08/2021, regarding the legality of the verified accesses, it is stated as follows:  
“From this, it can be concluded that some of the treatments expressed in the Background, such as habitual, general, or indiscriminate access to the entries of the entry register or for the exercise of supposed union rights, do not relate to the basis of legality and purposes contained in the aforementioned inventory of activities.”  
Consequently, in accordance with the evidence presented, the aforementioned facts constitute a violation of the provisions of Article 32 of the GDPR, which gives rise to the application of the corrective powers that Article 58 of the aforementioned Regulation grants to the Spanish Data Protection Agency.

In accordance with the above-stated legal basis, the documentation in the proceedings provides sufficient evidence to understand that the responding party violated Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR, which regulates the duty of confidentiality, manifested in the access by unauthorized third parties to the personal data contained in the document submitted by the claiming party to the CITY COUNCIL OF GIJÓN, as noted in the First Established Fact.  
This duty of confidentiality aims to prevent unauthorized leaks of data by the data subjects. In this case, the improper leakage of data has been acknowledged by the responding party.  
Consequently, the aforementioned facts constitute a violation of the provisions of Article 5.1 f) of the GDPR, which gives rise to the application of the corrective powers that Article 58 of the aforementioned Regulation grants to the Spanish Data Protection Agency.  
IV  
Claims of the responding party  
The CITY COUNCIL considers that the present sanctioning procedure lacks effectiveness, considering that it was initiated when this entity had already responded to the claiming party, the facts had been acknowledged, and measures had been adopted to prevent similar incidents, which have not been taken into account. It understands, for the same reasons, that the proceedings should be archived.  
However, this Agency does not share this approach, as it is appropriate to analyze and resolve what is necessary according to the factual situation subject to the claim. It is another matter to consider the reaction of the responding party to the reported facts regarding the obligation to adopt measures to adapt its actions to the regulations, which is analyzed in Legal Basis VII.  
On the other hand, the responding party warns about what was stated by the Supreme Court in Judgment number 188/2022, dated February 15, regarding the nature of the obligation to adopt measures, according to which it cannot be an obligation of result, but of means. In this regard, it is important to emphasize that the infringement of Article 32 is not declared in this case due to the fact that the measures adopted by the CITY COUNCIL OF GIJÓN did not achieve the expected result, but due to the lack of adequate measures, as expressed in the preceding Legal Bases.
