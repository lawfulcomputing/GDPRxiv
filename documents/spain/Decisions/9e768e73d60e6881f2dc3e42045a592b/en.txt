BACKGROUND  
FIRST: Mr. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on August 15, 2022. The complaint is directed against ORANGE ESPAGNE, S.A.U. with NIF A82009812 (hereinafter, the defendant or Orange). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The complainant states that, without his consent, Orange provided a third party with a duplicate of his mobile phone SIM card, which allowed this third party to access his banking data, resulting in financial harm.  
In this regard, he indicates that on August 1, 2022, his mobile phone stopped working, and he received several emails regarding a consumption notice of 100Mb from the contract and another email stating: "The activation of your eSIM card has been successfully processed."  
Following the incident, he contacted the defendant requesting the cancellation of the SIM card, to which the defendant indicated "that they could not cancel said card due to protocol," so he had to wait four days to physically receive a new one in order to cancel the previous one.  
He provides the following relevant documentation:  
- Complaint filed with Orange.  
- Screenshot of the messages received (including the message regarding the activation of the eSIM card).

- Screenshot of the conversation held with the defendant through chat. In this regard, it states that: “Article 22 (1) of the Regulation requires that ‘payment service providers shall ensure the confidentiality and integrity of the personalized security credentials of the payment service user, including authentication codes, during all phases of authentication’ and Article 22, paragraph 4, of the Delegated Regulation establishes that ‘payment service providers shall ensure that the processing and routing of personalized security credentials and authentication codes generated in accordance with Chapter II take place in secure environments in accordance with robust and widely recognized industry standards.’”

This legal reasoning represents a clear materialization of objective liability in the sanctioning sphere, which is not admissible in our legal system. In this regard, the Judgment of the Supreme Court 543/2022, of February 15, 2022, establishes, in its Legal Foundation Third, and sets precedent; that: “The obligation to adopt the necessary measures to guarantee the security of personal data cannot be considered an obligation of result, which implies that once a personal data breach occurs to a third party, there is liability regardless of the measures adopted and the activity carried out by the data controller or processor.” Thus, the Supreme Court defines this obligation as one of means, in which (Legal Foundation Third): “the commitment undertaken is to adopt the technical and organizational means, as well as to deploy diligent activity in their implementation and use that tends to achieve the expected result with means that can reasonably be classified as suitable and sufficient for its achievement, hence they are referred to as 'diligence obligations' or 'behavioral obligations.'  
Therefore, in addition to what has been stated and as has been evidenced, the entity has an adequate protocol for the proper processing of requests (whose effectiveness in fraud prevention is very high, exceeding 99%).

The ruling made by the National Court in its SAN of May 5, 2021 (Rec. 1437/2020) -even though that resolution deals with the circumstance of section e) of Article 83.2 of the GDPR, the commission of previous infringements- is extrapolable to the issue raised, the claim of the respondent that the "absence" of benefits be accepted as a mitigating factor given that both the GDPR and the LOPDGDD refer only to "the benefits obtained":  
- The respondent effectively resolved the incident subject to the claim (art. 83.2 c).  
It is appropriate to adjust the sanction to be imposed on the respondent and set it at the amount of €200,000 for the alleged infringement of Article 6.1) classified in Article 83.5.a) of the aforementioned GDPR.  
Therefore, in accordance with the applicable legislation and having assessed the criteria for adjusting the sanctions whose existence has been established,
