LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence and applicable regulations  
In accordance with the functions conferred on each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f) and h) of the GDPR, and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to resolve these investigative proceedings.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Legality of the processing of personal data  
The principles governing the processing are enumerated in Article 5 of the GDPR. In this regard, paragraph 1 letter a) states that: "Personal data shall be:  
a) processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject (lawfulness, fairness, and transparency);

The principle of lawfulness is primarily regulated in Article 6 of the GDPR. The circumstances that allow the processing of personal data to be considered lawful are enumerated in Article 6.1 of the GDPR:  
1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or for the implementation of pre-contractual measures at the request of the data subject;  
c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) the processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) the processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in letter f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the exercise of their functions."  
In this regard, Recital 40 of the GDPR states that "For processing to be lawful, personal data must be processed with the consent of the data subject or on another legitimate basis established by law, whether in this Regulation or under another Union or Member State law to which this Regulation relates, including the necessity of complying with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject or the necessity of executing a contract to which the data subject is a party or for taking steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract."  
III  
Conclusion

The documentation provided in the file records the existence of a credit line contracted for the amount of €12,000 formalized in the year 2008 between COFIDIS and B.B.B., with the claiming party listed as the second holder. In the copy of the referenced document, identifying data (name, surname, mobile number, date of birth, and ID number), professional and economic information of both holders appear. Additionally, COFIDIS possesses a copy of the ID of both holders, which was valid at the time of the credit application (it expired in April 2014).  
COFIDIS acknowledges having received emails in 2017 from the claiming party in which, on one hand, they deny having contracted the credit line in question, alleging the falsity of the signature appearing on the document; and, on the other hand, requesting the cancellation of their data. They provide a copy of the mentioned emails that prove the existence of the cancellation request from the claiming party and, in turn, the denial of the request by COFIDIS due to an outstanding debt.  
However, COFIDIS has reported that as soon as it became aware of the claim presented by the claiming party, it proceeded to adopt a series of measures. On one hand, the personal data of the claiming party related to the credit was deleted while remaining blocked. They provide a screenshot from their database showing that in the section "Blocking of the person" concerning the claiming party, it states "Yes," as well as an account extract corresponding to the credit line application in which the claiming party is no longer listed.  
On the other hand, they requested the removal of the claiming party's data from the Central Risk Information of the Bank of Spain, which was accepted, as shown in the provided screenshot.  
Furthermore, it is worth mentioning that COFIDIS currently has a "Direct Revolving Pre-acceptance and Acceptance Regulation" in which it establishes different actions for detecting fraud. They attach a copy of the document, dated 09/01/2023.  
The GDPR requires that the processing of personal data be lawful, which must be based on one of the legal bases listed in its article 6.1. In light of the documentation in the file, it seems evident that the processing of personal data carried out by COFIDIS is covered by the assumption detailed in paragraph b) of the aforementioned provision, without prejudice to the fact that such a relationship may be questioned before the corresponding jurisdiction given that the claiming party states that someone used their personal data to contract the credit line and forged their signature.  
Therefore, based on the information indicated in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found to substantiate the existence of an infringement within the jurisdictional scope of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, as the processing of personal data without a legitimate cause has not been substantiated.
