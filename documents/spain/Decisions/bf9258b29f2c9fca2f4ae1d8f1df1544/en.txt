LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, insofar as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Alleged Breach of Obligation. Legality of Processing  
In this case, we proceed to analyze the complaint dated 30/05/23, through which the following is reported: "installation of digital peephole" which allegedly affects their personal sphere and/or privacy in the alleged monitoring of entries/exits of the property.  
The facts initially concern the presence of a digital peephole installed on the access door to the dwelling of the property indicated by the complainant.  
The <digital peephole> is a digital device that incorporates a camera and a screen that allows observing who is on the other side of the door, its use being necessary for certain disabled individuals or for security reasons of the property, as it allows comfortably viewing who is ringing the bell or at the door of the property.  
The Instruction 1/2006, of November 8, from the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, regarding the processing of personal data for surveillance purposes through camera systems or video cameras, in its Article 1 "in fine" states: "References in this Instruction to video cameras and cameras shall also be understood to refer to any analogous technical means and, in general, to any system that allows the treatments provided for therein."  
Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR states the following: Personal data shall be: "adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed ('data minimization')."  
It should be noted that individuals are responsible for ensuring that the installed systems comply with current legislation, demonstrating that they meet all the requirements demanded by the applicable regulations.  
According to STS—Civil Chamber, Section 1, November 7, 2019 (No. 600/2019)—in accordance with the doctrine of the Constitutional Court, it recalls that "the constitutionality of any restrictive measure of fundamental rights is determined by the strict observance of the principle of proportionality."  
III  
On February 21, 2025, a written statement of allegations was received from the designated legal representative, alleging "personal security reasons" of the respondent due to the repeated behaviors of the complainant's neighbor.  
It is the maintained criterion of this Agency that there is no tolerance for uncivil or vandalistic acts of any nature (e.g., damage, threats, coercion, etc.) so that a restrictive interpretation of the norm results in a double punishment for the affected party, who, being deprived of the possibility of recording, suffers simultaneously new aggressions that are sometimes carried out furtively.  
In support of their claim, the respondent provides documentary evidence (Annex I) that certifies the filing of various complaints with the Police due to behaviors as described, which affect the personal freedom of the respondent and demonstrate a <real risk> against the actions of the owner(s) across the street.  
Additionally, an Order from the ***COURT B.B.B. (***LOCALITY.1) is provided regarding a Minor Offense Trial in which it is agreed to "Impose on Ms. B.B.B. the (…) to Ms. A.A.A. within a radius of five meters, to her (…)".  
The presence of a digital peephole, the installation of which is not denied by the respondent, is due to the reason stated, the continuous threats made by the referenced neighbor, which have been subject to criminal reproach through Judgment No. (…) of

Date 05/07/22 in which the existence of <minor threats> of Article 171.7 of the Penal Code is considered accredited.  
In this specific case, it is considered that the digital peephole serves a deterrent function against the aggravation of conflicts between the parties, being limited to the area that affects both parties, as they are neighbors door to door, with the risks that this entails.  
The effect of the same is limited to a confined space, such as the landing area, so the impact of the measure is the minimum necessary to fulfill its purpose, which is none other than to prevent the continuous situations described.  
Regarding the <personal> data that could be captured by the referenced device, it is important to emphasize that in the event of any criminal act being perpetrated (e.g., threats, coercion, property damage, etc.), the images must be forwarded to the Security Forces or to the nearest Investigating Court to the place of the events, which will be competent to analyze and assess their content.  
Without further considerations, it is worth remembering that fundamental rights are not absolute rights (e.g., STC 11/1981, April 8, STC 62/1982, October 15, STC 37/1989, Fj 7º, to cite a few references) where they may yield to other rights equally deserving of protection, as long as the principle of proportionality is observed, as is the case presented.  
Finally, a copy of the Minutes of the Extraordinary General Meeting dated 13/03/23 is provided, where in the <Annex> the statement of the respondent to install a <digital peephole> by police recommendation is recorded, due to the possibility of malicious acts against her person, having been voted on urgently by the owners in a positive manner.  
The Community of property owners is aware of the described situation, with internal knowledge and acceptance of the presence of the device, even being oriented in such a measure in connivance with the Security Forces of the State close to the place of the events, as a necessary measure to prevent future harms.  
IV  
Having examined the allegations and evidence provided and taking into account the context of the case, it is considered that the presence of the <digital peephole> is a measure proportional to the case presented, fulfilling a clear deterrent function against situations that affect the personal freedom of the respondent, all of which justify the permanence of the device in the installed location.  
It is recalled the significance of the rights at stake, as well as the rigor in the work of this body, which must avoid the instrumentalization of the same, in the face of facts related to criminal prosecutions, which even determine <prohibition of proximity> to the affected person's home.
