RESOLUTION OF RECOURSE FOR RECONSIDERATION
Examined the recourse for reconsideration filed by DIGI SPAIN TELECOM, S.L. (hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated March 14, 2023, and based on the following:

FACTS
FIRST: On March 14, 2023, a resolution was issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202201226, whereby DIGI SPAIN TELECOM, S.L. was imposed a fine of 70,000 euros (seventy thousand euros) for an infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR. This resolution, which was notified to the appellant on March 21, 2023, was issued following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), and subsidiarily under Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), regarding the processing of sanctioning procedures.

SECOND: As proven facts of the aforementioned sanctioning procedure, PS/00503/2022, the following was recorded:
FIRST: The complainant filed a complaint with this Agency on December 4, 2021, stating that the respondent provided a duplicate of their SIM card to a third party on November 13 of the same year, their mobile phone line stopped working, and hours later, they discovered unauthorized transactions in their bank account.
SECOND: DIGI certifies that the duplicate was issued on November 13, 2021, through a call received requesting the duplicate of the SIM card for the line ***PHONE.1.
THIRD: It is noted that the respondent, upon the caller exceeding the personal data of the line holder, proceeded to issue a request code for the duplicate SIM card, and that same day the requesting person presented themselves at a DIGI distribution point. There, they proceeded to issue the SIM card to said third party who was not the holder of the line; the duplicate was delivered at a DIGI distributor's establishment.
FOURTH: The respondent has acknowledged in their response that they reprimanded the distributor, who was definitively suspended from operational activity in order to prevent future conduct similar to that of the case.
FIFTH: It is evidenced in the communication from DIGI with the distributor, in which the latter responds that over the weekend (when the duplicate was issued), his brother stayed in the store very briefly and that it is possible that his brother did not request the original identity document due to ignorance.
SIXTH: The line was active from 17:00 hours until 23:26 (approximately) on November 13, 2021, and the complainant obtained a new duplicate regaining control of it the next day.
THIRD: The appellant submitted a recourse for reconsideration to this Spanish Agency for Data Protection on April 21, 2023. As the basis for the recourse, the appellant reiterates the arguments presented throughout the procedure and considers that from the proven facts it is possible to conclude that DIGI had a procedure in place, which was scrupulously followed, and that allowed it to carry out, in accordance with its obligations, a lawful data processing.
On the other hand, DIGI states that it makes every effort to identify and mitigate fraud attempts, and cannot be imposed an absolute responsibility for its detection; in the face of a third party who has the complainant's telephone, identifying, and banking data, who complies with the protocol, identifying themselves both by phone and in person.
Moreover, it points out that the AEPD bases its conclusions solely on the result; there is a possibility that even following the protocol and having requested a copy of the ID, it could have been false, so it cannot be concluded as the AEPD does, that if this operation had been carried out correctly, the duplicate should have been denied.
Additionally, DIGI argues that the AEPD unequivocally imposes an objective responsibility on it, in which, regardless of the diligence and measures taken, the entity's culpability is declared.

Regarding the lack of proportionality of the proposed sanction and that following the appropriate procedures a resolution is issued indicating the filing of the procedure EXP202201226.

Subsidiarily, in the event that the AEPD rules against the legal basis upheld by DIGI, it requests the AEPD to conclude the procedure by means of a warning and, as a last resort, to moderate or adjust the sanction included in the notified Proposal.

<<II  
Unfulfilled Obligation  
The respondent is accused of committing an infringement for violating Article 6 of the GDPR, "Lawfulness of processing," which states in its paragraph 1 the circumstances under which the processing of third-party data is considered lawful:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or for the implementation at the request of the data subject of pre-contractual measures;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child. The provisions of point f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.”

Regarding the claim that DIGI has not provided the alleged criminals with personal information of the claimant other than what they already had prior to this. Consequently, there has been no unauthorized processing of personal data.  
Indeed, the issuance of a duplicate is not sufficient to carry out banking operations in the name of the holders; certainly, to complete the fraud, it is necessary for a third party to "impersonate the identity" of the data holder before the financial entity.  
This, in principle, leads to processing outside the principle of lawfulness, as a third party is processing data, having access to it, without any legal basis, in addition to violating other principles such as confidentiality.  
For this reason, this is a process where the diligence provided by operators is fundamental to prevent this type of fraud and violations of the GDPR. Diligence that translates into the establishment of adequate measures to ensure that data processing is in compliance with the GDPR.  
Identical considerations apply to the actions of banking entities that provide payment services, in which this type of fraud begins, as the third party has access to the credentials of the affected user and impersonates them.  
As these entities are responsible for processing the data of their clients, they have the same obligations as those indicated so far for the operators regarding compliance with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD, as well as those derived from Royal Decree-Law 19/2018, of November 23, on payment services and other urgent financial measures.

It can be inferred that DIGI has provided a duplicate SIM card to a third party other than the legitimate holder of the mobile line, after a third person overcame the existing security policy, which evidences a breach of the duty to protect customer information.  
Denying the occurrence of negligent conduct on the part of DIGI would be equivalent to acknowledging that its behavior—by action or omission—has been diligent. Obviously, we do not share this perspective of the facts, as the lack of due diligence has been established. The SAN of October 17, 2007 (rec. 63/2006) is very illustrative; starting from the premise that these are entities whose activities involve continuous processing of customer data, it indicates that “...the Supreme Court has understood that there is imprudence whenever a legal duty of care is neglected, that is, when the offender does not behave with the required diligence. And in assessing the degree of diligence, the professionalism or lack thereof of the subject must be particularly weighed, and there is no doubt that, in the case now examined, when the activity of the appellant involves constant and abundant handling of personal data, it must be insisted upon the rigor and exquisite care to comply with the legal provisions in this regard.”  
It is established in the file that adequate security has not been guaranteed in the processing of personal data, given the outcome produced by the identity theft. That is to say, a third party has managed to access the personal data of the line holder.  
Regarding the assertion that the criminals have not managed to obtain personal data from DIGI, and therefore one cannot speak of a breach of protection measures, it should be noted that access to the duplicate of a SIM card that makes its holder identifiable corresponds to the definition of personal data in Article 4.1) of the GDPR.  
As for DIGI's responsibility, it should be indicated that, in general, DIGI processes its customers' data under the provisions of Article 6.1 b) of the GDPR, as it is considered a necessary processing for the execution of a contract in which the data subject is a party or for the application at the request of the data subject of pre-contractual measures. In other cases, it bases the legality of the processing on the grounds provided in Article 6.1.a), c), e), and f) of the GDPR.  
On the other hand, to complete the fraud, it is necessary for a third party to "impersonate the identity" of the data holder to receive the duplicate of the SIM card. This, in principle, entails processing outside the principle of legality since a third party is processing data, as they have access to it, without any legal basis, in addition to the violation of other principles such as confidentiality.  
Certainly, the principle of responsibility provided in Article 28 of the LRJSP states that: “Only individuals and legal entities may be sanctioned for acts constituting an administrative infringement, as well as, when a Law recognizes them the capacity to act, the groups of affected parties, unions, and entities without legal personality and independent or autonomous assets, that are held responsible for them by way of intent or negligence.”

However, the way in which liability is attributed to legal entities does not correspond to the forms of culpability that are attributable to human conduct, whether intentional or negligent. Thus, in the case of violations committed by legal entities, although the element of culpability must be present, it is necessarily applied differently than it is with natural persons.  
According to STC 246/1991, "(...) this distinct construction of the attribution of the authorship of the infringement to the legal person arises from the very nature of the legal fiction to which these subjects respond. They lack the volitional element in the strict sense, but not the capacity to infringe the norms to which they are subject. Capacity to infringe and, therefore, direct reproachability that derives from the legal good protected by the norm that is infringed and the necessity for such protection to be truly effective and the risk that, consequently, the legal person must assume, which is subject to compliance with that norm" (in this sense, STS of November 24, 2011, Rec 258/2009).  
To the above, it should be added, following the judgment of January 23, 1998, partially transcribed in the SSTS of October 9, 2009, Rec 5285/2005, and October 23, 2010, Rec 1067/2006, that "although the culpability of the conduct must also be subject to proof, it must be considered in terms of the assumption of the corresponding burden, that ordinarily the volitional and cognitive elements necessary to appreciate it form part of the proven typical conduct, and that their exclusion requires evidence of the absence of such elements, or in its normative aspect, that due diligence has been exercised by the party claiming their non-existence; in short, the mere invocation of the absence of fault is not sufficient for exculpation against a typically unlawful behavior."  
Consequently, the lack of culpability is dismissed. Ultimate responsibility for the processing remains attributed to the responsible party, who is the one who determines the existence of the processing and its purpose. Let us remember that, generally, operators process their clients' data under the provisions of Article 6.1 b) of the GDPR, as it is considered necessary processing for the execution of a contract in which the data subject is a party (...). In this regard, DIGI has a network of sales representatives, points of sale, and authorized distributors through a distribution contract to offer DIGI's services. Among these services offered from their points of sale is the issuance of duplicates of SIM cards corresponding to a mobile phone line.

“1. Each supervisory authority shall ensure that the imposition of administrative fines under this article for the infringements of this Regulation indicated in paragraphs 4, 9, and 6 are, in each individual case, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.”

The ruling made by the National Court in its SAN of May 5, 2021 (Rec. 1437/2020) regarding paragraph e) of Article 83.2 of the GDPR, the commission of prior infringements:  
“On the other hand, it considers that the absence of a prior infringement should be appreciated as a mitigating factor. Well, Article 83.2 of the GDPR establishes that the circumstance 'e) any prior infringement committed by the controller or the processor' must be taken into account for the imposition of the administrative fine, among others. This is an aggravating circumstance; the fact that the prerequisite for its application does not occur means that it cannot be taken into consideration, but it does not imply or allow, as the claimant intends, its application as a mitigating factor.”  
(III) Article 83.2.f) of the GDPR refers to “the degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority in order to remedy the infringement and mitigate the possible adverse effects of the infringement.” The response of the respondent to the information request from the Inspection Subdirectorate did not fulfill those purposes, so it cannot be classified under that mitigating factor.  
(IV) Regarding the application of Article 76.2.c) of the LOPDGDD, in connection with Article 83.2.k), the absence of benefits obtained, it should be noted that such circumstance can only operate as an aggravating factor and in no case as a mitigating factor.  
Article 83.2.k) of the GDPR refers to “any other aggravating or mitigating factor applicable to the circumstances of the case, such as the financial benefits obtained or the losses avoided, directly or indirectly, through the infringement.” And Article 76.2.c) of the LOPDGDD states that “2. In accordance with the provisions of Article 83.2.k) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following may also be taken into account: [..] c) The benefits obtained as a result of the commission of the infringement.” Both provisions mention as a factor that can be taken into account in the graduation of the sanction the “benefits” obtained, but not the “absence” of these, which is what DIGI alleges.  
Furthermore, according to Article 83.1 of the GDPR, the imposition of fines is governed by the following principles: they must be individualized for each particular case, be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. The acceptance that the absence of benefits operates as a mitigating factor is contrary to the spirit of Article 83.1 of the GDPR and to the principles governing the determination of the amount of the fine. If, as a result of the commission of an infringement of the GDPR, it is qualified as a mitigating factor that no benefits have existed, the dissuasive purpose fulfilled through the sanction is partially nullified. Accepting DIGI's thesis in a case like the one at hand would imply introducing an artificial reduction in the sanction that truly needs to be imposed; the one resulting from considering the circumstances of Article 83.2 GDPR that must indeed be assessed.  
The Administrative Litigation Chamber of the National Court has warned that the fact that in a specific case not all the elements that make up a modifying circumstance of responsibility that, by its nature, has an aggravating character are present, cannot lead to the conclusion that such circumstance is applicable as a mitigating factor. Upon receipt of the notification and once it is executive, if the date of executiveness falls between the 1st and the 15th of each month, inclusive, the deadline for voluntary payment will be until the 20th of the following month or the next business day, and if it falls between the 16th and the last day of each month, inclusive, the payment deadline will be until the 5th of the second following month or the next business day.  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 50 of the LOPDGDD, this Resolution will be made public once it has been notified to the interested parties.  
Against this resolution, which concludes the administrative procedure in accordance with Article 48.6 of the LOPDGDD, and in accordance with the provisions of Article 123 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (LPACAP), the interested parties may file a contentious-administrative appeal before the Contentious-Administrative Chamber of the National Court, in accordance with the provisions of Article 25 and Section 5 of the fourth additional provision of Law 29/1998, of July 13, regulating Contentious-Administrative Jurisdiction, within a period of two months from the day following the notification of this act, as provided in Article 46.1 of the aforementioned Law.  
Finally, it is noted that in accordance with the provisions of Article 90.3 a) LPACAP, the firm resolution in the administrative procedure may be provisionally suspended if the interested party expresses their intention to file a contentious-administrative appeal. If this is the case, the interested party must formally communicate this fact by writing to the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, submitting it through the Electronic Registry of the Agency [https://sedeagpd.gob.es/sede-electronica-web/], or through any of the other registries provided for in Article 16.4 of the aforementioned LPACAP. They must also provide the Agency with documentation that proves the effective filing of the contentious-administrative appeal. If the Agency does not have knowledge of the filing of the contentious-administrative appeal within two months from the day following the notification of this resolution, it will consider the provisional suspension to be concluded.
