I

Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, by the general rules on administrative procedures.”

III  
1. Firstly, the respondent alleges a lack of defense arising from the determination of the amount of the sanction in the initiation agreement, as the sanctioning body proceeds ab initio to set the amounts of the imposed sanctions, considering that this constitutes blameworthy behavior as it affects the exercise of their rights.  
It should be noted that the National Court, in its Judgment of 09/12/2022, which the respondent must be well aware of as the appellant in the administrative contentious matter raised and which was dismissed, addressed these and other issues such as the incompetence of the person signing the resolution:  
“FOURTH.- Secondly, it advocates the nullity of the challenged resolution due to the violation of the appellant's right to effective judicial protection and defense, having set in the initiation agreement "in audita parte" the amount of the sanction to be imposed, which ultimately coincides with that contained in the sanctioning resolution. Furthermore, there is a contamination of the inspection action by the competent body to resolve, which ab initio shows the instructing body the terms to which, in its opinion, the result of the procedure should be confined.  
It points out that Law 29/2015, of October 1, on Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations, does not introduce any novelty in Article 64 regarding the regulatory regime of the initiation agreements of sanctioning procedures and that the literalness of Article 85 does not, in its opinion, constitute an authorization to prejudge the case in the initiation agreement, with the consequent breach of the rights of the accused in the procedure.

Additionally, in its device 5, it is agreed to notify the aforementioned agreement to BANKIA S.A., granting it a period of ten business days to submit allegations and propose any evidence it deems appropriate, and it is indicated that "In accordance with the provisions of Article 85 of the LPACAP, in the event that the sanction to be imposed is a fine, it may acknowledge its responsibility within the period granted for the submission of allegations to this initiation agreement; which will entail a reduction of 20% of the sanction that may be imposed in this procedure (...) Likewise, it may at any time prior to the resolution of this procedure, voluntarily pay the proposed sanction, in accordance with the provisions of Article 85.2 of the LPACAP, which will imply a reduction of 20% of its amount (...). The reduction for the voluntary payment of the sanction is cumulative with the one corresponding to the acknowledgment of responsibility, provided that this acknowledgment of responsibility is made within the period granted to submit allegations to the opening of the procedure."  
Well, the aforementioned Article 64 establishes in its section 2 that the initiation agreement in sanctioning procedures must contain, "at least," for what concerns us here:  
"b) The facts that motivate the initiation of the procedure, their possible qualification, and the sanctions that may correspond, without prejudice to what results from the instruction (...)."  
d) Competent body for the resolution of the procedure (...), indicating the possibility that the alleged responsible party may voluntarily acknowledge their responsibility, with the effects provided for in Article 85."  
That is to say, it is required that the initiation agreement includes, at least, the facts, their possible qualification, and the possible sanctions to be imposed, as well as to inform, from the outset, of the possibility of applying the reductions allowed by Article 85 of the LPACAP, which presupposes an initial provisional determination of the sanctions that may correspond, without prejudice to what results from the instruction. This indication refers to the section d) of the aforementioned Article 64.2, which was not contemplated in the initiation agreement regulated in Article 13 of Royal Decree 1398/1993, of August 4, which approves the Regulation of the Procedure for the Exercise of the Sanctioning Power, repealed by the sole repeal provision of Law 39/2015.  
For its part, Article 85 LPACAP states:

In their written submissions to the Proposal, the respondent insists that Article 32.1 of the GDPR has not been violated and that there has been no leakage or unauthorized access to the data, and in this regard, the ruling of the Supreme Court on 15/02/2022 clearly establishes that the obligation imposed by Article 32 to adopt technical and organizational measures aimed at ensuring confidentiality is an obligation of means and not of results.  
However, the respondent themselves in their writing claims that there was an error in management by providing the claimant with documentation different from that requested.  
On the other hand, it is true that the Supreme Court in its ruling states that: “The obligation to adopt the necessary measures to ensure the security of personal data cannot be considered an obligation of result, which implies that once a leakage of personal data to a third party occurs, there is responsibility regardless of the measures adopted and the activity carried out by the data controller or processor.  
In obligations of result, there is a commitment to achieve a specific objective, ensuring the achievement of the proposed result, in this case, to guarantee the security of personal data and the non-existence of leaks or security breaches.  
In obligations of means, the commitment undertaken is to adopt the technical and organizational means, as well as to carry out diligent activity in their implementation and use, which tends to achieve the expected result with means that can reasonably be classified as suitable and sufficient for its achievement; hence they are referred to as "obligations of diligence" or "behavioral obligations."  
The difference lies in the responsibility in each case, as while in the obligation of result one is liable for a harmful result due to the failure of the security system, whatever its cause and the diligence used. In the obligation of means, it is sufficient to establish technically adequate measures and implement and use them with reasonable diligence.  
In these latter cases, the sufficiency of the security measures that the controller must establish must be related to the state of technology at any given time and the level of protection required in relation to the personal data processed, but a result is not guaranteed.”  
But it is also true that the Court confirms that the design of the necessary technical and organizational means is not sufficient, as their correct implementation and appropriate use are also necessary.
