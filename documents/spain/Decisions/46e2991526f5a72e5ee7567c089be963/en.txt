FACTS  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the data protection authority of Lower Saxony on September 14, 2021. The complaint is directed against OPEN BANK, S.A. with NIF A28021079 (hereinafter, OPENBANK). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The complainant states that they wanted to make a transfer from their German bank to OPENBANK in Spain. After sending the necessary documentation, OPENBANK requested additional documentation containing personal data regarding the origin of the money for the transfer. The complainant asserts that they would have proven the origin of the money through emails sent to OPENBANK.  
The complainant attaches copies of the emails exchanged with OPENBANK. LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 60.8 of the GDPR and as provided in Articles 47 and 48 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to resolve these investigation proceedings.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."

In the present case, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, it is noted that there is a processing of personal data, since OPENBANK collects and retains, among others, the following personal data of individuals: name and surname, date of birth, and email address, among other processing activities.  
OPENBANK carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, under Article 4.7 of the GDPR. Furthermore, this is a cross-border processing, given that OPENBANK is established in Spain, although it provides services to other countries in the European Union.  
The GDPR provides, in its Article 56.1, for cases of cross-border processing, as provided in its Article 4.23), in relation to the competence of the main supervisory authority, that, without prejudice to the provisions of Article 55, the supervisory authority of the main establishment or the only establishment of the controller or the processor shall be competent to act as the main supervisory authority for the cross-border processing carried out by such controller or processor in accordance with the procedure established in Article 60. In the case examined, as stated, OPENBANK has its main establishment in Spain, so the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to act as the main supervisory authority.  
For its part, Article 6.1.c) of the GDPR regulates the lawfulness of processing.  
III  
Lawfulness of Processing  
Article 6.1 "Lawfulness of Processing" of the GDPR states:  
"Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
(...)  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject.  
(...)."  
In the present case, the complainant states that, when attempting to make a transfer, OPENBANK requested additional documentation containing personal data, which they believe should not have been requested.  
In this regard, OPENBANK's banking activity is subject to Spanish legislation, which includes Law 10/2010, of April 28, on the prevention of money laundering and the financing of terrorism. Article 5 of this Law establishes that obligated subjects must know, as a measure of due diligence, the purpose and nature of the business relationship, and "...in particular, obligated subjects shall collect information from their clients in order to know the...

the nature of their professional or business activity and will adopt measures aimed at reasonably verifying the truthfulness of such information.  
Such measures will consist of the establishment and application of verification procedures for the activities declared by clients. These procedures will take into account the different level of risk and will be based on obtaining documents from clients related to the declared activity or obtaining information about it from sources other than the client themselves.  
Furthermore, according to Article 7, the obligated subjects will apply each of the due diligence measures and may determine the degree of application of the measures based on the risk and depending on the type of client, business relationship, product, or operation.  
Additionally, this article states that when there are indications of money laundering or terrorism financing, they will apply due diligence measures regardless of any exception, exemption, or threshold, or when there are doubts about the truthfulness or adequacy of the data obtained previously.  
In this case, and based on the information provided, there are not sufficient indications to understand that the requested information would not be processed in accordance with Spanish legislation regarding the prevention of money laundering, or that it would not be kept with the necessary security measures.  
Moreover, according to Spanish legislation, it is sufficient for the financial entity to have doubts about the truthfulness of the information provided in order to request any information it deems appropriate.  
Therefore, based on the aforementioned paragraphs, no evidence has been found that substantiates the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.
