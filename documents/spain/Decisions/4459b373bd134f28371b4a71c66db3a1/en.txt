**LEGAL GROUNDS**  
I  
**Competence and applicable regulations**  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of the GDPR, and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  

II  
**Preliminary issues**  
In this case, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, given that BBVA collects and retains, among others, the following personal data of individuals: name and surname, address, phone number, account number, among others.  
BBVA carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it determines the purposes and means of such activity, pursuant to Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  

III  
**Claims made**  
In relation to the claims made regarding the initiation agreement of the present sanctioning procedure, responses are provided as follows.  
1. Regarding the alleged violation by BBVA of Article 5.1(f) and 32 of the GDPR  
BBVA claims to have appropriate and adequate technical or organizational security measures in place relative to the risk. In order to substantiate the existence of such measures, it provides the documentation indicated in section five of the Established Facts.  
BBVA indicates that this Agency makes an erroneous interpretation of the GDPR, as it considers that the obligation imposed by Article 32 of the GDPR on the data controller is one of results. According to the Supreme Court ruling of 15/02/2022 (cassation appeal 7359/2020), it is an obligation of means.  
BBVA acknowledges that an undesirable outcome occurred as a result of the improper actions of one of its employees, who had access to the personal data of the complainant while providing services at the bank branch where the complainant had contracted the financial product; a series of measures were adopted. However, in BBVA's opinion, the employee had sufficient information and training in data protection to understand that their actions were not appropriate.  
In this regard, this Agency wishes to point out that the documents accompanying the written claims regarding the initiation agreement of the present sanctioning procedure focus on generic issues related not only to personal data protection but also to the conduct and working methods of employees. In no case are specific measures established to be implemented by BBVA to prevent situations similar to the issue that gave rise to the present procedure.  
On the other hand, this Agency does not consider that the obligation to implement security measures imposed by data protection regulations has a nature of an obligation of results rather than of means.  
However, it is equally true that BBVA did not adopt appropriate measures that "according to the state of technology and in relation to the nature of the processing carried out and the personal data in question, reasonably allow for the prevention of their alteration, loss, unauthorized processing, or access." It cannot be understood that a large entity such as BBVA, with a business volume of billions of euros and that continuously processes data of millions of people, did not have sufficient systems or mechanisms to prevent one of its employees from disclosing information about one of its clients to an unauthorized third party.  
Moreover, the actions of the employee do not exempt BBVA from responsibility. The company's liability in the sanctioning context for the actions of an employee that constitute a breach of data protection regulations has been confirmed by the jurisprudence of the Supreme Court. In this regard, it should be noted that the Supreme Court ruling no. 188/2022 (Contentious Chamber, Section 3), dated 15 February 2022 (rec. 7359/2020), mentioned by BBVA in its written claims regarding the initiation agreement, states in its Fourth Legal Ground: "The fact that it was the negligent action of an employee does not exempt it from its responsibility regarding the proper use of the security measures that should have guaranteed the adequate use of the designed data registration system. As we already stated in STS no. 196/2020, of 15 February 2021 (rec. 1916/2020), the data controller is also responsible for the actions of its employees and cannot excuse itself based on its diligent actions, separately from the actions of its employees, but rather it is the 'culpable' actions of these, resulting from the violation of existing security measures, that underpin the company's liability in the sanctioning context for the 'own' acts of its employees or officers, not those of third parties."

2. Violation of the non bis in idem principle  
BBVA indicates that the Initiation Agreement includes a first proposal for a sanction for non-compliance with article 5.1.f) of the GDPR due to the “absence or insufficiency of those security measures leading to the appreciation that my client has not complied with the principles of security in the processing of personal data and a second proposal for a sanction for non-compliance with article 32 of the GDPR because BBVA “has not adopted the appropriate technical and organizational measures based on the estimated possible risks (in addition to the information and training directed at its employees).” Thus, the same facts (the insufficient security measures adopted by BBVA for processing personal data) would constitute two infringements of the same protected legal good; meeting the triple identity characteristic of the non bis in idem principle.  
In this regard, this Agency wishes to point out that article 5.1.f) of the GDPR is violated when there is a loss of confidentiality, integrity, or availability of personal data, which may or may not occur due to the absence or deficiency of security measures. This principle merely determines the channel through which the maintenance of confidentiality, integrity, or availability can be achieved when it specifies “by applying appropriate technical and organizational measures,” which are not strictly security measures.  
BBVA concludes that the appropriate technical and organizational measures referred to in this provision are the security measures of article 32 of the GDPR. This would simplify the essence of the GDPR, whose compliance is not limited to the implementation of technical and organizational security measures; it would mean, in our case, reducing the guarantee required by the principle of integrity and confidentiality to its achievement solely through security measures.  
As we have pointed out previously, when article 5.1.f) of the GDPR refers to appropriate technical or organizational measures to guarantee the rights and freedoms of the data subjects within the framework of compliance management of the GDPR, it does so in the sense provided in article 25 of the GDPR regarding privacy by design.  
This provision determines that,

"Taking into account the state of the art, the cost of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying probability and severity that the processing entails for the rights and freedoms of individuals, the data controller shall apply, both at the time of determining the means of processing and at the time of the processing itself, appropriate technical and organizational measures, such as pseudonymization, designed to effectively apply the principles of data protection, such as data minimization, and to integrate the necessary safeguards into the processing, in order to comply with the requirements of this Regulation and protect the rights of the data subjects" (the underlining is ours).  
This Agency reiterates that there are multiple technical or organizational measures that are not security-related and that the data controller can implement as a means to guarantee this principle.  
However, Article 32 of the GDPR includes the obligation to implement appropriate technical and organizational security measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk. Security only.  
Moreover, its objective is to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, while in the case of Article 5.1(f) of the GDPR, confidentiality and integrity must be guaranteed. As can be seen, the two articles pursue different purposes, although they may be related.  
Delving into the examination of non bis in idem, the Judgment of the National Court of July 23, 2021 (rec. 1/2017) states that:  
“(...) According to the legislation and jurisprudence presented, the principle of non bis in idem prevents punishing the same subject twice for the same act based on the same grounds, understood as the same legal interest protected by the sanctioning norms in question. Indeed, when there is a triple identity of subject, act, and grounds, the sum of sanctions creates a sanction foreign to the proportionality assessment carried out by the legislator and materializes the imposition of a legally unprovided sanction that also violates the principle of proportionality.  
But for there to be talk of "bis in idem," a triple identity must exist between the compared terms: objective (same facts), subjective (against the same subjects), and causal (for the same grounds or reason for punishment):  
a) The subjective identity implies that the affected subject must be the same, regardless of the nature or judicial or administrative authority judging and independently of who the accuser or specific body that has resolved is, or whether it is judged alone or in conjunction with other affected parties.  
b) The factual identity implies that the judged facts must be the same, and excludes cases of real concurrence of infractions where there is not a single unlawful act but several.  
c) The identity of grounds or causal identity implies that the sanctioning measures cannot coincide if they respond to the same nature, that is, if they share the same teleological foundation, which occurs between criminal and administrative sanctions, but not between punitive and merely coercive measures.”

Taking as a reference what has been previously stated in the sanctioning procedure, the principle of non bis in idem has not been violated, since, although the facts are broadly understood as a consequence of a violation of personal data security, the infringement of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR specifically concerns a clear loss of confidentiality, while the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR pertains to the absence and deficiencies of the security measures (only security) detected, which are present independently of the violation of personal data security. In fact, if the security measures that BBVA had implemented had been detected by this Agency without the loss of confidentiality occurring, it would have only been sanctioned under Article 32 of the GDPR. This is in response to the allegations made by BBVA, which considers that both provisions require a single conduct, which is to implement adequate security. This is not true, since Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR is not limited to ensuring adequate security relative to the risk, but rather to ensuring integrity and availability. And not only through security measures, but through all types of appropriate technical or organizational measures.  
As we have indicated, Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR sanctions a loss of confidentiality only, while Article 32 of the GDPR addresses the absence and deficiencies of the security measures implemented by the data controller. We add that absent or deficient security measures infringe the GDPR regardless of whether or not there has been a loss of confidentiality and availability.  
Therefore, the principle of non bis in idem is not considered violated in the present sanctioning procedure.  
3. Subsidiarily, existence of medial concurrence between the two behaviors attributed to BBVA  
BBVA argues that the Initiation Agreement identifies a plurality of infringements that the entity would have supposedly committed when, in reality, one of them would be subsumed within the other, resulting in medial concurrence in the terms provided in Article 29.5 of Law 40/2015, of October 1, on the Legal Regime of the Public Sector. In this regard, this Agency wishes to remind that Article 29 of the LRJSP does not apply to the sanctioning regime imposed by the GDPR.  
The GDPR is a community regulation directly applicable in the Member States, which contains a new, closed, complete, and global system aimed at ensuring the protection of personal data uniformly throughout the European Union.  
In relation, specifically and also, to the sanctioning regime provided therein, its provisions apply immediately, directly, and integrally, providing a complete and gapless system that must be understood, interpreted, and integrated absolutely, completely, and integrally, thus leaving intact.

VII  
Security of processing  
Article 32 of the GDPR, “Security of processing,” establishes:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly verifying, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a consequence of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data only processes such data on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law.”  
In this case, at the time of the security breach, BBVA did not adopt sufficient technical and organizational security measures to prevent an employee from disclosing customer information to an unauthorized third party, thereby exposing their personal and financial data.  
While it is true that BBVA had some measures directed at employees regarding the protection of personal data, such as providing information and training; it is evident that the measures are insufficient to prevent an incident similar to the issue that gave rise to this procedure. Furthermore, it is not enough for BBVA, as the data controller, to implement security measures; it must also ensure their compliance.  
For all these reasons, in accordance with the evidence available at this moment in the proposed resolution of the sanctioning procedure, and without prejudice to what results from the instruction, it is considered that the known facts could constitute an infringement, attributable to BBVA, for violating Article 32 of the GDPR.
