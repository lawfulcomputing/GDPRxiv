BACKGROUND  
FIRST: Ms. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the claimant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on June 29, 2022. The complaint is directed against "Envialia."  
The complaint states:  
"Today, June 28 at 14:30, the courier called me from phone ***PHONE.1 to deliver a package from Carrefour. Since I was not at home, I instructed him to leave it with my neighbor on the first left, B.B.B. He said that was fine. When I arrived home 20 minutes later, my neighbor told me that nothing had been delivered to him. I called the transporter, and he told me that a guy in a cap passed by the entrance and said it was him, and he handed it over without further ado. I have complained to the transport company, and they tell me that a certain C.C.C. took it and that I should check with the neighbors, when they should be doing that. It is not only the lack of a solution for the literal theft of my package, but also that it contained, in addition to my goods, all my personal data, ID, phone number, address, name, surname, and an invoice of the purchased items with my banking details, data that I have never authorized them to give to a stranger who can use it unlawfully, causing me great harm. Therefore, I ask for your intervention."  
Along with the complaint, a thread of emails exchanged with "Envialia" is provided.

II  
Preliminary Issues  
In this case, THE BEE LOGISTICS was the company responsible for delivering the package to the claimant in the manner it had agreed with them; however, it delivered it to a third party without the consent of the claimant.  
THE BEE LOGISTICS is a company engaged in the development of postal and mail activities, including the transportation of goods by road, inland waterways, sea, or air, with any vehicle and load, as well as the transportation of passengers by inland waterways and other activities ancillary to land transport.  
In the exercise of this activity, THE BEE LOGISTICS carries out, among other treatments, the collection, consultation, use, and access to the following personal data of clients, such as: name, surname, ID number, foreign identification number, and/or passport, postal address, phone number, etc.  
THE BEE LOGISTICS carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
Article 4, section 12 of the GDPR broadly defines "personal data security breaches" (hereinafter security breach) as "all breaches of security that lead to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or unauthorized communication or access to such data."  
In this case, there is a personal data security breach under the circumstances mentioned above, categorized as a confidentiality breach since the personal data of the claimant (such as name and surname, postal address) were exposed to a third party when receiving a shipment of which they were the intended recipient.  
It should be noted that the receipt of a complaint regarding a security breach does not imply the direct imposition of a sanction; as it is necessary to analyze...

III  
Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR  
Article 5.1.f) “Principles relating to processing” of the GDPR states:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
(...)  
f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, by means of appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).”  
On June 28, 2022, the complainant received a call from an employee, as stated by the complainant, of ENVIALIA, responsible for managing a shipment of which the complainant was the recipient, sent by the company CARREFOUR to arrange the delivery of a package.  
As the complainant was not at home at that moment, they instructed the courier to leave the package at the neighbor's house on the 1st left, named B.B.B. However, their neighbor stated that they had not received any package, prompting the complainant to call the courier, who said that upon entering the building where the complainant's residence is located, a person told him that he was the one in charge of picking up the package and handed it over to him without further ado.  
In this case, it is noted that the personal data of the complainant, including their name and surname, as well as their postal address, were improperly exposed to a third party as a result of the receipt of the shipment of which they were the recipient, with THE BEE LOGISTICS being the company responsible for the delivery of the package.  
IV  
Classification of the infringement of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR  
The aforementioned infringement of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR constitutes the commission of the infringements classified in Article 83.5 of the GDPR, which under the heading “General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines” provides:  
“Violations of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an undertaking, an amount equivalent to 4% of the total annual global turnover of the preceding financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9; (…)”  
In this regard, the LOPDGDD, in its Article 71 “Infringements” establishes that “Acts and behaviors referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those that are contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements.”  
For the purposes of the limitation period, Article 72 “Very serious infringements” of the LOPDGDD indicates:  
“1. In accordance with what is established in Article 83.5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following shall be considered very serious and shall be subject to a three-year limitation period: infringements that constitute a substantial violation of the articles mentioned therein and, in particular, the following:  
a) The processing of personal data in violation of the principles and guarantees established in Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679. (…)”

VI  
Article 32 of the GDPR  
Article 32 "Security of processing" of the GDPR establishes:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a consequence of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data only processes such data on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law.”  
In the present case, at the time of the breach, it is noted that THE BEE LOGISTICS did not have reasonable security measures in place based on the estimated potential risks.  
The delivery of a shipment to a third party different from the one previously agreed upon with the recipient evidences the lack of appropriate technical and organizational security measures to ensure a level of security adequate to the risk in the processing of the personal data of the recipients of the shipments.
