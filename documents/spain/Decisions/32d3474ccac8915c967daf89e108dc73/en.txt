LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
According to the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."

II  
Before addressing the substance of the matter, it is necessary to respond to the complaint of the respondent regarding the date of commission of the facts, which is determined in the complaint submitted on 10/01/23.  
The fact that there was an "error" in the duplication of dates referring to 2024 does not cause defenselessness to the respondent, not only because they have the possibility of requesting a copy of the documentation contained in the administrative file; but also because they are aware of the date of installation of the video surveillance system.  
The respondent has been aware of the facts subject to transfer, as well as the fact that the installed system did not have the necessary legal backing, as it had not been legally exposed to the entire group of owners of the community.  
Likewise, the respondent has acknowledged taking the necessary measures to try to ensure that the system complies with current legality, an aspect that was specified in a later phase after the transfer of the facts by this agency.  
Thus, it is appropriate to dismiss the claim of defenselessness or lack of specification of the date of the facts subject to accusation, as their right to defense is not affected.  
III  
In the present case, the complaint dated 10/01/24 is examined, through which the following is conveyed:  
“installation of video surveillance cameras directed towards community areas without having the required authorization” (folio No. 1).  
The facts, therefore, are focused on determining whether there is the corresponding legal backing for the installation of the cameras at the time they were installed.  
Article 17.3 LPH (Law 14/1960) states the following:  
“The establishment or suppression of concierge, security, or other common services of general interest, whether or not they modify the constitutive title or the statutes, will require the favorable vote of three-fifths of the total number of owners who, in turn, represent three-fifths of the participation quotas.” (*the bold text belongs to this Agency).  
Article 6 GDPR (Regulation EU 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016):  
“Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:

e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller; (…).  
The GDPR maintains the principle that all data processing must be based on a legitimate foundation. It must be supported by one of the enabling bases established in a closed list in Article 6.1 GDPR.  
Therefore, the corresponding item on the Agenda must include the presentation, if applicable, of the installation of the mentioned “system,” explaining its purpose, affected areas, corresponding information, and it must expressly state the approval for its installation with the appropriate majorities and legal requirements.  
III  
In accordance with the evidence available in this warning procedure, it is considered that the party being complained against, as stated by the complainant, “has a video surveillance camera system without the proper authorization from the homeowners' association.”  
The known facts constitute an infringement attributable to the party being complained against under Article 6 GDPR, as reflected above, due to the lack of a legitimizing basis at the time the stated facts occurred.  
Article 83.5 GDPR provides: “Infringements of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an undertaking, an amount equivalent to up to 4% of the total annual global turnover of the preceding financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9 (…).  
Article 72, paragraph 1 of the LOPDGDD (LO 3/2018, December 5) regarding the statute of limitations for very serious infringements “shall expire after three years” and in particular the following:  
b) The processing of personal data without any of the conditions for lawful processing established in Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.

IV  
Adoption of measures  
The text of the resolution establishes what the infringements committed were and the facts that led to the violation of data protection regulations, from which it is clearly inferred what measures are to be adopted, without prejudice to the fact that the type of procedures, mechanisms, or specific instruments for implementing them correspond to the sanctioned party, as it is the data controller who fully knows their organization and must decide, based on proactive responsibility and a risk-based approach, how to comply with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.  
The party being complained against, as evidenced in a document dated 06/05/24, has proceeded to adopt measures to gather the necessary signatures to provide legal backing for the installed system, so no additional measures can be imposed.  
For the purpose of completing the content of this resolution, it is reminded that the cameras must be necessary to fulfill the intended purpose, and they must be properly informed through informative sign(s) in a visible area, specifying the responsible party for access to the images, as well as complying with all other legally required requirements, which can be consulted on the website of this agency www.aepd.es Guide Video Surveillance.
