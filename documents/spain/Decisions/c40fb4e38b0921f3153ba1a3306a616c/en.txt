II  
Preliminary issues  
In this case, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, since MOONTON was collecting and storing, among others, the following personal data of individuals: email address and IP address, among other processing activities.  
MOONTON was carrying out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it was the one determining the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
III  
Data minimization  
Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR "Principles relating to processing" establishes:  
"1. Personal data shall be:  
(...)  
c) adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed ('data minimization'); (…)”  
In this case, MOONTON had granted access to the Moderators to the following personal data of users located in Spain of the Forum: (i) username in the Forum and user identification number (User ID); (ii) the number of visits of users to the user spaces of the Forum; (iii) the gender declared by users (e.g., male, female, or undisclosed); and (iv) activity in the Forum, which includes "reputation," "wealth," and "contribution" to the Forum, all linked to users' posts, participation in the forum, and interactions between users.  
While MOONTON has highlighted that the only non-public information for all Forum users to which the moderators had access was: (i) the email address provided by users to register in the Forum; (ii) the registration date in the Forum; (iii) the date and time of the last activity of each user in the Forum; and (iv) the user's IP address (both the registration IP address and the last visit IP address).  
The purpose for which access to such data was granted to the Moderators was to moderate the Forums of MOONTON's games, for which MOONTON had granted them administrator permissions that allowed them to access this information.

XII  
Notification to the supervisory authority  
Article 33 “Notification of a personal data breach to the supervisory authority” of the GDPR establishes:  
“1. In the case of a personal data breach, the data controller shall notify the competent supervisory authority in accordance with Article 55 without undue delay and, where feasible, no later than 72 hours after becoming aware of it, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. If the notification to the supervisory authority does not take place within 72 hours, it shall be accompanied by the reasons for the delay.  
2. The processor shall notify the controller without undue delay of any personal data breaches of which it becomes aware.  
3. The notification referred to in paragraph 1 shall, at a minimum:  
a) describe the nature of the personal data breach, including, where possible, the categories and approximate number of affected data subjects, and the categories and approximate number of personal data records affected;  
b) communicate the name and contact details of the data protection officer or another contact point where more information can be obtained;  
c) describe the possible consequences of the personal data breach;  
d) describe the measures taken or proposed by the controller to remedy the personal data breach, including, where appropriate, the measures taken to mitigate any possible negative effects.
