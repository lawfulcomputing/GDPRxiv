From the procedure initiated by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  
BACKGROUND  
FIRST: Receipt of complaint dated 22/11/2022  
On 22/11/2022, a complaint (hereinafter, the complainant) was received by this AEPD against CLUB ATLÉTICO OSASUNA with NIF G31080179 (hereinafter, C.A. OSASUNA). The grounds for the complaint are that C.A. OSASUNA established a biometric facial recognition system (hereinafter SBRF) in April 2022 for the access of spectators to its stadium El Sadar.  
Attached is the press release published on 22/05/2022 by elespanol.com, at: https://www.elespanol.com/invertia/disruptores-innovadores/innovadores/tecnologicas/20220522/accesos-rapidos-seguros-sistema-reconocimiento-laliga-operativo/673682724_0.html.  
In summary, the news indicates that it has been a process in which La Liga has participated from the beginning, as the entity responsible for managing access to the stadiums of the First and Second Divisions, of the three existing methods for accessing the stadium, and that the process, currently available for members who register through the website, would be an optional method of access to the stadium.  
The complainant indicates that freedoms and fundamental rights are restricted, and that the lack of proportionality does not make the processing legitimate, even with any potential consent associated with its implementation.  
SECOND: Agreement of the Director to initiate preliminary investigation proceedings.  
As a result of the complaint and the news published in the press, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection sent a letter on 5/12/2022 to the General Subdirectorate of Data Inspection, for the purpose of conducting preliminary inspection proceedings to verify the proper observance of the regulations.  
THIRD: First request to C.A. OSASUNA during the preliminary investigation proceedings.  
The General Subdirectorate of Data Inspection proceeded to carry out preliminary investigation proceedings AI/00417/2022 to clarify the facts in question, by virtue of the functions assigned to the supervisory authorities in Article 57.1 and the powers granted in Article 58.1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and in accordance with the provisions of Title VII, Chapter I, Section Two, of the LOPDGDD. On 14/02/2023, C.A. OSASUNA was requested to provide information “related to the processing of biometric data referred to in the news published in the media.”  
On the other hand, it should also be noted that, at that time, other proceedings (file number EXP202213792) were being carried out in which the situation regarding the use of biometric systems for access to stadiums was being investigated (AI/00444/2022).

The DPIA provided analyzes the concurrence of the requirements necessary for the validity of consent in its section 5.2.3 (in particular, its nature as a manifestation of free, specific, informed, and unequivocal will), as well as the viability of this basis of legitimacy in the processing of biometric data, concluding in the validity of said legal basis."  
Literally, the DPIA states, within section 5 "Legal basis of consent," after indicating that consent must be lawful, for which it is required to comply with one of the legal bases of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, it proceeds to analyze whether there may be any cause that lifts the prohibition of processing, due to being a special data, "since only if any of the prohibitions is applicable would the study of the legal basis of processing make sense."  
In the following section of the DPIA, 5.2.1, it specifies that "the application of facial recognition techniques for biometric identification, and the consequent processing of the personal identifying data of the interested party linked to their facial pattern, or simply of the latter, has usually been the subject of analysis within the framework of public surveillance and security actions.  
Therefore, as this surveillance is based on systems that do not allow for the discrimination of users (i.e., the collection of the facial pattern of all individuals exposed to recording or data collection by a particular device occurs), consent is not analyzed in those cases as a legal basis for processing, as its obtaining is not possible or the fulfillment of the requirements demanded for it is evidently compromised (for example, consent cannot be considered to be given merely by the fact of crossing the area where data collection occurs and, at the same time, that consent is hardly free, given that the data will be collected in any case, without the interested party being able to refuse it or revoke a supposed consent given)."  
The DPIA considers that under the analysis of, for example, the Guidelines 3/2019 of the EDPB adopted on 29/01/2020, regarding the processing of personal data through video devices, it infers the existence of situations in which consent could be a basis.

1.3 Proportionality assessments carried out for the execution of activities through the processing of biometric data.  
It was responded that this is contained in section 6.4 of the DPIA, first analyzing the fact that the processing of biometric data of the data subjects “is suitable for achieving the purpose of recognition for access control—following the doctrine established by the AEPD in the resolution of procedure PS/00120/2021 (Mercadona case) and in the report of the Legal Advisory Office 47/2021,” “highlighting that the system, unlike those analyzed in the aforementioned AEPD documents, would be based on consent, thus recognition would only occur for those individuals who request it and freely authorize it.”  
- Section 6.4.3 of the DPIA, framed within the analysis of the principle of data minimization, and more specifically, of the “criteria regarding the application of the principle of minimization in the processing of biometric data (6.4.2), indicates that ‘the following must be taken into account to assess the necessity and proportionality of the processing:’”  
A- The processing will be limited solely to those who voluntarily wish to make use of this procedure; it is neither indiscriminate nor massive, preserving the alternative means of access.  
B- The processing involves an identification process, “and not authentication, which would be less invasive; however: it occurs on vectors and not directly on images or biometric information, and the search for correspondences between vectors would be limited not to all individuals accessing the stadium.”  
C- No remote facial recognition is performed in completely open public areas; rather, the identification process occurs just a few centimeters from the machine, in front of which the user must stand less than ONE meter from the terminal for their data to be captured, making it impossible to attempt “vectorization” of other individuals who do not have the specific intention of accessing the sports venue through the solution.  
D- “Only data will be processed based on the consent of the data subjects who freely wish to make use of the solution, which should be positively weighed for the purposes of verifying the proportionality of the processing, given that it is possible to appreciate from the conduct of the data subject, the concurrence in the processing of a mutual benefit for the Club and the individual, which streamlines access to the stadium.”  
E- The use of the solution is limited to subscribers over 14 years of age by virtue of Article 7 of the LOPDGDD in connection with Article 8 of the GDPR—the age will also be verified during the registration process, as the scanned image of the ID card is processed.”  
- It adds that this occurs within the framework of Article 11.1 of the Anti-Violence Law, which establishes that “all sports venues where state competitions are held…”

C.A. OSASUNA considers that the processing exceeds the triple test of suitability, necessity, and proportionality, indicating that:  
“In effect, the processing of the data carried out for registration in the solution is, as will be seen later, suitable for fulfilling the process of registration, as it allows for the full identification of the subscriber. Likewise, the processing of the facial vector is suitable for facilitating access to the El Sadar stadium through the readers incorporated into some of the entry turnstiles, by simply reading the image of the interested party and comparing it with the aforementioned facial vector.  
Secondly, the processing is necessary to achieve the purpose pursued by the processing, which is to facilitate access to the sports venue for those OSASUNA subscribers who wish to do so, through an agile and simple procedure. Certainly, at this point, it is possible to achieve the intended purpose (access to the El Sadar stadium) using a less intrusive means (the use of traditionally employed means, such as the use of a QR code reader incorporated into the subscription). However, the implementation of the processing analyzed in this DPIA does not prevent the use of this option. That is to say, the interested party can freely decide, without it affecting their rights as a subscriber, to opt for one treatment or another for access to the stadium. Thus, the necessity would be linked in this case to the will of the subscriber, which cannot be satisfied using a less intrusive means of access.  
Finally, regarding compliance with proportionality, it should be noted what the AEPD indicated in the previously reproduced documents, in the sense that this principle would be violated if subscribers were imposed, without any possible alternative, access to the venue through the described procedure and it could not be appreciated the concurrence of “more benefits or advantages for the general interest than detriments to other conflicting goods or values.” However, in this case, it is the subscriber themselves who weighs the preference between one system or another (at the time of registration and, additionally, at each access) and the prevalence of their particular interest over the rights they may consider affected, without their personal integrity being affected in any other way…”

It was answered that “the biometric comparison process is as follows:  
(i) “When a biometric access attempt occurs, the frame captured by the camera of the person activating the System is taken when they are positioned less than one meter from the screen and facing it;  
(ii) A face detection inference is performed on that frame.  
(iii) If the detected face exceeds a sufficient threshold of size and proximity to the biometric terminal, and quality in detection, it is processed in such a way that a mathematical vector calculated by the neural network is generated.  
(iv) The calculated vector for the detected face on the frame captured at that moment is compared based on mathematical distance with the vectors registered for the users enrolled in the System. It should be clarified that current biometric technology does not perform these comparisons based on characteristic points of the face, such as mouth size, distance between eyes, or curvature of the brow arch.  
(v) It is considered that the person present in front of the camera at that moment is identified as one of the registered individuals when the similarity between both vectors (the registered one and the one processed at that moment) exceeds the established threshold.  
(vi) Additionally, inferences are made to determine if that face is authentic and not an attempt at impersonation (such as, for example, presenting a printed image, using a mask, presenting an image or video from a screen, etc.).  
Therefore, for an ongoing biometric access attempt, the person will only be identified as registered if all the previous requirements are met: 1) a sufficiently close face is detected (less than ONE meter from the terminal), with sufficient size and quality, in the frame being processed, 2) the degree of similarity between the mathematical vector calculated for the detected face and the vector registered for the user is met, and 3) it is considered that it is not an attempt at impersonation.
