From the proceedings carried out by the Spanish Agency for the Protection of Data and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: Ms. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for the Protection of Data on 15/03/2023. The complaint is directed against AECORP 005, S.L. with NIF B05470380. The reasons for the complaint are as follows: the complainant states that the company AECORP 005 "is engaged in selling electricity contracts as an agent of the marketer FACTOR ENERGÍA S.A." She indicates that on 07/02/2023, she received a call in which they identified themselves as her energy company, offering her a more advantageous rate. The complainant points out that they had all her personal data, receiving after the call a text message with the request for electronic signature of the contract. Finally, she receives a text message confirming the contract, despite indicating that she did not accept the message. On 14/03/2023, she receives a charge in her bank account from FACTOR ENERGÍA.  
She provides:  
- A screenshot from her mobile showing an SMS from 07/02/2023 with the request for electronic signature of the contract and a second SMS with the confirmation of said signature on the same date.  
- A screenshot showing the details of the contracting request with the marketer FACTOR and date of receipt 22/02/2023, type of annual contract.  
- A copy of the receipt dated 14/03/2023 in the name of the complainant showing a creditor to FACTOR.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), on 04/05/2023, the complaint was forwarded to FACTOR and AECORP, for them to analyze it and inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in the data protection regulations.  
The forwarding, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was received by FACTOR on 12/05/2023 as evidenced by the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  
AECORP was not located within the timeframe for the forwarding of the complaint, as the notification to AECORP expired due to lapse and the reiteration of the request via postal services was returned as unknown despite having been sent to the address confirmed as correct by the AEAT. On 29/11/2023, the notification of reiteration sent on 16/05/2023 was accepted through the DEHÚ service.

Analysis of the case  
1. The facts revealed in this complaint materialize in the alleged fraudulent contracting for the supply of electricity to the complainant.  
Article 6 of the GDPR, Lawfulness of processing, provides in its paragraph 1:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provisions of point f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.”  
Also, Recital 40 of the GDPR states that “For processing to be lawful, personal data must be processed with the consent of the data subject or on another legitimate basis established by law, as long as…”

It is necessary to point out that Administrative Sanctioning Law, due to its specificity, applies, with some nuances but without exceptions, the guiding principles of criminal law, resulting in the clear full applicability of the principle of the presumption of innocence.  
The Constitutional Court in its Judgment 76/1990, of April 26, regarding the right to the presumption of innocence and the principle of culpability establishes:  
"There can be no doubt that the presumption of innocence applies without exceptions in the sanctioning legal framework and must be respected in the imposition of any sanctions, whether criminal, administrative in general, or tax in particular, since the exercise of ius puniendi in its various manifestations is conditioned by the evidence and a contradictory procedure in which the parties can defend their positions. In this sense, the right to the presumption of innocence entails: that the sanction is based on acts or incriminating evidence of the reproached conduct; that the burden of proof lies with the accuser, with no one being obliged to prove their innocence; and that any insufficiency in the results of the evidence presented, freely evaluated by the sanctioning body, must result in an acquittal."  
The Judgment of the Constitutional Court of 20/02/1989 indicates that "Our doctrine and criminal jurisprudence have maintained that, although both can be considered manifestations of a generic favor rei, there is a substantial difference between the right to the presumption of innocence, which unfolds its effectiveness when there is an absolute lack of evidence or when the evidence presented does not meet procedural guarantees, and the jurisprudential principle in dubio pro reo, which pertains to the moment of evaluation or appreciation of evidence, and which must be judged when, if that indispensable evidentiary activity occurs, there is a rational doubt about the actual concurrence of the objective and subjective elements that make up the criminal type in question."  

In short, those principles prevent attributing an administrative infraction when no incriminating evidence has been obtained and accredited regarding the facts that motivate this accusation or the involvement of the alleged infringer in them, applying the principle "in dubio pro reo" in case of doubt regarding a specific and decisive fact, which obliges, in any case, to resolve that doubt in the most favorable manner for the interested party.  
Therefore, it must be concluded that after analyzing the claimed facts and the actions carried out by this Agency, there are insufficient elements of judgment to attribute to the accused party the violation of the regulations on data protection, in addition to the fact that said entity acted with reasonable diligence in verifying the identity of the complaining party.
