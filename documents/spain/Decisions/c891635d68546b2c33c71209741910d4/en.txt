FACTS  
FIRST: On August 17, 2023, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202306651 issued a resolution in the following terms,  
“FIRST: TO IMPOSE on THE BEE LOGISTICS, S.L., with NIF B88640735, for an infringement of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR, a fine of €50,000 (FIFTY THOUSAND EUROS).  
TO IMPOSE on THE BEE LOGISTICS, S.L., with NIF B88640735, for an infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR classified under Article 83.4 of the GDPR, a fine of €20,000 (TWENTY THOUSAND EUROS).”

The subcontracting of THE BEE LOGISTICS to Mr. A.A.A. for the provision of services does not exempt it from its responsibilities as the data controller.  
In Article 4 of the GDPR, points 7 and 8 specify what is to be understood by data controller and data processor. Thus we have:  
“7) ‘data controller’ or ‘controller’ means the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be provided for by the law of the Union or of the Member States;  
8) ‘data processor’ or ‘processor’ means the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which processes personal data on behalf of the controller;…”  
In short, the data controller is the natural or legal person or public authority that decides on the processing of personal data, determining the purposes and means of such processing.

By virtue of the principle of proactive responsibility, the data controller must apply technical and organizational measures to comply with and be able to demonstrate compliance, taking into account the risk involved in the processing of personal data.  
For its part, the data processor is the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that provides a service to the controller that involves the processing of personal data on behalf of the controller.  
In this sense, the controller is the one who decides the "why" and the "how" regarding personal data, and the processor is the one who carries out the processing on behalf of the controller.  
The role of the data processor in the GDPR is defined in Article 28, which establishes the requirements that must be met regarding data protection, highlighting, for the purposes of this case, its paragraph 4:  
4. When a data processor engages another processor to carry out certain processing activities on behalf of the controller, the same data protection obligations as those stipulated in the contract or other legal act between the controller and the processor referred to in paragraph 3 shall be imposed on this other processor, through a contract or other legal act established in accordance with Union or Member State law, in particular the provision of sufficient guarantees for the application of appropriate technical and organizational measures in such a way that the processing is in compliance with the provisions of this Regulation. If that other processor fails to comply with its data protection obligations, the initial processor shall remain fully liable to the data controller for compliance with the obligations of the other processor. (…)  
We must remember in this regard that these specific obligations may be supervised by data protection authorities, without prejudice to the oversight that may be carried out regarding compliance with the GDPR or the LOPDGDD by the controller or the data processor.

FOURTH: On the measures that THE BEE LOGISTICS is implementing in its internal structure to prevent a situation like the one that occurred from happening again.  
It is important to emphasize that THE BEE LOGISTICS has never proactively violated any law or regulation that could apply to it. An example of this is that since its foundation, it has never received any fines or warnings of any kind, and it is not its intention for this to change, which is why it is implementing (and will continue to implement) the necessary measures and solutions to be fully compliant with the GDPR. Some examples of the steps and actions that THE BEE LOGISTICS is currently implementing to comply with data protection regulations, both at the European and national levels, are detailed below:  
- As a first step, it has decided to definitively discontinue the services of this provider “Gestoría Gefirinet,” centralizing all notification reception and management services, along with legal advice, in Delvy, as well as initiating the process of registering Delvy as its DPO so that all matters related to data protection are under the protection and control of specialized professionals who help THE BEE LOGISTICS to be fully compliant with the GDPR.  
- Training sessions have been organized for the month of November, which will be conducted by specialized data protection lawyers from Delvy, so that our employees and internal transporters are aware of their obligations in this area and how to act in each situation to avoid any type of security breach.  
- Delvy has begun to review and, if there is no written contract, to draft contracts with suppliers and clients so that all contractual relationships of THE BEE LOGISTICS are subject to written conditions and obligations regarding data protection depending on the role that each party is to assume (data controller-processor-sub-processor).  
- Delvy will review all documentation related to data protection (rights forms, activity logs, etc.) and, if necessary, will update it so that THE BEE LOGISTICS is fully adapted to the GDPR, paying special attention to reinforcing security measures and the actions of the responsible individuals or companies in the event of a security breach.  
Regarding the measures implemented by THE BEE LOGISTICS, it should be mentioned that all security measures adopted to prevent incidents like the one that occurred fall within its obligations to comply with data protection regulations; since, as the data controller, it is obliged to apply appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk presented by the data processing.  
However, this agency positively values the security measures adopted subsequently; although this does not negate the commission of the aforementioned infringements.  
In relation to the documentation and information provided by the appellant, we must mention that the figure of the data processor in the GDPR is defined in the aforementioned Article 28, which establishes the requirements that must be met regarding data protection, in which it is expressly stated that:
