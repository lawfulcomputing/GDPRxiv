FACTS  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint on February 8, 2021, with the data protection authority of Norway (Datatilsynet). The complaint is directed against FUNDACIÓN NORUEGA COSTA BLANCA with NIF G53739256 (hereinafter, "the FOUNDATION"). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The school FUNDACIÓN NORUEGA COSTA BLANCA, where the complainant worked, had a directory in the tool ***HERRAMIENTA.1, called “interne ansatte” [Unofficial translation: “internal employees”], which contained documents with reports on meetings and files that specifically and identifiably referred to staff at the center. This directory could be accessed by any employee of the school. The day after the complainant accessed this information, it was deleted, leading the complainant to suspect that the actions of employees in ***HERRAMIENTA.1 were being monitored by the school management without the employees' knowledge.  
Along with the complaint, the following is provided:  
- Printout of an email sent among school members, excluding the complainant, referencing a workplace accident that the complainant had related to a defect in the schoolyard roof.  
- Copy of the minutes of two meetings dated July 18, 2020, in which the complainant is mentioned as a candidate for a position at the school.  
- Copy of the minutes of a meeting dated September 23, 2020, discussing aspects regarding the hiring of the complainant and questions posed along with their answers, and an assessment of the complainant's suitability for the position.  
- Copy of the minutes of a meeting with an agency dated September 23, 2020, detailing a job offer made to the complainant.

Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  
II  
Preliminary Issues  
In the present case, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, it is established that there is a processing of personal data, given that the FOUNDATION collects and retains, among others, the following personal data of individuals: name and surname, among other processing activities.  
The FOUNDATION carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one that determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
III  
Principle of Presumption of Innocence  
In this case, it is claimed that the personal data of the affected individuals, contained in the FOUNDATION's database, were improperly exposed to third parties, since there was a directory in the ***TOOL.1 tool, called “interne ansatte” [Unofficial Translation: “internal employees”], which contained documents with reports on meetings and files that specifically and identifiably referred to personnel of the center. And that this directory could be accessed by any employee of the school.  
However, this circumstance has not been verified by this Agency during the on-site inspection dated October 18, 2023.  
The above must be connected with the validity in our Administrative Law of the principle of presumption of innocence recognized in Article 24.2 of the Spanish Constitution, in such a way that the exercise of the State's sanctioning power, in its various manifestations, is conditioned by the evidence and a contradictory procedure in which the parties can defend their positions.  
The principle of presumption of innocence prevents attributing an administrative infringement when there has not been obtained and verified evidence that substantiates the facts motivating the accusation or the involvement of the alleged infringer in them.  
In this case, it is not established that the data of the complaining party were accessible to all employees of the FOUNDATION.

The Constitutional Court (SSTC 131/2003 and 242/2005, among others) has ruled in this regard by indicating that one of the requirements inherent to the right to the presumption of innocence is that the sanction must be based on acts or incriminating evidence of the conduct attributed, and that the public administration acting bears the burden of proof regarding the commission of the administrative offense and the participation of the accused in it.  
For its part, Article 28.1 of Law 40/2015, of October 1, on the Legal Regime of the Public Sector establishes as one of the principles of the sanctioning power the principle of “Responsibility” and determines in this regard that:  
“Only individuals and legal entities, as well as, when a law recognizes them the capacity to act, groups of affected individuals, unions, and entities without legal personality and independent or autonomous assets, that are found responsible for the same due to intent or negligence, may be sanctioned for acts constituting an administrative infringement.”  
Likewise, it must be taken into account what is established in Article 53.2 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations, which states that:  
“In addition to the rights provided for in the previous section, in the case of administrative procedures of a sanctioning nature, the alleged responsible parties shall have the following rights: (…)  
b) To the presumption of non-existence of administrative responsibility until proven otherwise.”  
Therefore, based on the indications in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found that substantiates the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
All of this is without prejudice to any subsequent actions that this Agency may carry out, applying the investigative and corrective powers it holds.  
Thus, as it has not been possible to attribute responsibility for the processing, in accordance with what has been indicated, by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, IT IS AGREED:

THIRD: TO COMMUNICATE the decision to the data protection authority of Norway, to which the complaint has been submitted, so that it may notify A.A.A.
