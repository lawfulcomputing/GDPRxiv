I  
The Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve the present appeal, in accordance with the provisions of Article 123 of the LPACAP and Article 48.1 of the LOPDGDD.  

II  
Regarding the statements made by the appellant, it must be noted that throughout the procedure, the respondent defended that the data controller is the (…), and that during the evidentiary period, it was requested to provide, if any existed, any provision, of whatever rank, that attributed such competence, and the respondent reiterated, just as now in the appeal, that such a responsible party is listed in the RAT, although it also stated in evidence that: “The data controller became aware of the use of the aforementioned platform by the CMNSL when it received notification through the DPD Office of MINISDEF of the complaint filed by the interested party before the AEPD.”  

Regarding the role and performance attributed by law to data controllers and their data processors, it is necessary to mention, without needing to be cited, the various articles in the GDPR and the LOPDGDD that establish their legal obligations, and their non-transferable responsibilities by their own will to another body or center.  

As for the appellant's interpretation of the role played by the Director of the educational center in this case regarding the contracting of the data processing task, it should be indicated:  
a) The appellant interprets that the online subscription of the GOOGLE service by the School, although subscribed by its Director, would meet the requirements of Article 28 of the GDPR by attributing to the education regulations the capacity to collect data. However, the regulation indicates that it is the centers that may collect the data of their students, necessary for the exercise of their educational function. To this end, it is necessary to differentiate who collects the data from who is the data controller. The reference in the “Guide for Educational Centers” is generic information, with nuances existing thereafter. Thus, apart from the publication mentioned by the appellant, which was not indicated in the resolution, in “Who is the data controller in an educational center?”, there is an entry dated 15/09/2021 on the AEPD website in which a PDF informational strip indicates the distinction between interested parties, controllers, and processors, stating that the data controller is:  
“Natural or legal person, public authority, service, or body that determines the purposes and means of the processing carried out. The data controllers of personal data processed by public educational centers are usually the Educational Administration (Department of Education of the Autonomous Community). For the centers in Ceuta, Melilla, and those abroad, the responsible party is the Ministry of Education and Vocational Training. In the processing of personal data carried out by concerted and private centers, the responsible party is the educational center itself (foundation, religious congregation, cooperative, etc.)”  

In the section on processors:

"Natural or legal person, authority, service, or body that processes data on behalf of the controller. The controller determines to whom they delegate the processing that will be carried out. They must be chosen solely from those who provide sufficient guarantees of compliance with data protection regulations. The processor must always act within the framework of the instructions set by the controller. The flow of personal data between the controller and the processor does not constitute a transfer of data. Examples of processors in the educational field: providers of technological services, companies that manage the school cafeteria, the school route, extracurricular activities, etc."  
While it is true that the Director of the Menor Nuestra Señora de Loreto School signed the online agreement with GOOGLE, it was already indicated in the resolution that they were not the controller of the aforementioned processing, and that no contract or legal act containing the elements of Article 28 of the GDPR was provided.  
b) That the Director of a private educational institution, in this case, is not considered the controller of the processing, because there is already one designated in this case, does not mean that they are the processor, as it is usual for the educational institution, its teachers, and other staff, including, where applicable, the management, to refer and render responsibilities regarding the processing responsibility to the one designated as such.  
The European Data Protection Committee, in the development of the functions attributed by Article 70.1.e) of the GDPR, issued Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of "data controller" and "data processor" in the GDPR. In point 76, it states:  
“To be considered a processor, it is necessary to meet two fundamental conditions:  
a) to be an independent entity from the data controller; and  
b) to process personal data on behalf of the controller...”  
While in point 18 it indicates that “Even when a specific natural person is named to ensure compliance with data protection regulations, they will not be the data controller, but will act on behalf of the legal entity (company or public body), which, ultimately, will be responsible, as the data controller, for any potential violations of the regulations.”, reminding that although the School is a private entity in this case, it does not correspond to a commercial entity but is affiliated with the Ministry of Defense through the Orphans' Board of the Air Force "Nuestra Señora de Loreto" and the competencies that the Director of the School may assume must be explicit, without it being stated or inferred that they could process the data as a processor on behalf of the data controller.  
2- The automatic delegation of one processor to another is also not applicable when the GDPR states that the subcontracting of a processing task in its Article 28.2 of the GDPR:  
“The processor shall not engage another processor without the prior written authorization, specific or general, of the controller. In the latter case, the processor shall inform the controller of any intended changes concerning the addition or replacement of other processors, thus giving the controller the opportunity to object to such changes.”

3- Despite the dual configuration of the School detailed by the appellant, educational and organizational, it does not follow that the School or its Director is responsible for the processing of the computer tool owned by GOOGLE used during the pandemic period. Consequently, the responsibility for correctly informing about the collected data and its processing lies with the same responsible party, which does not imply that the educational Center, acting on its behalf, facilitates the delivery and availability of the aforementioned documentation.
