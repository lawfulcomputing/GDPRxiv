LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of the GDPR, and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Allegations to the initiation agreement  
In response to the actions taken by AECORP 005, S.L. in response to the information request, the following should be noted.  
The response to the information request provided during the instruction of this procedure does not affect the existence of the proven facts constituting an infringement.  
Regarding the assertion of lack of voluntariness, this does not exempt responsibility in this case. Certainly, the principle of responsibility provided for in Article 28.1 of Law 40/2015, of October 1, on the Legal Regime of the Public Sector, states that: "Only individuals and legal entities may be sanctioned for acts constituting administrative infringements, as well as, when a law recognizes their capacity to act, the groups of affected individuals, unions, and entities without legal personality and independent or autonomous assets, that are found responsible for them by way of intent or fault."  
However, according to the ruling in STS 7887/2011 of November 24, 2011, Rec. 258/2009, "(...) since its ruling 76/1990, of April 26, the Constitutional Court has declared that objective or faultless responsibility is not applicable in the administrative sanctioning field, a doctrine that is reaffirmed in ruling 164/2005, of June 20, 2005, whereby the possibility of imposing sanctions solely based on the result, without proving a minimum level of culpability even in terms of mere negligence, is excluded. However, the way of attributing responsibility to legal entities does not correspond to the forms of culpability that are imputable to human conduct."  
Thus, in the case of infringements committed by legal entities, although the element of culpability must be present (see the ruling of this Chamber of the Supreme Court of November 20, 2011, cassation appeal in the interest of law C/ Jorge Juan, 6 www.aepd.es 28001 – Madrid sedeagpd.gob.es  
4/7  
48/2007), it is necessarily applied differently than it is for individuals. According to STC 246/1991 "(...) this different construction of the attribution of authorship of the infringement to the legal entity arises from the very nature of the legal fiction to which these subjects respond. They lack the volitional element in the strict sense, but not the capacity to infringe the rules to which they are subject. The capacity to infringe and, therefore, the direct reproachability that derives from the legal good protected by the norm that is infringed and the necessity for that protection to be truly effective, and the risk that, consequently, must be assumed by the legal entity that is subject to compliance with that norm."  
To the above, it should be added, following the ruling of January 23, 1998, partially transcribed in STS 6262/2009, of October 9, 2009, Rec 5285/2005, and STS 6336/2009, of October 23, 2009, Rec 1067/2006, that "although the culpability of the conduct must also be subject to proof, it must be considered in terms of the assumption of the corresponding burden, that ordinarily the volitional and cognitive elements necessary to appreciate it form part of the proven typical conduct, and that their exclusion requires proof of the absence of such elements, or in its normative aspect, that the diligence required by the one claiming its non-existence has been exercised; in short, the mere invocation of the absence of fault is not sufficient for exculpation against a typically unlawful behavior."  
In this context, none of the circumstances present in the case allow for the exclusion of this subjective element of the infringement.  
Regarding the information communicated, this Agency acknowledges receipt of it, without this declaration implying any pronouncement on it.  
III  
Unfulfilled obligation  
In light of the facts presented, it is considered that AECORP 005, S.L. has not provided the information requested by the Spanish Data Protection Agency within the deadlines granted for that purpose.  
With the aforementioned conduct of AECORP 005, S.L., the investigative power conferred to supervisory authorities by Article 58.1 of the GDPR, in this case, the AEPD, has been obstructed.  
Therefore, the facts described in the section "Proven Facts" are deemed to constitute an infringement, attributable to AECORP 005, S.L., for violation of Article 58.1 of the GDPR, which states that each supervisory authority shall have, among its investigative powers:  
“a) to order the controller and the processor and, where applicable, the representative of the controller or the processor, to provide any information required for the performance of its tasks.”

IV  
Classification and Qualification of the Infraction  
The facts presented are considered constitutive of an infraction, attributable to AECORP 005, S.L.  
This infraction is classified under Article 83.5.e) of the GDPR, which defines it as: “failure to provide access in violation of Article 58, paragraph 1.”  
The same article establishes that this infraction may be sanctioned with a fine of up to twenty million euros (€20,000,000) or, in the case of a company, an amount equivalent to four percent (4%) of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount.  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations for infractions, the infraction attributed prescribes after three years, in accordance with Article 72.1 of the LOPDGDD, which qualifies the following conduct as very serious:  
“ñ) Failure to provide access to personal data, information, premises, equipment, and processing means required by the competent data protection authority for the exercise of its investigative powers.”  
V  
Imposed Sanction  
The fine imposed must be, in each individual case, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive, in accordance with the provisions of Article 83.1 of the GDPR. Consequently, the sanction to be imposed must be graduated according to the criteria established in Article 83.2 of the GDPR, and in accordance with the provisions of Article 76 of the LOPDGDD, regarding paragraph k) of the aforementioned Article 83.2 of the GDPR. Furthermore, to ensure a consistent application of the GDPR, the Guidelines 04/2022 issued by the European Data Protection Board on the calculation of fines under the GDPR must be taken into consideration.  
In light of the facts presented, it is considered appropriate to impose a sanction on AECORP 005, S.L. for the violation of Article 58.1 of the GDPR classified under Article 83.5 e) of the GDPR. The sanction to be imposed is an administrative fine in the amount of €6,000.00.  
Therefore, in accordance with the applicable legislation, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection RESOLVES:  
FIRST: TO IMPOSE on AECORP 005, S.L., with NIF B05470380, for an infraction of Article 58.1 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR, a fine of €6,000.00 (SIX THOUSAND euros).
