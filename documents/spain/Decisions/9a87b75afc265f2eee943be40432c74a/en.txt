First: Regarding the effective removal of the claimant's data and the reiteration of their re-entry into the system.  
As a preliminary matter, it is important to recall that in the complaint submitted by the claimant, they stated that after receiving a multitude of advertising emails sent by the entity, now the appellant, they requested, both in response to those emails and through the address provided in the privacy policy available on their website, to be removed from the advertising email distribution systems on three occasions. However, despite this request, they continued to receive such commercial communications.  
The complaint was accompanied by copies of the emails sent by the claimant requesting their removal from the distribution list, as well as the advertising emails received after those requests.  
The entity now claims that the claimant was removed on all occasions when they requested it and that subsequently, when they re-entered their system, given the general terms and conditions published on the website, the claimant knew, and was fully aware, "thereby giving their consent," that this would result in new advertising communications, which would justify the reactivation of the commercial communication sends.  
However, such a statement is not adequately substantiated in the file, as it is evident that from the documentation provided by the claimant along with their complaint, the emails sent on 14/09/23 and 19/09/23 to the address allzoners@allzone.es stand out, in which they express their explicit desire not to receive any more advertising emails, warning of the possibility of taking measures if the sending of such communications persists and even, on the same day 19/09/23, the claimant sent another email to the Data Protection Officer of the entity, at the address delegadodatos@allindigital.es, requesting for the third time to be removed from the advertising email distribution list.  
On the contrary, from the documentation provided by the appellant entity, specifically from the document titled "Client File," it appears that the claimant is listed as "removed" on 19/09/23 and in "active" status in the system on 20/09/23, after which they received up to 11 advertising emails on subsequent dates, but no evidence is provided to prove that the claimant gave their express consent to receive commercial advertising again after having stated their desire not to receive any more commercial communications up to three times. That is, no evidence is provided that could demonstrate that the claimant again gave their express consent to receive commercial communications.  
In this regard, Article 21 of the LSSI indicates that the sending of advertising or promotional communications by email or other equivalent electronic communication means that have not been previously requested or expressly authorized by the recipient is prohibited, and in the case at hand, the revocation of consent by the claimant was clear, reiterated, and documented, without the appellant ceasing to send commercial communications, so that, in this case, the allegations presented on this point cannot be taken into account.

Second: Regarding the application of Article 40 of the LSSI.  
As a preliminary matter, it is appropriate to recall that, in the resolution of the sanctioning file (PS/00197/2024), it was considered to adjust the sanction to be imposed according to the aggravating criterion established in Article 40.a) of the LSSI: “The existence of intentionality (paragraph a), as it is established that the complainant has attempted to unsubscribe from the list of advertising emails of the entity being complained about on several occasions, both through the link provided in the received emails and through the Data Protection Officer of the entity, but continues to receive advertising emails.”  
The entity being complained about argues that the imposed sanction did not correctly assess the criteria for adjusting the sanction under Article 40 of the LSSI, claiming, among other things, that there is no intentionality, that no benefits have been obtained, and that the conduct of the affected party has influenced the infringement. However, these allegations cannot be taken into account for the following reasons:  
First, the appellant persisted in sending commercial emails despite receiving up to three clear and unequivocal requests from the complainant to unsubscribe, which demonstrates at least negligent conduct, if not deliberate.  
According to Article 40.b of the LSSI, the duration of the infringement is a criterion to be considered in the adjustment of the sanction. Let us recall that the complainant requested to unsubscribe on 14/09/23, reiterating this request on 19/09/23, without any effect, continuing to receive commercial communications until 23/12/23. This is a period of more than three months, which evidences a persistence in the infringing conduct, but we must remember that this was not considered as an aggravating factor in the resolution, nor can it be considered as a mitigating factor.  
The appellant entity claims that it has no sanctioning history, which should be considered as a mitigating factor. However, the absence of recidivism does not exempt from responsibility nor justifies a reduction of the sanction. In fact, Article 40.c of the LSSI establishes that recidivism is an aggravating factor, but does not contemplate the lack of prior records as a mitigating factor.  
The appellant entity asserts that no harm has been caused to the complainant; however, Article 40.d of the LSSI does not limit harm to economic damages, but encompasses any impact arising from non-compliance with the regulations. The existence of harm does not require the complainant to prove a specific damage, but rather that the impact on their fundamental rights is assessed.  
The appellant entity claims that it has not obtained benefits from sending advertising emails; however, the very persistence in sending advertisements indicates a commercial purpose aimed at generating potential profit. Thus, valuing the absence of benefits as a mitigating factor would nullify the deterrent effect of the fine, as it diminishes the effect of the circumstances that effectively influence its quantification, providing the responsible party with a benefit to which they are not entitled. It would be an artificial reduction of the sanction that could lead to the understanding that violating the norm without obtaining benefits, financial or otherwise, is acceptable.

The appealing entity mentions that it has tried to satisfy the claimant and correct the situation; however, the lack of a diligent response in managing the claimant's definitive withdrawal demonstrates that the company did not adopt effective measures in a timely manner to mitigate the infringement.  
Finally, the appealing entity seeks to invoke the moderation of sanctions provided for in Article 39 bis of the LSSI, but this is not applicable in this case, as none of the circumstances provided for in that article significantly concur, since there is no qualified reduction of culpability or unlawfulness of the conduct, given that the infringement is evident by sending commercial communications after having requested on three occasions their desire not to receive them again. There has not been a diligent regularization of the irregular situation, as the claimant had to repeatedly insist on their withdrawal request without obtaining an effective response. It has not been proven that the conduct of the affected party induced the commission of the infringement, as the company has not demonstrated that the user granted new consent after requesting withdrawal, and finally, the infringer has not spontaneously acknowledged their culpability, but rather, on the contrary, has attempted to shift the responsibility to the claimant.  
III  
Late Resolution  
Due to reasons related to the functioning of the administrative body, therefore not attributable to the appealing party, as of the date of this document, the mandatory pronouncement of this Agency regarding the present appeal has not been issued.  
According to the provisions of Article 24 LPACAP, the meaning of administrative silence in the procedures for challenging acts and provisions is dismissive. Nevertheless, and despite the time elapsed, the AEPD is obliged to issue an express resolution and notify it in all procedures, regardless of their initiation form, as provided in Article 21.1 of the aforementioned Law.  
In cases of dismissal due to administrative silence, the express resolution after the expiration of the deadline will be adopted by the Administration without any binding connection to the meaning of the silence, as provided in Article 24.3 of the same law. Therefore, it is appropriate to issue the resolution that concludes the procedure of the appeal for reconsideration filed.  
III  
Conclusion  
Consequently, in the present appeal for reconsideration, the appealing party has not provided new facts or legal arguments that would allow for a reconsideration of the validity of the contested resolution; therefore, in view of the cited provisions and others of general application, the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection RESOLVES:  
FIRST: TO DISMISS the appeal for reconsideration filed by the entity ALL IN DIGITAL MARKETING, S.L. with NIF: B88369178 against the resolution issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on 11/11/24, in the procedure EXP202401528 (PS/00197/2024).
