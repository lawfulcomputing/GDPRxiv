Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, as far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  

II  
Allegations to the Proposal for Resolution  
The party being complained against has alleged that “the documents and proceedings carried out in the development of the procedure, which serve as antecedents and grounds for the administrative resolution that is ultimately issued, are part of and are recorded in the Protection File No. (...) including the proceedings in which the interviews held with the minor's parents are reproduced, given that the interview constitutes a fundamental assessment tool within the procedure where the processing activity is carried out” and that in accordance with the provisions of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter LPACAP), and upon their request, the affected parties were provided with the opportunity to know the data or actions of the administrative file so as not to limit their right of defense.  
Article 53, Rights of the interested party in the administrative procedure, establishes that:  
“1. In addition to the other rights provided for in this Law, the interested parties in an administrative procedure have the following rights:  
a) To know, at any time, the status of the processing of the procedures in which they have the status of interested parties; the meaning of the administrative silence that corresponds, in case the Administration does not issue or notify an express resolution within the term; the competent body for its instruction, if applicable, and resolution; and the procedural acts issued. They shall also have the right to access and obtain copies of the documents contained in the aforementioned procedures.  
Those who interact with Public Administrations through electronic means shall have the right to consult the information referred to in the previous paragraph, at the General Electronic Access Point of the Administration, which will function as an access portal. The obligation of the Administration to provide copies of the documents contained in the procedures shall be deemed fulfilled by making them available at the General Electronic Access Point of the competent Administration or at the corresponding electronic offices.  
b) To identify the authorities and personnel serving the Public Administrations under whose responsibility the procedures are processed.  
(…)”  
From this, we can deduce that access can be exercised at any time, and that such access includes:  
- The status of the processing of the procedure, that is, the phase of the administrative procedure in which it is located.  
- Physical access to the same file.  
- The right to obtain copies of the documents contained therein. (The right to obtain copies does not imply their free provision).  
- The right to identify the officials involved in it, etc.  
In this way, interested parties are facilitated to know the data, reports, actions, etc., that make up the administrative file in order not to limit their rights and to prevent the generation of defenselessness.  
However, Article 13, Rights of individuals in their relations with Public Administrations, also establishes that:  
“Those who, in accordance with Article 3, have the capacity to act before Public Administrations, are entitled, in their relations with them, to the following rights:  
“(…)  
h) To the protection of personal data, and in particular to the security and confidentiality of the data contained in the files, systems, and applications of the Public Administrations.  
(…)  
These rights are understood without prejudice to those recognized in Article 53 referred to the interested parties in the administrative procedure.”

In the Proposal for Resolution, it was already indicated that it was necessary to carry out a careful analysis of the consequences that could arise from the possibility of accessing sensitive data, personal data subject to special protection such as health data, as well as information contained in statements or declarations made during interviews throughout the procedure, which, when considered in isolation, do not seem to provide or reveal important information about private life, health, or other circumstances related to a specific person, but which, when considered together, may yield extremely sensitive and confidential information about the particular, private, and intimate situation of a person, which in any case should be anonymized.  

Health data constitutes a special category of data, according to Article 9.1 of the GDPR, the processing of which is generally prohibited, unless certain exceptions provided for in point 2 of the aforementioned Article 9 of the GDPR apply.  
The GDPR defines in its Article 4.15 health-related data as “personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, which reveal information about their health status.”  
Recital 35 of the GDPR clarifies this definition by stating that “Among the personal data related to health, all data related to the health status of the data subject that provide information about their past, present, or future physical or mental health status must be included. This includes information about the natural person.”

On the other hand, it is true that, as the respondent party points out, the interview is one of the techniques used during the instruction process of a Protection File with the aim of evaluating and making decisions regarding the family situation of a minor.  
However, this is not about questioning the mentioned technique, which can, as in the present case, verify or refute existing data regarding the possible existence of a situation of neglect, which does not concern that agency at all; rather, it is about the information provided during the interview or in reports regarding a specific personal situation, when they are conveyed together, as in the present case, following a request for the file by a third party, which may allow for a portrayal or judgment of the affected person.  
Therefore, the allegation made by the respondent party must be rejected.  
III  
Unfulfilled obligation: violation of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR  
In the present case, the facts materialize in the receipt by the complainant of a copy of the protection file of their minor child, containing specially protected data, and in which the complainant informs the acting technician of their intention to file a shared custody claim against their ex-wife, and that this information was not adequately protected, as a complete copy of the report was sent to their ex-wife, which could constitute a violation of the regulations regarding the protection of personal data.  
Article 5, Principles relating to processing, of the GDPR establishes that:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
(…)  
c) adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’);  
(…)”  
The documentation in the file provides clear evidence that the respondent violated Article 5 of the GDPR, principles relating to processing, by enabling access to personal data as a result of sending the neglect declaration file to their ex-wife, thereby violating the data minimization principle set forth in the aforementioned article.  
Article 5 of the GDPR refers to the general principles for data processing. In its paragraph c), it refers to the data minimization principle, indicating that data must be “adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed.”  
If we examine the definition, we can deduce that only the personal data that will be processed can be collected, that is, those that are strictly necessary for the processing; that they can only be collected when they are going to be processed and that they can only be used for the purpose for which they were collected, but not for any other objective.  
The complainant has stated that the file contains data about their health status and that, furthermore, in the interview held on ***DATE.1, they expressed to the technician that they were going to file a shared custody claim against their ex-wife and that, since she did not know, they did not want her to find out; this data and the statements of the complainant are included in the Social Services report and have been conveyed to their ex-wife without their consent.  
For its part, the respondent party has indicated that the parents of the minor requested a copy of the file, one on ***DATE.2 and another on ***DATE.1, being delivered in the offices of the Ministry of Social Services, Inclusion, Childhood, and Family; that the affected parties were allowed to know the data and actions included in the administrative file so as not to limit their right of defense and, therefore, to avoid generating defenselessness, complying at all times with the regulations governing the administrative procedure.  
In the present case, the respondent is attributed in the sanctioning procedure initiated a violation of the data minimization principle provided for in Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR, which states: “Personal data shall be:  
(…)  
c) adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’).  
(…)”  
It is worth mentioning the considerations regarding the data minimization principle made by the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) in Guidelines 4/2019, “on Article 25, Data protection by design and by default,” version 2.0, dated 20/10/2020, adopted in compliance with the function assigned to it by Article 70 of the GDPR to ensure its consistent application. In paragraph 74 of these guidelines, it is established that: “Firstly, data controllers must determine whether they even need to process personal data for their relevant purposes. The controller must verify whether the relevant purposes can be achieved by processing less personal data, or by using less detailed or aggregated personal data, or without having to process personal data at all. This verification must take place before any processing, but it can also be carried out at any time during the processing cycle.” (Paragraph 74).  
And in its paragraph 76, Guidelines 4/2019 indicate that “Essential elements by design and by default regarding data minimization” include, among others, the following:  
“• Avoidance of data: All processing of personal data will be avoided when possible to fulfill the relevant purpose.  
• Limitation: The amount of personal data collected will be limited to what is strictly necessary for the intended purpose.

• Access limitation: The processing of data will be configured in such a way that the number of people who need to access personal data to perform their functions is minimized, and access will be limited accordingly.  
• Relevance: Personal data must be relevant to the processing in question, and the data controller must be able to demonstrate such relevance.  
• Necessity: Each category of personal data will be necessary for the specified purposes and should only be processed if it is not possible to achieve the purpose by other means.  

Well, the responding party provided the file containing data and information that was neither necessary, nor appropriate, nor relevant, as it should only be processed in accordance with the intended purpose.  
Therefore, it is necessary to carry out a proper assessment of the information to be provided, weighing the circumstances surrounding a particular processing, analyzing the consequences that may arise from access to certain data such as health data, which is especially protected, as well as the private information disclosed through statements and declarations during an interview, whose transfer or provision may be considered excessive and unnecessary, violating the regulations on the protection of personal data, Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR.  
Also, in Recital 39 of the GDPR it is stated that: “...Personal data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purposes for which they are processed.”  
Therefore, it is considered that the conduct of the responding party in transferring certain data contained in the file revealing not only the health data of the claimant, which is especially protected, but also the impressions and private statements made during the interview with the social services technician, is deemed inappropriate, unnecessary, and not limited to the specific purpose, violating Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR, an infringement classified under Article 83.5 a) of the GDPR.

V  
Adoption of measures  
The corrective powers attributed to the AEPD as the supervisory authority are listed in Article 58.2, sections a) to j) of the GDPR.  
Once the infringement has been confirmed, it is appropriate to order the responsible party to adopt measures to adjust their actions to the aforementioned regulations, in accordance with what is established in the aforementioned Article 58.2 d) of the GDPR, according to which each supervisory authority may “order the controller or processor to ensure that the processing operations comply with the provisions of this Regulation, where appropriate, in a specific manner and within a specified period…”. The imposition of this measure is compatible with the sanction consisting of an administrative fine, as provided for in Article 83.2 of the GDPR.  
Therefore, it is deemed appropriate to order the respondent, within six months from the finality of the sanctioning resolution that, in any case, is issued, to adjust the processing activities subject to this procedure to the applicable regulations, in this case, to the provisions of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR.
