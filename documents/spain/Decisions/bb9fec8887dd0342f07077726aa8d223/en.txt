LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
The Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve the present appeal, in accordance with the provisions of article 123 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP) and article 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD).  
I  
Response to the allegations presented  
First of all, reference should be made to article 118 of the LPACAP, which states the following regarding Administrative Appeals:

Article 118. Hearing of interested parties.  
1. When new facts or documents not included in the original file need to be taken into account, they will be made available to the interested parties so that, within a period of no less than ten days and no more than fifteen, they may formulate allegations and present the documents and justifications they deem appropriate.  
Facts, documents, or allegations from the appellant will not be taken into account in the resolution of the appeals when they could have been provided during the allegation phase but were not. The practice of evidence cannot be requested when its lack of execution in the procedure in which the contested resolution was issued is attributable to the interested party.  
2. If there are other interested parties, they will be given, in any case, a copy of the appeal so that within the aforementioned period, they may allege whatever they deem appropriate.  
3. The appeal, reports, and proposals do not have the character of new documents for the purposes of this article. Neither will those that the interested parties have submitted to the file before the contested resolution was issued.  
As indicated in the third fact, the appellant has submitted various documents along with their request for reconsideration, documents that, however, cannot be taken into consideration in accordance with the aforementioned article 118 of the LPACAP. In this regard, the letter dated 13/05/2013, the excerpt from the bulletin of 11/07/2013, and the decree of admission to processing of the claim of 08/11/2022 are documents that were already in the possession of the appellant at the time of presenting their complaint and were not submitted at that time. Since they were not incorporated in due time, their introduction at this stage is inappropriate and cannot be admitted now.  
Likewise, the cadastral data sheets of 18/08/2023, the order of 01/01/2024, and the order of 15/07/2024, although being documents subsequent to the initial complaint, could have been presented with the information expansion writings that the appellant themselves formulated later. However, since they were not submitted in such writings and are prior to the filing of the procedure, they also cannot be taken into account at this stage. Consequently, the documentation presented with the request for reconsideration cannot be considered to substantiate a modification of the contested resolution.  
Furthermore, regarding the statements made by the appellant, this Agency is not competent to assess issues related to the ownership of the antuzano or, since the matter is sub iudice, must be resolved in the Courts.  
The appeal presented reveals the existence of ongoing judicial proceedings (negatory action of easement and an action for nullity of registration), which indicates that the discrepancy alleged by the appellant is based on a pending litigation regarding the ownership and delimitation of a property. Thus, the issue raised does not fall within the scope of data protection, but rather pertains to a civil and registry dispute that is outside the jurisdiction of this procedure.  
In this regard, the contested resolution does not prejudge or validate the registry entry, but simply refers to the information contained in the Property Registry presented by the responding party, the content of which enjoys a presumption of truthfulness in accordance with Article 38 of the Mortgage Law, unless its inaccuracy is proven by a final judicial ruling.  
Given that, at this moment, there is no record of a judicial pronouncement declaring the nullity of the aforementioned registry entry, the arguments of the appellant asserting that such entry lacks validity or that the registered area does not match reality cannot be taken into account. As long as the registry entry maintains its legal effectiveness, its content must prevail as sufficient proof of the registered right, without this administrative procedure being responsible for analyzing or assessing the correctness of the registry entries.  
For all the above reasons, and since the allegations presented in the appeal for reconsideration do not undermine the grounds for the agreed dismissal, but rather highlight a civil and registry issue outside the jurisdiction of this procedure, the appeal for reconsideration filed is dismissed, and the dismissal resolution is confirmed in all its terms.  
III  
Conclusion  
Consequently, in the present appeal for reconsideration, the appellant has not provided facts or legal arguments that would allow for a reconsideration of the validity of the contested resolution.  
IV  
Late Resolution  
Due to reasons related to the functioning of the administrative body, therefore not attributable to the appellant, as of the date of this document, the mandatory pronouncement of this Agency regarding the present appeal has not been issued.  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 24 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (LPACAP), the sense of administrative silence in the procedures for challenging acts and provisions is dismissive.  
Nevertheless, and despite the time elapsed, the Administration is obliged to issue an express resolution and to notify it in all procedures, regardless of their initiation form, as provided in Article 21.1 of the aforementioned LPACAP.
