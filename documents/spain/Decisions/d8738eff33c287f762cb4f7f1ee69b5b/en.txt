BACKGROUND  
FIRST: A.A.A. (*hereinafter, the claimant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on December 4, 2023. The complaint is directed against the entity INNOTEC CENTRO ESPECIAL DE EMPLEO, S.L. with NIF B05476965 (hereinafter, the respondent). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The claimant states that upon entering the respondent company (from which he resigned on (...)), (...) to be registered with Social Security, he was uninsured for 5 days. Furthermore, he expresses that his working hours exceeded the maximum of 38 and a half hours, being a Special Employment Center, without respecting the collective agreement and freezing his salary.  
Additionally, in the company's surveillance activities, according to the claimant, his superior has a (...) for the total and absolute control of all his workers, such that in the office of ***LOCALIDAD.1 there are surveillance cameras everywhere. Thus, the interior cameras of the office, (...). Moreover, these cameras capture audio.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
According to the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, insofar as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Unfulfilled Obligation  
In this case, the complaint dated 04/12/23 is examined, which essentially conveys the following:  
“presence of camera(s) of video surveillance permanently directed towards the workstation, with the purpose of excessive control and affecting their professional and personal privacy.”  
In support of their claim, the complainant provides documentary evidence (Annex I) that confirms the presence in the company of device(s) for capturing images, permanently directed towards what was their workstation.  
It should be noted that the employer may place surveillance cameras as deemed necessary, provided that they do not conflict with the fundamental rights of the workers.  
Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR states: “adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’).”  
It is worth remembering that individuals and/or companies are responsible for ensuring that the installed systems comply with current legislation, demonstrating that they meet all the requirements established by the applicable regulations.  
According to Article 20.3 of the Workers' Statute: "the employer may adopt the measures they deem most appropriate for surveillance and control to verify compliance by the worker with their obligations and labor duties."  
Article 89 of Organic Law 3/2018 (December 5) states:  
1. Employers may process images obtained through camera or video camera systems for the exercise of the control functions of workers or public employees provided for, respectively, in Article 20.3 of the Workers' Statute and in public service legislation, as long as these functions are exercised within their legal framework and with the inherent limits thereof. Employers must inform, in advance and in a clear, concise, and express manner, the workers or public employees and, where applicable, their representatives, about this measure.  
In the event that a flagrant act of illegality committed by workers or public employees has been captured, the duty to inform shall be deemed fulfilled when at least the device referred to in Article 22.4 of this organic law exists.  
1. In no case shall the installation of sound recording systems or video surveillance in places designated for the rest or recreation of workers or public employees, such as changing rooms, restrooms, cafeterias, and similar places, be permitted.  
1. The use of systems similar to those referred to in the previous sections for sound recording in the workplace shall only be permitted when the risks to the security of facilities, property, and persons arising from the activity carried out in the workplace are relevant, and always respecting the principles of proportionality, minimum intervention, and the guarantees provided in the previous sections. The deletion of sounds retained by these recording systems shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of section 3 of Article 22 of this law.  
The Constitutional Court, in Judgment 207/1996, states: “Furthermore, proportionality is a fundamental element in all areas where video surveillance systems are installed, given that there are numerous cases in which the violation of the aforementioned principle can lead to abusive situations, such as the installation of surveillance systems in common areas or restrooms of the workplace. Therefore, it is essential to avoid omnipresent surveillance to prevent the vulnerability of the individual.”

The presence of the aforementioned cameras in the entity being claimed implies that they must comply with the duty to inform the workers or, where applicable, their legal representatives, in accordance with Articles 13 and 14 of the GDPR, making it clear the "purpose of the processing of personal data."  
If the entity has legal representation for the entire workforce, in addition to informing the workers, the members of the entity's union must also be informed, as by virtue of their status and in accordance with Article 64 of the Workers' Statute (ET), they must be informed by any means that guarantees the receipt of the information, which can be done, for example, through a statement.  
The entity being claimed has acknowledged that two cameras have the capability to capture <audio>, taking into account the special characteristics of the workers at the Center, as well as the appropriateness of the measure under certain parameters.  
The above does not exclude the obligation to duly inform the entire workforce of the fact that the installed system has the capability to capture audio, as well as the reason for such a measure (purpose stated in a reliable document).  
Article 13 of the GDPR "Information to be provided when personal data are obtained from the data subject"

V  
The principle of the presumption of innocence prevents attributing an administrative infringement when there has not been obtained and verified evidence of the facts that motivate the accusation or of the involvement of the alleged infringer in them. Applying the principle "in dubio pro reo" in case of doubt regarding a specific and determined fact, which obliges in any case to resolve such doubt in the most favorable manner for the interested party.  
The presumption of innocence must govern without exceptions in the sanctioning system and must be respected in the imposition of any sanctions, as the exercise of the ius puniendi in its various manifestations is conditioned by the play of evidence and a contradictory procedure in which the parties can defend their positions. In this sense, the Constitutional Court in its Judgment 76/1990, of 26/04, considers that the right to the presumption of innocence entails: "that the sanction is based on acts or evidentiary means of charge or incriminating the reproached conduct; that the burden of proof lies with the accuser, without anyone being obliged to prove their own innocence; and that any insufficiency in the result of the evidence presented, freely evaluated by the sanctioning body, must result in an acquittal."  
The presumption of innocence governs without exceptions in the sanctioning system and must be respected in the imposition of any sanction, whether criminal or administrative (TCo 13/1981), as the exercise of the sanctioning right in any of its manifestations is conditioned by the play of evidence and a contradictory procedure in which the parties can defend their own positions.  
According to this principle, no sanction can be imposed based on the culpability of the accused if there is no evidentiary activity of charge, which in the assessment of the authorities or bodies called to resolve, destroys this presumption (TCo Auto 3-12-81).  
VI  
In view of the allegations made and having analyzed the evidence from both parties, first of all, it cannot be established that any camera was permanently directed towards the employee's workstation, especially when the photographs provided show the workspace, but not the direct focus on the monitor screen.

Regarding the information about the presence of cameras with the capability to capture audio, the defendant provides documentary evidence that includes a clause <Videovigilancia>, informing about the presence of cameras for labor control purposes.  
The defendant states that there are informational signs indicating that it is a video-monitored area for the relevant legal effects.  
Furthermore, a copy of the Labor Inspection Report is provided, which, after visiting the center, indicates that the alleged circumstances have not been substantiated.  
This agency, however, recommends that the contractual clauses clearly inform how to exercise rights (e.g., by providing a physical address or corporate email).  
The recording of voice, in general, is considered a disproportionate method of labor control, as it is possible to adopt equally effective measures that are less intrusive to individuals' privacy and the protection of their personal data, since it would capture both conversations relevant to business interests and strictly private conversations.  
It is also reminded that the presence of cameras with audio capability should be limited to work areas that require it due to the nature of the work to be performed, avoiding the capture of ambient sound from private conversations or in recreational spaces (e.g., employee break areas).  
Finally, it is noted that this agency may proceed to conduct inspections of the facilities, being fully informed of the recommendations that have been presented for informational purposes, ensuring at all times the proportionality of the measure in accordance with the fundamental rights that are intended to be protected.
