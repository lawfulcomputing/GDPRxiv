RESOLUTION OF SANCTIONING PROCEDURE  
From the procedure instructed by the Spanish Agency for the Protection of Data and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for the Protection of Data on May 24, 2022. The complaint is directed against COMUNIDAD DE PROPIETARIOS B.B.B. with NIF NIF.1 (hereinafter, the respondent or the Community of property owners). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
“Good morning  
I am writing to you regarding the following problem:  
In the entrance of my apartment building, the President has placed a list with my apartment number and the amount of the debt I owe to the community among those of other neighbors.  
I am completely locatable, physically, in person or by phone.  
Is it legal for that notice to be exposed for anyone who accesses the building?  
Does it not violate our right to privacy?  
I have photographs that prove what I indicated, and I request assistance to have this notice removed as soon as possible.”  
SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the complaint was forwarded to the respondent for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in the data protection regulations.  
The forwarding, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was recorded on May 31, 2022, as evidenced by the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  
On July 1, and registered on July 4, 2022, a response letter was received by this Agency from the property management administrator, indicating that this Property Management had not introduced the document subject to the complaint. However, it had engaged in discussions with its compliance advisors to seek their assistance and advice.  
It had conducted a search and understood that the complaint referred to the Community of Apartments B.B.B. located at ***ADDRESS.1.  
It understood that the complaint did not concern any of the rights recognized in Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR.  
Regarding the causes that had motivated the incident that led to the complaint, it was indicated:  
“This party understands that the cause was the ignorance by the board of directors of said community of the regulatory provisions regarding the protection of personal data.”  
Since this Property Management had not placed any document in the Community of Apartments B.B.B. where the complainant is a property owner, it understood that no measures were warranted. It then stated:  
“However, it will be communicated to the president of said community that for the placement of documentation containing personal data on the notice board of a community of property owners, it must act in accordance with the provisions of the Horizontal Property Law (art. 9.h. of the LPH).”  
It concluded by highlighting its commitment to regulatory compliance and its willingness to cooperate with this Agency.  
THIRD: On August 24, 2022, in accordance with Article 65 of the LOPDGDD, the complaint filed by the complainant was admitted for processing.  
FOURTH: The General Sub-Directorate for Data Inspection proceeded to carry out preliminary investigative actions to clarify the facts in question, by virtue of the functions assigned to control authorities in Article 57.1 and the powers granted in Article 58.1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and in accordance with the provisions established in Title VII, Chapter I, Section Two, of the LOPDGDD, being aware of the following aspects reflected in the preliminary investigation report dated May 19, 2023:

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
According to the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of 5 December, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, by the general rules on administrative procedures.”

Preliminary Issues

In the present case, according to the provisions of Article 4.1 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, given that the Community of property owners carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it is the one that determines the purposes and means, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR:  
“‘data controller’ or ‘controller’: the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be provided for by the law of the Union or of the Member States;  
Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines “personal data breaches” (hereinafter personal data breach) as “a breach of security that leads to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration of, or unauthorized disclosure of, or access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed;”  
In the present case, there is a personal data breach under the circumstances indicated above, categorized as a breach of confidentiality. The personal data of the claimant were accessed by third parties without their consent.  
In the complaint, the claimant states:  
“In the portal of my apartment building, the President has placed a list with my apartment number and the amount of the debt I owe to the community among those of other neighbors.”  
Along with the complaint, two photographs have been provided showing a sign placed on the notice board of the community of property owners of the building, locked, which lists five apartments along with the amounts of the debts owed to the community of property owners.  
According to the Article 29 Working Party, a “Breach of confidentiality” occurs when there is an unauthorized or accidental disclosure of personal data, or access to the same.  
III  
Obligation breached in relation to Article 5.1 f) of the GDPR  
Article 5 of the GDPR establishes the principles relating to the processing of personal data. Specifically, its paragraph 1.f) states the following:  
“Principles relating to processing”:  
1. Personal data shall be:  
(…)

f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, by applying appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).”  
In the present case, the principle of confidentiality in the processing of personal data has been violated. Such data, which were under the responsibility of the respondent, were published on the notice board of the community of property owners of the building, making them accessible to anyone passing by.  
In the first photograph provided by the claimant, a handwritten paper dated May 6, 2022, is seen with the title:  

“COMMUNITY OF PROPERTY OWNERS B.B.B. APARTMENTS PENDING PAYMENT”  
At the bottom, there is a list of five apartments, with an amount in euros indicated next to each one.  
In the second photograph provided by the claimant, it can be seen that the paper with the list of apartments has been placed on the notice board of the community of property owners, locked. According to the claimant, the mentioned board is located in the portal of their building.  
Consequently, the claimant's status as a debtor, along with that of four other neighbors, as well as the amounts of the debts owed to the rest of the community of property owners, would have been exposed to the other neighbors, as well as to anyone who accessed the building and consulted the content of the notice board.  
It is considered that the known facts could constitute an infringement, attributable to the Community of property owners, for violation of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR as a result of a breach of confidentiality.

In this regard, the LOPDGDD, in its Article 71 “Infringements” establishes that

VI  
Obligation breached in relation to Article 32 of the GDPR  
Article 32 "Security of processing" of the GDPR states: (the underlining is ours).  
"1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the varying likelihood and severity of risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data promptly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly verifying, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.

2. When assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a result of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.

In the document sent by the Property Administrator to this Agency on July 1, 2022, it was indicated:  
“(…) the president of said community will be informed that for the placement of documentation containing personal data on the notice board of a community of property owners, it must act in accordance with the provisions of the Horizontal Property Law (art. 9.h. of the LPH).”  
Indeed, Article 9.1 h) of Law 49/1960, of July 21, on Horizontal Property (hereinafter LPH) indicates as an obligation of the owner:  
"h) To communicate to the person performing the functions of secretary of the community, by any means that allows for proof of receipt, the address in Spain for the purposes of summons and notifications of all kinds related to the community. In the absence of this communication, the address for summons and notifications shall be deemed to be the apartment or premises belonging to the community, with full legal effects for those delivered to the occupant thereof.  
If a summons or notification to the owner cannot be carried out at the place provided for in the previous paragraph, it shall be deemed to have been made by placing the corresponding communication on the notice board of the community, or in a visible place for general use designated for this purpose, with due diligence expressing the date and reasons for proceeding with this form of notification, signed by the person performing the functions of secretary of the community, with the approval of the president. The notification made in this manner shall produce full legal effects within three calendar days."  

In the complaint document, the complainant states that they were fully locatable, physically, in person, or by phone.

In a case such as the one examined, the Community of Property Owners must have a procedure in place that allows for compliance with the provisions of Article 9.1 h) of the LPH prior to publication.  
In this regard, an attempt must be made to summon or notify the corresponding owner, and if it is impossible to carry it out at the designated location, the communication should be placed on the community's notice board or in a visible general-use area designated for this purpose.  
This procedure acts as a security measure that ensures that the personal data of that owner is not accessible to third parties. Communication through the notice board would only be carried out if the requirements set forth in Article 9.1 h) of the LPH are met and in accordance with the provisions outlined in that article.  
For all these reasons, it is considered that the known facts constitute an infringement consisting of the lack of appropriate technical and organizational measures to guarantee an adequate level of security relative to the risk of processing, ensuring confidentiality, attributable to the community of property owners, for violation of Article 32 of the GDPR.  

VII  
Classification and qualification of the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR  
The aforementioned infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR constitutes one of the infringements classified in Article 83.4 of the GDPR, which under the heading "General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines" states:  
“4. Infringements of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 10,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an undertaking, an amount equivalent to 2% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the obligations of the controller and the processor under Articles 8, 11, 25 to 39, 42, and 43; (…)”  
In this regard, the LOPDGDD, in its Article 71 “Infringements” establishes that “Acts and conduct referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those that are contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements.”  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations, Article 73 “Infringements considered serious” of the LOPDGDD indicates:  
“Based on what is established in Article 83.4 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following shall be considered serious and shall be subject to a two-year statute of limitations: infringements that constitute a substantial violation of the articles mentioned therein and, in particular, the following:
