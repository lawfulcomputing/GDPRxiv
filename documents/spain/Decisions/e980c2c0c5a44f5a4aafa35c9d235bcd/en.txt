Regarding the recordings kept by the respondent  
The respondent stated in their response to the transfer of the complaint the following:  
"the telephone calls received by Vodafone's customer service are only recorded randomly and for the purpose of quality of the service provided to the customer, as is expressly informed to those interested in carrying out any procedure through the telephone channel. […] the number of requests and procedures that Vodafone manages by telephone makes it practically unfeasible to record and keep all the transactions carried out by telephone with the customer service of this entity, so Vodafone only carries out the recording of a representative number of the total transactions carried out by telephone in order to evaluate the quality of customer service or to prove the contracting made by a new customer."  
Furthermore, regarding this issue, the respondent has previously specified that:  
"Telemarketing to customers: in this channel, a telephone contact is made with the customer in which the Vodafone offer is presented and the necessary data is collected to continue with the contracting. During the same call, if it is not possible to use the certified signature, the acceptance of the contract by the customer will be recorded, storing the recording in Vodafone's CRM. […] The rest of the recordings, except for a small percentage that are recorded for quality reasons, are not recorded."  
In this same vein, the respondent has stated that "due to the large volume of calls that Vodafone manages, not all telephone interactions are recorded. Vodafone records all those calls through which a contract is perfected, that is, those in which the recording is the only proof of the customer's consent. These recordings are stored for a period of six years in my client's own systems. The rest of the interactions are recorded randomly for quality reasons. The retention periods for quality recordings are not fixed, as a fixed amount of storage is contracted, and when the maximum capacity is reached, the oldest recordings begin to be overwritten."

LEGAL GROUNDS

I
Competence
According to the functions assigned to each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR) and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve these investigative proceedings. 

Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”

II
Right of access of the data subject
Article 15 of the GDPR, which regulates the right of access of the data subject, establishes the following:
“1. The data subject shall have the right to obtain from the controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning them are being processed and, where that is the case, access to the personal data and the following information:
a) the purposes of the processing;
b) the categories of personal data concerned;
c) the recipients or categories of recipients to whom the personal data have been or will be disclosed, in particular recipients in third countries or international organizations;
d) where possible, the envisaged period for which the personal data will be stored or, if not possible, the criteria used to determine that period;
e) the existence of the right to request from the controller rectification or erasure of personal data or restriction of processing of personal data concerning the data subject, or to object to such processing;
f) the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority;
g) when the personal data have not been obtained from the data subject, any available information as to their source;
h) the existence of automated decision-making, including profiling, referred to in Article 22, paragraphs 1 and 4, and at least in those cases, meaningful information about the logic involved, as well as the significance and the envisaged consequences of such processing for the data subject.
2. Where personal data are transferred to a third country or to an international organization, the data subject shall have the right to be informed of the appropriate safeguards under Article 46 relating to the transfer.
3. The controller shall provide a copy of the personal data undergoing processing. The controller may charge a reasonable fee based on the administrative costs for any further copies requested by the data subject. When the data subject makes the request by electronic means, and unless otherwise requested, the information shall be provided in a commonly used electronic format.
4. The right to obtain a copy mentioned in paragraph 3 shall not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others.”

III
Reasons for the complaint
The complaint is based on the fact that the claimant has been denied access to the recordings of the conversations they have had with the Customer Service Center of the respondent. The claimant specifically cites that, on August 11, 2022, they requested a copy of the recordings of the telephone interactions made in the five months preceding, expressly emphasizing the desire to obtain those held on May 12, 2022. 

The respondent, regarding the policy for the custody of recordings, has indicated that, on one hand, it records all calls through which a contract is perfected, and on the other hand, it randomly stores a percentage of the remaining calls for quality purposes. Additionally, it has stated that the former have a fixed retention period of six years, while the latter do not have a set period but are periodically overwritten according to the availability of the contracted storage.

Regarding the contracting procedure, the respondent has indicated that, once the process is initiated through the telephone channel, the perfection of the contract can be completed either through voice recording or through a certified signature system. It notes that, in the latter case, where the recording is not retained, a third party (Digitel) participates to certify the process of sending and digitally signing the contract. The details of this process, according to the information provided by Digitel, have been detailed in the Facts of this Resolution.

According to the information obtained during the investigation, the respondent contacted the claimant on the same day, August 11, 2022, providing a single recording, generated on August 10, 2022, for quality purposes, stating that no additional recordings were available. Regarding the interaction on May 12, 2022, the respondent has not provided further information.
12/12  
May 12, 2022, referred to by the claimant, states that the respondent indicates that, although the telephone conversation took place in the context of a contract, it was perfected through the certified signature system, not giving rise to the automatic storage of the audio file as contractual evidence. Both the respondent and Digitel, as the entity responsible for providing the certified signature platform, have provided evidence of the execution of the process.  
IV  
Conclusion  
Therefore, based on the information provided in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found that substantiates the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, as the respondent responded to the request for access to the requested recordings immediately.
