FACTS  
FIRST: On 05/03/24, A.A.A. (the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
The complaint is directed against the entity, EDA TV CONSULTING, S.L. with NIF: B88605266, owner of the website https://edatv.news/ (hereinafter, the defendant), for the alleged violation of Law 34/2002, of July 11, on Information Society Services and Electronic Commerce (LSSI).  
In their complaint, they state, among other issues, that the website https://edatv.news/ does not comply with the requirements established in the current regulations regarding the "Cookie Policy."  
SECOND: On 17/04/24, in accordance with the provisions of Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), this Agency forwarded the complaint to the defendant for analysis and to report back within one month on the matters raised in the complaint.  
THIRD: On 22/05/24, this Agency received a written response from the defendant in which, among other issues, they state regarding the "Cookie Policy" of their website the following:  
“(...) Regarding cookies, the fact that on the first visit and before accepting or rejecting cookies, the Google Analytics tracking cookies are already installed. Likewise, even if cookies are expressly rejected, Google Analytics cookies continue to be installed. EDA TV has two technological providers for the management and maintenance of the website and the use of cookies (specifically, the provider TOOOLS manages the website www.edatv.com and the provider TRIVIUM manages the website www.edatv.news).  
The convergence of these two providers in the provision of their maintenance and management services for each of the aforementioned websites has caused a misalignment in the configuration of both portals during a period (which coincided with the time of filing the complaint by this user), resulting in the situation where, despite the existence of a cookie acceptance and rejection banner that complied with the instructions of the AEPD as outlined in its latest Guide on the use of Cookies published in January 2024, cookies were downloaded to users' devices without their express acceptance or even downloaded when expressly rejected.  
This misalignment has already been resolved, and currently, cookies are not downloaded to users' devices without their prior express acceptance. Likewise, it can be observed in this screenshot that on the website www.edatv.news cookies are currently also not downloaded to users' devices without their prior and express consent.  
5. And, finally, the fact that cookies are used without adequately obtaining users' consent, with the existence of a paywall being verified. In the AEPD's Guide on the use of Cookies published in January 2024, it is regulated in point 3.2.10. “Possibility of denying access to the service in case of rejection of cookies,” the following: “Following the guidelines of the EDPB on consent, for consent to be given freely, access to services and functionalities must not be conditioned on the user's acceptance of the use of cookies. Therefore, so-called 'cookie walls' that do not offer an alternative to consent cannot be used, as explained below.  
This criterion is particularly important in cases where denial of access would prevent the exercise of a legally recognized right by the user, as access to a website may be the only means provided to the user to exercise such a right.  
There may be certain cases where the non-acceptance of the use of cookies prevents access to the website or the total or partial use of the service, as long as the user is adequately informed about this and an alternative is offered, not necessarily free, to access the service without the need to accept the use of cookies. As established by the EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent, the services of both alternatives must be genuinely equivalent, and it will not be valid for the equivalent service to be offered by an entity unrelated to the publisher.”

Considering the above, the AEPD itself, in its Guide on the instructions that website owners must follow regarding the web implementation of cookies published earlier this year, allowed the possibility that, as an alternative method to the non-acceptance of the use of cookies, they could offer users a paid alternative (“not necessarily free”) that would allow users to access the site’s services without necessarily accepting them. II  
On the possibility of withdrawing consent for the use of cookies once granted.  
Users must be able to withdraw their previously granted consent at any time. To this end, the publisher must ensure that it provides information to users in its cookie policy on how they can withdraw consent.  
The user must be able to revoke consent easily. The system offered to withdraw consent must be as easy as the one used when consent was granted. It will be considered that this ease exists, for example, when the user has simple and permanent access to the cookie management or configuration system.  
In this case, it is not possible to modify the consent granted for the use of "non-technical or necessary" cookies at any time during web browsing, as there is no permanent link or mechanism that redirects the user to the control panel to manage their consent whenever they wish.  

III  
Unfulfilled Obligation  
From the deficiency detected regarding the cookie policy on the website https://edatv.news/, that is, the impossibility of modifying consent at any time during web browsing when the user wishes to do so, it could imply on the part of the accused a violation of Article 22.2 of the LSSI, as it establishes the following:  
“Service providers may use data storage and retrieval devices on the terminal equipment of the recipients, provided that they have given their consent after being provided with clear and complete information about their use, in particular, regarding the purposes of data processing, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13, on the protection of personal data.

IV.  
Proposal and Graduation of the Sanction  
This infringement is classified as "minor" in Article 38.4 g) of the aforementioned Law, which considers as such: “Using data storage and retrieval devices when the information has not been provided or the consent of the service recipient has not been obtained in the terms required by Article 22.2.”, and may be sanctioned with a fine of up to €30,000, in accordance with Article 39 of the aforementioned LSSI.  
The LSSI, in its Article 40, establishes the “graduation of the amount of sanctions”, taking into account the following criteria: a) The existence of intent. b) The length of time during which the infringement has been committed. c) The recurrence of committing infringements of the same nature, when declared by a firm resolution. d) The nature and amount of the damages caused. e) The benefits obtained from the infringement. f) The volume of billing affected by the committed infringement. g) Adherence to a code of conduct or a self-regulatory advertising system applicable to the committed infringement, which complies with the provisions of Article 18 or in the eighth final provision and has been favorably reported by the competent authority or authorities.  
Based on the evidence obtained, and without prejudice to what results from the instruction, it is appropriate to impose, for the infringement of Article 22.2 of the LSSI, an initial sanction of €2,000 (two thousand euros).

II  
Termination of the procedure  
Article 85 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), under the heading "Termination in sanctioning procedures" provides the following:  
“1. Once a sanctioning procedure has been initiated, if the offender acknowledges their responsibility, the procedure may be resolved with the imposition of the appropriate sanction.  
2. When the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature or when it is possible to impose a pecuniary sanction and another non-pecuniary sanction but the impropriety of the latter has been justified, voluntary payment by the presumed responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except regarding the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for the damages caused by the commission of the infringement.  
3. In both cases, when the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature, the competent authority to resolve the procedure shall apply reductions of at least 20% on the amount of the proposed sanction, which shall be cumulative. The aforementioned reductions must be specified in the notification of initiation of the procedure, and their effectiveness shall be conditioned upon the withdrawal or renunciation of any action or administrative appeal against the sanction. The percentage of reduction provided in this section may be increased by regulation.”
