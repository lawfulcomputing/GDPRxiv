The expression 'supplementary means' must be analyzed in the context of Article 23 of the Notarial Law, which resorts to such expression in the absence of the notary personally and directly knowing the person requesting the provision of their public function (knowledge by notoriety). In the case of online and remote actions, this option is non-existent, which is why resorting to the supplementary means (use of identification documents issued by a competent public authority) is the only possible one.

Regarding the first of the mentioned aspects, notarial legislation requires that notaries certify in public deeds that they know the parties or have ensured their identity by the supplementary means established in the laws and regulations (art. 23 LN). However, the processing necessary for the execution of that contract is the identification of the client based on the notarial legislation itself, which does not require the processing of personal data such as the retention of the ID card, as we have seen, since this obligation of retention derives from the regulations on the prevention of money laundering. Consequently, an appropriate legal basis for the retention of the ID card would be that established in Article 6.1.c) GDPR, that is, when the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller (the notary) is subject. Therefore, while the processing of personal data consisting of the identification of individuals would be lawful both because it is necessary for the execution of a contract and because it is imposed by notarial legislation, the processing of personal data consisting of the retention of the ID card would only be lawful when specifically established by law, which, as we have seen, does not impose it in all cases, but only when it concerns "clients," that is, individuals who are going to be part of legal relationships, and this only in cases where the regulations on the prevention of money laundering are applicable.  
For this reason, outside the cases provided for in the aforementioned regulations, the retention of such identifying documents will require another basis of legitimacy distinct from "compliance with a legal obligation" contemplated in Article 6.1.c) since there is no obligation to retain copies of identifying documents in the indicated cases.  
Moreover, the obligation to retain the digitized copy of the ID card is enforceable against notaries and not against the General Council of Notaries, which will require a legal basis to legitimize this processing operation.  
Thus, from the above, it follows that the General Council of Notaries did not have a basis of legitimacy to retain copies of the ID cards.

The absence of a conflict of interest is closely linked to the requirement to act independently. Although DPOs may have other functions, they may only be assigned other tasks and responsibilities if these do not lead to conflicts of interest.  
This means, in particular, that the DPO cannot hold a position in the organization that leads him to determine the purposes and means of processing personal data. Due to the specific organizational structure of each organization, this must be considered on a case-by-case basis.  
As a general rule, conflicting positions within an organization may include senior management positions (such as CEO, COO, CFO, medical director, head of marketing, head of human resources, or head of the IT department) but also other lower positions in the organizational structure if such positions lead to the determination of the purposes and means of processing.  
Furthermore, a conflict of interest may also arise, for example, if a DPO is asked to represent the controller or the processor in court in cases related to data protection.  
Depending on the activities, size, and structure of the organization, it may be advisable practice for controllers and processors to:  
• determine the positions that could be incompatible with the DPO function;  
• develop internal rules for this purpose to avoid conflicts of interest;  
• include a more general explanation about conflicts of interest;  
• declare that their DPO has no conflict of interest regarding his functions as DPO, as a means of raising awareness about this requirement;  
• include safeguards in the internal rules of the organization and ensure that the job announcement for the DPO position or the service contract is sufficiently precise and detailed to avoid a conflict of interest. In this context, it should also be taken into account that conflicts of interest can take various forms depending on whether the DPO is hired internally or externally.”
