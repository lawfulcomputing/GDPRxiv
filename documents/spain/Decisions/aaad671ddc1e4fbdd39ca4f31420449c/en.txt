On July 10, 2022, a Local Police officer of the City Council of Valencia photographed both sides of the complainant's ID card with his personal mobile phone, after his partner had already identified him and noted his details in a notebook.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence and procedure  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Preliminary issues  
In this case, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, it is established that there was a processing of personal data, since the respondent collected, among others, the following personal data of individuals: name and surname and ID number, among other processing operations.  
The respondent carried out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
For its part, Article 5 of the GDPR establishes the principles of processing, and Article 32 of the GDPR regulates the appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an adequate level of security.

The responding party argues that it makes no sense to take the data from the ID card and then take photographs of it, as that information would already be available. Therefore, logically, the temporal sequence should have been as indicated by the police officer, that is, taking the photograph, transferring the information contained in it to the complaint report, and deleting the photograph.  
In this regard, this Agency wishes to point out that, for the purposes of this sanctioning procedure, it is irrelevant whether the photographs of the ID card were taken before or after the other officer noted the data contained therein, and thus this allegation is dismissed.  
The responding party continues by analyzing the proportionality of the means used to collect the data from the complainant, namely, taking photographs with the acting officer's personal device.  
It points out that Organic Law 4/2015, of March 30, on the protection of citizen security, provides in its article 9.2 that “All persons required to obtain the National Identity Document are also required to display it and allow the verification of the security measures referred to in paragraph 2 of article 8 when requested to do so by the authority or its agents, for the fulfillment of the purposes provided for in paragraph 1 of article 16,” while the latter article establishes that identification shall respect, among other principles, the principle of proportionality and comply with data protection regulations.  
The responding party acknowledges that taking the photograph through the acting officer's personal device constitutes a dysfunction and has been noted on numerous occasions by the Local Police Headquarters, both before and after the events occurred. However, it argues that such dysfunction was likely generated as a consequence of the concurrent circumstances (a tense situation with the citizen and refusal to identify themselves), which may have led the officer to use some means to prevent the situation from prolonging and evolving in such a way that more coercive measures would need to be adopted. Therefore, even acknowledging that the means used was not appropriate, it is considered that there may have been some proportionality in light of those circumstances, which could be taken into account by the AEPD when analyzing the facts and weighing their compliance with the principle of data minimization and the corresponding absence of infringement of article 5.1, c of the GDPR.  
In this regard, this Agency wishes to point out that the responding party itself acknowledges that using a personal phone to capture the image of the complainant's ID card was not appropriate. The fact that a tense situation occurred with an individual does not authorize the agents of the Local Police of Valencia to use such devices. In fact, Organic Law 4/2014 only obliges individuals to display the ID card and allow its verification when requested to do so by the authority or its agents; it does not authorize these agents to capture images with their personal devices.  
Therefore, this allegation is dismissed.

The use of a personal mobile phone to capture the image of a citizen's ID card is not covered by either the specific regulations applicable to the State Security Forces and Corps or by the regulations on personal data protection.  
Article 5 of the GDPR establishes the principles related to the processing of personal data by the controller and/or processor, and in its section 1.c) it specifies that “the processing of personal data must be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed,” known as the principle of “data minimization.”  
It should be clarified that this article does not limit the use of a large number of data, but rather the necessity of such data in relation to the purpose of the processing. That is, personal data will be “adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary” for the purpose for which they were collected, so that if the intended objective could have been achieved by other means, without excessive data processing, as in the present case of taking a photograph of the ID card, the latter should have been used in any case. This is also indicated in Recital 39 of the GDPR, which states: “Personal data should only be processed if the purpose of the processing could not reasonably be fulfilled by other means.”  
Therefore, only data that is “adequate, relevant and not excessive in relation to the purpose for which it is obtained or processed” will be processed. The categories of data selected for processing must be strictly necessary to achieve the stated objective, and the data controller must strictly limit the collection of data to that information which is directly related to the specific purpose being pursued.  
The fact that a police officer took a photograph of the ID card of the complaining party constitutes an excessive processing of personal data, contrary to the principle of “data minimization” (Art. 5.1.c) GDPR), considering that the existing situation at that moment allowed for a less invasive data collection. While security forces need to identify citizens in the exercise of their functions, it is not necessary to capture the image of their ID card; mere exhibition and, if necessary, notation is sufficient.  
Therefore, in accordance with the evidence available at this moment of resolution of the sanctioning procedure, it is considered that the known facts constitute an infringement, attributable to the party being claimed, for violation of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR.

VI  
Security of processing  
Article 32 “Security of processing” of the GDPR establishes:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. When assessing the adequacy of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a result of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this Article.

4. The data controller and the data processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data may only process such data in accordance with the instructions of the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law.  
The GDPR, in its Article 32, requires data controllers to adopt the necessary technical and organizational security measures to ensure that processing is in compliance with current regulations, as well as to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data may only process such data in accordance with the instructions of the controller.  
Furthermore, Recital (74) of the GDPR states: “The responsibility of the data controller for any processing of personal data carried out by him or on his behalf must be established. In particular, the controller must be obliged to implement appropriate and effective measures and must be able to demonstrate compliance of processing activities with this Regulation, including the effectiveness of the measures. Such measures must take into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.”  
Therefore, the collection of data from a citizen by agents of the State Security Forces and Corps must be carried out using technical and organizational methods that guarantee the security and confidentiality of personal data, following the precise instructions of the data controller.  
According to the file, one of the agents of the Local Police of the accused party took a photograph of the ID card of the claimant with his personal mobile phone. The accused party stated that the official took only one image of the ID and that the image of the document was deleted once the purpose for which it was taken was fulfilled and that “from the Headquarters, instructions have been repeatedly issued regarding not taking or receiving personal data through photographs or images by personal devices.” It was also stated that a subsequent Instruction from the Corps was adopted with precise indications regarding this matter and a prohibition for the members of the Corps to take images that may contain personal data on personal devices; which is mandatory compliance under penalty of incurring disciplinary responsibility.  
In the present case, at the time the events subject to this complaint occurred, the security measures adopted were neither sufficient nor appropriate to prevent the agents of the Local Police of the accused party from taking photographs of citizens' ID cards with their personal mobile phones.  
Therefore, in accordance with the evidence available at this moment of resolution of the sanctioning procedure, it is considered that the known facts constitute an infringement, attributable to the accused party, for violation of Article 32 of the GDPR.
