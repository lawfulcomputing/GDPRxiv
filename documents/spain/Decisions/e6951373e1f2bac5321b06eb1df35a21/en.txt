LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  
II  
The claimed facts are concretized in the impersonation in the contracting of the electricity supply located at the claimant's address, who did not make any request to change the gas and electricity supplier, nor authorized its change, which could violate the regulations on the protection of personal data.

III  
Article 6, Lawfulness of processing, of the GDPR in its paragraph 1, establishes that:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in letter f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.”

V  
1. It should be noted that the processing of data requires the existence of a legal basis that legitimizes it.  
In accordance with Article 6.1 of the GDPR, in addition to consent, there are other possible bases that legitimize the processing of data without the need for the authorization of its owner, in particular, when it is necessary for the
12/23  
Execution of a contract in which the affected party is a party or for the application, at their request, of pre-contractual measures, or when necessary for the satisfaction of legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, provided that such interests do not prevail over the interests or the fundamental rights and freedoms of the affected party that require the protection of such data. The processing is also considered lawful when necessary for compliance with a legal obligation applicable to the data controller, to protect vital interests of the affected party or another natural person, or for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of public powers conferred on the data controller.  
From the documentation in the file, it is evident that the respondent has violated Article 6.1 of the GDPR, by causing the termination of the electricity supply contract that the claimant had with the company IBERDROLA, registering their deceased father as the contractor for the same address and CUPS, without any legitimate basis for processing the data as contemplated in the aforementioned article.  
In this regard, the National Court stated in its ruling of 18/11/2022, Appeal 590/2022, “…That is to say, for the processing to be lawful, personal data must be processed with the consent of the data subject or on some other legitimate basis established in accordance with the law, and it is certainly necessary that the data processor is aware of the consent of the person granting it and in the context in which it is given.  
None of this occurs in the present case; there has been no consent from the claimant for the modification of the contract, consent which is defined as "Any manifestation of will, free, unequivocal, specific, and informed, by which the data subject consents to the processing of personal data concerning them." And it has not been proven by the plaintiff that such consent existed as required by Article 7.1.”

In a document dated 27/04/2022, it is stated that “In this regard, to request a change of supplier, it is not necessary to provide information and documentation other than that which is required to carry out a new contract and verify identity.”  
In connection with the above, in a case such as the one we are examining, where a change of supplier was requested along with a simultaneous change of account holder, especially after the first request for a change of supplier was rejected due to a new registration, it requires that the entity not only comply with the specific regulations of the electricity sector but also carry out a minimum due diligence to verify that the documentation and the person attempting to contract is who they claim to be and to effectively manage the requested change.  
It should be noted that various legal norms impact this matter, among which the Directive (EU) 2019/944 of the European Parliament and of the Council, dated 5/06/2019, on common rules for the internal market for electricity and which amends Directive 2012/27/EU (hereinafter, Directive 2019/944), is noteworthy. Article 23, “Data Management,” states in paragraph 3 that “The processing of personal data under this Directive shall be carried out in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2016/679”; Royal Decree 1955/2000, of December 1, which regulates the activities of transport, distribution, marketing, supply, and authorization procedures for electricity installations; Royal Decree 1435/2002, which regulates the basic conditions of contracts for the acquisition of energy and access to low voltage networks, dedicating its Article 7 to the “Information System of Supply Points that the distributor must maintain” (SIPS); Royal Decree 1011/2009, which regulates the Supplier Change Office, which is tasked with, among its functions (Article 3.s)), verifying the effective consent of consumers to the change of supplier; Law 24/2013, of December 26, on the Electricity Sector; the Third Additional Provision of Royal Decree 1074/2015; and the Resolution of the National Commission of Markets and Competition dated 17/12/2019 “by which the new formats of the information exchange files between distributors and marketers are approved and the Resolution of 20/12/2016 is modified.”

In deciding the imposition of an administrative fine and its amount in each individual case, the following shall be duly taken into account:  
a) the nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the processing operation in question as well as the number of data subjects affected and the level of damage and harm they have suffered;

c) any measures taken by the controller or processor to mitigate the damage and harm suffered by the data subjects;
