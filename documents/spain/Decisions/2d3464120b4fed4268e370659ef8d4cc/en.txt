FOURTH.- INTERPRETATION OF THE CONCEPT OF COMMERCIAL COMMUNICATION  
The LSSI defines in point f) of its Annex commercial communications as "Any form of communication aimed at the promotion, directly or indirectly, of the image or of the goods or services of a company, organization, or person engaged in an industrial, artisanal, or professional activity. For the purposes of this Law, data that allows direct access to the activity of a person, company, or organization, such as the domain name or email address, shall not be considered commercial communication, nor shall communications related to the goods, services, or image offered when they are produced by a third party and without economic consideration."  
Unlike what the AEPD has interpreted in the present file by stating that "it is evident" that WIZINK sent the claimant commercial or advertising messages for the purposes of the LSSI, the communication sent to the claimant regarding the "WiZink Extra" bonus program included in their card does not aim to promote the contracting of any product or service of the Bank, nor does it involve any economic consideration, but rather aims to inform the client in accordance with the card contract about which merchants are affiliated with said program.  
In this regard, it is noteworthy that the AEPD considers the communication sent to the claimant regarding the "WiZink Extra" program, which is the subject of this sanctioning file, to be commercial, when it is made under the umbrella of the card contract signed with the <<claimant>>, as established in that contract by indicating that the card incorporates a bonus program "WiZink Extra," and when the interpretation that the Audiencia Nacional has on this, in the ruling issued on May 16, 2019, Rec. 493/2017, where it analyzes the case of a client who has contracted a roadside assistance service for a breakdown of their vehicle, which to complete the coverage, said assistance is associated with a service consisting of sending them an SMS that says: "from Autoclub Mutua we offer you to rent a car from 21.30 euros per day. Call us at 915578205" CONSIDERING THAT IT IS NOT COMMERCIAL FOR THE PURPOSES OF ARTICLE 21 OF THE LSSI, indicating the following:

FIFTH.- NON-APPLICATION OF THE LAW ON INFORMATION SOCIETY SERVICES  
It should be emphasized that, said in strict terms of defense and with the utmost respect, the AEPD SHOULD NOT apply the LSSI IN THIS CASE, which directly implies that the sanction sought for the ALLEGED commission of an infringement of Article 21 of said regulation is not legally justified.  
This is based on the definitions included in the Annex of the aforementioned regulation:  
“For the purposes of this Law, the following shall be understood as: a) ‘Information society services’ or ‘services’: any service normally provided for remuneration, at a distance, by electronic means and at the individual request of the recipient. The concept of information society service also includes services that are not remunerated by their recipients, insofar as they constitute an economic activity for the service provider. The following are considered information society services, among others, and provided they represent an economic activity: 1. The contracting of goods or services by electronic means. 2. The organization and management of auctions by electronic means or of virtual markets and shopping centers. 3. The management of online purchases by groups of people. 4. The sending of commercial communications. 5. The provision of information by telematic means. Services that do not meet the characteristics indicated in the first paragraph of this section shall not be considered information society services…”  
Therefore, it is understood that the application of the aforementioned regulation is not pertinent given that the communication of the “WiZInk Extra” Program does not meet any of the characteristics described in the annex of the LSSI.  
With a similar criterion, the AEPD has interpreted in file E/07708/2019 regarding the sending of the following SMS by the claimed entity: “Hello, I am B.B.B. I recommend you to sell your motorcycle ***URL.3”, that with this SMS sent, no service of the law on information society is provided. Furthermore, in said resolution, it is indicated that the communication sent does not contain commercial information because the link does not lead to a possible sale of any kind of product or service. On the contrary, the page to which it redirects serves to assist those who are genuinely interested in selling their vehicle regarding relevant aspects such as appraisal value, tax value, the advertisement.  
And in accordance with the aforementioned, the AEPD concludes that: “In light of the norms transcribed in the preceding Foundation, the documentation in the file reveals that the electronic message received by the complainant was not a commercial communication and that through it no information society service was provided.”

Notwithstanding the above, if AEPD refers to the following links: (i) ***URL.1 as well as (ii) www.wizink.es/public/informacion-legal/proteccion-de-datos, it will see that the content matches DOCUMENT NUMBER 2 which is part of the response sent on 04/11/2022 to the client as a result of the complaint received from AEPD.  
- Likewise, it should be noted that while the communications sent to the complainant by my client since 05/05/2022 had an informative content, it is also true that WIZINK, upon becoming aware of the complaint filed by our client with AEPD, and proactively considering the client's wishes, took the necessary measures to ensure that the complainant would not receive any further communication regarding the “WiZINK Extra” program, despite remaining enrolled in it. Regarding any potential harm caused to the complainant, it has not been demonstrated that any harm has resulted from informing him of a bonus program that incorporates the card with the aim of keeping him informed about the merchants participating in said program.  
SEVENTH.- SUBSIDIARILY, THE PRESCRIPTION OF THE OFFENSE ATTRIBUTED TO WIZINK IS INVOKED  
As previously established, WIZINK has complied in this case with the provisions of Article 21 of the LSSI, having sent the complainant communications by email with exclusively informative content.  
Notwithstanding the above, the infraction that AEPD attributes to my client is classified as a minor infraction according to the provisions of Article 38.4.d) of the LSSI, which qualifies as such “the sending of commercial communications by email or other equivalent electronic communication means when the requirements established in Article 21 are not met and does not constitute a serious infraction.”  
While my client requests the filing of the proceedings having demonstrated that it has complied at all times with the provisions of said regulation, in the event that AEPD continues to interpret that my client should be attributed with a minor infraction of the LSSI, given that the aforementioned infraction prescribes after six months, it requests that it be considered subsidiarily that the alleged infraction of Article 21 LSSI has prescribed after the lapse of the 6 months provided for, which are counted from the date on which, in AEPD's interpretation, WIZINK committed the infraction, and therefore the case should be archived.  
That is to say, considering that the prescription period for the same is six (6) months and the Agreement to initiate the Sanctioning Procedure was notified to this party on 23 February 2023, such infractions are completely prescribed as more than six (6) months have elapsed since their alleged commission.  
In light of the above, I REQUEST THE SPANISH AGENCY FOR DATA PROTECTION to accept this written statement of allegations as presented in time and form, to admit it, and, after the appropriate procedures, to agree to archive the referenced file, rendering the sanctioning procedure initiated against WIZINK ineffective.  
Subsidiarily to the main request, if the Instructor does not grant the request, the aggravating criterion for the graduation of the sanction in accordance with Article 40 LSSI and the sixth allegation of this document should not be applied, and WIZINK's proactive action reflecting the client's expressed wish mentioned in the previous sixth allegation should be considered as a mitigating factor, and in that case, the amount of the proposed sanction for the minor infraction of LSSI should be reduced. The LSSI applies to the following services related to the Internet when they constitute an economic or lucrative activity for the service provider in question: Electronic commerce; Online contracting; Information and advertising; and Intermediation services.  
Who provides services related to the Internet? Well, according to the LSSI, they can be grouped into “Intermediation Service Providers” such as, Companies that provide Internet connection to their clients (ISPs); Data hosting service providers; Link services; Search engines or “Companies and individuals” that will be Legal entities and individuals who carry out economic activities through the Internet.  
Thus, these are concepts specific to the LSSI, all of which have a definition established in the regulation itself: on one hand, both the heading of the article and the literal wording of the article refer to the recipient, who according to section d) of the Annex of the LSSI is the “natural or legal person who uses, whether or not for professional reasons, a service of the information society.” That is to say, we are not talking about all types of users, but about recipients in a legal sense. On the other hand, the Annex of the LSSI defines in its section c) the service provider as the “natural or legal person who provides a service of the information society.” And by service of the information society, section a) understands “any service normally provided for a fee, at a distance, by electronic means and at the individual request of the recipient. The concept of service of the information society also includes services that are not remunerated by their recipients, insofar as they constitute an economic activity for the service provider.”

III.-  
Regarding the sending of emails with advertising messages after having previously opposed it:  
As we indicated earlier, the LSSI defines, in point f) of its Annex, “commercial communications” as:  
“Any form of communication aimed at the promotion, directly or indirectly, of the image or of the goods or services of a company, organization, or person engaged in commercial, industrial, artisanal, or professional activity.  
For the purposes of this Law, data that allows direct access to the activity of a person, company, or organization, such as the domain name or email address, will not be considered commercial communication, nor communications related to the goods, services, or image offered when they are prepared by a third party and without economic consideration.  
Therefore, it is evident that the emails sent by the entity WIZINK to the claimant on 08/09/22 and 03/11/22 (the latter email provided by the entity itself), after the claimant requested “no further advertising” on 05/05/22, contained an eminently advertising, commercial, or promotional nature, as it may be viewed, for the purposes of the LSSI and therefore violated what is established in this regulation.  
IV  
Classification of the possible infringement committed  
Article 21 of the LSSI states the following:  
“1. The sending of advertising or promotional communications by email or other equivalent electronic communication means that have not been previously requested or expressly authorized by the recipients is prohibited.  
2. The provisions of the previous section shall not apply when there is a prior contractual relationship, provided that the provider has lawfully obtained the contact details of the recipient and uses them to send commercial communications regarding products or services of its own company that are similar to those initially contracted with the customer.  
In any case, the provider must offer the recipient the possibility of opposing the processing of their data for promotional purposes through a simple and free procedure, both at the time of data collection and in each of the commercial communications directed to them.  
When communications have been sent by email, this means must necessarily consist of the inclusion of an email address or another valid electronic address where this right can be exercised, and the sending of communications that do not include such an address is prohibited.”
