In a subsidiary manner, in the event that this Agency understands that Vodafone has indeed violated Article 6.1 of the GDPR, no sanction can be imposed on my client for the reasons that will be seen below.  
I. Vodafone has not acted culpably, therefore no sanction can be imposed.  
We are facing criminal practices (which evolve over time) carried out by fraudsters and cybercriminals (in many cases acting through criminal organizations and organized gangs, such as some dismantled as a result of Vodafone's collaboration with the State Security Forces and Corps) who, acting with intent, utilize the unavoidable human intervention in Vodafone's system to take advantage of the fact that a specific person (the employee or operator processing the SIM change) may, in good faith and subject to the abuse of the criminal, relax the established protocols. These are criminals who, using tricks and criminal activities initiated at a time prior to the request for a SIM change (and, therefore, at a stage where neither Vodafone – nor any other operator – can do anything about it) steal data from entities, forge IDs, report thefts of said IDs, and obtain the personal data of the future victim in order to bypass the security protocols established by Vodafone. Once the identity has been impersonated and, if applicable, the security measures of the telephone operator have been surpassed, these third parties continue their criminal activities aimed at circumventing other security measures, those established by the banking entities of Vodafone's customers.  
Taking the above into account, it must be determined whether Vodafone has exercised the diligence required of it to ensure the legality of the processing of its customer's personal data and to prevent the duplication of SIM cards by third parties other than the holder. This is precisely what occurs in the present case, and in no case can the duplication of the Vodafone customer's SIM card imply that this entity has acted culpably. Indeed, all actions by Vodafone have always been directed towards the establishment and supervision of technical and organizational measures aimed at ensuring the security of its customers' personal data.  
Consequently, Vodafone has acted at all times in compliance with the due diligence required of it, and no culpability can be attributed to it. Therefore, taking into account the special nature of sanctioning administrative law that determines the impossibility of imposing sanctions without considering culpability and diligence, or the errors that may have led to the breach of a legal obligation, this party maintains the impropriety of imposing any sanction.

Regarding the application of Article 76.2.c) of the LOPDGDD, in connection with Article 83.2.k), the non-existence of benefits obtained, it should be noted that such circumstance can only operate as an aggravating factor and in no case as a mitigating circumstance.  
Article 83.2.k) of the GDPR refers to “any other aggravating or mitigating factor applicable to the circumstances of the case, such as financial benefits obtained or losses avoided, directly or indirectly, through the infringement.” And Article 76.2.c) of the LOPDGDD states that “2. In accordance with the provisions of Article 83.2.k) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following may also be taken into account: [...] c) The benefits obtained as a result of the commission of the infringement.” Both provisions mention as a factor that may be considered in the assessment of the sanction the “benefits” obtained, but not the “absence” of these, which is what Vodafone claims.  
Furthermore, according to Article 83.1 of the GDPR, the imposition of fines is governed by the following principles: they must be individualized for each particular case, be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. The admission that the absence of benefits operates as a mitigating factor is contrary to the spirit of Article 83.1 of the GDPR and to the principles governing the determination of the amount of the fine. If, as a result of the commission of a GDPR infringement, it is qualified as a mitigating factor that no benefits have existed, the dissuasive purpose fulfilled through the sanction is partially nullified. Accepting Vodafone's thesis in a case like the one at hand would imply introducing an artificial reduction in the sanction that truly should be imposed; the one resulting from considering the circumstances of Article 83.2 GDPR that must indeed be evaluated.  
The Administrative Litigation Chamber of the National Court has warned that the fact that in a specific case not all the elements that constitute a modifying circumstance of liability, which by its nature has an aggravating character, are present, cannot lead to the conclusion that such circumstance is applicable as a mitigating factor. The ruling made by the National Court in its SAN of May 5, 2021 (Rec. 1437/2020) -even though that resolution deals with the circumstance of subsection e) of Article 83.2 of the GDPR, the commission of previous infringements- is extrapolable to the issue raised, the claim of the respondent that the “absence” of benefits be accepted as a mitigating factor given that both the GDPR and the LOPDGDD refer only to “the benefits obtained.”

Recital 148 of the GDPR states, “In order to strengthen the application of the rules of this Regulation [...]” and indicates in this regard that “Special attention must nevertheless be paid to the nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, its intentional character [...] or to any relevant infringement [...]”.  
Thus, in accordance with paragraph e) of Article 83.2 of the GDPR, in determining the amount of the administrative fine, all previous infringements of the data controller or processor must be assessed in order to gauge the unlawfulness of the analyzed conduct or the culpability of the infringing party.  
Furthermore, a correct interpretation of the provision of Article 83.2.e) of the GDPR cannot overlook the purpose pursued by the regulation: to decide the amount of the administrative fine in the individual case presented, always ensuring that the sanction is proportional, effective, and dissuasive.
