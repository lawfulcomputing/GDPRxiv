RESOLUTION OF SANCTIONING PROCEDURE  
From the procedure instructed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST: Ms. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant), on 14/05/2021, filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection. The complaint is directed against METROPOLITAN SPAIN, S.L., with NIF B62936737 (hereinafter, the respondent).  
The reasons on which the complaint is based are as follows:  
The complainant is a member of the METROPOLITAN CLUB gym, located at c/ Cisneros 67 in Santander. She joined this center in September 2020, and until 6/05/2021, access was granted via a wristband and an identification card at the reception computer. Since the aforementioned date, users have been required to access the facilities using a fingerprint. She has refused to provide her fingerprint data, understanding that this is an excessive request for biometric data, and has been informed that they will terminate her membership, with no alternative provided.  
She provides a copy of the complaint form submitted to Consumer Affairs on May 6, 2021, a copy to be sent by the complainant to the General Directorate indicated on the form within 10 days, without any record of its submission.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of 5/12, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the complaint was forwarded to the respondent for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
The forwarding, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of 1/10, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was received on 7/06/2021, as evidenced by the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  
No response has been received to this forwarding letter.  

THIRD: On 14/08/2021, in accordance with and for the purposes of Article 65.5 of the LOPDGDD, the complaint was considered admitted for processing.

Only the non-acceptance of either of the two possibilities conditioned that the individual could not be a member of the gym, but at no time was the possibility of being a member conditioned on the acceptance of the biometric fingerprint, as was claimed by the complainant. Consent should not be considered freely given when the interested party does not have true or free choice, or "cannot deny or withdraw their consent without suffering any detriment."  
In this case, no detriment has occurred to the complainant or the other partners, as there exists another possibility of access to the center without a fingerprint, that of the image of the partner momentarily displayed on a reception screen, "and proof of this are the Excel files provided of all accesses between 1/02 and 31/07/2021, the date of their cancellation for whatever circumstance."

“biometric data”: personal data obtained through a specific technical processing, related to the physical, physiological, or behavioral characteristics of a natural person that allow or confirm the unique identification of that person, such as facial images or fingerprint data;  
To be considered biometric data in the sense of the GDPR, the processing of raw data, such as the physical, physiological, or behavioral characteristics of a natural person must involve a measurement of those characteristics. Thus, from the concept, it should not be overlooked:  
- The nature of the data: data related to the physical, physiological, or behavioral characteristics of a natural person;  
- The means and forms of processing: data “obtained through a specific technical processing”; which differentiates, for example, from images of a person that appear in a surveillance system, which cannot be considered biometric data unless they have been technically processed in a specific manner to contribute to the unique identification of that person. Recital 51 of the GDPR also refers to the systematic non-consideration of photographs as special category data processing, unless a specific technical processing means is applied that “allows the unique identification or authentication of a natural person.”  
- The purpose of the processing: the data must be used for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person.  
Biometric data has the particularity of being produced by the body itself and definitively characterizes it; these are not data about that person, but rather the data refers to the same person, which in principle cannot be modified by the will of the individual, nor can the person be released from them; they cannot be changed in the event of compromise, loss, or intrusion into the system. Furthermore, because biometric data is unique to a person and perpetual, the user uses the same data across different systems. Therefore, identity theft is not only perpetual over time but also affects all systems in which a user has stored their biometric data. The holder, therefore, does not have the possibility to use one biometric data for the bank and a different one for their health system; rather, they use the same information to verify their identity in both, and in the event of a vulnerability, all of them are affected simultaneously.
