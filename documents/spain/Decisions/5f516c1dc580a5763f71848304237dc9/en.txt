From the proceedings carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following:  
FACTS  
FIRST: On August 30 and September 24, 2022, two complaints were filed with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against BANCO BILBAO VIZCAYA ARGENTARIA, S.A. (hereinafter, the complained party).  
The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The complainant states that, on September 6, 2021, a third party fraudulently modified the address data related to the account owned by their mother (in which the complainant was listed as authorized), as well as the request for a second card associated with said account (which was not requested by the complainant or by their mother) and the subsequent fraudulent use of said card at ATMs.  
On October 5, 2021, the complainant informed the complained party of the facts, repeatedly requesting to know the manner and identity of the third party who modified the address data associated with the bank account, as well as the identity of the requester of the second card, without success, since on May 18 and January 21, 2022, the complained party stated that the disputed address was provided by the complainant when registering the contract related to the card, on September 6, 2021, as stated in the document titled "Data Protection Policy and Economic Activity Declaration," all "with the correct insertion of their personal keys," adding that "a communication was sent to their validated email in which the pre-contractual information and the card contract were attached for review prior to signing and an SMS to their validated mobile phone, informing of the card PIN (...). In the case presented to us and following the criteria of good practices required of entities, at BBVA we have acted fully in accordance with these criteria."  
Furthermore, regarding the unrecognized charges made with the disputed card, a response was received on February 28, 2022, indicating that "on a punctual basis" they had proceeded to attend to the complainant's request favorably, requesting that a bank account number be provided for the reimbursement.  
On the other hand, it is stated that the complainant's mother, on May 24, 2022, requested from the complained party (through the address derechosprotecciondatos@bbva.com) to know "the date on which she requested and the day she canceled" the disputed card, receiving a response (on June 3 and 28, 2022) indicating the following: "We regret to inform you that we have been unable to process your request, as the subject of it is excluded from the exercise of rights provided by the LOPD," indicating other channels to which to direct inquiries, among which was the Customer Service, with a response from that department dated July 29, 2022, indicating that "regarding the contract of the indicated card, we regret to inform you that, despite the efforts made, it has not been possible to locate said documentation."

Regarding the possible identity theft

In the written statement of allegations, there is a screenshot of the history of the keys sent to the validated phone number of the complainant (OTP-SMS) to validate each of the operations: change of address, request for a card, and change of the availability limit at ATMs of the card, which was initially 1,000 euros.  
In attached document 1, regarding the analysis of evidence of the disputed operations, a screenshot of the existing traces in their systems is presented, confirming that the change of address was made on September 6, 2021, at 13:42:01h and the request for a card a few minutes later at 13:47:00h, and the change of the card limit at 13:48:18, with all requests made from the IP ***IP.1 associated with the provider ORANGE ESPAGNE S.A.U., and through authentication on the BBVA NET platform using the personal username and password of the complainant.  
Additionally, in explanatory document EXP202210727_resto_documentos.pdf, received in this Agency on March 24, 2023, the digital signatures made from the phone number of the complainant are certified by the entity CAMERFIRMA for all the disputed operations: change of address, signing of the card contract, and limit increase.  
From the above, it follows that the person who carried out these operations must have known the remote banking credentials of the complainant in addition to having access to the SMS text messages from their phone number and possibly to the email inbox.  
Regarding the execution of the digital signatures of the operations through keys sent by SMS, this could only have been possible by having access to the complainant's phone number, either because prior to the signature a SIM card swap had been requested from the operator of that number, or because a duplicate SIM or eSIM for that number had been requested from the operator, or because the complainant's device had been compromised and SMS forwarding had been programmed. The first option has been ruled out because it would imply the loss of the line by the complainant, and in none of their writings has this fact been stated.

II  
Legality of processing  
Article 6 of the GDPR, which regulates the legality of processing, establishes the following:  
"1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary in order to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in point f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.  
2. Member States may maintain or introduce more specific provisions to adapt the application of the rules of this Regulation with respect to processing under paragraph 1, points c) and e), laying down more precise requirements for specific processing and other measures to ensure lawful and fair processing, including other specific situations of processing under Chapter IX.  
3. The basis for processing referred to in paragraph 1, points c) and e), shall be laid down by:  
a) Union law, or  
b) the law of the Member States applicable to the controller.  
The purpose of the processing shall be determined in that legal basis or, in relation to the processing referred to in paragraph 1, point e), shall be necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller. This legal basis may contain specific provisions to adapt the application of the rules of this Regulation, among others: the general conditions governing the lawfulness of processing by the controller; the types of data subject to processing; the data subjects

Security of Processing

"1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which shall include, where appropriate, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;

IV  
Conclusion  
It should be noted that there is no documentation provided by the claimant that includes the email sent to the defendant entity, dated January 26, 2022, stating that on September 6, 2021, at noon, they had difficulties opening the BBVA app on their mobile phone and had to enter the codes sent via SMS multiple times in that app, and that once inside the app, a window opened asking for the name and address of the account holder in order for it to finally open.  
This information is of utmost interest as in this case the claimant could have been a victim of some type of app or website forgery by the defendant, or the installation of malicious software on their mobile device.  
In fact, as previously mentioned, in the communication dated January 21, 2022, sent by the defendant to the claimant, it informed the latter that the change of address and the contracting of the disputed card that took place on September 6, 2021, through online banking, was done with the correct entry of their personal codes and that a communication was also sent to their validated email address at the entity, which included the pre-contractual information and the contract for the card, for their review before signing, and an SMS to their validated mobile phone, informing them of the card's PIN (document 11).  
Everything points to the fact that the claimant was a victim of impersonation of the mobile application or website, or of the installation of malicious software, with which a third party obtained their codes and accessed the SMS messages sent to the claimant in such a way that they could change their address, request the disputed bank card, and increase the withdrawal limit at ATMs. In this regard, it is verified that the defendant took reasonable security measures by obtaining the digital signature for each of the disputed operations from the validated phone number of the claimant and through the qualified certification entity CAMERFIRMA.
