RESOLUTION OF RECONSIDERATION APPEAL  
Examined the reconsideration appeal filed by DIGI SPAIN TELECOM, S.L. (hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated July 20, 2023, and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On July 20, 2023, a resolution was issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202213029, by virtue of which DIGI SPAIN TELECOM, S.L., with NIF B84919760, was imposed a fine of 70,000 euros (seventy thousand euros) for an infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR.  
This resolution, which was notified to the appellant on July 24, 2023, was issued following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), and subsidiarily under Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), regarding the processing of sanctioning procedures.  

SECOND: As proven facts of the aforementioned sanctioning procedure, EXP202213029, the following was recorded:  
1. The complainant filed a complaint with this Agency on October 27, 2022, stating that at 10:30 AM on October 26, 2022, their mobile phone ***PHONE.1 lost service, after which they received an email from DIGI explaining that a second SIM card had been activated with their number.  
2. DIGI stated that, on October 25, 2022, a third party identifying themselves as the complainant requested a duplicate SIM card for the mobile line ***PHONE.1, through the DIGI stand located in a shopping center, displaying the ID with the complainant's number, and the DIGI distributor proceeded to manage the request for the duplicate card.  
Subsequently, when the complainant contacted DIGI, they first suspended the line and later on October 26, 2022, they recovered it.  
3. DIGI stated that, on October 26, 2022, the DIGI customer service received a new call from a third party identifying themselves as the complainant, and when trying to confirm their identity, the call ended. DIGI recorded this incident and contacted the complainant to establish a security password to request a duplicate SIM card.  
4. DIGI stated that, on October 26, they received at a different establishment from the first a third request for a duplicate SIM card for the line ***PHONE.1. There, they proceeded to issue the SIM card to this third party who was not the holder of the line.  
5. The line was activated irregularly from 4:30 PM to 5:44 PM on the same day, at which point the DIGI agent proceeded to suspend the service.  
6. The complainant contacted DIGI again, and they provided another SIM which the complainant collected at a physical store in their locality, just like the first one, and in which they had to identify themselves with their original ID, but at no time were they asked for the filter that was established to provide the key to authorize and provide the SIM card.  

THIRD: The appellant submitted a reconsideration appeal on August 4, 2023, to this Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
As the basis for the appeal, the appellant reiterates the arguments presented throughout the procedure and considers that from the proven facts it is possible to conclude that DIGI had a procedure in place, which was scrupulously followed, and that allowed them to carry out, in accordance with their obligations, a lawful data processing.  
On the other hand, DIGI states that they make every effort to identify and mitigate fraud attempts, and cannot be imposed with absolute responsibility for its detection; before a third party who has the complainant's telephone, identifying, and banking data, who complies with the protocol, even identifying themselves in person.  
Furthermore, it points out that the AEPD bases its conclusions solely on the outcome, and there is a possibility that even following the protocol and having requested a copy of the ID, it could have been false, so it cannot be concluded as the AEPD does, that if this operation had been carried out correctly, the duplicate should have been denied.  
Additionally, DIGI argues that the AEPD unequivocally imposes an objective responsibility on them, in which, regardless of the diligence and measures taken, the entity's culpability is declared.  

Regarding the lack of proportionality of the proposed sanction and that, following the appropriate procedures, a resolution is issued indicating the filing of procedure EXP202213029.  

Subsidiarily, in the event that the AEPD rules against the legal basis upheld by DIGI, DIGI requests that the AEPD conclude the procedure by means of a warning and, as a last resort, moderate or adjust the sanction stated in the resolution notified to DIGI.

Certainly, the principle of responsibility provided in Article 28 of the LRJSP states that: "Only individuals and legal entities may be sanctioned for acts constituting administrative infractions, as well as, when a law recognizes them the capacity to act, the groups of affected parties, unions, and entities without legal personality and independent or autonomous patrimonies, that are held responsible for them on the grounds of intent or negligence."  
However, the way of attributing responsibility to legal entities does not correspond to the forms of culpability that are attributable to human conduct. Thus, in the case of infractions committed by legal entities, although the element of culpability must be present, it is necessarily applied differently than it is for individuals.  
According to STC 246/1991 "(...) this distinct construction of the attribution of the authorship of the infraction to the legal entity arises from the very nature of the legal fiction to which these subjects respond. The Judgment of the National Court of October 17, 2007 (rec. 63/2006), regarding entities whose activity involves continuous processing of customer data, indicates that “…the Supreme Court has understood that there is negligence whenever a legal duty of care is disregarded, that is, when the offender does not behave with the required diligence. And in assessing the degree of diligence, special consideration must be given to the professionalism of the subject, and there is no doubt that, in the case now examined, when the activity of the appellant involves constant and abundant handling of personal data, it must be insisted upon the rigor and exquisite care to comply with the legal provisions in this regard.”  
As mitigating factors:  
The defendant party effectively resolved the incident that was the subject of the complaint (art. 83.2 c).  
The balance of the circumstances contemplated in Article 83.2 of the GDPR, regarding the infringement committed by violating what is established in Article 6.1 of the GDPR, allows for a sanction of 70,000 euros (seventy thousand euros).  
Therefore, a lack of diligence can be appreciated in the actions of DIGI, without any lack of culpability being noted.  
The AEPD does not deviate from any reasoning nor does it attribute all responsibility to DIGI. It reproaches DIGI for the responsibility that corresponds to it as the controller of that specific processing “Issuance of a duplicate SIM card,” since according to the definition in Article 4.7 of the GDPR, it is the one who determines the purpose and means of the processing carried out.  
DIGI, in its capacity as an operator, must be more demanding when providing a duplicate SIM card. Identity verifications must be exhaustive to avoid identity theft problems.  
At the moment the duplicate SIM card is obtained, access to the second authentication factor is also gained, and therefore, from that moment on, banking fraud could materialize.  
Thus, after analyzing the allegations made in this optional appeal for reconsideration, it is verified that no new legal arguments have been presented that would allow for a reconsideration of the meaning of the sanctioning resolution issued on July 20, 2023.

Conclusion  
Consequently, in this appeal for reconsideration, the appellant has not provided new facts or legal arguments that would allow for a reconsideration of the validity of the contested resolution.
