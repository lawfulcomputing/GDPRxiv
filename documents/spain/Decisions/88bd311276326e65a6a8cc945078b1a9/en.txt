On this particular matter, the Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of "data controller" and "data processor" in the GDPR, adopted by the EDPB on July 7, 2021, detail the following:  
"Although the elements provided in Article 28 of the Regulation constitute its essential content, the contract must serve for the controller and the processor to clarify, through detailed instructions, how these fundamental elements will be applied in practice. Therefore, the data processing contract should not be limited to reproducing the provisions of the GDPR, but must include more specific and concrete information on how the requirements will be met and the level of security that will be required for the processing of personal data subject to the data processing contract. Far from being a merely formal exercise, the negotiation and stipulation of the conditions of the contract serve to specify the details of the processing."  
They add that "Generally, the data processing contract establishes who is the determining party (the data controller) and who is the party that follows the instructions (the data processor)." However, "If one party decides in practice how and why personal data is processed, that party will be the data controller, even if the contract stipulates that it is the processor."  
Regarding "The purposes and means," the aforementioned guidelines include the following considerations:

The determination of the purposes and means is equivalent to deciding, respectively, the why and the how of the processing: in a specific processing operation, the data controller is the party that determines why the processing takes place (that is, "for what purpose" or "for what") and how this objective will be achieved (that is, what means will be employed to achieve it). A natural or legal person that influences the processing of personal data in this way participates, therefore, in the determination of the purposes and means of such processing in accordance with the definition provided in Article 4, point 7, of the GDPR.  
The data controller must decide both on the purpose and on the means of the processing, as described further on. Consequently, it cannot limit itself to determining the purpose: it must also make decisions regarding the means of processing. In contrast, the party acting as the processor can never determine the purpose of the processing. In practice, if a data controller engages a processor to carry out the processing on its behalf, the processor will normally be able to make certain independent decisions about how to carry it out. The EDPB recognizes that the processor may enjoy some leeway to make certain decisions regarding the processing. In this sense, it is necessary to clarify what degree of influence on the "why" and the "how" entails that an entity is considered a data controller and to what extent the processor can make its own decisions.

In this regard, Recital 40 of the GDPR states:

IV  
Classification and qualification of the infringement  
The aforementioned infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR constitutes the commission of one of the infringements classified in Article 83.5 of the GDPR, which under the heading “General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines” provides that:  
“5. Infringements of the following provisions shall be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an undertaking, an amount equivalent to 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) The basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9.”  
The LOPDGD, for the purposes of the prescription of the infringement, qualifies in its Article 72.1 as a very serious infringement, with a prescription period of three years in this case, “b) The processing of personal data without any of the conditions for lawful processing established in Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.”

- The severity of the infringement, taking into account the scope of the processing operation, a circumstance provided for in Article 83.2.a) GDPR. - “The connection of the infringer's activity with the processing of personal data,” a circumstance provided for in Article 76.2.b) LOPDGDD in connection with Article 83.2.k) GDPR.  
The business activity of the respondent necessarily involves the processing of personal data, being one of the most important gas and electricity companies in Spain. This characteristic of its business activity impacts, reinforcing it, the diligence that must be exercised in complying with the principles governing the processing of personal data and in the quality and effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures that must be implemented to ensure respect for the fundamental right.

It is appropriate to graduate the sanction to be imposed on the respondent and set it at the amount of €100,000 for the infringement of Article 83.5 a) GDPR, classified as very serious for the purposes of prescription in Article 72.1b) of the LOPDGDD.
