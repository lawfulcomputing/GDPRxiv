BACKGROUND  
FIRST: On March 18, 2022, the Division of Technological Innovation of this Spanish Agency for Data Protection (hereinafter AEPD or the Agency) was notified of a security breach of personal data reported by I-DE REDES ELÉCTRICAS INTELIGENTES, S.A.U. with NIF A95075578 (hereinafter, I-DE) as the data controller, in which it informs this Agency of the following:  
On the afternoon of March 15, 2022, an attack against the management website for connections (GEA) of I-DE was detected. (…). At this moment, no impact on personal data is identified. The next day, March 16, a brute force attack directed against the same target (GEA) as the incident the previous day was detected. Measures were taken to repel it. On March 17, GEA was reopened, the activity log was analyzed, and it was concluded that there had been an extraction of personal data. It was indicated that the number of affected individuals is 4.5 million customers of this company.  
SECOND: On March 29, 2022, I-DE submitted a new notification expanding the information about the security breach reported on the 18th of the same month, indicating that, after a forensic analysis of the incident, the number of its customers whose data have been affected is 1.35 million and that it is also likely that there are affected data from customers of other companies in the Iberdrola group, as the attacker could potentially have surpassed the security conditions of the information exclusive to I-DE, jumping to ranges of information from other companies, which has already been communicated to the Company's Systems management for a detailed analysis of other impacts on other companies or businesses of the Iberdrola group.

Regulatory Framework  
- The regulatory framework of the electricity sector, Law 54/1997, of November 27, on the Electricity Sector, imposes an obligation of total separation between regulated activities, such as distribution, and liberalized activities, such as commercialization.  
- The right that electricity consumers have to access and connect to the electricity transport and distribution networks in Spanish territory is specifically set forth in Law 24/2013, of December 26, on the Electricity Sector.  
Distribution companies and marketing companies are two distinct entities within the Electricity Sector. In this regard, Law 24/2013, of December 26, on the Electricity Sector defines them as different subjects.  
- According to the regulation of the electricity sector, the consumer, in order to receive electricity at their home, needs to hold two differentiated contracts concerning their supply point (CUPS):  
   On one hand, the energy purchase contract, “supply contract,” which is signed between a consumer and an electricity marketing company.

   On the other hand, the network access contract or distribution or transport contract, “ATR contract,” which the consumer signs with the intermediation as a representative of the marketing company with which they have contracted the purchase of electricity.  
  Although it can also be signed directly with the company that owns the network, this is not typical for individual consumers but rather for large electricity-consuming companies, as indicated by I-DE, IBERCLI, and CURENERGÍA in their response.  
- When a customer wishes to contract electricity at a supply point or make any contractual modification, that customer approaches a marketer, who on behalf of the customer and as their representative contracts in their name the ATR contract, the network access contract.  
Any contractual modification requested by a marketer from a distributor is carried out through digital XML requests complying with the exchange formats between agents established by the National Commission on Markets and Competition (CNMC), by virtue of the Resolution of December 20, 2016, which approves the formats of the information exchange files between electricity and natural gas distributors and marketers, and the Resolution of December 17, 2019, which approves new formats of the information exchange files between distributors and marketers and modifies the Resolution of December 20, 2016.

ELEVENTH: On May 24, 2023, I-DE submitted a written request for the accumulation of the present file with EXP202305587, as well as the suspension of the deadline for the submission of allegations until this request is resolved, indicating the following:  
I-DE understands that the facts that serve as the basis for the exercise of the sanctioning power that the Agency seeks to exercise are or have a unique basis that affects both sanctioning files that have been opened as differentiated, and therefore requests the accumulation of both sanctioning procedures, understanding that there is a necessary connectivity between them, that is, it concerns the same situation that may result in the responsibility of both. I-DE understands that the terms of such responsibility, total, partial, in the degree of author, collaborator, or any other that may arise from the criminal references can only be assessed if the procedure is analyzed as a whole.  
I-DE argues that the lack of accumulation in the present case could imply a double imputation to two entities of the same facts, which moreover belong to the same business group, specifically the Iberdrola group of which IBERDROLA is the parent company.  
If both files are not accumulated, I-DE states that it would prevent clarifying the degree of responsibility of each of them, since the facts would be analyzed separately and without assessing the alleged simultaneous action, in terms of responsibility, of the two entities against which both procedures are directed. In this way, there could be a double imputation of the same facts to both entities without assessing whether it is shared or whether the separate sanctioning reproach directed against both should not be subject to reduction as a consequence of that alleged concurrence of responsibility. This limits, in the terms established in the constitutional jurisprudence that is reproduced below, the right to defense of I-DE, as it cannot analyze the concurrent circumstances in the case in a unified manner as a consequence of the fragmentation caused by the opening of two differentiated procedures.  

I-DE understands that the prerequisites established in Article 57 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP) that justify the accumulation of the procedures, as well as the individualization of the relevance of its application to the present case, are met:  
A) Existence of "intimate connection" or "substantial identity."

LEGAL GROUNDS  

I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  

II  
Preliminary Issues  
I-DE is a large company of the Iberdrola Group dedicated to the distribution of electric energy and, for this purpose, processes personal data as the controller of a very high number of individuals, as it states that it processes data from 21 million clients.  
Therefore, in accordance with the provisions of Article 4.1 of the GDPR, there is a processing of personal data, since I-DE carries out, among other processes, the collection, storage, consultation, use, deletion, etc., of personal data of individuals, such as: name, surname, ID number, postal address, telephone number, email address, banking data, data related to electricity supply and consumption, bank account, etc.  
Additionally, IBERDROLA S.A. provides various services to I-DE and other companies in the Group, including “IT infrastructure support and maintenance” and “Application development,” acting consequently as the data processor for I-DE.  

In the case at hand, the security breach suffered has affected personal data processed by I-DE in its capacity as the data controller, determining the purposes and means regarding those processes, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines “personal data security breaches” (hereinafter security breach) as “all breaches of security that lead to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or to the unauthorized communication or access to such data.”  
In this case, there is a personal data security breach under the circumstances mentioned above, categorized as a confidentiality breach, due to unauthorized access by a third party to personal data processed by I-DE.  

III  
On the Request for Joinder and Suspension of the Deadline for Submitting Allegations  
Regarding the request for the joinder of this file and EXP202305587 made by I-DE, it should be noted that Article 57 of the LPACAP establishes:  

“The administrative body that initiates or processes a procedure, regardless of the form of its initiation, may, ex officio or at the request of a party, order its joinder to others that have substantial identity or intimate connection, provided that it is the same body that must process and resolve the procedure.  
No appeal shall proceed against the joinder agreement.”  
(the underlining is ours)  

Therefore, it is a possibility that the Administration has, not being obliged to proceed with the joinder if requested. However, this does not prevent the subsequent motivation of the reasons why it has been deemed appropriate to process both sanctioning procedures separately.  
Thus, although the two sanctioning files, one directed against I-DE and the other against IBERDROLA, S.A., stem from the same security incident (the attack on the GEA application), it has produced two different and differentiated personal data breaches, as reflected in the Background Facts of this proposal, especially in Background Fact Eight, where the information gathered during the preliminary investigation phase carried out by this AEPD is noted.  
Thus, on one hand, the attack was initiated through a web application of I-DE, exploiting a vulnerability that allowed access to I-DE's database and affected the confidentiality of 1,350,000 clients of I-DE.
22/88  
Therefore, the present sanctioning procedure is directed exclusively at I-DE as the responsible party for the processing of personal data of its clients and as a consequence of an existing vulnerability in one of its web applications.  
On the other hand, the cyberattack not only affected personal data of I-DE, but also, by accessing I-DE's database, which was hosted on a system where databases of other companies in the same group coexist, it was possible to surpass the logical separation and access the databases of two other companies, IBERCLI and CURENERGÍA, affecting the confidentiality of personal data of clients of these two latter companies. These different databases of different companies are hosted or operated on a system maintained and supported by IBERDROLA, S.A., which, consequently, is responsible for the processing of all of them, that is, of I-DE, IBERCLI, and CURENERGÍA.  
This fact has led to the initiation of a sanctioning procedure against IBERDROLA, S.A., but for its responsibility as the processor for IBERCLI and CURENERGÍA and exclusively for the personal data breach that has only affected the personal data of clients of these two marketing companies, and solely considering the responsibility that IBERDROLA, S.A. may have regarding the configuration of the databases it manages concerning these two affected companies.  
In this regard, the impact on personal data of clients hosted in databases not belonging to I-DE cannot be part of this sanctioning procedure directed exclusively at examining the conduct of I-DE, since it is not responsible for the personal data of affected clients belonging to other companies, nor for the possible failure to adopt appropriate measures for their protection or for the absolute separation between them. Therefore, the management of the databases carried out by IBERDROLA, S.A. regarding those third companies must be analyzed independently, without I-DE being held accountable for possible breaches of data protection regulations that those third companies may have incurred.  
This was stated by I-DE in its response to the request made by this AEPD, submitted on August 1, 2022, in which, when asked for information about the data affected by the breach relating to clients of IBERCLI and CURENERGÍA, it responded that “I-DE does not have access to the data of individuals who may have been affected by the security breach and who are not clients of the aforementioned entity as their supply point is not assigned to the network managed by I-DE. This means that I-DE does not have the capacity to know any type of information related to those who, being clients of IBERCLI or CURENERGÍA, are not clients of this distributor.”  
Therefore, since the sanctioning procedures are directed at different subjects (two different companies), with the personal data of clients of different companies being affected, and I-DE having nothing to do with the data of external clients, and given that the processing relates to vulnerabilities or breaches regarding different systems (one a web application, the other a database), etc., this AEPD has not considered it appropriate to accumulate the two files, but rather to process the two sanctioning procedures separately, as the responsibility is clearly separated.

SIXTH. – ON THE VIOLATION OF THE PRINCIPLE OF PROPORTIONALITY  
I-DE alleges that the sanctions imposed violate the principle of proportionality, as the AEPD, in determining the amount of the sanctions, has resorted to completely generic criteria.  
Thus, regarding the alleged negligence in its actions, I-DE indicates that it has demonstrated that the events that occurred took place at a specific moment and were resolved very quickly, so the measures adopted in response to the incident mitigated its effects. This immediate resolution of the incident shows that they did indeed have planned actions in the event of a possible attack on their systems.  
In contrast, it should be noted that the appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an adequate level of security against the risks that the processing of personal data may pose to the rights and freedoms of individuals cannot be merely reactive measures, that is, to immediately resolve a personal data breach. Thus, Article 32 of the GDPR not only indicates that adequate security must be ensured but also that such measures must include the ability to guarantee the confidentiality, integrity, availability, and permanent resilience of the processing systems and services (letter b of Article 32.1 GDPR). Therefore, it is not enough to have measures to react as quickly as possible when confidentiality has been breached; there must also be adequate preventive measures in place to prevent such a breach. This is because measures aimed at safeguarding the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal data, that is, preventive measures aimed at avoiding any breach of these, are equally or more important.  
Therefore, it cannot be accepted that the measures implemented by I-DE were adequate because they allowed for the resolution of the incident afterward, as this only demonstrates the existence of corrective measures. However, what those reactive measures allowed was the cessation of the attack once it had occurred and the restoration of the service; that is, regarding the protection of personal data, it prevented a greater impact, and this has already been taken into account as a mitigating factor in the present sanctioning procedure, but in no way can they remedy the loss of confidentiality of the affected personal data, as this had already materialized.  
That is to say, the confidentiality of personal data is primarily guaranteed by preventive measures. In this regard, it has already been indicated in the response to Allegation Four of this Legal Basis the vulnerability contained in the GEA application, which was exploited by the cybercriminal for their attack and which was also perfectly identifiable in security assessments. In relation to this last point, it should not be forgotten that Article 32.2 GDPR stipulates that security measures must also include a process of regular verification, evaluation, and assessment of the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
Therefore, all of this merely reflects a lack of diligence on the part of I-DE in ensuring adequate security against the risks of the data processing it carries out. In this sense, it should not be forgotten that GEA is a web application, that is, it allows access from the internet to a database where personal data of millions of customers are stored, which constitutes large-scale processing, requiring adequate security measures for that web environment and specifically aimed at ensuring that there is no unauthorized access to such personal data.

However, for "bis in idem" to be applicable, a triple identity must exist among the compared terms: objective (same facts), subjective (against the same subjects), and causal (for the same grounds or reason for punishment):  
a) The subjective identity implies that the affected subject must be the same, regardless of the nature or judicial or administrative authority judging the case and irrespective of who the accuser or specific body that has resolved the matter is, or whether it is judged alone or in conjunction with other affected parties.  
b) The factual identity implies that the judged facts must be the same, and excludes cases of real concurrence of infractions where there are not the same unlawful act but several.  
c) The identity of grounds or causal identity implies that the sanctioning measures cannot coincide if they respond to the same nature, that is, if they share the same teleological foundation, which occurs between penal and administrative sanctions, but not between punitive and merely coercive measures.”  
Taking into account the aforementioned, in the sanctioning procedure, the principle of non bis in idem has not been violated, since, although broadly understood, the facts are detected as a consequence of a personal data breach, the infringement of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR is specified in a clear loss of confidentiality that affected certain clients, while the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR is reduced to the deficiency of the security measures (only security) detected, which are present independently of the personal data breach. In fact, if these security measures that I-DE had implemented had been detected by the AEPD without the loss of confidentiality occurring, it would have only been sanctioned under Article 32 of the GDPR.  
As we have indicated, Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR sanctions a loss of confidentiality and availability, while Article 32 of the GDPR addresses the deficiency of the security measures implemented by the data controller. We add that deficient security measures violate the GDPR, regardless of whether or not a personal data breach has occurred.  
Article 32 of the GDPR is violated regardless of whether or not a personal data breach occurs. That is to say, it is infringed by not having appropriate measures to ensure adequate security in the processing of data, without it being necessary or essential for a personal data security breach to occur that, in its case, may affect the confidentiality of the data.

VI  
Integrity and Confidentiality  
Article 5.1.f) "Principles relating to processing" of the GDPR states:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
(...)  
f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, by applying appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).”  
The principle of integrity and confidentiality of data requires a guarantee of security in the application of technical or organizational measures that prevent the alteration of personal data, its loss, unauthorized or unlawful processing or access. The existence of this fundamental right is not possible if confidentiality, integrity, and availability are not guaranteed.  
Thus, the integrity and confidentiality of personal data are considered essential to prevent data subjects from suffering negative effects. Therefore, they must be processed in a manner that ensures adequate integrity and confidentiality of personal data, especially to prevent unauthorized access, processing, or use of such data.  
Ultimately, it is the data controller who has the obligation to integrate the necessary guarantees in the processing, with the aim of, under the principle of proactive responsibility, complying with and being able to demonstrate compliance, while respecting the fundamental right to data protection.  
In this regard, it should be noted that the confidentiality of personal data is regulated in Article 5 of the GDPR and is, therefore, one of the principles relating to processing. The principles relating to processing are, on one hand, the starting point and the closing clause of the legal framework for data protection, constituting true guiding rules of the system with significant expansive force; on the other hand, having a high level of specificity, they are mandatory norms that can be infringed.  
Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR establishes a clear obligation of compliance consisting of preventing unauthorized or unlawful processing by implementing appropriate measures of all kinds to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the data.  
Consequently, data controllers must be in a position to guarantee the confidentiality of personal data to prevent a third party from accessing data that does not belong to them, as it is precisely their responsibility to process personal data in accordance with the GDPR and LOPDGDD. For this reason, the diligence exercised by them is fundamental to avoid such unauthorized access.

Article 32 does not establish static security measures; it is for the controller to determine which security measures are necessary to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal data.
