Regarding the first five, the AEPD confirms that they are technical and necessary cookies and, therefore, there is no infringement concerning them. However, it considers that there are two other activity cookies by default that are not technical, a qualification with which we do not agree:  
A) Regarding the cookie "_tracking__consent": The initiating resolution indicates that it is a behavioral advertising cookie, stating that "Shopify configures this cookie to store a user's preferences, provide a personalized shopping experience, and suggest related items." However, this is not true; as the name of the cookie indicates, it is aimed at storing user preferences related to privacy, more specifically, their choice in the cookie plugin. Therefore, this cookie is technical and necessary, as without it, it would not be possible to save the user's selection regarding the use of cookies.  
In the Cookie Policy, it is explained as follows: "Stores the user's tracking consent preferences." This information can be verified on the website that informs about the cookies available in Shopify: https://www.shopify.com/legal/cookies  
B) Regarding the cookie "Popup-cookie": The AEPD indicates that it has not been able to identify the purpose of the cookie. As its name indicates, it serves to ensure that the cookie plugin has been activated, that is, that it has appeared to the user when entering the website. The privacy policy states the purpose: "Indicates whether the user has seen the cookie notice."  
The initiation agreement alleges that after selecting the option <<Reject all>>, "it is observed that the website continues to use the same cookies detected at the beginning of the session," and this is because the cookies used at the beginning of the session are only the technical or necessary cookies. That is, if cookies are rejected, the technical cookies will continue to be used because they are necessary, although the other preference or advertising cookies will not be activated, which will only function if this type of cookie is expressly accepted.  
In that case, when the <<Accept>> option is clicked, it is observed that the website begins to use new cookies, apart from those initially detected at the beginning of the session, which correspond to the optional cookies.  
Consequently, only cookies with technical and necessary purposes are used when the user enters the website and has not given their express consent for other types of cookies, in accordance with the provisions of Article 22.2 of the LSSI and in Opinion 4/2012 of the GT29.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence.  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 43.1 of the LSSI and the provisions of Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
The fourth additional provision "Procedure in relation to the competencies attributed to the Spanish Agency for Data Protection by other laws" establishes that: "The provisions in Title VIII and its development regulations will apply to the procedures that the Spanish Agency for Data Protection must process in the exercise of the competencies attributed to it by other laws."  
II  
Response to the allegations presented.  
First: In section “fourth” of its written allegations, the entity being claimed argues that the cookie "_tracking__consent" is incorrectly classified as a targeting or behavioral advertising cookie because, they claim, this cookie
16/26

Well, let us begin by stating that the name of the cookie "_tracking_consent" can be quite descriptive as it suggests that it is related to the management or storage of consent for user tracking on the website. If the cookie "popup-cookie" is used solely to manage the behavior of the pop-up and improve the user experience (for example, to avoid showing the same pop-up repeatedly in the same session or in subsequent visits), it could be considered a technical cookie as it does not track the user beyond what is strictly necessary to provide the basic functionality of the site, such as remembering if the user has already closed a pop-up and not reopening it.

IV.  
Graduation of the Sanction  
This Infraction is classified as “minor” in Article 38.4 g) of the aforementioned Law, which considers as such: “Using storage and data retrieval devices when the information has not been provided or the consent of the service recipient has not been obtained in the terms required by Article 22.2.”, and may be sanctioned with a fine of up to €30,000, in accordance with Article 39 of the aforementioned LSSI.  
The LSSI, in its Article 40, establishes the “graduation of the amount of the sanctions”, taking into account the following criteria: a) The existence of intent. b) The period of time during which the infraction has been committed. c) The recurrence of infractions of the same nature, when declared by a firm resolution. d) The nature and amount of the damages caused. e) The benefits obtained from the infraction. f) The volume of billing affected by the committed infraction. g) Adherence to a code of conduct or an applicable advertising self-regulation system regarding the committed infraction, which complies with the provisions of Article 18 or in the eighth final provision and has been favorably reported by the competent authority or authorities.  
Based on the evidence obtained, it is considered appropriate to graduate the sanction to be imposed according to the following aggravating criteria established by Article 40 of the LSSI:  
- The existence of intent (section a). This expression must be interpreted as equivalent to the degree of culpability according to the Judgment of the National Court of 12/11/07 in Case No. 351/2006, as the responsibility of the entity claimed lies in the absence of an adequate system to the LSSI on its website.
