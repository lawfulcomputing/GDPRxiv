BACKGROUND

From the actions taken by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on September 27, 2022. The complaint is directed against SANITAS, S.A. DE SEGUROS, with NIF A28037042 (hereinafter, the respondent or SANITAS).  
The reason for the complaint is the contracting in her name and without her consent of a health insurance policy in which SANITAS and BBVA SEGUROS, S.A. de Seguros y Reaseguros (BBVA Seguros) are co-insurers. The registration of the contract in the name of the complainant resulted in the collection of the insurance premium through debits from the bank account held by her at Banco Bilbao Vizcaya Argentaria, S.A. (BBVA) from March to May 2022, both inclusive.  
She provides with her complaint a copy of the complaint letter she submitted to BBVA, to which she did not receive a response. The document is signed by the complainant on July 19, 2022, and bears a stamp with the legend “Banco Bilbao Vizcaya Argentaria, BBVA” “XXXX” “***LOCATION”. In it, she states that she is a client of BBVA; that in February 2022, she requested information from that banking entity about the health insurance it markets without having entered into any contract, and that in March 2022, a receipt for “health insurance” was charged to her account. She also indicates that, after what happened, she returned the receipt, recovering the amount deducted from her account, and informed her personal manager at BBVA to cancel the policy since she had not given consent for that contracting. She states that, despite this, the situation repeated itself in the following months and that since the premium owed had not been refunded in May, she requested her BBVA manager to provide her with a copy of the policy related to the health insurance, to which the entity ignored her request.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the complaint was forwarded to BBVA for it to analyze and inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
The transfer, which was notified electronically in accordance with the provisions of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was accepted on November 15, 2022, as evidenced by the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  
BBVA did not respond to the information request.  

THIRD: On December 27, 2022, in accordance with Article 65 of the LOPDGDD, the complaint filed by the complainant was admitted for processing.  
The admission for processing was notified to the complainant by postal mail, which was received on January 17, 2023, as evidenced by the documentation in the file.  

FOURTH: The General Subdirectorate of Data Inspection proceeded to carry out preliminary investigative actions to clarify the facts in question by virtue of the functions assigned to control authorities by Article 57.1 and the powers granted by Article 58.1, both of the Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and in accordance with the provisions of Title VII, Chapter I, Section Two, of the LOPDGDD.

II  
Applicable legal norms  
The GDPR regulates in its Article 5 the principles governing the processing of personal data and provides in paragraph 1:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
a) processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject (<<lawfulness, fairness, and transparency>>.

III  
Preliminary Issues  
Article 4 of the GDPR, paragraph 1, defines “personal data” as “any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘the data subject’); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, particularly by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.”  
Paragraph 2 of Article 4 of the GDPR understands “processing” as “any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval or consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.”  
The figure of the “data controller” or “controller” is defined in Article 4.7 of the GDPR as “the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its appointment may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.” (The underlining is ours)  
Article 26 of the GDPR provides that when two or more controllers jointly determine the purposes and means of processing, they shall be considered joint controllers. The provision requires joint controllers to mutually establish their “respective responsibilities in complying with the obligations imposed by the GDPR” and to determine the “functions” of the joint controllers and their relationships with the data subjects.  
On the other hand, the GDPR defines the figure of the “processor” or “processor” as “the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that processes personal data on behalf of the data controller” (Article 4.8)

1. Individualization of the responsibility of the joint controllers due to their participation in the processing.  
Without prejudice to the considerations on joint responsibility set out in the previous Foundation, it is necessary to emphasize that the participation of the joint controllers in the processing does not have to be necessarily identical or at the same level. In this sense, the Guidelines 7/2020 of the EDPB and the CJEU have repeatedly stated. The Guidelines 7/2020 (paragraph 58) state on the matter:  
“The existence of joint responsibility does not necessarily imply an identical responsibility of the different participants involved in the processing of personal data. Quite the contrary: the CJEU has clarified that the parties may participate in different phases of the processing and to different degrees. Therefore, the level of responsibility of each must be assessed based on all relevant circumstances of the specific case.” (The underlining is ours)  
The recent CJEU ruling of 05/12/2023, Case C-683/2021 states:  
“(42) However, the existence of joint responsibility does not necessarily translate into an equivalent responsibility of the various agents involved in the processing of personal data. On the contrary, these agents may be involved in different stages of that processing and to different degrees, so that the level of responsibility of each of them must be assessed taking into account all relevant circumstances of the specific case (judgment of June 5, 2018, Wirtschaftsakademie Schleswig-Holstein, C-210/16, EU:C:2018:388, paragraph 43). II
Termination of the procedure
Article 85 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter LPACAP), under the heading "Termination in sanctioning procedures," provides as follows:

"1. Once a sanctioning procedure has been initiated, if the offender acknowledges their responsibility, the procedure may be resolved with the imposition of the appropriate sanction.
2. When the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature or when a pecuniary sanction and another non-pecuniary sanction can be imposed but the impropriety of the latter has been justified, voluntary payment by the presumed responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except regarding the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for damages caused by the commission of the infringement.
3. In both cases, when the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature, the competent authority to resolve the procedure will apply reductions of at least 20% on the amount of the proposed sanction, which may be cumulative. The aforementioned reductions must be specified in the notification of initiation of the procedure, and their effectiveness will be conditioned upon the withdrawal or waiver of any action or appeal in the administrative route against the sanction. The percentage of reduction provided in this section may be increased by regulation."
