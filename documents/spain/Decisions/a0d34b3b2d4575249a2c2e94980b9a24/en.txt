LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
According to the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Preliminary Issues  
It should be noted that, according to Article 4.1 of the GDPR, "personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("the data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one whose identity can be determined, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific elements of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that person.  
Article 4.2 of the GDPR defines processing as "any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction."  
Images of a natural person are personal data, and their capture by a system of cameras or video cameras constitutes processing of personal data that is subject to data protection regulations, unless it is an exempt processing, such as domestic processing.  
Section 7 of the aforementioned Article 4 of the GDPR defines the "data controller" or "controller" as: the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; if the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.  
As has been established by the response to the transfer action carried out, the respondent is responsible for the surveillance system installed in its establishment and subject to this complaint.  
Therefore, we are dealing with a processing of personal data for which the respondent is responsible and which must comply with the requirements established by data protection regulations in this regard.  
III  
Data Protection Regulations
5/21  
In the present case, and as specified in the facts, the claim concerns the installation of a video surveillance system that, according to the claimant, may not be complying with the provisions of data protection regulations.  
In this regard, we must begin by indicating that Article 6.1 of the GDPR establishes the circumstances that allow for the lawful processing of personal data. More specifically, regarding processing for video surveillance purposes, Article 22 of the LOPDGDD - Processing for Video Surveillance Purposes - establishes that natural or legal persons, public or private, may carry out the processing of images through camera or video camera systems with the aim of preserving the safety of individuals and property, as well as their facilities.  
On the other hand, it should be noted that the processing of personal data is subject to the principles indicated in Article 5 of the GDPR and, specifically, in the case at hand, to what is stated in paragraph 1 c) of that provision, which provides as follows:  
Personal data shall be (…) “adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’).”  
This means that, in a specific processing operation, only the relevant personal data that is strictly necessary to fulfill the purpose for which it is processed may be processed. The processing must be appropriate and proportional to the purpose it is directed at. Likewise, relevance in the processing of data must occur both at the time of data collection and in the subsequent processing carried out on them. In accordance with the above, the processing of excessive data must be restricted or these must be deleted.  
The application of the data minimization principle in the context of video surveillance entails that images of public roads cannot be captured, since the processing of images in public places can only be carried out by the Security Forces and Corps. However, on some occasions, for the protection of private spaces where cameras have been installed on facades or indoors, it may be necessary to record a portion of the public road to ensure the security purpose. That is, cameras and video cameras installed for security purposes may not obtain images of the public road unless it is essential for that purpose or it is impossible to avoid it due to their location. In such an extraordinary case, the cameras may only capture the minimum portion necessary to preserve the safety of individuals and property, as well as their facilities.  
In this sense, paragraph 2 of Article 22 provides as follows:  
2. Images of the public road may only be captured to the extent that it is essential for the purpose mentioned in the previous paragraph. However, it will be possible to capture images of the public road to a greater extent when necessary to ensure the safety of property or...

On the other hand, Article 12.1 of the GDPR states that anyone who carries out the processing of personal data, such as capturing images through a video surveillance system, must provide the interested parties with the information indicated in Articles 13 and 14 of the GDPR.  
In order for the information duty provided for in Article 12 of the GDPR to be fulfilled in a concise and understandable manner for the affected party, the aforementioned Article 22 of the LOPDGDD provides for a "layered information" system in relation to video surveillance. In this sense, the first layer must refer, at a minimum, to the existence of the processing (video surveillance), the identity of the data controller, the possibility of exercising the rights provided for in Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR, and where to obtain more information about the processing of personal data.  
The information in the second layer must be available in a location easily accessible to the affected party, whether it be an information sheet at a reception, cashier, etc., placed in a visible public space or on a web address, and must refer to the remaining elements of Article 13 of the GDPR. It is not necessary to specify the precise location of the video surveillance equipment.  
This information duty will be considered fulfilled by placing an informative device in a sufficiently visible location, and at a minimum, at the entrances to the monitored areas, whether they are interior or exterior. If the monitored space has several entrances, such a sign indicating the video surveillance area must be placed at each of them.

V  
Breach of Obligation. Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR  
As noted, in the case examined, it is evident from the documents in the file that the data controller has installed a video surveillance system with, apparently, the purpose provided in Article 22 of the LOPDGDD. In this regard, it should be noted that, although the complaint indicated—and this was justified by one of the images provided by the complainant—the existence of a monitor displaying the images captured by the system visible to the entire establishment, it has been established in the response to the transfer that this monitor has been replaced by an application installed on the mobile phone of the data controller.  
Having established the above, according to the information in the file, the area captured by the cameras includes the tables of the establishment where customers...

They are located during their visit to the establishment—evidenced even in one of the images provided by the defendant to one of these clients—besides the area of the cash register, the gambling machines or slot machines, the tobacco vending machine, and the access door to the premises.  
Although it is observed that the camera focusing on the door of the establishment captures a portion of the public road, this capture is deemed proportionate as it only includes the essential part necessary for achieving the security purpose of protecting persons, property, and facilities, in accordance with Article 22.2 of the LOPDGDD.  
Similarly, it can be understood that the rest of the installed cameras are proportionate in compliance with the security purpose for which the video surveillance system is installed, except in the case of those that capture the tables of the establishment.  
By its very nature, the area of the dining tables is a location where those affected by the processing may remain for a long time, and in a situation where their fundamental right to the protection of personal data may be affected, as well as other rights and freedoms, such as their privacy or the free development of their personality, especially since these places are usually visited during leisure time.  
Furthermore, there is no special reason justifying the surveillance of areas such as the tables concerning the security of the establishment, as there might be for confined spaces such as the entrance/exit or the cash register.  
In this regard, the permanent capture of the area of the room and the tables when customers are present would not comply with the requirements of the principle of minimization of processing personal data "adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed."  
In this respect, it is important to note that, according to the information provided by the responsible party, even without further accreditation in this regard, the captured images are retained for a period of one week. Thus, the data of all customers who have visited the establishment are stored during that period.  
In this case, the impact on the fundamental right to the protection of personal data, understood as the power of disposition and control held by the data subject, is significantly greater than the alleged interest that the defendant seeks to achieve, which is the preservation of the security of property and persons in the area of the tables of the establishment.  
Likewise, from the information and documentation in the file, the defendant does not demonstrate the necessity of carrying out this processing of personal data based on the purpose concerning the area of the customers' tables in light of the circumstances described above. Therefore, and without prejudice to what results from the instruction, it is understood that the described facts could constitute a violation of the aforementioned Article 5.1 c).

Proposal for sanction  
In this case, based on the facts presented and without prejudice to the outcome of the instruction of the procedure, it is considered that the sanction to be imposed is an administrative fine.  
In order to determine the administrative fine to be imposed, the provisions of Articles 83.1 and 83.2 of the GDPR, previously indicated, must be observed.  
Furthermore, it is considered appropriate to adjust the sanction to be imposed according to the following criteria established in Article 83.2 of the GDPR:  
- the nature of the infringement for having a video surveillance system that does not have an adequate information sign and is directed towards the dining area, processing data of identifiable or potentially identifiable individuals, which may also affect other rights and freedoms of customers or passersby (Article 83.2 a) GDPR).  
- the intentionality or negligence of the infringement (Article 83.2 b) GDPR), given that, through negligence, the current applicable regulations have not been taken into account in order to properly inform the data subjects about their rights and the means available for their exercise.  
For all these reasons, in accordance with what has been stated, and without prejudice to the outcome of the instruction of the procedure, the initial assessment of the amount of the fine is 1,000 euros for the infringement of Article 13 of the GDPR.  

X  
Adoption of measures  
If the infringement is confirmed, it may be agreed to impose on the responsible party the adoption of appropriate measures to adjust their actions to the regulations mentioned in this act, in accordance with the provisions of the aforementioned Article 58.2 d) of the GDPR, according to which each supervisory authority may “order the controller or processor to ensure that the processing operations comply with the provisions of this Regulation, where appropriate, in a specific manner and within a specified period…”. The imposition of this measure is compatible with the sanction consisting of an administrative fine, as provided in Article 83.2 of the GDPR.  
In this case, it may be ordered that within one month from the resolution that concludes this sanctioning procedure becomes final and enforceable, the necessary measures be adopted:  
- To prevent the cameras that make up the video surveillance system from continuously focusing on the area of tables in the establishment.  
- To install an information sign about the existence of a video-surveilled area that complies with the requirements established in the data protection regulations.  
It is warned that failure to comply with the possible order to adopt measures imposed by this agency in the sanctioning resolution may be considered an administrative infringement in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, classified as an infringement in Articles 83.5 and 83.6, which may lead to the initiation of further administrative sanctioning proceedings.

II  
Termination of the Procedure  
Article 85 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), under the heading "Termination in Sanctioning Procedures" provides as follows:  
“1. Once a sanctioning procedure has been initiated, if the offender acknowledges their responsibility, the procedure may be resolved with the imposition of the appropriate sanction.  
2. When the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature or when a pecuniary sanction can be imposed along with a non-pecuniary sanction but the latter has been justified as inappropriate, voluntary payment by the alleged responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except regarding the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for the damages caused by the commission of the infringement.  
3. In both cases, when the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature, the competent body to resolve the procedure shall apply reductions of at least 20% on the amount of the proposed sanction, which shall be cumulative. The aforementioned reductions must be specified in the notification of initiation of the procedure, and their effectiveness will be conditioned upon the withdrawal or renunciation of any action or administrative appeal against the sanction. The percentage of reduction provided in this section may be increased by regulation.”
