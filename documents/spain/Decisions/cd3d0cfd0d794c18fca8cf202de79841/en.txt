RESOLUTION OF RECOURSE FOR RECONSIDERATION  
Examined the recourse for reconsideration filed by VODAFONE ESPAÑA, S.A.U. (hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated May 8, 2024, and based on the following:  
FACTS  
FIRST: On May 8, 2024, a resolution was issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202301160, by virtue of which a fine of 200,000 euros (two hundred thousand euros) was imposed on VODAFONE ESPAÑA, S.A.U. with NIF A80907397, for an infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR.  
This resolution, which was notified to the appellant on May 14, 2024, was issued following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), and subsidiarily under Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), regarding the processing of sanctioning procedures.  
SECOND: As proven facts of the aforementioned sanctioning procedure, PS/00425/2023, the following was recorded:  
“FIRST. - It is recorded in the file that on December 1, 2022, a third party requested a duplicate of the SIM card through access to the private area of the complainant at Lowi, a brand of Vodafone, and the third party provided a different address from that registered with Vodafone for the billing of the complainant to facilitate the delivery of the duplicate SIM card order.  
SECOND. - It is recorded in the file that the duplicate SIM was delivered on December 2, 2022, at the postal address provided by the third party.  
THIRD. - Vodafone acknowledges in its letter dated March 16, 2023, that it has been able to verify that it does not have evidence to confirm compliance with the security policy, nor evidence to confirm compliance with the complete security policy by calling 121 for the activation of the SIM card, stating that it does not have the recording of the mentioned telephone procedure since the call was not recorded.  
FOURTH. - It is recorded in the file that previously, on November 30, 2022, there were two attempts to change the email address via telephone, supposedly to access the complainant's private customer area; this request was not carried out as it did not pass the Security Policy.”  
THIRD: The appellant submitted on June 14, 2024, to this Spanish Agency for Data Protection, a recourse for reconsideration, in which it expresses its disagreement with the resolution, reiterating the arguments presented in its allegations, fundamentally based on the following grounds:  
1) Vodafone has acted diligently in that it has implemented the necessary processes to correctly identify its customers, with the adoption of technical and organizational measures being an obligation that is not absolute.  
2) For the delivery of a duplicate SIM, Vodafone has contracted an exclusive delivery service with the transport company collaborating with this entity.  
3) The issuance of a duplicate SIM does not involve access to banking information, passwords, email addresses, etc., of Vodafone's customers, but rather to the mobile and internet services that the customers of my represented entity may have contracted.  
4) There can be no appreciation of culpability in the infringements attributed to Vodafone and, consequently, no sanction can be imposed on it.  
5) Subsidiarily, in the event that the Agency understands that there has been an infringement and a sanction must be imposed on Vodafone, it should be graduated under the principle of proportionality, as well as taking into account the aggravating and mitigating circumstances mentioned.

The principle of culpability governs in sanctioning Administrative Law (Article 28 of Law 40/2015, on the Legal Regime of the Public Sector, LRJSP), whereby the subjective or culpability element is an indispensable condition for the emergence of sanctioning responsibility. Article 28 of the LRJSP, "Responsibility," states:  
“1. In light of this provision, sanctioning responsibility can be demanded on the basis of intent or negligence, with mere non-observance of the duty of care being sufficient in the latter case.  
The Constitutional Court, among others, in its ruling STC 76/1999, has declared that administrative sanctions share the same nature as criminal ones, being one of the manifestations of the State's ius puniendi, and that, as a requirement derived from the principles of legal certainty and criminal legality enshrined in Articles 9.3 and 25.1 of the CE, their existence is essential for imposing them.  
Regarding the culpability of legal entities, it is pertinent to cite STC 246/1991, December 19, 1991 (F.J. 2), according to which, concerning legal entities, the subjective element of culpability must necessarily be applied differently than it is for individuals, and it adds that “This different construction of the imputability of the authorship of the infraction to the legal entity arises from the very nature of the legal fiction to which these subjects respond. They lack the volitional element in the strict sense, but not the capacity to infringe the norms to which they are subject. The capacity for infringement and, therefore, direct reproachability that derives from the legal good protected by the norm that is infringed and the necessity for that protection to be genuinely effective [...]”.  
The decision to archive a sanctioning file may be based on the absence of the element of culpability when the responsible party for the unlawful conduct has acted with all the diligence that the circumstances of the case require.

IV  
Classification and Qualification of the Infringement  
The infringement is classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR, which considers as such:  
“5. The infringements of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to a maximum of 4% of the total annual global turnover of the preceding financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) The basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9.”  
The LOPDGD, for the purposes of the prescription of the infringement, qualifies in its Article 72.1 as a very serious infringement, with a prescription period of three years in this case, “b) The processing of personal data without any of the conditions for lawful processing established in Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 being met.”

Article 83.2.d) GDPR: “The degree of responsibility of the controller or processor, taking into account the technical or organizational measures they have applied under Articles 25 and 32;.”  

The respondent has limited itself to stating that the third party it contracted with exceeded the company's security policy without providing any evidence that it obtained from the person involved in the contracting any document that proved they were indeed the owner of the personal data they provided as their own or that it implemented any mechanism that allowed verifying the authenticity of the provided identity data.  

On the other hand, the principle of proactivity implies transferring to the data controller the obligation not only to comply with the regulations but also to be able to demonstrate their compliance. Among the mechanisms that the GDPR contemplates to achieve this are those provided for in Article 25, “data protection by design,” according to which the controller must apply “both at the time of determining the means of processing and at the time of the processing itself” technical and organizational measures that ensure effective application of the principles of the GDPR in the processing they carry out.  

Article 83.2.f) of the GDPR refers to “the degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority in order to remedy the infringement and mitigate the possible adverse effects of the infringement;.”  

The response of the respondent to the information request from the Inspection Subdirectorate did not fulfill those purposes, so it cannot be classified under that mitigating circumstance.  

Regarding the application of Article 76.2.c) of the LOPDGDD, in connection with Article 83.2.k), the non-existence of benefits obtained, it should be noted that such circumstance can only operate as an aggravating factor and in no case as a mitigating circumstance.  

Article 83.2.k) of the GDPR refers to “any other aggravating or mitigating factor applicable to the circumstances of the case, such as the financial benefits obtained or the losses avoided, directly or indirectly, through the infringement.” And Article 76.2.c) of the LOPDGDD states that “2. In accordance with the provisions of Article 83.2.k) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following may also be taken into account: [..] c) The benefits obtained as a result of the commission of the infringement.” Both provisions mention as a factor that can be taken into account in the graduation of the sanction the “benefits” obtained, but not the “absence” of these, which is what Vodafone claims.  

Furthermore, according to Article 83.1 of the GDPR, the imposition of monetary sanctions is governed by the following principles: they must be individualized for each particular case, be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. The admission that the absence of benefits operates as a mitigating factor is contrary to the spirit of Article 83.1 of the GDPR and to the principles governing the determination of the amount of the monetary sanction. If, as a result of the commission of an infringement of the GDPR, it is qualified as a mitigating factor that no benefits have existed, the dissuasive purpose fulfilled through the sanction is partially nullified. Accepting Vodafone's thesis in a case like the one at hand would imply introducing an artificial reduction in the sanction that truly needs to be imposed; the one that results from considering the circumstances of Article 83.2 GDPR that must indeed be evaluated.

The respondent argues that the previous sanctioning procedures relate to Vodafone customers who are not customers of the Lowi brand and whose duplicate SIMs were processed through channels different from the one analyzed in this case, and therefore should not be considered as aggravating factors.  
It is established that Lowi is a brand under the same legal denomination as Vodafone; they are the same company and therefore all previous infractions by the responsible party or the data processor must be taken into account in order to assess the unlawfulness of the analyzed conduct or the culpability of the infringing party.  
- The evident link between the business activity of the respondent and the processing of personal data of customers or third parties (Article 83.2.k of the GDPR in relation to Article 76.2.b of the LOPDGDD).  
The Judgment of the National Court of October 17, 2007 (rec. 63/2006), regarding entities whose activity involves continuous processing of customer data, indicates that “...the Supreme Court has understood that there is negligence whenever a legal duty of care is disregarded, that is, when the infringer does not behave with the required diligence. In assessing the degree of diligence, the professionalism or lack thereof of the subject must be especially weighed, and there is no doubt that, in the case now examined, when the activity of the appellant involves constant and abundant handling of personal data, it must be insisted upon the rigor and exquisite care to comply with the legal provisions in this regard.”  
It is appropriate to graduate the sanction to be imposed on the respondent and set it at the amount of €200,000 for the alleged infringement of Article 6.1) typified in Article 83.5.a) of the aforementioned GDPR.”  
In summary, in the analyzed case, the diligence employed by the respondent to identify the person who requested the duplicate SIM card is called into question, given that there were two attempts to change the email address via telephone and the impersonator did not pass the security policy, and despite this, Vodafone did not take the necessary measures to prevent these events from occurring.  
Therefore, a lack of diligence in Vodafone's actions can be appreciated, without being able to assess a lack of culpability.  
The AEPD does not deviate from any reasoning nor does it attribute all responsibility to Vodafone. It reproaches it for the responsibility that corresponds to it as the controller of that specific processing “Issuance of a duplicate SIM card,” since according to the definition in Article 4.7 of the GDPR, it is the one who determines the purpose and means of the processing carried out.  
Vodafone, in its capacity as an operator, must be more demanding when providing a duplicate SIM card. Identity verifications must be thorough to avoid identity theft problems.  
At the moment the duplicate SIM card is obtained, access to the second factor of authentication is also achieved, and therefore, from that moment, banking fraud could materialize.
