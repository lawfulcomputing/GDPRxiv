RESOLUTION ON THE ARCHIVING OF PROCEEDINGS  
From the proceedings carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following:  
FACTS  
FIRST: On July 4, 2023, a complaint was filed with entry registration number REGAGE23e00044555411 before the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against CAIXABANK, S.A. with NIF A08663619 (hereinafter, the complained party or CaixaBank). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The complainant states that they have received at their postal address (the residence in which they live and of which they are the owner) several communications from the complained party addressed to third parties, for which they have submitted numerous complaints to the complained party (the last one dated April 26, 2023) informing them of the existence of an incorrect data (address) associated with their person and requesting its deletion.  
In this regard, they state that they received a response from the complained party on July 4, 2023, apologizing for the incident, adding that the disputed postal address "is associated in our database to another client without having been able to obtain their consent to unlink the data. We regret any inconvenience this may cause you." Along with the complaint, a transcript of the received response is provided.  
SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the complaint was forwarded to the complained party for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
The forwarding, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was acknowledged on September 8, 2023, as evidenced by the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  
On October 25, 2023, a response was received by this Agency indicating that: <<CaixaBank, in its capacity as a financial entity, must observe certain information obligations to its clients, through the sending of communications via the notification channels that the clients and the Entity have contractually agreed upon.  
As can be seen, CaixaBank cannot simply proceed to delete data that has been contractually informed, and, subsequently, if applicable, has not been updated by its owner.  
This Entity has verified that the complainant's address is also associated with another CaixaBank client.  
Contact has been made with this client to verify if they have changed their address, and they have confirmed by phone that they no longer live there.  
CaixaBank has urged them to visit an office to request the effective modification of the data in the Entity's systems, without success up to the date of this writing.  
This Entity has given the pertinent instructions for the postal notifications of this client at that address to be canceled.  
Therefore, taking into account the above, this Entity cannot carry out the effective modification of that address in its systems until this client visits the office to sign the document certifying the modification of the data related to their postal address.  
Consequently, although this Entity has given the pertinent instructions for the cancellation of postal notifications of this client at that address, as long as the data is not effectively modified in the Entity's systems, it is possible that some postal communication may be sent to this client at the informed address, in order to comply with the established contractual relationship, as well as to fulfill, among other aspects, certain transparency obligations required by the Bank of Spain>>.  
THIRD: On October 4, 2023, in accordance with Article 65 of the LOPDGDD, the complaint filed was admitted for processing.

III  
Statements from the responding party  
In this case, the complainant states that they have received incorrect correspondence at their home from the responding party addressed to third parties.  
In response to the transfer and information actions carried out, a letter has been received at this Agency from the responding party, which, for the purposes here, CaixaBank, cannot, without further ado, proceed to delete data that has been contractually reported, and that, subsequently, if applicable, has not been updated by its owner.  
On the other hand, it indicates that it has verified that the complainant's address is also associated with another CaixaBank client.  
In relation to the above, they state that they have contacted the third party to verify if they have changed their address, and they have confirmed by phone that they no longer live there.  
In this regard, CaixaBank has urged the third party to visit one of its offices to request the effective modification of the data in its systems, without having obtained any results.  
Finally, CaixaBank has given the relevant instructions to unsubscribe the third party's postal notifications at the complainant's address. However, as long as the data is not effectively modified in CaixaBank's systems, it is possible that they may send some postal communication to the third party at the address that is recorded, in order to comply with the established contractual relationship, as well as to fulfill, among other aspects, certain transparency obligations required by the Bank of Spain.  

IV  
Conclusion  
In this case, once the reasons presented by the responding party, which are in the file, have been analyzed, it has been found that there are no rational indications of the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, and consequently, the opening of a sanctioning procedure is not warranted.  
It should be noted that, in Administrative Sanctioning Law, due to its specialty, the principles inspiring criminal law apply with some nuances but without exceptions, resulting in the clear full effect of the principle of presumption of innocence.  
In this sense, the Constitutional Court, in Judgment 76/1990, considers that the right to the presumption of innocence entails "that the sanction is based on acts or incriminating evidence of the reproached conduct; that the burden of proof lies with the accuser, without anyone being obliged to prove their own innocence; and that any insufficiency in the results of the evidence presented, freely assessed by the sanctioning body, must result in an acquittal." This principle is expressly included for sanctioning administrative procedures in Article 53.2.b) of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations, which recognizes the interested party's right "To the presumption of no existence of administrative liability until proven otherwise."  
In short, the application of the principle of presumption of innocence prevents attributing an administrative infringement when no evidence or indications have been obtained that derive from the existence of an infringement.  
In this case, from the actions carried out and the documentation in the file, the existence of an infringing action by the responding party within the jurisdiction of the AEPD is not inferred, so the complaint should be archived.  
All of this is without prejudice to any subsequent actions that this Agency may carry out, applying the investigative and corrective powers it holds.  
Thus, as it has not been possible to attribute responsibility for the processing as indicated, by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, IT IS AGREED:  
FIRST: TO ARCHIVE the present actions.
