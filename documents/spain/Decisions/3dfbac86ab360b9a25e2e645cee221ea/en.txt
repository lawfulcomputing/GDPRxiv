IV  
Unfulfilled Obligation: Violation of Article 5.1.e) of the GDPR  
1. The party being claimed against in this sanctioning procedure is attributed a violation of Article 5.1.e) of the GDPR, relating to the principle of "data retention limitation," under which the data controller shall retain personal data in a manner that allows the identification of the data subjects for the purposes of processing for no longer than necessary. The retention of data constitutes a processing operation. In this sense, Article 4.2 of the GDPR defines "processing" as "any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of making available, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure or destruction." (Emphasis added)  
Recital 39 of the GDPR refers to the principle of data retention limitation when it states:  
“[...] Personal data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purposes for which they are processed. This requires, in particular, ensuring that their retention period is limited to a strict minimum. Personal data should only be processed if the purpose of the processing cannot reasonably be achieved by other means. To ensure that personal data is not retained longer than necessary, the data controller must establish retention or periodic review periods. [...]” (Emphasis added)  
It should be noted that, pursuant to Article 17 of the GDPR, the data controller is obliged to "erase without delay" personal data that is no longer necessary for the purposes for which it was collected.  
Also, according to Article 32 of the LOPDGDD, the erasure of personal data involves its blocking, which consists of the identification and reservation of such data, adopting technical and organizational measures to prevent its processing, including its viewing, "except for making the data available to judges and courts, the Public Prosecutor's Office, or the competent Public Administrations, in particular the data protection authorities, for the enforcement of possible liabilities arising from processing and only for the limitation period of such liabilities. After this period, the data must be destroyed."  
These exceptions are the only purposes for which blocked data may be processed.  
Article 17 of the GDPR, "Right to erasure ('the right to be forgotten')", states:  
“1. The data subject shall have the right to obtain from the data controller the erasure of personal data concerning him or her without undue delay, and the data controller shall be obliged to erase personal data without undue delay when one of the following circumstances applies:

VI  
Sanction. Infringement of Article 5.1.e) GDPR  
The corrective powers granted to this supervisory authority by the GDPR are detailed in Article 58.2 of the GDPR, paragraphs a) to j). The provision mentions among them the authority to impose an administrative fine in accordance with Article 83 of the GDPR (Article 58.2.i).  
Regarding the infringement of Article 5.1.e) of the GDPR for which SISTEMAS TUBULARES is held responsible, this resolution agrees to impose an administrative fine as a corrective measure (Article 58.2.i of the GDPR).  
Article 83 of the GDPR, “General conditions for imposing administrative fines,” states in its paragraph 1 that the supervisory authority shall ensure that the imposition of fines for the infringements of this Regulation indicated in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6, complies, in each individual case, with the principles of “effectiveness, proportionality, and deterrent effect.”  
The principle of proportionality requires that there be a correlation between the infringement and the sanction, prohibiting unnecessary or excessive measures, so that it must be suitable to achieve the purposes that justify it. In order to ensure the proportionality of the sanction that may be imposed, it has been taken into consideration that, according to the available information, the turnover of the party being claimed against amounted to €3,616.51 in the 2021 financial year.  
For its part, Article 83.2 of the GDPR provides a list of criteria or factors that must be taken into account to determine the amount of the fine, with the aim of adjusting the fine to the severity of the infringement committed.  
In the decision to open this sanctioning file, it was noted that, in accordance with the criteria of Articles 83.1 and 83.2 of the GDPR, an administrative fine of €1,000 (one thousand euros) could be imposed on SISTEMAS TUBULARES for the infringement of Article 5.1.e) of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5.a) of the GDPR.  
In this resolution, it is agreed to impose on SISTEMAS TUBULARES, as responsible for the infringement of Article 5.1.e) of the GDPR, an infringement classified under Article 83.5.a) of the GDPR, an administrative fine of €1,000 (one thousand euros).

Furthermore, by virtue of the principle of proactive responsibility established in Article 5.2 of the GDPR, the data controller has the obligation to respect the principles of Article 5.1 of the GDPR, particularly the principle of confidentiality, as well as to be able to demonstrate compliance.  
As stated by the Legal Office of the AEPD (among others, in reports 17/2019 and 64/2020), the GDPR has represented a paradigm shift in addressing the regulation of the right to personal data protection, which is now based on the principle of "proactive responsibility." In this regard, the explanatory memorandum of the LOPDGDD states that "the greatest novelty presented by Regulation (EU) 2016/679 is the evolution from a model fundamentally based on compliance control to one that rests on the principle of active responsibility, which requires a prior assessment by the controller or the processor of the risks that the processing of personal data may generate, in order to adopt the appropriate measures based on that assessment."  
The "essence" of "proactive responsibility" - Opinion 3/2010 of the Article 29 Working Party (GT29), WP 173, issued during the validity of Directive 95/46/EC but whose reflections are applicable today - is the obligation of the data controller to implement measures that, under normal circumstances, ensure compliance with data protection standards in the context of processing operations and to have available documents that demonstrate to data subjects and supervisory authorities what measures have been adopted to achieve compliance with data protection standards.  
In consideration of the above, it is deemed established that the respondent incurred in an infringement of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR, principle of confidentiality, manifested in having disseminated to third parties personal data of the complainant whose confidentiality it was obliged to guarantee.

The corrective powers granted to this supervisory authority by the GDPR are detailed in Article 58.2 of the GDPR, paragraphs a) to j). The provision mentions among them the power to impose an administrative fine in accordance with Article 83 of the GDPR (Article 58.2.i).  
Regarding the infringement of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR for which SISTEMAS TUBULARES is held responsible, this resolution agrees to impose as a corrective measure an administrative fine (Article 58.2.i of the GDPR).  
Article 83 of the GDPR, "General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines," states in its paragraph 1 that the supervisory authority shall ensure that the imposition of fines for the infringements of this Regulation indicated in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 complies, in each individual case, with the principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and deterrence.  
The principle of proportionality requires that there be a correlation between the infringement and the sanction, prohibiting unnecessary or excessive measures, so that it must be suitable to achieve the purposes that justify it. In order to ensure the proportionality of the sanction that may be imposed, it has been taken into consideration that, according to the available information, the turnover of the respondent amounted to €3,616.51 in the 2021 financial year.  
On the other hand, Article 83.2 of the GDPR provides a list of criteria or factors that must be taken into account to determine the amount of the fine, with the purpose of adjusting the fine to the severity of the infringement committed.
