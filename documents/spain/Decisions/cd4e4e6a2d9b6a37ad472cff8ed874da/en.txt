II  
Security of Processing  
Article 32 of the GDPR stipulates the following:  
"1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly verifying, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a consequence of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data only processes such data on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law."

IV  
Conclusion  
The claimant became aware of the processing of her data by the defendant entity when the Tax Agency requested her to declare capital gains derived from gambling in the "Personal Income Tax" for the fiscal year 2018. Among these gains were those from the gambling operator BWIN, which is a brand of ELECTRAWORKS.  
ELECTRAWORKS has reported that the registration of the user account created with the claimant's data was completed on May 13, 2018, and the date of the last action with this account took place on May 19, 2018.  
ELECTRAWORKS indicates that it does not have documentation provided during the account registration because it was not necessary according to the Resolution of July 12, 2012, of the General Directorate of Gaming Regulation, which approves the provision that develops Articles 26 and 27 of Royal Decree 1613/2011, of November 14, regarding the identification of participants in games and the control of subjective prohibitions on participation.  
In the "Resolution of July 12, 2012, in its Fifth section.2, it is established that "In cases where the applicant can provide the national identity document number (DNI) or the foreign identification number (NIE), the gaming operator may proceed to verify the name, surname, and date of birth of the applicant through the Identity Verification System of Participants of the National Gaming Commission referred to in the following section."

And, in its Seventh section.3, it is established that “Once the consultation is carried out, if the data matches that of the Identity Verification System, the operator will obtain a positive response and the data will be considered verified.”  
On March 30, 2019, certain resolutions regarding gaming activities provided for in Law 13/2011, of May 27, on the regulation of gaming were amended. At that time, the new wording of section Fifth.2 of this Resolution of July 12, 2012, from the DGOJ, which has been indicated in conclusion 3, came into effect, establishing the following: “In cases where the applicant can provide the national identity document number (DNI) or the foreign identification number (NIE) in their registration process, operators must:  
a) Verify the identity data that appears in their user records, either through the Identity Verification System of the participants of the General Directorate of Gaming Regulation, or through other systems or alternative verification means.  
b) Documentarily verify the data provided by the participant.”  
On the other hand, it must be taken into account that the respondent began processing the data of the claimant on May 13, 2018, with the last deposit occurring 6 days later, on May 19.  
Even in the event that the processing was carried out without considering the requirements established by the applicable data protection regulations at the time of obtaining the data, the possible infringement would be time-barred because the statute of limitations for the most serious infringements is 3 years, in accordance with the provisions of the LOPDGDD; therefore, in this case, the infringement would have expired before the claim was received.  
In accordance with what has been indicated, by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, IT IS AGREED:

SECOND: TO NOTIFY this resolution to ELECTRAWORKS - CEUTA, S.A. and to the claimant.
