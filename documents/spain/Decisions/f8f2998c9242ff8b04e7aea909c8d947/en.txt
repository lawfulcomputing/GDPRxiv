LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the functions conferred to each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f) and h) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR) and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve these investigative proceedings.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Credit Information Systems  
In order to process personal data - in this case, to communicate the claimant's personal data to a third party for the purpose of sharing it with all entities associated with the common credit information system, after fulfilling certain requirements - it is necessary to first determine whether what is referred to as a "lawful basis" under the GDPR exists that legitimizes the data controller to carry out the processing operations necessary to fulfill the purposes in question.  
As clearly stated in Recital 40 of the GDPR: "For processing to be lawful, personal data must be processed with the consent of the data subject or on another legitimate basis established in law, whether in this Regulation or under another Union or Member State law to which this Regulation relates, including the necessity of complying with a legal obligation applicable to the data controller or the necessity of performing a contract to which the data subject is a party or for the purpose of taking steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract."  
Regarding this issue, the main rule is Article 6 of the GDPR, which under the heading "Lawfulness of processing" refers in its paragraph 1 to the cases in which the processing of personal data may be considered lawful in general:  
"1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular where the data subject is a child."

The provisions in letter f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to the processing carried out by public authorities in the exercise of their functions.”  
Article 20 of the LOPDGDD, which regulates credit information systems, states in its first section the following:  
"1. Unless proven otherwise, the processing of personal data related to the non-fulfillment of monetary, financial, or credit obligations by common credit information systems shall be presumed lawful when the following requirements are met:  
a) That the data has been provided by the creditor or by someone acting on their behalf or interest.  
b) That the data refers to certain, overdue, and enforceable debts, the existence or amount of which has not been subject to administrative or judicial claim by the debtor or through a binding alternative dispute resolution procedure between the parties.  
c) That the creditor has informed the affected party in the contract or at the time of requesting payment about the possibility of inclusion in such systems, indicating those in which they participate.  
The entity maintaining the credit information system with data related to the non-fulfillment of monetary, financial, or credit obligations must notify the affected party of the inclusion of such data and inform them about the possibility of exercising the rights established in Articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 within thirty days following the notification of the debt to the system, with the data being blocked during that period.  
d) That the data is only maintained in the system while the non-fulfillment persists, with a maximum limit of five years from the due date of the monetary, financial, or credit obligation.  
e) That the data related to a specific debtor can only be consulted when the person consulting the system has a contractual relationship with the affected party that involves the payment of a monetary amount or has requested the conclusion of a contract that implies financing, deferred payment, or periodic billing, as occurs, among other cases, in those provided for in the legislation on consumer credit contracts and real estate credit contracts.  
When the right to limit the processing of the data has been exercised before the system by contesting its accuracy in accordance with the provisions of Article 18.1.a) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the system will inform those who may consult it according to the previous paragraph about the mere existence of such circumstance, without providing the specific data regarding which the right has been exercised, while the request of the affected party is being resolved.  
f) That, in the event that the request for the conclusion of the contract is denied, or it is not concluded as a result of the consultation made, the person who consulted the system shall inform the affected party of the result of that consultation."

In the case of personal data processing operations, the data protection regulations provided for in the GDPR will fully apply to the entity responsible for transmitting these personal data to the file manager, regardless of whether it must also comply with other possible specific regulations that govern the requirements and conditions for inclusion in this file.  
In this case, the claiming party reports the improper communication of their personal data to the BADEXCUG file without indicating which installments are referred to, stating that their deletion request made on 12-4-23 has not been addressed, and that they have been included in the BADEXCUG file without the claimed party sending them a prior notice of inclusion in the file.  
Once the documentation provided by them has been examined, it is deduced that such deletion request referred to previous debts that have not been specified, but that, in any case, do not correspond to those for which the lack of prior notice of inclusion in the file is claimed, and that refer to the alleged non-payment of the two installments due on 19-2-23 and 19-3-23 of the loan agreed upon between the claiming and claimed parties, which were included in the file on 23-4-24, according to the response from Experian provided by the claimant, that is, on a date subsequent to the exercise of the right to deletion, which therefore does not affect the entry requested by the claimed party.  
It is therefore necessary to determine whether the claimed party failed to comply with the requirements set forth in Article 20 of the LOPDGDD, by having requested on 23-4-23 the inclusion in the BADEXCUG file—which has the character of a “credit information system”—of the installments of the financing loan subscribed with the claimant, which were due on 19/02/23 and 19/03/23.  
Regarding this issue, from the analysis of the documentation provided by both parties, it is deduced that the claimed party has not failed to comply with the requirements referred to by the claimant, for the following reasons:  
- First, the claimant indicates that the debt is inaccurate, but does not specify or detail the reason or to what extent, making a mere generic assertion in their claim. This assertion is not accompanied by any evidentiary basis, whereas the claimed party has proven that such debt was enforceable and was valid at the time it was registered on 23/04/23, providing the returned receipts and two.

However, on the other hand, the respondent also proves that the claimant stopped paying 26 out of 47 installments corresponding to the same vehicle financing loan, and states that they demanded payment for the 24 unpaid installments beforehand, and previously warned the claimant of the inclusion of the same in the file. Specifically, they provide 3 demands in which they do inform the claimant about the recording of their data in these files. All three are from 2022, prior to the events. The last of them is from October 2022.  
It should be noted that what Article 20.1.c) of the LOPDGDD requires from the creditor of the debt is the following: “c) That the creditor has informed the affected party in the contract or at the time of demanding payment about the possibility of inclusion in such systems, indicating those in which they participate”:  
Now, the regulation does not specify the manner and number of occasions in which such a demand for “possibility of inclusion in the file” must be made. Specifically, it does not expressly state that in cases where, as in the present case, multiple installments corresponding to the same contractual relationship are unpaid, it is mandatory for the creditor to send a separate demand for inclusion in the file for each unpaid installment.  
In the absence of specific provisions, we must interpret the obligation set forth in the aforementioned provision according to its literal wording (literal interpretation), and in accordance with the spirit and purpose of the law (teleological or functional interpretation of the norms), in the sense provided in Article 3.1 of the Civil Code, which establishes that “1. The norms shall be interpreted according to the proper meaning of their words, in relation to the context, historical and legislative background, and the social reality of the time in which they are to be applied, fundamentally considering the spirit and purpose of those.”  
Thus, from the literal wording of the obligation, which determines that the creditor must be informed about the “possibility” of being included in the file at the time of demanding payment; and the functional interpretation of the provision, whose clear purpose is for the debtor to know that by failing to comply with any installment of this installment loan, they will be included in the file, it is deduced that having already received several demands for inclusion in the file after the non-payment of other previous installments of the same loan, the claimant was correctly informed of this possibility, fulfilling the creditor entity's obligations under Article 20.1.c) of the LOPDGDD.  
Based on this, we can conclude that this requirement has not been breached, given that it has been evidenced that the claimant had already defaulted on other installments previously and had been informed by the respondent that they would be included in the file, thus knowing that in the event of a new default, they would be included again in said file.  
Consequently, from all of the above, it is deduced that there is no violation of the applicable regulations by the respondent entity that justifies the initiation of a sanctioning procedure against it.
