II  
Right of access to personal data  
The rights of access, rectification, deletion, opposition, the right to limit processing, and the right to data portability of individuals regarding personal data protection are regulated in Articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, regarding the protection of natural persons in relation to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data (hereinafter, GDPR), as well as in Articles 13 to 18 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD).  
Regarding the right of individuals to obtain the personal data “concerning them” from the data controller, Articles 12 and 15 of the GDPR, as well as Articles 12 and 13 of the LOPDGDD, are particularly applicable, all of which must be interpreted within the framework established in Recitals 59 to 64 of the GDPR.  
Thus, the main content of the right and its scope or content is established in Article 15 of the GDPR, which states the following:  
“1. The data subject shall have the right to obtain from the controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning them is being processed and, where that is the case, access to the personal data and the following information:  
a) the purposes of the processing;  
b) the categories of personal data concerned;  
c) the recipients or categories of recipients to whom the personal data have been or will be disclosed, in particular recipients in third countries or international organizations;  
d) where possible, the envisaged period for which the personal data will be stored or, if not possible, the criteria used to determine that period;  
e) the existence of the right to request from the controller rectification or erasure of personal data or restriction of processing of personal data concerning the data subject, or to object to such processing;  
f) the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority;

In this regard, the provisions set forth by the European Data Protection Board (hereinafter, EDPB) in Guideline number 1/2022, adopted on March 28, 2023, which approves the Application Guide for Article 15 of the GDPR, regarding the Right of Access of the Data Subject, should be applied. Point 4.2 specifies the scope of personal data that can be requested by data subjects when it concerns another person, warning against the danger of making a "too restrictive" interpretation to automatically deny access.

This Guideline establishes the following (the underlining is ours):  
“4.2 The personal data referred to by the right of access  
In accordance with Article 15, paragraph 1 of the GDPR, ‘the data subject shall have the right to obtain from the controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning him or her is being processed and, where that is the case, access to the personal data and the following information’ (the underlining is ours).  

Several elements can be derived from paragraph 1 of Article 15 of the GDPR. The paragraph expressly refers to ‘the personal data concerning him or her’ (4.2.1), which ‘is being processed’ (4.2.2) by the data controller:  
1.1.1. ‘personal data concerning him or her’  
The right of access can only be exercised with respect to personal data related to the data subject requesting access or, where applicable, by an authorized person or representative (see section 3.4). There are also situations where the data does not have a link to the person exercising the right of access, but rather to another person. However, the data subject only has the right to personal data relating to themselves, excluding data that exclusively concerns another person.  

However, the classification of data as personal data relating to the data subject does not depend on whether such personal data also relates to another person. Therefore, it is possible for personal data to relate to more than one person at the same time. This does not automatically mean that access is granted to personal data also related to another person, as the data controller must comply with Article 15, paragraph 4, of the GDPR.  

The words ‘personal data concerning him or her’ should not be interpreted in a ‘too restrictive’ manner by data controllers, as has already been stated by the Article 29 Working Party regarding the right to data portability.”  

Consequently, according to the EDPB, the highest European authority in this matter, the right of access under Article 15 of the GDPR goes beyond requesting one’s own personal data—limited to the data subject—and extends to those that concern them. A data subject such as the complainant can exercise the right of access in relation to all personal data held by the data controller concerning their identity, including those that have been collected in an instance of impersonation.

IV  
Classification and Qualification of the Violation  
In accordance with the evidence available at this moment of resolution of the warning procedure, it is considered that the respondent has violated the right of access provided in Article 15 of the GDPR by denying the information requested by the claimant on July 20, 2022, regarding the account fraudulently opened in the name of the claimant on the BWIN website, for which the respondent is responsible.  
The violation of Article 15 of the GDPR is classified in Article 83.5.b) of the GDPR, which states:  
“Violations of the following provisions shall be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to a maximum of 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
b) the rights of data subjects under Articles 12 to 22;”  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations for violations, the violation attributed prescribes after 3 years, in accordance with Article 72.1.k) of the LOPDGDD, which provides that:  
“1. In accordance with what is established in Article 83.5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following violations shall be considered very serious and shall prescribe after three years, which involve a substantial breach of the articles mentioned therein and, in particular, the following: (…)  
k) The obstruction or hindrance or repeated failure to attend to the exercise of the rights established in Articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679….”
