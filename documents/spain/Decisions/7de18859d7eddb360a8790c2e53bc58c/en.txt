RESOLUTION OF RECOURSE FOR RECONSIDERATION  
Examined the recourse for reconsideration filed by VACACIONES EDREAMS, S.L.  
(hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Director of the  
Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated July 26, 2023, and based on the following  
FACTS  
FIRST: On July 26, 2023, a resolution was issued by the Director of the  
Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202306257, by virtue of which  
VACACIONES EDREAMS, S.L., was ordered, due to an infringement of  
Article 44 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR, to adapt the  
data processing activity carried out through the Google Analytics service to the  
provisions of Articles 44 and following of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the  
European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, particularly by  
ceasing the international transfer of data until it is proven that the Google  
Analytics service complies with the aforementioned provisions of the Regulation.  
This resolution, which was notified to the appellant on July 31, 2023, was issued  
following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure, in  
accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on  
Personal Data Protection and guarantee of digital rights (LOPDGDD), and  
supplementarily under Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common  
Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP),  
regarding the processing of sanctioning procedures.

Google states that Google Analytics by default uses the HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS) protocol, which instructs browsers that support HTTP over SSL (HTTPS) to use that encryption protocol for all communications between end users, websites, and Google Analytics servers.  

Google states that it protects service-to-service communications at the application layer through a mutual authentication and encryption system. Google states that after a handshake protocol between the client and server is completed and the client and server negotiate the necessary shared cryptographic secrets to encrypt and authenticate network traffic, TLS ensures the integrity of RPC (Remote Procedure Call) traffic, enforcing integrity and optional encryption, using the negotiated shared secrets. Google supports multiple protocols to ensure integrity, for example, AES-GMAC (Advanced Encryption Standard) with 128-bit keys. Whenever traffic leaves a physical boundary controlled by or on behalf of Google, for example, in transit across a WAN (Wide Area Network) between data centers, all protocols are automatically updated to provide encryption as well as integrity guarantees.

Google states that it encrypts Google Analytics data stored at rest in its data centers using the Advanced Encryption Standard. Each data center is protected by six layers of physical security designed to prevent unauthorized access.  
The "at rest" encryption in this section refers to the encryption used to protect user data stored on a disk (including solid-state drives) or on backup media. All user data is encrypted at the storage level, generally using the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES256). Data is typically encrypted at multiple levels in Google's production storage stack in the data centers, even at the hardware level, without requiring any action from Google customers.  
Google states that it uses common cryptographic libraries that incorporate Google's validated FIPS 140-2 module to implement encryption consistently across all products. The consistent use of common libraries means that only a small team of cryptographers needs to implement and maintain this tightly controlled and reviewed code.  

Google states that it builds dedicated servers for its data centers and maintains a leading security team in the industry to ensure that Google's data centers are among the most secure in the world. Google's production data centers are protected by multiple layers of security to prevent any unauthorized access to the data.  
Google states that it limits access to personal advertising and analytics data to Google personnel who need it to perform their job.  
Google states that customers using Google Analytics can enable IP anonymization to instruct Google to anonymize all IP addresses immediately after they are collected. If enabled, the full IP address is never written to disk, as all anonymization occurs in memory almost instantaneously after receiving the request.  
Google states that to the extent that Google Analytics data for measurement transferred by customers is personal data, it would need to be considered as pseudonymous. The Google Analytics Terms of Service mandate that no data be transferred to Google that Google could use or recognize as personally identifiable information (PII).  
Google has obtained ISO 27001 certification and will allow customers or a third-party auditor designated by a customer to conduct audits (including inspections) to verify compliance with Google's obligations.

“FIFTH.- Lack of subjective element and culpability.”  
As EDREAMS has already stated in its Written Submissions, the requirement of culpability of the subject who engages in the unlawful conduct is necessary for the imposition of an administrative sanction.  
In response to this allegation, as already mentioned in the response to the Fourth allegation of the appeal for reconsideration, EDREAMS acknowledges point 10 of the “Terms of Data Processing of Google Ads,” whereby the data controller has agreed that Google may store and process personal data of the customer (i.e., personal data of the claimant and any user visiting the website in question) in any country where Google or any of its “sub-processors” maintain facilities, including the U.S., regardless of whether the Standard Contractual Clauses have been modified from those in effect at the time of the events subject to the claim, attributing the status of exporter to Google Ireland. Thus, the actions of Google LLC are confined to what is stipulated and, on behalf of EDREAMS, carrying out the processing of the personal data necessary for the proper provision of the service, which determines the administrative responsibility of the data controller.  
For all the above reasons, this allegation is dismissed.  
III  
Conclusion  
Consequently, in the present appeal for reconsideration, the appellant has not provided new facts or legal arguments that would allow for a reconsideration of the validity of the contested resolution.
