RESOLUTION OF RECONSIDERATION APPEAL  
Examined the reconsideration appeal filed by A.A.A. (*hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated January 27, 2025, and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On January 27, 2025, a resolution was issued by the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202310123, by virtue of which the following corrective measure was imposed on A.A.A. for the appropriate legal effects:  
TO ISSUE A WARNING to A.A.A., with NIF ***NIF.1, for an infringement of Article 5.1 c) GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 GDPR.  
This resolution, which was notified to the appellant on February 7, 2025, was issued following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (LOPDGDD), and supplementary to Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), regarding the processing of sanctioning procedures.  

SECOND: As proven facts of the aforementioned sanctioning procedure, PA/00063/2023, the following was recorded:  
First. The facts arise from the complaint dated 11/07/23, through which the installation of cameras that are "pointed towards their private property" is reported.  
Documentary evidence is provided that proves the presence of device(s) in the windows of the party being complained against (Annex I).  
Second. It is established that A.A.A. with NIF ***NIF.1 is the main responsible party for the video surveillance system, who has not proven the legality of the system.  
Third. It is established that a video surveillance device is installed that is pointed towards the private property of the complainant without justified cause.  
Fourth. It is established that notification was made to the address ***DIRECCION.1.  

THIRD: The appellant submitted on February 23, 2025, to this Spanish Agency for Data Protection, a reconsideration appeal, fundamentally based on the following aspects:  
First. The initiation Agreement of the File occurred through a Resolution dated July 24, 2024, electronically signed on the same date.  
The warning Resolution that is now being appealed is indicated as dated, in the heading, January 27, 2025, electronically signed on February 7, 2025.  
From the simple computation of the time elapsed between the initiation Agreement and the Resolution that concluded its processing, it follows that the time limit set in the regulation has been exceeded, and the consequence is as established, the declaration of expiration of the File, which must be declared when resolving this Appeal.  
Second. In the complaint referred to in the Resolution presented by B.B.B., ownership of the property towards which the camera is directed is attributed, a matter that this party has denied from the outset, because precisely that space is their property, and is subject to an action for possession and recovery, which would imply the existence of a prejudicial civil matter.  
Third. Furthermore, the conduct reported by B.B.B., whose details are recorded in the File, is perpetrated by him, as he has installed a surveillance camera in a cabin of his property, directed towards my property, which has captured images of myself, my daughter C.C.C., and her minor children D.D.D. and E.E.E., in addition to my husband F.F.F. (QUEP), which has been presented in various judicial proceedings, as evidenced by documents 3 to 7, which are submitted.  
For that conduct, I FORMALLY FILE A COMPLAINT, remaining at the Agency's disposal for the submission of the data and supporting evidence.  
For the above reasons, I REQUEST: That this document and the attached documents be accepted, and that the Reconsideration Appeal be admitted and processed, declaring the expiration of the File or granting it and revoking the appealed resolution, rendering the warning ineffective, as it is thus a matter of Justice that I respectfully request in Santander for Madrid on February 20, 2025.  

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
The Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve this appeal, in accordance with the provisions of Article 123 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP) and Article 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD).

II  
Before delving into the substance of the matter, it is important to recall that a Recurso de Reposición (Appeal for Reconsideration) is a legal instrument to request the acting Public Administration to review, if applicable, an act of the same that is not considered to be in accordance with the law or erroneous.  
It is not the channel to simultaneously file a Complaint regarding a hypothetical administrative infringement committed by the opposing party (original Claimant).  
Therefore, the legal requirements for its submission are outlined in the current applicable regulations, specifically in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations, to which we refer literally.  
It is worth recalling the principle of <good faith> in the exercise of actions as stated in Article 7.1 of the Civil Code, as well as an effective appearance of injury to some right within the framework of data protection, after having minimally verified the existence of a violation or hypothetical administrative infringement, avoiding the extrapolation of civil judicial matters <sub iudice> (*still pending adjudication) to this Agency.  
Apart from this, the local Security Forces and Corps (e.g., Local Police) have the authority to go to the scene of the events, verify them, or even make recommendations to the parties, and may, if applicable, issue an Inspection Report for its transfer to this Agency, which will ultimately be responsible for analyzing the facts.  
Having stated the above, it is appropriate to pause on the first allegation made by the Appellant regarding the alleged expiration of the administrative procedure.  
The Initiation Agreement is dated 07/24/24 and was notified in due time and form to the respondent on 08/07/24, as evidenced in the system of this AEPD.  
Article 42, section 1 of Law 39/2015 (October 1) provides:  
“All notifications made on paper must be made available to the interested party at the electronic headquarters of the acting Administration or Agency so that they can voluntarily access the content thereof” (*the bold text belongs to this agency).  
The Resolution of the Presidency of this agency is dated 01/27/25 and was notified on 02/07/25, after being made available at the electronic headquarters on the same day, at which point access occurred and, consequently, the effective notification of the administrative act took place.  
Therefore, the notification was made after the expiration of the six-month period established in the LOPDGDD (LO 3/2018, December 5)—Article 64, section 3.

III  
The claimant in their appeal document expresses their disagreement with the essential content of the resolution of this Agency regarding the <ownership> of the land on which the image capture device(s) is oriented.  
In the case at hand, the installation of a camera system is an undisputed fact, as is the orientation towards an area in conflict between the parties with opposing interests, which would imply a logical reasoning towards the processing of personal data as a <coercive> measure, so the set of indications could undermine the presumption of innocence (recalling evidentiary criteria STS (2nd Chamber) of December 22, rec. No. 10279/2021).  
The appellant in their appeal document refers to “the plot being subject to a possessory protection action,” so as of the date, one cannot speak in strict terms of private property as the matter is sub iudice.  
The expectation of a right in the sense of the claim to be the owner of a plot with exclusive character requires a document(s) that credibly certifies this in law, which is not the case with the documentation presented by the appellant.  
The known facts could therefore potentially constitute an infringement, attributable to the respondent under Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR, as they continue, according to the statements of the appellant themselves, in their current location, given that they consider such land to be private.  
According to the TS (e.g., STS September 28, 2022) “in the case of continued infringements, the date to be considered for determining the applicability of the norm is that on which the last infringing act is declared proven.”  
Therefore, the above would justify that the device in question could be subject to removal or disconnection from its current location, as it is a presumed infringement that continues to occur at the present time.  
Thus, this agency may agree to open a new administrative procedure for the appropriate legal effects, in which the respondent may again present their reasoning or provide the appropriate documentation (including certifying its removal), although this Agency will assess that they have already been extensively warned that the system must comply with current legality (principle of proactive responsibility).  
III  
Conclusion  
Consequently, in the present appeal for reconsideration, the appellant has made the necessary legal arguments that allow for the reconsideration of the validity of the contested resolution, in terms of the untimeliness of the resolution period of the administrative procedure.
