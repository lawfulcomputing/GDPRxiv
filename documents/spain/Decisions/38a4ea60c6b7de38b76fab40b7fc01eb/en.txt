FACTS  
FIRST: On December 4, 2022, a complaint was filed with entry registration number REGAGE22e00055517818 before the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against A.A.A. with NIF ***NIF.1 (hereinafter, the complained party). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The complainant states that they are the owner of a property whose access is via a path that connects to a public access road, being a path over which there is an easement of passage, with a series of properties belonging to the complained party located along this path, which qualify as servient estates in the sections that coincide with said properties. LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
In accordance with the functions conferred upon each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR) and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve these investigative actions.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  
II  
In this case, the complaint dated 04/12/22 is analyzed, through which the following is conveyed:  
“a set of surveillance cameras (at least 6 units) has been installed in several areas around the servitude, focusing on it and on outdoor areas, intimidating and violating current regulations (…)”—folio No. 2 point 7 Complaint document—.  
The facts focus on the data protection framework regarding the alleged infringement of third-party rights with the installed cameras through the “processing of the same” outside the cases permitted by current regulations.  
Article 5.1 c) GDPR provides as follows: Personal data shall be:  
“adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’).  
The party being complained against, in exercising its right to defense in the context of this procedure, responds to this agency in a document dated 17/02/23 denying the illegality of the system, arguing that it has an informative sign indicating it is a <video-surveilled area>, as well as that the images obtained are of a private nature.  
“It is installed in a private space, at the accesses or entrances, facade, and in private property/land, and the few images of public space are essential for the purpose of surveillance intended (vital interest of the affected person or another person, specifically, to ensure the life, safety, and physical integrity of myself and my family (deterrent measure)”—folio No. 2 Defense document dated 17/02/23—.  
It also states “conflicts” of various kinds with the complainant that have resulted, according to its statements, in “damages” and “threats” towards its person, providing copies of Complaints filed with the State Security Forces and Corps.  
After analyzing the arguments of both parties, this agency finds that the main conflict centers on the passage through the servitude between the parcels of their property, which is the real issue between them.  
Both present extensive documentation regarding the purported nature of the path as a servitude (private or public nature), when the reality of the facts is that such issues exceed the jurisdictional framework of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
The Judgment of the National Court of April 1, 2011, appeal 2223/2010, in its Legal Foundation IV, last paragraph states the following:  
“The importance and significance of data protection regulations and the relevance of the constitutional rights at stake advise that they should not be put at the service of personal disputes that must be resolved in different areas that should only be relevant in the domestic sphere and not in a jurisdictional context. The seriousness involved in exercising the sanctioning power advises that the corresponding administrative and jurisdictional mechanisms should only be activated when it is assumed that a true violation of the fundamental right to data protection has occurred. Such a circumstance does not occur in the present case.”  
III  
Regarding the presence of the cameras without delving into “complex” issues from a civil law perspective and given the “special” nature of the Canary servitude as a customary figure, the following should be noted.

It is important to remember that a <servitude> is a type of path or portion of traversable land used by neighboring property owners, which runs through the privately owned lands of each of them, but it is not exclusively owned by any of the owners of the neighboring estates who hold the right of passage, with the purpose of accessing a public road.  
It is not a public road, nor a private road for public use, as the rights of passage are held solely, and in solidarity, by each of the holders of the Servitude.  
The Civil Code does not recognize the "Servitude" as an institution, having received a nature letter in the jurisprudence of the Supreme Court as a customary institution applicable in the Canary Islands [STS 10/07/1985 (RJ 1985, 3967)].  
The presence of image capture devices (personal data) must comply with the current regulations in force on the matter, seeking a balance between the rights that may be affected, but not making disproportionate use of them or with the purpose of continuing the neighborhood dispute, which is what underlies the present claim.  
We are not dealing with a public road; rather, the use of it is limited to the usufructuaries thereof, therefore, the impact from the perspective of data protection is lesser, limited to the few owners who make use of it.  
Among the parties due to the conflict regarding the use and nature of the road, various events have occurred that have led to multiple complaints and the judicialization of the conflict between them (e.g., property damage crimes, threats, etc.).  
The video surveillance cameras have the essential purpose of protecting property, facilities, and individuals, serving as evidence obtained through recording to substantiate behaviors contrary to the legal order.  
The parties do not raise violations within the framework of data protection, as the data of both are widely known due to the various lawsuits between them, but rather civil issues regarding the use of the road in question and its ownership, which they are eager to demonstrate to this Agency through the corresponding property deeds and their analysis, if applicable, by this body.  
Article 22 of the LOPDGDD (LO 3/2018, December 5) states:  
1.  
Natural or legal persons, public or private, may carry out the processing of images through camera or video camera systems for the purpose of preserving the safety of individuals and property, as well as their facilities.  
2.  
Images of public roads may only be captured to the extent that it is essential for the purpose mentioned in the previous section.  
The responding party has argued various behaviors of the claiming party that justify the presence of cameras focused on part of the servitude area, as necessary to prevent behaviors, including criminal ones.  
Furthermore, the responding party certifies having informative sign(s) in the access area to their properties indicating that it is located at C/ Jorge Juan, 6

No complaints have been received from other neighbors in the area who might feel intimidated by the presence of the cameras, nor has it been demonstrated that there has been treatment outside the cases permitted by the current regulations, much less involving minors as stated in the complaint.  
According to the above, after analyzing the case as a whole, the presence of the video surveillance cameras is permitted, adhering to the criteria of this agency, limiting as much as possible to the private area of the properties owned by the claimant, reminding that the images obtained must be made available to the competent authorities in case of capturing the described behaviors, while avoiding at all times, as a recommendation, to conduct surveillance of the access area (entrances/exits) of the claimant's property, if applicable.  
All of this is without prejudice to the fact that the matter may be subject to analysis in the appropriate civil judicial instances, whose criteria do not have to coincide with those argued by this Agency.  
IV  
Therefore, based on what is indicated in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found that proves the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
The parties are recommended to redirect their relationships to the minimum rules of neighborly coexistence, making peaceful use of the land subject to servitude for their corresponding parcels or, if applicable, to resolve their disputes in the various judicial instances, as these are the competent authorities in the matter they present.  
In accordance with the above, by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, IT IS AGREED:
