LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers attributed in Article 50 of LO 7/2021 to data protection authorities and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
Finally, Article 52 "Applicable regime to the procedures processed before the data protection authorities" of Organic Law 7/2021 establishes that:  
"1. In the event that the interested parties consider that the processing of personal data has violated the provisions of this Organic Law or that their request to exercise the rights recognized in Articles 21, 22, and 23 has not been addressed, they shall have the right to file a complaint with the data protection authority.

II  
Applicable legal regime  
Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, which repeals Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation), establishes in its Article 2 a series of personal data processing operations to which it does not apply. Among these operations, subsection d) refers to the processing of personal data "by competent authorities for the purposes of preventing, investigating, detecting, or prosecuting criminal offenses, or for the execution of criminal sanctions, including protection against threats to public security and their prevention."  
For processing of this nature, the current Organic Law 7/2021, of May 26, is applicable, through which Directive (EU) 2016/680 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data by competent authorities for the purposes of preventing, investigating, detecting, or prosecuting criminal offenses or for the execution of criminal sanctions, and on the free movement of such data, is transposed into our legal system.  
In this regard, this organic law aims, according to its first article, "to establish the rules relating to the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data by competent authorities, for the purposes of preventing, detecting, investigating, and prosecuting criminal offenses or for the execution of criminal sanctions, including protection and prevention against threats to public security."  
Furthermore, Article Two, by delimiting its scope of application, establishes that this regulation shall apply "to the total or partially automated processing of personal data, as well as to the non-automated processing of personal data contained in or intended to be included in a file, carried out by competent authorities for the purposes of preventing, detecting, investigating, and prosecuting criminal offenses and for the execution of criminal sanctions, including protection and prevention against threats to public security."

Article 5 of Organic Law 7/2021 contains a series of definitions, in particular, it states the following:  
"For the purposes of this Organic Law 7/2021, the following definitions shall apply:  
a) 'personal data': any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('the data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one whose identity can be determined, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific elements of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person;  
b) 'processing': any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction;  
(...)  
g) 'data controller' or 'controller': the competent authority that alone or jointly with others determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data; where the purposes and means of the processing are determined by Union law or by Spanish legislation, such rules may designate the controller or the criteria for its designation.  
h) 'data processor' or 'processor': the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that processes personal data on behalf of the controller;"

Article 37 "Security of processing" of the LO states:  
“1. The controller and the processor, taking into account the state of the art and the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the levels of risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, especially with regard to the processing of the categories of personal data referred to in Article 13. In particular, they shall apply to the processing of personal data the measures included in the National Security Scheme.  
2. With regard to automated processing, the controller or processor, following a risk assessment, shall implement control measures for the following purposes:  
a) In access control to equipment, deny access to unauthorized persons to the equipment used for processing.  
b) In control of data media, prevent these from being read, copied, modified, or deleted by unauthorized persons.  
c) In storage control, prevent unauthorized personal data from being introduced, or that such data can be inspected, modified, or deleted without authorization.  
d) In user control, prevent automated processing systems from being used by unauthorized persons through data transmission facilities.  
e) In access control to data, ensure that authorized persons to use an automated processing system can only access the personal data for which they have been authorized.  
f) In transmission control, ensure that it is possible to verify and establish to which bodies personal data have been transmitted or may be transmitted, or to whose disposal personal data may be placed through data communication equipment.  
g) In input control, ensure that it can be verified and confirmed, a posteriori, which personal data have been entered into the automated processing systems, at what time, and who entered them.  
h) In transport control, prevent personal data from being read, copied, modified, or deleted without authorization during transfers of personal data or during the transport of data media.  
i) In recovery control, ensure that installed systems can be restored in the event of interruption.  
j) In reliability and integrity control, ensure that the system functions do not present defects, that operational errors are indicated, and that stored personal data is not degraded due to system malfunctions. V  
Conclusions after the allegations to the Proposal for resolution  
As stated, the POLICE were charged in the initiation agreement of the present sanctioning procedure with the violations of Articles 6.1 and 37 of LO 7/2021. These articles refer to the obligation of the controller to apply appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of personal data.  
Regarding the technical measures, the POLICE have provided in their written allegations a report regarding compliance with the National Security Scheme.  
On this particular matter, it should first be clarified that Royal Decree 311/2022, of May 3, which regulates the National Security Scheme, aims at information security by establishing the minimum necessary requirements for adequate protection of information.  
Thus, the first article of said Royal Decree establishes that the aforementioned scheme “is constituted by the basic principles and minimum necessary requirements for adequate protection of the information processed and the services provided by the entities within its scope of application, in order to ensure access, confidentiality, integrity, traceability, authenticity, availability, and preservation of the data, information, and services used by electronic means that they manage in the exercise of their competencies.”  
Therefore, compliance with the National Security Scheme does not fully encompass the obligations imposed by Article 37 of LO 7/2021 on the controllers, as they aim not at information security, but at the protection of the rights and freedoms of individuals. From the very content of Article 37 of LO 7/2021, it is inferred that the controllers are obliged to apply to the processing of personal data the measures included in the National Security Scheme.

It should be added that compliance with the minimum requirements contained in the National Security Framework does not, by itself, guarantee the adequate security required by Article 6.1.f) of LO 7/2021, which goes beyond the mere application of security measures.  
In fact, the second article of the aforementioned Royal Decree makes an express reference to the regulations on personal data protection when the information system processes data of that nature:  
“When an information system processes personal data, the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, which repeals Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation), and in Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights, or, where appropriate, Organic Law 7/2021, of May 26, on the protection of personal data processed for the purposes of prevention, detection, investigation, and prosecution of criminal offenses and the execution of criminal sanctions, the rest of the applicable regulations, as well as the criteria established by the Spanish Data Protection Agency or, within its jurisdiction, by the autonomous data protection authorities, without prejudice to the requirements established in this Royal Decree, shall apply.”  
Well, as can be observed, in the case at hand, regarding the security of personal data, the regulations on data protection are fully applicable, and, in particular, given the nature of the processing, the aforementioned Organic Law 7/2021, of May 26, cannot limit the adoption of measures for the protection of personal data to compliance with the rules relating to the National Security Framework.  
In fact, the first additional provision of the LOPDGDD states that the National Security Framework must include the measures that must be implemented in the event of processing personal data to prevent its loss, alteration, or unauthorized access when an information system processes personal data. This mandate implicitly carries the prevalence that the protection of personal data has as a fundamental right over the National Security Framework. Thus, the National Security Framework is subordinate to the regulations on personal data protection and to the specific instruments for assessing the risk regarding the rights and freedoms of natural persons provided for in the data protection regulations, as also stated, in compliance with such mandate, in Article 3 of Royal Decree 311/2022, by determining that it will be the National Security Framework that must adapt to the criteria for determining the risk in the processing of data established in the data protection regulations, particularly as it concerns LO 7/2021.

In this regard, it should be recalled that, while data controllers must be accountable for the actions of their employees, this cannot be asserted when the employee uses their own means and pursues personal interests that diverge from the purposes established by the data controller. In such cases, the employee will have acted determining ends and means that are unrelated to those determined by the controller.
