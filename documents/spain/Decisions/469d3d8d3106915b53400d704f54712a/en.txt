LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence and procedure  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, by the general rules on administrative procedures in a subsidiary manner."  
II  
Preliminary issues  
The publication of the minutes regarding the collective dismissal process referred to in this procedure, which was carried out by the respondent on the notice board of the works council, is considered an operation of personal data processing, which is subject to the regime provided for in the GDPR, as well as its implementing provisions, in accordance with the provisions of Article 4.1, 2, and 7 of the GDPR, which states the following:  
“Article 4 Definitions

The publication of the minutes carried out on February 4, 2023 (body and annex) on the company committee's notice board, making it accessible and visible to all employees and individuals who could access the location where it was displayed, constitutes a personal data processing operation, in the sense expressed in Article 4.2 of the GDPR, since it involved the disclosure or communication of the personal data of the 43 workers appearing therein to all those who had access to the aforementioned notice board of the company committee.  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 4.7 of the GDPR, it should be noted that the data controller is the person or body that decides on the purposes and means of the processing operation in question (which in this case is publishing the minutes on the notice board).  
According to the provisions of the Guide "Data Protection in Labor Relations" published by the AEPD in May 2021, the person responsible for the processing of the operation of disclosing personal data on company notice boards is considered to be "the one who physically places the information on the notice board."  
There is consistent evidence that the person responsible for this processing operation consisting of publishing the minutes on the company committee's notice board was the president of the Company Committee, against whom the present procedure is directed, since:  
- It is accredited and recognized that the respondent held the triple status of signatory of the minutes, president of the company committee, and member of the monitoring committee provided for in the minutes to respond to incidents arising from the collective dismissal process, so he had a copy of the minutes and its annex, having access to the personal data it contained and to the company committee's notice board.  
- Furthermore, it was the respondent who responded to the information request made by the claimant on February 4, 2023, informing him that: "there is a complete copy of said minutes on the company's personnel notice board in Puerto Naos available for consultation by all workers." This response was sent on the same day as the publication of the minutes and list, implying that the respondent was aware that this publication had been made for general dissemination, implicitly assuming his agreement with this dissemination.  
- Such rational indications that the respondent was the one who physically placed the minutes and its annex on the notice board are confirmed when knowledge of the email dated February 6, 2023, which is attached to this procedure, is obtained. In this email, the respondent sent to the Director of DIMAGAZA's offices, he implicitly acknowledges having posted the minutes and annex on the company committee's notice board "by mistake," and having removed it, literally stating, in response to the company director regarding the publication of the annex, the following: "And I truly regret it because all the documents were stapled and I didn't realize it at that moment. In any case, I have removed them."  
Consequently, the respondent is the presumed responsible party to whom this sanctioning procedure should be directed for the unauthorized disclosure of personal data that resulted from the publication of the aforementioned minutes on the company committee's notice board, in accordance with the provisions of Article 70.1 a) of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the protection of personal data and guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPPDD):

Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR  
Article 5.1.f) "Principles relating to processing" of the GDPR establishes that the data controller must protect the integrity and confidentiality of the personal data being processed, in the following terms:  
“1. Personal data shall be: (…)  
f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, by applying appropriate technical or organizational measures.”  
Specifically, Article 5 of the LOPDGDD develops this duty of confidentiality, stating the following:  
“1. The controllers and processors of personal data, as well as all persons involved in any phase of this, shall be subject to the duty of confidentiality referred to in Article 5.1.f) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.

2. The general obligation indicated in the previous section shall be complementary to the duties of professional secrecy in accordance with the applicable regulations.  
3. The obligations established in the previous sections shall remain in effect even after the relationship of the obligated party with the data controller or processor has ended.”  

According to the provisions of the “Guide on Data Protection in Employment Relationships” by the AEPD from May 2021: “Both duties (confidentiality and professional secrecy) are necessary and constitute a guarantee for the fundamental right to data protection. Secrecy and confidentiality ensure that personal data is only known by the affected individual and by those users of the organization whose profile grants them the competence to use, consult, modify, or include the data in the information systems.”  

The so-called principle of integrity and confidentiality of personal data is violated when what Article 4, Section 12 of the GDPR broadly defines as “personal data security breaches” (hereinafter referred to as personal data breach) occurs. These are defined as “all security violations that result in the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or unauthorized communication or access to such data.”  

In this case, it is evidenced that a personal data breach occurred under the circumstances mentioned above, categorized as a confidentiality breach; as a consequence of having made public 9 personal data of 43 employees of the company who were affected by the collective dismissal process initiated therein, by publishing the minutes and its annex on the company committee's notice board, exposing the personal data contained therein, as acknowledged in the email he sent to the company director on February 6, 2023.  

That the duty to inform workers is essential in the labor field is an unquestionable fact, but like any other right, it is not absolute or unlimited, but must always be exercised within the limits established by the legislator, among which is the protection of personal data, in accordance with the provisions of Article 18 of our Constitution. The protection of both rights (information and personal data protection) is compatible. It is only required that the members who make up these bodies act with the due diligence that corresponds to them by virtue of their position and as responsible for the processing of personal data, which requires that they be properly trained on how the processing should be carried out without violating the current special data protection regulations.  

According to the provisions of the aforementioned Guide on personal data protection in employment relationships, the access to personal data known by the members of the Works Council (as representatives of the workers) allows them to carry out certain processing operations that must be governed by the

The entity responsible for the processing will be the one that materially places the information on the bulletin board.

In this case, there is no doubt that the publication of the minutes along with its annex on the company committee's bulletin board by its president violated these limits, breaching the regulations on personal data protection, since:  
- There is no legal provision that allows for the full publication of the content of the minutes issued during a consultation period held in connection with a collective dismissal for technical reasons, such as the present case.

In our Labor Law, dismissal, in its various forms, is communicated in writing and individually to the dismissed worker, who is the interested party and recipient of this communication duty. And collective dismissal is no exception, since Article 51.4 of the Workers' Statute states that the dismissal must be individually notified to each affected worker, indicating the following: “4. Once the agreement has been reached or the decision has been communicated to the representatives of the workers, the employer may notify the dismissals individually to the affected workers, which must be done in accordance with the provisions of Article 53.1. In any case, at least thirty days must have elapsed between the date of communication of the opening of the consultation period to the labor authority and the effective date of the dismissal.” This is confirmed by Article 14 of Royal Decree 1483/2012, of October 29, which approves the Regulation of collective dismissal procedures, which indicates that this notification will take the form of a written communication individually notified to the worker according to Article 53 of the Workers' Statute, and will correspond, moreover, to the company itself, and not to the Works Council.  
In conclusion, from the labor regulations outlined, it is deduced that the legitimate interest in knowing about the dismissal belongs to the dismissed worker, and is fulfilled with the individual notification to them, which will be carried out by the company, subsequent to the minutes of the conclusion of the consultation period that agrees to the termination of the contracts. Had it been considered that all workers have a legitimate interest in knowing what has been agreed during the consultation phase of the collective dismissal process, including the identity of the workers affected by the dismissal, the possibility or obligation to publish or make known in a general manner the result of the final minutes of the consultation period would have been provided for in the regulations governing collective dismissal processes, and this is not the case.  
-  
On the other hand, while it is true that the representatives of the workers have the authority to publish on the bulletin board that the company makes available to them “matters that are of interest to the workers,” in accordance with Articles 81 and 68.d) of the Workers' Statute.  
But this authority cannot operate as an unlimited open clause that allows for the publication or revelation of personal data affecting all workers indiscriminately, but must be exercised with due respect to the regulations on the protection of personal data.  
That is, in the event that the president of the Works Council had considered that the publication of the minutes agreeing to conclude the consultation period of the collective dismissal process should be known by all workers, as it is a matter of general interest, they could have done so while protecting the personal data of the workers affected by the dismissal, anonymizing them prior to publication. It should not be forgotten that, in addition to being a representative of the workers, the

The respondent was also responsible for the processing of the personal data contained in the record that he decided to disclose for public knowledge, and therefore he should have acted in accordance with the provisions of the personal data protection regulations.  
However, as he himself acknowledges in the aforementioned email dated February 6, 2023, he did not act with the diligence required of a data controller in possession of personal data, as he had a copy of said record, and he omitted his duty of confidentiality regarding the personal data that appeared in the body of the record (which should have been anonymized), and in the annex (which should not have been stapled to the notice board).  
This implied a violation of the principle of integrity and confidentiality contained in Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR, since the respondent had the right to know the personal data contained in the record of the collective dismissal process, and the duty to inform the workers of matters of their interest, but this did not authorize him to disclose all the personal data contained in the record of the conclusion of the collective dismissal process without any limitation and without anonymizing it.  
Consequently, in accordance with the evidence available at this time, and without prejudice to the outcome of the investigation, it is considered that the facts presented could violate the provisions of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR, for unlawfully disclosing the personal data of the 43 workers that appeared in the record of the conclusion of the consultation period of the collective dismissal that was published on the notice board of the works council by the respondent.

Article 85 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), under the heading "Termination in sanctioning procedures" provides as follows:  
"1. Once a sanctioning procedure has been initiated, if the offender acknowledges their responsibility, the procedure may be resolved with the imposition of the appropriate sanction.  
2. When the sanction is solely pecuniary or when it is possible to impose a pecuniary sanction and another non-pecuniary sanction but the impropriety of the latter has been justified, the voluntary payment by the presumed responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except regarding the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for the damages caused by the commission of the infringement.
