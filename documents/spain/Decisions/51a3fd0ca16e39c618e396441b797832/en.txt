1. The documentation in the file provides clear evidence that the respondent violated Article 5 of the GDPR, principles related to processing, by transferring to a third party, the tenant of the claimant, by the exclusive agent of the respondent, the settlement containing personal data: name and address of the claimant (landlord), address of the leased premises, monthly rent, policy number, insured amount, and deductible, etc., which constitutes a breach of the duty of confidentiality.

This duty of confidentiality must be understood as aimed at preventing unauthorized leaks of data not consented to by the data subjects and is an obligation that falls not only on the data controller and processor but also on anyone involved in any phase of the processing, complementing the duty of professional secrecy.  
Therefore, it is considered that the respondent has violated the GDPR by infringing what is stated in Article 5.1.f) of the same.

However, the claimant appealed to the Provincial Court of Madrid, which issued an Order on 06/16/2022, dismissing the appeal and affirming what was stated in the Order issued in the first instance.  
In the proceedings conducted, it has been established that the exclusive agent acted in violation of the implemented measures and instructions given by the responsible party, including confidentiality, by revealing a document that solely pertained to the relationship between the policyholder and the insurance company, unrelated to the relationship that the landlord (claimant) had with their tenant (third party).  
The same agent acknowledged before the Court the delivery of the documentation to the tenant, and the same judicial body attributed to the agent a lack of professionalism.  
6. Well, in light of the above, the respondent has violated the personal data of the claimant by delivering the document containing the payment made by the insurer to the claimant for advance rent of unpaid rent to their tenant, a document that was presented in court without their knowledge, being responsible for the processing according to the definition given in section 7 of Article 4 of the GDPR, which considers as such "the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing," while the exclusive agent, responsible for the processing, is, according to section 8 of the aforementioned provision, "the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that processes personal data on behalf of the data controller."  
It should be noted that Article 28.10 of the GDPR invoked by the respondent refers to "if a processor infringes this Regulation by determining the purposes and means of the processing, it will be considered a controller with respect to that processing," that is, it refers to the infringement of the determination of the purposes and means of the processing.  
Now, the fact that the exclusive agent, as the processor, decided to show a document to a third party, the claimant's tenant, and on which this third party took a photograph and subsequently presented in court, does not mean that they used it to determine their own means and purposes. What are these?  
Their action is not determined by a commercial, financial, economic, or similar purpose, nor is there any particular purpose perceived; nor has the respondent provided what those own purposes and means were that led the exclusive agent to act in such a manner; according to what was stated in the court hearing, they did it without thinking that the document would be used in court and to reassure the claimant's tenant.  
The contract entered into between the respondent and the agent allows the latter, as deduced from Annex IV, clause 2, Identification of the affected information, access to the personal data of clients, policyholders, beneficiaries, insured parties, or third parties involved in conflicts with them (in judicial proceedings,

Indeed, one thing is the leak, an act reported to the criminal jurisdiction that ended with the provisional dismissal order of the Provincial Court of Madrid, and another is the duty of confidentiality of the data, as well as the implementation of security measures that those responsible for processing are obliged to adopt to prevent access to personal data without the corresponding authorization or consent, which subsequently motivated the adoption of new measures to prevent incidents like the one claimed.  
IV  
The infringement attributed to the respondent is classified in Article 83.5 a) of the GDPR, which considers that the infringement of “the basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9” is sanctionable, according to paragraph 5 of the aforementioned Article 83 of the cited Regulation, “with administrative fines of up to €20,000,000 or, in the case of a company, an amount equivalent to 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount.”  
The LOPDGDD in its Article 71, Infringements, states that: “Acts and conduct referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those that are contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements.”  
And in its Article 72, it considers for the purposes of prescription that they are: “Infringements considered very serious:
