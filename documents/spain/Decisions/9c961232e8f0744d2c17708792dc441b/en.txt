“The security of the Reby application is based on an oAuth-type authorization system. The security measures have undergone minor evolutions over time, but essentially those applied before August 16, 2021, and those currently applied are similar. For example, it can be highlighted that initially, a token with a proprietary format was used, and with the latest version of the app, we switched to using JWT tokens as explained below.  
JWT tokens follow the RFC 7519 specification: (***URL.1) The tokens contain the session identifier for that specific user on the Reby domain. They are signed by the oAuth server, so that the Reby backend can check a manipulated token and immediately discard the request.  
For added security, each user session maintains two tokens. One short-lived (a few hours) that we call the access token and another long-lived (about 30 days), which we call the refresh token. The refresh tokens are used to obtain fresh access tokens before they expire.

II  
Possible infringements  
This case is related to the technological platform REBY and the provision of bikesharing services offered by four other operators: AVANT, TUCYCLE, IBERSCOT, and OIZ.  
Both in the letter dated 12/03/2021 sent by the CITY COUNCIL OF BARCELONA and in the complaint dated 26/05/2021 filed by A.A.A., it is highlighted that personal data is communicated without the consent of the users of the aforementioned companies who register in the corresponding mobile "apps" to access the vehicle rental service. It is noted that when a person registers in one of the "apps" of these entities and subsequently installs another one, the second recognizes the user and reuses the data provided by the other "collaborating platform," with the aim of "streamlining the process." Additionally, none of the entities clearly inform who the "collaborating platforms" are from which personal data may be shared.  
In both cases, it was deemed appropriate to admit the letter from the City Council and the complaint for the necessary verifications regarding the information received from REBY. This led to the opening of preliminary investigation proceedings.  
At this point, it is necessary to clarify the role under which the entities involved act, from the perspective of personal data protection.  
The entities AVANT, TUCYCLE, IBERSCOT, and OIZ are the companies that were awarded the temporary licenses for special common use of public domain for the parking of mopeds and motorcycles for shared use under an economic exploitation regime in the municipal term of Barcelona, called by the CITY COUNCIL OF BARCELONA on 10/10/2019. Consequently, the entity REBY was not the beneficiary of this license.  
In light of the documentation in the file, the Inspection Services verified that throughout the year 2020, REBY entered into service provision contracts with each of the aforementioned operators, which aim, among other things, to provide the service of the platform and App to the data controller and the maintenance services of the platform and the App. In any case, REBY acts in the events as a data processor.

The figures of "data controller" and "data processor" are defined in Article 4 of the GDPR as follows:  
“Data controller or controller: the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; if the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the data controller or the specific criteria for its appointment may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.”  
“Data processor or processor: the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that processes personal data on behalf of the data controller.”  
Article 24 of the GDPR, concerning the "Responsibility of the data controller," establishes the following:  
“1. Taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is in compliance with this Regulation. Such measures shall be reviewed and updated as necessary.  
2. Where appropriate in relation to the processing activities, the measures referred to in paragraph 1 shall include the implementation, by the data controller, of appropriate data protection policies…”  
Report 0064/2020 from the Legal Office of this Agency has emphatically stated that “The GDPR has represented a paradigm shift in addressing the regulation of the right to the protection of personal data, which is now based on the principle of 'accountability' or 'proactive responsibility' as repeatedly pointed out by the AEPD (Report 17/2019, among many others) and is reflected in the Explanatory Memorandum of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD).”  
The aforementioned report continues as follows:  
“…the criteria on how to attribute the different roles remain the same (paragraph 11), reiterates that these are functional concepts, aimed at assigning responsibilities according to the actual roles of the parties (paragraph 12), which implies that in most cases, attention must be paid to the circumstances of the specific case (case by case) considering their actual activities rather than the formal designation of an actor as 'controller' or 'processor' (for example, in a contract), as well as autonomous concepts, whose interpretation must be carried out under the European regulations on the protection of personal data (paragraph 13), and taking into account (paragraph 24) that the need for a factual assessment also means that the role of a data controller does not derive from the nature of an entity that is processing data but from its activities.”

According to the above, the concepts of data controller and data processor are not formal, but functional, and must address the specific case.  
On the other hand, the existence of a data processor depends on a decision made by the data controller, who may decide to carry out certain processing operations themselves or to contract all or part of the processing with a processor.  
The essence of the data processor's function is that personal data is processed on behalf of and for the account of the data controller. In practice, it is the controller who determines the purpose and the means, at least the essential ones, while the data processor has the function of providing services to the data controllers. In other words, "acting on behalf of and for the account of the data controller" means that the data processor is at the service of the interests of the data controller in carrying out a specific task and, therefore, follows the instructions established by the controller, at least regarding the purpose and essential means of the entrusted processing.  
The data controller is the one who has the obligation to ensure compliance with data protection regulations and the protection of the rights of the data subjects, as well as to be able to demonstrate it (Articles 5.2, 24, 28, and 32 of the GDPR).  
The control of compliance with the law extends throughout the processing, from beginning to end. The data controller must act, in any case, diligently, consciously, committedly, and actively.  
This mandate from the legislator is independent of whether the processing is carried out directly by the data controller or whether it is performed using a data processor.  
In the present case, it is the 4 operators (AVANT, OIZ, TUCYCLE, and IBERSCOT) who decide to contract REBY to provide its technological platform and thus offer an adequate bikesharing service; assuming the role of data processor, since it acts fully on behalf of and for the account of these, which are the entities responsible for the processing in all respects regarding data protection.  
Furthermore, registering as a user from the REBY mobile app does not grant the role of data controller, as the terms of use and privacy policy that apply are those corresponding to the selected operator. Thus, it is the operator in question, and not REBY, who determines the processing operations of the personal data that will be carried out, that is, the why (the purpose) and the how (the means) personal data should be processed to achieve the intended purpose. Evidence of this are the screenshots provided by REBY in which a checkbox indicates the following: "Hello! The vehicle you are going to use is not from Reby, but from [*]. If you continue, you are accepting their Terms and Conditions and their Privacy Policy."  
In this sense, Article 28.10 of the GDPR also addresses the criterion of determining the purposes and means of processing when establishing the

As indicated, REBY acts as the data processor of the personal data of users, and any harm caused regarding the rights and freedoms of the affected data subjects must be attributed to the operators, as data controllers; without prejudice to what is provided in Article 28.10 of the GDPR.  
Finally, it should be noted that there is no evidence of the processing of personal data of users of the services outside of those derived from the contractual relationship signed by REBY with the aforementioned operators.  
Currently, REBY has completely ceased its activity, being in a pre-insolvency situation, and its mobile app does not allow for new registrations.  
In light of the above, the proceedings should be archived, without prejudice to the Agency applying the investigative and corrective powers it holds, which may carry out subsequent actions related to the data processing referred to in the complaint.
