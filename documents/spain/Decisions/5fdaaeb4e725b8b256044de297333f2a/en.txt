RESOLUTION OF THE RECOURSE FOR RECONSIDERATION  
Examined the recourse for reconsideration filed by BALLESPE, S.L. (hereinafter, the  
recurring party) against the resolution issued by the Deputy Director General of  
Data Inspection, due to the vacancy of the Presidency and Deputy Presidency of the  
Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated February 19, 2025, and based on the  
following  
FACTS  
FIRST: On February 19, 2025, a resolution was issued by the Deputy Director  
General of Data Inspection, due to the vacancy of the Presidency and Deputy  
Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202305556,  
by virtue of which BALLESPE, S.L. was imposed a violation of Article 5.1.c) of  
the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR, and a fine of 500.00 euros  
(FIVE HUNDRED euros).  
Furthermore, it was ordered that pursuant to Article 58.2.d) of the GDPR, within  
one month from the date this resolution became final and enforceable, it must  
demonstrate that it had proceeded to reorient the video cameras, so as to avoid  
excessive capture of public thoroughfare. In the event that, even with the  
reorientation of these, the capture of public thoroughfare continued to be excessive,  
it would have to demonstrate the incorporation of privacy masks into the current  
system or, failing that, the removal of the video surveillance system.  
This resolution, which was notified to the recurring party on February 25, 2025,  
was issued following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure,  
in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on  
Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), and  
supplementarily under Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative  
Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), regarding the  
processing of sanctioning procedures.  
SECOND: As proven facts of the aforementioned sanctioning procedure,  
PS/00360/2023, the following was recorded:  
PROVEN FACTS  
“FIRST: There is a video surveillance system on the facade of a building located  
at ***ADDRESS.1.  
SECOND: The video surveillance system consists of 3 cameras, of which only two  
are operational and excessively capture the public thoroughfare.  
THIRD: The responsible party for the video surveillance system is the company  
BALLESPE, S.L.”

THIRD: The recurring party submitted on March 20, 2025, to this Spanish Agency

- The violation of its right to defense due to defects in the notification of the  
initiation agreement.  
- The viewing of the surveillance cameras is not contrary to data protection

They insist that in the previous proceedings there is our letter dated July 18, 2023

- The existence of an error in the appreciation of the facts leading to a lack of

affecting the public thoroughfare.  
- The application of corrective measures should replace the imposed sanction.  
Subsidiarily, in case any irregularity is considered to exist, it requests the  
application of a corrective measure instead of the imposed sanction, in accordance  
with the principle of proportionality and the preventive purpose of data protection  
regulations.  
LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
The Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve this recourse  
in accordance with the provisions of Article 123 of Law 39/2015, of October 1,  
on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter,  
LPACAP) and Article 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal  
Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD).

Response to the allegations presented

firstly by the recurring party that its right to defense has been violated due to  
defects in the notification of the initiation agreement of the sanctioning procedure,  
indicating that this constitutes “the violation of the right to defense under Article 24

The claiming party continues to assert that “In matters of notifications, Article 24 of the Spanish Constitution is violated by the so-called material defenselessness that prevents the fulfillment of its purpose, which is to communicate the resolution in terms that allow maintaining the allegations or formulating the resources established in the legal system against said resolution. And this basic element must be predicated of the electronic administrative notification, which must guarantee the fundamental right to effective judicial protection.  
In this regard: The Supreme Court ruling of November 16, 2016 (STS 4991/2016) recalls how the electronic format “has in no way represented, nor is it representing, a paradigm shift, as the core and the foundations on which any approach to this matter must revolve remain the same given their constitutional importance” (FJ 3º).  
The Supreme Court ruling 1254/2020, of September 29, states that while electronic notification is valid, effective knowledge of the act by the recipient must be guaranteed, as well as that the Administration must exhaust the notification mechanisms provided for in the current regulations before considering an act validly notified, as established in STS 1974/2021, of May 24, and STS 2765/2020, of July 15.  
In the present case, it must also be weighed that my represented party has fulfilled the minimum duty of diligence in receiving the electronic notification regarding the prior actions carried out, accessing the communications and providing the requested information, and in this direction, the diligence of the Administration to ensure the proper success of its communication act has certain limits, one of which is the conduct of the recipient who, in the present case, shows a determined intention to accept the notification.  
Therefore, even acting diligently, since the notification was not opened, the act cannot be considered validly notified, generating defenselessness, as the Administration should have exhausted the notification mechanisms provided for in the current regulations before considering an act validly notified.”  
In response to such assertions, it should be indicated that on April 13, 2023, a complaint was filed with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against the appellant for the orientation of the cameras of a video surveillance system for which it is responsible, which would be violating data protection regulations to the extent that it was capturing excessive images of public thoroughfares (sidewalk, roadway, and parked vehicles, among others), requesting the AEPD on June 23, 2023, to provide images from the field of vision of the cameras, or to proceed with their reorientation or the introduction of privacy masks.  
The appellant responded to the AEPD's request on two occasions, on July 4 and 18, 2023, using electronic notification in all communications.  
It is pertinent to note that the appellant is a legal entity, and as such, is obliged to communicate electronically with the AEPD, in accordance with Article 14.2 a) of the LPACAP, and that such notifications will be deemed rejected when...

Secondly, the appellant claims that the viewing of surveillance cameras is not contrary to data protection legislation.  
They support their assertion by stating that “the GDPR in its Article 5.1(c) incorporates the principle of data minimization, establishing that personal data shall be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed, which implies that the communication must be limited to the minimum necessary or essential for the intended purpose.  
In the present case, we understand that the viewing would be in accordance with this principle as it does not extend beyond the images related to that purpose, which is none other than being able to exercise judicial claims arising from unlawful acts or liability, for which it is necessary to know the identity of the possible responsible party in order to direct the corresponding claim or legal action against them (Article 399 of the Civil Procedure Act or Article 277 of the Criminal Procedure Act).  
In relation to the above, we cannot forget the obligation to report the commission of the crime as provided in Articles 259, 262, and 264 of the Criminal Procedure Act, which would be covered under Article 6.1(c) of the GDPR as it concerns the fulfillment of a legal obligation of the data controller, and for which it is necessary to inform the authority of any information related to the reported fact and its circumstances (Article 267 of the Criminal Procedure Act).  
Therefore, regarding the necessary balancing between my client's interest in knowing the data related to the identity of the perpetrator of these acts is legitimate and must prevail over the right to data protection of the interested parties when the purpose of the data communication is to exercise the rights that correspond to them, since in the tension between both rights, the fundamental right to access justice and effective judicial protection must prevail over the right to data protection in this case, especially considering that the legislation establishes as a procedural burden the need to go to court to claim and exercise the rights of individuals, and this burden could not be fulfilled without knowing against whom the legal action must be directed.  
Therefore, with the limitations regarding the principles of purpose of the transfer and minimization of processing, it must be considered that the communication of data proposed in the present case is not contrary to data protection legislation.”  
This allegation, based on the purpose of exercising judicial actions, cannot justify the processing of personal data which, as is the case here, stems from an excess of capture. The possibility of a criminal act occurring does not legitimize the recording of individuals (it is recalled that the image is a data).

In this regard, the AEPD is acting under the authority granted in Article 83.2 of the GDPR, which states that “Administrative fines shall be imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, as an additional or substitute measure for the measures provided for in Article 58, paragraph 2, letters a) to h) and j).” This provision allows for the compatibility of the imposition of measures with the sanction consisting of an administrative fine. Furthermore, it should be noted that failure to comply with the measures imposed by this body may be considered an administrative infringement in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, classified as an infringement in its Articles 83.5 and 83.6, which may lead to the initiation of further administrative sanctioning proceedings.  
III  
Conclusion  
Consequently, in the present appeal for reconsideration, the appellant has not provided new facts or legal arguments that would allow for a reconsideration of the validity of the contested resolution.
