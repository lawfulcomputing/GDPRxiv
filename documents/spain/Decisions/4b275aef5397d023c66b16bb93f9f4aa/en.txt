LEGAL GROUNDS  
All administrative actions of a sanctioning nature must comply with the principle of typicity, defined in Article 27 of Law 40/2015, of October 1, on the Legal Regime of the Public Sector, as follows: “1. Only violations of the legal order that are provided for as such violations by a Law constitute administrative infringements (…). 2. Only by committing administrative infringements may sanctions be imposed, which, in any case, will be delimited by Law.”  
The imposed sanction is not in accordance with the principle of typicity, in that:  
- The Resolution is based on a violation of Article 22.2 LSSI, which states (the underlining is our own) “Service providers may use storage and data retrieval devices on the terminal equipment of the recipients, provided that they have given their consent…” during the period from April 19, 2023, to June 6, 2023.  
- The term “Use,” in accordance with the definition included in the Dictionary of the Spanish Language of the Royal Spanish Academy, means “To make something serve a purpose” or “To take advantage of something or someone.”  
- MASSIMO DUTTI, once the third-party cookies had been rejected by the user, could not use them as they were blocked through Javascript code, that is, it could not make those cookies serve any purpose, nor could it take advantage of them.  
- Consequently, the non-compliance that the Resolution establishes concerning the period from April 19, 2023, to June 6, 2023, did not occur, as MASSIMO DUTTI complied with the provisions of Article 22.2 LSSI, both prior to and during that period, through the explained blocking solution that did not allow the use of cookies once rejected.  
- Therefore, it is considered that there is no legal basis for imposing a sanction against my client for violation of Article 22.2 LSSI concerning the Facts recorded in the sanctioning Resolution, as those facts do not correspond in any way to the type of non-compliance with the Regulations that is intended to be attributed to MASSIMO DUTTI.

I  
Competence.  
The Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve this appeal, in accordance with the provisions of Article 123 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter LPACAP) and Article 43.1, second paragraph, of the LSSI.  
II  
Response to the allegations  
The joint assessment of the documentation in the procedure brings to the attention of the AEPD a view of the reported action that has been reflected in the facts declared proven above. However, in response to the allegations presented by the entity being appealed in the appeal for reconsideration, the following should be noted:  
a).- Regarding point "First," concerning the assertion that "MASSIMO DUTTI, under no circumstances could use non-technical or necessary third-party cookies that the user had rejected once initial consent was given, since the cookie blocking solution used by my client prior to the implementation of the deletion/expiration solution consisted of blocking those cookies with rejection through Javascript code (...).  
It should be noted that, during the verification carried out by this Agency on 03/16/23 on the website https://www.massimodutti.com/, it was confirmed that when the user gave consent for the use of third-party cookies, the following cookies were started to be used: MUID; ts; CONSEND; SOCS; CLID; _ga; _gid; ttp; u; NID; AEC; _pinterest_ct_ua and 1P_JAR, but there was no mechanism or access to a permanent control panel that allowed the user to withdraw consent after it had been given, if the user so desired, as can be seen in the screenshots obtained from the website (actions taken on 02/07/23 "Consent revocation action," page 6196 of the file and in the action taken on 03/16/23, page 6205 of the file).  
It was due to these deficiencies detected on the website in question that, on 03/22/23, the sanctioning procedure PS/00051/2023 was initiated.  
On 04/18/23, the entity being appealed submitted a written statement of allegations regarding the initiation of the file in which it denied that, concerning the impossibility of managing the cookies used once initial consent was given, there was no...

no mechanism that allowed the user to reject the cookies used, as there was a link to the "Cookie Settings" where cookies could be rejected with a simple click, since the link to "Cookie Settings" was always available in the horizontal menu located in the footer of the website.  
Based on these allegations, this agency re-checked the cookie policy of the website https://www.massimodutti.com/ on 19/04/23, confirming that, regarding the withdrawal of consent once given, it could now be verified that there was a mechanism or access to the permanent control panel at the bottom of the webpage "Cookie Settings" that allowed access to the control panel for managing cookies whenever the user desired, as can be seen in the diligence carried out by this Agency dated 18/04/23, page 6254 of the file.  

However, if the user wished to withdraw consent and reject the use of cookies by clicking on the "Reject All" option or moving the cursor from the "ON" position to the "OFF" position of the different cookie groups and clicking on "Confirm my Preferences" in the control panel, it was observed that the website continued to use the third-party cookies that had been installed when they were accepted at the beginning. That is, the website's responsible party had enabled a mechanism through which the user could withdraw the consent given for the use of third-party cookies, but it did not work as it was verified that the website continued to use the same unnecessary third-party cookies that had been installed initially, as was confirmed in the diligence carried out by this Agency dated 18/04/23, page 6254 of the file.  
Therefore, on 24/04/23, in the proposed resolution, it was suggested that the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency proceed to sanction the entity for violating the provisions of Article 22.2 of the LSSI, regarding the irregularity detected in its "Cookie Policy," in the sense of continuing to use unnecessary third-party cookies when the user wishes to withdraw consent.  
The allegations that the entity presented to this proposed resolution, which it has reiterated in the current appeal for reconsideration, stated that "In case the user had rejected the cookies during navigation, they remained in the browser's cookie system, but in no case were they used, as their use was blocked through Javascript code."  
On 06/06/23, this Agency conducted a new check of the website in question, verifying regarding the detected irregularity that, now, if the user wishes to withdraw the consent initially given for the use of unnecessary third-party cookies, the website removed them, reverting to using only the technical or necessary cookies detected at the beginning, as can be confirmed in the diligence carried out by this Agency dated 06/06/23, page 6307 of the file.  
Therefore, on 14/06/23, the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency issued a resolution of the sanctioning file PS/0051/2023, sanctioning the entity "GRUPO MASSIMO DUTTI, S.A. with NIE: A78115201, indicating therein, "(...) although it is confirmed that the responsible party of the website has
8/9  
modified the cookie policy adapting it to current regulations, this does not eliminate the proven non-compliance, since this Agency conducted the first check of the website on 16/03/23 (…)”.  
Therefore, although in the allegations presented by the entity in this appeal document, it is stated that, “although the rejected cookies remained visible to the user, they were never used as they were blocked,” the evidence shows that, when the sanctioning file was opened, there was no mechanism that allowed the user to withdraw the consent given for the use of third-party cookies.  
And that later, when the entity became aware of the opening of said file, there is evidence that it modified the website urging the permanent mechanism that allowed the user to access the “Control Panel” to modify the consent given, although it was verified that it did not work correctly since, even if the user withdrew the consent given, the non-essential third-party cookies continued to appear on the device.  
The entity now states that “although they remained visible, they were blocked” but, if this was true as the entity claims, at no time was the user informed of this fact. That is, the user was not informed, for example, that their request to withdraw consent had been addressed and that the cookies had been blocked, even though they continued to appear on the device as active.  
Later, when the entity became aware that the proposed resolution suggested sanctioning the entity for these deficiencies, the entity implemented, according to itself, the technological solution that the rejected cookies be removed from the system instead of blocked, claiming that it was due to “its concern for the user”.

Conclusion
