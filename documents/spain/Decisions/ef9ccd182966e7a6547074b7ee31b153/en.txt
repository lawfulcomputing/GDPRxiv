From the above, it is evident that the present sanctioning procedure should be archived.  
It must be taken into account that Administrative Sanctioning Law, due to its specialty, applies, with some nuances but without exceptions, the guiding principles of criminal law, resulting in the clear full validity of the principle of presumption of innocence.  
In this sense, the Constitutional Court, in Judgment 76/1990, considers that the right to the presumption of innocence entails “that the sanction is based on acts or incriminating evidence of the reproached conduct; that the burden of proof corresponds to the accuser, without anyone being obliged to prove their own innocence; and that any insufficiency in the results of the evidence presented, freely assessed by the sanctioning body, must translate into an acquittal ruling.” This principle is expressly included for sanctioning administrative procedures in Article 53.2.b) of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations, which recognizes the interested party the right “to the presumption of non-existence of administrative liability until proven otherwise.”  
In short, the application of the principle of presumption of innocence prevents attributing an administrative infringement when no evidence or indications have been obtained from which the existence of an infringement can be derived.  
In this case, from the actions carried out and the documentation in the file, there is no inference of an infringing action by the accused party within the jurisdiction of the AEPD, which is why the complaint should be archived.  
All of this is without prejudice to the Agency, applying the investigative and corrective powers it holds, being able to carry out subsequent actions related to the data processing referred to in the complaint.
