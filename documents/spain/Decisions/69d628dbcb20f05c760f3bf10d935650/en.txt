LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
According to the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of the GDPR, and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Preliminary Issues  
The reported facts materialize in the submission by the reported party to the complainant, after requesting access, in their capacity as complainant, to various administrative files processed by the City Council related to sanctioning procedures for traffic violations, insufficiently anonymized, granting access to various personal information of third parties, which violates the regulations on personal data protection.  
According to the provisions of Article 4.1 of the GDPR, there is a processing of personal data, since the reported party carries out, among other treatments, the collection, registration, conservation, use, access, and deletion of the following personal data of individuals, such as: name and surname, N.I.F, postal address, telephone number, email address, etc.  
The City Council carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR: "‘data controller’ or ‘controller’: the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its appointment may be provided for by the law of the Union or of the Member States."

Article 4(12) of the GDPR broadly defines "personal data breaches" (hereinafter personal data breach) as "all breaches of security that lead to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration of, or unauthorized disclosure of, or access to, personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed."  
In this case, there is a personal data breach under the circumstances indicated above, categorized as a confidentiality breach due to the exposure of personal data of other individuals to an unauthorized third party.  
Within the principles of processing provided for in Article 5 of the GDPR, the integrity and confidentiality of personal data are guaranteed in paragraph 1(f) of the aforementioned article. For its part, the security of personal data is regulated in Articles 32, 33, and 34 of the GDPR, which govern the security of processing, the notification of a personal data breach to the supervisory authority, and the communication to the data subject, respectively.  
III  
Response to the Allegations Presented  
In response to the allegations presented by the reported party, the following should be noted:  
FIRST REASON: Firstly, the reported party claims that it was not necessary to anonymize the personal data of the complainant since it was their own data and was intended solely for them. However, this justification is insufficient because, although the complainant's data does not need to be anonymized, data protection regulations require that any processing of personal data must be carried out ensuring the rights of all affected parties, not just the complainant. This means that in the files provided to any person, including the complainant, the data of third parties who have not consented to the disclosure of their personal information must be removed or anonymized.  
Therefore, the fact that the files provided to the complainant contained data of third parties without proper anonymization constitutes a violation of data protection regulations, particularly of the principle of confidentiality (Article 5.1(f) of the GDPR). The delivery of documents under these conditions not only exposes personal data of third parties but also allows for the identification of other individuals involved in the sanctioning procedures, which aggravates the infringing conduct.  
SECOND REASON: The City Council argues that it complied with a request from the Ombudsman by providing the complainant access to the requested information, and that the high volume of work, due to the prolific activity of the complainant, may have caused some isolated incidents in the processing of data.  
In this regard, it should be noted that compliance with a request from the Ombudsman does not exempt the City Council from its obligation to comply with data protection regulations.

Data Protection. The fact that the Ombudsman has requested that information be provided to the claimant does not imply that documents containing personal data of third parties should be delivered without the appropriate measures of anonymization or pseudonymization. The City Council has the obligation to ensure at all times the protection of the personal data of all individuals involved in the files, regardless of the amount of information requested or the volume of administrative work.

Among these measures is the obligation to ensure that all personal data is adequately protected, even in situations of high workload.  
Furthermore, the assertion that "isolated incidents" occurred due to the volume of documents does not exempt from liability. Each time a file containing personal data is provided without the appropriate guarantees, a violation of the rights of the affected individuals occurs. Ultimately, the obligation of the responding party, especially in its capacity as a Public Administration, is to guarantee the protection of all personal data in its possession, and any deficiency in this regard constitutes a breach, regardless of whether it is considered "isolated" or not.

The loss of confidentiality poses a risk that may lead to material or immaterial damages for individuals (Recital 85 of the GDPR). In this sense, Recital 75 of the GDPR establishes that: “The risks to the rights and freedoms of individuals, of varying severity and probability, may arise from the processing of data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial harm, particularly in cases where the processing may lead to discrimination issues, identity theft or fraud, financial losses, damage to reputation, loss of confidentiality of data subject to professional secrecy, unauthorized reversal of pseudonymization, or any other significant economic or social harm; in cases where individuals are deprived of their rights and freedoms or prevented from exercising control over their personal data; …”.

VII  
             Second obligation breached: Article 32.1 of the GDPR  
The respondent is also attributed with the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR “Security of processing,” which states:  
Article 32 “Security of processing” of the GDPR establishes:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data promptly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly verifying, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a result of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data may only process such data on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law.” (the underlining is ours)
