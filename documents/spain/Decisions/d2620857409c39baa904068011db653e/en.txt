LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence and procedure  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Response to the allegations against the initiation agreement  
The present procedure has been initiated for the alleged commission of an infringement of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR, which regulates the Principle of data minimization, allegedly committed by the respondent by imposing on the claimant the requirement to provide a complete image or photograph of his identity document in order to check in at their hospitality establishment (Posada el Azufral), which has a physical reception and the possibility of online check-in for travelers. The respondent has presented allegations against the initiation agreement that have been fully reproduced in the initiation agreement.  
Once the allegations presented have been analyzed, they must be dismissed for the following reasons:  
1.  
The processing of personal data for the registration of the entry and exit of travelers is automated, carried out through the PARTEE application contracted with the entity YNEEDS, YOUR INTELLIGENT IMAGING COMPANY S.L.U, requiring the guest to go through a process via one of the 4 options made available to them:  
A) Online check-in without providing the Identity Document or passport.  
B) Online check-in providing the Identity Document or passport.  
C) In-person check-in without providing the Identity Document or passport.  
D) In-person check-in providing the Identity Document or passport.

1.  
The respondent insists that it is necessary to require the delivery of a copy of the identity document to formalize the traveler's reservation, as it is needed to fulfill its obligation to verify the authenticity of the personal data contained therein, in accordance with the provisions of Royal Decree 933/2021, of October 26, which establishes the obligations of documentary registration and information of individuals or legal entities engaged in hospitality and motor vehicle rental activities (hereinafter, RD 933/2021).  
In this regard, it is pertinent to reproduce the arguments contained in the initiation agreement, which are fully shared, in which it is explained in detail why the regulations governing the registration books and entry reports of travelers in hospitality establishments, as well as the obligation to communicate the information contained in the registration sheets to the State Security Forces and Corps, do not require at any time that a copy/photo of the identity document be provided, nor that all the data contained in that document be reported.  
This regulation is fundamentally constituted by Organic Law 4/2015, of March 30, on the protection of citizen security (hereinafter, LO 4/2015), and Royal Decree 933/2021, of October 26, which establishes the obligations of documentary registration and information of individuals or legal entities engaged in hospitality and motor vehicle rental activities (hereinafter, RD 933/2021).  
Article 24 of LO 4/2015 provides in its first section the following:  
“Individuals or legal entities engaged in activities relevant to citizen security, such as hospitality (...) shall be subject to the obligations of documentary registration and information in the terms established by the applicable provisions.”  
These are currently established by the aforementioned RD 933/2021, to which the respondent refers, with Annex I. a), in its section 3 setting forth the data of travelers that must be mandatorily included in the so-called “Registration Sheet” that the responsible entity of the hotel must submit to the State Security Forces and Corps when it comes to professional hospitality establishments. Specifically, the following data of the traveler are legally required:  
“a) Name.  
b) First surname.  
c) Second surname.  
d) Gender.  
e) Identity document number.  
f) Document support number.  
g) Type of document (DNI, passport, TIE).  
h) Nationality.  
i) Date of birth.  
j) Place of usual residence.– Full address.– Locality.– Country.  
k) Landline phone.  
l) Mobile phone.  
m) Email address.  
n) Number of travelers.  
o) Relationship between travelers (in the case that any are minors).”  
Consequently, according to this regulation, it is inferred that it is not mandatory to collect, register, or communicate to the competent authorities the image, photocopy, or complete photograph of the identity document of each traveler, but only some of the data contained therein such as: name and surnames, identification number, support number, type of document (DNI; passport… etc.), nationality, and date of birth.  
It must be taken into account that the photograph or photocopy of the traveler's DNI (on both sides), passport, or other identity documents contains personal data that exceeds those required by this regulation, such as: the image or face of the traveler, the equipment number, or the names of the traveler's parents, regarding which there is no legal obligation to collect,

It is argued that the PARTEE application does not store the photograph of the document, but rather performs an automatic data dump that originates from the scanning of the document, and that the traveler is informed that their personal data is not stored nor will it be used for other purposes.  
In this regard, the respondent states: “At the moment guests make a reservation, they receive an online communication (email) days before their arrival for the online check-in. This check-in is done from the Client's device. The application allows for the voluntary and intelligent scanning of the ID card, directly obtaining the necessary data, and the guest can skip this process if they fill in the data manually.  
In the event that the Client opts to provide their ID for the intelligent scanning of information, the copy of the national identity document is not stored on the application’s servers, but is only used temporarily for the extraction of necessary information.”  
This argument cannot be accepted for several reasons:  
- From the privacy policy of PARTEE, it can be inferred that the application indeed incorporates an intelligent data dump scanning, typically performed using optical character recognition (OCR) software that automatically identifies the characters of a specific alphabet and stores them as data, that is, it converts the image into text, and enables the completion of the “customer form” or “traveler entry form,” which does not necessarily require the digital image to be stored. However, the use of these intelligent scans is not incompatible with the fact that, in addition to taking the data and automatically filling it in the form, the scanned images could be stored in the application for a certain period, as it is possible to configure various types of settings that allow for this archiving.  
- In the present case, there is no doubt that when the traveler provides the photograph during the online check-in process, PARTEE does indeed store the images for 5 days, and that, furthermore, these are sent by email to the respondent, as expressly configured by the respondent, who states that they do not delete “the data used for the traveler report for a period of 3 years,” among which is this photograph, in order to comply with the deadlines set by tax legislation. III  
Unfulfilled obligation. Excessive data processing  
The processing of personal data of individuals who book accommodation in hotel establishments (referred to as "travelers") must be governed by the principles outlined in Article 5 of the GDPR.  
It is noteworthy, due to its relevance to the present case, the so-called "Principle of lawfulness and transparency" and "Principle of data minimization" provided in the first paragraph of the same, letters a) and c), respectively, which state:  
“Article 5 Principles relating to processing  
1. Personal data shall be:  
a) processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject ("lawfulness, fairness, and transparency").  
(...)  
c) adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed ("data minimization").”  
Furthermore, Article 5.2 of the GDPR states: “The data controller shall be responsible for compliance with the provisions of paragraph 1 and shall be able to demonstrate it.”  
Article 6 of the GDPR, relating to "Lawfulness of processing," determines in its paragraph 1 the circumstances under which the regulation allows the processing of personal data of a third party, referred to as "lawful bases." If none of these circumstances or conditions apply, the processing will not be legitimate or considered lawful under the GDPR:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if it meets at least one of the following conditions:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or for the implementation of pre-contractual measures at the request of the data subject;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary to protect vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person.  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in letter f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.”  
In the present case, once the allegations and documentation provided by the respondent during the phases of transferring the complaint and the allegations against the initiation agreement in the previous Legal Basis have been examined, it can be concluded that the respondent does not have a lawful basis to request the submission of a copy or photograph of the identity document of the travelers as a condition for their registration (check-in), which constitutes excessive and unnecessary processing of personal data contrary to the Principle of Data Minimization in Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR.  
Given the concurrent circumstances, where the establishment has a prior voluntary online check-in process but requires the traveler to provide a photograph or image of their identity document as a condition for accommodation, it is understood that requiring the traveler to provide the image or photograph of these documents constitutes excessive processing of personal data, as they contain personal data that is inadequate, irrelevant, and unnecessary for the specific purpose of the processing in question (compliance with the legal obligations regarding the registration of travelers' check-in and check-out).  
As previously stated, the photograph or photocopy of the traveler's ID (on both sides), passport, or other identity verification documents contains personal data that exceeds what is required by this regulation, such as: the image or face of the traveler, the equipment number, or the names of the traveler's parents, regarding which there is no obligation.
