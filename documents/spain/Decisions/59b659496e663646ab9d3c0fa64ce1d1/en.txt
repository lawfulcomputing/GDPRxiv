LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  
II  
Allegations to the initiation agreement  
In response to the allegations presented by the entity being claimed, it is appropriate to point out what is stated below.  
First, regarding the nature of the public office held by the claimant and alleged by the party being claimed, it is important to highlight that the regulations on personal data protection and the rights derived from it apply universally to all natural persons, regardless of the position or office held by such persons. This is evident from the very scope of application established in the GDPR, whose first article states that:  
“This Regulation establishes the rules relating to the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the rules relating to the free movement of such data.”

While there are exceptions for certain areas indicated by Article 2 of the same regulation (among which the case at hand is not included), the subjective scope of application is always the same, as long as the affected party is a natural person for its application to proceed and all the principles recognized therein must be respected. Consequently, the claiming party, even as a member of a municipal corporation and subject to political debate, retains their rights under data protection regulations, including the right to be protected against the unlawful or improper processing of their personal data.  
On the other hand, the access to information that members of the municipal corporation claim, which is recognized by local regulations, aims to facilitate the exercise or development of the functions of the aforementioned members.  
Secondly, regarding the allegation that specific data of the claiming party was not provided, it must be indicated that mere knowledge of the existence of a seizure order against them constitutes personal data related to their financial situation. Knowledge of such information, even in general terms, can have a significant negative impact on the reputation of the affected individual, in addition to violating the principle of confidentiality guaranteed by the GDPR.  
This assertion is consistent with the concept of "processing" established by Article 4 of the GDPR:  
“any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure or destruction;”  
Finally, the claiming party asserts that any additional specific data related to the administrative situation of the claimant was introduced by the claimant themselves as part of the political discussion dynamics. In this regard, it is appropriate to point out that any additional specific data that may have been expressed during the plenary interventions arises from the breach of confidentiality regarding the claimant's data that had occurred and which was subsequently disclosed publicly.  
This reaction from the claiming party, consisting of a defense following the unauthorized and public access and dissemination of their tax situation, should not be interpreted as a validation of the access to such information that had occurred.  
III  
Unfulfilled obligation of Article 5.1 f) GDPR  
According to paragraph 1.f) of Article 5 GDPR, data must be:

"treaties in such a way as to ensure an adequate security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, through the application of appropriate technical or organizational measures ('integrity and confidentiality')"  
Similarly, Recital 39 of the GDPR states: "Personal data must be processed in a manner that ensures an adequate level of security and confidentiality of personal data, including to prevent unauthorized access or use of such data and the equipment used for processing."  
The principle of confidentiality, according to the aforementioned Article 5.1 f) of the GDPR, requires the protection of personal data against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage. This principle is essential to guarantee the security of personal data, constituting a key pillar in the protection of individual rights in this area. Conversely, the violation of this principle can have unintended consequences, reflecting its importance within the regulatory framework of data protection.  
In the present case, after reviewing the documents provided by both parties, a breach of the aforementioned principle of confidentiality is evident.  
Compliance with the principle of confidentiality entails the adoption and implementation of measures of all kinds, both technical and organizational. In this case, the fact that certain councilors of the City Council could access personal tax information of the responding party highlights the absence or inadequacy of such measures. Specifically, the possibility of accessing personal information of one of the councilors through the General Registry of the City Council suggests a lack of adoption of appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure the security, in this case, of the claimant's data, and to prevent access to personal data by unauthorized third parties.  
Thus, free access to the registry manifests a lack of adequate access controls and profiling within the city council, which should have limited access to personal data based on necessity and relevance for the functions of the councilors. This is particularly relevant in the context of the General Registry, which stores documents addressed not only to the city council but also to other administrations, as permitted by Article 16 of Law 39/2015 on Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations, and among whose content special categories of personal data as provided in Article 9 of the GDPR can be found.  
It is worth noting that, although Article 77 of Law 7/1985 of April 2 on the Bases of Local Regime (LBRL) establishes that members of local Corporations have the right to access data and information for the development of their function, such access is regulated and does not imply indiscriminate access to all information, especially when it comes to personal data. Article 77 itself establishes, on the one hand, a material limit, stating that the data must be necessary for the exercise of their function; and on the other hand, a procedural limit, indicating that it must be carried out through a request that will be resolved with justification within five days.

following. The free and unlimited access to the content of the resources exceeds the content of the right of access by members of the Local Corporations regulated by the aforementioned article, as well as by Articles 14 to 16 of the Regulation on Organization and Functioning and Legal Regime of Local Entities (ROF) that develop this procedure.  
In the case at hand, the information regarding the tax situation of the claimant originated from a seizure order that had been registered at the City Council, to which the councilors had access. However, access to this information should have been limited to authorized and competent personnel within the City Council to fulfill the corresponding administrative purposes, thus preventing unauthorized processing of such personal data.  
The violation of the principle of confidentiality lies, therefore, in that the tax data of the claimant were accessible to all councilors of the City Council, without any justification supporting such indiscriminate access. Confidentiality implies, among other measures, limiting access to personal data solely to those individuals who need to know it based on their specific responsibilities and always under certain pre-established criteria. The failure to implement measures that adequately restricted this access, allowing access to personal data without an objective reason, resulted in the City Council's non-compliance with its obligation to guarantee the confidentiality of the claimant's data. Consequently, this lack of security measures violates the principle of confidentiality of Article 5.1 f) by failing to protect the data against unnecessary and unauthorized access within the same Corporation.

V  
Unfulfilled obligation of Article 32 of the GDPR  
Article 32 "Security of Processing" of the GDPR establishes:  
"1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the varying likelihood and severity of risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly verifying, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a result of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this Article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data may only process such data on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law.”  

It is necessary to point out that the aforementioned provision does not establish a list of specific security measures according to the data being processed, but rather establishes the obligation for the controller and the processor to implement

technical and organizational measures that are appropriate to the risk involved in the processing, taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, and the risks of likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.  
Likewise, the security measures must be adequate and proportionate to the identified risk, determining those technical and organizational measures that are appropriate considering pseudonymization and encryption, the ability to ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience, the ability to restore availability and access to data after an incident, verification process (which is not an audit), evaluation, and assessment of the effectiveness of the measures.  
In any case, when assessing the adequacy of the measures adopted, particular attention must be paid to the risks presented by the processing of data, as a consequence of the destruction, loss, or accidental or unlawful alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial harm.  
For its part, Recital 83 of the GDPR states that “(83) In order to maintain security and prevent processing from infringing the provisions of this Regulation, the controller or the processor must assess the inherent risks of the processing and implement measures to mitigate them, such as encryption. These measures must ensure an adequate level of security, including confidentiality, taking into account the state of the art and the cost of their implementation in relation to the risks and the nature of the personal data that must be protected. When assessing the risk in relation to data security, the risks arising from the processing of personal data must be taken into account, such as the destruction, loss, or accidental or unlawful alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data, which are particularly likely to cause physical, material, or immaterial harm.”  
In this regard, the audit report presented by the responding party, which has been incorporated into this file, is particularly relevant. Within this report, there is a section related to “Access control, physical access, and access logging,” within which the following is indicated:

Therefore, there must be an access control policy that includes an inventory of the information, the departments with access to the information, their permissions, and privileges.

Given the above, in the case at hand, the absence on the part of the respondent of the use of any instrument aimed at ensuring an adequate level of security for personal data appropriate to the level of risk arising from its processing highlights a breach of Article 32 of the GDPR by failing to comply with the obligation to adopt technical and security measures appropriate to the risk. In this regard, it is noteworthy that the report specifically emphasizes that the Local Corporation has neither documented nor implemented an access control system that limits access to the data only to users who need to know such information based on their specific responsibilities.  
In fact, as indicated in the fourth proven fact, the audit report, which aims to determine the situation and adequacy of the respondent to the requirements established by the regulations on data protection, qualifies it in its conclusions as “UNFAVORABLE”:  
“In the auditor's opinion, the situation of the Valdemorillo City Council regarding the level of compliance with current legislation on the security of personal data concerning its processing is UNFAVORABLE.”  
It is important to highlight that the described conduct represents a breach independent of the principle of confidentiality, as the absence of adequate security measures not only affects the complainant but potentially exposes the personal data of any person whose information is under the custody of the City Council. By not having adequate security measures, the integrity and protection of all personal data processed by the corporation are put at risk, reflecting a deficiency in the management of data security beyond the particular case of the complainant, which substantiates in this case the attribution of the infringement provided for in Article 32 of the GDPR.  
VI  
Classification and qualification of the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR  
The aforementioned infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR constitutes the commission of the infringements classified in Article 83.4 of the GDPR, which under the heading “General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines” states: “Infringements of the following provisions shall be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 10,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to a maximum of 2% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the obligations of the controller and the processor under Articles 8, 11, 25 to 39, 42, and 43; (…)”  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations, Article 73 “Infringements considered serious” of the LOPDGDD indicates: “According to what is established in Article 83.4 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following infringements are considered serious and shall be subject to a two-year statute of limitations:  
(…) f) The failure to adopt those technical and organizational measures that are appropriate to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk of processing, as required by Article 32.1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.

VIII  
Adoption of measures  
In accordance with the provisions of the aforementioned Article 58.2 d) of the GDPR, according to which each supervisory authority may “order the controller or processor to ensure that the processing operations comply with the provisions of this Regulation, where appropriate, in a specific manner and within a specified time frame.”  
In this case, the controller is required to notify this Agency within three months of the adoption of the following measures:  
- Adopt the appropriate organizational and technical measures to ensure an adequate level of security for the risk, specifically preventing free and general access to the content held in the general registry of the municipality by implementing an access control system through which only access to the documentation necessary for the exercise of the functions of authorized persons is permitted.  
It is warned that failure to comply with the possible order to adopt measures imposed by this agency in the resolution of this procedure may be considered an administrative infringement in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, classified as an infringement in its Articles 83.5 and 83.6, and such conduct may lead to the initiation of further administrative sanctioning procedures.
