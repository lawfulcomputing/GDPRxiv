From the proceedings carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST:  
On July 4, 2023, a complaint was filed with entry registration number REGAGE23e00044414781 before the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against the GENERAL DIRECTORATE OF POLICE with NIF S2816015H (hereinafter, D.G.POL.). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The complainant, who (...) from CEPOL (European Agency for Police Training), refers to unauthorized access to their health data contained in the SIGESPOL file by a medical professional from the respondent. It states that, through a Resolution dated ***DATE.1, the initiation of a retirement procedure (...) against the complainant was agreed upon, the origin of which is an internal note from the Health Area (Occupational Health), signed by the aforementioned medical professional, which states that "After reviewing the clinical-labor documentation of the National Police Officer Ms. A.A.A., this Area proposes the initiation of the appropriate procedures for evaluation by the Medical Tribunal of the National Police, for the purpose of a possible change of administrative situation to JIF."  
It considers that their health data has been processed without their consent, without legal authorization, and for a purpose different from that for which the data was collected. It refers to alleged discrimination based on their sexual orientation and their connection with the JUPOL union, (...).  
No additional documentation is provided.  
SECOND:  
In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the aforementioned complaint was forwarded to D.G.POL., for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in the data protection regulations.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I Competence  
In accordance with the functions conferred to each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of the GDPR and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Presidency of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to resolve these investigative proceedings.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II Legitimacy of the processing  
The first paragraph of Article 6 of the GDPR establishes the following:  
"1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary in order to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular where the data subject is a child.  
The provision in point f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks."  
In this case, the claimant believes that her health data has been processed without a legal basis, as she did not provide explicit consent, and refers to unauthorized accesses by a physician of the responding party to her health data contained in the

Information regarding this aspect was requested from the responding party, and a written response was received by this Agency on 08/08/2023, clarifying that the legal basis for the processing would be contained in the Register of Treatment Activities (RAT) of the Ministry of the Interior and would be Law 9/2015, on the Personnel Regime of the National Police, Organic Law 4/2010, of May 20, on the Disciplinary Regime of the National Police Corps, Royal Decree 5/2015, of October 30, which approves the consolidated text of the Basic Statute of Public Employees, Organic Law 2/1986, of March 13, on Security Forces and Bodies.  
Therefore, the bases for legitimizing the processing of the data contained in the SIGESPOL file would be those established in Article 6.1, paragraphs c) and e) of the GDPR.  
Regarding the authorization of the Medical Officer to access the personal data of the claimant, the D.G.POL. states that they were authorized to consult the data directly related to the functions they perform in accordance with their job position, including the medical data of police officers collected in the SIGESPOL application, to which only officials assigned to the Health Area have access, both from central services and from regional health units, and this access would be limited to the data of officials who are assigned to the police facilities existing in the territorial scope in which they perform their functions.  
As for the purpose of the processing, it is stated in the RAT of the Ministry of the Interior. It is noted that the first of the purposes of the processing listed in the RAT is the management of matters related to human resources of the General Directorate of the Police, among which is the control of the fitness of police officers to perform their functions, in accordance with Article 80 of Organic Law 9/2015, of July 28, on the Personnel Regime of the National Police, which states in point 1 that “The National Police will ensure that its members maintain the necessary psychophysical conditions for the performance of their functions. To this end, there will be a health service whose competencies and organization will be developed by regulation.”  
Therefore, based on the information available, this Agency considers that no evidence has been found to substantiate a possible infringement of Article 6 of the GDPR.
