FOURTH. - ON THE INFRINGEMENT AND SANCTION. ON THE REQUEST FOR ARCHIVING OF THE SANCTIONING FILE.  
Articles 38.4 d) and 39.1 c) of the LSSI establish that minor infringements include "the sending of commercial communications by email or other equivalent electronic means when the requirements established in Article 21 are not met and do not constitute a serious infringement," carrying a penalty of a fine of up to thirty thousand euros (€30,000).  
For its part, Article 39 bis 2 establishes that: "The sanctioning bodies, considering the nature of the facts and the significant concurrence of the criteria established in the previous section, may decide not to initiate the opening of the sanctioning procedure and, instead, warn the responsible party, so that within the period determined by the sanctioning body, they prove the adoption of the corrective measures that, in each case, are pertinent, provided that the following conditions are met:  
a) That the facts constitute a minor or serious infringement according to the provisions of this Law.  
b) That the competent body has not previously sanctioned or warned the infringer as a result of the commission of infringements provided for in this Law."  
Considering the nature of the reported facts, they can be considered of little relevance; that, moreover, these facts have not caused any damage or harm to the complainant, the diligent attitude that this party has maintained at all times, attending to communications and attempting to rectify what occurred; the qualification of the facts as a minor infringement and the lack of previous sanctioning files, constituting this the first offense committed by this party, we request the AEPD to proceed with the archiving of the present sanctioning file with the legal warnings.  
FIFTH. - ON THE REQUEST FOR MODERATION OF THE SANCTION.  
That, if despite all the circumstances outlined above, the body to which I am addressing deems it appropriate to impose an economic sanction for the events that occurred, dismissing the request for archiving the sanctioning file, this party requests that the proposed sanction be graduated.  
Thus, the requested reduction is appropriate when there is a qualified decrease in the culpability of the accused or in the unlawfulness of the act as a result of the significant concurrence of several of the criteria set forth in Article 40.  
As we have previously stated, this party was unaware of the unlawfulness of the communications sent, considering them at all times as prior communications to any commercial communication, so there was no intention to violate the provisions of the LSSI.  
The reported facts have consisted of the sending of three emails, the first of which was in October 2021 and the other two, sent erroneously due to a lack of coordination between the different email accounts used, in April and May 2022.  
There is no recurrence in the commission of similar acts as the present sanctioning file is the first initiated against this party.  
No material harm has been caused to the complainant, beyond the inconveniences that the sending of the two emails may have caused them while exercising their right of opposition.

II.-2  
Administrative Infraction  
The facts presented constitute a violation of Article 21 of the LSSI by the entity being claimed for sending two advertising emails without prior request or explicit authorization from the recipient, regardless of whether the recipient's data is public and available on different websites.  
In this regard, Article 21 of the LSSI states the following:  
“1. The sending of advertising or promotional communications by email or other equivalent electronic communication means that have not been previously requested or expressly authorized by the recipients is prohibited.  
2. The provisions of the previous section shall not apply when there is a prior contractual relationship, provided that the provider has lawfully obtained the contact data of the recipient and uses it to send commercial communications regarding products or services of its own company that are similar to those initially contracted with the client.  
In any case, the provider must offer the recipient the possibility to oppose the processing of their data for promotional purposes through a simple and free procedure, both at the time of data collection and in each of the commercial communications sent to them.  
When the communications have been sent by email, this means must necessarily consist of the inclusion of an email address or another valid electronic address where this right can be exercised, and the sending of communications that do not include such address is prohibited.”  
II.-3  
Graduation of the Sanction  
The aforementioned infraction is classified as “minor” in Article 38.4.d) of said regulation, which qualifies as such, “The sending of commercial communications by email or other equivalent electronic communication means when the requirements established in Article 21 are not met and does not constitute a serious infraction.”

Article 29 of Law 40/2015, of October 1, on the Legal Regime of the Public Sector, regarding the “Principle of Proportionality” establishes the following:  
1. Administrative sanctions, whether or not of a pecuniary nature, may not, under any circumstances, imply, directly or subsidiarily, deprivation of liberty.  
2. The establishment of pecuniary sanctions must ensure that the commission of the classified infractions does not result in more benefit for the infringer than compliance with the infringed norms.  
3. In the normative determination of the sanctioning regime, as well as in the imposition of sanctions by Public Administrations, the due suitability and necessity of the sanction to be imposed and its adequacy to the seriousness of the constitutive fact of the infraction must be observed.  
The graduation of the sanction will particularly consider the following criteria:  
a) The degree of culpability or the existence of intent.  
b) The continuity or persistence in the infringing conduct.  
c) The nature of the damages caused.
