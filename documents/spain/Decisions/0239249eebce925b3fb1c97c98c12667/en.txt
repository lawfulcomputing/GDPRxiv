The entity JUSTICIAPP is the owner of the platform "Justiciapp," accessible through mobile devices and on the web "***URL.2" for registered users.  
The exercise of the right of access pertains to the processing of personal data resulting from the use of the "Justiciapp" platform, including those processes involving the use of cookies related to the Google Analytics service and the international transfer of personal data.  
It is advisable, first and foremost, to determine the status under which the claimed entity intervenes.  
According to the provisions established in Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, the processing of personal data is confirmed, since the entity JUSTICIAPP collects and processes data through the aforementioned platform and website, related to registered users.  
JUSTICIAPP carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR, which defines the figure of "data controller" as follows:  
“Data controller: the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; if the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the data controller or the specific criteria for its appointment may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.”  
The data controller is so from the moment it decides the purposes and means of the processing. This is unequivocally expressed in the Guidelines 07/2020 of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) on the concepts of data controller and processor in the GDPR:  
“A data controller is the entity that determines the purposes and means of the processing, that is, the why and how of the processing. The data controller must decide on both purposes and means. However, some more practical aspects of the implementation ("non-essential means") can be left to the processor. It is not necessary for the controller to actually have access to the data being processed to qualify as a controller” (unofficial translation).  
Determining who decides the means and purposes of data processing is crucial for establishing who is responsible for compliance with personal data protection regulations, and in particular who must provide information to the data subjects or address the rights of the data subject, etc.  
In this case, it is established that JUSTICIAPP is the owner of the "Justiciapp" platform and the website of the same name and is the one who determines the purposes and means regarding the processing of data that involves access to and use of these means by users. Therefore, it is the entity responsible for such processing.

. Personal data and international data transfers.  
In its written exercise of the right, the claimant expressly requests that they be informed of the personal data processed in the event that the registration or browsing on the website installs Google Analytics cookies and whether this implies the international transfer of personal data.  
Therefore, taking into account the object of the right exercised by the claimant, it is necessary to analyze whether the use of cookies by the website involved, at the time the right was exercised, the collection of personal data from users.  
Regarding this issue, the AEPD decided to carry out preliminary investigative actions, the result of which, as noted in the background of this act, has allowed for the confirmation that the use of cookies by the "Justiciapp" website involves the collection of personal data from users and its international transfer.  
It is important to highlight the information provided to users on the website at the time the right of access was exercised by the claimant.  
Thus, in the Cookie Policy, it was stated that "JUSTICIAPP, S.L., by automatically obtaining information from the website through Cookies, may collect personal data from Users, relating to their computing device, the use of services, and their behavioral habits. This information may include, among others, data about language, the pages or functionalities of the website most used, keywords, date, time, duration of time Users spend on certain types of pages, IP address, advertising identifier, and similar information related to the use of the website. JUSTICIAPP, S.L., can collect such information through the use of cookies.  
Additionally, there was a warning about the use of Google Analytics.  
Well, the actions carried out allow us to conclude that Google Analytics cookies collect personal data from users, which are subject to international transfer.  
Among these data are unique user identifiers (_ga and _gid), the IP address, as well as other data associated with the browser and the browsing itself or system information, among others.  
Article 4.1 of the GDPR defines personal data as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('the data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific factors characteristic of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person."  
C/ Jorge Juan, 6  
www.aepd.es  
28001 – Madrid  
sedeagpd.gob.es
14/23  
It should be noted that online identifiers, such as IP addresses or information stored in cookies, can commonly be used to identify a user, especially when combined with other similar types of information. This is illustrated in Recital 30 of the GDPR, which states that the assignment of online identifiers such as IP addresses and cookie identifiers to natural persons or their devices can "leave traces that, particularly when combined with unique identifiers and other information received by the servers, can be used to create profiles of natural persons and identify them."  
The CJEU has already stated that IP addresses are personal data (see Case C-597-19, paragraph 102 and C-582/14, paragraph 49). The IP address does not lose its nature as personal data simply because the means of identification reside with third parties. Furthermore, the case in question is very different, as the IP address can be combined with other elements.  
Regarding unique identifiers, they are intended to differentiate individuals and make them identifiable. These identifiers can also be combined with other information, such as the address of the visited website, metadata related to the browser and operating system, the time and data related to the website visit, and the IP address. This combination of information further differentiates individuals.  
That is to say, the combination of several elements may allow for the individual identification of visitors to the "Justiciapp" website, where Google Analytics is implemented, regardless of whether these visitors can be identified through unique identifiers, without the need for such data combination.  
If the contrary were decided, the scope of the right to data protection, guaranteed by Article 8 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, would be undermined, as it would allow companies to specifically select individuals along with personal information (for example, when visiting a specific website) while simultaneously denying them any right to protection against such singling out. Such a restrictive opinion that would undermine the level of protection for individuals is also not in line with the jurisprudence of the Court of Justice of the European Union (hereinafter, CJEU), which has repeatedly ruled that the scope of the GDPR must be understood very broadly (see, for example, Judgment C-439/19, paragraph 61).  
For all these reasons, it must be considered that the data in question are personal data within the meaning of Article 4.1 of the GDPR.  
All of those personal data collected through a website that integrates the Google Analytics function are transferred to Google's servers in the United States. Moreover, in the terms accepted by the holder of a website that incorporates this function, it is contemplated that Google may store and process personal data in any country where Google or any of its data processors maintain facilities. It is known that Google has facilities in the U.S.

This mandatory information is related to the protection system established for international transfers in Articles 44 and following of the GDPR.  
Article 44 establishes a general principle, according to which data may only be exported if, on the one hand, the processing subject to the transfer is lawful and complies with the provisions of the GDPR and, on the other, if it meets the conditions established in Chapter V of the same legal text (Articles 44 to 50).  
Article 45 of the GDPR establishes, as a main rule, that a transfer of personal data may be carried out if the recipient country, territory, or international organization guarantees an adequate level of protection recognized by an adequacy decision issued by the European Commission.  
In the absence of such an adequacy decision, Article 46 of the GDPR authorizes transfers if the data controller or processor has provided adequate safeguards, materialized through the instruments referred to in the same article.  
Among the safeguards that data controllers can offer in accordance with the provisions of this Article 46 of the GDPR are the signing of standard contractual clauses approved by the European Commission. However, it should be noted that the CJEU has stated that there are situations in which these clauses do not constitute a sufficient means to ensure the effective protection of personal data transferred to a third country and may require the adoption of complementary measures.  
The next scenario contemplated by the GDPR, in the absence of an adequacy decision and adequate safeguards, is to allow transfers if any of the conditions set forth in Article 49.1 of the GDPR are met.  
If none of the circumstances provided for in the preceding articles were to occur, the carrying out of international transfers of personal data would not be covered, and in such case, the use of the Google Analytics service would be inappropriate.
