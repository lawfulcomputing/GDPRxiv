LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the functions conferred on each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of the GDPR and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Presidency of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to resolve these investigative proceedings.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  

II  
Preliminary Issues  
In this case, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, since MSGI carries out, among other processes, the collection, registration, organization, storage, modification, consultation, and use of personal data of individuals, such as names, surnames, postal addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, and bank details of the owners of the Community ***DIRECCIÓN.1, among others.  
The responding party carries out this activity in its capacity as a data processor, as it processes this personal data on behalf of the Community of Owners ***DIRECCIÓN.1, which is responsible for this processing in question, all in accordance with the aforementioned Article 4.8 of the GDPR.  
In the case of communities of owners, the legitimacy for the processing of personal data of the community members is based on the fulfillment of a legal obligation established in Law 49/1960, of July 21, on horizontal property (LPH).  
According to the provisions of Article 28.3 of the GDPR, the relationship between the community of owners – responsible – and the administrator – data processor – shall be governed by a contract or legal act with the minimum content established by that provision.  
This data processing agreement was signed by both parties on 01/05/2023 and is provided by the responding party, on 03/07/2024 with entry registration number REGAGE24e00049757032, along with the written response to the information request from this Agency, as Document No. 2.  

III  
Legal Assessment of the Claimed Facts  
Article 5 "Principles relating to processing" of the GDPR states:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
(...)  
d) accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; every reasonable step must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate are erased or rectified without delay (‘accuracy’);”  
For its part, Article 4 of the LOPDGDD provides:  
“1. In accordance with Article 5.1.d) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the data shall be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date.
6/8  
2. For the purposes set forth in Article 5.1.d) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the data controller shall not be held liable, provided that it has taken all reasonable measures to ensure the prompt deletion or rectification of the inaccuracy of personal data, with respect to the purposes for which they are processed, when the inaccurate data:  
a) Were obtained by the controller directly from the data subject.  
b) Were obtained by the controller from a mediator or intermediary in cases where the applicable regulations in the sector to which the data controller belongs established the possibility of intervention by a mediator or intermediary who collects the data of the affected individuals in their own name for transmission to the controller. The mediator or intermediary shall assume the responsibilities that may arise in the event of communication to the controller of data that do not correspond to those provided by the affected individual.  
c) Were processed by the controller after having been received from another controller by virtue of the exercise by the affected individual of the right to data portability in accordance with Article 20 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and the provisions of this organic law.  
d) Were obtained from a public register by the controller.  
These provisions enshrine the principle of "accuracy," which imposes on the data controller the obligation to verify the accuracy of the data of the data subject through the implementation of appropriate measures and indicates the circumstances in which, in their case, the controller receiving the personal data shall not be held liable for their inaccuracy.  
In this case, the complaint originated due to the notification by MSGI, in its capacity as property administrator, of communications related to a Community of Property Owners to a third party different from the interested party (the complainant). This is due to the inaccuracy of the contact data provided by the previous property administrator to MSGI. The information transfer sheet from the previous property administrator indicated, as the main contact email, the email address of that third party and, as secondary, the email address of the complainant.  
It is also worth noting that this third party was listed in the records of the Community of Property Owners as the owner of the same property as the complainant or, at least, linked to it. The email addresses of both the complainant and this third party were associated with the same property, with the circumstance that the address of the third party was marked as the main contact email.  
On the other hand, according to the documentation in the file, it is not evidenced that the responding entity was aware of the inaccuracy of the personal data of the complainant before the latter sent their response to the MSGI service quality questionnaire.
