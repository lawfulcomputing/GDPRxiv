From the actions taken by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following:  
BACKGROUND  
FIRST: On 30/09/22, Mr. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection. The complaint was directed against the entity CHATWITH.IO WORLDWIDE, S.L. with CIF B88184239, owner of the website ***URL.1 (the complained party), for the alleged violation of data protection regulations: Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 27/04/16, regarding the Protection of Natural Persons with regard to the Processing of Personal Data and the Free Movement of such Data (GDPR) and Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), and against Law 34/2002, of July 11, on Information Society Services and Electronic Commerce (LSSI).  
The reasons on which the complaint was based are:  
“The company IURIS MARKETING S.L. (currently CHATWITH.IO WORLDWIDE S.L.) is the owner of the website ***URL.1.  
The complained party processes the personal data of visitors accessing the website, obtaining them both directly from the interested parties through information requests when they attempt to contact the lawyers advertised on the page, and through the use of cookies that are installed on the visitor's computer when consulting the website.  
This is reported by the data controller itself through a message that appears when accessing iurisnow.com, in a pop-up window that overlaps the main window and that under the title “Privacy and Transparency” informs the user that both the website owner and its partners carry out the processing of the personal data of the interested parties.  
Specifically, it is indicated in the first part of the informative text that “We and our partners use cookies to Store or access information on a device. III.-1  
On the use of dark patterns of overloading and skipping  
According to the complainant, the data controller uses dark patterns of overloading and skipping in the design of the user interface through which the privacy options that will apply to the processing of the visitor's data on the website are configured, specifically when the interested party expresses their opposition to the processing of data based on the legitimate interest of the controller and third parties, which they refer to as "partners".  
The term "dark patterns" refers to interfaces or user experience implementations designed to influence the behavior and decisions of individuals in their interaction with websites, apps, and social networks, in such a way that they make decisions potentially harmful to the protection of their personal data.  
As stated in Recital (39) of the GDPR:  
All processing of personal data must be lawful and fair. For natural persons, it must be entirely clear that personal data concerning them are being collected, used, consulted, or otherwise processed, as well as the extent to which such data are or will be processed.  
The principle of transparency requires that all information and communication relating to the processing of such data be easily accessible and easy to understand, and that plain and clear language be used. This principle particularly refers to the information provided to data subjects about the identity of the data controller and the purposes of the processing, as well as additional information to ensure fair and transparent processing with respect to the affected natural persons and their right to obtain confirmation and communication of the personal data concerning them that are subject to processing, (...).  
In this regard, Article 5 of the GDPR outlines the "Principles relating to processing" and in paragraph 1.a) establishes that:
21/31  
Personal data shall be processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency."  
In application of the principle of fairness established in Article 5.1.a, data controllers must ensure that dark patterns are not employed, at least, in relation to decisions regarding the processing of their personal data.  
The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) adopted for public consultation its "Guidelines on dark patterns in social media interfaces, How to recognize and avoid them." These guidelines, like the AEPD's guide, take Article 5.1.a of the GDPR as a starting point to assess when a design pattern in a user interface corresponds to a dark pattern.  
Dark patterns can present themselves to the user in various processing operations, such as during the registration or sign-up process on a social network, when logging in, or in other scenarios such as in the configuration of privacy options, in cookie banners, during the exercise of rights, in the content of a communication informing about a personal data breach, or even when attempting to unsubscribe from the platform.  
According to the EDPB Guidelines, dark patterns can be classified into the following categories:  
1. Overloading: This consists of presenting too many options to the person who has to make decisions, which ultimately generates fatigue for the user, who ends up sharing more personal information than desired. The most common techniques to produce this overload fatigue are repeatedly showing questions, creating privacy mazes, and displaying too many options.  
2. Skipping: This involves designing the interface or user experience in such a way that the user does not think about certain aspects related to the protection of their data, or that they forget them.  
3. Stirring: This appeals to the emotions of users or uses visual nudges in the form of effects to influence decisions.  
4. Hindering: This aims to create obstacles so that the user cannot easily perform certain actions. This is done through techniques such as placing privacy settings in inaccessible areas, making it very complicated to reach them, or providing misleading information about the effects of certain actions.  
5. Fickle: The interface presents an unstable and inconsistent design that does not allow the user to perform the desired actions.  
6. Left in the dark: Information or privacy configuration options are hidden or presented unclearly using erratic language, contradictory, or ambiguous information.
