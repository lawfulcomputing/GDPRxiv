RESOLUTION OF FILE OF ACTIONS  
From the actions carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On December 1, 2022, a complaint was filed by A.A.A. with NIF ***NIF.1 (hereinafter, the complainant) with entry registration number REGAGE22e00055079621 before the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against CTT EXPRESSO - SERVIÇOS POSTAIS E LOGISTICA SA SUCURSAL EN ESPAÑA with NIF W1000101D (hereinafter, the respondent). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
- In their writing, the complainant states that they were the recipient of a package, with the respondent being responsible for its delivery. On November 29, 2022, they received a notification about a supposed failed delivery attempt of the mentioned package due to their absence, an assertion that the complainant denies.  
- In light of this circumstance, they contacted the respondent, who assured them that the package would be delivered the following day. However, on the same day, they received an email notification from the respondent, claiming that the package had been delivered.  
- After this situation, the complainant contacted the respondent again, who admitted that the delivery person had signed for the receipt in the name of the recipient to avoid delays in delivery, given the entity's commitment to deliver within a maximum period of 24 hours and to avoid, in this way, internal problems.  
- The actual delivery of the package took place the following day, after the complainant appeared at the respondent's offices, requested a complaint form, and requested the presence of the Local Police.  
- The complainant argues that there has been an improper use of their personal data, as it has been processed to confirm a delivery that was not made until the following day.  
For evidentiary purposes, a copy of the various communications received regarding the shipment of the package, as well as the complaint form submitted by the complainant, is attached to the complaint submitted.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the functions conferred to each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR) and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to resolve these investigative actions.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Principles relating to processing  
Letter d) of Article 5.1 of the GDPR advocates:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
a) processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject (‘lawfulness, fairness, and transparency’);  
b) collected for specified, legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes; in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1, further processing of personal data for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes, or statistical purposes shall not be considered incompatible with the initial purposes (‘purpose limitation’);  
c) adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’);  
d) accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; all reasonable steps must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate with respect to the purposes for which they are processed are erased or rectified without delay (‘accuracy’);  
e) kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes of the processing of personal data; personal data may be stored for longer periods insofar as they are processed solely for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes, or statistical purposes, in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1, without prejudice to the application of appropriate technical and organizational measures required by this Regulation to protect the rights and freedoms of the data subject (‘storage limitation’);  
f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, by applying appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).”  
III  
Lawfulness of processing  
Similarly, Article 6 of the GDPR develops the lawfulness of processing:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;

IV  
Conclusion  
In the case at hand, the incident reported by the respondent, consisting of the carrier simulating the delivery of the package despite not having carried it out, although it constitutes a bad practice and a violation of the company's internal procedures, does not render the processing of personal data unlawful in the terms indicated by the GDPR.  
Thus, the processing of personal data (identifying data and address of the recipient) remained within the necessary framework for the execution of the delivery contract. Therefore, the original purpose of the data processing was not altered, regardless of whether the delivery ultimately did not take place effectively.  
Similarly, there is no indication of any additional misuse of the personal data of the respondent. The investigative actions indicate that the data were not used for any other purpose nor were they inappropriately shared with third parties. This implies that, aside from the incident of the non-delivery confirmation, there was no other processing of data distinct from that purpose.  
In conclusion, although the simulation of the delivery constitutes a misconduct on the part of the carrier, such actions do not necessarily imply that the processing of personal data by the company was unlawful under the GDPR. The legal basis for the processing of the data (fulfillment of a contract) remained intact, even though the effective delivery did not take place, and on the other hand, the company acted diligently in addressing the incident once it was reported.  
Furthermore, it is pertinent to highlight that, throughout the process, the package remained within the postal operator's distribution network, with no loss of the shipment occurring, thereby mitigating any potential risk regarding the personal data of the claimant. This internal management demonstrates that the principle of confidentiality of personal data was not violated, as the integrity of the package and the recipient's information were kept protected at all times.  
Therefore, based on the information provided in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found to substantiate the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, all without prejudice to any subsequent actions that this Agency may carry out, applying the investigative and corrective powers it holds.  
Thus, as it has not been possible to attribute responsibility for the processing as indicated, by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, IT IS AGREED:
