RESOLUTION OF RECONSIDERATION APPEAL  
Examined the reconsideration appeal filed by CAIXABANK PAYMENTS & CONSUMER EFC, EP, S.A.U. (hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated January 30, 2023, and based on the following:  
FACTS  
FIRST: On January 30, 2023, a resolution was issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202209339, by virtue of which CAIXABANK PAYMENTS & CONSUMER EFC, EP, S.A.U., with NIF A08980153, was imposed a fine of 70,000 euros (seventy thousand euros) for an infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR. This resolution, which was notified to the appellant on January 30, 2023, was issued following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (LOPDGDD), and subsidiarily under Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), regarding the processing of sanctioning procedures.  
SECOND: As proven facts of the aforementioned sanctioning procedure, PS/00482/2022, the following were recorded:  
1. The claimant states that Caixabank Payments & Consumer EFC, EP, S.A.U., through collection companies, is demanding payment of a debt that was annulled by Judgment 66/2019 of the Court of First Instance No. 1 of Inca, ordinary trial 262/20218, dated May 13, 2019.  
2. It is recorded in the letter from the CaixaBank Group, dated December 24, 2020, that they verified the nullity of the contract, and therefore have given orders to cease the collection claims that were being received.  
3. The claimant provides a screenshot of the SMS received from the collection company on behalf of the defendant, dated July 23, 2021, demanding payment of the debt.  
4. It is recorded that the entity being sued in the procedure declaring the radical nullity of the "Visa Classic Club Ahora" card contract is the entity CaixaBank Payments, E.F.C. E.P., S.A., with NIF A08980153.  
5. It is recorded in the Fifth Legal Ground (substance of the matter) of Judgment 66/2019 of the Court of First Instance No. 1 of Inca, ordinary trial 262/20218, dated May 13, 2019, that <<therefore, the appropriate action is to declare the nullity of the card contract signed on March 6, 2015, between the parties (Visa Classic Club Ahora contract no. ***ACCOUNT.1) due to the usurious nature of the remunerative interest, with the effect that the plaintiff is only obliged to return to the lender the amount received for capital that remains to be amortized. The consequence of this declaration of nullity is that the plaintiff will only be obliged to return to the defendant the capital effectively disbursed, discounting any amount that exceeds said capital>>.  
6. It is confirmed that the claimant paid, in compliance with the aforementioned Judgment, on September 30, 2020, the total amount of XXX,XX€, and that the aforementioned Court, on November 6, 2020, issued to the legal representation of CaixaBank Payments & Consumer the corresponding payment order for that amount (XXX,XX €).  
7. The personal data related to the card contract signed on March 6, 2015, by the interested party, were subject to cancellation/deletion by the defendant.  
THIRD: The appellant filed a reconsideration appeal on February 28, 2023, with this Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  

As the basis for the appeal, the appellant reiterates the arguments presented throughout the procedure and considers that the conduct susceptible to infringement is not attributable to the appellant, and therefore, it cannot be condemned or sanctioned except for facts that can be attributed to it, and that by accepting the appeal, another resolution should be issued in its place revoking the contested resolution, annulling it, and rendering the imposed sanction ineffective.

In response to the allegations presented by the defendant entity, the following should be noted:

In the opinion of the defendant, the opening agreement is null and void due to the defenselessness caused by the fact that the AEPD has set the amount of the sanction in it, instead of merely expressing the limits of the possible sanction; because the aggravating factors have not been justified, and because the opening agreement makes an assessment of the defendant's culpability without the opportunity for the defendant to express themselves on this matter. It also adds that the opening agreement exceeds the content provided for in Article 68 of the LOPDGDD and that, by having set the amount of the sanction in the opening agreement of the procedure, the impartiality of the instructing body has been compromised, which thus knows, before initiating the procedure, the criteria of the body to which the file must be submitted, which in their opinion determines a "clear breach of the principle of separation between the instructive phase and the sanction."

The defendant understands that the rules of Article 85 of the LPACAP do not apply to the present case but rather to situations where the sanctioning norm imposes a fixed and objective fine, and that the application made of this provision in the opening agreement does not respect its literal wording, according to which the amount of the pecuniary sanction may be determined "once the sanctioning procedure has been initiated," thus, the entity argues, "the act of initiation is being assimilated" to the fact that the procedure is initiated.

The arguments put forth by the defendant cannot be accepted.  
The opening agreement complies with the provisions of Article 68 of the LOPDGDD, according to which it is sufficient to specify the facts that motivate the opening, identify the person or entity against whom the procedure is directed, the infringement that may have been committed, and its possible sanction. Article 64.2 of the LPACAP expresses the same sentiment, referring to the minimum content of the initiation agreement. According to this provision, among other details, it must contain "the facts that motivate the initiation of the procedure, its possible legal qualification, and the sanctions that may correspond, without prejudice to what results from the instruction." Therefore, in this case, not only are the requirements mentioned in the cited provisions met, but it goes further, offering reasoning that justifies the possible legal qualification of the facts and mentioning the circumstances that may influence the determination of the sanction.

It cannot be overlooked that Article 85 of the LPACAP - which contemplates the possibility of applying reductions to the amount of the sanction in the event that the offender acknowledges their responsibility and in the case of voluntary payment of the sanction - requires these reductions to be determined in the notification of the initiation agreement of the procedure, which necessarily implies that the amount of the sanction corresponding to the alleged facts must be set in that agreement. This justifies sufficiently that the modifying circumstances of the responsibility are referred to in it, as they directly affect the determination of the amount of the sanction.

Against the thesis defended by the respondent, Article 85 of the LPACAP does not provide that the amount of the sanction is to be determined once the procedure has begun. On the contrary, it is the acknowledgment of responsibility and the voluntary payment of the sanction that must occur after that moment, but not the determination of its amount.  
Regarding the opinion of the respondent that Article 85 of the LPACAP could only apply to cases where the sanctioning norm imposes a fixed and objective fine, and that the application made of this provision in the initiation agreement does not respect its literal wording, it should be noted that the AEPD has been applying Article 85 of the LPACAP in the same manner since the entry into force of the aforementioned law, without the Administrative Litigation Chamber of the National Court, to which administrative litigation can be appealed against its resolutions, ever having ruled in line with the criterion that this entity defends.  
It cannot be accepted either that having indicated in the initiation agreement the sanction that could correspond to the respondent for the alleged infringement is determinative of defenselessness or constitutes a breach of the principle of separation of the phases of instruction and resolution, since this Agency merely complies with one of the requirements set forth in the aforementioned regulations. Furthermore, Articles 68 of the LOPDGDD and 64.2 of the LPACAP also require that the agreement to open the procedure specify the sanction that may correspond.  
Thus, the alleged breach of "the principle of separation of the instructive phase and sanction" that the respondent claims—an assertion that this Agency denies—would, if it existed, be the consequence of the correct application that this Agency has been making of a legal provision, Article 85 of the LPACAP.  
Regarding what the respondent has stated, that having set the amount of the sanction and the modifying circumstances of responsibility that could be appreciated in the opening agreement, they have not had the opportunity to express themselves, we limit ourselves to pointing out that the administrative procedure begins precisely with the opening agreement, and it is from that moment on—not before—that Article 53 of the LPACAP recognizes a series of rights for the interested party, including the one provided for in Article 53.1.e).  
Finally, concerning the nullity defect that, in the opinion of the respondent, the procedure suffers as a consequence of the defenselessness they claim to have experienced, the following should be indicated: First, the respondent does not specify in which section of Article 47.1 of the LPACAP they base the nullity they invoke.  
Second, the invoked nullity of the procedure could in no case be based on section a) of Article 47.1 of the LPACAP, in connection with the supposed breach of the separation between the instructive and resolution phases according to Article 24.2 of the C.E. This is because, according to Constitutional Court Judgments 74/2004 and 175/2005, the principle enshrined in Article 24.2 of the C.E., according to which the one who instructs does not resolve, is not applicable to the administrative procedure, so that in this area we are not dealing with a right of constitutional rank.

And finally, thirdly, in the event that the nullity of the procedure is sought to be based on the reason set forth in section e) of article 47.1 of the LPACAP, it seems appropriate to bring to mind the STC 78/1999, of April 26, which in its Legal Foundation 2 states:  
“Thus, according to reiterated constitutional doctrine summarized in the legal foundation 3 of STC 62/1998, 'the acceptance of a protection appeal due to the existence of violations of procedural norms does not simply result from the appreciation of the possible infringement of the right due to the existence of a more or less serious procedural defect, but it is necessary to prove the effective concurrence of a state of material or real defenselessness' (STC 126/1991, legal foundation 5; STC 290/1993, legal foundation 4). For a defenselessness with constitutional relevance to be considered, which places the interested party outside any possibility of alleging and defending their rights in the process, it is not enough to have a merely formal violation; it is necessary that this formal infringement results in a material effect of defenselessness, an effective and real detriment to the right of defense (STC 149/1998, legal foundation 3), with the consequent real and effective harm to the affected parties (SSTC 155/1988, legal foundation 4, and 112/1989, legal foundation 2)".  
In light of the above, the request of the respondent for the declaration of nullity of the administrative sanctioning procedure in question must be rejected.

The lack of diligence displayed by the entity in fulfilling the obligations imposed by the regulations on the protection of personal data is, therefore, evident. A diligent compliance with the principle of lawfulness in the processing of third-party data requires that the data controller be in a position to prove it (principle of proactive responsibility).  
In accordance with the available evidence, it is estimated that the conduct of the responding party could violate Article 6.1 of the GDPR and could constitute the infringement typified in Article 83.5.a) of the aforementioned Regulation 2016/679.  
In this regard, Recital 40 of the GDPR states:  
“(40) For processing to be lawful, personal data must be processed with the consent of the data subject or on another legitimate basis laid down by law, whether in this Regulation or by virtue of other Union or Member State law to which this Regulation relates, including the necessity of complying with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject or the necessity of performing a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract.”

Sanction  
The determination of the sanction to be imposed in this case requires observing the provisions of Articles 83.1 and 2 of the GDPR, which respectively state the following:  
“1. Each supervisory authority shall ensure that the imposition of administrative fines under this article for the infringements of this Regulation referred to in paragraphs 4, 9, and 6 are, in each individual case, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.”  
“2. Administrative fines shall be imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, as an additional or substitute measure to those provided for in Article 58, paragraph 2, letters a) to h) and j). In deciding on the imposition of an administrative fine and its amount in each individual case, the following shall be duly taken into account:  
1. the nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the processing operation in question, as well as the number of data subjects affected and the level of damage and harm they have suffered;  
b) the intentionality or negligence in the infringement;  
c) any measures taken by the controller or processor to mitigate the damage and harm suffered by the data subjects;  
d) the degree of responsibility of the controller or processor, taking into account the technical or organizational measures they have implemented pursuant to Articles 25 and 32;  
e) any previous infringements committed by the controller or processor;  
f) the degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority in order to remedy the infringement and mitigate the possible adverse effects of the infringement;  
g) the categories of personal data affected by the infringement;  
h) the manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the infringement, in particular whether the controller or processor notified the infringement and, if so, to what extent;  
i) when the measures referred to in Article 58, paragraph 2, have been previously ordered against the controller or processor in relation to the same matter, the compliance with such measures;  
j) adherence to codes of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to certification mechanisms approved under Article 42, and  
k) any other aggravating or mitigating factor applicable to the circumstances of the case, such as the financial benefits obtained or the losses avoided, directly or indirectly, through the infringement.”
