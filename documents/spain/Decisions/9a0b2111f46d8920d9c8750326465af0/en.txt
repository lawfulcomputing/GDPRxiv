II  
In the present case, we proceed to examine the complaint dated 25/04/23, through which the “presence of video surveillance cameras” that may be monitoring a transit path without legal basis for doing so is brought to this AEPD. A special type of path is the "Serventía," which we find in the Canary Islands, Asturias, and Galicia as a point of reference. It constitutes a path or portion of traversable land used by neighboring properties, which runs through privately owned land of each of them, but is not exclusively owned by any of the owners of the neighboring estates who hold the right of way, with the purpose of accessing a public road.  
Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR states the following:  
Personal data shall be: “adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’).  
It should be noted that individuals are responsible for ensuring that the installed systems comply with current legislation, demonstrating that they meet all the requirements established by the applicable regulations.  
The installation of this type of device must have the mandatory informational sign, indicating the purposes and the responsible party for the processing of personal data, if applicable.  
In any case, the cameras must be oriented towards private space, avoiding intimidating neighboring residents with such devices, as well as monitoring their transit areas without justified cause.  
Furthermore, this type of device cannot capture images of public space, as this is the exclusive competence of the State Security Forces and Corps, in accordance with the current LO 4/1997, August 4, which regulates the use of video cameras by the Security Forces and Corps in public places.  
It is worth remembering that even in the case of a “simulated” camera, it must be preferentially oriented towards private space, as it is considered that this type of device can affect the privacy of third parties, who feel intimidated by it under the belief that they are subject to permanent recording.  
Individuals cannot install devices to capture images of public space and/or the transit of third parties, except in cases permitted by the regulations.

From the photographs provided on 11/09/24, it is inferred that one of the cameras is proportionately oriented towards the access door to the garage area, considering the measure adequate for the purpose of protecting the property and its belongings.  
The respondent may install security cameras aimed at protecting their private residence, without needing the authorization of the other neighbors who use the transit path, provided that these are exclusively oriented towards their private property (e.g., access doors, garage entrance, etc.).  
The second camera is installed above an access door to the residence, in such a way that the angle of orientation allows inferring that it is limited to what is necessary for the protection of access to the residence, not being considered invasive of the right of way.  
Article 4.3 of the Instruction of November 8, from the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, on the processing of personal data for surveillance purposes through camera or video camera systems states:  
“The cameras and video cameras installed in private spaces may not capture images of public spaces unless it is essential for the intended surveillance purpose, or it is impossible to avoid it due to their location. In any case, any unnecessary data processing for the intended purpose must be avoided” (*the underlining belongs to this AEPD).  
As a recommendation, it is emphasized that the measure should be proportionate to the intended purpose, and excessive capture of the path (serventía) is not permitted, especially since the residence has a garage, so the angle of the camera should be oriented as close as possible to the door of the garage, avoiding capturing the entire width.  
Therefore, based on the images provided by the respondent, excessive capture of the path (serventía) has not been demonstrated, which is why such an infringement of Article 5 of the GDPR is not confirmed.  
IV  
Upon examining the images provided by the respondent, it is noted that they do not have an informational sign in accordance with data protection regulations, as they only have that of the installing company.  
The informational sign, located in a visible area, must indicate the responsible party for the processing, the purpose of the processing, and how to exercise the rights (e.g., an address to which one can effectively direct inquiries).  
Article 13 of the GDPR “Information to be provided when personal data is obtained from the data subject”  
1. When personal data relating to a data subject is obtained from that data subject, the data controller shall provide, at the time of obtaining such data,

Article 22, section 4 of LO 3/2018 (December 5) states:  
The information duty provided for in Article 12 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 shall be deemed fulfilled by placing an information device in a sufficiently visible location identifying, at least, the existence of the processing, the identity of the controller, and the possibility of exercising the rights provided for in Articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679. An access code or internet address to this information may also be included in the information device (*the bold text belongs to this agency).

VI  
Adoption of measures  
In the present procedure, it is established based on the examined evidence that a device(s) has been installed in an area that is in “conflict” between the parties, which lacks a legally compliant information sign, as it does not inform about the purpose of the system or how to exercise the rights.  
In this resolution, the manner in which the informative sign must be presented to be considered legally compliant has been established, referring to what is stated in the previous legal basis.  

The text of the resolution has established what the infringements committed were and the facts that led to the violation of data protection regulations, from which it is clearly inferred what measures are to be adopted, without prejudice to the fact that the type of procedures, mechanisms, or specific instruments for implementing them correspond to the sanctioned party, as it is the data controller who fully knows their organization and must decide, based on proactive responsibility and a risk-based approach, how to comply with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.  
It is noted that failure to comply with the possible order to adopt measures imposed by this agency in the resolution of the present procedure may be considered an administrative infringement in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, classified as an infringement in its Articles 83.5 and 83.6, which may lead to the initiation of further administrative sanctioning procedures.  
Therefore, in accordance with the applicable legislation and having assessed the criteria for the graduation of sanctions whose existence has been established,  
the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection RESOLVES:  
FIRST: TO ISSUE A WARNING to Ms. B.B.B., with NIF ***NIF.1, for an infringement of Article 13 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 letter b) of the GDPR.  
SECOND: TO ORDER THE FILING regarding the infringement of Article 5.1 c) GDPR, as the commission of the infringement has not been established.  
THIRD: TO ORDER Ms. B.B.B., with NIF ***NIF.1, that pursuant to Article 58.2.d) of the GDPR, within ONE MONTH from the date this resolution becomes final and enforceable, to prove compliance with the following measures:  
- To prove, if applicable, the placement of a legally compliant information sign, duly completed (e.g., by providing a photograph with date and time).
