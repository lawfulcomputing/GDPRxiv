RESOLUTION OF THE SANCTIONING PROCEDURE  
From the actions carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following:  
BACKGROUND  
FIRST: On 08/11/23, A.A.A. (the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
The complaint is directed against the entity ABANCA SERVICIOS FINANCIEROS, E.F.C., S.A. (ASF), with CIF: A28197036 (the defendant), for the alleged violation of data protection regulations: Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27/04/16, concerning the Protection of Natural Persons with regard to the Processing of Personal Data and the Free Movement of such Data (GDPR), and Organic Law 3/2018 of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD).  
In his complaint, he states that his personal data is registered in common credit information systems at the request of the defendant due to a debt with which he does not agree, as he has never been requested to pay nor informed about the possibility of including his data in such files.  
Along with the complaint, he attaches the response provided to him by ASNEF on 27/10/23 regarding the inclusion of his personal data at the request of the entity ASF, where they inform him that after forwarding his request for data deletion to the entity ASF, it has confirmed the existence of the debt and therefore the permanence of the personal data in the file.  

SECOND: On 11/12/23, in accordance with Article 65.4 of the LOPDGDD, the complaint was forwarded to the defendant for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements established in the data protection regulations.  
The forwarding, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015 of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (LPACAP), was received on 11/12/23, as recorded in the acknowledgment in the file.  
THIRD: On 11/01/24, the defendant submitted a response, emphasizing the following points:  
- That on 03/11/21, the complainant signed a credit card contract (Credipago ***REFERENCE.1) with ASF, through which a credit line of a total amount of 5,409.95 euros was granted. A copy of the aforementioned contract is attached.  
- That the aforementioned contract expressly informed about the possibility of communicating personal data to solvency files if the same was breached: point vii) of section 5 of Clause 16 "Basic Information on Data Protection".  
- That the first three receipts issued for collection were unpaid by the complainant (December 2021 and January and February 2022), so ASF assigned the collection of the payment to the company ISGF Informes Comerciales, S.L. (ISGF).  
- That on 14/01/22, ISGF sent the complainant communication ***REFERENCE.2 informing him of the debt and the possibility that the data regarding it could be communicated to solvency files. The unpaid receipts were recovered; however, the remaining receipts issued for collection since 01/03/22 remained unpaid.  
- That on 04/05/22, ISGF sent the complainant communication ***REFERENCE.3 informing him of the debt, and again of the possibility that ASF could communicate the data regarding it to the solvency file.  
- That 60 days after the date of the first unpaid receipt that was not recovered and prior communication to the interested party, ASF notified the credit information file ASNEF-EQUIFAX of the data related to the debt, with the date of registration in the file being 05/09/22.  
- The complainant submitted a complaint to ASNEF EQUIFAX on 18/10/23, in which he requested the deletion of his data from that file, receiving a response from this entity on 27/10/23 in which his request for deletion of his data was denied after ASF confirmed both the existence of the debt and the permanence of the data in the file.  
- That on 12/12/23, after receiving the information request from this Agency, ASF proceeded to initiate the precautionary deletion of the complainant's data in the credit information file.

II.  
Presumption of Lawfulness  
Article 4 of the GDPR defines, in its paragraphs 1, 2, and 11, the terms “personal data”; “processing” and “consent” as follows:  
“1) ‘personal data’: any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘the data subject’); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person;  
“2) ‘processing’: any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction;  
“11) ‘consent of the data subject’: any freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of the data subject’s wishes, by which he or she, by a statement or by a clear affirmative action, signifies agreement to the processing of personal data relating to him or her.”  
For its part, Article 6.1 of the GDPR establishes the “lawfulness of processing” in the following terms:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies: a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes; b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract; c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject; d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person; e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller; f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, provided that…

Therefore, data processing requires the existence of a legal basis that legitimizes it. Thus, Article 6.1 of the GDPR, in addition to consent, establishes other possible bases that legitimize data processing without the need for the authorization of its owner, in particular, when it is necessary for the execution of a contract in which the data subject is a party or for the application, at their request, of pre-contractual measures, or when it is necessary for the satisfaction of legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, provided that such interests do not prevail over the interests or the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject that require the protection of such data.  
Processing is also considered lawful when it is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation applicable to the data controller, to protect vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person, or for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller.

For its part, the STS of 20/12/22 (rec. 2737/22) analyzes the significance of art. 20.1 c) of the LOPDGDD, regarding the requirement of prior payment demand and states:  
“12.- Therefore, the fact that the current art. 20.1.c) of Organic Law 3/2018 does not expressly establish the requirement of prior payment demand does not mean that the regulation of art. 38.1.c of the regulation approved by Royal Decree 1720/2007 opposes or is incompatible with the new legal norm and must therefore be considered repealed. Moreover, the new legal norm contains the mention of the existence of such prior requirement by providing that the warning of communication of the data to the file must be made either in that prior requirement or at the time of entering into the contract. This mention, which did not exist in the previous law, implies that the new legal provision presupposes the necessary existence of such prior requirement, which is one of the moments, along with the celebration of the contract, in which the creditor can warn the debtor about the communication of their data to the delinquent file in case of non-payment of the debt.  
13.- The conclusion of the above is that the prior payment demand, provided for in art. 20.1.c of Organic Law 3/2018, remains exigible, whose function and justification have been expressed by this chamber in numerous rulings (among the latest, ruling 604/2022, of September 14): it prevents the data of individuals who, due to a simple oversight, a banking error to which they are not privy, or any other circumstance of similar nature, have ceased to meet a matured and enforceable monetary obligation from being included in these records, which means that the data of non-payment is not pertinent for assessing their solvency. What is not essential with the new regulation is that in that payment demand it is warned of the possibility of including their data in a delinquent file in case of non-payment, as that warning may have been made at the time of contracting. 16.- In conclusion, we can affirm that under the new legal regime there are three distinguishable obligations: i) The creditor must inform the affected party, in the contract or at the time of requesting payment, about the possibility of inclusion in such systems, indicating those in which they participate (art. 20.1.c], first paragraph, of Organic Law 3/2018, which repeals art. 39 of the regulation approved by Royal Decree 1720/2007, as the latter required that the information be provided cumulatively at both moments). ii) The creditor, or anyone acting on their behalf or interest, must request payment from the debtor prior to communicating their data to the delinquent file (art. 38.1.c of the regulation approved by Royal Decree 1720/2007) and will be obliged to keep sufficient documentation available to the responsible party of the common file and the Spanish Agency for Data Protection that proves compliance with this requirement and the others required by the applicable regulations, in accordance with art. 38.3 of said regulation. iii) The entity that maintains the credit information system with data relating to the non-fulfillment of monetary, financial, or credit obligations must notify the affected party of the inclusion of such data and inform them about the possibility of exercising the rights established in articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 within thirty days following the notification of the debt to the system, with the data remaining blocked during that period (art. 20.1.c], second paragraph, of LO 3/2018). The notification must be made through a reliable, auditable, and independent means from the notifying entity, which allows it to prove the effective execution (art. 40.3).  
It should be noted that what article 20.1.c) of the LOPDGDD requires from the creditor of the debt is the following: “c) That the creditor has informed the affected party in the contract or at the time of requesting payment about the possibility of inclusion in such systems, indicating those in which they participate.” However, the regulations do not specify the form and number of occasions on which they must inform about the possibility of inclusion in the file. It is also not expressly stated that in cases where multiple receipts corresponding to the same credit card contract are unpaid, it is mandatory for the creditor to send an independent payment request for each unpaid receipt. However, regarding this particular issue, it is pertinent to bring up the doctrine of the National Court as recorded in its ruling of 23/07/2010, Rec. 796/2009, concerning the payment request for the debt due to non-payment of credit installments, in which it is stated that “The Chamber considers that we are faced with a single debt, with payment in installments for the convenience of the complainant, a debt of which the complainant knows all the details, including the consequences of non-payment, and, in this sense, a new request is not necessary for the one already made due to the non-payment of subsequent installments,” a doctrine that applies to the present case as the debt arises from the purchase transactions carried out by the claiming party under a single credit contract.
