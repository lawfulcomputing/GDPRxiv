RESOLUTION ON THE ARCHIVING OF PROCEEDINGS  
From the proceedings carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On January 12, 2023, a complaint was filed, with entry registration number REGAGE23e00002228872, before the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against the CITY COUNCIL OF SAN CRISTÓBAL DE LA LAGUNA, with NIF P3802300H (hereinafter, the complained party). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
On November 23, 2022, the complaining party filed a report of workplace harassment against a colleague.  
According to the complaint, during the processing of the aforementioned report, the Operational Procedure for the Prevention and Action in cases of workplace harassment was not followed, as the confidentiality of their identity was not guaranteed. In this regard, it is stated that the established protocol determines that the complaint must be submitted in a sealed envelope to ensure maximum confidentiality, addressed to the Occupational Risk Prevention Unit and submitted to the Human Resources Department, which will initiate the file and be responsible for safeguarding it.  
However, it is stated that not only was the protocol not respected, but the envelope was opened and delivered to ***POSITION.1, who even urged them to withdraw the complaint.  
The complaining party indicates that they have an audio recording of that conversation and that another colleague is a witness to all the events. Furthermore, it is noted that all employees of the complained party are aware of the existence of the labor dispute, causing them serious moral and psychological harm.  
Along with the complaint, they provide the report submitted to the City Council along with the proof of entry registration, dated November 23, 2022.  
They also provide the Operational Procedure for the Prevention and Action in cases of workplace harassment of the City Council, dated May 15, 2010.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), this complaint was forwarded to the complained party for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in the data protection regulations.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the functions that article 57.1 a), f) and h) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR) assigns to each control authority and as provided in articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve these investigative actions.  
Furthermore, article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued in its development and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."

"1. Personal data shall be:

IV  
Conclusion  
In the present case, a claim is made against the City Council of San Cristóbal de La Laguna, as the claimant submitted a complaint of workplace harassment to the City Council and it did not guarantee the confidentiality of their identity since the provisions of the operational procedure for the prevention and action in cases of workplace harassment were not followed during the processing of said complaint. As the only evidence, the claimant provides the complaint submitted to the City Council along with the registration receipt dated November 23, 2022, and the Operational Procedure for the Prevention and Action in Cases of Workplace Harassment of the City Council, dated May 15, 2010, without providing any evidence regarding the facts that motivate the claim made before this AEPD.  

However, the investigative actions carried out have not allowed for the verification of what was stated by the claimant, as they did not respond to the request from the Inspection in which they were asked for the recording of the conversation held with the ***POSITION.1, so the mere presentation of these documents is not sufficient evidence to undermine the right to the presumption of innocence of the respondent.  

On the other hand, the claimant states in their complaint that the protocol established by the respondent for cases of complaints of workplace harassment ("Operational Procedure for the Prevention and Action in Cases of Workplace Harassment") was not respected.

To Administrative Sanctioning Law, due to its specialty, the guiding principles of criminal law apply, with some nuances, but without exceptions, resulting in the clear full validity of the principle of presumption of innocence.  
As the Constitutional Court has repeatedly stated, "the presumption of innocence applies without exceptions in the sanctioning legal framework and must be respected in the imposition of any sanctions, whether criminal or administrative, since the exercise of ius puniendi in its various manifestations is conditioned by Article 24.2 of the Constitution to the play of evidence and to an adversarial procedure in which the parties can defend their positions." (Judgment No. 74/2004, of April 22, from the Second Chamber of the Constitutional Court).  
In this sense, the Constitutional Court, in Judgment 76/1990, considers that the right to the presumption of innocence entails "that the sanction must be based on acts or incriminating evidence of the reproached conduct; that the burden of proof corresponds to the accuser, without anyone being obliged to prove their own innocence; and that any insufficiency in the result of the evidence presented, freely assessed by the sanctioning body, must result in an acquittal."  
The Judgment of the Constitutional Court of 20/02/1989 indicates that "Our doctrine and criminal jurisprudence have maintained that, although both can be considered as manifestations of a generic favor rei, there is a substantial difference between the right to the presumption of innocence, which unfolds its effectiveness when there is an absolute lack of evidence or when the evidence presented does not meet procedural guarantees, and the jurisprudential principle in dubio pro reo, which belongs to the moment of the assessment or appreciation of evidence, and which must be judged when that indispensable evidentiary activity occurs, there is a rational doubt about the real concurrence of the objective and subjective elements that integrate the criminal type in question."  
In short, those principles prevent imputing an administrative infraction when no incriminating evidence has been obtained and accredited regarding the facts that motivate this accusation or the involvement of the alleged infringer in them, applying the principle "in dubio pro reo" in case of doubt regarding a specific and determining fact, which obliges, in any case, to resolve that doubt in the most favorable way for the interested party.  
Consequently, in light of the documentation in the file and in accordance with what is indicated in the previous paragraphs, it is appropriate to archive the present proceedings, as no breach of data protection regulations has been observed, since the established facts do not evidence conduct that falls under the infractions provided for and sanctioned in the GDPR.  
Therefore, based on what is indicated in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found that substantiates the existence of an infraction within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
All of this is without prejudice to any possible subsequent actions that this Agency may carry out, exercising the investigative and corrective powers it holds.
