BACKGROUND  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the claimant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on July 18, 2023. The complaint is directed against UNIVERSIDADE DE VIGO with NIF Q8650002B (hereinafter, the respondent). The reasons on which the complaint is based are as follows:  
The claimant states that he is a professor at the respondent university and that it uses a digital platform called ***PLATAFORMA.1 to inform students about their evaluation and to include reports for those students. He points out that this platform includes the ID number of the professors, which is freely accessible to users of the platform. The claimant believes that this exposure is inappropriate and claims to have requested the anonymization of his ID number from the respondent, without having his request addressed.  
Along with the complaint, an image from the platform ***PLATAFORMA.1 is provided, which shows a list of professors, including the claimant, along with their ID numbers.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  
II  
Preliminary Issues  
In the present case, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, as the responding party carries out, among other treatments, the collection, registration, and organization of the following personal data of individuals, such as: name and surname and national identity document number.  

The responding party carried out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one that determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines “personal data security breaches” (hereinafter, personal data breach) as “any security breach that results in the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration of, or unauthorized disclosure of, or access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.”  
In this case, there is a personal data breach under the circumstances indicated above, categorized as a confidentiality breach, as the ID numbers of various teachers who share a subject or teachers who perform tutoring tasks for TFG and TFM could be viewed through the platform managed by the responding party, exposing, without legitimate justification, their personal data.

“1. Personal data shall be:

its loss, destruction, or accidental damage, through the application of appropriate technical or organizational measures ("integrity and confidentiality").”  
The principle of confidentiality and integrity, as set out in Article 5.1 f) of the GDPR, obliges data controllers to ensure that personal data is processed in a manner that guarantees its confidentiality and integrity, avoiding unauthorized access, misuse, or disclosures to unauthorized third parties. This includes implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect the data against potential breaches, both external and internal. Such measures must be adequate to prevent risks to the rights and freedoms of individuals from materializing and must be reviewed periodically to ensure their effectiveness.  
In the present case, the breach of personal data occurred because the technical and organizational measures necessary to ensure the confidentiality of the ID numbers of all professors sharing a subject or supervisors of TFG and TFM were not adopted, including that of the claimant. It should be noted that the use of the ID number as an identifier on a platform accessible by multiple users significantly increases the risks associated with the confidentiality and integrity of the data, especially when this information is unnecessarily exposed to unauthorized users. The lack of implementation of specific adequate measures would have allowed the breach to occur, thereby violating the principle of confidentiality.  
In conclusion, the respondent has failed to comply with its obligations under Article 5.1 f) of the GDPR by not ensuring the confidentiality and integrity of the personal data of the teaching staff on the platform ***PLATAFORMA.1, allowing unauthorized access to their ID numbers. Although corrective measures have been adopted, these do not exempt the responsibility arising from the breaches identified at the time of the claim.  
From the proven facts, it is evident that there was a lack of implementation of specific technical measures to prevent users of the platform ***PLATAFORMA.1 from viewing the ID numbers of the teaching staff. Although the migration of subjects to the system ***SISTEMA.1 is detailed, this measure has a future implementation date, so it does not immediately resolve the unauthorized access issue. Furthermore, the confidentiality notice included on the platform is limited to a measure of awareness, without establishing technical restrictions that effectively prevent unauthorized access.  
In conclusion, the respondent has violated the principle of confidentiality by not timely implementing technical and organizational measures of all kinds, adequate to guarantee the confidentiality and integrity of the personal data of users on the platform ***PLATAFORMA.1, both prior to the claim and subsequently upon becoming aware of it.

As indicated in the first finding of fact, it has been established in this case that the users' ID card (DNI) was used as an identifier on the student management platform (***PLATFORM.1), used by the defendant, which, as indicated by the same, was launched as a new virtual campus of the institution in the 2020/2021 academic year, dates on which the GDPR was already in force and there was an obligation to comply with Article 25 of the GDPR, so at the time of designing the system, its obligations regarding data protection should have been taken into account.  
The identifier has been linked to the data displayed on the platform (access to the platform is granted with the student and teacher ID cards, which is why the ID cards of teachers and students are shown to teachers who share subjects or are tutors of TFG or TFM).  
Consequently, as configured, the platform used has resulted in the commission of an infringement due to the failure to adequately analyze the risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, nor to foresee or apply from the design the appropriate technical and organizational measures required by Article 25 of the GDPR.  
In this specific case, the failure to foresee these measures from the design allows any teacher to access the ID cards of their colleagues if they share the same subject or are tutors of TFG and TFM.  
For all the above reasons, it is evident that the defendant has failed to comply with the obligation to design appropriate technical and organizational measures, as they should have adopted another solution that did not share personal data, specifically the ID cards of teachers, which are accessible to other users.  
It is considered that the known facts constitute an infringement, attributable to the defendant, for violation of Article 25 of the GDPR.  
VIII  
Classification of the infringement of Article 25 and qualification for the purposes of prescription  
If confirmed, the aforementioned infringement of Article 25 of the GDPR could constitute the commission of the infringements classified in Article 83.4 of the GDPR, which under the heading “General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines” provides:  
“Violations of the following provisions shall be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 10,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an undertaking, an amount equivalent to 2% at most of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the obligations of the controller and the processor under Articles 8, 11, 25 to 39, 42, and 43; (…)”  
In this regard, the LOPDGDD, in its Article 71 “Infringements” establishes that:  
“Acts and behaviors referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those that are contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements.”  
For the purposes of the limitation period, Article 73 “Infringements considered serious” of the LOPDGDD indicates:  
“Based on what is established in Article 83.4 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following are considered serious and shall be subject to a limitation period of two years:  
(…)  
d) The failure to adopt those technical and organizational measures that are appropriate to effectively apply the principles of data protection by design, as well as the failure to integrate the necessary safeguards in the processing, in the terms required by Article 25 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679. (…)
