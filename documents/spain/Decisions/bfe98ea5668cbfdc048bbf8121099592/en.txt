PRELIMINARY ALLEGATION  
- that it has not infringed Articles 5.1 f) and 32 of the GDPR. It dedicates Allegations Two and Three to this issue.  
- it acknowledges responsibility for the alleged infringement of Article 34 of the GDPR, relating to the communication of breaches to the affected individuals. It dedicates Allegation Four to this issue.

Before presenting the reasons why it believes that there has been no infringement of Articles 5.1 f) and 32 of the GDPR, it makes a series of clarifications regarding the nature and dynamics of the attacks that led to the incidents suffered by CCC.  
FIRST ALLEGATION. Preliminary clarifications regarding the security incidents suffered by CCC  
Firstly, regarding the obtaining of credentials, it states that the incidents originated from the acquisition of credentials (username and password) by a third party. The acquisition of these credentials occurred in an environment external to CCC. That is to say, access to this first personal data (credentials) did not occur in databases for which CCC is responsible. In this regard, it points out that the hash function used to protect the credentials is currently considered robust. It provides Document 1 as an explanatory document.  
Secondly, regarding access requests, it indicates that the number of affected accounts reported to the Agency in each of the communicated security breaches corresponds to the number of accounts for which the third party confirmed the validity of the credentials; these are not accounts to which the third party had access, nor did it have access to the personal data of those customers.  
Thirdly, once the third party has confirmed the validity of the credentials, it proceeds to sell them through various channels. It provides Document 2, which includes two screenshots from ***APP.1 showing how credentials from various merchants, including CCC, are offered. In this way, the third party will obtain financial compensation from the sale of these verified credentials, and the third party that purchases the credentials will be able to use them to access the customer area and attempt to commit economic fraud through the use of the chequeAhorro.  
However, it notes that access will not occur if: there is no buyer or the buyer does not use the credentials; if the compromised credentials have been reset; and/or a two-factor authentication has been implemented.  
Additionally, it makes some further clarifications:  
It understands that there has been no unauthorized or unlawful access to 118,895 customer accounts and, therefore, no impact on the integrity and/or confidentiality of the personal data of the holders of those accounts in all these cases.  
It considers that there are only 234 cases in which the integrity of the personal data of customers has been affected; and 973 cases in which the confidentiality of the Affected Personal Data has been compromised.  
The First, Second, Third, and Fourth Breaches affected the CCC application, while the Fifth Breach affected the website.  
(...)  
In relation to the penetration tests (also known as pentests) conducted after the First Breach, it wishes to clarify that these are not measures adopted reactively and that CCC conducts pentests periodically for both the website and the application prior to the incidents. In this regard, it clarifies that certain security measures (gray box) were deactivated for the audits, which is why the recommendations and improvements identified in those audits do not correspond to reality and should be qualified.  
Based on the clarifications made in this First Allegation, it then proceeds to assess the existence of an infringement of Articles 5.1 f) (Second Allegation) and 32 of the GDPR (Third Allegation).

a) As communicated in the First Breach, it has been verified that there were only 234 cases of customers whose personal data were modified. The data that were modified included the email address, delivery address, as well as, in some cases, the phone number and name.

ALLEGATION THREE: CCC adopted appropriate security measures.  
It states that CCC adopted appropriate security measures, without prejudice to the fact that, if Articles 5.1 f) and 32 of the GDPR were deemed infringed (which is not the case), we would be facing a single infringement and that the alleged lack of appropriate technical and organizational measures should be understood as encompassed, as a means of perpetration, in the infringement of the principles of integrity and confidentiality.  
(...)  
Regarding the pentests of ***COMPANY.1, it indicates that CCC did not conduct the pentest reactively after the first breach. The pentests are part of a wide range of technical and organizational measures adopted by CCC to preserve the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the personal data processed.  
The pentests were – and continue to be – conducted periodically for both the website and the application. In this regard, it refers to the service provision contract SOC signed with ***COMPANY.1 (Document 4), which includes a web application security audit service. Additionally, it provides as Document 10 a certificate prepared by ***COMPANY.1 certifying the pentests conducted prior to the First Breach.  
In this sense, it states that it is true that the report refers to alleged "deficiencies in the controls implemented in CCC's information systems." However, it indicates that it must be taken into account that certain security measures (grey box) were deactivated for the audits, so the recommendations and improvements detected in those audits do not correspond to reality and must be qualified. It attaches tables listing the short-term recommendations identified in the pentests and includes a justification for their lack of relevance.  
For all the above reasons, it understands that the technical and organizational measures adopted by CCC were indeed appropriate according to what is stated in Article 32 of the GDPR. II  
Preliminary Issues  
The public limited company Centros Comerciales Carrefour is a company dedicated to the operation of both large retail spaces and establishments located within them and, specifically, is specialized in the marketing in non-specialized establishments, with a predominance of food and beverages, also providing e-commerce services, for which it processes personal data of its customers and employees, understanding personal data as: "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person."  
It carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR: "data controller" or "controller": the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; if the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its appointment may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.  
An identifiable natural person is considered to be one whose identity can be determined, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that person.  
Furthermore, "processing" must be understood as "any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction."  
Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines "personal data breaches" as "all breaches of security that lead to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to personal data."

X  
Unfulfilled obligation. Communication of a personal data security breach to the affected individual.  
Article 34 “Communication of a personal data security breach to the affected individual” of the GDPR establishes:  
“1. When it is likely that a personal data security breach results in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller shall communicate it to the affected individual without undue delay.  
2. The communication to the affected individual referred to in paragraph 1 of this article shall describe in clear and simple language the nature of the personal data security breach and shall contain at least the information and measures referred to in Article 33, paragraph 3, letters b), c) and d).  
3. The communication to the affected individual referred to in paragraph 1 shall not be necessary if any of the following conditions are met:  
a) the data controller has implemented appropriate technical and organizational protection measures, and these measures have been applied to the personal data affected by the personal data security breach, in particular those that render the personal data unintelligible to any person not authorized to access it, such as encryption;  
b) the data controller has taken further measures that ensure that the high risk to the rights and freedoms of the affected individual referred to in paragraph 1 is no longer likely to materialize;  
c) it would involve a disproportionate effort. In this case, a public communication or a similar measure will be opted for, which informs the affected individuals equally effectively.  
4. When the data controller has not yet communicated the personal data security breach to the affected individual, the supervisory authority, after considering the likelihood that such a breach entails a high risk, may require it to do so or may decide that one of the conditions mentioned in paragraph 3 is met.”  
Regarding the first, second, and third personal data breaches, it should be noted that, although Carrefour has not proven that it communicated to the affected individuals in the terms provided in Article 34 of the GDPR, the possible infringement due to its absence must be considered time-barred, by virtue of the provisions of Article 74 of the LOPDGDD.  
As for the fourth and fifth personal data breaches, in this case, it has been confirmed that a communication was sent on June 23, 2023, to 9,202 affected individuals and on September 11, 2023, to 10,959 affected individuals, corresponding to the fourth and fifth security breaches, respectively.

In relation to the content that this communication must have, Article 34.2 of the GDPR refers to Article 33 of the GDPR. Thus, the content is that collected in Article 33, paragraph 3, letters b), c), and d) of the GDPR, which establishes:  
“3. The notification referred to in paragraph 1 shall, at a minimum:  

b) communicate the name and contact details of the data protection officer or another point of contact where more information can be obtained;  
c) describe the possible consequences of the personal data security breach;  
d) describe the measures taken or proposed by the data controller to remedy the security breach, including, where appropriate, the measures taken to mitigate the possible negative effects.”  
In this case, the content of the communication sent does not meet the requirements set forth in Article 33.3, paragraphs b), c), and d), and 34.2 of the GDPR, since it refers to the circumstance that the password has been reset, which, for an average consumer reading the notice, may give the impression that there was a potential for a breach to occur, but does not evidence a loss of confidentiality as occurred.  
The wording of the communication does not adequately inform about the existence of a security breach, does not provide affected individuals with information about the nature of the security breach suffered, the personal data affected, the consequences of the breach, the measures taken or proposed to remedy the breach, measures to mitigate the possible negative effects, contact details of the DPO or another point of contact for obtaining additional information.  
In this regard, it should be noted that Article 34.2 of the GDPR states that the information to the affected individuals shall “describe in clear and simple language the nature of the personal data security breach.”  
Consequently, it is considered that the established facts constitute an infringement, attributable to the investigated entity for violation of Article 34 of the GDPR.
