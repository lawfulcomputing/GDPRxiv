On this aspect, it should be noted that the authorization must be recorded in the corresponding Minutes of the ordinary (extraordinary) Meeting as an Agenda Item that must be submitted for authorization and explanation to the group of attending property owners.  
The Minutes of the Meeting of property owners have probative value regarding the agreements adopted, with the specific formalities and necessary backing to certify what is recorded therein.  
From the perspective of data protection, it is the responsibility of the data controller, in this case, the person who installed the system, to prove that they have all the documentation regarding the legality of the system.  
The purported set of emails to a series of neighbors informing them of their presence in common areas cannot be considered valid documentation for this agency, without prejudice to any considerations that may exist in other branches of law regarding whether such an act could constitute a presumed "agreement" regarding the Community of property owners.  
The presence of the camera(s) in the indicated locations implies an impact on common areas, processing data of third parties, regardless of the informational emails sent to the group of property owners of the building, as they are operational, without the indicated backing for the mentioned processing.  
Therefore, reiterating what has already been extensively reasoned in the proposal phase, the claim for the validity of the aforementioned emails as proof of informed and legal consent regarding the installation of the video surveillance system must also be dismissed.

III  
In the present case, we proceed to analyze the claim dated 20/10/22 through which the "presence of camera(s) directed towards common areas" is reported as not being properly signposted and affecting the rights of third parties who feel intimidated by it.  
The installation of cameras in Communities of Owners must be authorized by the owners, with such agreement recorded in the corresponding Minutes where this possibility is expressly stated.  
For the installation of surveillance services in a community of owners, the votes in favor of 3/5 of the total owners are required, who must also represent at least 3/5 of the participation quotas.  
It is necessary to remember that individuals with such devices are affecting common areas "processing data of third parties" who feel intimidated by the presence of these types of devices, regardless of the consequences that the alteration of community elements may have within the framework of the current LPH.  
That is to say, common areas are all those elements that are part of the property but whose use we share with the rest of the neighbors, as they are part of the Community and not exclusive to each dwelling.  
Ignorance of the law does not exempt compliance, so neighbors (owners) cannot claim their lack of knowledge to misuse the facilities.  
Article 6 of the GDPR (Regulation EU 679/2016, April 27) states:  
The processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
It is worth noting that the installation of video surveillance cameras is an exceptional measure, such that if there are less invasive measures for the protection of the property (e.g., security door, sound alarm, etc.), these should be chosen instead of proceeding to install cameras that also affect common areas without the informed consent of all the owners of the property.  
The first requirement for the processing of personal data to be lawful is that it has a legitimate basis.  
Therefore, it is considered that the respondent does not have a legitimate basis to proceed with the installation of the device(s) for capturing images in community areas, being an initially disproportionate measure, which entails the imposition of a violation of the current Article 6 of the GDPR.

IV  
In accordance with the evidence available in this sanctioning procedure, it is considered that the respondent has proceeded to install a series of video surveillance camera(s) on a community wall without having properly formatted informational signage indicating that it is a <Video-surveilled Area> or indicating, where applicable, the purpose and the controller of the processing.  
Furthermore, the identification of the legal basis is essential to be able to demonstrate compliance with the provisions of the GDPR.  
Article 72, paragraph 1 of the LOPDGDD (LO 3/2018, December 5) regarding the statute of limitations for very serious infringements states that "they shall prescribe after three years" and in particular the following:  
b) The processing of personal data without any of the conditions for lawful processing established in Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 being met.  
The known facts constitute an infringement attributable to the respondent for violating the content of Article 6 of the GDPR, as previously mentioned.  
V  
From the allegations and evidence provided, it is not established that the signs installed in the vicinity of the doors of the apartments indicate the controller of the processing or the way to exercise rights under the current data protection regulations.  
It should be noted that mere viewing of images (not recording) is considered, according to the consolidated criteria of this agency, a <data processing> as it affects an identified or identifiable natural person, which requires the installation of the required information about the Video-surveilled Area in a visible location.  
Article 22, paragraph 4 of the LOPDGDD states: "The information duty provided for in Article 12 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 shall be deemed fulfilled by placing an informational device in a sufficiently visible location identifying, at least, the existence of the processing, the identity of the controller, and the possibility of exercising the rights provided for in Articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679. An access code or internet address to this information may also be included in the informational device."  
The aforementioned facts imply an infringement of the content of Article 13 of the GDPR, as there are no informational sign(s) with an effective address to which one could direct or indicate, where applicable, the main controller of the data processing.

b) The rights of data subjects under Articles 12 to 22 (...).
