SANCTIONING PROCEDURE RESOLUTION  
From the procedure instructed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on 25/09/2023. The complaint is directed against the DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION AND EMPLOYMENT of the GOVERNMENT OF EXTREMADURA, with NIF S0611001I (hereinafter, the respondent). The reasons for the complaint are as follows: the complainant and their spouse applied in June for assistance for the purchase of textbooks and school supplies; the entity responsible for processing the resolution of the aforementioned assistance is C.E.I.P Nuestra Señora de la Soledad (the respondent); on 04/09/2023, the respondent sent a message to the parents of students enrolled in the school with an attached Excel file, informing them that the file contained the resolution of the requested assistance; the complainant states that upon checking the file, they observed that in the tab "3rd Course Non-Scholarship Income Order," the economic data of the families of students who had not received scholarships appeared; according to the complainant, data protection regulations have been violated by revealing the income level of their family unit.  
Along with the complaint, copies of the ID cards and Family Book are provided; the application for assistance addressed to the Department along with the attached documentation.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the complaint was forwarded to the respondent for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
The transfer, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP) by electronic notification, was not collected by the responsible party within the availability period, being understood as rejected in accordance with the provisions of Article 43.2 of the LPACAP on 30/10/2023, as stated in the certificate in the file.  
Although the notification was validly carried out by electronic means, with the procedure being considered completed in accordance with the provisions of Article 41.5 of the LPACAP, an informational copy was sent by postal mail, which was duly notified on 21/11/2023. In this notification, the obligation to communicate electronically with the Administration was reminded, and information was provided on the means of access to such notifications, reiterating that, henceforth, notifications would be made exclusively by electronic means.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
For its part, Article 90.2 of the LPACAP states that: “in the resolution, facts different from those determined during the procedure cannot be accepted, regardless of their different legal valuation. However, when the competent authority for resolution considers that the infringement or sanction is of greater severity than that determined in the proposed resolution, the accused will be notified to provide any allegations they deem appropriate within a period of fifteen days.”  
II  
Obligation breached: Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR  
The reported facts materialize in the sending by the defendant of a message through the Rayuela platform, attaching a file in Excel format that contained the resolution of the requested aid, listing non-scholarship students for 3rd grade along with the economic data of their family units, including that of the claimant, which violates the regulations on personal data protection.  
The defendant processes the data in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means related to it, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
Moreover, the data controller must also implement the technical and organizational measures that ensure the security of personal data when carrying out the processing. And be able to demonstrate compliance with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.  
Firstly, the processing carried out could constitute a breach of Article 5, Principles relating to processing, of the GDPR which states that:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
(...)  
f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, by applying appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).  
(...)”
5/11  
From the documentation in the file, it is concluded that the respondent violated Article 5 of the GDPR, principles related to processing, by enabling access to personal data, including that of the claimant, to unauthorized third parties, through the sending of a message to the parents of students at the center that contained a file in Excel format which included the resolution of aid requested for the acquisition of textbooks and school materials. The document also contained data corresponding to the income level of the families of the students listed.

The claimant indicated in their submission that the provided file contained the economic data of the family units of students who had not been granted scholarships, considering that this violates the regulations on data protection.

In this regard, the aforementioned Article 5.1(f) of the GDPR provides that personal data shall be processed in such a way as to ensure its security, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, through the application of appropriate technical or organizational measures. 

On the other hand, Recital 39 of the GDPR, in clear reference to the principle set out in Article 5.1(f) of the GDPR, states that: “Personal data must be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security and confidentiality of personal data, including to prevent unauthorized access or use of such data and the equipment used for processing.”

The loss of confidentiality poses a risk that may lead to material or immaterial damages for individuals (Recital 85 of the GDPR). In this sense, Recital 75 of the GDPR establishes that: “Risks to the rights and freedoms of individuals, of varying severity and probability, may arise from the processing of data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial harm, particularly in cases where the processing may lead to discrimination issues, identity theft or fraud, financial losses, damage to reputation, loss of confidentiality of data subject to professional secrecy, unauthorized reversal of pseudonymization, or any other significant economic or social harm; in cases where individuals are deprived of their rights and freedoms or are prevented from exercising control over their personal data; …”

Therefore, access by unauthorized third parties to personal data means that such data may be used for unknown, even fraudulent, purposes, resulting in a total and absolute loss of control over them.

In this same sense, Article 28 of the LOPDGDD, regarding the obligations of the data controller within the framework of Articles 24 and 25 of the GDPR to ensure and demonstrate that the processing is in compliance with the GDPR concerning various risks, provides that,

Despite the fact that the respondent has claimed that the reported facts materialize in the sending via the Rayuela platform of a message with an attached file addressed exclusively to the interested parties or applicants for scholarships, it does not consider that sensitive data has been affected.  
Sensitive data or data subject to special protection are those that refer to the most intimate sphere of their holder, or whose improper use may give rise to discrimination or may entail a serious risk to them.  
In any case, the respondent has confirmed the facts in a document dated 12/02/2024 and has stated that upon consulting CEIP Ntra. Sra. de la Soledad, it maintains that:

In accordance with the above, it is considered that the respondent has violated the principle of integrity and confidentiality of the data, a principle enshrined in Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR, an infringement classified in Article 83.5.a) of the GDPR.  
III  
Classification of the infringement of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR  
Article 83.5 a) of the GDPR considers that the infringement of “the basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent in accordance with Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9” is subject to sanction.  
For its part, the LOPDGDD in its Article 71, Infringements, states that:  
“Acts and behaviors referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those that are contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements.”  
Also, the LOPDGDD, for the purposes of prescription, in its Article 72 indicates:  
“Very serious infringements:  
1. Based on what is established in Article 83.5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following are considered very serious and will prescribe in three years: infringements that constitute a substantial violation of the articles mentioned therein, and in particular, the following:  
a) The processing of personal data violating the principles and guarantees established in Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.  
(…)”  
IV  
Article 32.1 of the GDPR: security of processing

a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;

“1. The regime established in this article shall apply to the processing for which they are responsible or processors:

In the case examined, the present sanctioning procedure is based on the fact that the party being claimed, as stated in the facts, has violated the regulations regarding the protection of personal data, particularly the principle of integrity and confidentiality of the data.  
As noted, in accordance with the evidence available, such conduct constitutes an infringement by the party being claimed of the provisions of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR.  
It should be noted that the GDPR, without prejudice to what is established in its Article 83, contemplates in its Article 77 the possibility of declaring the infringement and, furthermore, establishing the measures that should be taken to correct the data processing.
