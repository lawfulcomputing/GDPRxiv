3. On the incompetence of the Spanish Data Protection Agency.  
It states that Orange Bank S.A. Branch in Spain is, as its name indicates, a branch of the French bank Orange Bank S.A., a French financial company established in Montreuil, France. Orange Bank S.A. is 100% owned by Orange SA, the parent company of the France Telecom group.  
It explains that by opening a branch in Spain, the French company, Orange Bank SA, has not modified its legal personality, domicile, or nationality in any way. The branch is an office of the French company. Therefore, for the attribution of responsibility, only the French company can be considered. It is impossible to attribute that responsibility to the branch, as it lacks its own legal personality, making it impossible to accuse it or impose any potential sanction. Legally, any action against the branch is an act executed against the parent company of the branch.  
It considers that the fact of being registered in the Commercial Registry, having a Spanish NIF, and having authorization from the Bank of Spain are merely reflections of the principle of territorial unity, whose effectiveness requires certain tax and regulatory formalities to ensure compliance with the specific rules and competencies of each Member State in those aspects and areas where the competence is exclusive and not a reflection of a common competence.  
In summary, it argues that the AEPD cannot act against the branch in Spain of the French company Orange Bank S.A., because the branch lacks its own legal personality that is distinct from the French company. Any action against the French company Orange Bank S.A., regardless of whether it is notified to its branch in Spain or any other office of the company, can only be carried out by the main supervisory authority.  
That is to say, it considers that only the French CNIL could have acted, as it is the only interlocutor with Orange Bank S.A. regarding the cross-border processing that the company has carried out in Spain through its branch in Spain.  
In conclusion, since the data processing subject to this procedure is cross-border, as the issue has arisen from the activity in Spain of a company of French nationality, Orange Bank S.A., through its branch, the only supervisory authority with the competence to act against Orange Bank S.A. is the main supervisory authority, that is, the one competent in the Member State of its main establishment. The CNIL is the only authority that could have acted against Orange Bank S.A.  
In conclusion, it asserts that the lack of competence and procedure being reported renders the actions taken by the AEPD null and void, as established by Article 47.1 of Law 39/2015.
