LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the functions conferred to each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR) and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve these investigative proceedings.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Preliminary Issues  
The respondent carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of the aforementioned Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
It should be noted that "spock.es is a platform whose mission is to provide small consumers with tools that allow them to manage and reduce their energy costs easily, transparently, and impartially.  
Currently, it conducts collective purchases of electricity and gas, that is, it facilitates its users to join together to negotiate their electricity and gas rates in an aggregated manner. By doing so in a fully automated manner, thanks to the intensive use of information technologies, significant administrative/operational savings are achieved, which benefits the user."  
III  
From the documentation in the file, it can be concluded that there is an absence of the subjective element of culpability necessary in the respondent's actions to exercise the sanctioning power.  
The complainant states in their writing that the spock.es platform promotes collective purchases of electricity and that those interested in participating must provide their personal data; among the requested data is a copy of the ID of the holder of the supply point (CUPS), and although there is a checkbox regarding the privacy policy, it cannot be linked to it, considering that it could violate regulations on the protection of personal data.  
From the documentation in the file, it must be concluded that there are not sufficient elements of judgment to proceed with the opening of a sanctioning file. Thus, contrary to what the complainant has stated, it has been verified that the link provided by them supplies information regarding security policy and legal notice.  
And simulating the registration process, it has been verified that the form requests the following data: name, surname, ID (not a copy), address of the supply, CUPS code, in addition to the mobile number and email address requested prior to the data collection process. Therefore, it could not be established that the process requires a copy of the ID, as the complainant states.

However, in the response provided on 01/18/2024 to the information request made by the acting inspector, in point 2.2 Regarding the registration of a Supply (electricity or gas) we request the following information, it is stated that “We do not require a copy of the ID,” but as previously indicated, this could not be verified.  
At this point, it is necessary to indicate that the Constitutional Court in its Judgment 76/1990, of April 26, regarding the right to the presumption of innocence and the principle of culpability establishes: “There can be no doubt that the presumption of innocence applies without exceptions in the sanctioning legal framework and must be respected in the imposition of any sanctions, whether criminal, administrative in general, or tax-related in particular, since the exercise of ius puniendi in its various manifestations is conditioned by the evidence and a contradictory procedure in which the parties can defend their positions. In this sense, the right to the presumption of innocence entails: that the sanction is based on acts or incriminating evidence of the reproached conduct; that the burden of proof lies with the accuser, with no one being obliged to prove their innocence; and that any insufficiency in the results of the evidence presented, freely evaluated by the sanctioning body, must result in an acquittal.”  
The Judgment of the Constitutional Court of 02/20/1989 indicates that “Our doctrine and criminal jurisprudence have maintained that, although both can be considered manifestations of a generic favor rei, there is a substantial difference between the right to the presumption of innocence, which unfolds its effectiveness when there is an absolute lack of evidence or when the evidence presented does not meet procedural guarantees, and the jurisprudential principle in dubio pro reo, which pertains to the moment of evaluation or assessment of evidence, and which must judge when, if that indispensable evidentiary activity occurs, there is a rational doubt about the real concurrence of the objective and subjective elements that make up the criminal type in question.”  
In short, those principles prevent imputing an administrative infraction when no incriminating evidence has been obtained and accredited regarding the facts that motivate this accusation or the involvement of the alleged infringer in them, applying the principle “in dubio pro reo” in case of doubt regarding a specific and determining fact, which obliges in any case to resolve such doubt in the most favorable manner for the interested party.  
Consequently, the filing of the present preliminary investigation proceedings is appropriate as no breach of data protection regulations has been observed, since the established facts do not evidence conduct that falls within the infractions provided for and sanctioned in the GDPR.  
IV  
Therefore, based on what is indicated in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found that proves the existence of an infraction within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.
