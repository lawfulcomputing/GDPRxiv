2. Report on the operation of IBERSCOT through its application and in relation to aggregation platforms.  
IBERSCOT is a company that provides shared mobility services, currently offering its users the possibility to rent shared motorcycles in the city of Barcelona through its mobile application. (…)  
Additionally, IBERSCOT also offers its users the possibility to check the availability of its vehicles on various platforms (such as the SMOU app, provided by the municipal company BARCELONA DE SERVICIOS MUNICIPALES, SA (B:SM)) and even to contract its services on multiple platforms. Among these platforms is the Reby App (offered by Reby Rides S.L.), which provides an aggregation service to IBERSCOT through its mobile application. This service consists of aggregating the offer of shared mobility services through the Reby App (Mobility as a Service or MaaS platform). In this way, any user of the application can geolocate and rent different shared mobility vehicles (scooters, motorcycles, and bicycles), including the motorcycles from IBERSCOT. (…)  
The operation of the service requires that the user, first, registers on the Reby platform, where they consent for only Reby to process their personal data. Subsequently, if the user wishes to use the services offered by another rental service operator appearing in the aggregator, for example, IBERSCOT, they must register as a user of that operator beforehand.  
To facilitate the registration process of a user of the Reby MaaS platform with IBERSCOT, the user is previously asked to expressly consent, in advance, to Reby communicating certain personal data to IBERSCOT. The ultimate goal of this is to facilitate the registration process for the user interested in accessing a new service within the Reby platform.  
Therefore, after analyzing the registration process that IBERSCOT users follow to register for the Reby mobility service, it is considered that the process already requires the user to consent to the communication of their data to other mobility operators. On the other hand, in the section on obtaining personal data in IBERSCOT's Privacy Policy, as the Agency itself was able to verify, it includes the possibility of receiving data from third-party collaborating companies, such as Reby, always with the authorization of the interested party. (…)

Regarding the security measures implemented to prevent an operator from accessing the data of a user registered with IBERSCOT when that user did not provide their consent for it. In response to this issue, the aforementioned entity informed as follows:  
“The main basis is that the interface with the user (frontend) and the data management platform (backend) communicate securely and ensure security. To guarantee these points, IBERSCOT has a method based on the generation of a session token that is created each time a user logs into IBERSCOT. This session token is generated at the moment of identifying the user and unequivocally associates the user with a company. That is, the session token for the same person across different operators is different.  
The session token is passed as an HTTP header in all requests to the API made by the IBERSCOT app against the backend. The backend checks the validity of the token and rejects the connection if the validation fails. If the token is considered correct, an automatic mechanism injects the user and IBERSCOT company identifier into the context of the HTTP request of the backend.  
All execution flows of the backend will use the injected user and company identifier in the context each time a query needs to be made on the database.  
The context mechanism is built on the native “context” library of the Go programming language. (https://pkg.go.dev/context). Therefore, we have absolute confidence that the contexts are perfectly isolated between different API calls.  
Thus, it is guaranteed that the information related to a user in one company does not mix with another company. For example, when a person in IBERSCOT requests to see their travel history or payment history, the information returned is related to the session token, which intrinsically contains the identifier of the user and company for which information is requested.”

I  
Competence and Applicable Regulations  
In accordance with the functions conferred on each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of the GDPR and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to issue this resolution.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, insofar as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  

II  
Possible Violations  
On 06/11/2020, a complaint was received by this Agency directed against the entities TUCYCLE, AVANT, OIZ, and IBERSCOT regarding the transfer of personal data of users who register in the corresponding mobile "apps" to access the vehicle rental service. This complaint warns that when a person registers in one of the "apps" of these entities and subsequently installs another one, the second recognizes the user and reuses the data provided by the other "collaborating platform," with the aim of "streamlining the process."  
The same complaint highlighted that none of these entities informed the user that their personal data was shared with third companies, which were shared among the mentioned entities.  

This complaint led to the proceedings indicated with the number E/10004/2020, in which, by resolution dated 25/02/2021, it was agreed to archive the complaint in question as the initiation of a sanctioning procedure was not warranted since the complaint had been addressed.  
The archiving of proceedings was agreed upon after following the procedures regulated in Article 65.4 of the LOPDGDD, provided to assess whether or not to admit the complaint for processing. This article states the following:  
“4. Before resolving on the admission of the complaint for processing, the Spanish Agency for Data Protection may refer it to the data protection officer that may have been designated by the data controller or processor, or to the supervisory body established for the application of the codes of conduct for the purposes provided in Articles 37 and 38.2 of this organic law.  
The Spanish Agency for Data Protection may also refer the complaint to the data controller or processor when no data protection officer has been designated nor is adhered to extrajudicial conflict resolution mechanisms, in which case the data controller or processor must respond to the complaint within one month.”

In turn, Article 37 "Intervention of the Data Protection Officer in case of complaint before the data protection authorities" of the LOPDGDD cited in the previous provision establishes:  
“1. When the data controller or the data processor has designated a data protection officer, the affected party may, prior to filing a complaint against them before the Spanish Agency for Data Protection or, where applicable, before the regional data protection authorities, address the data protection officer of the entity against which the complaint is made.  
In this case, the data protection officer shall communicate to the affected party the decision that has been adopted within a maximum period of two months from the receipt of the complaint.  
2. When the affected party files a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection or, where applicable, with the regional data protection authorities, those authorities may forward the complaint to the data protection officer so that they respond within one month.  
If, after this period, the data protection officer has not communicated to the competent data protection authority the response given to the complaint, that authority shall continue the procedure in accordance with the provisions of Title VIII of this organic law and its implementing regulations.  
3. The procedure before the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be that established in Title VIII of this organic law and its implementing regulations. Likewise, the autonomous communities shall regulate the corresponding procedure before their regional data protection authorities.”  
According to the above, the indicated mechanism prior to the admission of complaints filed with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection consists of forwarding them to the Data Protection Officers designated by the data controllers or processors, or to them when they have not been designated, so that they proceed to analyze the complaints and respond within one month.  
Thus, when the actions taken in light of the complaint address the issues raised by the complaining party and the responsible entity adopts the necessary measures to align its actions with the requirements set forth in the data protection regulations, this Agency may decide not to continue the procedure following the admission of the complaint, established in Title VIII of the LOPDGDD, which contemplates the possibility of carrying out preliminary actions and initiating a sanctioning procedure, if applicable.  
This occurred in the aforementioned precedent (E/10004/2020), in which the filing of the complaint was agreed upon, considering the response given by the entity IBERSCOT.

Shortly after the resolution that concluded the previous actions was issued, on 12/03/2023, this Agency received a letter from the CITY COUNCIL OF BARCELONA, which included a technical report dated 29/01/2021 from the Mobility and Infrastructure Management. This letter reports the same factual circumstances already analyzed in the aforementioned precedent, regarding the communication of user data between the different companies providing bikesharing services, for not informing their users of such communications and for not obtaining their consent. A fifth company, Reby Rides, S.L., is also reported for sharing the databases of the records and users without their prior consent.  
Furthermore, the facts that motivated the letter in question occurred in a time period prior to the issuance of the resolution archiving file number E/10004/2020. Specifically, the verifications were carried out on the following dates: 10/08/2020, 26/10/2020, and 01/12/2020.  
Subsequently, a complaint was received by this Agency from A.A.A. on 26/05/2021 against the same entities and Reby, in which the same facts are reiterated, specifically, the lack of information regarding the communication of user data between the different companies, as well as the failure to obtain their consent.  
In both cases, it was deemed appropriate to admit the letter and complaint submitted for the necessary verifications regarding the information received from IBERSCOT.  
Thus, it was agreed to open preliminary investigation proceedings to verify whether the measures reported by IBERSCOT in that precedent were implemented effectively and adequately.  
These actions have allowed for the confirmation that the entities involved shared users' personal data from the beginning of their activity in the city of Barcelona in the summer of 2020, without the consent of the affected individuals and without

The entity Reby, which provided the necessary technological platform for the service to users, has acknowledged that there was only one record per user in the database, associated with their phone number, to which all operators had access. This entity stated that such access was only granted when the user had consented, but also clarified that a user was not allowed to register unless they provided their consent to check if their phone number was already registered with another operator and, if so, for the operator they were registering with to access the only record of that user existing in the database; and this was due to the fact that the software did not allow for defining different users associated with the same phone number.  
Regarding the information provided to users, the Inspection Services verified that initially users were not informed about this "sharing" of data with mobility entities collaborating with IBERSCOT, although the details of the recipients of the information were included in the versions of the IBERSCOT Privacy Policy dated 25/01/2021 and 19/05/2021. The first of these versions predates the resolution of dismissal issued in the file E/10004/2020 and the letter from the CITY COUNCIL OF BARCELONA, while the second coincides with the filing of the complaint by A.A.A.  
Notwithstanding the above, it is recorded in the proceedings that the entity IBERSCOT informed the Inspection Services that, following the requests from this Agency and the modifications made to simplify procedures, as of 16/08/2021 they ceased to check if a user was registered with any of the other collaborating mobility entities and stopped the data communications, with no evidence to the contrary.  
On the other hand, the Inspection Services verified that, on a date after August 2021, the IBERSCOT mobile app no longer collected consents, that during the user registration process consent for data communication to third parties was not required, but only for the data processing necessary for the provision of the service; that its Privacy Policy contained no reference to data sharing with other collaborating mobility companies; and that in its Record of Processing Activities, in the version from August 2021, the entry regarding data communication to "third mobility companies providing their services jointly" disappeared.  
It can therefore be concluded that with the indicated measures IBERSCOT carried out the regulatory adjustments required by this Agency.  
In accordance with the above, this Agency deems it appropriate to agree to the dismissal of the complaints that have led to these proceedings, in relation to the entity IBERSCOT.  
It is also taken into account, (i) that the reported data sharing had ended at the time the complaint was admitted for processing; and (ii) IBERSCOT has not provided service since 16/02/2023, having been verified that currently the IBERSCOT app does not allow registration of new users.  
It is therefore appropriate to archive the proceedings, without prejudice to the Agency, applying the investigative and corrective powers it holds, being able to carry out subsequent actions related to the data processing referred to in the complaint.
