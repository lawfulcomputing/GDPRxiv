Generali argues that the AEPD follows a reverse reasoning: it takes the outcome of the security breach to deduce the lack of security measures, the absence of protection by design, and the lack of a DPIA. LEGAL GROUNDS  
I Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II Termination of the procedure  
Article 85, second paragraph, of the LPCAP establishes that “When the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature or when it is possible to impose a pecuniary sanction and another of a non-pecuniary nature but the impropriety of the second has been justified, voluntary payment by the alleged responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except in relation to the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for the damages caused by the commission of the infringement.”  
Such voluntary payment implies, as indicated in the third paragraph of the same article, the corresponding reduction on the amount of the sanction: “In both cases, when the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature, the competent authority to resolve the procedure shall apply reductions of at least 20% on the amount of the proposed sanction, which shall be cumulative. The aforementioned reductions must be determined in the notification of initiation of the procedure and their effectiveness shall be conditioned on the withdrawal or renunciation of any action or administrative appeal against the sanction.”  
In these terms, taking into account the voluntary payment made and communicated by Generali before the resolution proposal was issued by this authority, the termination of the present sanctioning procedure is carried out in the terms indicated in the dispositive part of this resolution.

Insufficiency of security measures appropriate to the risk (Article 32 of the GDPR)

The principle of data protection by design reflects a fundamental shift from a reactive stance to a proactive one, which is a manifestation of the risk-based approach promoted by the GDPR, emphasizing anticipation and prevention in the management of personal data. Under this approach, the responsibility to protect personal data begins at the earliest stages of planning any data processing activity. This means that the data controller must incorporate measures that ensure protection at the very moment when the processing of personal data is being designed and planned. This proactive approach involves identifying and addressing potential risks from the outset, integrating the necessary safeguards directly into the data processing processes and systems.  
In this regard, when applying data protection by design, all elements that constitute data processing must be considered, including the nature of the data, the purposes of processing, as well as the possible consequences for the rights and freedoms of the affected individuals. The aim is to ensure that data protection principles, such as minimization, purpose limitation, and data security, are effectively and consistently implemented throughout the entire data processing lifecycle.  
This is expressed in the Guidelines 4/2019 of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) regarding Article 25 Data Protection by Design and by Default, adopted on October 20, 2020. In the aforementioned Guidelines, it is stated that:  
“The ‘moment of determining the means of processing’ refers to the period of time when the controller is deciding how the processing will be carried out and how it will occur, as well as the mechanisms that will be used to carry out such processing. Considering data protection by design from the outset is crucial for the correct application of the principles and for the protection of the rights of the data subjects.
