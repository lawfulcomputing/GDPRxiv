LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence.  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 43.1 of the LSSI and Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Presidency of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
The Fourth Additional Provision "Procedure in relation to the competencies attributed to the Spanish Data Protection Agency by other laws" establishes that: "The provisions in Title VIII and its development regulations will apply to the procedures that the Spanish Data Protection Agency must process in the exercise of the competencies attributed to it by other laws."  
II.-  
Statute of Limitations of the Infraction  
The facts stated by the complainant relate to the receipt of advertising emails after having requested not to receive them again, attaching copies of the emails received on 01/03/24; 04/03/24; and 11/04/24 to corroborate this.  
Well, the fact that commercial communications are sent without having been previously requested or expressly authorized may constitute a violation of the provisions of Article 2.1 of the LSSI, which states the following: "1. The sending of advertising or promotional communications by email or other equivalent electronic communication means that have not been previously requested or expressly authorized by the recipients is prohibited..."  
The aforementioned infraction is classified as "minor" in Article 38.4.d) of that regulation, which qualifies as such, "The sending of commercial communications by email or other equivalent electronic communication means when the requirements established in Article 21 are not met and does not constitute a serious infraction."  
However, Article 45 of the aforementioned regulation (LSSI) establishes, regarding the "Statute of Limitations" of infractions, that minor infractions will expire after six months.  
Therefore, according to the provisions of the transcribed article, the infraction must be understood as expired if, more than six months have passed from the date of the events, the initiation of the proceedings has not been notified to the respondent.  
In the case at hand, from the documentation provided by the complainant, it is verified that the last advertising email received from the entity being complained about dates from 11/04/24, therefore, based on Article 45 of the LSSI, the alleged infraction must be understood as expired since the six-month period prescribed as the maximum time for the expiration of the infraction has been exceeded.  
Thus, in accordance with the aforementioned, the Presidency of the Spanish Data Protection Agency
