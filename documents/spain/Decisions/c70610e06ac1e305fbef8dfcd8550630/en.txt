Response to the Allegations Presented by the Respondent  
Before proceeding to respond to the allegations presented by the respondent, it should be noted that Article 4 of the GDPR defines personal data as “any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘the data subject’); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.”

2. The respondent further states that the expression “(…)” does not reveal any health data.  
Health data constitutes a special category of data, according to Article 9.1 of the GDPR, the processing of which is generally prohibited, unless certain exceptions provided for in paragraph 2 of the aforementioned Article 9 of the GDPR apply.  
The GDPR defines in its Article 4.15 health data as “personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, which reveal information about their health status.”  
Recital 35 of the GDPR clarifies this definition by stating that “Among the personal data related to health, all data concerning the health status of the data subject that provide information about their physical or mental health past, present, or future should be included. This includes information about the natural person collected in connection with their registration for health care purposes, or in connection with the provision of such care, in accordance with Directive 2011/24/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council; any number, symbol, or data assigned to a natural person that uniquely identifies them for health purposes; information obtained from tests or examinations of a part of the body or of a bodily substance, including that derived from genetic data and biological samples, and any information relating, for example, to a disease, a disability, the risk of suffering from diseases, medical history, clinical treatment, or the physiological or biomedical status of the data subject, regardless of its source, for example, a doctor or other health professional, a hospital, a medical device, or an in vitro diagnostic test.”  
The Court of Justice (CJEU) interpreted this concept broadly, considering as health-related data the information concerning all aspects, both physical and mental, of a person's health, whether related to their health status or indicating that they are in a situation of partial sick leave.

Article 5.1(f) of the GDPR  
The facts revealed consist of the existence of a personal data breach resulting from the dissemination of personal data of the claimant through the Municipality's platform called Bando Móvil. This allowed unauthorized access by third parties to the personal data of the claimant, specifically, the (...).  
Regarding these facts, the respondent acts as the data controller. As such, it is obliged to demonstrate compliance with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD and to apply the technical and organizational measures that ensure the security of personal data when carrying out the processing, in accordance with the provisions of Article 5.1(f) of the GDPR, which states:  
“Article 5 Principles relating to processing:  
1. Personal data shall be:

f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).”  
In relation to this principle, Recital 39 of the aforementioned GDPR states:  
“[...] Personal data must be processed in a manner that ensures adequate security and confidentiality of the personal data, including to prevent unauthorized access or use of such data and of the equipment used for processing.”  
The documentation in the file provides proven facts that the respondent violated Article 5.1(f) of the GDPR regarding the principles relating to processing.  
The documentation in the file provides sufficient evidence that the respondent violated Article 5.1 of the GDPR, principles relating to processing, due to a personal data breach caused by the publication of information related to the claimant indicating personal data concerning them, without having adequately ensured the confidentiality and integrity of the data.  
In this regard, the aforementioned Article 5.1(f) of the GDPR provides that personal data shall be processed in such a way as to ensure their security, including protection against

unauthorized or unlawful processing and against their loss, destruction, or accidental damage, through the application of appropriate technical or organizational measures.  
The loss of confidentiality poses a risk that may lead to damages, whether material or immaterial, for individuals (considering 85 of the GDPR). In this regard, considering 75 of the GDPR states: “The risks to the rights and freedoms of individuals, of varying severity and probability, may arise from the processing of data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial harm, particularly in cases where the processing could lead to discrimination issues, identity theft or fraud, financial losses, damage to reputation, loss of confidentiality of data subject to professional secrecy, unauthorized reversal of pseudonymization, or any other significant economic or social harm; in cases where individuals are deprived of their rights and freedoms or prevented from exercising control over their personal data; …”.  
Therefore, unauthorized access to personal data implies that such data may be used for unknown, even fraudulent, purposes, resulting in a total and absolute loss of control over them.  
In this same sense, Article 28.2 of the LOPDGDD, regarding the obligations of the data controller within the framework of Articles 24 and 25 of the GDPR to ensure and demonstrate that the processing is compliant with the GDPR concerning various risks, provides that:  
“2. For the adoption of the measures referred to in the previous paragraph, the controllers and processors shall take into account, in particular, the greater risks that could arise in the following cases:  
a) When the processing could generate situations of discrimination, identity theft or fraud, financial losses, damage to reputation, loss of confidentiality of data subject to professional secrecy, unauthorized reversal of pseudonymization, or any other significant economic, moral, or social harm to the affected parties. …”.  
And the Judgment of the Constitutional Court (STC) 292/2000 in the seventh legal foundation states:  
“… it turns out that the content of the fundamental right to data protection consists of a power of disposition and control over personal data that empowers the individual to decide which of those data to provide to a third party, whether the State or a private individual, or which data this third party may collect, and that also allows the individual to know who holds that personal data and for what purpose, being able to oppose that possession or use. These powers of disposition and control over personal data, which are part of the content of the fundamental right to data protection, are legally specified in the authority to consent to the collection, obtaining, and access to personal data, its subsequent storage and processing, as well as its possible use or uses by a third party, whether the State or a private individual.”  
In light of the above, the events that occurred are considered to constitute an infringement, attributable to the claimed City Council, for violation of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR.

In this same sense, Recital 83 of the GDPR states that:  
“(83) In order to maintain security and prevent processing from infringing the provisions of this Regulation, the controller or the processor must assess the inherent risks of the processing and implement measures to mitigate them, such as encryption. These measures must ensure an adequate level of security, including confidentiality, taking into account the state of the art and the costs of their implementation in relation to the risks and the nature of the personal data that must be protected. When assessing the risk in relation to data security, the risks arising from the processing of personal data must be taken into account, such as the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data, which may in particular cause physical, material, or immaterial damage.”  
The liability of the respondent party is determined by the lack of appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an adequate level of security relative to the risk, as it is responsible for making decisions aimed at effectively implementing such measures. In this case, it is evident that the claimed municipality, in relation to the data being processed, does not have adequate security measures in place, regardless of the breach.  

It should be emphasized that the adequacy of the level of security to the risk must be assessed by the controller and reconsidered periodically based on the results obtained, taking into account - among other factors - the risks that may arise from the unauthorized communication of such data. The technical and organizational security measures that must be applied are those relevant to respond to the existing risk, evaluating, among other factors, the state of the art, the costs of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, and the probability and severity risks to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.  
One of the requirements established by the GDPR for controllers and processors conducting processing activities with personal data is the need to carry out a risk analysis of information security in order to establish security and control measures aimed at complying with the principles of protection by design and by default that guarantee the rights and freedoms of individuals.  
The absence of adequate technical and organizational security measures, which has resulted in the dissemination of the personal data of the claimant on the public platform "Bando Móvil" accessible to all citizens of the aforementioned municipality, constitutes a violation of the provisions established in Article 32 of the GDPR.

IX  
Classification of Article 9 of the GDPR  
The infringement of Article 9 of the GDPR, for which the respondent is held responsible, is classified in Article 83.5 of the GDPR:  
“Violations of the following provisions shall be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an undertaking, an amount equivalent to a maximum of 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) The basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9.”  
In turn, the LOPDGDD in its Article 72.1.e) qualifies as a very serious infringement, for the purposes of prescription, “The processing of personal data of the categories referred to in Article 9 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 without any of the circumstances provided for in that provision and in this Organic Law.”

XI
Adoption of measures
Furthermore, the aforementioned Article 77 of the LOPDGDD provides that the resolution issued may establish the measures that the infringing entity must adopt to cease the infringing conduct, correct the effects of the infringement that has been committed, and carry out the necessary adjustments, in this case, to the requirements set forth in Articles 5.1.f), 9, and 32 of the GDPR, also having to provide evidence of compliance with what is required.
Thus, the responsible entity may be required to adjust its actions to the personal data protection regulations, to the extent expressed in the previous Legal Grounds.
In this act, the infringements committed and the facts that give rise to the violation of data protection regulations are established, from which it is clearly inferred what measures are to be adopted, without prejudice to the fact that the type of procedures, mechanisms, or specific instruments for implementing them corresponds to the sanctioned party, as it is the data controller who fully knows its organization and must decide, based on proactive responsibility and a risk-based approach, how to comply with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.
During the processing of the sanctioning procedure, the summoned party has communicated to this AEPD the corrective measures it plans to adopt in light of the security and confidentiality incidents that are the subject of the file, among which are security measures for facilities, equipment, and services (email, web applications, and browsing), user identification and authentication, or training and awareness.
The validity of these measures is not questioned, but they are declared insufficient to the extent that none of them contemplate the establishment of mechanisms aimed at preventing incidents like the one analyzed in this case. Beyond the elimination of the Mobile Bulletin that is the subject of the complaint and the commitment to comply with personal data protection regulations, explicitly stated, there is no measure specifically aimed at preventing the dissemination of personal data through the platforms of the City Council.
Consequently, it is appropriate to require the summoned party to adopt, within three months from the date of the enforceability of this sanctioning resolution, all types of technical and organizational measures, including appropriate security measures to ensure an adequate level of security relative to the risk, in such a way that compliance with the provisions of Articles 5.1.f), 9, and 32 of the GDPR is ensured, so that the principles of integrity and confidentiality and adequate security of personal data are respected.
Among the measures to be adopted, the establishment of mechanisms prior to the dissemination of personal data may be considered so that such dissemination does not occur without a legal basis that legitimizes it.
It is warned that failure to comply with the order to adopt measures imposed by this agency in the sanctioning resolution may be considered an administrative infringement in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, classified as an infringement in its Articles 83.5 and 83.6, which may motivate such conduct to open a further sanctioning administrative procedure.
