From the proceeding initiated by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the claimant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on November 15, 2022. The complaint is directed against HOTEL TURIA, S.A. with NIF A46388245 (hereinafter, the respondent). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The claimant states that on November 5, 2022, they requested via email access to the recordings from the video surveillance system of the parking lot where their vehicle was parked, between November 2 and 5, 2022, as well as the limitation of its processing for preservation purposes. On November 10, 2022, they received a response indicating that they did not retain images from that period and contradicted themselves by stating that the cameras are only for viewing and surveillance, although in the same text they mentioned that sometimes the recordings are made available to the court, which indicates that there would indeed be a processing of image preservation. The claimant believes that their request has not been adequately addressed.  
Along with the notification, an email is provided in which they exercise their requests for access and limitation and a copy of the response received.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), this complaint was forwarded to the respondent, so that they could analyze it and inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
The forwarding of the complaint, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was received by the responsible party on January 2, 2023, as noted in the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.

II  
Response to the Allegations  
In response to the written allegations of the responding party, we proceed, first of all, to address them.  
Regarding their first allegation concerning the claims presented during the transfer of the complaint, we can only reiterate what has already been stated regarding the impossibility of considering that this implies a non-violation of the rights of access and limitation exercised. The regulations are clear in this regard: upon receiving a request for the exercise of rights, the responsible entity must evaluate it individually to determine its specific content, taking into account that the obligation to facilitate the exercise of the rights of the interested parties, as provided in Article 12.2 of the GDPR, entails that the responsible party grants the broadest possible effect to the exercised right.  
This evaluation must first establish the personal data referred to in the request. As a rule, it should be understood that the request refers to all data, unless the interested party clearly specifies otherwise, and this limitation, included in the request, does not raise doubts about the true intention of the interested party that would require the responsible party to interpret it.  
In determining the subject matter referred to in the request, the context in which it occurs, the specific circumstances of the case, and any information that the interested party includes in it may be considered. The aim is for the evaluation carried out by the responsible party regarding the content of the request for the exercise of rights to be clear and unequivocal, always keeping in mind that an average citizen does not know the regulations in detail nor can adequately assess them, given their complexity.  
If the request is expressed in very general terms or its subject matter cannot be determined for any reason, the responsible party may not apply limits or restrictions to the exercised right and will be obliged, before responding, to request additional information from the interested party to specify the information or treatments referred to in the request. The evaluation carried out by the responsible entity must always reflect the situation at the time it received the request, thus ensuring that the response aligns with the information available at that time.  
Regarding the response time, the responsible party is also obliged to establish procedures that enable requests to be addressed without undue delay, adapting these procedures to the circumstances of the processing. When data is retained for a short period, the attention to rights must occur before the data is deleted, preventing this deletion from taking place during the processing of the request. In these cases, if it is necessary to request additional information from the interested party regarding the subject of their complaint, the scheduled deletion of the data must be taken into account, and appropriate measures must be adopted to ensure that it does not occur before addressing the right.  
Thus, if it is not possible to send the required response to the interested party before the scheduled deletion of the personal data, the data must be retained for longer.

That is to say, since the claimant had exercised their rights of access and limitation regarding the images from the parking cameras, the images should have been, at the very least, preserved in order to determine whether or not the requests should be addressed during the time necessary for that purpose. Furthermore, this party finds it noteworthy that up to three different preservation periods for the images have been communicated by the responding party. Indeed, although the claimant was initially informed that the images were not preserved, it was later indicated to this agency that they were preserved for a period of 24/48 hours, only to finally state in their written allegations that the preservation period of 72 hours has been extended to 10 days.  
In this regard, it is recalled what is established in Article 22 of the LOPDGDD, particularly its third paragraph, according to which “Data shall be deleted within a maximum period of one month from its capture, unless they must be retained to prove the commission of acts that violate the integrity of persons, property, or facilities. In such cases, the images must be made available to the competent authority within a maximum period of seventy-two hours from the time knowledge of the existence of the recording is obtained.”  
That is to say, the applicable regulation establishes the obligation to delete surveillance images within a maximum period of one month, without prejudice to the exceptions determined therein.  
On the other hand, regarding the information provided by the responding party concerning the measures it has implemented in order to comply with data protection regulations and the special importance it places on ensuring the protection of personal data, it is considered that no further comments are warranted.

Regarding the right of data subjects to access their personal data "concerning them" from the data controller, Articles 12 and 15 of the GDPR, as well as Articles 12 and 13 of the LOPDGDD, are of special application, all of which must be interpreted within the framework established in Recitals 59 to 64 of the GDPR.  
Article 15 of the GDPR, which regulates the right of access of the data subject, establishes the following:  
"1. The data subject shall have the right to obtain from the data controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning them is being processed and, if so, the right of access to the personal data and to the following information:  
a. the purposes of the processing;  
b. the categories of personal data concerned;  
c. the recipients or categories of recipients to whom the personal data have been or will be disclosed, in particular recipients in third countries or international organizations;  
d. where possible, the envisaged period for which the personal data will be stored or, if not possible, the criteria used to determine that period;  
e. the existence of the right to request from the controller rectification or erasure of personal data or restriction of processing of personal data concerning the data subject, or to object to such processing;  
f. the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority;  
g. when the personal data have not been obtained from the data subject, any available information as to their source;  
h. the existence of automated decision-making, including profiling, referred to in Article 22, paragraphs 1 and 4, and, at least in those cases, meaningful information about the logic involved, as well as the significance and the envisaged consequences of such processing for the data subject.  
2. When personal data are transferred to a third country or to an international organization, the data subject shall have the right to be informed of the appropriate safeguards pursuant to Article 46 relating to the transfer.  
3. The data controller shall provide a copy of the personal data undergoing processing. The controller may charge a reasonable fee based on the administrative costs for any further copies requested by the data subject. When the data subject makes the request by electronic means, and unless otherwise requested, the information shall be provided in a commonly used electronic format.  
4. The right to obtain a copy mentioned in paragraph 3 shall not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others."

IV  
Right to Restriction of Processing  
Article 18 of the GDPR regulates the right to restriction of processing, establishing the following:  
"1. The data subject shall have the right to obtain from the data controller the restriction of processing of personal data when one of the following conditions is met:  
a. the data subject contests the accuracy of the personal data for a period enabling the controller to verify the accuracy of the data;  
b. the processing is unlawful and the data subject opposes the deletion of personal data and requests instead the restriction of its use;  
c. the controller no longer needs the personal data for the purposes of processing, but the data subject needs them for the formulation, exercise, or defense of claims;  
d. the data subject has objected to processing under Article 21(1), while verifying whether the legitimate grounds of the controller override those of the data subject.  
2. When the processing of personal data has been restricted under paragraph 1, such data may only be processed, except for storage, with the consent of the data subject or for the formulation, exercise, or defense of claims, or for the protection of the rights of another natural or legal person or for important public interest reasons of the Union or of a particular Member State.

V  
Unfulfilled obligation. Attention to requests for the exercise of rights  
In this case, the claimant exercised the right to limitation of processing and the right of access to their personal data before the respondent, in relation to the images captured by their video surveillance system from November 2, 2022, at 08:30, until November 5, 2022, the same day, November 5, and should have avoided the deletion of these images, preserving them until the resolution of the amicable or judicial claim.  
However, according to the documentation in the file, it is stated in the first response given to the claimant that such images have not been preserved.  
In accordance with the available evidence, it is considered that the known facts constitute two infringements, attributable to the respondent, for violation of the provisions of Articles 15 and 18 of the GDPR transcribed in the previous grounds.  
VI  
Classification and qualification of the infringement  
If confirmed, the aforementioned infringements of Articles 15 and 18 of the GDPR could constitute the commission of the infringement classified in Article 83.5.b) of the GDPR, which under the heading "General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines" states: that violations of the provisions of the rights of data subjects under Articles 12 to 22 are considered infringements (Art. 83.5.b GDPR).  
The known facts constitute an infringement, attributable to the respondent, according to the provisions of Articles 15 and 18 of the GDPR transcribed in the previous grounds.  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations for the infringements, the attributed infringement prescribes after three years, in accordance with Article 72.k) of the LOPDGDD, which qualifies the following conduct as very serious:  
“k) The obstruction or hindrance or repeated failure to attend to the exercise of the rights established in Articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.”
