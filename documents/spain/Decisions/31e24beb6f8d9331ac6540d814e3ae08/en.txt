LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
According to the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  
II  
Response to the allegations presented against the Proposed Resolution  
We proceed to respond to them in the order presented by Vodafone.  
FIRST. - The respondent has acted diligently insofar as it has implemented the necessary processes to correctly identify its customers, with the adoption of technical and organizational measures being an obligation that is not absolute.  
First of all, regarding the assertion that the adoption of technical and organizational measures is not an absolute obligation, it should be noted that in the case at hand, it was the respondent itself that admitted that its own security procedures and policies were not followed. In this sense, assessing whether the previously adapted measures were adequate or not is irrelevant, since these measures were not followed by the respondent itself.  
The adoption of technical and organizational measures presupposes compliance with them; otherwise, the right to data protection is not guaranteed.  
In the present case, we are not sanctioning the existence or absence of technical and organizational measures, but rather the fact that, in this specific case, the processing of the claimant's personal data was carried out without complying with the lawfulness requirements established in Article 6.1 of the GDPR. The infringement of this article confirms that the respondent allowed unauthorized processing of the data, thereby violating the rights of the claimant.

Secondly, Vodafone points out that we are dealing with a third party whose purpose is, through criminal activity, to circumvent such security measures.  
In this regard, Vodafone refers, in its defense, to the set of security measures it has adopted, which are the minimum required of any organization with the characteristics and in the context in which a telecommunications operator operates.  
Regarding this issue, it is important to indicate that the illicit processing of the claimant's data by Vodafone was not due to a third party circumventing the security measures, but rather to the failure to comply with internal security protocols by one of its agents, as the respondent itself has acknowledged. As indicated in the proven fact second, the agent did not adequately verify the request for the duplicate SIM, which facilitated fraudulent access and illicit processing of the claimant's data.  
In this sense, the mere existence of a security policy cannot justify a violation of the principle of lawfulness provided for in Article 6.1 of the GDPR. In addition to existing, these measures must be effective, and the data controller must ensure that they are strictly complied with at all times. Otherwise, the adopted measures lack effectiveness and may lead to unlawful processing, as occurs in the present case.  
Therefore, the issue here does not lie in the level of sophistication of the criminal attack by the third party, but in the failure to comply with the security measures themselves that allowed the unlawful processing of personal data. The respondent cannot exempt itself from responsibility by appealing to the existence of minimum measures when the failure was in the application of those measures, allowing an infringement of the lawfulness of the processing.  
SECOND. - The issuance of a duplicate SIM does not entail access to banking information, passwords, email addresses, etc., of the customers of the respondent, but rather to the mobile and internet services they may have contracted.  
Vodafone states that the issuance of a duplicate SIM does not entail access to banking information, passwords, email addresses, etc., of the customers of the respondent, but rather to the mobile and internet services they may have contracted.  
The respondent states that the issuance of duplicate SIM cards is not sufficient to carry out banking operations on behalf of the holders; indeed, to complete the fraud, it is necessary for a third party to "impersonate the identity" of the data holder before the financial entity.  
However, this assertion does not exempt the respondent from responsibility, since obtaining a duplicate SIM card is a necessary element to access such data and carry out banking operations on behalf of the holders, as stated in the ruling of 05/13/2024, Rec. 0002336/2021, of the Court of Appeal.

National declaring that “(…) in the first phase of this type of fraud, the impersonator fraudulently obtains the access data or online banking credentials of the client, but lacks knowledge of the verification code, the second factor of authentication, to execute any operation. At the moment when they obtain the duplicated SIM card, they also gain access to this second factor of authentication and, therefore, from that moment on, they can carry out any asset disposal acts they wish.”  
Furthermore, the mere use of a SIM card by an unauthorized third party already implies an unlawful processing. It must be taken into account that the possession of a duplicate card increases the risk for its holder, as the impersonator may possess additional data that can lead to more serious consequences, such as the execution of unwanted financial operations, as occurred in the case of the claimant.  
For this reason, providing a duplicate card is a process where the diligence exercised by the operators is fundamental to prevent this type of fraud and violations of the GDPR. This diligence translates into compliance with their own Security Policy to ensure that data processing is in accordance with the GDPR, which in the case at hand, as acknowledged by the defendant, has not taken place.  
Vodafone insists that regarding the degree of responsibility that may be attributed to them, it cannot depend on the actions of a third party that is beyond their control, namely: the security measures implemented by one or another banking entity or even the fact that the affected party has or does not have online banking.  
In relation to this allegation, in addition to what has already been indicated, the degree of responsibility falls within their scope and not that of third parties, noting that the SAN -Contentious Administrative Chamber- of May 5, 2021, establishes that: “On the other hand, regarding the fact that we are facing the fraud of a third party, as we stated in the SAN of October 3, 2013 (Rec. 54/2012): 'Precisely for that reason, it is necessary to ensure that the person contracting is who they really say they are and appropriate preventive measures must be taken to verify the identity of a person whose personal data will be subject to processing.'”

The Constitutional Court has repeatedly declared that the principles of penal law, among which is the principle of culpability, are applicable, with certain nuances, to administrative sanctioning law, as both are manifestations of the punitive order of the State (STC 18/1987, 150/1991), and that objective or non-fault liability cannot be admitted in the administrative sanctioning sphere, whereby the possibility of imposing sanctions solely based on the result is excluded, without proving a minimum level of culpability even in the case of mere negligence (SSTC 76/1990 and 164/2005).  
The principle of culpability, guaranteed by Article 25 of the Constitution, limits the exercise of the State's "ius puniendi" and requires, as stated by the Constitutional Court in judgment 129/2003, of June 20, that the imposition of the sanction is based on the requirement of the subjective element of fault, to guarantee the principle of responsibility and the right to a sanctioning procedure with all guarantees (STS of March 1, 2012, Rec 1298/2009).  
Certainly, the principle of culpability, provided for in Article 130.1 of Law 30/1992, of November 26, on the Legal Regime of Public Administrations and the Common Administrative Procedure, states that only those responsible for acts constituting administrative infractions can be sanctioned, even in the case of simple non-compliance.
