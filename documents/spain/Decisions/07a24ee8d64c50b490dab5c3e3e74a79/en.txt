LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence and applicable law  
In accordance with the provisions of Articles 58.2 and 60 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 and 68.2 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  
II  
Preliminary issues  
In the present case, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, it is established that there is a processing of personal data, since YUDAYA collects and retains, among others, the following personal data of natural persons: ID document number, type of document, date of issue of the document, first surname, second surname, name, sex, date of birth, country of nationality, and date of entry into its facilities, among other processing activities.  
YUDAYA carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
The GDPR provides, in its Article 56.1, for cases of cross-border processing, as foreseen in its Article 4.23), in relation to the competence of the main supervisory authority, that, without prejudice to the provisions of Article 55, the supervisory authority of the main establishment or of the only establishment of the controller or processor shall be competent to act as the main supervisory authority for the cross-border processing carried out by such controller or processor in accordance with the procedure established in Article 60. In the case examined, as stated, YUDAYA has its only establishment in Spain, C/ Jorge Juan, 6, 28001 – Madrid, so the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to act as the main supervisory authority.  
Among the principles of processing provided for in Article 13 of the GDPR, it regulates the “information that must be provided when personal data is obtained from the data subject” and in Article 15 of the GDPR it regulates the “right of access of the data subject.”  
III  
Information that must be provided when personal data is obtained from the data subject  
Article 13, “Information that must be provided when personal data is obtained from the data subject,” of the GDPR establishes:  
“1. When personal data relating to a data subject is obtained from the data subject, the controller shall provide the data subject with all the information indicated below at the time the data is obtained:  
a) the identity and contact details of the controller and, where applicable, of its representative;  
b) the contact details of the data protection officer, where applicable;  
c) the purposes of the processing for which the personal data are intended and the legal basis for the processing;  
d) where the processing is based on Article 6(1)(f), the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or a third party;  
e) the recipients or categories of recipients of the personal data, where applicable;  
f) where applicable, the intention of the controller to transfer personal data to a third country or international organization and the existence or absence of an adequacy decision by the Commission, or, in the case of transfers referred to in Articles 46 or 47 or Article 49(1), second paragraph, reference to the appropriate safeguards and the means to obtain a copy of them or the fact that they have been provided.  
2. In addition to the information mentioned in paragraph 1, the controller shall provide the data subject, at the time the personal data is obtained, with the following information necessary to ensure fair and transparent processing of data:  
a) the period for which the personal data will be stored or, when that is not possible, the criteria used to determine that period;  
b) the existence of the right to request from the controller access to personal data concerning the data subject, and their rectification or erasure, or the restriction of their processing, or to object to the processing, as well as the right to data portability;  
c) where the processing is based on Article 6(1)(a) or Article 9(2)(a), the existence of the right to withdraw consent at any time, without affecting the lawfulness of the processing based on the consent before its withdrawal;  
d) the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority;

In the present case, there is no evidence that YUDAYA provided the claimant with the information required by Article 13 of the GDPR at the time when their data was obtained. On January 2, 2022, the claimant registered at the HD Acuario Lifestyle hotel, without the hotel staff who attended to them providing the information that YUDAYA, as the data controller, is obliged to provide regarding the data of its clients. The principle of transparency requires that all information directed to the public or the data subject be concise, easily accessible, and easy to understand, and that clear and simple language be used (Recital 58 of the GDPR), and must be provided at the time the data is obtained.  

The fact that the hotel registration took place outside the hours in which this service is provided does not prevent the claimant from being duly informed about the processing in question. The failure to comply with the obligation to inform the claimant as stipulated in Article 13 of the GDPR cannot be attributed to the need to comply with the obligation regulated in Order INT 1922/2003, whose Article four establishes that the information contained in the registration sheets must be communicated within twenty-four hours following the start of each traveler's accommodation. Consequently, the processing of the personal data of the claimant related to the compliance with the legal obligation established in Article 25.1 of Organic Law 4/2015, of March 30, whose regulatory development at the time of the events was contained in Order INT 1922/2003, could have been carried out by the accommodation staff belonging to YUDAYA in a more appropriate manner at a later time after their check-in, providing them with the relevant information regarding data protection, within the aforementioned 24-hour period from the start of their stay at the accommodation.  
In response to the request for information made by this Agency, YUDAYA states that there are informational posters at the hotel reception with basic information regarding data protection related to the management of the stay at the hotel, compliance with the obligations of the registration book available to the State Security Forces and Corps, and surveillance activities. The controller may charge a reasonable fee based on administrative costs for any further copies requested by the data subject. When the data subject submits the request electronically, and unless they request it to be provided in another way, the information shall be provided in a commonly used electronic format.  
4. The right to obtain a copy mentioned in paragraph 3 shall not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others.  
In this case, it is recorded in the file that, on January 10, 2022, the complainant exercised the right of access to their personal data through an email sent to "Acuario hdhotels" (the name of the establishment owned by YUDAYA), specifically requesting information related to the processing of the scanning of their identity document, as stated in the complaint filed (unofficial translation): “I contacted the hotel to confirm why they scanned my ID without complying with the information obligation of the GDPR.” On January 11, 2022, the complainant exchanged several emails with employees of the YUDAYA accommodation, insisting that their request to exercise the right of access to information regarding the data protection related to the digitization of the scanning of their identity document be addressed; it is stated in one of the emails (unofficial translation): “I should have been informed about how our personal data is processed and who does it,” without their request being addressed, since, although the hotel staff sent the registration document with basic information regarding data protection, this document lacks its reverse side, where the information should have been provided in detail, a circumstance that was communicated by the complainant on the same day, January 11, 2022.  
On the other hand, the basic information sent by YUDAYA on January 11, 2022, to the complainant, in response to their request to exercise the right of access, is incorrect. Regarding the purpose of the processing, it states: “Purpose: Management of reservations. Sending commercial communications,” and does not include compliance with the provisions of Order INT 1922/2003 as a purpose of the processing. Although it is not part of the content of the right of access, the information regarding the legal basis for the processing contained in the document sent to the complainant is also incorrect: “Legitimization: Execution of a Contract and by Consent of the data subject,” when the legitimization for obtaining the personal data of the data subject regarding the obligation imposed by Order INT 1922/2003 is based on compliance with a legal obligation applicable to the data controller.

Termination of the procedure  
Article 85 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), under the heading "Termination in sanctioning procedures" provides as follows:  
“1. Once a sanctioning procedure has been initiated, if the offender acknowledges their responsibility, the procedure may be resolved with the imposition of the appropriate sanction.  
2. When the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature or when it is possible to impose a pecuniary sanction and another non-pecuniary sanction but the impropriety of the latter has been justified, voluntary payment by the presumed responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except regarding the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for damages caused by the commission of the infringement.

The percentage of reduction provided in this section may be increased by regulation.”
