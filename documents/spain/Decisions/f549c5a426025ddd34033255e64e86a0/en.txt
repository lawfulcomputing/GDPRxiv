II  
Preliminary Issues  
In the present case, according to the provisions of articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, since GUAW collects and retains, among others, the following personal data of individuals: name and surname and email address, among other processing activities. GUAW carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, pursuant to article 4.7 of the GDPR. Furthermore, it is a cross-border processing, as GUAW is established in Spain, although it provides services to other countries in the European Union.  
III  
Claims Made  
Regarding the claims made concerning the initiation agreement of the present procedure, a response is provided according to the order presented by GUAW:  
First. Evidence on the data deletion process carried out  
GUAW claims that, as the Agency itself indicates, the possible infringement of article 12 of the GDPR would be time-barred, so that the entire warning procedure derives from whether it is proven "that the personal data of the claimant has been duly deleted."  
GUAW considers that the documented evidence submitted in the letter dated June 21, 2023, in response to a request for information, is sufficient and relevant, and therefore resubmits it as Annexes I and II. GUAW insists that "...it has been confirmed that the company proceeded to delete the data, but in doing so, all conversations held to date with the claimant and, until that moment, user of the website were also deleted. To substantiate this point, two documents are attached: the internal process of deleting information of users or clients of GUAW (Annex I) and a screenshot of the current database with the existing information (Annex II). After proceeding with the deletion of data, obviously, Mr. A.A.A. has not received any further communication from PETSONIC."  
Attached as Annex III to the letter of claims is the proof of submission of the letter with registration number ***REGISTRO.1.  
In this regard, this Agency agrees with GUAW that the possible infringement of article 12 of the GDPR would be time-barred and that the object of the present procedure is to verify whether there has been an infringement of article 17 of the GDPR, for which GUAW must prove to this Agency that the personal data of the claimant has been duly deleted. In this sense, this Agency wishes to emphasize that no new evidence has been provided, but rather what was already submitted along with the letter of June 21, 2023, in response to the information request.  
In this letter, a bill of the claimant's order was provided as Annex III, with all its delivery details (which matched the billing details). And as Annex II, screenshots of the alleged GUAW systems were provided, containing information about such order, in which the shipping address section displayed the legend "Customer data deleted by the official GDPR module." This same screenshot of GUAW's systems was submitted along with the letter of claims regarding the initiation agreement of the present procedure.  
However, as indicated in the aforementioned initiation agreement, this Agency considers that such screenshots are not sufficient to duly prove the deletion of the personal data of the claimant.  
Therefore, this claim is dismissed.

In this regard, this Agency wishes to point out that the possibility for this Agency to dismiss a complaint under Article 65.3 of the LOPDGDD is not an obligation but a power recognized by law, and in this case, it has opted for the admission of the complaint for processing, as it was deemed appropriate to investigate the statements made in the aforementioned complaint.  
Regarding GUAW's action of deleting the data, this has not been duly evidenced before this Agency, but in any case, it has only occurred after the intervention of this Agency, which is when the complaining party has seen their request satisfied and not before.  
Regarding the review of GUAW's internal protocols, this Agency positively values such action; however, this does not preclude insisting that in the present case it has not been demonstrated that the request for the deletion of the personal data of the complaining party has been duly addressed, which is the subject of this proceeding.  
For all the above reasons, the present allegation is dismissed.  
IV  
Right to deletion  
Article 17 of the GDPR “Right to deletion (“the right to be forgotten”)” states:  
“1. The data subject shall have the right to obtain from the data controller the deletion of personal data concerning them without undue delay, and the data controller shall be obliged to delete the personal data without undue delay when one of the following circumstances applies:  
a) the personal data are no longer necessary in relation to the purposes for which they were collected or otherwise processed;  
b) the data subject withdraws consent on which the processing is based in accordance with Article 6(1)(a) or Article 9(2)(a), and this is not based on another legal ground;  
c) the data subject objects to the processing pursuant to Article 21(1), and there are no overriding legitimate grounds for the processing, or the data subject objects to the processing pursuant to Article 21(2);  
d) the personal data have been unlawfully processed;  
e) the personal data must be deleted for compliance with a legal obligation under Union or Member State law applicable to the data controller;  
f) the personal data have been collected in relation to the offer of information society services referred to in Article 8(1).  
2. When the data controller has made the personal data public and is obliged to delete such data under paragraph 1, the data controller, taking into account the available technology and the cost of implementation, shall take reasonable steps, including technical measures, to inform controllers who are processing the personal data of the data subject's request for deletion of any link to those personal data, or any copy or replication of the same.

In the present case, on August 9, 2022, the complainant requested the website https://www.petsonic.fr to delete their personal data in accordance with Article 17.1 of the GDPR, without having received a response from GUAW until at least June 2023, once this Agency intervened.  
That is to say, the legal deadline established to respond to the complainant's request regarding the deletion of their personal data, whether or not their request was acted upon, has been significantly exceeded.  
However, for the purposes of the statute of limitations for infringements, Article 74 of the LOPDGDD classifies the following conduct as minor:  
“Minor infringements shall be those remaining formal infringements of the articles mentioned in paragraphs 4 and 5 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and shall prescribe after one year (…)”

V

The aforementioned infringement of Article 17 of the GDPR constitutes one of the infringements classified in Article 83.5 of the GDPR, which under the heading “General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines” provides:  
“5. Infringements of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an undertaking, an amount equivalent to 4% of the total global annual turnover of the preceding financial year, whichever is higher:  
(…)  
b) the rights of data subjects under Articles 12 to 22; (…)”  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations for infringements, the alleged infringement is subject to a one-year limitation period, in accordance with Article 72 of the LOPDGDD, which classifies the following conduct as very serious:  
“1. In accordance with what is established in Article 83.5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, very serious infringements shall be those that involve a substantial violation of the articles mentioned therein and, in particular, the following:  
(…)  
k) The obstruction or hindrance or repeated failure to address the exercise of the rights established in Articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679. (…)”
