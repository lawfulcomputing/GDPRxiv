LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
The Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve the present appeal, in accordance with the provisions of Article 123 of Law 39/2015, of 1/10, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP) and Article 48.1 and 2 of Organic Law 3/2018, of 5/12, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD).

III  
The object of the GDPR according to its article 1 is:  
"1. This Regulation establishes the rules relating to the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the rules relating to the free movement of such data."

2. This Regulation protects the fundamental rights and freedoms of individuals and, in particular, their right to the protection of personal data.  
In relation to the terms in which the claim is made, it has not been proven that the peephole acquired by the respondent recorded images or video, as both the instructions for the model, the email from the technical staff provided by the respondent, as well as the express acknowledgment of the claimant that she has acquired an identical one, rule this out.  
The images of the claimant obtained by the respondent and communicated at the time by him to the person acting as President of the Community are personal data in the sense of Article 4 of the GDPR, which defines them as: “any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘data subject’); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific elements of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.” It is clear that the images—photos—obtained, in this case, are personal data of the claimant.  
Accepting the reality that images of the claimant were obtained, the GDPR defines the processing of personal data in its Article 4.2 as: “any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure or destruction.”  
In this case, personal data processing is carried out through the collection of images of the claimant, which were indicated to have been transferred to the then President of the Community by the respondent, without any date recorded for when this transfer occurred.  
The next issue is to determine whether this data processing is covered by any legal basis. To determine the legal basis that legitimizes this data processing, we will refer to Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27/04/2016, General Data Protection Regulation (hereinafter GDPR), which establishes in its Article 6.1 the legal bases that legitimize data processing.  

“Legality of processing  
1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;

c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject;  
d) the processing is necessary to protect vital interests of the data subject or another natural person;  
e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller;  
f) the processing is necessary for the purposes of legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, provided that such interests do not override the interests or the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject requiring protection of personal data, particularly when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in item f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the exercise of their functions.  
From the reading of the article, a general prohibition on data processing is derived unless one of the six legal bases listed from a) to f) is met. This is consistent with the general determination that is also integrated into Article 8.2 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, which states that data shall be processed for specific purposes and based on the consent of the affected person or under another legitimate basis provided by law.  
The applicable sectoral regulation for property owner communities under horizontal property regime, Law 49/1960, of 21/07, on horizontal property implies that it is applicable by legal imperative when acquiring a property that operates under this regime, which is a modality of community of goods over the ownership of common elements. This regime implies that it is the responsibility of its members to maintain the common elements in co-ownership, with all owners obliged to bear the costs incurred for their maintenance, as well as to respect the general installations and common elements and to keep the property in good condition.  
On the other hand, among the obligations of each owner, Article 9.1 of the LPH states:  

a) To respect the general installations of the community and other common elements, whether for general use or private use of any of the owners, whether or not included in their apartment or premises, making appropriate use of them and avoiding at all times causing damage or deterioration.  
g) To exercise due diligence in the use of the property and in their relations with other owners and to be accountable to them for any violations committed and for any damages caused.  
It is unnecessary to mention that each owner would be subject to these obligations, which could be enforced by the other co-owners interested in the proper maintenance of the common elements.  
In this case, the claiming owner was in a common space accessible and potentially traversed by residents of the property, and photographs were taken of her while she was apparently performing cleaning tasks in the landing of the property. Likewise, in this situation, she could have been seen by anyone other than the person capturing the images, given that she was in a space accessible to neighbors and people related to them.

The act of transferring the images to the President of the Community, who is part of it and is also a representative body of the same, does not imply communicating the data to a third party, since in relation to what happens with the assets corresponding to it, he is responsible for the management of the regime of those common assets whose preservation is the responsibility of all owners, including the respondent. However, the President represents them in the concurrence of the collective of persons holding rights over the same building and affecting the respective holders. The data from the photographs have not left the circle of administration and management of the same Community, which is responsible for demanding the repair of any possible damages caused to the common assets. The capturing of the images in the photographs only reflects a moment in which the facts subject to the communication of the photographs of interest occur, pursuing the transmission to the then-President a general interest of the owners in not having to bear the costs of potential damages caused to the common assets, and the taking of the images may be necessary and suitable as graphic evidence against a mere verbal statement, for example.  
Therefore, given the context, the purpose of safeguarding and defending the general and legitimate interests to which they are destined, and the minimal impact on the claimant's right compared to the common protection to which the photographs are destined, does not constitute any infringement of the principles and legitimizing basis of the processing under the GDPR, given the specific framework in which it occurs and the objective to which it is directed.  
Consequently, this appeal for reconsideration does not alter the validity of the contested resolution.  
IV  
Due to reasons related to the functioning of the administrative body, therefore not attributable to the appealing party, as of the date of this document, the mandatory pronouncement of this Agency regarding the present appeal has not been issued.  
According to the provisions of Article 24 of the LPACAP, the meaning of administrative silence in the procedures for challenging acts and provisions is dismissive.  
Nonetheless, and despite the time that has passed, the Administration is obliged to issue an express resolution and to notify it in all procedures, regardless of their form of initiation, as provided in Article 21.1 of the aforementioned LPACAP.  
Therefore, it is appropriate to issue the resolution that concludes the procedure of the appeal for reconsideration filed.
