Examined the reinstatement appeal filed by Mr. A.A.A. (*hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated August 21, 2023, and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On August 21, 2023, a resolution was issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202207332, by virtue of which a fine of 300 euros (THREE HUNDRED euros) was imposed on A.A.A.  
TO IMPOSE on Mr. A.A.A., with NIF ***NIF.1, for an infringement of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR.  
This resolution, which was notified to the appellant on September 28, 2023, was issued following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), and subsidiarily under Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), regarding the processing of sanctioning procedures.

Images of the location of the cameras are provided.

II  
Before addressing the substance of the matter, the following issues need to be clarified. It is primarily the responsibility of the system's controller to demonstrate the legality of the system by providing all necessary documentation, which must be updated at the time of the specific facts indicated.  
The initial facts are specified in the month of the complaint's submission to this agency, namely, July of the year 2022.  
Article 30 of Law 40/2015 (October 1) states: “Infringements and sanctions shall be subject to the statute of limitations as provided in the laws that establish them.”  
For its part, the current LOPDGDD (LO 3/2015, December 8), in its Article 72, defines as a very serious infringement in letter a) “…violating the principles and guarantees established in Article 5 of the GDPR…”, setting a statute of limitations of three years, a period that will be interrupted by the initiation of the procedure with the knowledge of the interested party (art. 75 LOPDGDD).  
Thus, it is appropriate to dismiss the argued claim of the statute of limitations for the indicated administrative infringement, as the facts fall within the timeframes established by the Legislator as outlined.  
III  
In the present case, we proceed to examine the document qualified as a Recurso de Reposición dated 30/10/23, through which the annulment of the imposed sanction is requested, considering that the infringement described in the procedure does not exist.  
The “facts” stem from the initial complaint dated 04/07/22, in which the presence of video surveillance cameras in the residence located at ***ADDRESS.1 is reported, which is considered to affect a public area without justified cause.  
The respondent does not deny the presence of the cameras, nor does he contest his responsibility for the installation, an aspect that he does not question during the course of the procedure.  
On 15/02/23, the first written allegations to the Initiation Agreement were received, indicating that the notifications from this agency “were collected in an abandoned house near his home.”  
Subsequently, a new document dated 12/05/23 was received, in which he alleges the following:  
“All the documentation proving the necessary administrative authorization to have cameras installed on the façade of my house has already been submitted to respond to the notifications received from the files with reference numbers E/03416/2015 and A/00163/2016 from the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, as they were installed by a security company that proceeded to request the necessary permits for this. Therefore, please review these files to verify that the documentation was provided; if any additional document is required, please let me know.”  
In essence, he refers to previous procedures, distant in time (year 2016), considering sufficient to prove the legality of the system the fact that it was installed by a security company.  
On 16/08/23, a written statement of allegations to the <Proposal for Resolution> notified in due time and form was received, providing a copy of the technical report from the installing company—Liser Informática—stating that “the six cameras installed in your home have not had active recording since May 2022, as the motherboard of the video recorder is burned out (...).”  
No other data is attached along with the technical report, so according to the respondent's allegations, it should be concluded that he has a system of six cameras, but that they have not been operational since at least May 2022.  
Previously, the same camera system had been the subject of another complaint, determining the irregularity of the system, issuing a Warning with measures within the framework of AI/00163/2016, so despite the time elapsed, the system's controller is aware of the matter, the problems that the camera system has already caused previously, and the indications of this agency.  
Consequently, the described conduct is considered “serious” negligence, as he should have initially adopted all necessary measures to prove the legality of the system, as well as provided conclusive evidence of the inoperability of the cameras.  
IV  
As a result, in the present recurso de reposición, the appellant has reiterated arguments previously dismissed, considering that the allegations made are insufficient to prove the legality of the system, thus it is appropriate to confirm the infringement of Article 5.1 c) GDPR for having improperly oriented cameras towards public space that processed third-party data disproportionately, as well as, where applicable, the imposed administrative sanction.  
This Agency presents the following <recommendations> in order to avoid future complaints regarding the same “facts” given the impact of the cameras on the tranquility of public thoroughfares; it should analyze the possibility of replacing the exterior cameras with an interior alarm system whose effectiveness is the same, to prevent burglary in the home, review, if applicable, the signage that must be compliant with current regulations in case the exterior cameras are maintained, as well as, if applicable, repair the system in question or proceed to prove that they serve merely as a deterrent.
