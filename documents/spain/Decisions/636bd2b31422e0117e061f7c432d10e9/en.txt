II  
Before addressing the substance of the matter, it is appropriate to briefly respond to the alleged lack of standing of the complainant in the case presented.  
Article 62 of Law 39/2015 (October 1) states the following:  
“1. A complaint is understood to be the act by which any person, whether or not fulfilling a legal obligation, informs an administrative body of the existence of a certain fact that could justify the initiation of an administrative procedure ex officio.”  
Therefore, we find ourselves in the described case, that is, a person who reports to this agency a presumed irregularity in a matter related to the jurisdictional framework of this Agency, regardless of whether they are a resident of the locality when reporting such facts for analysis by this Agency, so the claim must be dismissed.

“installation of cameras in public spaces that record and store data of the public without any warning” (file no. 1).  
The regulation for the installation of cameras in public places or spaces is found in Organic Law 4/1997, of August 4 (Official State Gazette of the 5th), which regulates the use of video cameras by the security forces in public places; the development of which is carried out through Royal Decree 596/1999, of April 16 (Official State Gazette of the 19th), which approves the Regulation for the development and execution of that Organic Law.  
According to Article 1 of Organic Law 4/1997, its purpose is to regulate the use of video cameras by the Security Forces in public places, whether open or closed, and their subsequent processing, in order to contribute to ensuring citizen coexistence and eradicating violence and the peaceful use of public roads and spaces.  
For the installation and use of a recording system in public spaces, it is necessary, according to Article 3 of the aforementioned Law, to obtain authorization from the Government Delegate of each Autonomous Community, prior to a report from a Commission presided over by the President of the Superior Court of Justice of that Community, and whose resolution must be motivated and referred in each case to the specific public place in question and subject to any necessary conditions and limitations of use.  
Thus, to proceed with the installation of a camera system in a <public> area, the Mayor or Councilor of the competent security area must request the corresponding authorization from the Government Delegation, specifying the physical scope subject to control by the system, the qualifications of the persons responsible for operating the image processing system, and the period during which the recordings are intended to be made.  
Article 13 of the GDPR Information that must be provided when personal data is obtained from the data subject states:  
1. When personal data relating to a data subject is obtained from that data subject, the data controller shall provide all the information indicated below at the time the data is obtained:  
a) the identity and contact details of the controller and, where applicable, of its representative;  
b) the contact details of the data protection officer, where applicable;  
c) the purposes of the processing for which the personal data are intended and the legal basis for the processing;  
d) where the processing is based on Article 6(1)(f), the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or a third party;  
e) the recipients or categories of recipients of the personal data, where applicable;  
f) where applicable, the intention of the controller to transfer personal data to a third country or international organization and the existence or absence of an adequacy decision by the Commission, or, in the case of transfers referred to in Articles 46 or 47 or Article 49(1), second paragraph, reference to the appropriate or suitable safeguards and the means to obtain a copy of these or the fact that they have been provided (…).  
It should also be noted that the installation and use of video surveillance systems involve the processing of personal data, so compliance with the provisions related to the Organic Law on Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD) is required.  
To this end, at least one informative sign must be placed in the monitored areas, located in a sufficiently visible place, both in open and closed spaces, for the purpose of fulfilling the information duty provided for in the aforementioned Article 13.  
Article 22, section 4 of the LOPDGDD (LO 3/2018, December 5) states:  
“The information duty provided for in Article 12 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 shall be deemed fulfilled by placing an informative device in a sufficiently visible location identifying, at least, the existence of the processing, the identity of the controller, and the possibility of exercising the rights provided for in Articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.”  
The entire citizenry must be aware that it is a video-monitored area, the purpose of the processing, and an effective way to exercise their rights if necessary.

V  
The principle of the presumption of innocence prevents attributing an administrative infraction when no evidence has been obtained and verified that substantiates the facts motivating the accusation or the involvement of the alleged infringer in the same. Applying the principle "in dubio pro reo" in case of doubt regarding a specific and determined fact, which obliges in any case to resolve such doubt in the most favorable manner for the interested party.  
The presumption of innocence must govern without exceptions in the sanctioning legal framework and must be respected in the imposition of any sanctions, as the exercise of the ius puniendi in its various manifestations is conditioned on the play of evidence and a contradictory procedure in which one's own positions can be defended. In this sense, the Constitutional Court in its Judgment 76/1990, of 26/04, considers that the right to the presumption of innocence entails: "that the sanction is based on acts or incriminating evidence of the reproached conduct; that the burden of proof corresponds to the accuser, without anyone being obliged to prove their own innocence; and that any insufficiency in the result of the evidence presented, freely evaluated by the sanctioning body, must translate into an acquittal pronouncement.  
The presumption of innocence governs without exceptions in the sanctioning legal framework and must be respected in the imposition of any sanction, whether criminal or administrative (TCo 13/1981), as the exercise of the sanctioning right in any of its manifestations is conditioned on the play of evidence and a contradictory procedure in which one's own positions can be defended.  
According to this principle, no sanction can be imposed based on the culpability of the accused if there is no incriminating evidence that, in the appreciation of the authorities or bodies called to resolve, destroys this presumption (TCo Auto 3-12-81).  
VI  
Taking into account the allegations of the main responsible party for the installation, as well as their veracity, it is considered that the video surveillance camera system is not in an <operational> state, such that the cameras do not process personal data associated with an identified or identifiable natural person, which is why it is appropriate to order the filing of the current procedure.  
However, it is brought to the attention of the City Council that this body may carry out an on-site inspection at the location of the cameras in case of a new complaint, and must, as soon as it has the necessary administrative authorizations, proceed to legally inform the entire local citizenry of their presence.  
Information regarding this can be found free of charge on the website of this Agency www.aepd.es Video Surveillance Guide.
