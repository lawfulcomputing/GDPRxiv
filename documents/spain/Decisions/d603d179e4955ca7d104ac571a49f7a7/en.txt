LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
Following the modifications made to the current LOPDGDD (LO 3/2018, December 5) by Law 11/2023, of May 8, transposing European Union directives regarding the accessibility of certain products and services, migration of highly qualified persons, taxation, and digitalization of notarial and registry actions; and which modifies Law 12/2011, of May 27, on civil liability for nuclear damage or damage caused by radioactive materials, the procedural deadline established in Article 64.2 "in fine" is applied to the present procedure.  
The procedure will have a maximum duration of twelve months from the date of the initiation agreement. After this period, it will expire, and consequently, the proceedings will be archived.  
II  
In this case, the complaint dated 02/01/23 is examined, through which the main fact presented is the presence of video surveillance cameras that could affect the rights protected by data protection regulations.  
Documentary evidence is provided showing the presence of image capture devices in the brush area, without a priori assessing the orientation, although the complainant's statements emphasize the impact on private space.  
Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR states the following: Personal data shall be:  
“adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’).”  
It should be noted that individuals are responsible for ensuring that the installed systems comply with current legislation, proving that they meet all the requirements imposed by the applicable regulations.  
The installation of such devices must have the mandatory informative sign, indicating the purposes and the responsible party for the processing of personal data, if applicable.  
In any case, the cameras must be oriented towards private space, avoiding intimidating neighboring residents with such devices, as well as monitoring transit areas without justified cause.  
Moreover, with such devices, images of public space cannot be obtained, as this is the exclusive competence of the State Security Forces and Corps, under the terms of the current LO 4/1997, August 4, which regulates the use of video cameras by the Security Forces and Corps in public places.  
It is worth noting that even in the case of a "simulated" camera, it must be preferably oriented towards private space, as it is considered that such devices can affect the privacy of third parties, who feel intimidated by it under the belief of being subject to permanent recording.  
Individuals cannot install devices for obtaining images of public space and/or transit of third parties, except in cases permitted by the regulations.  
The purpose of such devices must be the security of the property and its inhabitants, avoiding the infringement of the rights of third parties who may feel intimidated by them.  
The cameras must be limited to the protection of the property owned by the individual in such a way that they do not affect the area of third parties who may feel intimidated by them, as it affects their area of free transit.

The responding party submits an Order of <Provisional Dismissal> from the Court of Instruction No. 2 (Tenerife) dated ***DATE.1, which states the following:  
“…a video surveillance camera installed in an avocado tree had no other purpose, in the opinion of the Civil Guard agents who went to the site, than to observe the residents of the complainant's home. Subsequently, this Court requested (…) which concluded that the complainant's patio area could not be recorded from the fixed camera installed in the avocado tree, and that the only option to record the main patio would be to change the installation of the device.”  
This body considers that the “appraisals” of the State Security Forces and Corps are sufficient to establish that at the time the device in question was deployed, it was not affecting the <private> area of the respondent.  
The mere observation of any type of device does not, in principle, constitute data processing, beyond the fact that given the proximity of the properties, they can be observed effortlessly from the complainant's property.  
Article 77, section 5 of Law 39/2015 (October 1) states:  
“5. The documents formalized by officials recognized as authorities, which, observing the corresponding legal requirements, record the facts established by them, shall serve as proof of these facts unless proven otherwise” (*the bold text belongs to this AEPD).  
The presumption of innocence applies without exceptions in the sanctioning legal framework and must be respected in the imposition of any sanction, whether criminal or administrative (TCo 13/1981), as the exercise of the sanctioning right in any of its manifestations is conditioned by the evidence and a contradictory procedure in which the parties can defend their positions.  
According to this principle, no sanction can be imposed based on the culpability of the accused if there is no incriminating evidence that, in the assessment of the authorities or bodies called to resolve, destroys this presumption (TCo Order 3-12-81).  
IV  
In accordance with the above, although the photographs provided regarding the signs do not prove the “data controller” and are of poor clarity, considering that the cameras only capture the interior of the respondent's private property, the placement of the signs is not necessary as it is <personal and domestic scope>.  
Owners of private properties have the freedom to install security cameras on their private property, as long as they adhere to the limits of their property or, in the case of affecting external limits, indicate in any case in a legal manner that it is a <monitored area>.  
However, it is always advisable that the signs (if any) comply with current regulations, indicating the data controller, the purpose, and the manner of exercising the respective rights, being necessary in the case that the cameras capture the minimum essential external space.  
V  
According to the above, it can be concluded that the installed cameras do not affect the scope of the complainant's private property, without any explanation having been given regarding the camera at the top of the avocado tree, this body considering the analysis carried out by the State Security Forces and Corps dispatched to the scene sufficient, as well as the fact that no data processing of the complainant or any third party has been proven.  
However, it is considered that the installation of a camera at the top of a tree is not the most suitable location, due to the ease of changing the orientation of its capture angle, and the fact that observation by third parties may lead to new complaints, being advisable in the opinion of this body a better location for it.  
The rest of the issues arising from the poor neighborly relationship between the parties are outside the scope of this body and should be resolved, if necessary, in the appropriate judicial instances.  
Therefore, in accordance with the applicable legislation,
