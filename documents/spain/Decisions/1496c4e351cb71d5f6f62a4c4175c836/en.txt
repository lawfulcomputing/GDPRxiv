I  
Competence and procedure  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, by the general rules on administrative procedures in a subsidiary manner."  
Considering the nature of the facts that have led to the proceedings and the concurrent circumstances, this warning procedure is being conducted in accordance with the provisions of Article 64.3 of the LOPDGDD.  
II  
Preliminary issues  
Article 4.2 of the GDPR defines "processing" as:  
“any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure or destruction.”  
For its part, Article 4.7 of the GDPR defines the "data controller" or "controller" as:  
“the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be provided for by the law of the Union or of the Member States.”  
In this case, according to the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, since AXA SEGUROS collects and processes, through the Google Analytics service, among other data, the following personal data of natural persons: unique user identifiers (_ga and _gid), the IP address, as well as other data associated with the browser and the browsing itself, among other processes.  
AXA SEGUROS carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.

Response to the allegations  
Below, we proceed to respond to the allegations presented by the responding entity, in the order in which they are formulated in their writing:  
“FIRST – REGULATION OF INTERNATIONAL DATA TRANSFERS”  
According to AXA SEGUROS: “In the specific case of the services provided by the supplier subject to this procedure, the Responding Party strictly followed the indications made by the EDPB in Recommendations 01/2020, using the draft version published prior to its final approval on June 18, 2021, as a reference, in order to ensure that any potential international data transfers complied with the guarantees required by the European authority as soon as possible, even adopting them before they were definitively approved in June 2021.”  
Regarding the regulations in force at the time of the transfers: “AXA conducted a detailed analysis of the local legislation of the U.S., in order to assess whether the supplier subject to this procedure, as a provider located in the U.S., would have the capacity to comply with the contractual obligations subscribed with AXA regarding the processing of data in this procedure, or if there would be any limitations arising from its national regulations.  
In this regard, it is stated that the supplier could comply with the stipulated measures and achieve an adequate level of protection, considering the possibility of access by U.S. public authorities as residual and not sufficient to conclude that the contractually assumed guarantees would not be met.”  
Subsequently, AXA SEGUROS enumerates a series of complementary measures such as the management of IPs, the User ID functionality, and personal data collected through the implementation (quote_id), in order to try to ensure a level of protection equivalent to that established in the GDPR for those data transferred to the U.S.  
In response to these arguments, first, regarding the measures adopted under Recommendations 01/2020, concerning measures that complement transfer instruments to ensure compliance with the level of protection of EU personal data, they cannot enable standard contractual clauses as an adequate means for making international data transfers to the U.S. since the CJEU stated in case C-311/18 ("Schrems II") that their use could not alone guarantee a level of protection, as required by Article 44 of the GDPR, as it could not prevent the possibilities of access by U.S. services.

Furthermore, the following comments can be made regarding the stated measures:  
“▪ IP Management:”  
This configuration option in IP management does not prevent their collection, which in itself constitutes the processing of personal data of the data subjects, regardless of subsequent anonymization. For example, in Google Analytics 4, according to Google’s “Privacy and Data in the EU” (https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12017362?hl=en&ref_topic=2919631), “…IP address data is used solely to obtain geolocation data and is discarded immediately,” so information that can be provided by the IP is used before anonymization. The act of collecting the IP itself constitutes a processing operation.  
“▪ User ID: unique user identifier.”  
While the use of the User ID functionality may increase the possibilities of identifying users by being able to associate their own identifiers with individual users so that their behavior can be connected across different sessions and various devices and platforms; the lack of use of this functionality does not prevent users of the website www.directseguros.es from being identifiable through the combination of unique identifiers with other elements, as reasoned in point 2 of the Legal Basis IV of this resolution. In this way, other data such as IP or information stored in cookies can commonly be used to identify a user, especially when combined with other similar types of information. This is illustrated in Recital 30 of the GDPR, according to which the assignment of online identifiers such as IP addresses and cookie identifiers to natural persons or their devices can “leave traces that, particularly when combined with unique identifiers and other information received by servers, can be used to create profiles of natural persons and identify them.”

In this allegation, AXA SEGUROS argues that “from the date of this claim until the present request, there is no record of any request for information disclosure by U.S. public entities, which would imply an international transfer of data to this country.”  
In response to this assertion, it should be noted that Google does not always notify its users of information requests. Moreover, the fact that, as AXA SEGUROS claims, there has been no access to the data collected on the website www.directseguros.es, that is, that the risk of access to the data transferred without the guarantees established in the GDPR has not materialized, does not prevent the international transfer of data from being contrary to the GDPR. From the reading of the access statistics data referred to in Established Fact Nine, it can be verified that access by U.S. intelligence agencies to the personal data collected by Google represented a real risk.  
For all the above reasons, this allegation is dismissed.

2.- On the qualification of the data subject to processing as personal data  
It can be stated that the data collected under the Google Analytics function and transferred to the United States of America constitutes personal data.  
Article 4.1 of the GDPR defines personal data as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('the data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific factors characteristic of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person."  
It should be noted that online identifiers, such as IP addresses or information stored in cookies, can commonly be used to identify a user, especially when combined with other types of similar information. This is illustrated in Recital 30 of the GDPR, which states that the assignment of online identifiers such as IP addresses, cookie identifiers to natural persons or their devices can "leave traces that, in particular when combined with unique identifiers and other information received by the servers, can be used to create profiles of natural persons and identify them." In the particular case where the data controller claims not to have the ability to identify the user through the use (alone or combined with other data points) of such identifiers, it would be expected to disclose the specific means deployed to ensure the anonymity of the collected identifiers. Without such details, they cannot be considered anonymous.  
Therefore, it is necessary to examine to what extent the implementation of Google Analytics on a website allows the website administrator and Google to identify a data subject (a visitor to the website in question).

- Cookies _ga and _gid

- IP  
- sr (screen resolution), among other parameters.

In this context, it is noteworthy that standard contractual clauses are a transfer instrument in the sense of Chapter V of the Regulation and were not challenged as such by the CJEU in its judgment of July 16, 2020 (C-311/18). However, the CJEU considered that the contractual nature of these clauses implied that they could not be binding on the authorities of third countries. In particular, the CJEU stated that: "...Therefore, while there are situations in which, depending on the legislation and practices in force in the third country in question, the recipient of such a transfer is in a position to guarantee the necessary protection of the data solely on the basis of standard data protection clauses, there are others in which the content of such standard clauses may not constitute a sufficient means to ensure, in practice, the effective protection of the personal data transferred to the third country in question. This is particularly the case when the legislation of that third country allows its public authorities to interfere with the rights of the data subjects to whom the data relates" (C-311/18, paragraph 126, emphasis added).  
No further analysis of the legal situation in the United States is necessary, as the CJEU has already provided it in its aforementioned judgment. Indeed, the CJEU considered that regulatory surveillance programs such as Section 702 of FISA and E.O. 12333 in conjunction with PPD-28 do not meet the minimum requirements established by Union law regarding the principle of proportionality, so that surveillance programs based on these provisions cannot be considered limited to what is strictly necessary (C-311/18, paragraph 184). Furthermore, the CJEU found that the legal framework in question did not confer on data subjects rights that are actionable before the courts against U.S. authorities, from which it follows that these individuals do not have the right to effective judicial protection (C-311/18, paragraph 192).

The measures to complement the standard clauses for data protection can be classified into three categories: contractual, technical, or organizational (see Recommendations 01/2020, point 74).

FIRST: TO ISSUE A WARNING to AXA SEGUROS GENERALES, S.A. DE SEGUROS Y REASEGUROS, with NIF A60917978, for an infringement of Article 44 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR.

They must also provide the Agency with documentation that proves the effective filing of the administrative contentious appeal. If the Agency is not aware of the filing of the administrative contentious appeal within two months from the day following the notification of this resolution, it would consider the provisional suspension to be concluded.
