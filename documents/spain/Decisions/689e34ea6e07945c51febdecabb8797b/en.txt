IV  
Unfulfilled Obligation. Data Minimization  
Letter c) of Article 5.1 of the GDPR establishes:  
"1. Personal data shall be:  
(…)  
c) adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’);"  
In the present case, the complainant states that to be a verified user of ***APP.1, it is necessary to upload a copy of the ID or identity document to the application, with that copy being stored in the application’s database.  
As for being a “verified user,” these are users who have been approved by the administrator for one of the following reasons: they requested that their identity be verified, the administrator deemed it necessary to verify the user’s age due to doubts that they might be a minor, and the administrator deemed it necessary to verify the user’s identity due to doubts that it might be an impersonation.  
Being a verified user allows the possibility of subscribing to paid services, which users can additionally contract to gain access to additional tools, especially aimed at users who wish to make more effective use of ***APP.1.

In its response, A.A.A. confirms the requirement to attach a copy of the ID card or identity document to the application and states that this information is uploaded to the platform and stored in the database until the administrator verifies that the identity matches that declared by the user. This implies an express acknowledgment that A.A.A. is processing personal data excessively and unnecessarily:  
“As described in the data collection process outlined in section 4 above, the verification process includes the temporary collection of a scanned copy of the interested party's ID card. Although the technical measures adopted and the short retention period of the copy minimize the associated risk, they do not eliminate it entirely.” (page 25 of 81).  
Furthermore, once A.A.A. became aware of the filing of the complaint with this Agency, it commissioned a report from an external expert ((...)), which explicitly states:  
“The collection of scanned copies of the ID card as a method of verifying identity is intrusive and does not comply with the provisions of current legal regulations.”  
Moreover, on 13/7/2024, as an immediate preventive measure, A.A.A. deactivated the account verification mechanism.  

This is because verifying the accuracy of the data can be accomplished without the need to request a copy of the ID card or identity document, as there are other equally valid alternatives that allow for reliable verification of identity through identity verification systems, in accordance with legal requirements, without including a copy of the ID card. It is the respondent itself who states in its writing:  
“To ensure compliance with the GDPR, it is planned to change the method of identity verification to outsource this task to an external company through an API service, in order to further minimize the impact that obtaining images of users' identity documents for verification has on privacy and to achieve stricter compliance with the GDPR. For this purpose, the following services have been consulted: (…), (…) and (…). Currently, the analysis of technical requirements is underway as a preliminary step to contracting. When integrated into the current solution, the account verification process will be reactivated.” (Page 26 of 81)  
Recital 39 of the GDPR states that:  
“…personal data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purposes for which they are processed. This requires, in particular, ensuring that their retention period is limited to a strict minimum. Personal data should only be processed if the purpose of the processing cannot reasonably be achieved by other means. To ensure that personal data is not kept longer than necessary, the data controller must establish deadlines for their deletion or periodic review…”  
Thus, in conclusion, it is excessive to require a copy of the ID card to be a verified user in the ***APP.1 application, as the purpose of that processing can be achieved by other means.  
Therefore, in accordance with the evidence available at this moment in the initiation of sanctioning procedure, it is considered that the known facts could constitute an infringement attributable to A.A.A. for violation of the previously transcribed article.  
V  
Classification of the infringement of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR and qualification for the purposes of prescription  
Article 83.5 of the GDPR classifies as an administrative infringement the violation of the following article, which will be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of a company, an amount equivalent to a maximum of 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
"a) the basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9;"  
For its part, the LOPDGDD in its Article 71, Infringements, states that:  
“Acts and behaviors referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements.”  
For the sole purposes of the prescription period, Article 72.1 of the LOPDGDD establishes the following:  
"Based on what is established in Article 83.5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, infringements that constitute a substantial violation of the articles mentioned therein are considered very serious and will prescribe in three years, particularly the following:  
a) The processing of personal data violating the principles and guarantees established in Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679."

VII  
Corrective Measures  
If the infringement is confirmed, the resolution issued may establish the corrective measures that the infringing entity must adopt to put an end to the non-compliance with personal data protection legislation, in this case, Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR, in accordance with the provisions of the aforementioned Article 58.2.d) of the GDPR, under which each supervisory authority may "order the controller or processor to ensure that the processing operations comply with the provisions of this Regulation, where appropriate, in a specific manner and within a specified time frame."  
Thus, the responsible entity may be required to align its actions with personal data protection regulations, to the extent expressed in the previous Legal Grounds.  
In this act, the alleged infringement committed and the facts that could lead to this possible violation of data protection regulations are established, from which it is clearly inferred what measures should be adopted, without prejudice to the fact that the type of procedures, mechanisms, or specific instruments for implementing them corresponds to the sanctioned party, as it is the data controller who fully understands its organization and must decide, based on proactive responsibility and a risk-based approach, how to comply with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.  
However, in this case, regardless of the above, in accordance with the evidence available at this moment regarding the initiation of sanctioning proceedings, the resolution adopted may require A.A.A. to adopt the following measures within 3 months from the date of effectiveness of the resolution concluding this procedure:

It is warned that failure to comply with the possible order to adopt measures imposed by this agency in the resolution of the present sanctioning procedure may be considered an administrative infringement in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, classified as an infringement in its Articles 83.5 and 83.6, which may motivate such conduct to initiate a further administrative sanctioning procedure.  
It is also reminded that neither the acknowledgment of the infringement committed nor, where applicable, the voluntary payment of the proposed amounts, exempts the obligation to adopt the pertinent measures to cease the conduct or to correct the effects of the infringement committed and to provide evidence to this AEPD of compliance with that obligation.
