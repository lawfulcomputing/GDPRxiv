RESOLUTION OF FILE OF PROCEEDINGS  
From the proceedings carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following:  

FACTS  
FIRST: On June 7, 2022, a complaint was filed with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against RESIDENCIAS DEL BUEN RETIRO S.L., and the CANTABRIAN HEALTH SERVICE.  
The reasons on which the complainant bases the complaint are as follows:  
After receiving a call from his ex-wife, which made him suspect unauthorized access to his medical history, he requested access to the list of accesses to his medical history from May 17, 2016, to May 17, 2021, from the Primary Care Management of the claimed Health Service. From the documentation obtained, he determines consultations of his medical history from two healthcare centers that have no justification: "***CENTER.1" and "***CENTER.2".  
By means of a written communication dated October 4, 2021, he informed the claimed Health Service of the events that occurred with the Geriatric Residence ***CENTER.1, having received no response.  
Additionally, through a burofax, dated March 14, 2022, he directed a written communication to the Geriatric Residence ***CENTER.1 informing them of the unauthorized accesses to his medical history. He states that the residence contacted him and informed him that it is not possible for the physician of the residence to have accessed his medical history at those dates and times.  
On the other hand, he claims that on May 7, 2021, he went to the Health Center ***CENTER.2 to undergo a PCR test, and on the same day, his ex-wife called him to confront him about the test he had just taken, stating that three friends of his ex-wife work at that health center.  
Relevant documentation provided by the complainant:  
- Two Reports issued by the Primary Care Management of the accesses from May 17, 2016, to May 17, 2021, where the Centers are listed: ***CENTER.1, ***CENTER.3, and C.S. ***CENTER.2.  
- Proof of submission of the written communication addressed to the Management Directorate of the Cantabrian Health Service dated October 4, 2021, requesting the identity of the individuals who accessed the medical history from the GERIATRIC RESIDENCE OF ***CENTER.1 on specific dates.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the functions that Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR) confers on each control authority and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve these investigative actions.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II

"1. Law 41/2002, of November 14, regulating patient autonomy and rights and obligations regarding information and clinical documentation, in its Article 16 dedicated to the uses of the clinical history, states:  
1. The clinical history is an instrument primarily intended to guarantee adequate assistance to the patient. The healthcare professionals at the center who perform the diagnosis or treatment of the patient have access to the clinical history as a fundamental tool for their proper assistance.  
2. Each center shall establish methods that allow access to the clinical history of each patient by the professionals assisting them at all times.  
3. Access to the clinical history for judicial, epidemiological, public health, research, or teaching purposes is governed by the provisions of the current legislation on personal data protection, and by Law 14/1986, of April 25, General Health, and other applicable regulations in each case. Access to the clinical history for these purposes requires the preservation of the patient's personal identification data, separated from clinical-assistance data, so that, as a general rule, anonymity is ensured, unless the patient has given their consent to not separate them.  
The exceptions are the research cases provided for in section 2 of the seventeenth additional provision of the Organic Law on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights.  
Also exempted are the cases of judicial authority research in which the unification of identifying data with clinical-assistance data is deemed essential, in which case the provisions of the judges and courts in the corresponding process shall apply. Access to the data and documents of the clinical history is strictly limited to the specific purposes of each case.  
When necessary for the prevention of a serious risk or danger to public health, the health administrations referred to in Law 33/2011, of October 4, General Public Health, may access the identifying data of patients for epidemiological or public health protection reasons. Access must be carried out, in any case, by a healthcare professional subject to professional secrecy or by another person also subject to an equivalent obligation of secrecy, following justification by the Administration requesting access to the data.  
4. The administrative and management staff of healthcare centers may only access the data of the clinical history related to their own functions.  
5. Properly accredited healthcare personnel who perform inspection, evaluation, accreditation, and planning functions have access to clinical histories in the fulfillment of their duties to verify the quality of care, respect for patient rights, or any other obligation of the center in relation to patients and users or the healthcare administration itself.  
6. Personnel accessing the data of the clinical history in the exercise of their functions are subject to the duty of confidentiality.  
7. The Autonomous Communities shall regulate the procedure to record access to the clinical history and its use.  
Article 32 of the GDPR establishes the principle of "data security," imposing on the data controller and processor the obligation to adopt technical and organizational measures that guarantee it, adding, among other requirements in section 4, that such measures aim to prevent, among other things,

other aspects, unauthorized access.  
The respondent has reported the following regarding security measures:  
- They maintain a record of access by healthcare professionals to the clinical history (HC), in compliance with the traceability obligations imposed by Royal Decree 3/2010, of January 8, which regulates the National Security Scheme, as they have been able to verify the accesses made by healthcare professionals.  
- Access to the claimant's HC is inherent to their professional and care relationship with that patient; a conclusion reached after opening a reserved information file to determine under what conditions and circumstances such access to the clinical history by SCS professionals has occurred.  
Therefore, it has not been demonstrated that the actions of the respondent as the entity responsible for the processing have not been in accordance with the data protection regulations analyzed in the previous paragraphs.
