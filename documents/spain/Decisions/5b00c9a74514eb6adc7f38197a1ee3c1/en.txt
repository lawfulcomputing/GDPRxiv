RESOLUTION OF SANCTIONING PROCEDURE  
From the procedure instructed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST: Mr. A.A.A. (hereinafter the claimant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on 06/10/2022. The complaint is directed against the CONSELLERÍA DEL MAR with NIF S1511001H (hereinafter the respondent). The reasons for the complaint are as follows: the respondent notified the affected party of the record of a complaint. Along with the record of his complaint (380/21/C), 17 additional records containing third-party data were attached. According to the claimant, a colleague informed him that he had received at his home the record containing the claimant's data and that of other members of the Cofradía de Cabo de Cruz.  
Along with the complaint, he submitted the notification made by the respondent.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), on 29/11/2022, this complaint was forwarded to the respondent for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements established in the data protection regulations.  
The forwarding, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was acknowledged on 30/11/2022 as stated in the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  
The respondent, in a letter dated 28/12/2022, stated, in summary, that the Secretaría Xeral Técnica of the Consellería do Mar is responsible for the processing of personal data arising from the initiation of sanctioning procedures in the field of fishing; that upon receiving the complaint, the DPD initiated investigations regarding the claimed facts; that the notification of the agreement to initiate the sanctioning procedures was carried out by postal mail, attaching to each of them, in accordance with the provisions of current legislation, a copy of the corresponding record; that at the time of photocopying and enveloping the documentation of the files for sending to the affected persons, an error occurred that resulted in the inclusion, in one case, of a list of records, which is actually part of an Official Communication sent by Guardapescas to the Territorial Headquarters of A Coruña of the Consellería and which should not have been included in the documentation sent along with the Agreement to initiate the sanctioning procedure, and in another case, the first page of the record of complaint corresponding to another interested party was sent to one of the interested parties; however, in no case and contrary to what the claimant stated, were 17 additional records corresponding to third parties attached to the record of his complaint, but rather a list of names and surnames associated with a record number, without containing any other personal data. Neither was the complete record corresponding to the claimant sent to any third party, but rather, due to an error in photocopying the file, one page of the record of the claimant was also photocopied on the front of one of the pages of the notification addressed to this third party.

FIFTH: After being notified of the initiation agreement, the respondent submitted a written statement of allegations on 28/12/2022, stating, in summary, the following: that within the functions of the brotherhoods in defense of the interests of the professionals they comprise, the brotherhoods are responsible for monitoring the areas of public maritime and maritime-terrestrial domain entrusted to them for their use; that the functions of inspection and monitoring of fishing and shellfish activities are carried out by the brotherhoods through the figure of maritime guards, duly authorized according to their specific regulations; that, likewise, the Xeral Technical Secretariat of the Conselleria do Mar holds the status of responsible for the processing of personal data arising from the initiation of sanctioning procedures in fishing matters, for which it collaborates with the brotherhoods; that upon receiving the complaint, the DPD initiated the corresponding investigation work on the facts presented, gathering information from the different technical units that could be involved, requesting a report from the Territorial Headquarters of A Coruña, where the events that led to the complaint occurred, and from whose analysis it is concluded that:  
• The complaint records prepared against the interested parties led to the corresponding sanctioning procedures.  
• The notification of the initiation agreement was carried out via postal mail, attaching to each of them a copy of the corresponding record.  
• However, in view of the information conveyed, at the time of photocopying and enveloping the documentation in the files for sending to the affected individuals, an error occurred that resulted in the inclusion in that shipment of a list of records that should not have been included in the documentation sent along with the initiation agreement of the sanctioning procedure and, in another case, the first page of the complaint record corresponding to another interested party was sent to one of the affected individuals.  
In no case were the other 17 records corresponding to third parties attached to the complaint record; rather, by mistake, a list of names associated with a record number was photocopied and included in the postal notification sent to the claimant, without containing any other personal data.  
Once the incident was analyzed and properly recorded after the DPD of the Conselleria do Mar became effectively aware of it, it was resolved to adopt a series of measures to prevent incidents like the one that led to the complaint.

- To consider as reproduced for evidentiary purposes the complaint filed by the claimant and its documentation, the documents obtained and generated that are part of the procedure.  
- To consider as reproduced for evidentiary purposes the allegations to the initiation agreement of the referenced sanctioning procedure, submitted by the respondent and the documentation provided.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."

II  
The reported facts materialize in that the notification carried out is considered to have infringed the personal data protection regulations by containing, along with the record of the complaint related to the respondent, documents that contain data of third parties.  
Article 58 of the GDPR, Powers, states:  
“2. Each supervisory authority shall have all of the following corrective powers:  
(...)  
b) to impose an administrative fine on the controller or processor with a warning when the processing operations have infringed the provisions of this Regulation;  
(...).”  
III  
Firstly, such processing could constitute an infringement of Article 5, Principles relating to processing, of the GDPR, which establishes that:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
(...)  
f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, by applying appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).  
(...)”  
IV  
Article 83.5 a) of the GDPR considers that the infringement of “the basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent in accordance with Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9” is subject to sanction.  
In turn, the LOPDGDD in its Article 71, Infringements, states: “Acts and conduct referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those that are contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements.”  
Also, the LOPDGDD, for the purposes of prescription, in its Article 72 indicates: “Infringements considered very serious:  
1. In accordance with what is established in Article 83.5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following are considered very serious and shall prescribe after three years: infringements that constitute a substantial violation of the articles mentioned therein and, in particular, the following:  
a) The processing of personal data violating the principles and guarantees established in Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.  
(...)”  
V  
1. The documentation in the file provides clear evidence that the respondent violated Article 5 of the GDPR, principles relating to processing, by allowing the

access to personal data belonging to third parties by attaching to the notification of the complaint report a list of names and surnames of 17 interested parties along with the corresponding report number.  
The duty of confidentiality must be understood as having the purpose of preventing unauthorized leaks of data not consented to by the data subjects.  
The respondent has stated that the cause of the incident was “a regrettable human error at the moment of photocopying the documents that were to accompany the notifications of the initiation agreements of the different sanctioning procedures. Obviously, the intention of the staff of this Conselleria was in no case to reveal personal data to third parties not interested in each of the notified sanctioning procedures, nor to cause any harm to the affected parties, but solely to comply with the obligations arising from the corresponding administrative procedure.”  
This error caused the notification sent to the claimant to include “a list of names and surnames of 17 other people, ..., associated with report numbers... it also resulted in one of the interested parties receiving the first page of the complaint report corresponding to the claimant, which includes the identifying data of the interested party and the vessel.”  
Therefore, the conduct of the respondent is considered to violate the principle of confidentiality, in accordance with what is stated in Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5.a) of the aforementioned Regulation.  
VI  
Secondly, the respondent is attributed with the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR “Security of processing,” which establishes that:  

“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly verifying, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a result of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data is subject to a duty of confidentiality.”

VIII  
1. The GDPR defines personal data security breaches as “all breaches of security that lead to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or to unauthorized communication or access to such data.”  
The documentation in the file provides clear evidence that the respondent has violated Article 32.1 of the GDPR, as a security incident occurred, breaching the established technical and organizational measures.  
It should be noted that the GDPR in the cited provision does not establish a list of security measures applicable according to the data being processed, but rather states that the controller and the processor shall implement technical and organizational measures that are appropriate to the risk posed by the processing, taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, and the risks of likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.  
Furthermore, the security measures must be adequate and proportionate to the identified risk, indicating that the determination of the technical and organizational measures should be made considering: pseudonymization and encryption, the ability to

to guarantee confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience, the ability to restore availability and access to data after an incident, verification process (not audit), assessment, and evaluation of the effectiveness of the measures.  
In any case, when assessing the adequacy of the level of security, particular consideration will be given to the risks presented by data processing, as a consequence of the destruction, loss, or accidental or unlawful alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial harm.  
In this same sense, Recital 83 of the GDPR states that:  
“(83) In order to maintain security and prevent processing from infringing the provisions of this Regulation, the controller or processor must assess the inherent risks of processing and implement measures to mitigate them, such as encryption. These measures must ensure an adequate level of security, including confidentiality, taking into account the state of the art and the cost of their implementation in relation to the risks and the nature of the personal data that must be protected. When assessing the risk in relation to data security, the risks arising from the processing of personal data must be taken into account, such as the destruction, loss, or accidental or unlawful alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data, particularly susceptible to causing physical, material, or immaterial harm.”  
2. In the present case, as recorded in the file, the AEPD forwarded the complaint filed to the respondent for analysis and to inform this Agency of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
The respondent, as already noted in the previous reasoning, has confirmed that due to a regrettable human error at the time of photocopying the documents that were to accompany the notifications of the initiation agreement of the different sanctioning procedures, the following occurred:  
“- in one case, a list of minutes that should not have been included in the documentation sent along with the initiation agreement of the sanctioning procedure and  
- in another case, the first page of the minutes of the complaint corresponding to another interested party was sent to one of the interested parties.”  
It should be noted that the responsibility of the respondent is determined by the security incident highlighted by the complainant, as measures were not adopted to prevent errors such as the one that occurred, since they are responsible for making decisions aimed at effectively implementing and adapting the appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an adequate level of security in relation to the risk, ensuring the confidentiality of the data, restoring its availability, and preventing access to it in the event of a physical or technical incident.  
In accordance with the above, it is estimated that the respondent would presumably be responsible for the infringement of the GDPR: the violation of Article 32, an infringement classified in Article 83.4.a).

In the case examined, the sanctioning procedure arises from the fact that the respondent, as stated in the proven facts, has violated the regulations regarding the protection of personal data, both the principle of confidentiality of the data and the technical and organizational measures implemented.  
Such conduct constitutes an infringement by the respondent of the provisions of articles 5.1.f) and 32.1 of the GDPR.  
It should be noted that the GDPR, without prejudice to what is established in its article 83, contemplates in its article 77 the possibility of resorting to a warning sanction to correct personal data processing that does not comply with its provisions, when the controllers or processors listed in paragraph 1 commit any of the infringements referred to in articles 72 to 74 of this organic law.  
Additionally, article 58 of the GDPR, in its paragraph 2 d), states that each supervisory authority may “order the controller or processor to ensure that the processing operations comply with the provisions of this Regulation, where appropriate, in a specified manner and within a specified time frame…”.  
However, the respondent has stated that, once the incident was analyzed, it was decided to adopt a series of measures to prevent such situations from occurring in the future:  
“• Address all units of this Conselleria, whether involved in the facts subject to the complaint or not, in order to expressly reiterate aspects such as the following:  
- The obligation to review any documentation that is intended to be sent by postal mail or personally delivered to the affected parties in advance, in order to detect potential errors in its content with the necessary advance notice.  
- To remind again in general the main rules and best practices to be considered for protecting documentation in paper format that contains personal data, in order to ensure its security and prevent unauthorized access to it, its theft, or the manipulation of information by third parties.  
- To reiterate in writing to users their obligations regarding security and the consequences of non-compliance. Among these obligations, special emphasis will be placed on the obligation to inform the data protection officer of the Conselleria of any incident they become aware of that could affect the security of personal data or information in general, through the appropriate channels.  
For these purposes, a communication was prepared addressed to the staff of the Conselleria do Mar, which is planned to be sent via email once the current Christmas holiday period is over, in order to capture the maximum possible attention from the intended recipients.  
• To maintain within the staff training and awareness plan, the training and awareness actions for users carried out to date, which are mentioned in the following section of this document.  
In any case, this Conselleria expresses its full cooperation in establishing any measures or actions that the Spanish Agency for Data Protection deems appropriate to implement.”
