In the case analyzed in the Supreme Court Judgment 593/2022, of July 28, regarding the publication by a media outlet of images obtained from a video uploaded to YouTube with the consent of the protagonist, the prevalence of the right to freedom of information is declared, considering, among other reasons, (i) that the disseminated information is verified and holds public interest, (ii) the plaintiff is a person who enjoys public projection, (iii) the disseminated photos do not correspond to intimate or especially private moments and the interested party tolerates their public exposure through the YouTube platform, (iv) the photos are not disconnected from the disseminated information, (v) the treatment of the information is respectful of the person, and (vi) the disseminated photographs were uploaded to YouTube with the consent of the interested party, in such a way that anyone can access them, although it is not recorded that the interested party had given their consent for their dissemination in the programs of the defendant.

Image and voice as personal data and other preliminary issues  
The physical image and voice of a person, according to Article 4.1 of the GDPR, are personal data as they make the person identifiable, and their protection is therefore the subject of said GDPR:  
“‘personal data’: any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘the data subject’); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural or social identity of that natural person;”  
Report 139/2017 from the Legal Office of this Agency states that “the image as well as the voice of a person is personal data, just as any information that allows determining, directly or indirectly, their identity will be.”  
The physical image and voice of a person, according to Article 4.1 of the GDPR, are personal data and their protection is therefore the subject of said Regulation.  
Thus, the processing of a person's image and voice is subject to data protection regulations. Article 4.2 of the GDPR defines the concept of “processing” of personal data.  
“any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of making available, comparison or interconnection, restriction, erasure or destruction.”  
The CJEU (Second Chamber) ruling of February 14, 2019, in case C 345/17, Sergejs Buivids, states the following:  
“It should be considered that publishing, on a video-sharing website where users can upload, view, and share videos, an audio recording, such as the one in question, which contains personal data, constitutes a total or partially automated processing of that data, within the meaning of Articles 2(b) and 3(1) of Directive 95/46.”

Unfulfilled Obligation  
The inclusion of a person's image or a video containing their image and voice in journalistic publications, under conditions that identify or make them identifiable (without applying any technique that prevents their identification), in this case, also alongside their name, constitutes a processing of personal data. Therefore, the entity that carries out this processing is obliged to comply with the principles and obligations regarding data protection that apply to the data controller as provided in the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.  
Such principles and obligations do not diminish simply because we are dealing with a data controller that is a media outlet.  
Moreover, the responsible entity is particularly obliged to consider that the dissemination through a media outlet of the image and voice of the claimant and their name, enabling their identification by third parties, implies a greater risk for the interested party, making it very likely that they may suffer harm to their rights and freedoms. Therefore, it must exercise extreme caution so that it cannot be concluded that such data processing is not proportional or necessary in relation to the information purposes pursued.  
Thus, in general terms, it must take into account the principle of proactive responsibility, established in Article 5.2 of the GDPR, the assessment of risks, and the implementation of appropriate security measures. Obligations that are even more relevant when the risk is particularly sensitive.  
In this regard, and regardless of what the legal basis legitimizing the processing may be, every data controller must respect the principles of processing set out in Article 5 of the GDPR. In the conflict between the fundamental rights to freedom of information and the fundamental right to the protection of personal data, even though equal degrees of protection are recognized for both constitutional rights, it is usually the former that is granted precedence by our courts, after evaluating and weighing all the elements at play.  
However, preponderance does not mean prevalence when, considering all the circumstances present in a specific case, the limits established by law and jurisprudence are exceeded.  
In this regard, the Article 29 Working Party in its Opinion 06/2014 on the concept of legitimate interest of the data controller under Article 7 of Directive 95/46/EC, when examining the legal basis of legitimate interest under Article 7.1.f) of Directive 95/46/EC, which is fully transferable to the current Article 6.1.f) of the GDPR, includes the right to freedom of expression or information as one of the scenarios in which the issue of legitimate interest may arise, asserting that “without prejudice to whether the interests of the data controller will ultimately prevail over the interests and rights of the data subjects when the balancing test is carried out.”

. Balance between the fundamental right to freedom of information and the fundamental right to the protection of personal data.  
In the specific case examined, as we have indicated, the entity EL ESPAÑOL published, on the website referred to in the facts, the image of the claimant to illustrate a news story about social networks, in which her name is also mentioned, inserting links that led to the video subject to the claim that has motivated this sanctioning procedure.

The reconciliation of both rights is nothing new, as the European legislator mandates such reconciliation in Article 85 of the GDPR.  
As we have seen previously, the fundamental right to freedom of information is not unlimited, since the jurisprudential interpretation when confronting it with other rights and freedoms does not allow it in all cases and with full breadth; rather, despite the prevalence that courts usually grant it, it can be limited by other fundamental rights that must also be respected. This limitation is observed when the personal data provided was unnecessary for the essence of the content of the information.  
In this case, the image of the claimant and their voice, as well as their name, without constraining the provision of information, must be made compatible with the principle of data minimization, applicable to the manner, the medium in which the information is provided and disseminated due to the immediate impact on the personal data and the identification of the person.  
Precisely because the public interest in the news is not denied in this specific case, it is not a matter of diminishing the fundamental right to freedom of information due to the prevalence of the fundamental right to the protection of personal data, but rather of making them fully compatible so that both are absolutely guaranteed. That is, the freedom of information of the media is not called into question, but rather the balancing with the right to data protection based on the proportionality and necessity of publishing the specific personal data of the image or the name of the person. Such a situation could have been resolved by using technical procedures to prevent the recognition of the image and voice, both security measures, applied depending on the case in a routine manner by the media, and by not indicating the name of the claimant.  
Furthermore, we must emphasize that the claimant is an anonymous person, and our Constitutional Court, in all STC 58/2018 of June 4, states that public authorities, public officials, and public figures or those engaged in activities that involve public notoriety "voluntarily accept the risk that their subjective personal rights may be affected by criticism, opinions, or adverse revelations and, therefore, the right to information reaches, in relation to them, its maximum level of legitimizing effectiveness, insofar as their life and moral conduct participate in the general interest with greater intensity than that of private individuals who, without a vocation for public projection, find themselves circumstantially involved in matters of public significance, to whom, consequently, a superior sphere of privacy must be recognized, which prevents granting general significance to facts or behaviors that would have it if referred to public figures."  
The CJEU (Second Chamber) ruling of February 14, 2019, in case C 345/17, Sergejs Buivids mentions various criteria for weighing between the right to respect for privacy and the right to freedom of expression, among which are "the contribution to a debate of general interest, the notoriety of the affected person, the subject of the report, the previous behavior of the interested party, the content, the form and...

In the present case, (i) the claimant is not a public figure; rather, it is of interest that they are not recognized by third parties, in such a way that their fundamental rights are fully guaranteed; that is to say, we are not dealing with a person of public relevance, in the sense that such relevance is sufficient to understand that it entails, ex lege, a dispossession of their fundamental right to the protection of their personal data, and (ii) although we are dealing with facts “of public relevance,” in the sense that they are revealed as “necessary” for the exposition of ideas or opinions of public interest, that necessity does not extend to providing data that identifies the claimant.

VIII  
Adoption of measures  
The violations in this matter may lead to the imposition on the responsible party of the obligation to adopt appropriate measures to align their actions with the regulations mentioned in this act, in accordance with the provisions of the aforementioned Article 58.2.d) of the GDPR, which states that each supervisory authority may “order the controller or processor to ensure that the processing operations are in compliance with the provisions of this Regulation, where appropriate, in a specified manner and within a specified time frame…”. The imposition of this measure is compatible with the sanction consisting of an administrative fine, as provided in Article 83.2 of the GDPR.  

In such a case, this Agency may require the responsible entity to, within the determined timeframe, adjust its actions to comply with personal data protection regulations, to the extent expressed in the Legal Grounds of this agreement.  
This act establishes the violations committed and the facts that lead to the breach of data protection regulations, from which it is clearly inferred what measures are to be adopted, without prejudice to the fact that the type of procedures, mechanisms, or specific instruments for implementing them corresponds to the sanctioned party, as it is the data controller who fully understands their organization and must decide, based on proactive responsibility and a risk-based approach, how to comply with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.  

However, in this case, regardless of the above, it is important to clarify that adjusting actions to the regulations imposes the definitive cessation of the processing of personal data referred to in the procedure, with the definitive removal of such data from the website “elespanol.com” (link to the video, photograph, and name).  
This adjustment can be made by completely removing the content accessible through the URL mentioned in the proceedings, or by applying the methods permitted by technology to prevent the identification of the complainant, or by deleting their personal data (image, voice, and name) from the published content.  
It must be carried out in such a way that it prevents access to personal data, its dissemination, and the availability of the original by third parties, avoiding, to the extent that the state of technology allows, the re-uploading or reloading of exact copies or replicas by internet users of the published content. In any case, its preservation will be guaranteed for the purposes of safeguarding evidence that may be necessary during the course of the police or administrative investigation or the judicial process that may be initiated, including any appeals that may be filed against this.
