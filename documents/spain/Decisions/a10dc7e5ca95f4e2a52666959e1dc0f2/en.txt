FACTS  
FIRST: On 24/02/2023, a resolution was issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202100603, by virtue of which a fine of 200,000 euros was imposed on GSMA LIMITED, with NIF N4004237F:  
“For a violation of Article 35 of the GDPR, in accordance with Article 83.4.a) of the GDPR, and for the purposes of prescription, considered as serious under Article 73.t) of the LOPDGDD.”

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
The Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency is competent to resolve this appeal in accordance with the provisions of Article 123 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter LPACAP) and Article 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD).  

II  
Response to the allegations presented  
Analyzing the allegation of the affected individuals regarding the scope of the processing in relation to the circumstance contained in the resolution under Article 83.2 a) of the GDPR, it is observed that, in the table provided, it does not indicate how many times those 7,585 individuals accessed per day, or during the four days that the Congress lasted, once the registration was completed.  
In the contested resolution, it is not considered as a proven fact that there were more than 20,000 attendees, now certified at 17,000, nor that they were directly affected by the processing, that is, that they used the RF system. On the contrary, it was estimated that, given that it was a mass attendance event, the actual number of registered RF users of 7,585 does not contradict that factor. That is, the type of mass attendance event was considered as an aggravating factor, not the exact number of direct affected individuals by this type of processing.  
Furthermore, within the high percentage represented by the 7,585 attendees who opted for this RF system, out of the total of 17,462, it would exceed 40%, a high degree to be taken into consideration. Moreover, there is no estimation of the intensity of RF use by days, so it is agreed that even with this data, it affects a large mass of attendees who must use the system intensively each time they access the venue. This aggravating element, along with the undisputed “intentionality or negligence in the infringement” and especially the link to the professional activity of the habitual processing of personal data, could not diminish the aggravated unlawfulness that the nature of the processing and the affected individuals on whom the alleged infringement falls, absence of a DPIA.  
Thus, it is estimated that the circumstances considered in the resolution are appropriate in their grading.  
Regarding the allegation of the appellant that they were sanctioned for “lack of diligence” in the DPIA, it was not indicated as such in the resolution, but rather as a reference to the conduct, to aggravate it, not to the qualification of the DPIA. What the resolution concludes is that a DPIA that does not contemplate its essential elements is neither effective nor fulfills any objective.  

III  
Conclusion  
Consequently, in the present appeal for reconsideration, the appellant has not provided new facts or legal arguments that would allow for a reconsideration of the validity of the contested resolution.
