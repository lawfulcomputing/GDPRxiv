In accordance with the provisions of Article 114.1.b) of the LGTEL, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this sanctioning procedure.  
II  
The known facts could constitute an infringement attributable to the complained party for violation of Article 48.1.b) of Law 9/2014, of May 9, General Telecommunications Law (hereinafter, LGT), relating to the "Right to the protection of personal data and privacy in relation to unsolicited communications, with traffic and location data, and with subscriber directories" included in Title III, which provides as follows:  
“1. Regarding the protection of personal data and privacy in relation to unsolicited communications, end users of electronic communications services shall have the following rights:  
a) Not to receive automated calls without human intervention or fax messages for commercial communication purposes without having given their prior and informed consent for this.  
b) To oppose receiving unwanted calls for commercial communication purposes made through systems other than those established in the previous letter and to be informed of this right.”  
III  
In this case, the complainant reports receiving commercial calls on their mobile line ***PHONE.1, made from line ***PHONE.2 on June 20 and 22 and July 1, 2022, despite their request for suppression on June 25 and 27, 2022.  
VODAFONE, for its part, states that the number ***PHONE.1, although included in the internal Robinson List, due to usual operational procedures, a human error occurred on the part of the agent who was supposed to upload this new information into the routing system, and consequently, the collaborating agents of Vodafone, particularly VESALEADS, were able to make the calls that led to this incident.  
However, the reported entity states that the reported incident is currently resolved and that the information upload in the routing systems has been adequately corrected.  
IV  
Therefore, the reported entity acknowledges having made commercial calls despite the complainant being registered in the Robinson List, which constitutes a violation of Article 48.1.b) of the LGT that recognizes end users' right to oppose receiving unwanted calls for commercial communication purposes, and, once such opposition has been exercised and expressed, their data cannot be used for this purpose.  
Although the aforementioned article does not establish such a right, we must refer to the data protection regulations that govern the right of opposition: Article 21 of the GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, regarding the Protection of Natural Persons with regard to the Processing of Personal Data and the Free Movement of such Data) and Article 23 of the LOPDGDD (Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights).  
This infringement is classified as "minor" in Article 78.11) of said regulation, which considers as such: “The non-compliance with public service obligations, public obligations, and the violation of the rights of consumers and end users as established in Title III of the Law and its implementing regulations,” which may be sanctioned with a fine of up to €50,000, in accordance with Article 79.d) of the aforementioned LGT.  
V  
In accordance with the evidence available at this moment of the initiation agreement of the sanctioning procedure, and without prejudice to what results from the instruction, it is considered appropriate to grade the sanction to be imposed according to the following criteria established in Article 80.1 of the LGT:  
“a) The severity of the infringements previously committed by the subject being sanctioned.  
b) The social impact of the infringements.  
c) The benefit that the infringer has obtained from the act that is the subject of the infringement.  
d) The damage caused and its repair.  
e) The voluntary compliance with the precautionary measures that, if applicable, are imposed in the sanctioning procedure.  
f) The refusal or obstruction to access the facilities or to provide the requested information or documentation.  
g) The cessation of the infringing activity, prior to or during the processing of the sanctioning file.”  
The aforementioned article adds that “For the determination of the sanction, the economic situation of the infringer will also be taken into account, derived from their assets, their income, their possible family burdens, and other personal circumstances that they can prove affect them…”

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 114.1.b) of Law 11/2022, of June 28, General Telecommunications Law (hereinafter LGTel) and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for the Protection of Data is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for the Protection of Data will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
Finally, the Fourth Additional Provision "Procedure in relation to the powers attributed to the Spanish Agency for the Protection of Data by other laws" establishes that: "The provisions in Title VIII and its development regulations will apply to the procedures that the Spanish Agency for the Protection of Data must process in exercising the powers attributed to it by other laws."  
II  
Termination of the procedure  
Article 85 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter LPACAP), under the heading "Termination in sanctioning procedures" provides as follows:  
“1. Once a sanctioning procedure has been initiated, if the offender acknowledges their responsibility, the procedure may be resolved with the imposition of the appropriate sanction.  
2. When the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature or when it is possible to impose a pecuniary sanction and another of a non-pecuniary nature but the impropriety of the latter has been justified, voluntary payment by the presumed responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except in relation to the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for the damages caused by the commission of the infringement.  
3. In both cases, when the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature, the competent authority to resolve the procedure will apply reductions of at least 20% on the amount of the proposed sanction, which will be cumulative. The aforementioned reductions must be specified in the notification of initiation of the procedure and their effectiveness will be conditioned upon the withdrawal or waiver of any action or appeal in the administrative route against the sanction. The percentage of reduction provided in this section may be increased by regulation.”  
In accordance with the above,
