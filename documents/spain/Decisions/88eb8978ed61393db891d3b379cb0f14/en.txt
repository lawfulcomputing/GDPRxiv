LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of the GDPR and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Presidency of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  

II  
Preliminary Issues  
In this case, according to the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, since the respondent carries out, among other processes, the collection, registration, organization, dissemination, and conservation of, among others, the following personal data of individuals: name, identification number, location data, email address, and economic data in relation to the Community of property owners.  
The respondent carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines "personal data security breaches" (hereinafter, security breach) as "all breaches of security that lead to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or unauthorized communication or access to such data."  
In this case, there is evidence of a personal data breach under the circumstances indicated above, categorized as a confidentiality breach, as lists of unpaid debts were sent by email to all community members and were visible on the community bulletin board without being one of the cases permitted by Law 49/1960, of July 21, on Horizontal Property (hereinafter, LPH).  
According to the Article 29 Working Party, a "Confidentiality Breach" occurs when there is an unauthorized or accidental disclosure of personal data, or access to it.  
Within the principles of processing provided for in Article 5 of the GDPR, the integrity and confidentiality of personal data are guaranteed in paragraph 1.f) of Article 5 of the GDPR.

The personal data protection regulations, while generally prohibiting the publication on notice boards of lists of debtors and debts to the community, allow for such publication in specific cases, provided that the reason for such publication is expressly indicated. Firstly, in relation to the convening of a meeting of owners, in order to inform about possible limitations on the voting rights of owners who are not up to date with their payments. The other case would involve the notification of community debts when it has not been possible to carry it out in accordance with Article 9 of the LPH. This is stated in the sectoral guide on Property Management, published regarding homeowners' associations, which indicates the following:  
"Generally, the publication on the community notice board of a list of owners who are not up to date with their payments is not covered by the data protection regulations. However, the LPH allows for the disclosure of the identity of debtors and their debts to the community, incorporating such data into the notice of the meeting. Exceptionally, when there is no record of the address of an owner, due to this information not being provided to the community, the notice of the homeowners' meeting may be published on the community notice board—or in another visible place designated for this purpose. In this case, the reasons for such publication must be stated, along with the published document, and a diligence must be recorded by the person performing the functions of secretary."  
It has not been proven by the responding party that the publication on the community notice board was carried out after unsuccessful attempts to notify the debt, in accordance with Article 9 of the LPH.  
In this regard, the claim of the responding party that the treatment consisting of the publication of debts, which includes only the apartment and the debt, would not constitute "easily decipherable information" cannot be accepted. The publication of personal data on the community notice board outside the cases established in the LPH has constituted a violation of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR due to a lack of confidentiality.  
It should be noted that according to Article 4.1 of the GDPR, personal data is defined as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person; an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that person."  
The aforementioned guide also states the following:  
"The publication of the list of owners with overdue and pending payments, through individualized and periodic communication sent to each of them, is also not covered by the data protection regulations. Even in cases where there is no explicit mention of the personal identification of the debtor neighbor or neighbors, indicating only the data relating to the property, such publication..."

This communication is also not in accordance with data protection regulations, as such data or reference is always associated with that of the property owner, which leaves no doubt that it concerns personal data, referring to identifiable natural persons."

In this case, it was established that the personal data of the affected individuals, contained in the database of the responding party, were improperly exposed to third parties on several occasions: through an email dated 24/03/2023 in which the complainants and other owners were mentioned by their first and last names, labeling them as debtors, and on dates 31/03/2023, 03/04/2023, 05/04/2023, 04/07/2023, and 20/11/2023, in lists of debtors sent to all community members. Both facts have been evidenced by the emails provided in the complaint and in the photos of the notice board submitted by the complainant.  
It is not recorded in the file, nor has it been provided by the responding party, that either the publication on the notice board or the sending by email of lists of debtors was carried out in the context of a call for a community meeting. It is also not recorded that the publication on the notice board was due to the need to notify debts, as neither has this been alleged by the responding party nor has documentation of prior notification attempts been provided to substantiate it.  
It is therefore concluded that the publication of personal data on the community notice board and the repeated sending by email of lists of unpaid debts constitutes a violation of the principle of confidentiality in the processing of personal data, being the responsibility of the responding party. It should be noted that:  
- The personal data of debtor individuals are not published on the notice board as part of a call for a meeting of the community of property owners.  
- They are also not published on the notice board as an additional procedure following an attempt to notify the alleged debts, since this circumstance has not been substantiated.  
- Likewise, the emails in which the successive lists of debtors were sent did not correspond to calls for a meeting of the community of property owners.
