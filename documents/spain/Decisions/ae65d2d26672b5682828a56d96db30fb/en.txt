“As a Professional College representing the members, as well as in defense of their rights, given the multiple complaints presented by them, we wish to report the following facts: The candidacy that was not elected in the elections of last year 2020, specifically in the month of September, and therefore having passed the electoral propaganda period, continues to send communications to the members, repeatedly and up to this very moment.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence and applicable regulations  
In accordance with the functions conferred on each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of the GDPR and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of the LOPDGDD, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to issue this resolution.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  

II  
Legality of the processing of personal data  
The principles governing the processing are enumerated in Article 5 of the GDPR. In this regard, paragraph 1 letter a) states: “Personal data shall be:  
a) Processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject (lawfulness, fairness, and transparency);  
(…)”  
The principle of lawfulness is primarily regulated in Article 6 of the GDPR. The circumstances that allow for the lawful processing of personal data are listed in Article 6.1 of the GDPR:  
1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or for the implementation at the request of the data subject of pre-contractual measures;  
c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject;  
d) the processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller;  
f) the processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in letter f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the exercise of their functions.”  
In this regard, Recital 40 of the GDPR states that “For processing to be lawful, personal data must be processed with the consent of the data subject or on some other legitimate basis laid down by law, whether in this Regulation or by virtue of other Union or Member State law to which this Regulation relates, including the necessity of complying with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject or the necessity of performing a contract to which the data subject is party or for the purpose of taking steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract.”  

III  
Conclusion  
The complaint highlights the sending of emails to members by the responding party outside the electoral period, despite the candidacy not being elected in the elections held in September 2020. Furthermore, it is unclear how personal data of the members was obtained, and there is a noted breach of current regulations on the responding party's website regarding the duty to inform.  
For its part, the responding candidacy states that it has been active since 2018 until May 2022 after several electoral calls, as the elections were annulled judicially and subsequently by the College itself; until they concluded in May 2022 with a ruling against the responding party. First of all, it should be noted that Article 6.1 of the GDPR does not only provide for the consent of the affected party as the sole legal basis legitimizing the processing of personal data. Therefore, there are other grounds that ensure that such processing is carried out legally.  
Upon examining the documentation in the administrative file, it is evident that several emails were sent to a member, B.B.B., from different email addresses that would belong to the responding party (***EMAIL.1, ***EMAIL.2, and ***EMAIL.3) containing informational/promotional content, coinciding with the time period during which it was active. In this regard, it should be noted that half of the emails provided by the complainant include the option to not receive further communications, with messages such as “To change your subscription, click here” or “You are receiving this email because you subscribed to our newsletter. Unsubscribe.” Furthermore, prior to the admission of the complaint for processing, this Agency accessed the website, ***URL.1, proving the existence of a Privacy Policy that informed about the mechanisms provided for exercising the rights of, among others, opposition.  
However, the complainant has not provided any documentation that proves the exercise of the corresponding right by the affected member to terminate the processing of her personal data, nor the irregularities she claims regarding the website.  
In addition to the above, it should be taken into account that the responding party has been inactive since May 2022, that is, before the filing of the complaint and, consequently, before the admission of the complaint for processing; coinciding with the last email provided by the complainant, dated 29/05/2022. Moreover, it has been verified that currently their website directs to a page that does not exist.  
Therefore, it is appropriate to archive the proceedings, without prejudice to the Agency applying the investigative and corrective powers it holds, being able to carry out subsequent actions related to the data processing referred to in the complaint.  
In accordance with what has been stated, by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, IT IS AGREED:  
FIRST: TO ARCHIVE the present proceedings.  
SECOND: TO NOTIFY this resolution to CANDIDATURA COPCYL and to the complainant.
