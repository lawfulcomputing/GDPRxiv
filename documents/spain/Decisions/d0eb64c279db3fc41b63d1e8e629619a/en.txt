RESOLUTION OF SANCTIONING PROCEDURE  
From the procedure instructed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following:  
BACKGROUND  
FIRST: The Spanish Agency for Data Protection became aware on July 21, 2023, through a complaint filed by FACUA CASTILLA Y LEON - CONSUMIDORES EN ACCIÓN (hereinafter, the complainant) of certain facts that could violate data protection legislation. The complaint is directed against AYUNTAMIENTO DE RENEDO DE ESGUEVA with NIF P4713400B (hereinafter, AYUNTAMIENTO) in relation to the following facts:  
The complainant states that the AYUNTAMIENTO published on its official Facebook account, on June 28, 2023, the results of the draw to be a member of the electoral tables. The published lists include the names and two surnames of each member (regular and substitute) and level of education.  
Along with the complaint, screenshots of the AYUNTAMIENTO's publication on Facebook, dated June 28, 2023, regarding the results of the draw to be a member of the electoral tables were provided.  
SECOND: On January 30, 2024, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection agreed to initiate sanctioning proceedings against AYUNTAMIENTO, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 63 and 64 of the LPACAP, for the alleged infringement of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR, and for the alleged infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.4 of the GDPR, in which it was indicated that there was a period of ten days to submit allegations.  
This initiation agreement, which was notified to AYUNTAMIENTO on January 30, 2024, in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (LPACAP), was recorded, as evidenced by the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  
THIRD: On February 8, 2024, a written response was received from AYUNTAMIENTO in this Agency, in due time and form, in which it presented allegations to the initiation agreement, stating in summary that:  
“1. The immediate removal of the publications on the official Facebook account of this City Council that were the subject of the complaint has been carried out.  
2. These publications were made due to the insistence and numerous requests from residents since many of them were on vacation, making notification difficult.”  
FOURTH: On February 16, 2024, a proposal for resolution was formulated, proposing that the Director of the AEPD declare that AYUNTAMIENTO DE RENEDO DE ESGUEVA, with NIF P4713400B, has infringed the provisions of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR, an infringement classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR, as well as the provisions of Article 32 of the GDPR, an infringement classified under Article 83.4 of the GDPR.  
This proposal for resolution, which was notified to AYUNTAMIENTO in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (LPACAP), was recorded on February 16, 2024, as evidenced by the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  
FIFTH: On February 29, 2024, a written response was received from AYUNTAMIENTO in this Agency, in due time and form, in which it presented allegations to the proposal for resolution, stating in summary that:  
“1. This City Council has carried out the necessary tasks to ensure compliance with data protection regulations, attaching the report from the Data Protection Officer in this regard.  
2. This City Council is very aware of the need to comply with said regulations and will take all necessary measures to that end.”  
From the actions carried out in this procedure and the documentation in the file, the following has been established:  
PROVEN FACTS  
FIRST: On June 28, 2023, the Ayuntamiento de Renedo de Esgueva published on its official Facebook account the results of the draw to be a member of the electoral tables (pages 7 to 12 of the file). The published lists include the names and two surnames of each member (regular and substitute), level of education, and data of the assigned table.

In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.

II  
Preliminary Issues  
In the present case, in accordance with the provisions of Article 4.1 of the GDPR, it is recorded that there is a processing of personal data, since the CITY COUNCIL, in compliance with the regulations related to the electoral regime, carries out a processing activity, such as the designation of members of the electoral tables, in which the following personal data of individuals are processed, among others: name, surname, and level of education, understanding personal data as: "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person."  
The CITY COUNCIL carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the entity that determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR, which defines the "data controller" or "controller" as: "the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of processing, the data controller or the specific criteria for its appointment may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States."  
An identifiable natural person is considered to be one whose identity can be determined, directly or indirectly, in particular by means of an identifier, such as a name, identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that person.  
Moreover, processing should be understood as "any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination, or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction."

For its part, Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines "personal data security breaches" as "all breaches of security that result in the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or unauthorized communication or access to such data."  
Within the principles of processing set forth in Article 5 of the GDPR, the integrity and confidentiality of personal data are guaranteed in paragraph 1.f) of Article 5 of the GDPR. In turn, the security of personal data is regulated in Article 32 of the GDPR, which governs the security of processing.  
III  
Response to the allegations regarding the initiation agreement and the allegations regarding the proposed resolution  
A) Regarding the allegations made against the initiation agreement of the present sanctioning procedure, a response is provided according to the order presented by the responding party in their writing:  
“1. The immediate removal of the publications on the official Facebook account of this City Council that have been the subject of complaint has been carried out.”  
The removal of the publication of the announcement that prompted the initiation of this procedure does not imply a change in responsibility regarding the alleged facts.  
“2. These publications were made due to the insistence and numerous requests from residents since many of them were on vacation, making notification difficult.”  
As reasoned in the initiation agreement, according to the Central Electoral Board in its Agreement 663/2011, the public nature of the draw for the designation of the members of the Electoral Table provided for in Article 26.2 of Organic Law 5/1985, of June 19, on the General Electoral Regime (hereinafter, LOREG), does not determine the need for publicity of the resulting data, especially considering that they are not definitive, as they may be subject to excuse. The communication of the designation as a member of the electoral table must be subject to notification, which will be carried out in accordance with the corresponding official model. In any case, any measure taken to provide information about the result of the draw to the designated citizens, in order to facilitate their participation as members of the electoral tables, must respect the confidentiality principle provided for in Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR.  
For all the above reasons, the allegations are dismissed.

IV  
Integrity and confidentiality  
Article 5 of the GDPR, under the title "Principles relating to processing," establishes in its paragraph 1.f) the following:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
(...)  
f) processed in such a way as to ensure an adequate level of security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, by applying appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).”  
In the present case, it is established that the CITY COUNCIL published on its official Facebook account, on June 28, 2023, the lists of individuals who had been designated to be members of the electoral tables, as a result of the corresponding draw among the registered individuals in the municipality. These published lists include the names and two surnames of each member (vocal and substitutes) and level of education, personal data to which unauthorized third parties had access. The principle of confidentiality aims to prevent unauthorized leaks of personal data by the holders of such data.  
Agreement 663/2011 of the Central Electoral Board establishes the following regarding the publication of the composition of the electoral tables:  
“1º) LOREG establishes the public nature of the draw for the designation of the members of the Table in Article 26.2 LOREG, but not the publicity of the resulting data, especially considering that they are not definitive as they may be subject to excuse. This Presidency does not understand the justification for such publicity, with the only exception being that the interested party may verify, by entering their personal data, whether they have been designated as a member of an electoral table, in the same way that the interested party can verify their census data.

VII  
Security of Processing  
Article 32.1, “Security of Processing,” of the GDPR states:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the varying likelihood and severity of risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which shall include, as appropriate:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
(…)”.  

It should be noted that the GDPR in the aforementioned provision does not establish a list of security measures that are applicable according to the data being processed, but rather states that the controller and the processor shall implement technical and organizational measures that are appropriate to the risk posed by the processing, taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, and the likelihood and severity of risks to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.  
Furthermore, the security measures must be adequate and proportionate to the identified risk, indicating that the determination of technical and organizational measures must be made considering: pseudonymization and encryption, the ability to ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience, the ability to restore availability and access to data after an incident, and a process of verification (which is not an audit), assessment, and evaluation of the effectiveness of the measures.

In any case, when assessing the adequacy of the level of security, particular attention will be paid to the risks presented by data processing, such as the destruction, loss, or accidental or unlawful alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial harm.  
In this same regard, Recital 83 of the GDPR states:  
“(83) In order to maintain security and prevent processing from infringing the provisions of this Regulation, the controller or the processor must assess the risks inherent in the processing and implement measures to mitigate them, such as encryption. These measures must ensure an adequate level of security, including confidentiality, taking into account the state of the art and the costs of their implementation in relation to the risks and the nature of the personal data that must be protected. When assessing the risk in relation to data security, the risks arising from the processing of personal data must be taken into account, such as the destruction, loss, or accidental or unlawful alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data, particularly those that could cause physical, material, or immaterial harm.”  
Recital 75 of the GDPR lists a series of factors or situations associated with risks to the guarantees of the rights and freedoms of data subjects:  
“The risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, of varying severity and probability, may arise from the processing of data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial harm, particularly in cases where the processing could lead to discrimination, identity theft or fraud, financial losses, damage to reputation, loss of confidentiality of data subject to professional secrecy, unauthorized reversal of pseudonymization, or any other significant economic or social harm; in cases where data subjects are deprived of their rights and freedoms or prevented from exercising control over their personal data; in cases where the personal data processed reveal ethnic or racial origin, political opinions, religion or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, and the processing of genetic data, data concerning health, or data concerning a person's sex life, or criminal convictions and related security measures; in cases where personal aspects are evaluated, in particular the analysis or prediction of aspects related to work performance, economic situation, health, personal preferences or interests, reliability or behavior, situation or movements, in order to create or use personal profiles; in cases where personal data of vulnerable individuals, particularly children, are processed; or in cases where the processing involves a large amount of personal data and affects a large number of data subjects.”

In this regard, Recital 74 of the GDPR states that:
