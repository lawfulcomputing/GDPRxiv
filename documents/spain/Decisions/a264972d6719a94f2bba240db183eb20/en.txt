RESOLUTION OF RECONSIDERATION APPEAL  
Examined the reconsideration appeal filed by ASYMECO, S.A. (hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated December 13, 2024, and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On December 13, 2024, a resolution was issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202313227, by virtue of which ASYMECO, S.A. was imposed:  
- For a violation of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR, a fine of €2,500 (two thousand five hundred euros).  
- For a violation of Article 32 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.4 of the GDPR, a fine of €2,500 (two thousand five hundred euros).  
Said resolution, which was notified to the appellant on December 13, 2024, was issued following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), and subsidiarily in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), regarding the processing of sanctioning procedures.

Consequently, we proceed to respond to what is included in the response of the defendant party to the initial transfer of the claim by this Agency:  
- “FIRST.” According to ASYMECO, WhatsApp is a work tool and a usual communication channel between the company and the workers, and there was prior consent from the worker to use this communication channel with the company; it does not involve their inclusion in a WhatsApp group, which would indeed have required their consent. No communications have been sent outside of working hours, the worker has not been required to contact any client through their mobile device or through a social network, communications with clients are conducted via the company’s corporate email and by phone (from the company phone). At no time has there been any sanction or reproach from the company to the claimant if any communication has not been answered, and lastly, the worker's right to disconnection or rest has not been violated. As proof of all this, ASYMECO states that all workers are witnesses to the above and that the WhatsApp histories of all workers are available to this agency.  
In response to all these allegations, it should be noted regarding the infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR that ASYMECO has not demonstrated that, once the employment relationship was terminated, it had the consent of the claimant to use their private phone number for the purpose of contacting them.  
Regarding the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR, whether the communications occurred during working hours or not, as well as the existence or non-existence of the claimant's consent, are not determinative of administrative responsibility, since the lack of appropriate security measures on the claimant's private device occurs independently of the time at which the personal data of ASYMECO's clients were sent or whether consent had been granted.  
Contrary to what ASYMECO has stated, the messages reproduced in the Established Facts clearly show the claimant's opposition to their private number being used as a usual communication channel with the company, therefore, it is also not “accepted by both parties by mutual agreement for such communication.”  
Regarding the fact that the claimant has not had to use their personal phone as a means of communication with ASYMECO's clients, this circumstance is not raised by the claimant, nor is it considered as an infringement in this procedure. Similarly, the absence of reproach from ASYMECO to the worker for not having answered any communication must be addressed.  
Finally, ASYMECO points out that all workers are witnesses to the above, but it does not provide, not even any testimony from other workers, but rather any document that proves the consent regarding the use of their personal phone granted by the workers prior to the start of the employment relationship.

For all the above reasons, the present allegation is dismissed.  
- “SECOND.- LONG-STANDING CUSTOM OF THE PROFESSIONAL OFFICE BETWEEN EMPLOYEES AND EMPLOYER”  
In response to this argument, it should be noted that the company's activities involving the processing of personal data must comply with the provisions of the GDPR, therefore reasons of “economy,” “custom,” or “agility” in communications must be rejected.  
For all the above reasons, the present allegation is dismissed.  
- “THIRD.- THE CLAIMANT ACTS AGAINST THEIR OWN ACTIONS”  
The WhatsApp conversations that ASYMECO reproduces in this allegation were already presented by the claiming party along with the complaint (page 5 of the file). From the email “(...)@asymeco.com,” a message was sent to the claiming party indicating that they should contact the relevant company representative by phone, and that it was not appropriate to use corporate email for “personal matters,” when what the claiming party raised were issues related to the delay in the payment of their salary, which can be considered a matter related to the functioning of the company.  
For all the above reasons, the present allegation is dismissed.  
- “FOURTH.- RETALIATION/ CLAIMANT'S EXPUREO MOBILE/ LABOR CONFLICT”  
According to ASYMECO, the complaint originates from a conflict generated exclusively by the claiming party, and this avenue is being used by the worker for undoubtedly spurious purposes, providing conversations with the claiming party regarding their professional performance and working conditions.  
From reading the facts presented by the claiming party in their complaint, as well as the conversations held with ASYMECO during July 2022 and August 2023, it can be observed that the claiming party has made a series of reasoned warnings about the use of an application through which third-party data was sent to a private mobile phone, without knowing the security measures of the device, such as, for example, who may have access to it. The substantiated nature of such facts renders irrelevant the allegation concerning the reasons for which the claiming party filed the complaint, and issues such as the evaluation of professional performance or the existence of any type of conflict between the company and the worker regarding the latter's working conditions should not be considered. In summary, it can be said that it is not the responsibility of this Agency to analyze circumstances that are unrelated to the facts that are the subject of the present procedure for which the claiming party filed the complaint.

“CHALLENGE AND ASSESSMENT OF THE DOCUMENTATION PROVIDED BY THE CLAIMANT”  
ASYMECO merely states that the images of the WhatsApp conversations alone do not prove the conversation with the claimant. However, it limits itself to stating this generically, without demonstrating in what way they would be inaccurate or providing any evidence regarding the falsity of the conversations. Therefore, the evidence provided by the claimant must be considered valid.  
For all the above reasons, this allegation is dismissed.  
“ABSENCE OF ANY HARM”  
The fact that the claimant has not suffered a specific harm does not exempt from the obligation to comply with Article 6.1 of the GDPR. Data protection is a fundamental right that does not depend on the damage caused, but on the existence of a legitimate basis for processing. Therefore, any processing of data without a valid legal basis is unlawful and subject to sanction, even in the absence of material or immaterial damages.  
For all the above reasons, this allegation is dismissed.  
“AGGRAVATING CIRCUMSTANCE”  
ASYMECO generically opposes the aggravating factors applied to its infringing conduct: “the aggravating factors do not occur in the current case,” without further motivation to refute the existence of such aggravating factors. Only, regarding the aggravating factor of the link of the activity of this company, it states that “it should be noted that our professional activity is not specifically dedicated to the processing of data.”  
In response to this argument, it should be noted that the application of the aggravating factor related to “the link of the infringer's activity with the processing of personal data” is justified because ASYMECO engages in business activities that routinely involve the processing of personal data, both of its clients and of their employees, so it must have sufficient experience and appropriate technical and organizational measures to comply with data protection regulations. This aggravating factor is not limited, as ASYMECO intends, to those companies that are specifically dedicated to data protection, but rather to those that routinely carry out personal data processing.  
For all the above reasons, this allegation is dismissed.
