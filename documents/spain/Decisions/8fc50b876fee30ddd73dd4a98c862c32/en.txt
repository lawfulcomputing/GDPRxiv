RESOLUTION OF RECONSIDERATION APPEAL  
Examined the reconsideration appeal filed by NATURGY IBERIA, S.A. (hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated April 17, 2024, and based on the following:  
FACTS  
FIRST: On April 17, 2024, a resolution was issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202210927, by virtue of which NATURGY IBERIA, S.A. with NIF A08431090 was imposed a fine of 100,000 euros (one hundred thousand euros) for an infringement of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR.  
This resolution, which was notified to the appellant on April 19, 2024, was issued following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights (LOPDGDD), and supplementarily under Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), regarding the processing of sanctioning procedures.  
SECOND: As proven facts of the aforementioned sanctioning procedure, PS/00366/2023, the following was recorded:  
FIRST. – The file contains both the confirmation email of the hiring and the contract entered into on behalf of the complainant with Naturgy Iberia, both of which show the date of September 5, 2022.  
SECOND. – The file contains the name of the complainant in the invoices issued by Naturgy Iberia in July and August 2022, which were charged to their bank account. Subsequently, the respondent proceeded to refund those amounts.  
THIRD. – The complainant exercised their right of access before Naturgy Iberia on July 29, 2022, via an email requesting a copy of the call for the hiring of certain services.  
And, on August 10, 2022, Naturgy Iberia sent the complainant a copy of the voice recording of the hiring, divided into two files.  
FOURTH. - On November 21, 2022, and December 5, 2023, the respondent acknowledged that: “YELLOW THINKS acknowledged to NATURGY that: (i) One of its sales agents used the credentials of another sales agent from the same company to manage said hiring. (ii) These sales agents engaged in irregular practices to carry out said hiring. (iii) The phone used to confirm the hiring does not belong to the Complainant, but is somehow linked to the sales agent of that company. (iv) Because NATURGY requires its partner companies to record the contractual speech, the sales agent of YELLOW THINKS would have simulated a contractual speech accepted by the Complainant and uploaded it to NATURGY's systems.”  
THIRD: The appellant submitted on May 20, 2024, to this Spanish Agency for Data Protection, a reconsideration appeal, reiterating the arguments presented throughout the procedure, stating in summary that at no time can Naturgy be considered responsible for the processing regarding the processing carried out by YELLOW THINKS, S.L., which has completely deviated from the instructions issued by Naturgy, and requests that this appeal be deemed presented, with the allegations contained therein, and that, following the appropriate procedures, the case be archived.

Regarding the second issue raised: “NATURGY was not notified, as an interested party, of the acceptance of the appeal for reconsideration filed by the Claimant against the resolution to archive the complaint against my client.”  
For the existence of a situation of defenselessness to be appreciated, it is not sufficient for a formal infringement to have occurred; there must also have been a material defenselessness. In the words of the Constitutional Court, STC 290/1993, legal foundation 4: “For a defenselessness with constitutional relevance to be recognized, which places the interested party outside any possibility of alleging and defending their rights in the process, it is not enough to have a merely formal violation; it is necessary that this formal infringement results in a material effect of defenselessness, an effective and real detriment to the right of defense (STC 149/1998, legal foundation 3), with the consequent real and effective harm to the affected interested parties (SSTC 155/1988, legal foundation 4, and 112/1989, legal foundation 2).”  
On this particular matter, the National Court has also ruled, among others, in its Judgment, Administrative Litigation Chamber, Section 1, of June 25, 2009 (rec. 638/2008), which states that, “this Chamber has reiterated on numerous occasions (SAN 8-3-2006, Rec. 319/2004, among others), echoing the doctrine of the Constitutional Court, that for a procedural defect to lead to the annulment of the contested act, it is necessary that it does not involve mere procedural irregularities, but rather defects that cause a situation of material defenselessness, not merely formal; that is, that they have caused the appellant a real detriment to their right of defense, resulting in real and effective harm (SSTC 155/1988, of July 22, 212/1994, of July 13, and 78/1999, of April 26).”  
In short, for the prohibition of defenselessness to be breached, there must indeed be a situation of defenselessness, which has not occurred in this case since, on June 26, 2023, the appeal filed was sent to Naturgy Iberia, S.A., within the framework established in Article 118.1 of the LPACAP, for the purpose of allowing them to make allegations and present the documents and justifications they deemed appropriate. Furthermore, the responding party has been able to allege in the sanctioning procedure everything they considered appropriate to their rights, so no material defenselessness has occurred.

Unfulfilled Obligation  
Well, the responding party is accused of committing an infringement for violating Article 6 of the GDPR, “Lawfulness of processing,” which states in its paragraph 1 the circumstances under which the processing of third-party data is considered lawful:

“1. The processing shall only be lawful if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or for the implementation of pre-contractual measures at the request of the data subject;  
c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject;  
d) the processing is necessary to protect vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller;

The provision in paragraph f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.”  
In the present case, according to the provisions of Article 4.1 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, since Naturgy Iberia carries out, among other processing activities, the collection and storage of the following personal data of natural persons, such as: name and surname, identity document, address, telephone number, bank details, supply data, among other processing activities.  
Naturgy Iberia carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
It has been established that the respondent activated the gas and electricity contracts and maintenance services, and invoices were issued between July 22, 2022, and August 29 of the same year, although subsequently, in response to the complaint made to this Agency by the respondent, Naturgy Iberia canceled all contracts that could still be active and proceeded to annul all invoices that could have been generated during the period in which the contracts were active, proceeding to refund the amount of all those invoices that had already been paid by the complainant.  
In any case, the documentation in the file evidences that the respondent violated Article 6.1 of the GDPR, as it processed the personal data of the complainant without having any legal basis for doing so. The personal data of the complainant were incorporated into the company's information systems, without having demonstrated that they had legitimately contracted, or that they had obtained their consent for the collection and subsequent processing of their personal data, or that there was any other cause that made the processing lawful.  
The personal data of the complainant were registered in the files of the respondent and were processed for the issuance of invoices for services associated with the complainant. Consequently, it has carried out processing of personal data without having demonstrated that it has the legal authorization for it.  
Article 6.1 of the GDPR states that processing “shall be lawful if it is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party.”  
It was therefore essential that the respondent demonstrated to this Agency that the complainant had contracted with it for the supply of gas and electricity; that at the time of contracting it had exercised the diligence that the circumstances of the case required to ensure that the person who processed the cancellation of the gas and electricity contracts, registering the new ones with the complainant, was indeed their holder.  
It is therefore necessary to first note that, in a response dated November 21, 2022, sent to Naturgy Iberia, it stated that: “In the recording, agent B is identified even though in the contracting the identifier of agent A appears because B used the agent keys of A. It is noted that this practice is not permitted by NATURGY.  
- After asking agent A, the agent acknowledged having engaged in irregular practices when carrying out the contracting (without specifying which).  
- They cannot explain what type of collaboration there was between agents A and B.  
- They acknowledge that the phone used to confirm the contracting does not belong to the Complainant but is somehow linked to agent A.  
- When asked about the measures taken after learning of the facts, they incur in inconsistencies, initially stating regarding agent A that they had proceeded to dismiss her, although later they indicated that they had not done so.  
- They state that agent B no longer works in their company. […]  
- Naturgy has canceled all contracts that could still be active.  
- Naturgy has contacted the Complainant to apologize and offer to assist them in reinstating the gas and electricity contracts with their previous marketing companies, although the Complainant has indicated that they preferred to manage the change to another marketer on their own.  
- Naturgy has proceeded to annul all invoices that could have been generated during the period in which the contracts were active with Naturgy, and will proceed to refund the amount of all those invoices that had already been paid by the Complainant.”

On the other hand, in the allegations to the initiation agreement, dated December 5, 2023, Naturgy states: “This company acknowledged to NATURGY that: (i) One of its sales agents used the credentials of another sales agent from the same company to manage said contracting. (ii) These sales agents engaged in irregular practices to carry out said contracting. In light of the above, the Respondent fails to demonstrate that it acted diligently and consequently there was an unlawful processing of the personal data of the Claimant, thereby violating Article 6 of the GDPR.  
In this regard, Recital 40 of the GDPR states:  
“(40) For processing to be lawful, personal data must be processed with the consent of the data subject or on another legitimate basis established by law, whether in this Regulation, including the necessity of complying with a legal obligation applicable to the data controller or the necessity of performing a contract to which the data subject is party or for the purpose of taking steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract.”  
Based on the above, it is considered that Naturgy has violated Article 6.1 of the GDPR.

V  
Sanction of fine. Determination of the amount  
In order to determine the administrative fine to be imposed, the provisions of Articles 83.1 and 83.2 of the GDPR must be observed, which state:  
“Each supervisory authority shall ensure that the imposition of administrative fines under this article for the infringements of this Regulation indicated in paragraphs 4, 9, and 6 are, in each individual case, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.”  
“Administrative fines shall be imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, as an additional or substitute measure to those provided for in Article 58, paragraph 2, letters a) to h) and j). In deciding to impose an administrative fine and its amount in each individual case, the following shall be duly taken into account:  
a) the nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the processing operation in question as well as the number of data subjects affected and the level of damage and detriment suffered;  
b) the intentionality or negligence in the infringement;  
c) any measures taken by the controller or processor to mitigate the damage and detriment suffered by the data subjects;  
d) the degree of responsibility of the controller or processor, taking into account the technical or organizational measures they have implemented pursuant to Articles 25 and 32;  
e) any previous infringement committed by the controller or processor;  
f) the degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority in order to remedy the infringement and mitigate the possible adverse effects of the infringement;  
g) the categories of personal data affected by the infringement;  
h) the manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the infringement, in particular whether the controller or processor notified the infringement and, if so, to what extent;  
i) when the measures indicated in Article 58, paragraph 2, have previously been ordered against the controller or processor in relation to the same matter, the compliance with such measures;

The business activity of the respondent necessarily involves the processing of personal data, being one of the most important gas and electricity companies in Spain. This characteristic of its business activity impacts, reinforcing it, the diligence that must be exercised in compliance with the principles governing the processing of personal data and in the quality and effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures that must be implemented to ensure respect for the fundamental right.  
The Judgment of the National Court of October 17, 2007 (rec. 63/2006), regarding entities whose activity involves continuous processing of customer data, indicates that “…the Supreme Court has understood that there is negligence whenever a legal duty of care is disregarded, that is, when the infringer does not behave with the required diligence. It is appropriate to graduate the sanction to be imposed on the respondent and set it at the amount of €100,000 for the infringement of Article 83.5 a) GDPR, classified as very serious for the purposes of prescription in Article 72.1b) of the LOPDGDD.
