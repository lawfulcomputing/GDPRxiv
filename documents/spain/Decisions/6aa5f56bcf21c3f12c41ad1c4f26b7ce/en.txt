In accordance with the provisions of Article 55 of the GDPR, the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to perform the functions assigned to it in Article 57, including the enforcement of the Regulation and promoting awareness among data controllers and processors regarding their obligations, as well as addressing complaints submitted by a data subject and investigating, as appropriate, the reasons for such complaints.

III  
Rights of Individuals Regarding Personal Data Protection  
The rights of access, rectification, erasure, objection, the right to restriction of processing, and the right to data portability of individuals regarding personal data protection are regulated in Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR, as well as in Articles 13 to 18 of the LOPDGDD.  
Regarding the right of access of data subjects to obtain personal data "concerning them" from the data controller, Articles 12 and 15 of the GDPR, as well as Articles 12 and 13 of the LOPDGDD, are particularly applicable, all of which must be interpreted within the framework established in Recitals 59 to 64 of the GDPR.

“4.2 The personal data referred to by the right of access  
In accordance with Article 15, paragraph 1 of the GDPR, ‘the data subject shall have the right to obtain from the data controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning them is being processed and, where that is the case, access to the personal data and the following information’” (the emphasis is ours).  
Several elements can be inferred from paragraph 1 of Article 15 of the GDPR. The paragraph expressly refers to “the personal data concerning them” (4.2.1), which “is being processed” (4.2.2) by the data controller:  
4.2.1. “personal data concerning them”  
The right of access can only be exercised with respect to the personal data related to the data subject requesting access or, where applicable, by an authorized person or representative (see section 3.4). There are also situations where the data does not have a link to the person exercising the right of access, but rather to another person. However, the data subject only has the right to personal data relating to themselves, excluding data that exclusively concerns another person.  
However, the classification of data as personal data relating to the data subject does not depend on whether such personal data also refers to another person. Therefore, it is possible for personal data to refer to more than one person at the same time. This does not automatically mean that access is granted to personal data also related to another person, as the data controller must comply with Article 15, paragraph 4, of the GDPR.  
The words “personal data concerning them” should not be interpreted in a “too restrictive” manner by data controllers, as has already been indicated.

Consequently, according to the EDPB, the highest European authority in this matter, the right of access under Article 15 of the GDPR goes beyond merely requesting one's own personal data—limited to the data subject—and extends to those that “concern them.” An interested party may request information about all personal data that the data controller holds in relation to their identity, including data collected in a supposed identity theft.  
Furthermore, the Guidelines 1/2022 expressly mention that the right to know about operations carried out in cases of accounts opened due to “identity theft” can be admitted (the emphasis is ours):  
“On the other hand, there are situations where the link between the data and several individuals may seem blurred for the controller, as in the case of identity theft. In the event of identity theft, one person acts fraudulently on behalf of another person. In this context, it is important to remember that the victim must receive information about all personal data that the data controller holds in relation to their identity, including data collected based on the actions of the fraudster. In other words, even after the data controller becomes aware of the identity theft, the personal data associated or related to the identity of the victim constitutes personal data of the interested party.
