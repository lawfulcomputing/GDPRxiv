III  
In this case, we proceed to examine the complaint dated 22/08/23, through which it is reported the "presence of cameras in the establishment without being properly signposted," considering the complainant that these may disproportionately affect their privacy.  
Documentary evidence is provided that verifies the presence of devices without the exact orientation of the same being appreciated.  
The respondent provides a video from one of the cameras, located in the bar area, with the understood purpose of protecting the cash register, although it does not provide images from the system of any of the other four cameras installed in the establishment, nor justifies the presence or purpose of the same.  
Therefore, it is considered that the respondent has not correctly explained the exact location of all the cameras, as well as the justification for their installation, which could observe more than necessary for the purpose of the system.  
Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR states the following: Personal data shall be: "adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed ('data minimization')."  
It should be noted that individuals are responsible for ensuring that the installed systems comply with current legislation, demonstrating that they meet all the requirements established by the applicable regulations.  
The installation of such devices must have the mandatory informative sign, indicating the purposes and the responsible party for the processing of personal data, if applicable.  
Surveillance cameras for bars and restaurants are a reality in many establishments, as their installation often has a deterrent purpose, that is, through the visible presence of cameras, the owners of such establishments hope that anyone wishing to commit any kind of crime will think twice knowing they are being watched.  
Cameras must be located in those locations permitted by the regulations, that is, in public use spaces, where privacy is not presumed, such as the main bar area as long as they do not directly face the tables of the establishment or restaurant area, the dining room, the cash area, the kitchen, the entrance door(s), etc.  
Therefore, the person responsible for an establishment that carries out processing for surveillance purposes in the aforementioned terms may place surveillance cameras for the previously described purpose only in the appropriate places and under the circumstances necessary to preserve safety within the establishment itself.  
When analyzing proportionality, special attention must be paid to whether the measure is capable of achieving the proposed objective and whether less intrusive means can be adopted in relation to the Fundamental Right to the Protection of Personal Data, as it involves preserving the power of disposition and control of personal data by the data subject, as well as preventing interference with other fundamental rights and freedoms such as the privacy of individuals or the free development of their personality.  
According to Article 20.3 of the Workers' Statute: "the employer may adopt the measures they deem most appropriate for surveillance and control to verify compliance by the worker with their labor obligations and duties."  
Article 89 of LO 3/2018 (December 5) states:  
1. Employers may process images obtained through camera or video camera systems for the exercise of the control functions of workers or public employees provided for, respectively, in Article 20.3 of the Workers' Statute and in public function legislation, provided that these functions are exercised within their legal framework and with the inherent limits thereof. Employers must inform, prior to and in an express, clear, and concise manner, the workers or public employees and, where applicable, their representatives, about this measure.  
In the event that the commission of a flagrant illegal act by workers or public employees has been captured, the duty to inform shall be considered fulfilled when at least the device referred to in Article 22.4 of this organic law exists.  
1. Under no circumstances shall the installation of sound recording systems or video surveillance in places designated for the rest or recreation of workers or public employees, such as changing rooms, restrooms, dining rooms, and similar areas, be permitted.  
1. The use of systems similar to those referred to in the previous sections for sound recording in the workplace shall only be permitted when the risks to the security of facilities, property, and persons arising from the activity carried out in the workplace are relevant, and always respecting the principle of proportionality, the principle of minimal intervention, and the guarantees provided in the previous sections.

IV  
Classification and Qualification of the Infraction  
In accordance with the evidence available in the sanctioning procedure, it is considered that the party being claimed has not legally clarified the facts subject to the claim regarding the proportionality of the installed system, which may obtain recordings of what occurs in the establishment in a manner disproportionate to the purpose of the system and in unnecessary spaces for that purpose.  
The presence of the installed cameras must be limited to what is necessary for the protection of the establishment (e.g., access door) or in key points duly justified, such as the control of the area where the cash register is located; the presence of other cameras is considered intrusive and disproportionate to the purpose of the system.  
The known facts are therefore constitutive of an infraction, attributable to the party being claimed for the infringement of Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR, previously mentioned.  
Article 83.5 of the GDPR states: “Infringements of the following provisions shall be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to a maximum of 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9;

“a) The processing of personal data in violation of the principles and guarantees established in Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.
