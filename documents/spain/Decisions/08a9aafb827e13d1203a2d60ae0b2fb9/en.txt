RESOLUTION ON THE ARCHIVING OF PROCEEDINGS  
From the actions carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: The Spanish Agency for Data Protection has become aware of certain facts that could violate data protection legislation. On July 18, 2023, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection urged the General Subdirectorate for Data Inspection (SGID) to initiate the preliminary investigative actions referred to in Article 67 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD) to investigate TYRCEO Data Solutions S.L. with NIF B16570236 (hereinafter, the investigated party) in relation to the following facts:  
On July 17, 2023, the newspaper El Confidencial published a news article about the possible use by the Spanish Socialist Workers' Party of a technological tool that allows for the identification of specific segments of the population for electoral purposes (***URL.1), which has also been disseminated in other media such as ONDA CERO (***URL.2).  
According to the published information, the Spanish Socialist Workers' Party would have acquired software called TESELA that would allow for the identification of potential voters to carry out a direct mailing of specific propaganda with the intention of mobilizing them for the electoral appointment on July 23.  
In particular, the software called TESELA allows for directing electoral advertisements to extremely specific segments of the population by inputting the parameters of the profile to be contacted, from age to gender, including purchasing power, education level, and neighborhood of residence; subsequently, TESELA identifies individuals who match the predetermined data and then sends advertising campaigns through Facebook, Instagram, and other networks of the Meta group, using the chosen messages.  
In this way, according to the news, TESELA allows for hyper-segmenting the recipients of the advertising and makes communication more effective, allocating 10 and 15-second videos on specific themes, which are complemented by traditional dynamic banners with campaign slogans and programmatic advertising.  
In addition to TESELA, the aforementioned party would be using another application called BLOISE, already used in previous campaigns, through which Spain is divided into 37,000 portions, one for each electoral section, and studies the composition of each of those demarcations considering variables such as voting evolution, the percentage of retirees, income level, and the presence of immigrants, among other data.  
The analysis of this information allows campaign managers to identify areas with the greatest potential for voter growth and, in combination with TESELA, would signify an even more precise way to allocate electoral propaganda.  
Considering the relevance of the information, it is necessary for this Agency to gain an in-depth understanding of the described facts, particularly the operational parameters of the TESELA application and the treatments carried out to determine, if applicable, the consequences that may arise for the rights and freedoms of individuals.  
In relation to the above, the need to assess the implications regarding personal data protection requires the opening of an investigation to verify these aspects and, if necessary, the proper observance of the regulations.  
Therefore, in accordance with the aforementioned and in accordance with Article 67.2 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the Guarantee of Digital Rights, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection  

IT IS AGREED to open an ex officio investigation and to instruct the General Subdirectorate for Data Inspection to initiate preliminary investigative actions aimed at verifying these facts and their authorship.

Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines "personal data security breaches" (hereinafter security breach) as "all breaches of security that lead to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or unauthorized communication or access to such data."  
Within the principles of processing provided in Article 5 of the GDPR, the integrity and confidentiality of personal data is guaranteed in paragraph 1.f) of Article 5 of the GDPR.

“1. Personal data shall be:

IV  
Conclusion  
From the preliminary investigative actions initiated at the request of the Director of the Spanish Data Protection Agency following the publication in the media of the possible use by the PSOE of software called TESELA to identify individuals fitting a certain profile to subsequently direct specific advertising messages to them through social networks, it is evident that the TESELA software is a system that collects information from various sources that allows it to categorize, at the level of census section or postal code, the following variables: electoral results (municipal, regional, general), demographic characteristics (gender, age range, marital status, income level, origin -native or foreign-, education level, and housing tenure status), topics of interest (housing, employment, culture, environment, and education), profile (“Animal Lovers,” “Hunting Enthusiasts,” “Gamers,” “Fitness,” “Motorcyclists,” “Preppy”).

In accordance with the provisions of Article 50 of the LOPDGDD, this Resolution will be made public once it has been notified to the interested parties. Therefore, given that this is a technical matter, it is necessary to indicate the difference between data aggregation and data disaggregation.  
It should be noted that data aggregation consists of a data processing that can be performed manually or through specialized software, allowing for the retrieval of search query data in a summarized format, which is significant and useful for the end user.  
On the other hand, data disaggregation refers to the separation of information collected from multiple sources to enhance the understanding of a situation, grouping data by age, sex, geographic area, education, ethnicity, or other socioeconomic variables.  
Thus, while disaggregated data allows for the identification of each individual based on the collected data, when the information is aggregated instead of disaggregated, it is not possible to individually identify each person.  
During the investigation conducted by the AEPD, it could not be established that the Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE) could access, through the TESELA software, data specifically attributable to identifiable individuals. Therefore, the AEPD considers that the software used by the PSOE for profiling and sending advertising consists of aggregated data processing, such that no information used allows for the individualized identification of persons, and thus there would be no processing of personal data, nor would Article 5.1 f) of the GDPR have been violated by the investigated party.  
Therefore, since it has not been possible to attribute responsibility for the processing of personal data in a manner that allows for the individualized identification of each person, as indicated by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, IT IS AGREED:
