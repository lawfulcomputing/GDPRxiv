RESOLUTION OF FILE OF ACTIONS  
From the actions carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On April 21, 2022, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection agreed to initiate investigative actions regarding the facts described below:  
As a result of the communication dated March 30, 2022, made by the SECRETARY OF STATE FOR DIGITALIZATION AND ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE (hereinafter SEDIA), in which this Agency was informed of two writings from the ASSOCIACIÓ PER A LA DEFENSIÓ DE L’ADMINISTRAT I EL CONTRIBUENT (ADAC) and ANF AUTHORITY OF CERTIFICATION (ANF AC), which report alleged irregularities related to the issuance of qualified electronic certificates by trust service providers.  
The practices carried out by various entities are referenced, consisting of the instrumentalization of qualified electronic certificates for unauthorized access to Public Administration sites and the obtaining of reports from them, such as criminal records, tax declarations, Social Security information, and information from the Central Risk Information of the Bank of Spain (CIRBE).  
The opening of investigative actions is urged due to the need to analyze the implications regarding personal data protection and information security, in order to clarify the reported facts.  
Claimed: CIRBOX TECNOLOGÍA Y SEGURIDAD, S.L. with NIF B88571310.  
Facts according to statements from the claiming party:  
SEDIA forwards the complaints filed by ANF AC and ADAC that were directed to that agency. In them, a possible fraudulent use of electronic certificates is stated to access and sell the personal information of third parties (the holders of the certificates) through the use of the CIRBOX platform (whose responsible party is CIRBOX TECNOLOGÍA Y SEGURIDAD, S.L., hereinafter CIRBOX).  
They indicate that with the instrumentalization of qualified electronic certificates, they are de facto selling to third parties private and confidential information of citizens and companies. It is indicated that the certificates may have been issued without the knowledge of the holders, allowing access to information related to their holders from different Public Administrations.  
They provide a link to a video about the operation of the CIRBOX platform, in which it is observed that the client of the platform (usually a company) must enter the mobile phone number and the email address of the person from whom the data is to be obtained (data holder, hereinafter end user).  
The claimants point out that the client of the platform could register their own phone number instead of the end user's phone number, and likewise with the email address, thus accessing the data without the knowledge of its holder, the end user.  
They state that they have conducted tests by registering as a client of the platform, and in three completed requests (related to two different people), the same email address was entered as the data of the end users, which CIRBOX should have identified as that of its client, and the same mobile phone number for all of them.  
A second income report was requested regarding one of those persons, whose request was canceled by CIRBOX, which claimed as a reason that the email and mobile phone had been repeated from previous requests.  
A request was made in the name of a new end user, providing the ID of another person, which was detected by CIRBOX, canceling the request, communicating the cessation of the relationship, and canceling access to the platform.  
They indicate that there is no or is not disclosed the privacy policy of the service, that no contract of data processing manager is signed, that the end user is not informed of the processing of their personal data, that they do not have the explicit consent of the user, and that the reports can be downloaded as many times as desired.  
Date on which the reported facts took place: March 7, 2022.

CONCLUSIONS  
The inspection carried out has revealed that the CIRBOX platform is primarily designed for companies that offer financing to their clients in the marketing of their products, and that for the study of this financing, they request reports that certify their situation. The aforementioned platform facilitates the obtaining of reports, which are delivered directly to the company that requests them.  
It has been evidenced that the CIRBOX platform uses digital certificates for access to the electronic offices of public administrations and that in relation to these digital certificates, the entity CIRBOX, responsible for the platform, acts as a Registration Authority (RA) and UANATACA as a Certification Authority (CA).  
The client companies of CIRBOX act as a Physical Verification Point (PVP) in order to certify the identity of the end user when applying for the electronic certificate.  
Regarding the accreditation of the identity of the data holder, it is evident that the following controls exist:  
- CIRBOX signs a PVP agreement with its client company.  
- In these PVP agreements, it is stipulated that identification will be carried out by internal personnel of the client company and according to the criteria established in the eIDAS Regulation.  
- At the time of identification, a certificate must be issued, which is sent to CIRBOX to certify the identification, along with an image of the identity document.  
- The specific employees of the client who will perform the identification tasks of the holders of the digital certificates sign a document where they are authorized to do so and informed of the instructions for identification, including among other instructions the verification of the accuracy and authenticity of the information.

I  
Competence  
In accordance with the functions conferred to each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR) and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve these investigative actions.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  

II  
Legality and security of processing  
Article 6 of the GDPR, under the heading "Lawfulness of processing," details in its paragraph 1 the circumstances under which data processing is considered lawful:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary in order to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in point f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.”  
In turn, Article 6.1 of the LOPDGDD indicates, regarding the processing of personal data based on the consent of the affected party that: “1. In accordance with the provisions of Article 4.11 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, consent of the affected party means any freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes by which he or she, by a statement or by a clear affirmative action, signifies agreement to the processing of personal data concerning him or her (...).”  
For its part, the security of processing, as laid down in Article 32 of the GDPR, establishes that:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing, as well as the varying risks of likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which shall include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data in a timely manner in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, account shall be taken, in particular, of the risks that are presented by processing, in particular as a result of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration of, or unauthorized disclosure of, or access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may be used as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this Article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and who has access to personal data does not process such data except on instructions from the controller, unless he or she is required to do so by Union or Member State law.”  
Recital 74 of the GDPR states:

"It must be established that the data controller is responsible for any processing of personal data carried out by themselves or on their behalf. In particular, the controller must be obliged to implement appropriate and effective measures and must be able to demonstrate compliance of the processing activities with this Regulation, including the effectiveness of the measures. It should be noted that the GDPR in the aforementioned provision does not establish a list of security measures applicable according to the data being processed, but rather states that the controller and the processor shall implement technical and organizational measures that are appropriate to the risk posed by the processing, taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, and the risks of likelihood and severity to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.  
Furthermore, the security measures must be adequate and proportionate to the detected risk, indicating that the determination of the technical and organizational measures must be made taking into account: pseudonymization and encryption, the ability to ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience, the ability to restore availability and access to data after an incident, verification processes (not audits), evaluation, and assessment of the effectiveness of the measures.  
In any case, when assessing the adequacy of the level of security, particular attention shall be paid to the risks posed by the processing of data, as a consequence of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial damage.  
In this same regard, Recital 83 of the GDPR states that:  
“(83) In order to maintain security and prevent processing from infringing the provisions of this Regulation, the controller or processor must assess the inherent risks of the processing and implement measures to mitigate them, such as encryption. These measures must ensure an adequate level of security, including confidentiality, taking into account the state of the art and the cost of their implementation with respect to the risks and the nature of the personal data to be protected. When assessing the risk in relation to data security, the risks arising from the processing of personal data must be taken into account, such as the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data, particularly those that could cause physical, material, or immaterial damage.”

The entity CIRBOX uses digital certificates for access to the electronic headquarters of public administrations and in relation to these digital certificates, the entity CIRBOX, responsible for the platform, acts as a Registration Authority (RA).  
Indeed, a Registration Authority (RA) is an entity that unequivocally identifies the applicant for a certificate. The Registration Authority provides the Certification Authority with the verified data of the applicant so that the Certification Authority can issue the corresponding certificate, and UANATACA acts as the Certification Authority (CA).  
It should be noted that a Certification Authority (CA) is a trusted entity responsible for issuing and revoking digital certificates used in transactions and electronic signatures. This trust is achieved thanks to the figure of the CA, which acts as an intervening party in the relationship between companies or between companies and individuals. Thus, when any transaction occurs between two parties, the CA provides trust to the documents managed and signed as it is not an interested party.  
Thanks to the infrastructure of cryptographic keys that a Certification Authority has, the identity of the signer as well as the content of the transactions carried out are trusted and guaranteed.  
Some of the functions of the Certification Authority include: providing services such as the publication of certificates, lists of revoked certificates, validity checks of certificates, etc. Additionally, the CA records the exact date and time when a document has been electronically signed, which is known as timestamping.  
Likewise, the client companies of CIRBOX act as a Physical Verification Point (PVP) in order to verify the identity of the end user when applying for the electronic certificate.

In conclusion, it has been established that there are no rational indications of the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, and therefore, the initiation of a sanctioning procedure is not warranted.  
It should be noted that Administrative Sanctioning Law, due to its specificity, applies, with some nuances but without exceptions, the guiding principles of criminal law, making it clear that the principle of presumption of innocence is fully applicable.  
In this regard, the Constitutional Court, in Judgment 76/1990, considers that the right to the presumption of innocence entails “that the sanction is based on acts or incriminating evidence of the reproached conduct; that the burden of proof lies with the accuser, without anyone being obliged to prove their own innocence; and that any insufficiency in the results of the evidence presented, freely assessed by the sanctioning body, must result in an acquittal.” This principle is expressly enshrined for sanctioning administrative procedures in Article 53.2.b) of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations, which recognizes the interested party's right “To the presumption of non-existence of administrative liability until proven otherwise.”  
In summary, the application of the principle of presumption of innocence prevents the imputation of an administrative infringement when no evidence or indications have been obtained that derive from the existence of an infringement.  
In this case, from the actions taken and the documentation in the procedure, there is no inference of an infringing act by CIRBOX within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, therefore the complaint should be archived.
