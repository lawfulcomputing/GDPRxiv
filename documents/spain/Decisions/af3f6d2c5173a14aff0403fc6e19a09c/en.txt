II  
Applicable Legal Regime  
Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, establishes in its Article 2 a series of personal data processing activities to which it does not apply. Among these processing activities, subsection d) refers to those carried out "by competent authorities for the purposes of preventing, investigating, detecting, or prosecuting criminal offenses, or enforcing criminal penalties, including protection against threats to public security and their prevention."  
For processing of this nature, the current Organic Law 7/2021, of May 26, is applicable, through which Directive (EU) 2016/680 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data by competent authorities for the purposes of preventing, investigating, detecting, or prosecuting criminal offenses or enforcing criminal penalties, and the free movement of such data, is transposed into our legal system.  
In this regard, this organic law aims, according to its first article, "to establish the rules relating to the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data by competent authorities for the purposes of preventing, detecting, investigating, and prosecuting criminal offenses or enforcing criminal penalties, including protection and prevention against threats to public security."

In this sense, in the present case, the conditions set forth in the infringing type are met since, as mentioned earlier, the obligation to conduct an impact assessment on the protection of the personal data of the interested parties was violated despite the circumstances required by Article 35 being present, which implied, given the nature of the processing of the system as well as the personal data, a serious risk for those individuals.  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations for infractions, the infraction attributed prescribes after two years, in accordance with Article 63 of Organic Law 7/2021, which establishes this period for those infractions classified as serious, as is the case here.

This characteristic of independence derives from the functions attributed to the aforementioned figure, which, at a minimum, must be entrusted to them by the data controller, as enumerated in Article 42 of the same regulation:  
“The data controller shall assign to the data protection officer at least the following functions:  
a) Inform and advise the data controller and employees dealing with the same, regarding the obligations that they have under this Organic Law and other applicable data protection provisions.  
b) Supervise compliance with the provisions of this Organic Law and other applicable data protection provisions, as well as what is established in the data controller's policies regarding personal data protection, including the assignment of responsibilities, awareness and training of personnel participating in the processing operations, and the corresponding audits.  
c) Provide the requested advice regarding the data protection impact assessment and supervise its execution.  
d) Cooperate with the data protection authority under the terms of the current legislation.  
e) Act as a contact point for the data protection authority on matters related to processing, including the prior consultation referred to in Article 36, and carry out consultations, where appropriate, on any other matter.”  
From the reading of this article, it follows that this enumeration is not exhaustive; on the contrary, it is possible to assign other types of functions related to the supervisory and advisory role. However, it is consistent with its nature that the assignment of these functions should not affect its independence or create a conflict of interest. “In the second place, it is no less true that the data controller or their representative must ensure that these other functions and duties do not imply a ‘conflict of interest.’ Given the meaning of these terms in common language, it is appropriate to consider that, in accordance with the objective pursued by Article 38, paragraph 6, of the GDPR, the data protection officer cannot be entrusted with the execution of functions or duties that may impair the performance of the functions they exercise as a data protection officer.”

The determination of the existence of a conflict of interest, in the sense of Article 38, paragraph 6, of the GDPR, must be made on a case-by-case basis, based on an assessment of the entirety of the relevant circumstances, in particular, the organizational structure of the data controller or their representative and in light of all applicable regulations, including any policies of the latter.”  
In the case at hand, as evidenced by the documentation in the file of the present procedure, the alleged conflict of interest consists of allowing or assigning the defense in a sanctioning procedure regarding data protection to the DPO by formulating and presenting allegations against the initiation agreement of said procedure, which was directed towards the organization for which they provide services. In these terms, in order to determine the existence or not of a conflict of interest and following the ruling issued by the CJEU, it is necessary to analyze the entirety of the relevant circumstances of this situation.  
In this regard, we must first recall that the role of the DPO, as a general rule, is to supervise, advise, and inform about data protection practices within the organization, maintaining a neutral and impartial stance. In fact, the explanatory memorandum defines it as “the advisory and supervisory body or figure for data protection controllers.” In this sense, if the DPO assumes the defense of the organization in a sanctioning procedure, whether administrative or judicial, their ability to remain independent and impartial may be compromised.  
On the other hand, the presentation of allegations in defense of the organization in a sanctioning procedure regarding data protection may be seen as an alignment with its interests, rather than maintaining a position focused precisely on supervision and advising in this matter. In such a case, they could find themselves in the position of having to review and evaluate their own advice or the decisions they had previously supervised or approved. This circumstance creates a scenario of "judge and

part", where the DPO would be evaluating the adequacy of their own actions or recommendations, which results in a clear conflict of interest. Furthermore, there is a risk that the DPO, by becoming involved in the defense of the organization in a specific sanctioning procedure, may face internal pressures from the data controller. Such pressures can influence their decisions, compromising their autonomy and ability to act in the best interest of data protection. Similarly, the credibility of the DPO before the organization's employees and third parties (including the data protection authorities themselves) may be affected, which erodes trust in their ability to perform their functions in an objective and independent manner.  
The nature of the personal data processed in this case, which aims at the prevention, detection, investigation, and prosecution of criminal offenses and the enforcement of criminal sanctions, should not be overlooked. The nature of these matters, due to their direct impact on rights and freedoms, makes the independence and oversight of the DPO even more necessary. In fact, unlike what occurs with the GDPR, in the case of these matters, Law 7/2021 establishes as a general rule the mandatory existence of a DPO, except for judicial bodies or the Public Prosecutor's Office when the processing of personal data is carried out in the exercise of their judicial functions, as stated in Article 40.  
Finally, it is worth referencing the guidelines on data protection officers adopted by the Article 29 Data Protection Working Party, which, although also referring to the GDPR, is useful in terms of the interpretation and definition of "conflict of interest":  
"Article 38, paragraph 6, allows DPOs to 'perform other functions and tasks.' However, it requires that the organization ensures that 'such functions and tasks do not give rise to a conflict of interest.'  
The absence of a conflict of interest is closely linked to the requirement to act independently. Although DPOs may have other functions, they may only be assigned other tasks and responsibilities if these do not give rise to conflicts of interest.  
This means, in particular, that the DPO cannot hold a position in the organization that leads them to determine the purposes and means of processing personal data. Due to the specific organizational structure of each organization, this must be considered on a case-by-case basis.  
As a general rule, conflicting positions within an organization may include senior management positions (such as CEO, COO, CFO, CMO, head of the marketing department, head of human resources, or head of the IT department) but also other lower positions in the organizational structure if such positions lead to the determination of the purposes and means of processing.  
Additionally, a conflict of interest may arise, for example, if a DPO is asked to represent the data controller or the processor before the courts in cases related to data protection."  
IX  
Classification and Qualification  
Article 59, paragraph d) classifies the following infringement as very serious:  
“d) The failure to appoint a data protection officer in the terms provided in Article 40 or to enable their effective participation in all matters related to the protection of personal data, not supporting them or interfering with the performance of their functions.”  
According to the above, the attribution of functions to a Data Protection Officer (DPO) implies causing a conflict of interest, which can be considered an interference in their established functions, given their role as the principal or advisory and supervisory figure for data protection controllers, as recognized by Law 7/2021.  
Such interference occurs when the task of submitting allegations in the present sanctioning procedure is assigned to them, as the DPO is required to assume a role that significantly deviates from their supervisory and advisory functions. The new responsibility could interfere with their primary duties and diminish the independence that characterizes them.  
It should be noted that, as stated in this file, the initiation agreement was directed against the data controller and not against the DPO, which excludes any type of confusion and/or error between the said controller and this authority.  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations for infringements, the infringement attributed prescribes after two years, in accordance with Article 63 of Law 7/2021, which establishes this period for those infringements classified as serious, as is the case here.
