Examined the reconsideration appeal filed by THE PHONE HOUSE SPAIN, S.L. (hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated December 27, 2023, and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On December 27, 2023, a resolution was issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202306260, by virtue of which THE PHONE HOUSE SPAIN, S.L. (TPHS) with NIF B81846206 was imposed:  
- for a violation of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR, an administrative fine of €4,000,000 (four million euros).

4) Intent or negligence in the infringement  
As has been clearly demonstrated throughout the processing of the entire sanctioning procedure, the culpable conduct attributable to the appellant has been established, whose legal response is contained in the contested resolution. It is worth recalling what we stated on page 213.  
Regarding the alleged lack of negligence, it is noted that the consideration of its existence has been perfectly argued throughout the sanctioning procedure, both in the Initiation Agreement and in the Proposal for Resolution, as the absence of certain measures, as well as deficiencies in others, reflects a clear lack of the duty of diligence that must be required of a company with the nature of data processing it carries out, as well as due to the massive nature of these.  
And this is without accepting that the condition of victim or harmed by the cyberattack allows evading its responsibility for the lack of measures that could have at least mitigated the consequences of the personal data breach.  
5) Linkage of the infringer's activity with the processing of personal data  
The appellant argues that its activity cannot be considered, in a generic manner, as an aggravating factor and points out that the aforementioned Guidelines 4/2022 exclude the specific activity and business sector of the infringer as a criterion to be considered in the imposition and quantification of sanctions for the infringement of data protection regulations.  
The appellant seems to overlook the fact that the circumstance taken into consideration is not the activity it performs in itself, but the clear fact that, in its execution, it carries out massive processing of personal data. And it is evident that, being a company accustomed to processing personal data, its requirement for diligence must be greater.  
In short, as argued in the contested resolution:  
It should be noted that for the purposes of deciding the imposition of an administrative fine, the same consideration cannot be given to an infringement committed by a natural person or a small company not accustomed to processing personal data, as to a large company like TPHS, accustomed to processing personal data of millions of clients and non-clients, with a long history in this regard. Of course, it is considered that the infringement is more serious.

therefore, when determining the amount of the fines, not only the business level of the companies is taken into account. This is just one more circumstance.
