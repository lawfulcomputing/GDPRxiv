Considering the complaint filed on October 2, 2022, with this Agency by Mr. A.A.A. against UNITARIMA, S.A. for not having duly attended to his right of rectification.  

After carrying out the procedural actions provided for in Title VIII of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the following FACTS have been established:  

FIRST: Mr. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) exercised the right of access before UNITARIMA, S.A. with NIF A82323049 (hereinafter, the respondent).  
Once this right of access was attended to, the complainant again addressed the respondent requesting the rectification of “Signature of work report No. XXXXX dated 20/01/2022 where it indicates through my signature that I am the responsible person of the construction company A.A.A. when that is not correct,” without receiving a response.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of the LOPDGDD, which has provided a mechanism prior to the admission of complaints filed with the AEPD, consisting of transferring them to the Data Protection Delegates designated by the controllers or processors of the data, for the purposes provided for in Article 37 of the aforementioned regulation, or to them when they have not been designated, the complaint was forwarded to the respondent entity for analysis and to provide a response to the complainant and to this Agency within one month.  
The respondent indicates that, once the right of access was attended to, a request was subsequently addressed, dated June 20, 2022, for the right of erasure on June 23, 2022, and an exercise of rectification, which has led to the present procedure.  
It states that, on July 19, 2022, an email was sent to the complainant, as this was the means used to exercise the right, explaining that the rectification of his signature is not appropriate.  
“According to what is stated in the GDPR, personal data must be rectified when it is incorrect. The data of the signature we understand is correct, since according to you it is your signature, but you have included it in a document where it should not appear (the work report you refer to), since as indicated in the document itself (CONFORME, The person in charge of the Construction Company) that is where it should be signed.”

SECOND: In accordance with the provisions of Article 55 of the GDPR, the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to perform the functions that

In accordance with this regulation, prior to the admission of the complaint that gives rise to this procedure, it was forwarded to the responsible entity for analysis, requiring a response to this Agency within one month and to demonstrate that the necessary response had been provided to the complainant, in the event of exercising the rights regulated in articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR.  
This admission agreement determines the opening of the present procedure for the lack of attention to a request for the exercise of the rights established in articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR, regulated in article 64.1 of the LOPDGDD, according to which:  
“1. When the procedure refers exclusively to the lack of attention to a request for the exercise of the rights established in articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, it will be initiated by an admission agreement, which will be adopted in accordance with the provisions of the following article.  
In this case, the deadline for resolving the procedure will be six months from the date on which the admission agreement was notified to the complainant. After this period, the interested party may consider their complaint accepted.”  
The clarification of administrative responsibilities within the framework of a sanctioning procedure is not deemed appropriate, whose exceptional nature implies that, whenever possible, preference should be given to alternative mechanisms that are supported by current regulations.  
It is the exclusive competence of this Agency to assess whether there are administrative responsibilities that should be clarified in a sanctioning procedure and, consequently, the decision regarding its initiation, with no obligation to initiate a procedure upon any request made by a third party. Such a decision must be based on the existence of elements that justify the initiation of sanctioning activity, circumstances that do not occur in the present case, considering that with the present procedure the guarantees and rights of the complainant are duly restored.

The formal aspects related to the exercise of these rights are established in Articles 12 of the GDPR and 12 of the LOPDGDD.  
Additionally, consideration is given to what is expressed in Recitals 59 and following of the GDPR.  
In accordance with the provisions of these regulations, the data controller must establish formulas and mechanisms to facilitate the data subject's exercise of their rights, which will be free of charge (without prejudice to the provisions of Articles 12.5 and 15.3 of the GDPR), and is obliged to respond to requests made no later than one month, unless it can demonstrate that it is unable to identify the data subject, and must express its reasons in case it does not intend to comply with such request. The burden of proof for compliance with the duty to respond to the request for the exercise of rights made by the affected party lies with the data controller.  
The communication directed to the data subject regarding their request must be expressed in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible manner, using clear and simple language.  
FOURTH: Article 16 of the GDPR, which regulates the right to rectification of inaccurate personal data, establishes the following:  
"The data subject shall have the right to obtain from the data controller, without undue delay, the rectification of inaccurate personal data concerning them. Taking into account the purposes of the processing, the data subject shall have the right to have incomplete personal data completed, including by means of a supplementary statement."  
FIFTH: In the case analyzed here, the claimant exercised their right to rectification, arguing that they had signed a work report in a place that did not correspond to them.  
During the processing of this procedure, although initially the responding party indicated that "(...) the only way to 'rectify' that document would be to remove their signature by some manual means, since it is a paper document," it later sent an email to the claimant indicating that they would proceed to address their right, stating that "(...) they will proceed to limit its processing (block the data solely for the purposes of its conservation) in order to address possible liabilities arising from the processing of their data (...)"  
As a consequence of the above, it is considered that the claimant's request for their signature not to appear in the work report where they stamped it has been addressed, and therefore, it is appropriate to uphold, for formal reasons, the complaint submitted.  
Regarding the claimant's statements that the signature appearing on the delivery proof issued by the postal service should be directed to the entity issuing that delivery proof to raise that issue.
