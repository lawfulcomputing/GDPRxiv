However, ex novo, the existence of other photographs that are completely unknown to this party is now asserted, specifically one related to a window facing outward, without further specification, which generates the consequent defenselessness.  
In this regard, it is important to remember that when analyzing the possibilities of defense in a procedure, even if it is one of warning, it is necessary to distinguish two major aspects of defenselessness: the right to accusation and the right to defense.  
The right to accusation in Article 64.2 of the LPAC includes the right of the accused to be informed of the accusation, a guarantee that is explicitly enshrined in Article 24.2 of the CE, and which arises from various international instruments and declarations, having been recognized and proclaimed by numerous constitutional and ordinary rulings that have also highlighted the extension of this principle rooted in the criminal field to the administrative sanctioning sphere.  
In this sense, it is worth citing the STC 23/2007 of February 12:  
"Among the guarantees transferable to the administrative sanctioning procedure, the TC includes [...] the right to know the charges formulated against the accused and the consequent right to the invariability of the essential facts subject to accusation and sanction. It is also required that the charge sheet contain the essential elements of the sanctionable fact and its legal qualification to allow the exercise of the right to defense [...]. Therefore, there can be no implicit or tacit accusation, but the accusation must be formulated expressly and in terms that are not vague or indeterminate, although it is not required that the facts subject to accusation be exhaustively detailed, but it is sufficient that the charge sheet contains the relevant and essential facts to make a legal qualification."  
Thus, having established the constitutive elements of the right to accusation, the problems that the case law presents mostly relate to the degree of precision or detail required in the information of the facts, as the issue lies in verifying whether the information provided to the accused complies with the guarantee, that is, in the discussion about whether the description of the data of the accusation must be maximally detailed or if it is sufficient for it to be succinct; the solution will always result from the combination of two concepts: the sufficiency of the information and the absence of defenselessness; in reality, two sides of the same coin.  
In this regard, in the notification of the initiation agreement now communicated, the information about the facts is not sufficiently fulfilled since, unless it is a mistake and pertains to another file, the facts have not been effectively reflected, having been provided at the time only one photograph related to the area of the video intercom and now referring to unknown photographs, one of which, it seems, pertains to a camera located in a window, with it being unknown whether it belongs to the residence of the accused or which window is being referred to, which causes confusion and, above all, a great deal of insecurity, violating the reasonable and proportionate level of defense that can be deployed against such an accusation lacking in specificity, and this injury entails a violation of a fundamental right, resulting in absolute nullity of the act that commits it.

The act of initiation is the instrument that materializes the fundamental right to be informed of the content of the accusation, enshrined in Article 24.2 of the Constitution.  
This right ensures that every accused individual has full knowledge of the reasons that have led the competent authority to decide to initiate a procedure aimed at determining their responsibility for the alleged commission of an administrative infraction, as well as the set of procedural rights that correspond to them in such a situation.  
It is a fundamental guarantee that derives from the more generic right of defense, to the extent that it is not possible to defend oneself against what is not known (STC 116/2007). This right requires that the accused's knowledge of the accusation against them occurs as soon as possible, as provided by Article 6.3 of the European Convention on Human Rights.  
The right to be informed of the accusation implies that the initiation agreement must be the most complete possible manifestation of the assessment carried out by the Administration regarding what it considers a possible infraction, and not a generic reference to a compilation of facts or information contained in a report-proposal that lacks sufficient capacity to rationally substantiate the Administration's suspicions. If the right to be informed of the accusation demands that this information be made known to the accused as soon as possible, its content would be disregarded if the Administration could systematically resort to the exception provided in Article 64.3 of the LPAC, which allows for the legal qualification of the facts to be postponed to a later time through the charge sheet, which, for this reason, must be interpreted restrictively.  
Thus, the act of initiation must be a complete administrative decision in all its component elements and, for this reason, self-sufficient to convey to the accused a concrete and specific accusation, which is closely linked to the requirement of its proper motivation.  
If every accused individual in a sanctioning procedure has the right to be informed of the accusation as soon as possible, this right is not satisfied by mere formal or ritual compliance, but must respect a certain and specific content that ensures a thorough understanding of the accusation and allows for the exercise of the right of defense (Alarcón Sotomayor).  
In this regard, the LPAC establishes in its Article 64.2.b) that the initiation agreement must include: "The facts that motivate the initiation of the procedure, their possible qualification, and the sanctions that may correspond, without prejudice to what results from the instruction."  
The content of the initiation agreement must be specific in determining the relevant facts attributed to the accused (Nieto García, 2012, p. 500) as well as in the legal interpretation of the same, although they may vary as a consequence of the development of the instruction proceedings of the respective procedure.

The facts that motivate the initiation must be presented clearly and intelligibly and must be sufficiently accredited. The Administration will not suffice with merely alluding to a relevant fact; it must indicate the means through which it is rationally demonstrable and provide the accused with knowledge of the evidence against them for corresponding debate. The mere mention of relevant facts without them being duly accredited places the accused in a position of defenselessness, as they must practically demonstrate that such a fact did not occur.  
The possible qualification of the facts as constitutive of an administrative infraction must be the manifestation of a subsumption judgment, through which the relevant facts selected by the Administration end up fitting into the norm typifying an administrative infraction. It is about exhibiting a reasoning that, starting from the identified and proven relevant facts, can reach a provisional conclusion, certainly, about the commission of an administrative infraction attributable to the accused; that is, a sufficient motivation is required, explaining how the conduct of the accused affected a particular norm (Alarcón Sotomayor).

The image is a personal data  
The physical image of a person, according to Article 4.1 of the GDPR, is a personal data and its protection is therefore subject to this Regulation.  
In Article 4.2 of the GDPR, the concept of "processing" of personal data is defined, indicating:  
“‘processing’: any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure or destruction;”  
Images generated by a system of cameras or video cameras are personal data, so their processing is subject to data protection regulations.  
It is therefore pertinent to analyze whether the processing of personal data (images of individuals) carried out through the reported video surveillance system complies with the provisions established in the GDPR.  

IV  
Violation of Article 5.1 c) GDPR  
Article 5.1 c) GDPR states the following: Personal data shall be:  
“adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’).”  
Regarding processing for video surveillance purposes, Article 22 of the LOPDGDD establishes that natural or legal persons, public or private, may carry out the processing of images through camera or video camera systems for the purpose of ensuring the safety of persons and property, as well as their facilities.  
The processing of personal data is subject to the other principles of processing contained in Article 5 of the GDPR. We will highlight the principle of data minimization contained in Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR, which states that personal data shall be “adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed.”  
This means that in a specific processing, only the relevant personal data that is appropriate and strictly necessary to fulfill the purpose for which it is processed may be processed. The processing must be adjusted and proportional to the purpose it is directed towards. The relevance in the processing of data must occur both at the time of data collection and in the subsequent processing carried out on them.

In accordance with the above, the processing of excessive data must be restricted or the data must be deleted.  
The application of the principle of data minimization in the area of video surveillance implies that images of public roads cannot be captured, since the processing of images in public places can only be carried out by the Security Forces and Corps with prior governmental authorization.  
On some occasions, for the protection of private spaces, where cameras have been installed on facades or indoors, it may be necessary to record a portion of the public road to ensure the purpose of security.  
That is to say, cameras and video cameras installed for security purposes may not obtain images of the public road unless it is essential for that purpose, or it is impossible to avoid it due to their location. And, in such an extraordinary case, the cameras may only capture the minimum portion necessary to preserve the safety of persons and property, as well as their facilities.  
Under no circumstances will the use of surveillance practices beyond the environment subject to the installation be permitted, and in particular, it must not affect surrounding public spaces, adjacent buildings, and vehicles other than those accessing the monitored space.  
The installed cameras may not obtain images of private spaces belonging to third parties and/or public spaces without a duly justified cause, nor may they affect the privacy of passersby freely moving through the area.  
Therefore, the placement of cameras directed towards the private property of neighbors with the intention of intimidating them or affecting their private sphere without justified cause is not permitted.  
Images may also not be captured or recorded in spaces owned by third parties without the consent of their owners, or, where applicable, of the persons present in them.  
In this case, the documentation included in the file proves that the complainant has installed a video surveillance camera that captured a public road space, according to the details noted in the proven facts, which constitutes a violation of the aforementioned Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR.  
Once the present warning procedure was initiated, the complainant proceeded to reorient the camera so that it no longer captured public road space. However, this does not prevent the indicated violation from being considered consummated.

In accordance with the above, the processing of images through a video surveillance system, in order to comply with current regulations, must meet the following requirements:  
1.- Natural or legal persons, public or private, may establish a video surveillance system for the purpose of preserving the safety of individuals and property, as well as their facilities.  
It must be assessed whether the intended purpose can be achieved in a less intrusive manner for the rights and freedoms of citizens. Personal data should only be processed if the purpose of the processing cannot be reasonably achieved by other means, considering Recital 39 of the GDPR.  
“Personal data should only be processed if the purpose of the processing cannot be reasonably achieved by other means.”  
2.- The images obtained cannot be used for a subsequent purpose incompatible with the one that motivated the installation of the video surveillance system.  
3.- The processing of images through the installation of camera or video camera systems must be lawful and comply with the principles of proportionality and data minimization, as already indicated.  
4.- The images may be retained for a maximum period of one month, except in cases where they must be retained to prove the commission of acts that threaten the integrity of individuals, property, or facilities.  
In this second case, they must be made available to the competent authority within a maximum period of 72 hours from the moment knowledge of the existence of the recording is obtained.  
5.- When the system is connected to an alarm center, it may only be installed by a private security company that meets the requirements set forth in Article 5 of Law 5/2014 on Private Security, dated April 4.

In order for the information duty provided for in Article 12 of the GDPR to be fulfilled in a concise and understandable manner for the affected party, the aforementioned Article 22 of the LOPDGDD provides for a "layered information" system in relation to video surveillance.  
In this regard, the first layer must refer, at a minimum, to the existence of the processing (video surveillance), the identity of the data controller, the possibility of exercising the rights provided for in Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR, and where to obtain more information about the processing of personal data.  
The information in the second layer must be available in a location easily accessible to the affected party, whether it be an information sheet at a reception, cashier, etc., placed in a visible public space or on a website, and must refer to the remaining elements of Article 13 of the GDPR.  
It is not necessary to specify the precise location of the video surveillance equipment.  
This information duty will be considered fulfilled by placing an informational device in a sufficiently visible location, and at a minimum, at the entrances to the monitored areas, whether interior or exterior. If the monitored space has several entrances, the distinguishing sign of the monitored area must be placed at each of them.  
This information must be provided in advance - Recital 39 of the GDPR. The objective is to make the context of the surveillance clear.  
In this case, the analysis of the images from the field of view of the cameras of the video surveillance system has confirmed that one of the cameras captured a portion of public road, which, according to the claimant, as the responsible party for the installed system, was obliged to comply with the information duty established in Article 13 of the GDPR and in Article 22.4 of the LOPDGDD, by installing the required informational sign.

Without prejudice to the provisions of Article 83 of the GDPR, the aforementioned Regulation provides in paragraph 2.b) of Article 58 “Powers” the following:  
“Each supervisory authority shall have all of the following corrective powers indicated below:  
(…)  
b) issue a warning to any data controller or processor when the processing operations have infringed the provisions of this Regulation;  
(…)”  
Furthermore, Article 64 of the LOPDGDD, which regulates the “Form of initiation of the procedure and duration,” states in its third paragraph that:  
“3. When appropriate, taking into account the nature of the facts and duly considering the criteria established in Article 83.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, the Spanish Data Protection Agency, after hearing the data controller or processor, may issue a warning, as well as order the data controller or processor to adopt corrective measures aimed at ending the possible non-compliance with data protection legislation in a specified manner and within the specified timeframe.  
The procedure shall have a maximum duration of six months from the date of the initiation agreement. After that period, it will expire, and consequently, the file of actions will occur.  
The provisions of the second and third paragraphs of paragraph 2 of this article shall apply in this case.”  
In this case, given the circumstances present in this procedure, it is deemed appropriate to issue a warning to the respondent for the infringements of Articles 5.1.c) and 13 of the GDPR.
