LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Preliminary Issues  
In the present case, in accordance with Article 4.1 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, since the respondent carries out, among other processing activities, the collection, registration, storage, use, access, and deletion of personal data of individuals, such as: name and surname, N.I.F, postal address, telephone number, email address, etc.  
The respondent carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines "personal data security breaches" (hereinafter personal data breach) as "all breaches of security that lead to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration of, or unauthorized disclosure or access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed."  
In this case, there is a personal data breach under the circumstances indicated above, categorized as a confidentiality breach due to the exposure of the claimant's personal data to an unauthorized third party.  
Within the principles of processing provided for in Article 5 of the GDPR, the integrity and confidentiality of personal data are guaranteed in paragraph 1.f) of the aforementioned article. For its part, the security of personal data is regulated in Article 32 of the GDPR, which governs the security of processing.  
III  
Allegations to the Initiation Agreement  
In response to the allegations presented by the respondent entity, the following should be noted:  
The respondent entity claims that the documentation provided shows the measures taken with the intention of rectifying the error made, both internally and with the staff, thereby ensuring compliance with the GDPR.  
It details below the documentation it attaches.  
Upon analyzing the documentation, it should be noted that part of the documentation that it claims to provide in its statement of allegations is not received, and the documentation it does provide does not substantiate the controls and security measures associated with the confidentiality of the information directed at employees. There is also no report on the technical and organizational measures adopted to prevent similar incidents, implementation dates, and controls carried out to verify their effectiveness. Finally, there is also no record of the notification of the personal data breach to this Agency.  
It is evident that the mere mention of such documentation does not constitute or substantiate the implementation of such measures.  
This Agency is surprised that nothing has been provided to date despite the requests made both during the preliminary proceedings and in the allegations phase.  
Consequently, the information provided allows us to affirm that there is a deficiency in the compliance with the proactive obligations that the current data protection regulations require of the data controller. The principle of proactive responsibility is the central axis of the GDPR and requires, for its compliance, a conscious, diligent, and continuous attitude on the part of the entities. This attitude is not based solely on reacting when something occurs, but requires always being ahead, which means that, preventively, security measures and controls must be implemented to guarantee the privacy of personal data. And to demonstrate compliance, a matter that has not been substantiated.

“1. Personal data shall be:

f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, by applying appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).”  
In this case, it is established that the personal data of the complainant was improperly exposed to a third party, as the complaint of the customer (here the complainant) was merged with that of another customer, which caused that other customer to receive copies of the communications maintained with the complainant, which included their personal data (name and surname, phone number, postal address, and email).

VIII  
Obligation Breached  
Article 32 "Security of Processing" of the GDPR establishes:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the varying probabilities and severity of risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly verifying, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a consequence of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this Article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data only processes such data on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law.” (the underlining is ours)  

It should be noted that the GDPR in the cited provision does not establish a list of security measures applicable according to the data being processed, but rather establishes that the controller and the processor shall implement technical and organizational measures that are appropriate to the risk posed by the processing, taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, and the probabilities and severity of risks to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.  
Furthermore, the security measures must be adequate and proportionate to the identified risk, indicating that the determination of technical and organizational measures must be made taking into account: pseudonymization and encryption, the ability to ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience, the ability to restore availability and access to data after an incident, and a process of verification (which is not an audit), assessment, and evaluation of the effectiveness of the measures.  
In any case, in assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, as a consequence of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial damage.

In this same sense, Recital 83 of the GDPR states that “(83) In order to maintain security and prevent processing from infringing the provisions of this Regulation, the controller or the processor must assess the inherent risks of the processing and implement measures to mitigate them, such as encryption. These measures must ensure an adequate level of security, including confidentiality, taking into account the state of the art and the cost of their implementation in relation to the risks and the nature of the personal data that needs to be protected. When assessing the risk in relation to data security, the risks arising from the processing of personal data must be taken into account, such as the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data, which may particularly cause physical, material, or immaterial damage.”  
In the present case, AAW is engaged in online sales through the internet of certain products, electronically addressing the claims made by customers, which poses a risk in the processing of personal data that must be evaluated and taken into account.  
Therefore, it must exercise special diligence in the technical and organizational measures to ensure adequate security in the processing of personal data, establishing appropriate procedures and measures for this purpose. Among others, establishing a secure system in the post-sale claims management process.  
According to the defendant's own statements, it uses a system to respond to customers via tickets, which are managed manually, and when a customer sends several different emails or multiple messages through different channels, the person in charge of managing them “merges” some tickets and others from the same customer to maintain a single thread of conversation.  
Therefore, at the time of the breach, it cannot be stated that the defendant had adequate security measures in place based on the potential estimated risks to prevent the incident, specifically regarding the process of handling customer claims, since it is carried out through a manual process and, according to their statements, it is not unusual for customers to send messages through different channels, resulting in the generation of different tickets related to the same claim, with the person in charge merging them and managing them in a single thread of conversation, this system being susceptible to errors, as has been demonstrated in the case at hand, which allowed a third party to access the personal data of the claimant.  
The responsibility of the defendant is therefore determined by the security breach evidenced in the claim and by the lack of sufficient accreditation, through the documentation provided, of the controls and security measures aimed at preventing incidents like the one that occurred, since it is responsible for making decisions intended to effectively implement the appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an adequate level of security relative to the risk.  
Consequently, it is considered that the established facts constitute an infringement, attributable to the defendant, for violation of Article 32 of the GDPR.
