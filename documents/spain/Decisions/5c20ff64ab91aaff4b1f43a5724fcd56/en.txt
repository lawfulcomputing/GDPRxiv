RESOLUTION OF SANCTIONING PROCEDURE  
From the procedure conducted by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST: On April 11, 2023, six complaints were filed with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection regarding a possible infringement attributable to MAD COOL FESTIVAL, S.L. with NIF B87381042 (hereinafter, MAD COOL).  
The facts brought to the attention of this authority are as follows:  
The complainants state that they were affected by the personal data breach that occurred on the website https://www.madcoolarea.es/area/ on April 11, 2023.  
On that day, MAD COOL opened said webpage to confirm the shipping details of wristbands for the music festival it was organizing. Once users entered their access data (email account and entry code), they could view the personal data of other individuals who were also registered on the platform.  
Along with the submissions, the following is provided:  
- Screenshots of the platform of the respondent at the time of the incident, showing third-party data (name, surname, email, phone number, postal address, postal code, city, and country).  
- An email sent by the respondent on April 11, 2023, urging access to its webpage to confirm the postal address for the shipment of wristbands.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), this complaint was forwarded to MAD COOL for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
MAD COOL responded to the request by written communication dated June 23 and 30, 2023.  

THIRD: On July 10, 2023, in accordance with Article 65 of the LOPDGDD, the complaint was admitted for processing.

1.- Notification of the breach.

2.- Analysis of the breach.  
2.1. Chronology of events.  
The chronology of the events of the incident according to the General Subdirectorate for Data Inspection is as follows:  
On April 11, 2023, MAD COOL launched a confirmation form for the personal data of users who purchased tickets for the festival taking place in July of that year. At the time of this launch, the security incident occurred that caused the breach.  
The origin of the problem was due to the fact that during the morning of the launch of the web form, a migration of the platform to a new server was carried out, and during that migration, an existing micro-cache on that server was not deactivated. MAD COOL states regarding this server configuration error: “it is, therefore, a human error, isolated and completely unintentional.”  
This 2-second micro-cache caused that from the moment a user accessed the form, for a maximum of 2 seconds, subsequent users accessing it could see their data. Once those 2 seconds elapsed, the user's data would no longer be displayed. According to the statements provided: “When a user logged in to the form, the next users accessing during the following two seconds could see another person's data instead of their own. Users only saw their own data when no one had logged in before those two seconds.”

These actions were carried out by the data processor, who managed the incident at a technical level. III  
Unfulfilled Obligation. Integrity and Confidentiality  
Letter f) of Article 5.1 of the GDPR states:  
"1. Personal data shall be:  
(...)  
f) processed in such a way as to ensure an adequate level of security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, through the application of appropriate technical or organizational measures ('integrity and confidentiality')."  
The aforementioned principle of confidentiality obliges data controllers to ensure that personal data is processed securely, preventing unauthorized access, misuse, or disclosure to unauthorized third parties. This includes implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect the data against potential personal data breaches, both external and internal.

Article 32 of the GDPR stipulates the following:  
"1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying probability and severity to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly verifying, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a consequence of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data only processes such data on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law."  

The aforementioned Article 32 of the GDPR aims to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, in relation to the processing of personal data, taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying probability and severity to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. Thus, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational security measures to ensure such a level of security appropriate to the risk. It is worth noting that for this article to be considered breached, it is not necessary for there to have been improper processing or a personal data breach; rather, the mere failure to adopt or implement appropriate technical and organizational measures constitutes a violation of the aforementioned article.  

In the present case, despite the existing security measures prior to the personal data breach, such as the implementation of HTTPS and CSRF protection, the absence of a security measure that was subsequently adopted reactively after the occurrence of the personal data breach is noteworthy.

Independent security from the personal data breach: the implementation of VPN to secure communications between authorized users and servers. Thus, during the course of the investigative proceedings, the defendant stated the following:  
“Regarding the development environment, the data controller states that additional measures have been adopted such as (…) and reinforcement actions in the production deployment of new versions, such as agile methodologies and code review.”  
From this statement, it follows that the entity adopted the implementation of a VPN reactively, after the personal data breach, which shows that this measure was not in place beforehand. The nature of the activity carried out, which includes remote access and the management of sensitive personal data in a server environment, makes the adoption of a VPN an essential measure to ensure the security of communications and prevent unauthorized access.  
The implementation of a VPN in this context allows for an additional layer of protection, ensuring that connections between users and servers are made in an encrypted and secure manner. This is particularly relevant in activities where access to the technological environment poses an inherent risk of exposure or intrusion if measures like this are not adopted. By not having a VPN prior to the incident, a level of security proportional to the risk would not have been guaranteed, which would constitute a breach of Article 32 of the GDPR.  
As previously indicated, Article 32 requires the adoption of appropriate technical and organizational security measures to ensure a level of security adequate to the risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons arising from the processing of personal data. The absence of this basic security measure, as the VPN is widely recognized as a standard practice for protecting data in similar technological environments, would highlight a defect in the identification, assessment, and management of risks by the entity. The lack of this security measure is independent of the personal data breach, as its absence neither enabled nor had a direct impact on the personal data breach.  
Consequently, the omission of its implementation would constitute a violation of the aforementioned Article 32 of the GDPR, regardless of whether the personal data breach occurred or not, for failing to adopt appropriate security measures to ensure a level of security adequate to the risk; in this case, the non-implementation of a VPN.  
Therefore, in accordance with the evidence available at this time of resolution of the sanctioning procedure, it is considered that the known facts constitute an infringement, attributable to MAD COOL, for violation of the previously transcribed article.
