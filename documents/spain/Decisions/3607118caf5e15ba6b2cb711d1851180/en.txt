BACKGROUND  
FIRST: A.A.A., B.B.B., and C.C.C. (hereinafter, the claiming parties) respectively submitted written complaints to the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on January 5, 6, and 10, 2023. The complaints were directed against D.D.D. with NIF ***NIF.1 (hereinafter, the claimed party).  
The complainants stated in their writings that they received an email on January 4, 2023, from the address ***EMAIL.1, which included multiple email addresses as recipients, among which were the addresses of the complainants themselves, and in which the email addresses of the other recipients to whom it was addressed were visible.  
They claim that the mentioned email addresses are visible because the BCC functionality was not used in the aforementioned email, which allows for the concealment of recipients.  
Along with these complaints, a copy of the mentioned email dated January 4, 2023, was provided, where approximately 151 email addresses are listed as recipients, including the email addresses of the complainants, with the subject “COMMUNICATION REPLACEMENT SHOW SHEA COULEÉ BARCELONA” and sent from the address ***EMAIL.1.

PROVEN FACTS  
FIRST: From the instruction of this procedure, as well as from the investigative actions and the documents in the file, it is evidenced that an email was sent on January 4, 2023, from the address ***EMAIL.1, an address that was intended for a plurality of recipients, among which were the complaining parties, and in which the email addresses of the other recipients to whom it was addressed could be visualized. The email addresses appeared visible because the BCC functionality was not used in the aforementioned email, which allows each recipient to remain unaware of the other recipients.  
THIRD: Likewise, it is evidenced that the mentioned address ***EMAIL.1 through which the email subject to this procedure was sent, is linked to the domain (...), which is owned by the complained party, through a web hosting service.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, and 64.3 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Unfulfilled Obligations  
Article 5 of the GDPR establishes the principles relating to processing, indicating, among other issues, that personal data shall be:  
“f) processed in such a way as to ensure an adequate level of security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, by applying appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).”  
Similarly, Article 32 of the GDPR expressly refers to the security of processing:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the varying risks of probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk.”
5/8  
appropriate organizational measures to ensure an adequate level of security relative to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regular verification, evaluation, and assessment of the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the adequacy of the level of security, particular consideration will be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, particularly as a consequence of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.”  
In this case, it has been established that the respondent sent an email to multiple recipients without concealing their addresses, which allowed the addresses of others to be visible, thereby violating the confidentiality principle provided for in the aforementioned Article 5.1 f) of the GDPR regarding the processing of personal data.  
In this regard, to ensure this principle, the respondent should have adopted the relevant prior technical or organizational measures of all kinds that ensured adequate processing and prevented unauthorized or unlawful processing.  
On the other hand, the respondent, as the data controller of such data, should have adopted technical and organizational measures that ensured an adequate level of security relative to the risk, which, however, were not implemented. The absence of measures to ensure the level of security demonstrates non-compliance with the provision of Article 32 of the GDPR, since the considerable number of recipients to whom the email was addressed posed a risk that required the adoption of appropriate measures to prevent a personal data breach, as occurred in this case.  
III  
Classification and Qualification of the Violations  
On one hand, the non-compliance with the confidentiality principle provided for in the aforementioned Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR constitutes an infringement attributable to the party D.D.D., as provided in Article 83.5 of the same regulation, which expressly states:  
“Violations of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent in accordance with Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9;”  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations for infringements, this infringement is subject to a three-year limitation period, in accordance with Article 72.1.a) of the LOPDGDD, which qualifies the following conduct as very serious:  
“a) The processing of personal data in violation of the principles and guarantees established in Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.”  
On the other hand, the failure to adopt the technical and organizational measures to ensure an adequate level of security relative to the risk as provided in Article 32 of the GDPR constitutes an infringement as provided in Article 83.4, which states, “Violations of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 10,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to 2% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the obligations of the controller and the processor in accordance with Articles 8, 11, 25 to 39, 42, and 43;”  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations for infringements, the alleged infringement is subject to a two-year limitation period, in accordance with Article 73 f) of the LOPDGDD, which qualifies the following conduct as serious:  
“f) The failure to adopt those technical and organizational measures that are appropriate to ensure an adequate level of security relative to the risk of processing, in the terms required by Article 32.1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.”

V  
Adoption of measures
