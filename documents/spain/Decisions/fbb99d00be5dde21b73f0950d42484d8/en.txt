Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
Finally, the Fourth Additional Provision "Procedure in relation to the powers attributed to the Spanish Agency for Data Protection by other laws" establishes that: "The provisions in Title VIII and its implementing regulations shall apply to the procedures that the Spanish Agency for Data Protection must process in exercising the powers attributed to it by other laws."  

II  
Right to the protection of personal data and privacy in relation to unsolicited communications, traffic and location data, and subscriber directories.  
Article 66.1.b) of Law 11/2022, of June 28, General Telecommunications establishes:  
“Regarding the protection of personal data and privacy in relation to unsolicited communications, end users of publicly available interpersonal communication services based on numbering shall have the following rights:  
b) not to receive unwanted calls for commercial communication purposes, unless there is prior consent from the user themselves to receive such commercial communications or unless the communication can be justified under another legal basis provided for in Article 6.1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 on the processing of personal data.”  
In turn, Article 6.1 of the GDPR states:  
“Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.”  
In the case at hand, the complainant indicated that they had received numerous commercial calls from the operator YOIGO and provided a recording in which the complainant could be heard denying being a customer of YOIGO.  
They also provided copies of invoices from the company PARLEM, stating that it was their telephone operator.  
In the preliminary proceedings, XFERA stated that the complainant is registered as a customer of the PARLEM brand since 2021 with five lines, both fixed and mobile.  
It adds that XFERA is a telecommunications operator providing the services contracted by PARLEM customers. In this regard, it states that PARLEM acts as a marketer, under its own brand, of XFERA's services.  
For these purposes, PARLEM and XFERA signed a collaboration agreement for the marketing of mobile telephone services in the white label modality dated October 29, 2014, and a collaboration and service provision agreement for the marketing of convergent and fixed electronic communications services in the white label modality dated June 18, 2018.  
It adds that PARLEM, within the framework of these contracts, is configured as the data processor, and there is an obligation to inform in all cases that the service provider under these agreements is XFERA, providing an extract of this last contract to substantiate this circumstance.  
Thus, in this context, the customer enters into a contract with PARLEM for the services provided by it, and with XFERA or another operator that may provide the services that PARLEM does not cover.  
Regarding the services provided by XFERA, it states that they are provided directly by XFERA to the customer, but under the PARLEM brand.  
The customer establishes a contractual relationship with XFERA by accepting its general contracting conditions, with PARLEM acting as the data processor.

III  
Conclusion  
The claiming party is a client of PARLEM, which is the brand in Catalonia of YOIGO, therefore this entity has their data, as they have contracted numerous telecommunications services with XFERA MÓVILES S.A.U., which acts as the data controller. XFERA offers telecommunications services through different brands, including PARLEM or YOIGO.  
There are no fraudulent contracts in the name of the affected party.  
On the other hand, in the call recorded by the affected party, they identify themselves as from the “YOIGO information area.” In it, the claiming party states that they are not a client of YOIGO, but it is also noted that they do not provide commercial information.  
Therefore, based on the information indicated in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found that substantiates the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
All of this is without prejudice to any possible subsequent actions that this Agency may carry out, applying the investigative and corrective powers it holds.  
Thus, in accordance with what has been indicated, by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, IT IS AGREED:
