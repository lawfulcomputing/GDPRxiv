In accordance with the functions conferred upon each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR) and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve these investigative actions.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  

II  
Preliminary Issues  
In the present case, in accordance with the provisions of Article 4.1 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data by including the personal data of the complaining party in the solvency file.  
Likewise, the responding party carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR: “data controller” or “controller”: the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing.  

III  
Obligation Breached. Lawfulness of Processing  
Regarding the lawfulness of the processing of personal data, Article 6.1 of the GDPR establishes the following:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in point f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the exercise of their functions.”  
In the same vein, Article 20 of the LOPDGDD indicates the following:  
Article 20. Credit Information Systems  
1. Unless proven otherwise, the processing of personal data relating to the non-fulfillment of monetary, financial, or credit obligations by common credit information systems shall be presumed lawful when the following requirements are met:  
c) That the creditor has informed the affected party in the contract or at the time of requesting payment about the possibility of inclusion in such systems, indicating those in which they participate.  
In the present case, the personal data of the complaining party would have been included in the solvency file at the request of the responding party without prior notification demanding the existing debt and warning of its inclusion in that file, which means that the processing would lack the required lawfulness as stated in the transcribed article.  
The complaining party requested the cancellation of the data from the data controller Experian on 14/07/2023, to which Experian responded on 20/07/2023. After conducting the relevant checks, they communicated that the cancellation had been carried out in their BADEXCUG file for the operations indicated below:

The existence of this debt was the reason for the inclusion of the identifying data of the claimant in the negative solvency files with which Vodafone collaborates, and it indicates that payment was requested from the claimant.  
The responding party states that it has verified with Experian, its collaborator, that a payment request for both debts was sent on 21/08/2023, sent to two addresses listed for the claimant, providing the delivery receipt to the company Correos, a document proving that a series of prior payment requests were processed and that the ones subject to this claim are included in that batch, being mass communications, sent by ordinary mail.  
In this regard, the responding party claims that this communication was made through Correos, although it has not proven the delivery of the same, the acknowledgment of receipt, nor its non-return through a certificate from the postal operator.  
Therefore, the described facts could constitute a violation of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, due to the alleged unlawfulness of the processing, since the notification of the inclusion of personal data in solvency files must be able to be proven by the responding entity, which can provide the following documents:  
- copy of the letter communicating the inclusion;  
- certificate from an external company to which the generation, mailing, and provision of the letter in the post or courier service has been entrusted (traceability), as well as the proof from the post or courier service of having received the letter;  
- and certificate from an external company of non-return of the letter.  
In this case, the responding party, in its response to the information request made by the AEPD, has not been able to prove these aspects, since the mere sending of the payment request by postal means does not prove its receipt, so the mandatory payment request prior to inclusion in the delinquent file cannot be considered made. However, the responding party states in its response to the transfer that, as a precautionary measure and as a demonstration of good practice and willingness, the identifying data of the responding party were removed from the solvency file as of October 20, 2023.  
Based on the above, Article 65.6 of the LOPDGDD could be applied since the responding party has taken measures to remove the claimant's data from credit information systems, so that, although it does not prove that the letter was not returned, in any case, it has proceeded with the removal.
