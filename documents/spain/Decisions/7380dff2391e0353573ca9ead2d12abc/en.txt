II  
Preliminary Issues  
In the present case, according to the provisions of articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, since COMERCIAL GIRONA, as the data controller, carries out the collection, storage, and communication of, among others, the following personal data of individuals: name, email address, and password of customers, among other processing activities.  
COMERCIAL GIRONA carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
Article 32 of the GDPR regulates the security measures that must be adopted to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk presented by the processing of personal data.  
III  
Security Measures  
Article 32 "Security of Processing" of the GDPR states:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly verifying, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a result of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data may only process such data on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law.”  
In the email sent by COMERCIAL GIRONA to the complainant notifying them of their registration as a customer, in addition to the registration email, the user's password is included.  
Regarding the security of email, the "Good Practices Report" from May 2021, CNN-CERT BP02, from the National Cryptologic Center, a service attached to the National Intelligence Center, whose mission is to contribute to the improvement of Spanish cybersecurity, outlines a series of vulnerabilities of email and the various ways in which they can be attacked, as well as security recommendations. In section 4.2 of this Report, "Security of Communications via Email" is described, with the following assertions on pages 37 to 39:  
“The protocol involved in this sending process is SMTP. This protocol has been used since 1982, and when it was implemented, security measures such as encryption or authentication of communications were not taken into account. This means that the entire sending process described above would be carried out in plain text, meaning that at any point in the transmission, an attacker could see and manipulate the content of the emails. Due to these shortcomings in SMTP, various technologies and extensions have been developed that allow for the incorporation of security measures to ensure authentication, integrity, and encryption of email communications. Some of the most well-known technologies are STARTTLS, SPF, DKIM, and DMARC… Although the most well-known email providers such as Google, Yahoo, and Outlook encrypt and authenticate emails using these types of technologies, many organizations continue to use email carelessly. It should also be noted that these technologies must be implemented both at the source and at the destination for them to be utilized. Furthermore, some of these measures are susceptible to attacks. For example, STARTTLS is vulnerable to downgrade attacks, where an attacker in a man-in-the-middle situation can force the TLS negotiation not to take place (it would be enough to replace the STARTTLS string).  
Even if the TLS communication is successfully established, the mail servers through which the email passes until it reaches its destination would have access to its content. Due to these facts, it is concluded that it is not sufficient to delegate the security of email to the underlying technologies responsible for delivering it to its recipient.”

In light of the security shortcomings mentioned above, it is evident that email is not an adequate means to ensure the integrity and confidentiality in the transmission of personal data, posing a risk to the clients of COMERCIAL GIRONA, to whom their password is sent along with the username via email when they register on the website of the aforementioned entity. The fact that the email is only sent to the client who has entered the data does not guarantee that the data cannot be intercepted.  
On the other hand, sending passwords to the email addresses of COMERCIAL GIRONA's clients means that they can be stored in that medium, which constitutes a practice that may pose a risk to clients, as these passwords, along with their respective usernames, would be vulnerable to any type of cyber attack. It would be of no use for COMERCIAL GIRONA to keep them encrypted, as stated in its response to the transfer of the complaint, when the emails of its clients would have no additional protection.  
It should be noted that the GDPR does not establish a list of security measures applicable according to the data being processed, but rather, by virtue of the proactive responsibility principle of Article 5.2 of the GDPR itself, which entails the requirement that the data controller ensures the effective privacy and integrity of the data, both the controller and the processor shall apply technical and organizational measures that are appropriate to the risk involved in the processing, taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of the data subjects. Furthermore, the controller must be in a position to demonstrate that it has implemented those measures and that they are adequate to achieve the intended purpose.  
Moreover, the security measures must be appropriate and proportionate to the identified risk, indicating that the determination of the technical and organizational measures should be carried out taking into account: pseudonymization and encryption, the ability to ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience, the ability to restore availability and access to data after an incident, verification process (not audit), assessment, and evaluation of the effectiveness of the measures.  
In any case, when evaluating the adequacy of the level of security, particular attention shall be paid to the risks presented by the data processing, as a consequence of the destruction, loss, or accidental or unlawful alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data that could cause physical, material, or immaterial damage.  
In this same sense, Recital 83 of the GDPR states that:  
“(83) In order to maintain security and prevent processing from infringing the provisions of this Regulation, the controller or the processor must assess the inherent risks of the processing and apply measures to mitigate them, such as encryption.”

The measures must guarantee an adequate level of security, including confidentiality, taking into account the state of the art and the cost of their implementation concerning the risks and the nature of the personal data that must be protected. When assessing the risk in relation to data security, the risks arising from the processing of personal data must be taken into account, such as the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data, particularly those that may cause physical, material, or immaterial harm.  
In light of the above, it must be considered that the technical and organizational measures applied by COMERCIAL GIRONA in the email communication regarding the creation of a customer account by the claiming party do not guarantee an adequate level of security concerning the risk, as email cannot be considered an appropriate means for sending and storing user passwords, and even more so when the user to whom it belongs is sent together.  
In accordance with the evidence available at this moment in the proposal for resolution of the sanctioning procedure, it is considered that the known facts could constitute an infringement attributable to COMERCIAL GIRONA for violation of Article 32 of the GDPR.  
IV  
Classification of the infringement of Article 32  
The aforementioned infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR constitutes the commission of the infringements classified in Article 83.4 of the GDPR, which under the heading "General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines" states:  
"Violations of the following provisions shall be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 10,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to a maximum of 2% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the obligations of the controller and the processor under Articles 8, 11, 25 to 39, 42, and 43; (…)”  
In this regard, the LOPDGDD, in its Article 71 "Infringements," establishes that "Acts and behaviors referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those that are contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements."  
For the purposes of the statute of limitations, Article 73 "Infringements considered serious" of the LOPDGDD indicates:  
"Based on what is established in Article 83.4 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following are considered serious and shall be subject to a two-year statute of limitations: infringements that constitute a substantial violation of the articles mentioned therein and, in particular, the following:  
(…)  
f) The failure to adopt those technical and organizational measures that are appropriate to guarantee an adequate level of security concerning the risk of processing, as required by Article 32.1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.  
V  
Sanction for the infringement of Article 32 of the GDPR  
For the purpose of deciding on the imposition of an administrative fine and its amount, in accordance with the evidence available at this moment in the initiation agreement of the sanctioning procedure, and without prejudice to what results from the instruction, it is considered appropriate to scale the sanction to be imposed according to the following criteria established in Article 83.2 of the GDPR:  
As circumstances of 83.2 of the GDPR for the purpose of determining the degree of severity of the infringement:  
- The nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the processing operation in question (paragraph a): The responsibility for the infringement committed by COMERCIAL GIRONA is increased because the purpose of the processing is related to the main activity of the controller.  
- The categories of personal data affected by the infringement (paragraph g): Since it concerns the password for identification on a website, it is a data that must be specially protected. Otherwise, any access to this data by unauthorized third parties poses a risk of identity theft of the user using the COMERCIAL GIRONA website.  
It is also considered appropriate to scale the sanction to be imposed according to the following criteria established in paragraph 2 of Article 76 "Sanctions and corrective measures" of the LOPDGDD:  
As an aggravating factor:  
- The link of the infringer's activity with the processing of personal data (paragraph b): The business activity carried out by COMERCIAL GIRONA, through the sale of books online, requires continuous processing of personal data of those customers who register on its website.  
The balance of the circumstances contemplated in Article 83.2 of the GDPR and 76.2 of the LOPDGDD, regarding the infringement committed by violating what is established in Article 32 of the GDPR, allows for the proposal of a sanction of €20,000 (twenty thousand euros).

“FIRST”  
According to COMERCIAL GIRONA, since the beginning of its activities, it has committed to comply with data protection regulations and has implemented a privacy policy, control processes, and technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of personal data.  
Notwithstanding all these measures implemented for compliance with data protection regulations, until the initiation of the present procedure, COMERCIAL GIRONA had not adopted any specific technical or organizational measures aimed at ensuring an adequate level of security relative to the risk, preventing an email that is automatically sent when a user registers on their website from containing the username and password, despite the fact that the complainant contacted COMERCIAL GIRONA to highlight the error of this circumstance, as described by the respondent in the response to the transfer of the complaint “…the user contacted the Responsible Party asking for explanations regarding the sending of the access data confirmation.” The only measure adopted by COMERCIAL GIRONA was to inform the complainant that “no one in the organization or developers of the website has access to users' passwords, as they are encrypted and only the user who registered will have knowledge of them.”  
For all the above reasons, this allegation is dismissed.

“FIFTH.- Communication of the solution to the affected party.”  
In this allegation, COMERCIAL GIRONA summarizes some of the ideas previously expressed, such as the use of "two-factor authentication" for access to its website and that the platform does not store customers' banking data. According to COMERCIAL GIRONA, even if this password had been exposed, it would not cause any damage or harm to the data subject, although the concept of zero risk does not exist since there is always an inherent risk that, once measures and guarantees have been applied, is minimized. COMERCIAL GIRONA, in the stages of conception and design of the lifecycle of this processing, defined and applied a series of controls to reduce the probability or impact of the identified risk factors.  
In response to this argument, it should be noted that the claimant states in their complaint the following: "I obviously have doubts about the security of this company regarding the banking data used for the purchase of school supplies," which suggests that, in addition to other personal data, financial personal data is also collected during registration on the COMERCIAL GIRONA website. This is confirmed by the provisions in the Record of Processing Activities provided by COMERCIAL GIRONA, concerning the processing "web users online sales," where the following categories of data subject to processing are mentioned, including "banking data: For charges on credit cards or direct debit payments."  
Although no economic damage or fraud has materialized directly, the exposure of the username and password in the same email poses a significant risk to the integrity and confidentiality of the data. This fact is particularly relevant considering that the transmission of data via unencrypted emails or through insecure protocols (such as SMTP without TLS encryption) could facilitate the interception of credentials.  
As indicated in Recital 83 of the GDPR, security measures must ensure an adequate level of security, including confidentiality. Article 32 of the GDPR establishes the obligation for controllers to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to avoid such risks, especially in scenarios involving the transmission of sensitive data such as passwords.  
Despite COMERCIAL GIRONA's argument that two-factor authentication was used and that the credentials cannot be used alone, the fact that both elements are sent together in a vulnerable medium (email) considerably weakens the security of that data. The GDPR is not limited to the material consequences of the risk but also takes into account the probability and severity of the risk in terms of unauthorized access, reputational damage, and other possible immaterial harms.  
Consequently, contrary to what COMERCIAL GIRONA has expressed, the sending of the username and password in a single email without any security measures constitutes a lack of a robust system to prevent risks to personal data, which confirms the company's liability, even without direct damage occurring, due to the lack of diligence in implementing security measures that jeopardize the rights of the data subjects.  
For all the reasons stated, this allegation is dismissed.  
“In relation to Due Diligence.”  
In this section, COMERCIAL GIRONA alleges a series of measures implemented, such as two-factor authentication, the record of processing activities, risk analysis, and the contract with the processor regarding the security of the processing, thereby fulfilling the obligation of means established by Article 32 of the GDPR.  
COMERCIAL GIRONA is the data controller and, as such, has the obligation to ensure the security of the data throughout the entire process, from its collection to its storage and transmission. The fact that part of the process involves a data processor does not exempt the company from this responsibility, as it must demonstrate that it has implemented, and continues to evaluate, security measures that respond to current and emerging risks, which has not occurred in this case.

The GDPR, in its Article 32, requires that security measures be proportional to the risk and adequate to ensure the confidentiality of the data. Sending both the username and password in the same email poses a serious vulnerability. If such an email were intercepted, an attacker would have access to the complete credentials, allowing them to impersonate the client on the platform.  
A common practice to protect account security is not to send credentials together, and in many cases, it is preferred not to send the password in plain text, but rather through a temporary link for the user to set their own password. The GDPR does not mandate a specific measure, but these are widely used practices as minimum security measures in data processing.  
The fact that no immediate negative consequences have occurred does not diminish the seriousness of the breach, nor does it exempt the company from its responsibility for adopting insufficient security measures. The risk of data breach remains present and could cause future harm.  
In this regard, the Supreme Court ruling No. 188/2022 referred to by COMERCIAL GIRONA, in its allegations, establishes that, in matters of data protection, security measures are obligations of means and not of results. This means that the company must demonstrate that it has implemented adequate measures at all times, regardless of whether the security breach has had effective consequences or not.  
For all the above reasons, this allegation is dismissed.  
“SIXTH.-“  
COMERCIAL GIRONA questions the factors that aggravate its liability in the subject of this procedure, arguing that the purpose of the processing is to validate on the website, which does not allow proceeding with the purchase without the use of the “center code.” It claims that there are no special categories of data involved and that any access to this data by unauthorized third parties does not pose any present or future risk of identity theft of the user, given that the validity of these passwords is limited to only 1.5 months, that is, 45 days.  
However, the model of the informative letter attached to the allegations, under the title “TEXTBOOKS 2023/2024,” does explain that the center code is provided to make the purchase of books, along with registration on the website, which must be repeated even if it was done the previous year, so the purpose of the processing is indeed to carry out the purchase of books.  
Regarding the category of data processed, the claimant states in their complaint the following: “I obviously have doubts about the security of this company regarding the banking data used for the purchase of school materials,” and in the Record of Processing Activities provided by COMERCIAL GIRONA, concerning the processing “online sales web users,” the categories of data subject to processing are mentioned, including “banking data: for charges on credit cards or direct debit of payments.”  
As previously motivated, while the use of the “double password” represents a reinforced measure regarding access to the COMERCIAL GIRONA website, the lack of security measures in the communication of the center code means that this measure lacks adequate guarantees. Furthermore, this center code is known by all families of each center, and there is no guarantee that it cannot be disseminated or used without any control.  
Finally, the inaction of COMERCIAL GIRONA must be taken into account. Neither the inquiry made by the claimant nor the transfer of the complaint has prompted the company to modify the registration system on its website; the fact is that until the notification of the initiation agreement of this procedure, it continued to employ insecure methods. This fact suggests a lack of commitment to continuous improvement and the proactive responsibility required by the GDPR.  
For all the above reasons, this allegation is dismissed.  
“SEVENTH.-“  
In this allegation, COMERCIAL GIRONA argues that the amount of the sanction is disproportionate given its economic capacity, not having taken into account that its revenues in recent years have been severely affected by the social and health circumstances experienced as a result of the pandemic.  
In response to this argument, it should be noted that the amount of the infringement is calculated in accordance with the provisions of Article 83.4.a) of the GDPR, and taking into account the balance of the circumstances contemplated in Articles 83.2 of the GDPR and 76.2 of the LOPDGDD.  
The GDPR is clear that security measures must be proportional to the risk, and in this case, the lack of adequate security measures, combined with the practice of sending credentials via email, justifies a sanction. Data protection is not a relative right, and the sanction seeks not only to correct but also to deter future negligent behaviors in the protection of personal data.  
For all the above reasons, this allegation is dismissed.

II  
Termination of the procedure  
Article 85 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter LPACAP), under the heading "Termination in sanctioning procedures" provides as follows:  
“1. Once a sanctioning procedure has been initiated, if the offender acknowledges their responsibility, the procedure may be resolved with the imposition of the appropriate sanction.  
2. When the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature or when it is possible to impose a pecuniary sanction and another non-pecuniary sanction but the impropriety of the latter has been justified, voluntary payment by the presumed responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except regarding the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for damages caused by the commission of the infringement.  
3. In both cases, when the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature, the competent authority to resolve the procedure shall apply reductions of at least 20% on the amount of the proposed sanction, which shall be cumulative. The aforementioned reductions must be specified in the notification of the initiation of the procedure, and their effectiveness shall be conditioned upon the withdrawal or renunciation of any action or administrative appeal against the sanction. The percentage of reduction provided in this section may be increased by regulation.”
