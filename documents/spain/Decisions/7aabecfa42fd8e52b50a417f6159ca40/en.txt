RESOLUTION OF SANCTIONING PROCEDURE  
From the procedure instructed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST: On July 31, 2023, B.B.B. (hereinafter, the claimant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
The complaint is directed against the DEPARTMENT OF CULTURE, TOURISM AND SPORTS OF THE GOVERNMENT OF CANTABRIA with NIF S3933002B (hereinafter, the respondent).  
The grounds for the complaint are as follows:  
The claimant was the beneficiary of the award of a contract tendered by the respondent.  
On April 4, 2023, the respondent published the announcement of the contract award on the Public Sector Contracting Platform. The aforementioned act includes the name, surname, and full ID number of the claimant.  
According to the claimant, the respondent would have violated data protection regulations by publishing the aforementioned data.  
Along with the complaint, a printout of the award announcement is provided.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), on September 21, 2023, the complaint was forwarded to the respondent for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
The forwarding, which was carried out in accordance with the provisions established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was acknowledged on September 22, 2023, as evidenced by the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  
No response has been received to this forwarding letter.  

THIRD: The complaint was also forwarded to the MINISTRY OF FINANCE AND PUBLIC FUNCTION, responsible for the Public Sector Contracting Platform where the announcement subject to the complaint was published. In response to this procedure, the Ministry informed as follows:  
"The contracting bodies are responsible for the processing of personal data of the information and documents they publish on their contracting profiles, as provided in Article 63.1 of Law 9/2017, of November 8, on Public Sector Contracts, which transposes the Directives of the European Parliament and of the Council 2014/23/EU and 2014/24/EU, of February 26, 2014 (LCSP). (...)  
Contact has been made with the contracting body to indicate that it should annul the award announcement, modify the NIF of the awardee by obscuring positions four to seven, and republish the announcement. Subsequently, it should formally request the SGCCE to delete the original annulled announcement from the publications area labeled as historical. Finally, regarding the right to be forgotten, it should complete the form that Google has enabled for this purpose to request that the document be de-indexed.  
(...) The aforementioned contracting body:  
- Did not comply with the instructions issued by the PLACSP for compliance with personal data protection regulations.  
- Did not request the PLACSP to obscure the personal data of the aforementioned bidder before publishing the award announcement. Indeed, before publishing announcements in the PLACSP, contracting bodies have a "Preview" button available.  
(...) In order not to depend on the diligence of the contracting bodies when following instructions or requesting the PLACSP to obscure personal data, a functionality is being developed in the PLACSP to automatically obscure personal data in the award announcements that are published."  

FOURTH: On October 31, 2023, in accordance with Article 65 of the LOPDGDD, the complaint filed by the claimant was admitted for processing.  

FIFTH: On February 23, 2024, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection decided to initiate sanctioning proceedings against the respondent, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 63 and 64 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), for the alleged infringement of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR, classified in Article 83.5 of the GDPR.

II  
Preliminary Issues  
In this case, the claimant states that on 04/04/23, the respondent published on the Public Sector Procurement Platform the announcement of the award of the contract.  
In the aforementioned act, the name, surname, and full DNI of the claimant are included.  
The respondent carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR:  
"data controller" or "controller": the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.  
In relation to these facts, it should also be noted that Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights, hereinafter the LOPDGDD, in its seventh additional provision, in its first section, regarding the identification of the interested parties in notifications through announcements and publications of administrative acts, states the following:  
"1. When it is necessary to publish an administrative act that contains personal data of the affected party, the affected party shall be identified by their name and surname, adding four random numerical digits from the national identity document, foreign identity number, passport, or equivalent document. When the publication refers to a plurality of affected parties, these random digits must be alternated."

When notification is made by means of announcements, particularly in the cases referred to in Article 44 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations, the affected party shall be identified exclusively by the complete number of their national identity document, foreign identity number, passport, or equivalent document. Under no circumstances should the first and last names be published together with the complete number of the national identity document, foreign identity number, passport, or equivalent document.  
III  
Violation of Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR  
Article 5 of the GDPR establishes that “personal data shall be:  
a) processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject (‘lawfulness, fairness, and transparency’);  
b) collected for specified, legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes; in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1, further processing of personal data for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes, or statistical purposes shall not be considered incompatible with the initial purposes (‘purpose limitation’);  
c) adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’);  
d) accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; all reasonable steps must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate with respect to the purposes for which they are processed are erased or rectified without delay (‘accuracy’);  
e) kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes of the processing of personal data; personal data may be stored for longer periods as long as they are processed solely for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes, or statistical purposes, in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1, without prejudice to the application of appropriate technical and organizational measures required by this Regulation to protect the rights and freedoms of the data subject (‘storage limitation’);  
f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, by applying appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’).  
The data controller shall be responsible for compliance with the provisions of paragraph 1 and be able to demonstrate it (‘accountability’).”

The responding party has contacted the Office of Supervision and Information of Expenditure of the Ministry of Economy, Finance, and European Funds, General Intervention of the Government of Cantabria, through the Systemic Integration Mailbox of the Public Sector Procurement Platform, to incorporate a new functionality that allows for the anonymization of the NIF of individuals.  
However, as the responding party indicates, despite the implementations made, it is not possible to apply it to previous announcements.  
Thus, when the announcement of the award of a contract is published on the Public Sector Procurement Platform, which includes the name, last names, and complete DNI of the claimant, the principle of data minimization is being violated, which requires that their processing be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed. It is considered that the responding party, by publishing on the Public Sector Procurement Platform the announcement of the award of a contract, which includes the name, last names, and complete DNI of the claimant, has violated Article 5.1 c) of the GDPR, as it is not necessary to indicate in the award announcement the name, last names, and complete DNI of the claimant.

V  
Responsibility  
Law 40/2015, of October 1, on the Legal Regime of the Public Sector, establishes in Chapter III regarding the "Principles of the Sanctioning Power," in article 28 under the heading "Responsibility," the following:  
“1. Only individuals and legal entities may be sanctioned for acts constituting administrative violations, as well as, when a law recognizes them the capacity to act, groups of affected parties, unions and entities without legal personality, and independent or autonomous assets, that are found responsible for them by intent or negligence.”  
Considering the factors presented, for the purposes provided in article 64.2 b) of law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations, the sanction corresponding to the acts that motivate the initiation of the procedure and its possible qualification would be an administrative fine.  
However, in accordance with article 83.7 of the GDPR, and the provisions of article 77.2 of the LOPDGDD, due to the category of the subject allegedly responsible for the infringement, such sanction would be replaced by the declaration of the infringement.  
In view of the above, it is hereby resolved:  
FIRST: TO DECLARE that A.A.A., with NIF S3933002B, has infringed the provisions of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR, an infringement classified in Article 83.5 of the GDPR.
