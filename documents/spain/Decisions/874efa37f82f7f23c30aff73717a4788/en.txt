BACKGROUND  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the claiming party) filed a complaint on July 27, 2021, with the Data Protection Commission (hereinafter, DPC), the data protection authority of Ireland. The complaint is directed against IBERIA LÍNEAS AÉREAS DE ESPAÑA, S.A. OPERADORA with NIF A85850394 (hereinafter, IBERIA). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The claiming party, along with her husband, was to take a flight from Quito (Ecuador) to Dublin (Ireland) with a stopover in Madrid on May 31, 2021. At that time, due to COVID-19 restrictions, tourism travel was not permitted, and to justify that the trip was for family reasons, they were directed to the IBERIA offices at the Quito airport, where they were asked for a copy of their identity document, their husband's identity document, and their marriage certificate to send it to the police at Madrid airport to decide whether that documentation was valid for the trip. Ultimately, they were allowed to make the trip, but they were left wondering whether the police at Madrid airport had been contacted or how their data had been used. Since then, the claiming party indicates that she has requested information from IBERIA, without receiving a response, regarding why and how her data was used on 5 occasions (the last on July 4, 2021) through emails sent to the data protection officer (hereinafter, DPD) of IBERIA (OficinaDPO@iberia.es) and to that of its parent company (iag.data.protection@iairgroup.com).

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence and procedure  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  
II  
Preliminary issues  
In this case, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, as IBERIA collects and retains, among others, the following personal data of individuals: name and surname and email address, among other processing activities.  
IBERIA carries out this activity in its capacity as data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR. Additionally, this is a cross-border processing, given that IBERIA is established in Spain, although it provides services to other countries in the European Union.  
The GDPR provides, in its Article 56.1, for cases of cross-border processing, as foreseen in its Article 4.23), in relation to the competence of the main supervisory authority, that, without prejudice to the provisions of Article 55, the supervisory authority of the main establishment or the only establishment of the controller or processor shall be competent to act as the main supervisory authority for the cross-border processing carried out by that controller or processor in accordance with the procedure established in Article 60. In the case examined, as stated, IBERIA has its main establishment in Spain, so the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to act as the main supervisory authority.  
On its part, Article 15 of the GDPR regulates the right of access of data subjects to their personal data.
