FACTS  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on November 18, 2023. The complaint is directed against EUROBOX, S.A. with NIF A11611282 (hereinafter, EUROBOX). The reasons on which the complaint is based are as follows:  
The complainant states that their account at ***ACCOUNT.1 has been suspended and to reactivate it, they have been asked for documentation proving their identity, residence, and employment and financial situation. They indicate, "I answer all their questions, despite completely doubting the legality of these," and after having provided all the documentation requested by the entity, their account has still not been reactivated.  
Along with the notification, a series of emails exchanged between the complainant and EUROBOX is attached.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the complaint was forwarded to EUROBOX, so that they could analyze it and inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.

THIRD: On February 18, 2024, in accordance with Article 65 of the LOPDGDD, the complaint filed by the complainant was admitted for processing.  

FOURTH: On February 21, 2024, a written response to the forwarding of the complaint was received by this Agency, indicating that they requested a series of documents from the complainant because their behavior in using EUROBOX's services was indicative of a professional player, which is specifically prohibited by Article 2 a) of Law 13/2011, and as a consequent measure implemented to address violations of the terms and conditions governing said gaming platform, concluding that there had indeed been a breach of the terms and conditions of the contract, for which the complainant's account was suspended, without informing them as it was confidential information of the operator. On the other hand, regarding the request for the chat conversations of EUROBOX requested by the complainant, the company claims to have sent them and that they had no record of any request to exercise the right of access. However, there was a request for information dated October 23, 2023, which EUROBOX acknowledges was not properly attended to due to human error.  

FIFTH: According to the report collected from the AXESOR tool, the entity EUROBOX, S.A. is a microenterprise established in 1981, with a turnover of 557,369 euros in 2021.

II  
Preliminary Issues  
In the present case, according to the provisions of articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, since EUROBOX, in the course of its business activity, collects and retains personal data of individuals, among other processes.  
EUROBOX carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
Among the principles of processing provided for in article 5.1 of the GDPR, there is, in its paragraph c), the principle of data minimization, while article 13 of the GDPR regulates the "information that must be provided when personal data is obtained from the data subject."  
III  
Data Minimization  
Article 5 of the GDPR, "Principles relating to processing," establishes that personal data shall be:  
“a) […]  
c) adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’);  
[…]  
The data controller shall be responsible for compliance with the provisions of paragraph 1 and shall be able to demonstrate it (‘proactive responsibility’).”  
For our constitutional jurisprudence, if the right to privacy acts as a barrier against interference or intrusions by others (STC 142/1993, of 22/04), the right to data protection "consists of a power of disposition and control over personal data" (SSTC 290 and 292/2000, of 30/11); if the right to privacy limits third parties' knowledge of certain aspects of the person (intimate aspects or those related to their private and family life), the right to data protection provides guarantees of disposition and control regarding personal data that may or may not belong to the realm of intimacy and that may be subject to knowledge and handling by others; if the right to privacy is a right of abstention by others regarding our personal sphere, the right to data protection primarily implies self-determination over our data.

In the present case, on October 16, 2023, EUROBOX requests the following data from the complainant: registered address, current profession, gross annual salary, whether they play with their own funds or those provided by third parties, and the last payslip or quarterly declaration in the case of being self-employed. The request for this data, according to this email, is based on EUROBOX's "Safer Gaming" policy. However, in the response to the transfer of the complaint made by this Agency, EUROBOX stated that "The suspension of the gaming account was taken in response to the identification of behavior indicative of a professional player, as specifically prohibited by Art 2 a) Law 13/201, and as a consequential measure implemented to address violations of the established T&Cs governing the gaming platform." Therefore, the purpose for which all this data is requested is not clear, nor is it clear to what extent it is necessary to identify the behaviors referred to by EUROBOX in order to achieve safer gaming or whether the behavior of the complainant is indicative of a professional player.  
Regarding EUROBOX's Privacy Policy, in its section 2, it includes information related to the processing of personal data that does not require user consent, and in section 3, those that require user consent (pages 11 to 14 of the file). In both sections, the personal data that will be processed by EUROBOX is listed. In none of these sections is it expressly stated that data such as profession, gross annual remuneration, or the source of all their income may be collected. There is a mention of "any other information that may be exchanged between EUROBOX and the User," which would imply that EUROBOX could request all kinds of data from its users. On the other hand, in clause 7 of the contract (page 34 of the file), there is no provision for the requirement of this data from EUROBOX users in the event of breach of contract or applicable law; it only mentions the possible suspension of the service or the closure of their user registration.  
EUROBOX cannot request all kinds of data from its users, as occurs in the emails sent to the complainant, in which, in no case, is it justified why such data is needed nor do they seem relevant for the purpose for which they are requested.  
It is worth mentioning the considerations regarding the principle of data minimization made by the European Data Protection Board (EDPB), which, in compliance with the objective of ensuring the consistent application of the General Data Protection Regulation, attributed by article 70 of the GDPR, adopted on October 20, 2020, Guidelines 4/2019 on Article 25 Data Protection by Design and by Default (https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2021-04/edpb_guidelines_201904_dataprotection_by_design_and_by_default_v2.0_es.pdf). In paragraph 76 of these guidelines, it is established that  
“Essential elements from design and by default regarding data minimization may include the following:  
• Data avoidance: All processing of personal data will be avoided when possible to fulfill the relevant purpose.  
• Limitation: The amount of personal data collected will be limited to what is strictly necessary for the intended purpose.

• Relevance: Personal data must be relevant to the processing in question, and the data controller must be able to demonstrate such relevance.  
• Necessity: Each category of personal data must be necessary for the specified purposes and may only be processed if it is not possible to achieve the purpose by other means.  
From the application of these characteristics of the data minimization principle, it follows that the collection of the claimant's data carried out by EUROBOX cannot be justified, as the personal data is neither necessary nor relevant to a processing purpose that has not even been properly identified.  
Therefore, in accordance with the evidence available at this time regarding the initiation of sanctioning proceedings, and without prejudice to the outcome of the investigation, it is considered that the known facts could constitute an infringement attributable to EUROBOX for violation of Article 5.1(c) of the GDPR.  
IV  
Classification and qualification of the infringement  
If confirmed, the aforementioned infringement of Article 5.1(c) of the GDPR could constitute the commission of the infringements classified in Article 83.5 of the GDPR, which under the heading "General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines" states:  
"Violations of the following provisions shall be subject to administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an enterprise, an amount equivalent to a maximum of 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:

In this regard, the LOPDGDD, in its Article 71, establishes that "Acts and conduct referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements."
