I  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, by this organic law, by the regulatory provisions issued in its development, and, as long as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."

The processing of personal data in situations of health emergencies remains subject to the regulations on personal data protection (GDPR and LOPDGDD), and therefore all its principles, contained in Article 5 of the GDPR, apply, including the principle of processing personal data lawfully, fairly, and transparently, the limitation of purpose, the principle of data retention limitation, and, of course, it is important to emphasize the principle of data minimization.  
Furthermore, it must be taken into account that the specific purpose related to competition and health to preserve the health of athletes is distinct from what may be the processing activity aimed at obtaining a federative license, from which different categories of data arise, different risks to the rights and freedoms of the affected individuals, and specific rights derived from the exercise of rights concerning transparency and information contained in Articles 12 and 13 of the GDPR.  
In the situation of the health crisis caused by COVID-19, the respondent adopts measures aimed at preventing new COVID-19 infections, under its instructions.  
Athletes belonging to the respondent Federation have various options to upload data to the respondent's website for the purposes decided by it. The collection, storage, and use of that data imply that the respondent is processing personal data of the athletes, health data for which there should, in principle, be a lawful basis for processing and one of the grounds that enables the specific processing of this health data.

1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:

Participants, the respondent adds the legal basis for processing under vital interest as per Article 6.1(d) of the GDPR, “the processing is necessary to protect vital interests of the data subject or another natural person.”  
Recital (46) of the GDPR already acknowledges that, in exceptional situations, such as an epidemic, the legal basis for processing may be multiple, based both on public interest and on the vital interest of the data subject or another natural person.  
(46) The processing of personal data must also be considered lawful when it is necessary to protect an essential interest for the life of the data subject or that of another natural person. In principle, personal data should only be processed on the basis of the vital interest of another natural person when the processing cannot be manifestly based on a different legal basis. Certain types of processing may respond to both significant public interest reasons and the vital interests of the data subject, such as when the processing is necessary for humanitarian purposes, including the control of epidemics and their spread, or in situations of humanitarian emergency, especially in cases of natural or man-made disasters.  
Article 6.1(d) of the GDPR considers not only that the vital interest is a sufficient legal basis for processing to protect the “data subject,” in this case, the athletes competing against each other, but also that this legal basis can be used to protect the vital interests “of another natural person,” which by extension implies that they can be both unidentified or identifiable persons, as well as unnamed, in terms of holding an interest worthy of being safeguarded.  
Furthermore, it does not follow, as stated in Article 6.3 of the GDPR, that the necessity for the legal basis of processing due to vital interest must be established by Union law or the applicable laws of the Member States to the data controller, as would be the case if the legal basis were compliance with a mission in the public interest.  
Analyzing this legal basis, it is considered that it would cover the processing arising from the pandemic situation within the specific framework of the competition.  
As an element to consider in the processing carried out, it must also be assessed whether the respondent exceeds the following threshold that entails the prohibition of processing health data of these federated athletes. That is, unless one of the limited and enumerated circumstances set forth in point 2 applies, the processing of health data will not be lawful.

subsequent use for other purposes in the generality of the Member States and, in particular in Spain, as one of the measures adopted by health authorities to prevent the spread of infections in various establishments, through its display.  
In light of the adoption by various Member States of unilateral initiatives to issue COVID-19 certificates that could imply restrictions on the right to free movement and consequently hinder the functioning of the internal market, the European Council took the initiative to develop a common approach, as well as to urgently advance work on interoperable and non-discriminatory digital certificates related to COVID-19. As a result of this initiative, a proposal for a regulation was developed concerning a framework for the issuance, verification, and acceptance of interoperable COVID-19 certificates for vaccination, diagnostic testing, and recovery in order to facilitate free movement during the pandemic.  
The legal basis for the proposal was Article 21 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, which recognizes and guarantees the right to free movement within the Union. The guarantees for free movement for public health reasons must meet the criteria of necessity and proportionality. The entities that can access the certificate are limited to the competent authorities of the destination Member State and to cross-border operators of passenger transport services, airlines, and shipping companies that are obliged to cooperate with such authorities.  
Regulation 2021/953 relates to the possibility for Member States to limit the fundamental right to free movement for public health reasons and culminates the coordinated approach to restricting the free movement of persons in response to the pandemic within the EU. Its recital 48 states:  
“The Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council applies to the processing of personal data carried out in the application of this Regulation. This Regulation establishes the legal basis for the processing of personal data within the meaning of Article 6(1)(c) and Article 9(2)(g) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, necessary for the issuance and verification of the interoperable certificates established in this Regulation. It does not regulate the processing of personal data related to vaccination documentation, diagnostic testing, or recovery for other purposes, such as pharmacovigilance or the maintenance of personal medical records. Member States may process personal data for other purposes if the legal basis for their processing for other purposes, including the corresponding retention periods, is established in national law, which must comply with Union law on data protection and the principles of effectiveness, necessity, and proportionality, and must include specific provisions that clearly determine the scope and extent of the processing, the specific purpose in question, the categories of entities that may verify the certificate, as well as the relevant safeguards to prevent discrimination and abuse, taking into account the risks to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects. When the certificate is used for non-medical purposes, the personal data accessed during the verification process must not be retained, as provided for in this Regulation.”
