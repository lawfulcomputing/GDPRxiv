Consequently, according to the provisions of this regulation, it is inferred that it is not mandatory to collect, register, or communicate to the competent authorities the image, photocopy, or complete image of the identity document of each traveler, but only some of the data contained therein such as: name and surnames, identification number, support number, type of document (DNI; passport, etc.), nationality, and date of birth.  
It should be noted that the image or photocopy of the traveler's DNI (on both sides), passport, or other identity verification documents contains personal data that exceeds those required by this regulation, such as: the image or face of the traveler, the equipment number, or the names of the traveler's parents, for which there is no legal obligation to collect, register, and communicate, according to the aforementioned regulation. All of these would be personal data whose processing cannot be justified under the lawful basis of article 6.1.c) of the GDPR, constituting excessive processing that is contrary to the principle of data minimization provided for in article 5.1.c) of the GDPR.  
In this case, the respondent acknowledges that its online reservation process required the claimant and the other 4 travelers to provide such a copy or image of the DNI, as is usually required from other clients, which implies an acknowledgment that it is processing personal data that exceeds what is necessary for compliance with that legal obligation.  
It should be clarified that article 4.3 of RD 933/2021 states: “The forms and sheets will be provided by the accommodation or vehicle rental establishment, which will be responsible for the accuracy of the data recorded in them, so that they match the documents or systems that verify the identity of the persons, which must be exhibited or provided by the users of these services.”  
However, the obligation to verify the accuracy of the data provided for in article 4.3 of RD 933/2021 can be fulfilled without the need to request the delivery or scanning of such a copy or image, as there are other equally valid alternatives that allow this verification to be carried out reliably.  
Thus, in conclusion, it should be noted that there is no lawful basis in this case to require the guest to attach such a copy/image of the DNI to an application, and the property owner contracting the accommodation should seek alternative check-in methods that allow compliance with the aforementioned regulation without the need to request and store the image of the identity document of its guests.

However, the mere offer for the guest to send the image of the ID via message to the interlocutor of the CHECK IN company means that the contracted procedure allows this possibility of requesting and receiving the image via messaging and inserting it into the application, and therefore, it is designed to permit it. And we must not forget that the design of the processing is considered an operation of processing according to the jurisprudence of the Court of Justice of the European Union.  
-  
On the other hand, it should be noted that according to the privacy policy of the Checkin guest provided as Annex I, it states that the data is stored for a period of 3 years. And it does not refer in any case to its automatic deletion, as the respondent claims.  
“4. How long is the information stored?  
In general, we store the information for as long as necessary to carry out the different treatments, and at least, in accordance with tourism regulations, for a period of 3 years.  
Therefore, there is evidence that the respondent, who is responsible for processing the personal data of guests renting their property, decided to contract the use of an application that required providing a copy of the ID to finalize their online registration; and that if it is not provided at the guest's initiative, it is configured for the contracted company to request it via a message to the phone number indicated by the guest. And the image of that ID, obtained to verify the guest's identity, is kept for a period of 3 years.  
It is their obligation, by application of the Principle of Proactive Responsibility of Article 24 of the GDPR, to have informed themselves and examined all existing alternatives and applications in the current market that allow for online check-in without violating data protection regulations, and at the same time, to comply with their legal obligations to communicate to the Security Forces and Corps.  
Given the concurrent circumstances, it is understood that the requirement to provide a photocopy or image of the traveler's identity document constitutes an excessive processing of personal data for which the hospitality establishment is authorized according to the GDPR, since these are inadequate, irrelevant, and unnecessary for the specific purpose of the processing in question (check-in and check-out of guests).  
Consequently, in accordance with the available evidence at the time the procedure is opened, and without prejudice to what results from the instruction, the aforementioned facts could constitute a violation by the respondent of the provisions of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR, which encompasses the Principle of Data Minimization.  
V  
Classification and qualification of the infringement  
The known facts could constitute an infringement, attributable to the respondent, of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR, with the scope expressed in the previous Legal Grounds, which, if confirmed, could imply the commission of the infringement classified in Article 83.5, paragraph a) of the GDPR, which under the heading “General conditions for the imposition of administrative fines” states that:  
“Violations of the following provisions shall be sanctioned, in accordance with paragraph 2, with administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, in the case of an undertaking, an amount equivalent to a maximum of 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, opting for the higher amount:  
a) the basic principles for processing, including the conditions for consent under Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9.”  
In this regard, the LOPDGDD, in its Article 71 establishes that “Acts and behaviors referred to in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as those that are contrary to this organic law, constitute infringements.”  
For the purposes of the limitation period, Article 72 of the LOPDGDD indicates:

VI  
Proposal for Sanction  
The violation of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR, as provided in Article 83.5 of the GDPR, may be sanctioned with a fine of up to 20 million euros, at most, since the responsible party is a natural person.  
The fine must be, in each individual case, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive, in accordance with the provisions of Article 83.1 of the GDPR.  
In order to determine the administrative fine to be imposed, the provisions of Article 83.2 of the GDPR must be observed, which states:  
“2. Administrative fines shall be imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, as an additional or substitute measure for those contemplated in Article 58, paragraph 2, letters a) to h) and j). In deciding the imposition of an administrative fine and its amount in each individual case, the following shall be duly taken into account:  
a) the nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the processing operation in question, as well as the number of data subjects affected and the level of damage suffered;  
b) the intentionality or negligence in the infringement;  
c) any measures taken by the controller or processor to mitigate the damage suffered by the data subjects;  
d) the degree of responsibility of the controller or processor, taking into account the technical or organizational measures they have implemented under Articles 25 and 32;  
e) any previous infringement committed by the controller or processor;  
f) the degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority in order to remedy the infringement and mitigate the possible adverse effects of the infringement;  
g) the categories of personal data affected by the infringement;  
h) the manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the infringement, in particular whether the controller or processor notified the infringement and, if so, to what extent;  
i) when the measures indicated in Article 58, paragraph 2, have been previously ordered against the controller or processor in relation to the same matter, compliance with those measures;  
j) adherence to codes of conduct under Article 40 or to certification mechanisms approved under Article 42,  
k) any other aggravating or mitigating factor applicable to the circumstances of the case, such as the financial benefits obtained or the losses avoided, directly or indirectly, through the infringement.”  

In relation to letter k) of Article 83.2 of the GDPR, the LOPDGDD, in its Article 76, “Sanctions and corrective measures,” provides:  
“1. The sanctions provided for in paragraphs 4, 5, and 6 of Article 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 shall be applied taking into account the criteria for graduation established in paragraph 2 of the aforementioned article.  
2. In accordance with the provisions of Article 83.2.k) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following may also be taken into account:  
a) The continued nature of the infringement.  
b) The connection of the infringer's activity with the processing of personal data.  
c) The benefits obtained as a result of the commission of the infringement.  
d) The possibility that the conduct of the affected party could have included the commission of the infringement.  
e) The existence of a merger by absorption after the commission of the infringement, which cannot be attributed to the absorbing entity.  
f) The impact on the rights of minors.  
g) Having, when not mandatory, a data protection officer.  
h) The voluntary submission by the controller or processor to alternative dispute resolution mechanisms, in cases where there are disputes between them and any data subject.”  

Taking into account the aforementioned graduation criteria, in light of the facts under review, it is considered that the sanction that could correspond in the event of confirming the occurrence of the aforementioned infringement would be an administrative fine of €1,000 (ONE THOUSAND EUROS).
