- Through the eSPacio application, used for managing rights of access, deletion, rectification, opposition, and limitation of processing.

In the event that the reporting entity does not confirm the address as correct within a week, through one channel or another, the data will be removed from the file and will be blocked, and cannot be reactivated until the entity confirms the data as correct or provides a new address. The procedure establishes that "notifications of entries that are returned for reasons other than refusal must be notified to the entities that provided the information and request their express confirmation regarding whether the address to which the communication was sent corresponds or not with the contractually agreed address for notifications. To this end, the procedure provides a mechanism whereby data related to returned letters will be notified through a file that will be sent weekly to each entity. The design of the record and the format of the fields are detailed in the aforementioned document.  

The confirmation by the entities that the address is correct, that is, the one agreed upon for notifications, can be made in two ways: first, through the weekly contribution file (page 8, first paragraph of the Procedure), or through the application with which Experian communicates with the entities regarding matters related to the Consumer Protection Service (page 8 last paragraph of the Procedure: "this confirmation can also be made through the consumer protection service SPC6").  
The file is sent to the entities via EDITRAN (which is the secure communication protocol) through which the contribution files and error files are sent, and at the same time is uploaded to the "eSPaCio" application of the Consumer Protection Service, where the representatives of the entities also securely access, so that they can choose the way in which they can confirm or not that the address is correct.  
Entities have a period of one week to update the data (page 8, second paragraph of the Procedure, "entities will have a period to respond of 1 week..."), a reasonable period for the entity to internally verify if the address agreed upon for notifications with its client is the one provided to the file. Considering that the weekly process of sending (or uploading to the eSPaCio application) the file of correct returns is carried out every Monday or Tuesday of each week, and the updates of Badexcug are made on Sundays, the response is expected in the "first or second update process after the sending of the file" (page 7).  
As stated, the confirmation that the address is correct can occur either in the contribution file or through the eSPaCio application, but in any case, if by the second update process after the sending of the file the entity has not confirmed the address, the record will be removed.  
The removal of the record will be included in the corresponding "Error Files" of the weekly update, as stated in the current document titled "Error File Update - Format CCI Version 2.15" (in effect at the time the events occurred).  
This procedure, like the previous one, is also part of the Framework Agreement between Experian and the Interbank Cooperation Center (CCI) dated February 25, 2002, to which the participating entities in BADEXCUG adhere.

FOURTH.- SIMILARITY WITH PREVIOUS PROCEDURES. JURISPRUDENCE OF THE SUPREME COURT ON NOTIFICATIONS IN RELATION TO SOLVENCY FILES.  

The same situation that occurs in the present sanctioning procedure has occurred on previous occasions. This party is interested in alleging that Experian has always followed exactly the same procedure, which has always been approved by the AEPD. It is worth noting that my client has never been sanctioned for failing to send a notification, although on numerous occasions, interested parties have reported that they had not received the relevant notifications, as the Agency, after examining the evidence provided by my client, has dismissed the complaint.  

Procedures have even been opened against EXPERIAN in which the failure to comply with the duty to notify was alleged, and in all of them, the case was closed once my client presented the set of documents indicated earlier (such as PS/0317/2008, PS/0531/2008, or PS/00054/2011).  

In fact, there are dozens of resolutions from the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in which it has archived complaints from interested parties who claimed they had not been notified, issuing resolutions that expressly validate EXPERIAN's procedure to demonstrate compliance with the duty to notify inclusion. For example, the procedures E/02222/2006, E/01011/2017, and E/02639/2016 can be cited, as well as in the cases of PS/00419/2016 and PS/00024/2017, all of which predate the entry into force of the GDPR, and, without being exhaustive, the procedures EXP202306491, EXP202306876, EXP202209186, among others, after the entry into force of the GDPR.  

The last sanctioning procedure cited (PS/00024/2017) is particularly relevant in this case as it involved a situation identical to that of the present sanctioning procedure: my client had submitted the same documents that have now been provided in the Information Request EXP202302756, but it lacked the certificate from the third provider responsible for managing returns, confirming the return or lack thereof of a specific notification. This defect is remedied by the submission of said certificate.  

Furthermore, this party wishes to highlight that, in recent months, the jurisprudence of the Supreme Court has decisively leaned towards validating the accreditation of the duty to send a notification or prior payment request to the interested party by ordinary mail, definitively resolving the controversy regarding the failure to comply with the duty to notify when notifications are sent in accordance with the system provided in Article 40 RLOPD, that is, by ordinary mail, proving the delivery of a batch or set of them to the postal services.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the powers granted to each supervisory authority by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and as established in Articles 47, 48.1, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this procedure.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states: "The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, in so far as they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures."  
II  
Applicable Obligation  
In this case, according to the provisions of Article 4.1 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data by including the personal data of the complainant in the solvency file. Likewise, EXPERIAN carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the one who determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR: "Data controller" or "controller": the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing.  
Royal Decree 1720/2007, of December 21, which approves the Regulation for the development of Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13, on the protection of personal data, in its Article 40, regarding the notification of inclusion, establishes the following:  
“1. The controller of the common file must notify the interested parties regarding whom they have registered personal data, within thirty days from such registration, a reference of those who have been included, also informing them of the possibility of exercising their rights of access, rectification, cancellation, and opposition, under the terms established by Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13.  
2. A notification shall be made for each specific and determined debt, regardless of whether it is with the same or different creditors.  
3. The notification must be made through a reliable, auditable, and independent means from the notifying entity, which allows it to prove the effective completion of the deliveries.  
4. In any case, it will be necessary for the file controller to know if the notification has been returned for any reason, in which case they may not proceed with the processing of the data related to that interested party. Returns in which the recipient has refused to receive the delivery shall not be considered sufficient to prevent the processing of the data related to an interested party.  
5. If the notification of inclusion is returned, the controller of the common file will verify with the creditor entity that the address used for this notification corresponds to that contractually agreed with the client for communication purposes and will not proceed with the processing of the data if the aforementioned entity does not confirm the accuracy of this information.”  
Regarding the lawfulness of the processing of personal data, Article 6.1 of the GDPR establishes the following:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;  
d) the processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) the processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.
