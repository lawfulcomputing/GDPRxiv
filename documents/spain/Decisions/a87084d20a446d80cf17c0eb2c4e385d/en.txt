RESOLUTION FOR ARCHIVING OF PROCEEDINGS  
From the proceedings carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following:  

FACTS  
FIRST: The Spanish Agency for Data Protection became aware through the press of certain facts that could violate data protection legislation.  
On January 26, 2024, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection urged the General Subdirectorate for Data Inspection (SGID) to initiate the preliminary investigation proceedings referred to in Article 67 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD) to investigate ORANGE ESPAGNE, S.A.U. with NIF A82009812 (hereinafter, ORANGE) in relation to the following facts:  
“As a result of news appearing in the media, this Agency has become aware of a possible security breach that may have affected the company Orange Espagne, S.A.U. and that may have left the information of many of its customers exposed and susceptible to being used for fraudulent purposes. Among the data that may have been exposed are the name, surname, postal address, telephone number, email, ID number, date of birth, nationality, and the IBAN code of the customers' bank accounts.  
According to the published news, the company sent a communication to all possible affected parties to inform them of what happened, advising its users to exercise special caution over the following months with emails, messages, or calls whose origin or sender they could not confirm, especially those requesting banking information or credentials. It also warned its customers to regularly review the information circulating about them on the Internet to detect any possible fraudulent use of their private data.”  

SECOND: The General Subdirectorate for Data Inspection proceeded to carry out preliminary investigation proceedings to clarify the facts in question, by virtue of the functions assigned to the control authorities in Article 57.1 and the powers granted in Article 58.1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR), and in accordance with the provisions of Title VII, Chapter I, Section Two, of the LOPDGDD, being aware of the following aspects:  
The inspector obtained evidence from several news articles published in various media and social networks regarding a possible security breach suffered by ORANGE.

From the analyzed news, the following relevant evidence stands out:  
Several publications made on TWITTER through the account ***ACCOUNT.1 where evidence is disseminated of having obtained, as a result of public leaks, the credentials of ORANGE for access to RIPE NCC (European IP Networks Coordination Center), having managed to enter the application and make modifications to the routing system.  
Several news articles published on different web portals about a sudden failure suffered by ORANGE that left many of its customers without connectivity and that resulted from the hijacking of ORANGE's account at RIPE NCC. From the published news, the following relevant statements for the investigation are extracted:  
“The attack on Orange involved the manipulation of the Internet routing system known as BGP (Border Gateway Protocol). The attacker obtained Orange's credentials at RIPE NCC, allowing them to redirect the Internet traffic of Orange's customers, which caused failures when accessing websites and applications. This method is known as BGP hijacking. It involves taking control of online traffic routes to intercept the data flowing through them or, as in this case, redirecting them.”  
“Orange has not provided details about this attack, but an unconfirmed theory suggests that the attacker stole the credentials of the account at RIPE NCC through a phishing attack on an employee of the operator in September 2023. This attack injected malicious software (infostealer) that allowed the attacker to obtain the username and password of that account. According to the published details, it was an extremely simple password lacking two-factor verification, which allowed the attacker to access solely with the password.”  
Regarding the news published in various media, all refer to a possible attack suffered by ORANGE from which the access credentials to the company's RIPE NCC were leaked; however, none of these refer to the possible impact on personal data caused by this attack. On March 13, 2024, a response to the previous request was received, from which the following relevant information was extracted:  
o  
“The incident originated due to unauthorized access to the RIPE website by an unauthorized user. RIPE is a technical database that only contains technical administrative data related to telecommunications for routing internet traffic. Through RIPE, there is no access, either directly or indirectly, to personal data. Except for the RIPE application, the attacker did not gain access to any of the applications whose credentials they might have obtained. They did not access the content of the infected PC, nor the information that could be found on it.”  
o  
“The applications whose credentials may have been obtained are of a technical nature, containing information on deployments, reconfigurations, network infrastructure, etc., without providing access to personal information. There is no evidence of unauthorized access; there is no evidence that any of the obtained credentials were used; in the applications, there are no spikes in activity, following a normal activity pattern of the service.”  
o  
“There has been no security breach in the terms of Article 33 of the GDPR. The incident was managed as a service incident, as its availability could have been affected, transferring a technical report to the Secretary of State for Telecommunications and Digital Infrastructures in accordance with Article 22 of Order IET/1090/2014, of June 16, which regulates the conditions relating to service quality in the provision of electronic communications services.”  
They indicate the chronology of events with the most significant milestones of the incident:  
o  
On January 3, 2024, with information published on the internet, it was detected that the origin of the RIPE password leak was from a PC of an ORANGE provider, dated September 4, 2023, due to an infection by the RACCOON stealer (malware designed to track and obtain sensitive information from a PC).  
o  
On January 4, 2024, information about the leak was obtained, and an internal investigation began, ruling out that any other compromised credentials could have been used fraudulently. On this same date, the affected PC was located.  
o  
On January 8, 2024, ORANGE commissioned a forensic report of the PC from the specialized company ZEROLYNX.  
o  
On January 18, 2024, ORANGE received the forensic report, which confirmed the infection of the PC on September 4, 2023, detecting that the malware accessed the information contained in the folder "***FOLDER.1" where the information about the password cache of the browsers was stored, stating that no evidence of access to information outside of that folder was found.

They indicate that:  
“The forensic analysis concluded that the user of the device downloaded on September 4, 2023, at 10:12:20 a file named 'KMSPICO.rar' that contained Stealer-type malware. At 10:12:59, they extracted and executed it. The malicious application would have collected different information from the user by accessing the path '***PATH.1' where the user's web browsing profile information is stored, including the cache of credentials stored in the browsers. There is no evidence of access by the malware to other folders on the device. There are indications that the malware was stopped by Windows Defender and quarantined at 10:14:29.”  
The Data Inspector concludes that there is no other evidence of possible impact on personal data, so the security incident cannot be considered a security breach.  
LEGAL GROUNDS
