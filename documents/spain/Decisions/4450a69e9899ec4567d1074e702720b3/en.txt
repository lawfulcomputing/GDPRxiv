FOURTH – Blocking Procedure Implemented by IBERIA CARDS.  
As has been outlined throughout this document, IBERIA CARDS has internally adopted a series of measures to ensure compliance with regulations and the effective deletion of those personal data that, once the purpose for which they were collected has been fulfilled and the established legal deadlines have passed, are no longer necessary for the development of IBERIA CARDS' activity. Thus, it is of interest for this party to inform the AEPD about the deletion and blocking procedure that IBERIA CARDS has implemented for these purposes. This procedure and its technical characteristics are succinctly outlined below, detailing the process of cancellation and blocking implemented by IBERIA CARDS from the request for cancellation by the interested party to its complete deletion:  
(i) Once the client communicates their desire to cancel, IBERIA CARDS responds to their request in a timely manner, informing them of the cancellation of their Card and the blocking of their personal data. If the identity of the applicant is not verified, IBERIA CARDS may require additional documentation in order to verify the identity of the applicant and thus proceed with the effective handling of their request and exercise of rights.  
(ii) After processing the client's cancellation, the internal blocking of their personal data and the information associated with their contract is carried out. From that moment on, the blocked data is only accessible to the Deputy Operations Director of the Company, who acts as Administrator, and is the only person with sufficient permissions in IBERIA CARDS to, if necessary, access the content of this information.  
(iii) The data of the canceled client automatically ceases to be visible on the platforms and applications used by the sales staff and/or personnel of IBERIA CARDS in their daily activities. Thus, through logical instructions executed during the blocking process, the personal data associated with that client is stored and managed solely in the "Back-End" environment of those applications. That is to say, its visibility in the interface or "Front End" by the personnel of IBERIA CARDS ceases.

II  
Preliminary Issues  
We must begin by recalling that Article 4 of the GDPR defines, in its paragraphs 1 and 2, the terms “personal data” and “processing” as follows:  
“1) ‘personal data’: any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘the data subject’); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific elements of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person;  
2) ‘processing’: any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment, restriction, erasure or destruction;  
In the present case, the data is processed after deletion and blocking to ascertain the contract that the claimant had prior to the deletion, and denying the promotion due to a new registration would constitute processing of personal data in accordance with the provisions of the aforementioned article and, therefore, processing of personal data subject to the provisions of the GDPR.  
III  
Response to the Allegations Presented  
In response to the allegations made by the responding entity, the following should be noted:  
FIRST ALLEGATION - STATEMENT OF FACTS  
After enumerating how the events occurred, the responding party states that the receipt of a new request or detection of a match with a recent customer does not imply the processing of data from other categories of data that may be stored and blocked, except for those that are necessarily required to determine that it is not a new customer.

However, it is established that Iberia Cards, in its response dated October 31, 2023, to the claimant upon re-registering, indicates that “(…)”. Hence, the responding party processed the claimant's data for purposes other than those provided for by the regulations.  
Consequently, the data was processed after its deletion and blocking to ascertain the contract that the claimant had before the deletion request and based on this, deny the promotion due to a new registration.  
Therefore, this allegation cannot be upheld.  
SECOND - Legitimacy of the different processing activities carried out by IBERIA CARDS in interaction with the Claimant  
The responding party states that in this case, following the claimant's express request exercising their right of access to the data that had been blocked after the cancellation of the main card and the associated card and the exercise of the right to deletion, IBERIA CARDS acted legitimately. On one hand, IBERIA CARDS fulfilled its obligations to address the exercise of the claimant's data protection rights and, on the other hand, in order to exercise its right of defense against possible claims from the claimant, responded to them following their instructions.  
Indeed, blocking prevents processing, but it does not imply that the personal data held by the data controller and processed while the contractual relationship lasted cannot be made available to the affected party, in case, as happens in this case, responsibilities arising from its processing may arise.  
Article 32 of the LOPDGDD requires the data controller to block the data, but while such blocking makes certain processing operations impossible, it does not automatically imply their physical deletion. The purpose of blocking is to reserve the data to prevent its active processing or viewing, leaving it available to the Courts, the Public Prosecutor's Office, or other competent Public Administrations, particularly the personal data protection authorities, for the requirements of possible responsibilities arising from the processing and for the statute of limitations for these responsibilities. However, this provision cannot be interpreted as a restriction on the data subject's right of access, as attending to such access does not imply processing of data per se, but rather the exercise of compliance with an obligation imposed by personal data protection regulations. Limitations or restrictions on the right of access are established in Articles 12.5, 15.4, and 23 of the GDPR, with no other exemptions or exceptions existing according to the Guidelines 01/2022 on the rights of data subjects — Right of Access adopted on March 28, 2023.  
For the reasons stated, this allegation is dismissed.
