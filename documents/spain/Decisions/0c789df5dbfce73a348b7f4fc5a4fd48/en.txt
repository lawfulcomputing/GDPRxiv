BACKGROUND  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the claimant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection (AEPD) on 14/01/2022. The complaint is directed against LÍNEA DIRECTA ASEGURADORA, S.A., INSURANCE AND REINSURANCE COMPANY, with NIF A80871031 (hereinafter, the respondent or LÍNEA DIRECTA). The fact on which the complaint is based is as follows:  
The exclusive insurance agent of LÍNEA DIRECTA, the company MAJOREL S.P. SOLUTIONS, S.A. (hereinafter, MAJOREL), without obtaining her consent, has consulted through the website of the General Directorate of Traffic (DGT) the points balance associated with her driving license. To carry out this consultation, MAJOREL previously obtained an access key from the DGT, for which it had to provide personal data of the claimant - the NIF and the date of issuance of her driving license - and an email address - in this case, an address that the claimant does not own - to which the DGT sent the key. Upon receiving the key, it accessed the information regarding the points registered with the DGT.  
The claimant states:  
“Today, 13/01/2022, I requested a quote for car insurance from the insurance company Línea Directa Aseguradora. I was called by a mediator (MAJOREL SP SOLUTIONS, S.A.U.) […]. LINEA DIRECTA concludes:  
1. That the consent of the claiming party was not necessary for the consultation of the points on their license before the DGT. It states: "we are not faced with the need to consent to the processing of the points balance, as the legal basis for the processing of this information, necessary for the execution or celebration of the contract, would be art. 6.1.b) GDPR, insofar as the balance was applied to the pre-contractual measure that necessarily required such processing (discount campaign for having 15 points)."  
2. That, even if consent were necessary, -a thesis it denies- according to Recital 32 of the GDPR, valid consent is considered to be "any other statement or conduct that clearly indicates in this context that the data subject accepts the proposed processing of their personal data."  
3. That, according to Guidelines 5/2020 of the European Data Protection Board, in example 15, "consent may consist of actions such as waving to a smart camera, or even in adverse contexts of imbalance, such as the workplace, the European Committee considers valid sitting in a certain area to be recorded, as it is understood that people who have sat in that place consent to such recording. That is, privacy legislation does not require a rigid formula that only goes through 'Do you consent to the processing of your data?', but rather that consent can be inferred from the conduct of the data subject, analyzed in the context in question, provided that there is clarity."  
4. That there was a tacit mandate from the claimant to carry out the consultation: "Well, having analyzed the context, taking into account the conversation, we believe that not only is there a tacit mandate to carry out the consultation, but also consent derived from the conduct of the claimant themselves."  
5. In any case, it is not considered necessary to obtain consent for the processing of the points balance "when this information is clearly necessary to apply a discount from a specific campaign (art. 6.1.b) GDPR) - discount applicable for having a points balance insofar as this reflects being a good driver - but rather mere authorization (mandate or representation) to carry out the consultation on behalf of the claimant, authorization that can be tacit, or that, even if it does not exist, is remedied by its subsequent ratification (art. 1892 Civil Code)."

The issue that the instructing body takes into consideration to estimate that the practice of the proposed evidence is not appropriate is not only that it was unnecessary to the extent that there is already sufficient evidence of the aspects that want to be accredited, but fundamentally, that, contrary to what the responding party considers, from the point of view of non-compliance with data protection regulations, this issue does not affect the application of Article 28.10 of the GDPR at all.  
This is because the factual premise to which Article 28.10 of the GDPR links the legal effect of considering the processor responsible for the processing is that the processor infringes the GDPR by determining the purposes and means of the processing. This is different from the situation where the processor has not complied with the orders and instructions that were documented and provided by the controller.  
The deviation of the purposes and means of processing referred to in Article 28.10 of the GDPR is connected with the purpose of the processing designed by the controller and with the means provided for its fulfillment: in this case, the consultation of the points balance held by the DGT using the DGT's website, with the employee of MAJOREL authenticating with the claimant's data (NIF and date of issuance of their ID card) and providing an email address that the interested party does not know, where they receive the access key to the information. Thus, there is no deviation in the purposes and means of processing when the processor deviates from the instructions and does not obtain the claimant's consent to consult their points balance through the DGT's website, but the data is processed for the purpose intended by the controller and on their behalf. That and no other is the correct meaning of Article 28.10 of the GDPR, which is why the incorrect comments on the matter that may have been included in the initiation agreement cannot alter the true meaning of the regulation.

The data processor must process the data solely following the instructions of the data controller. Nevertheless, the instructions of the data controller may leave some margin of discretion regarding how to best serve its interests, allowing the processor to choose the most appropriate technical and organizational means. Article 6.1.b) of the GDPR refers to processing that is “necessary” for the development and execution of a contract or for the application of pre-contractual measures, and the term “necessity” has its own independent meaning in Community Law.
