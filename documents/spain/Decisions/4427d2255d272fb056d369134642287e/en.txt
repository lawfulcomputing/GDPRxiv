The respondent is charged with committing an infringement for violating Article 6 of the GDPR, “Lawfulness of processing,” which states in its paragraph 1 the circumstances under which the processing of third-party data is considered lawful:

c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;

To this end, it is necessary to take into account the Guidelines 04/2022 of the European Data Protection Committee on the calculation of administrative fines in accordance with the GDPR, in its version 2.1 adopted on May 24, 2023, which state that “the ordinary duty of cooperation must be considered mandatory and, therefore, must be considered neutral (and not a mitigating factor).”  
This is further confirmed in the same Guidelines of the EDPB regarding the application and imposition of administrative fines for the purposes of Regulation 2016/679, adopted on October 3, 2017, which asserts that “That said, it would not be appropriate to additionally take into account the cooperation required by law; for example, in any case, the entity is required to allow the supervisory authority access to the premises to conduct audits or inspections.”  
Regarding the application of Article 76.2.c) of the LOPDGDD, in connection with Article 83.2.k), the non-existence of benefits obtained, it should be noted that such circumstance can only operate as an aggravating factor and in no case as a mitigating circumstance.  
Article 83.2.k) of the GDPR refers to “any other aggravating or mitigating factor applicable to the circumstances of the case, such as financial benefits obtained or losses avoided, directly or indirectly, through the infringement.” And Article 76.2.c) of the LOPDGDD states that “2. In accordance with the provisions of Article 83.2.k) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the following may also be taken into account: [...] c) The benefits obtained as a result of the commission of the infringement.” Both provisions mention as a factor that can be considered in the grading of the sanction the “benefits” obtained, but not the “absence” of these, which is what Orange claims.  
Furthermore, according to Article 83.1 of the GDPR, the imposition of fines is governed by the following principles: they must be individualized for each particular case, be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. The admission that the absence of benefits operates as a mitigating factor is contrary to the spirit of Article 83.1 of the GDPR and to the principles governing the determination of the amount of the fine. If, as a result of the commission of a GDPR infringement, it is qualified as a mitigating factor that no benefits have existed, the dissuasive purpose fulfilled through the sanction is partially nullified. Accepting ORANGE's thesis in a case like the one at hand would imply introducing an artificial reduction in the sanction that truly should be imposed; that which results from considering the circumstances of Article 83.2 GDPR that must indeed be evaluated.
