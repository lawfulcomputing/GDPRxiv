II  
Preliminary Issues  
In the factual scenario analyzed, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 4.1 and 4.2 of the GDPR, it is established that BEEDIGITAL, in the course of its business activity, carries out the processing of personal data, including the data of the complainant (name and surname, ID number, mobile phone, landline, email) to which it had access as they were communicated by ADIGITAL when processing the request made by the complainant in 2018 through the Robinson List service for revocation of consent to receive commercial calls from that entity, then known as PÁGINAS AMARILLAS.  
Article 4.2 of the GDPR defines processing as "any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure or destruction."  
Article 4.7 of the GDPR defines the controller as "the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be laid down by the law of the Union or of the Member States."  
By virtue of Article 4.7 of the GDPR, BEEDIGITAL is the data controller of the complainant's data, as it determines the purposes and means of that processing. By virtue of its status as controller, BEEDIGITAL is obliged to observe the principles governing the processing of personal data and to comply with the obligations imposed by the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.

2.- The violation of the principle of confidentiality requires the concurrence of two elements for its consummation, as they together integrate the infringing conduct that is subsumed in the sanctioning type of Article 83.5.a) of the GDPR. On one hand, that there has been unauthorized communication or access by third parties to such data. On the other hand, that the unlawful communication or access has occurred as a consequence of the failure by the data controller to apply the technical or organizational measures that were appropriate to ensure confidentiality in consideration of the existing risk.  
The first of the mentioned elements entails a result, in particular, a result related to confidentiality: that there has been unauthorized communication or access by third parties to personal data.  
In light of the above and in view of the definition of “personal data breach” in Article 4.12 of the GDPR as “any breach of security that results in the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration of, or unauthorized communication or access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed” - a definition that includes as an integral element the mention of a result: destruction, loss, alteration, communication, or access - it is evident that cases of violation of confidentiality, when the result has occurred as a consequence of not having adopted the relevant measures, constitute an infringement of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR.  
The other element that must be present for it to be appreciated that a certain processing violates Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR is that the result, the unlawful communication or access, or in other words, the loss of confidentiality, has occurred due to the failure to adopt the technical and organizational measures of all kinds that were appropriate in consideration of the existing risk.

II  
Termination of the procedure  
Article 85 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter LPACAP), under the heading "Termination in sanctioning procedures" provides as follows:  
“1. Once a sanctioning procedure has been initiated, if the offender acknowledges their responsibility, the procedure may be resolved with the imposition of the appropriate sanction.  
2. When the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature or when a pecuniary sanction can be imposed alongside a non-pecuniary sanction but the latter has been justified as inappropriate, voluntary payment by the alleged responsible party, at any time prior to the resolution, will imply the termination of the procedure, except regarding the restoration of the altered situation or the determination of compensation for damages caused by the commission of the infringement.  
3. In both cases, when the sanction is solely of a pecuniary nature, the competent authority to resolve the procedure shall apply reductions of at least 20% on the amount of the proposed sanction, which shall be cumulative. The aforementioned reductions must be specified in the notification of initiation of the procedure, and their effectiveness shall be conditional upon the withdrawal or renunciation of any action or appeal in the administrative route against the sanction. The percentage of reduction provided in this section may be increased by regulation.”
