BACKGROUND  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on October 13, 2022. The complaint is directed against CAIXABANK, S.A. with NIF A08663619 (hereinafter, CAIXA). The reasons on which the complaint is based are as follows:  
The complainant states that, on March 28, 2022, CAIXA, without her consent, provided her ex-husband "the tax information for 2021 of all my products at Caixabank, of which I am the holder."  
She states that she became aware of what happened because, subsequently, her ex-husband handed over that documentation to their daughter.  
As a result of what occurred, on April 19, 2022, she filed a complaint with CAIXA, receiving confirmation of receipt on April 22, 2022.

In that response, she was informed that the complaint must be made by the data subject or by a person with sufficient powers to do so (with their lawyer needing to present a notarized power of attorney).  
She states that, on July 11, 2022, she personally delivered, at an office of the respondent, a copy of the powers referred to for the attention of the data protection officer.  
Since she did not receive any type of response regarding the outcome of the investigations into a possible breach of the duty of confidentiality, she filed a complaint with this Agency.  
Along with the complaint, she submitted:  
- A copy of the general power of attorney for litigation and special powers for other faculties, dated July 8, 2022.  
- A copy of the documentation related to the disputed tax information.  
- A copy of the response from the respondent, dated May 13, 2022, communicating the initiation of the relevant internal investigation actions.  
- A copy of the response from the data protection officer, dated June 10, 2022.

FIRST. – THE OCCURRED EVENT DOES NOT CONSTITUTE A REPORTABLE SECURITY BREACH  
We are faced with an incident caused by a person, an employee of the Entity, in relation to certain information owned by the complainant (the Tax Certificate corresponding to the year 2021, which summarizes the income from movable capital), which was made available to her ex-spouse, with the complainant stating that she did not authorize such disclosure.  
This is an isolated incident, involving one person and one identified recipient, affecting a very small volume of data, which, in no case, constitutes sensitive data.  
Although the complainant has not communicated the damages resulting from the actions of the office staff, any potential consequences would be estimated to have a low level of severity.  
According to the previous assessment of the relevant risk factors, conducted in conjunction with the "Guide for the Notification of Personal Data Breaches" published by this Agency, it can only be concluded that the occurred event does not constitute a reportable security breach, neither to the Control Authority nor to the data subject.  
In response to this allegation, this Agency must refer to the provisions of Article 5.1 f) of the GDPR,

THIRD. – ON THE ALLEGED INFRINGEMENT OF ARTICLE 32 GDPR  
This Entity is in absolute disagreement with this accusation made by the Agency in the Initiation Agreement.  
Firstly, we must point out that it seems that this Agency is double-imputing CaixaBank for the same alleged infringement: the alleged lack of adequate or appropriate security measures, which could be incurring a violation of the principles of the sanctioning procedure (specifically, the non bis in idem principle), and, therefore, violating the rights of my represented party.  
Secondly, we bring to attention all the aspects already indicated in the previous allegation SECOND, to reiterate that CaixaBank applies appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure the adequate security of the data it processes.  
To this end, we must emphasize that the protocols, procedures, systems, and measures established by CaixaBank are aimed at preventing anyone from accessing information they have no right to know, thus avoiding breaches of confidentiality. A single isolated action ignoring these protocols, procedures, systems, and measures, in the context of the volume of data and information that is managed daily by all employees of CaixaBank, is proof that the protocols, procedures, systems, and measures function appropriately because they are known and applied by the employees of the Entity.  
Moreover, we find that this Agency makes an extrapolation from a strictly singular situation to, without conducting any evidentiary activity in this regard, impute to my represented party the non-compliance with the proactive responsibility obligations to which it is subject under data protection regulations.  
Indeed, the Initiation Agreement incurs in maximalism by understanding that once a strictly singular event has occurred, this circumstance determines, in the eyes of the Agency, but without evidence to refute it, that my client has not adopted sufficient security measures to preserve the rights of the data subjects and guarantee the confidentiality and integrity of the data.  
Thus, the Agency in the Initiation Agreement links the alleged non-compliance with the provisions of Article 32 GDPR with the occurrence of the result that happened as a consequence of a mere and fortuitous human error.  
This accusation neither fits nor corresponds to the objective facts that occurred, and is, apart from being excessive, totally contrary to the legal system, as it implies legal uncertainty and null predictability of the applicable legal norms.  
In this regard, it is considered necessary to bring to attention a ruling from the Supreme Court dated February 15, 2022 (cassation appeal 7359/2020), where the High Court clearly states that the obligation imposed by Article 32 GDPR to adopt technical and organizational measures aimed at ensuring the confidentiality, availability, and integrity of information is an obligation of means and not of result, as the Agency has traditionally considered and supported by the doctrine of the National Court.  
“In obligations of means, the commitment undertaken is to adopt the technical and organizational means, as well as to deploy diligent activity in their implementation and use that tends to achieve the expected result with means that...

The difference lies in the responsibility in each case, as while in the obligation of result one is liable for a harmful outcome due to the failure of the security system, regardless of its cause and the diligence used. Regarding the principle of non bis in idem alleged, the Judgment of the National Court of July 23, 2021 (rec. 1/2017) states that,  
“(...) According to the legislation and jurisprudence presented, the principle of non bis in idem prevents punishing the same subject twice for the same act based on the same grounds, understood as the same legal interest protected by the sanctioning norms in question. Indeed, when there is a triple identity of subject, act, and grounds, the sum of sanctions creates a sanction foreign to the proportionality assessment carried out by the legislator and materializes the imposition of a legally unprovided sanction that also violates the principle of proportionality.  
However, for "bis in idem" to be applicable, there must be a triple identity between the compared terms: objective (same facts), subjective (against the same subjects), and causal (for the same grounds or reason for punishment):  
a) The subjective identity implies that the affected subject must be the same, regardless of the nature or judicial or administrative authority judging the case and independently of who the accuser or specific body that has resolved is, whether judged alone or in conjunction with other affected parties.  
b) The factual identity implies that the judged facts must be the same, and excludes cases of real concurrence of infractions where there is not a single unlawful act but several.  
c) The identity of grounds or causal identity implies that the sanctioning measures cannot coincide if they respond to the same nature, that is, if they share the same teleological foundation, which occurs between criminal and administrative sanctions, but not between punitive and merely coercive measures.”

Absence or deficiency of security measures, we add, that infringe the GDPR regardless of whether there has been a loss of confidentiality and availability.  
Regarding the doubt about whether Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR imposes, like Article 32 of the GDPR, an obligation of means, or whether it is an obligation of result, it should be noted that Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR incorporates the principle of integrity and confidentiality and determines that personal data shall be processed in such a way as to ensure an adequate level of security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, through the application of appropriate technical or organizational measures. It may occur that the measures are not adequate without there being a loss of integrity and/or confidentiality. This is an obligation of means.  
Article 32 of the GDPR imposes on data controllers the obligation to determine and establish appropriate technical and organizational security measures to ensure an adequate level of security relative to the risk based on the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of individuals.

VI  
Article 32 of the GDPR  
Article 32 "Security of processing" of the GDPR establishes:  
“1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, which may include, among others:  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data quickly in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a process for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
2. In assessing the appropriateness of the level of security, particular account shall be taken of the risks presented by the processing of data, in particular as a consequence of the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or the unauthorized communication or access to such data.  
3. Adherence to an approved code of conduct pursuant to Article 40 or to an approved certification mechanism pursuant to Article 42 may serve as an element to demonstrate compliance with the requirements set out in paragraph 1 of this article.  
4. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor and having access to personal data only processes such data on instructions from the controller, unless he or she is required to do so by Union or Member State law.”  
In the present case, at the time of the personal data breach, there is no record that CAIXA had reasonable security measures in place based on the estimated potential risks.
