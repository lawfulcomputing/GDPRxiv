“FOURTH.- FACTUAL ISSUES OF THE RESOLUTION THAT ARE INCORRECT AND MAY BE DETERMINATIVE”  
“- Tenth Background.”  
In this subsection, MAILTRACK alleges “the general principle of extension of the initially granted deadlines” and that the copy of the file was delivered to it the day before the deadline for allegations expired.  
In response to these arguments, first of all, the LPACAP does not establish the cited “general principle of extension of granted deadlines,” as the wording of Article 32.1 of the LPACAP indicates that the granting of an extension must be interpreted and applied by the Administration restrictively (“…may grant…”).  
On the other hand, once the allegation procedure regarding the initiation agreement referred to by MAILTRACK was concluded, it had a period of ten days to formulate allegations, when, on May 2, 2023, the proposal for resolution was notified to it, so it had sufficient time to consult the copy of the file.  
Finally, MAILTRACK does not provide any evidence that the agreement to deny the extension of the deadline for allegations and the copy of the file were received on different days, contrary to the receipt in the file issued by the courier company.

According to MAILTRACK, based on its interpretation of the EDPB guidelines, a processor can still be considered as such despite having some leeway to make decisions about how to carry out the processing, as long as those decisions are made regarding “non-essential means,” without this Agency providing explanations on what it considers essential means. “B. MAILTRACK DOES NOT DETERMINE THE PURPOSES OF THE PROCESSING”  
According to MAILTRACK, this Agency confuses the utility of its service with the purposes for which it is used, without the data of the recipients of the emails being processed for the purposes referred to in the contested resolution, such as “improving services” or “sending commercial communications,” but rather that they are processed solely and exclusively in accordance with the user's instructions, who acts as the controller by determining the purposes of this data.  
In response to this allegation, as already stated in the contested resolution, in its capacity as a service provider to businesses and citizens, MAILTRACK determines the purposes and means of data processing in the provision of its service. This reasoning has also been applied in a similar case by the Austrian Supreme Court, which, applying the principles of the CJEU, does not classify all users of a social media platform as controllers of the processing in accordance with Article 4(7) of the GDPR, as this would make all users a controller (see the Austrian Higher Regional Court, Decision of 6/23/2021-6 Ob 56/21, BeckRS 2021, 19302, para. 138, www.beck-online.de).

“1. REGARDING THE INFRINGEMENT DUE TO LACK OF INFORMATION:”  
In this subsection, MAILTRACK argues that, as a processor, it is not responsible for complying with the obligations established in Articles 13 and 14 of the GDPR.  
Although MAILTRACK believes it provides clear information in its Privacy Policy, it does not include the essential information for which it is attributed the violation of Articles 13 and 14 of the GDPR, namely, that it has the status of data controller.  
“2. REGARDING THE INFRINGEMENT DUE TO LACK OF LAWFULNESS OF THE PROCESSING:”  
Similarly to the previous subsection, MAILTRACK asserts that, given its status as a processor, the attribution of this infringement is inappropriate, as it is the users, in their capacity as data controllers of the recipients' data of the emails sent through the MAILTRACK service, who can and must determine the lawfulness and bases that legitimize the use of that information.  
In response to this argument, in this case, the claimant states that in the processing in question, their consent was not previously requested for the collection and subsequent processing operations of their personal data. Neither the design of the MAILTRACK tool nor its Terms of Service distinguish between users of its service to whom the domestic exception applies or not.  
“3. REGARDING THE INFRINGEMENT OF THE OBLIGATION OF FAIRNESS AND TRANSPARENCY IN THE PROCESSING OF PERSONAL DATA”  
In this subsection, MAILTRACK reiterates what was indicated in the Allegations to the Proposal for Resolution, reproducing arguments that have already been addressed in the grounds of the contested resolution. Similarly, it repeats arguments from the appeal for reconsideration that have already been answered in other sections.  
Regarding the grounds for the infringement of Article 5.1.a) of the GDPR, in which this Agency pointed out that “the recipients of the emails in some cases do not even realize that they are being tracked, because the notice ‘Sender notified by MAILTRACK’ in the tracked email can be disabled by users if they purchase a ‘PRO’ or ‘ADVANCED’ subscription,” it should be added that, as MAILTRACK argues in its FOURTH allegation of this appeal, specifically in the allegations regarding the Eighth Proven Fact, the free version also allows the removal of the notification message that the email has been sent using the MAILTRACK tool: “free users must (and therefore can) remove it manually.”

Regarding the mechanism for excluding the processing of data, MAILTRACK presents along with the appeal the "Document No. 2," which does not undermine what was stated by the contested resolution, in that the recipient of the emails must undertake a series of cumbersome operations to avoid the processing of their data, because this document only certifies that the user of the service can exclude certain recipients, not that the recipients can prevent their data from being processed without their knowledge, as can occur with the MAILTRACK tool, as this Agency has been made aware, as developed in the previous paragraph.  
Consequently, both MAILTRACK and the users of its tool can process personal data of the recipients of the emails without their knowledge, even in the free version of its tool, and without there being an accessible way for the recipients of the emails using the MAILTRACK service to avoid the processing of their personal data.  
For all the above reasons, this allegation is dismissed.  
TWELFTH.- APPLICATION OF MITIGATING FACTORS  
“1) The nature, severity, and duration of the infringement, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the processing operation in question, as well as the number of affected parties and the level of damage and harm they have suffered, pursuant to Article 83.2 a) of the GDPR:”  
According to MAILTRACK, if it were considered responsible for the processing of its tool, the "paid users," for whom the domestic exception would not apply, should be excluded from the users.  
In response to this allegation, it should be noted that the number of users of the MAILTRACK service mentioned in the contested resolution highlights the amount of data processing that has taken place without the necessary guarantees.  
In a subsidiary manner, MAILTRACK does not provide any evidence regarding the users of its service who qualify as entrepreneurs or professionals.  
“2) Any measures taken by the controller or processor to mitigate the possible damage and harm suffered by the data subjects, pursuant to Article 83.2 c) of the GDPR:”  
According to MAILTRACK, it has gradually implemented support measures and improvements to avoid possible harm and enhance the transparency of the service, enabling the recipient of the emails to avoid tracking by the user.  
Regarding these circumstances alleged by MAILTRACK, it was already sufficiently motivated in the resolution how, to avoid the processing by MAILTRACK, the recipient of the emails would have to undertake a series of cumbersome operations, and that the information on how to perform these operations was not even available on the MAILTRACK website.  
“3) The degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority in order to remedy the infringement and mitigate the possible adverse effects of the infringement, pursuant to Article 83.2 f) of the GDPR.”  
MAILTRACK claims that it has modified its Privacy Policy and its Frequently Asked Questions section, which demonstrates its involvement and commitment to the rights of users and recipients.  
However, MAILTRACK has not taken any measures to ensure the lawfulness of the processing carried out with its tool, based on any of the grounds established in Article 6.1 of the GDPR. It has also not provided the recipients of the emails with an accessible and user-friendly system to avoid the processing of their data without depending on the decision of the MAILTRACK user; nor is it even communicated to all users that their personal data will be processed, due to the possibility that the MAILTRACK tool user may acquire paid versions that allow for the automatic removal of the notification message that the sent email is being tracked with MAILTRACK or, in the case of the free version, may be manually removed.  
For all the above reasons, this allegation is dismissed.
