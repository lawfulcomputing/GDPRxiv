RESOLUTION OF THE SANCTIONING PROCEDURE  
From the actions carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST: On 17/01/22, Mr. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
The complaint was directed against the entity COLVACAL 2019, S.L. with CIF: B67503292 (hereinafter, the complained party), for the alleged violation of data protection regulations: Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 27/04/16, regarding the Protection of Natural Persons with regard to the Processing of Personal Data and the Free Movement of such Data (GDPR), Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), and Law 11/2022, of June 28, General Telecommunications Law (LGTEL).  
The reasons stated in the complaint were, essentially, that, despite being registered in the Robinson List since February 2021, on 12/01/22, at 10:48 hours, he received a call from the phone ***PHONE.1 making a commercial offer from MasqLuz, without ever having been a customer of that company.  
Along with the complaint, the following documentation was submitted:  
- Certificate of the registration of the complainant's phone number in the Robinson List since 16/02/2021.  
- Screenshot showing the receipt of a phone call on January 12, 2022, at 10:48 hours, from the calling line ***PHONE.1.  

SECOND: On 10/02/22, this Agency sent a letter to the entity MASQLUZ 2020, S.L., requesting information regarding the complaint filed, in accordance with the provisions of Article 65.4 of the LOPDGDD.  
THIRD: On 15/03/22, a response was received by this Agency from the entity to the request made, stating, in essence, that they do not know the ownership of the phone line ***PHONE.1 and that they have not processed any personal data of the complainant at any time.  
FOURTH: On 17/03/22, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection issued an agreement to admit the processing of the complaint filed, in accordance with Article 65 of the LOPDGDD, upon finding possible rational indications of a violation of the regulations within the scope of the competencies of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I-  
Competence  
The Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this sanctioning procedure, by virtue of the powers granted by Article 58.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 27/04/16, regarding the Protection of Natural Persons in relation to the Processing of Personal Data and the Free Movement of such Data (GDPR) and according to the provisions of Articles 47, 64.2, and 68.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD).  
In accordance with the provisions of Article 114.1.b) of the LGTEL, the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to initiate and resolve this sanctioning procedure.  
II.-  
On commercial telephone calls:  
According to the complainant's statement, he received a commercial phone call from the phone number ***PHONE.1, on 12/01/22, without having previously requested it and without having any commercial relationship with the entity that called. He also indicates that he has been included in the Robinson List since February 2021.  
II.-2  
Committed infringement  
Regarding the rights of users concerning telephone calls with advertising messages they receive, Article 48.1.b) of Law 9/2014, of May 9, General Telecommunications (LGT), in force when the events occurred on 12/01/22, states the following:  
“1. Regarding the protection of personal data and privacy in relation to unsolicited communications, end users of electronic communications services shall have the following rights:  
b) To object to receiving unwanted calls for commercial communication purposes made through systems other than those established in the previous letter and to be informed of this right.”  
However, since the aforementioned article does not explicitly configure the right to object, we must refer to the data protection regulations where it is indeed regulated. That is, Articles 21 GDPR and 23 LOPDGDD respectively.  
Thus, Article 21 GDPR establishes, regarding the right to object to the processing of personal data, that:  
“1. The data subject shall have the right to object at any time, on grounds relating to his or her particular situation, to personal data concerning him or her being processed based on the provisions of Article 6(1)(e) or (f), including profiling based on those provisions.  
The controller shall cease to process the personal data unless it demonstrates compelling legitimate grounds for the processing which override the interests, rights, and freedoms of the data subject, or for the establishment, exercise, or defense of legal claims.  
2. Where the processing of personal data is for direct marketing purposes, the data subject shall have the right to object at any time to the processing of personal data concerning him or her, including profiling to the extent that it is related to such marketing.  
3. When the data subject objects to processing for direct marketing purposes, the personal data shall no longer be processed for such purposes.  
4. At the latest at the time of the first communication with the data subject, the right referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2 shall be explicitly mentioned to the data subject and shall be presented clearly and separately from any other information.  
5. In the context of the use of information society services, and notwithstanding the provisions of Directive 2002/58/EC, the data subject may exercise his or her right to object by automated means using technical specifications. (…)”.  
While Article 23.4 LOPDGDD establishes that:

"Those who intend to carry out direct marketing communications must first consult the advertising exclusion systems that may affect their actions, excluding from processing the data of those affected who have expressed their opposition or refusal to it. For these purposes, to consider the previous obligation fulfilled, it will be sufficient to consult the exclusion systems included in the list published by the competent control authority."  
From all of the above, it follows that making commercial calls to the complainant after registering their telephone line in the Robinson List Service constitutes a violation of the provisions of Article 48.1.b) of the LGT, in force when the events occurred on 01/12/22.  

II.-3  
Sanction  
This infringement is classified as "minor," in Article 78.11) of the LGT, which defines it as: "The non-compliance with public service obligations, public obligations, and the violation of the rights of consumers and end users as established in Title III of the Law and its implementing regulations," and may be sanctioned with a fine of up to €50,000, in accordance with Article 79.d) of the aforementioned LGT.  
According to these criteria, it is deemed appropriate to impose a sanction of €500 (five hundred euros) on the entity being complained against for the infringement of Article 48.1.b) of the LGT, in relation to Article 23.4 of the LOPDGDD, regarding the commercial telephone call after registering their telephone line in the Robinson List Service.  
Therefore, in light of the above, by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection,  
RESOLVES  
FIRST: TO IMPOSE on the entity COLVACAL 2019, S.L. with CIF: B67503292, for the infringement of Article 48.1.b) of the LGT, in force when the events occurred, in relation to Article 23.4 of the LOPDGDD, a sanction of €500 (five hundred euros).
