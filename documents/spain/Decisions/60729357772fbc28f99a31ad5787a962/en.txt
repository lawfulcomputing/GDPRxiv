RESOLUTION OF RECONSIDERATION APPEAL  
Examined the reconsideration appeal filed by 4T OCIO Y CAFÉ 2009, S.L. (hereinafter, the appellant) against the resolution issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection dated November 13, 2024, and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On November 13, 2024, a resolution was issued by the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection in file EXP202308315, by virtue of which a fine of 500 euros (FIVE HUNDRED euros) was imposed on 4T OCIO Y CAFÉ 2009, S.L. with NIF B91806885, for an infringement of Article 6 of the GDPR, classified under Article 83.5 of the GDPR, and it was ordered that pursuant to Article 58.2.d) of the GDPR, within one month from the date the resolution became final and enforceable, it must prove that it had proceeded to remove the system of cameras or video cameras placed in the common areas/porticos of ***ADDRESS.1, in the locality of ***LOCALITY.1, (...), where there is currently a hospitality establishment called ***ESTABLISHMENT.1, owned by the company 4T OCIO Y CAFÉ 2009, S.L., unless it proved the obtaining of the agreement of the community of property owners for the placement of the cameras.  
This resolution, which was notified to the appellant on November 14, 2024, was issued following the processing of the corresponding sanctioning procedure PS/00402/2023, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), and subsidiarily in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), regarding the processing of sanctioning procedures.  

SECOND: As proven facts of the aforementioned sanctioning procedure, PS/00402/2023, it was recorded:  
FIRST: A hospitality establishment currently called ***ESTABLISHMENT.1, owned by the company 4T OCIO Y CAFÉ 2009, S.L., has installed a video surveillance system at ***ADDRESS.1 of ***LOCALITY.1 (...), without the express consent of the community of property owners of said building.  
SECOND: There is a contract for alarms and video surveillance and a certificate of installation, both with the company PROSEGUR/MOVISTAR ALARMAS, provided by the company 4T OCIO Y CAFÉ 2009 S.L.  
The video surveillance system consists of 9 cameras, of which three are capturing the outdoor terrace of the establishment and the rest the interior of the establishment.  
Photographs of the video surveillance system and of informative signs indicating that it is a video-monitored area are provided, which include the identification of the data controller and an address to exercise data protection rights.  

THIRD: The appellant submitted on November 27, 2024, before this Spanish Agency for Data Protection, a reconsideration appeal, basing it fundamentally on the following aspects:  
“1- Firstly, the cameras installed in the porticos provide security both for the business itself and for the building against vandalism, theft, or other incidents.

4. Since that moment the board of property owners was dissolved and for more than 10 years there was no such community, so the legal steps for authorization had to be done with the entity that could authorize such competencies, in this case, the own CITY COUNCIL OF ***LOCALITY.1.  
On March 9, 2018, we requested from the city council of ***LOCALITY.1 (since there was no active community) the feasibility to process the license for the expansion of the establishment to include the portico area as private space and to be able to request its use for said activity.  
5. In response to such request and presenting the relevant urban reports, the city council itself, according to its regulations, authorizes us FAVORABLY to request said permission, allowing the granting of the license for ESTABLISHMENT OF RECREATION WITH COVERED PRIVATE TERRACE.  
6. Following the legal procedures, the city council itself posts it on its notice board and sends a letter to the community, to inform them of our intention to expand the license, to which IN NO CASE DO THEY OPPOSE OR PRESENT AN APPEAL FOR SUCH EXPANSION TO BE CARRIED OUT.  
After the lack of response, nor opposition from the community, the file is processed, granting 4T OCIO Y CAFÉ 2009 S.L THE LICENSE FOR ESTABLISHMENT OF RECREATION WITH PRIVATE TERRACE and consequently the use of the same as a private space and of the establishment and activity itself.

II  
Response to the allegations presented  
The appellant has asserted that the video surveillance system complies with current regulations as it considers that: it has consent for the use of the common area granted by the individual who represented 60% of the homeowners' association in 2011, as well as the corresponding licenses granted by the ***LOCALITY City Council as a recreational establishment with a covered private terrace and, therefore, asserts that “the porticoes where the security cameras are installed are not the property of the building but of the establishment, as evidenced by the licenses attached.”  

It should be noted, first of all, that Article 118.1 of the LPACAP establishes that “facts, documents, or allegations from the appellant will not be taken into account in the resolution of the appeals when they could have been submitted during the allegations phase but were not.” In this regard, the documentation and information now provided with the appeal could have been submitted within the allegations period granted after the initiation agreement of the corresponding sanctioning procedure was issued, as it was already in the possession of the claiming party at that time.  

The aforementioned initiation agreement was deemed notified in accordance with Article 43.2 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations on September 3, 2024. After the period granted for the formulation of allegations had elapsed, no response was received from the appellant. Hence, as indicated in the resolution now being appealed, in accordance with 64.2.f) of the LPACAP, since the initiation agreement contained a precise statement regarding the attributed responsibility (facts detailing the accusation, infringement of the GDPR attributed to the claimed party, and the sanction to be imposed), it was considered a proposal for resolution.  

Therefore, it is not possible to evaluate at this stage of the appeal the information, allegations, and documents that, although they could have been presented within the allegations period for the initiation agreement, were not submitted at that time for evaluation by this Agency. Nevertheless, it is worth highlighting that the documentation now provided would not have changed the outcome of the resolution issued.  

The allegations and documents submitted by the appellant along with the appeal for reconsideration do not prove that the claimed party had authorization from the homeowners' association for the installation of a video surveillance system, nor do they demonstrate that the homeowners' association authorized the exclusive use of the porticoes of the building. The document dated January 4, 2011, submitted for these purposes clearly states, “The majority of the community of the building (…) with more than 60% of the building, authorizes the company 4T OCIO Y CAFÉ 2009, S.L, with CIF B91806885 (the company owning ***ESTABLISHMENT.1), for the exclusive use of the common areas of the building, under the porticoes and in front of the facades of the premises 9 and C/ Jorge Juan, 6.”

Furthermore, it cannot be considered that the documents submitted to prove the existence of a license from the Town Hall of ***LOCALIDAD.1 for the use of the premises as a recreational establishment in accordance with Law 13/1999, of December 15, on Public Shows and Recreational Activities of Andalusia and Decree 155/2018, of July 31, which approves the Catalog of Public Shows, Recreational Activities, and Public Establishments of Andalusia, can substitute for the authorization of the community of property owners for the installation of a surveillance system or that they address the private use of the common area. The granting of this license allows, according to the aforementioned regulations, “to offer the attending public the leisure and recreational activity, allowing the installation of terraces and tables exclusively for the consumption of drinks and, where appropriate, food (…)”, but in no case does that license address whether the promoter or beneficiary of the same is or is not the exclusive owner of the physical space in which it is located.  
Finally, regarding the appellant's assertion that “the porticos where the security cameras are installed are not the property of the building but of the establishment, as evidenced by the attached licenses,” this Agency understands, without prejudice to what has already been stated, that a distinction must be made between the singular and exclusive right of ownership over the apartment or premises and the co-ownership of the common elements or areas as provided by Articles 396 of the Civil Code and 3 of Law 49/1960, of July 21, on Horizontal Property. Thus, all owners have the use and enjoyment of the common elements. The above does not prevent the determination of the private use of common elements or areas, whether by agreement of the community of property owners in the terms determined by its specific regulations or as stated in the constitutive title or the statutes of the community. In any case, the common areas of private use remain the property of the community of property owners, even if their use and enjoyment are assigned to the owner of an apartment or premises (sometimes preferentially; at other times, exclusively), and may, where appropriate, be limited to a specific use.  
In short, without prejudice to what is established in Article 118 of the LPACAP, the documentation, information, and allegations made during the appeal phase do not undermine the meaning of the resolution that is now being appealed, as they do not discredit the necessity of obtaining the authorization of the community of property owners for the installation of the surveillance system in the porticos of the building.

In the resolution that is intended to be challenged, the legal grounds are deemed to be reproduced, it was indicated that the installation of a video surveillance system is subject to the regulations regarding the protection of personal data, as the images captured by a camera system are personal data as defined by Article 4.1 of the GDPR. It was also noted that any processing of personal data requires a legal basis as provided for in Article 6 of the GDPR. Its absence, in the present case due to the appellant lacking authorization from the community of property owners, without it being proven that such authorization was unnecessary, justifies the adoption of the resolution now being challenged.  
III  
Conclusion  
Consequently, in the present appeal for reconsideration, the appellant has not provided new facts or legal arguments that would allow for a reconsideration of the validity of the challenged resolution.  
In view of the cited provisions and other generally applicable regulations,
