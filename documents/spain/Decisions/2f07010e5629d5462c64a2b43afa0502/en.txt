RESOLUTION OF SANCTIONING PROCEDURE  
From the procedure instructed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

BACKGROUND  
FIRST: A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on 08/09/2021. The complaint is directed against B.B.B. with NIF ***NIF.1 (hereinafter, the respondent). The grounds for the complaint are that to participate in the “Gomera Paradise Trail” race, personal data associated with vaccinations and COVID diagnostic tests are collected.  
It includes:  
1) Printout of a message from “Gomera Paradise,” dated 19/08/2021, to the complainant, featuring the logo of “X Gomera Paradise,” stating “vaccination certificate or antigen test or PCR.” It informs: “after analyzing the current situation arising from the COVID-19 pandemic, the organization has decided to request participants, as recommended by various public bodies, the following:  
- Certificate of complete vaccination or, failing that, a certificate of having the relevant dose due to having already contracted COVID-19.  
- Alternatively, within a maximum of 48 hours prior to the event, a negative PCR or antigen test result.  
The format will be carried out through the website gomeraparadise.com, where the organization will enable a private participant area, through which they will access using the registration ID and the email provided at the time of registration (attached file of the registration confirmation). In their account, there will be a section where they can attach the certificate via PDF or JPG.”  

SECOND: The complaint led to the initiation of sanctioning procedure EXP202103884, action code PS/00291/2022, which was archived due to expiration of the file on 11/07/2023.  
In that resolution, it was indicated:  
“As for the effects, Article 95 of Law 39/2015, of 1/10, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP) states:  
“3. Expiration will not, by itself, produce the prescription of actions by the individual or the Administration, but expired procedures will not interrupt the prescription period.  
In cases where it is possible to initiate a new procedure due to the absence of prescription, the acts and procedures whose content would have remained the same had expiration not occurred may be incorporated into it. In any case, in the new procedure, the procedures for allegations, evidence proposal, and hearing of the interested party must be completed.”  
“In this specific case, it was a few days after the registration period for the race closed, on 19/08/2021, when the respondent sent participants an email, stating: “after analyzing the current situation arising from the COVID-19 pandemic, the organization has decided to request participants, as recommended by various public bodies, the following:  
- Certificate of complete vaccination or, failing that, a certificate of having the relevant dose due to having already contracted COVID-19.  
- Alternatively, within a maximum of 48 hours prior to the event, a negative PCR or antigen test result.”  
The aforementioned resolution was notified to the respondent on 11/07/2023, and is final in the administrative route, as the period of one month has elapsed without the complainant having filed a voluntary appeal for reconsideration.

FIFTH: On 27/09/2023, allegations were received from the respondent, stating:  
- The request for data related to COVID vaccination and diagnostic tests concerning the event was based on the free, unequivocal, and informed consent of the data subjects, which exempts from the prohibition of processing.  
It adds that consent can be unequivocal and granted implicitly when it is inferred from an action of the data subject, such as uploading their data to the platform.  
- On 19/08/2021, the respondent communicated to the data subjects the rules and conditions that would govern the celebration of the event, among which the aforementioned documentation was requested, and informed them of the procedure for providing it in case they decided to do so, “from the awareness and solidarity towards the rest of the participants.”  
To clarify the content of this documentation, on the 24th of the same month and year, the respondent sent the participants a second communication clarifying the voluntary, optional, and non-mandatory nature of providing this data, specifying the time frame (23/08 to Sunday 5/09: vaccination certificate), in the case of PCR or antigen tests, it must be provided to the organization at the time of collecting race numbers.  
The respondent considers that these documents constitute proof of the lawfulness of the processing and of the unequivocal consent of the users.  
In the communication of 24/08/2021, it stated: “At no time have we communicated the obligation to present documentation or be removed from the event,” and the respondent adds that “in no case was participation in the event denied to anyone for not providing documentation related to health data, nor was there any collection or verification on-site on the same day of the event.” “Proof of this is that the number of participants in the event totaled 1,350, and only 690 of those participants voluntarily uploaded the certificate in question to the platform, with 660 participants having not provided a COVID certificate, diagnostic test, or any similar document.”  
The respondent states that “on the day of the race, no COVID vaccination certificate or similar was requested, and there was no one asking participants for any type of document at the entrance of the sporting event.”  
- The respondent states that the purpose of the processing was to ensure the safety and health of the participants in light of the pandemic and health crisis arising from COVID-19, as informed, for example, in the communication of 24/08/2021.  
The respondent states that their intention was to act diligently regarding the criteria and guidelines that the health authority had provided at that time, considering that, at the time of the request and processing of the data, the measures and circumstances were different for those who were vaccinated compared to those who were not.

III  
The lawful processing of personal data requires compliance with the data protection principles set forth in article 5 of the GDPR and one of the legal bases determined by article 6 of the GDPR.  
Article 6.1 of the GDPR establishes the circumstances under which the processing of personal data can be considered lawful.  
1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or for the implementation at the request of the data subject of pre-contractual measures;  
c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject;

If we add to this that the sanctions must be "in each individual case" effective, proportionate, and dissuasive, in accordance with the provisions of Article 83.1 of the GDPR, admitting the absence of benefits as a mitigating factor is not only contrary to the factual premises contemplated in Article 76.2.c), but also contrary to what is established in Article 83.2.k) of the GDPR and the principles indicated. Thus, valuing the absence of benefits as a mitigating factor would nullify the dissuasive effect of the fine, to the extent that it diminishes the effect of the circumstances that effectively influence its quantification, providing the responsible party with a benefit to which they are not entitled. It would be an artificial reduction of the sanction that could lead to the understanding that infringing the regulation without obtaining benefits, financial or otherwise, "will not produce a negative effect proportional to the seriousness of the infringing act." It should also be added that "In any case, the administrative fines established in the GDPR, in accordance with the provisions of its Article 83.2, are imposed based on the circumstances of each individual case," and, in the present case, it is not considered that the absence of benefits is an adequate and determining factor for assessing the seriousness of the infringing conduct. Only in the case that this absence of benefits is relevant for determining the degree of unlawfulness and culpability present in the specific infringing act can it be considered as a mitigating factor, in application of Article 83.2.k) of the GDPR, which refers to "any other aggravating or mitigating factor applicable to the circumstances of the case." This paragraph leaves a door open for those cases where the absence of benefits may be considered a mitigating circumstance, but not in accordance with the literal and teleological interpretation of the legislator according to the provision of Article 83.2.k) of the GDPR. The Judgment of the National Court, dated 05/05/2021, rec. 1437/2020, also indicates: "It considers, on the other hand, that the non-commission of a previous infringement should be appreciated as a mitigating factor. Well, Article 83.2 of the GDPR establishes that the circumstance 'e) any previous infringement committed by the responsible party or the data processor' must be taken into account for the imposition of the administrative fine, among others. It is an aggravating circumstance; the fact that the prerequisite for its application does not occur means that it cannot be taken into consideration, but it does not imply or allow, as the plaintiff intends, its application as a mitigating factor"; applied to the case under review, the lack of the prerequisite for its application regarding Article 76.2.c) of the LOPDGDD, that is, obtaining benefits as a consequence of the infringement, does not allow its application as a mitigating factor.
