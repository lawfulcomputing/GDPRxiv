From the actions carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: On July 9, 2023, a complaint was filed with entry registration number REGAGE(…) with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection against CTT EXPRESSO-SERVIÇOS POSTAIS E LOGISTICA SA BRANCH IN SPAIN with NIF W1000101D (hereinafter, CTT EXPRESSO). The reasons for the complaint are as follows:  
The complainant states that the entity being complained about was responsible for the delivery management of a package for which the complainant was the recipient and indicates that on May 24, 2023, they were notified of the delivery of the package, although it was not delivered to their home, nor did they have any record of it being delivered to neighbors. The delivery note for said package references an ID number of the person who supposedly collected the package, which is different from that of the complainant, as well as a signature that is not that of the complainant. Therefore, the complainant understands that it was delivered to a third party, unknown to them, implying an ineffective adoption of security measures in the processing of their data included on the package label.  
Along with the notification, an image of the delivery note for the package addressed to the complainant is provided.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), on September 5, 2023, the complaint was forwarded to CTT EXPRESSO for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
The forwarding, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP), was acknowledged on September 11, 2023, as recorded in the acknowledgment of receipt in the file.  
On October 4, 2023, a response was received by this Agency indicating that the Data Protection Officer of CTT, after analyzing the sanctioning resolution of the AEPD directed against a logistics sector company (File No.: PS/00280/2022), proceeded in November 2022 to prepare a Technical Report. As a result of this, a working group was created with the different managers involved in the daily operations of delivering goods to customers, with the aim of adapting work processes to the recommendations of the report. Essentially, instructions were given so that, in the delivery operations to customers, they would be informed of the possibility of not authorizing the delivery of their package to a third party in case of absence at their home and, consequently, not making the delivery if the customer had expressed their refusal in this regard.  
Regarding this complaint, CTT has reviewed the case of the complainant and has verified that the delivery was made to the person who was at the indicated address, who was only identified by their ID. On the other hand, the signature that appears on the delivery receipt is not a signature; it is merely a line. It does not have any handwriting resembling a signature.  
According to CTT, the complainant was informed at all times about the delivery process, and the delivery person who made the delivery confirms that they delivered to the address indicated by the complainant. In any case, our Customer Service department has attempted to contact the complainant by phone on numerous occasions, without receiving a response.  

THIRD: On October 9, 2023, in accordance with Article 65 of the LOPDGDD, the complaint was admitted for processing.

II  
Preliminary Issues  
Article 4.2) of the GDPR defines "processing" as:  
“any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of enabling access, comparison or interconnection, restriction, erasure or destruction.”  
For its part, Article 4.7) of the GDPR defines the "data controller" or "controller" as:  
“the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.”  
In the present case, in accordance with the provisions of Article 4.1 of the GDPR, there is evidence of the processing of personal data, given that CTT EXPRESSO carries out, among other processing activities, the storage and use of the following personal data of natural persons, such as: name, surname, identification number, and location data.  
CTT EXPRESSO carries out this activity in its capacity as the data controller, as it is the entity that determines the purposes and means of such activity, by virtue of the aforementioned Article 4.7 of the GDPR.  
Article 4, paragraph 12 of the GDPR broadly defines “personal data security breaches” (hereinafter security breach) as “all breaches of security that lead to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration of, or unauthorized communication or access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.”

III  
Presumption of Innocence  
In the present case, the claiming party states that they were notified of the delivery of a package, although this was not delivered to their home, nor did they have any knowledge that it had been delivered to a neighbor. The delivery receipt of said package shows an ID number of the person who supposedly collected the package, which is unknown to the claiming party, as well as a signature that is not that of the claiming party.  
On the other hand, according to the response from CTT EXPRESO to the information request made by this Agency, their delivery operation to customers involves delivering to a third party in case of absence at the customer's home or not making the delivery if the customer has expressed their refusal in this regard. For this purpose, the customer is informed on two occasions about the shipment of the package and the option to withdraw from delivery in case of absence. Furthermore, CTT EXPRESO states that the claiming party was informed about the shipping process, and that the delivery person claimed to have delivered the goods to a person who was at the claiming party's home.  
This Agency understands that the confidentiality of the sender's data of a shipment will only be affected when access by a third party to personal data that is not public or that was not previously known by that third party is made possible.  
The examination of the documents in the file highlights the absence of evidence capable of justifying the initiation of a sanctioning procedure, as it has not been proven that personal data of the claiming party that is not public or that was not previously known by a third party has been exposed, since there is no record that the package was delivered to a location other than that of the claiming party, and the error in the ID number on the delivery receipt does not constitute sufficient evidence to substantiate such a circumstance.  
In this regard, it should be emphasized that in the field of administrative sanctions, the principles inspiring criminal law apply, with some nuances but without exceptions, and the principle of presumption of innocence has full effect, which must govern without exceptions in the sanctioning framework and must be respected in the imposition of any sanctions.  
The Constitutional Court in Judgment 76/1990 (STC) considers that the right to the presumption of innocence entails “that the sanction is based on acts or incriminating evidence of the reproached conduct; that the burden of proof corresponds to the accuser, without anyone being obliged to prove their own innocence; and that any insufficiency in the result of the evidence presented, freely evaluated by the sanctioning body, must result in an acquittal.”  
In accordance with this approach, Article 53.2 of the LPACAP establishes that, in the case of administrative procedures of a sanctioning nature, the alleged responsible parties shall have the following rights:

As the Supreme Court clarified in its Judgment of October 26, 1998, the validity of the principle of presumption of innocence “does not oppose that judicial conviction in a process can be formed on the basis of circumstantial evidence, but for this evidence to be able to undermine said presumption, it must satisfy the following constitutional requirements: the indications must be fully proven – they cannot be mere suspicions – and the reasoning by which, based on the proven indications, it has been concluded that the accused committed the infringing conduct must be explicit, otherwise neither the subsumption would be legally founded nor would there be a way to determine if the deductive process is arbitrary, irrational, or absurd, that is, if the right to the presumption of innocence has been violated by considering that the evidentiary activity can be understood as incriminating.”  
Thus, it is necessary to distinguish true circumstantial evidence from mere suspicions or conjectures. The Constitutional Court has stated in its Judgment 24/1997 that “the criteria for distinguishing between circumstantial evidence capable of undermining the presumption of innocence and mere suspicions are based on: a) The circumstantial evidence must stem from fully proven facts. b) The facts constituting the crime must be deduced from those indications (completely proven facts) through a reasoned mental process in accordance with the rules of human judgment, explicitly stated in the convicting judgment (SSTC 174/1985, 175/1985, 229/1988, 107/1989, 384/1993, and 206/1994, among others).”  
In short, the principle of presumption of innocence prevents attributing an administrative infraction when no incriminating evidence has been obtained and verified that substantiates the facts motivating the accusation or the involvement of the alleged infringer in them.  
In this case, as stated, there are no rational indications that would allow attributing to the claimed party the commission of the infractions of the personal data protection regulations reported, so it cannot be considered that the right to the presumption of innocence that protects them has been undermined.
