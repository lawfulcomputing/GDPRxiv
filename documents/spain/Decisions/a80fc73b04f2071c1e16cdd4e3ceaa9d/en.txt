FACTS  
FIRST: On July 4, 2023, a complaint was filed with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection for a possible infringement attributable to ULPIA TRAJANA ALAMEDA S.L. with NIF B90468273 (hereinafter, ULPIA).  
The facts brought to the attention of this authority were:  
The complainant states that they reserved a tourist apartment through the platform ***PLATFORM.1, and that the owner required the use of the application ***APPLICATION.1 to carry out the online check-in process.  
They state that the aforementioned application requires photographing both sides of the ID card, as well as a photograph of the document holder to complete the check-in and reserve an apartment.  
They claim that they contacted the respondent on June 21, 2023, via email requesting justification regarding this matter, but did not receive a response.

In the GUIDE ON PRESENCE CONTROL TREATMENTS THROUGH BIOMETRIC SYSTEMS issued by the AEPD in November 2023, in section IV. BIOMETRIC DATA AND SPECIAL CATEGORIES OF DATA, it specifies:  
“The Guidelines 05/2022 from the European Data Protection Board (EDPB), on the use of facial recognition in the field of law enforcement (see Version 2.0, dated April 26, 2023), determine, in their section 12, that the concept of biometric data encompasses both ‘authentication’ and ‘identification’, and although they are distinct concepts, in both procedures data aimed at identifying a natural person are processed, so both are included in the concept of ‘data processing’, and more specifically, they are treatments of personal data of special categories. Consequently, the general prohibition established in Article 9.1 of the GDPR extends to both, so this prohibition must apply not only to treatments aimed at identification but also to cases of biometric data processing aimed at the authentication or verification of the person concerning the previously established pattern for them.”

The GDPR defines in its Article 4.14 biometric data as personal data resulting from or produced by technical processing, according to its literal meaning "personal data obtained from a specific technical processing, relating to the physical, physiological, or behavioral characteristics of a natural person that allow or confirm the unique identification of that person, such as facial images or fingerprint data."  
Biometric data are a type of personal data generated during the technical processing of biometric characteristics for the purposes of biometric recognition.  
Thus, they are defined by their very purpose of allowing identification or confirming/verifying the same, with the addition in either case of being "unique to that person," in the case raised in the complaint that is the origin of this initiation of sanctioning procedure, based on two images, that of the ID card and a photo as in this case.  
The term "biometry" derives from the Greek words "bio" (life) and "metrics" (measurement), reflecting its focus on the measurement and analysis of biological traits.  
Biometric recognition is the automated recognition of individuals based on their biological and behavioral characteristics.

While traditional authentication methods such as passwords require a 100% match character by character to allow the user to access, for example, an account or application (deterministic methods), biometric methods are referred to as “probabilistic,” because they are probabilistic technologies that can automatically recognize individuals by their face or fingerprint for authentication or identification (point 23 of Opinion 11/2024 on the use of facial recognition to streamline passenger flow in airports).  

In both the biometric identification and authentication (verification) systems, the facial recognition techniques used are based on an estimated match between templates: the one being compared and the reference one that may have been previously registered. From this perspective, they are probabilistic techniques: the comparison deduces a greater or lesser probability that the person is indeed the one to be authenticated or identified; if this probability exceeds a certain threshold in the system, defined by its user or developer, the system will understand that there is a match.  

The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) refers to this probability regarding the uncertainty of the result, which is not definitive as it is based on probabilities that two faces or images of faces correspond to the same person, as a “confidence score” (27 of Guidelines 5/2022 on the use of facial recognition in law enforcement).  

A biometric system works with the biometric data obtained from a person, from which an algorithm selects features to create a biometric template. The system then checks the identity of the person against the biometric database. It can do this in a second while comparing hundreds of millions of biometric data in the database.

A biometric template, or facial vector, is a form of writing of a human biometric characteristic, such as a face, a fingerprint, or a person's voice, in such a way that it can be efficiently and effectively interpreted by a machine for a specific purpose or purposes. This registration, both through its storage and its real-time matching, can allow for the singularization of a person.
