From the proceedings carried out by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection and based on the following  

FACTS  
FIRST: Ms. A.A.A. (hereinafter, the complainant) filed a complaint with the Spanish Agency for Data Protection on 20/09/2023. The complaint is directed against INTERESTELAR SEVILLA A.I.E. with NIF V40651846 (hereinafter, the respondent). The reasons for the complaint are as follows: the complainant points out that the respondent requires that, once the passes for the next edition of the Interestelar festival are purchased, buyers must personalize them with the data and a photo of each attendee as a mandatory condition to be able to download them; each buyer has a period of 20 days to do this for free, after which there will be a cost of €20; in the Privacy Policy of the respondent, it is stated, "For the purchase of tickets, the legitimizing basis that allows the processing of your data will be the execution of a contract in which you are a party (Art. 6.1.b of the GDPR) and your consent (Art. 6.1.a of the GDPR) to request your image in order to expedite access to the festival and allow security personnel to identify you as the holder of the pass quickly." Nevertheless, if the image is not sent, the pass cannot be formalized; the Privacy Policy also does not clarify the purpose of this processing.  

SECOND: In accordance with Article 65.4 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), on 31/10/2023, the aforementioned complaint was forwarded to the respondent for analysis and to inform this Agency within one month of the actions taken to comply with the requirements set forth in data protection regulations.  
The transfer, which was carried out in accordance with the rules established in Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations (hereinafter, LPACAP) by means of electronic notification, was not collected by the responsible party within the availability period, being understood as rejected in accordance with the provisions of Article 43.2 of the LPACAP on 11/11/2023, as stated in the certificate in the file.  
Although the notification was validly carried out by electronic means, considering the procedure completed in accordance with the provisions of Article 41.5 of the LPACAP, an informational copy was sent by postal mail, which was duly notified on 13/11/2023. In this notification, the obligation to communicate electronically with the Administration was reminded, and the means of access to such notifications were provided, reiterating that, henceforth, notifications would be made exclusively by electronic means.

LEGAL GROUNDS  
I  
Competence  
In accordance with the functions conferred upon each supervisory authority by Article 57.1 a), f), and h) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter GDPR) and as provided in Articles 47 and 48.1 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD), the Director of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is competent to resolve these investigative proceedings.  
Furthermore, Article 63.2 of the LOPDGDD states that: “The procedures processed by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection shall be governed by the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, the regulatory provisions issued for its development, and, to the extent that they do not contradict them, subsidiarily by the general rules on administrative procedures.”  
II  
Article 5, Principles relating to processing, of the GDPR establishes that:  
“1. Personal data shall be:  
(…)  
c) adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’);  
(…)”  
Article 5 of the GDPR refers to the general principles for data processing. In its paragraph c), it refers to the principle of data minimization, indicating that the data must be “adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed.”  
If we examine the definition, we can deduce that only the personal data that will be processed can be collected, that is, those that are strictly necessary for the processing; that they may only be collected when they are going to be processed and that they may only be used for the purpose for which they were collected, but not for any other objective.

Article 5.1.c) enshrines the principle of data minimization and implies that the processing of personal data must be adjusted and proportionate to the purpose it is directed at, clearly determining the purposes for which the data is collected and processed, and the processing of excessive data must be restricted or the data must be deleted.  
Moreover, the relevance in the processing of data must occur both in the collection phase and in the subsequent processing that is carried out.  
It should be noted that the GDPR limits the use or processing of data by necessity and not by excess; that is, personal data must be adequate, necessary, relevant, and limited to the need for which they were collected.  
Law 11/2023, of May 8, on the transposition of European Union directives regarding the accessibility of certain products and services, migration of highly qualified persons, taxation, and digitalization of notarial and registration actions; and which amends Law 12/2011, of May 27, on civil liability for nuclear damage or damage caused by radioactive materials, introduces modifications to the LOPDGDD, both in the processing of certain procedures of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection (AEPD) and in its Statute.  
Among the new features introduced is a new paragraph 6 added to Article 65 of the LOPDGDD which establishes that, even if a claim is admitted for processing, the data controller or processor demonstrates having adopted measures for compliance with applicable regulations, the Spanish Agency for Data Protection may also resolve to archive the claim when the circumstances allow for alternatives to corrective action, provided that no prior investigative actions or any of the procedures regulated by law have been initiated.

6. After the admission for processing, if the data controller or processor demonstrates that they have adopted measures to comply with the applicable regulations, the Spanish Agency for Data Protection may resolve to archive the complaint when specific circumstances advise the adoption of other more moderate solutions or alternatives to corrective action, provided that no prior investigative actions or any of the procedures regulated in this organic law have been initiated.  
In the present case, the processing carried out using images for the identification of attendees at events, the same party being complained about stated that initially it was considered that this measure could help resolve the security problem of the attendees, but that "analysis and studies of the issue, accompanied by expert advisors in privacy and data protection, concluded that this measure could be excessive in terms of the principle of minimization of art. 5.1.c of the GDPR, even though such images would be deleted at the end of the festival and would only be used for proper verification by security personnel."  
However, after subsequent analyses and studies, the data controller has demonstrated that they have adopted measures that allow compliance with data protection regulations, and states "...in October 2023, together with their privacy consultants, it was proactively decided not to carry out the processing activity related to the images of the attendees for the verification process, as it was understood that this request was not adequately justified nor proportional in terms of the pursued purpose. Therefore, on October 18, 2023, the images provided by the attendees were deleted and removed, previously informing the interested parties who had already provided their image about this decision. The communication was made progressively and through individual communication, which we provide in this administrative file, along with the record of the communication sent to the complainant and its opening on November 13, 2023, which we understand is of interest for this file."  
Consequently, and in accordance with the previous evidence, the present file must be concluded with its archiving due to the occurrence of a violation of data protection regulations, specifically the infringement of article 5.1.c) of the GDPR.

Therefore, based on the information indicated in the previous paragraphs, no evidence has been found that substantiates the existence of an infringement within the jurisdiction of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.  
All of this is without prejudice to any possible subsequent actions that this Agency may carry out, applying the investigative and corrective powers it holds.
