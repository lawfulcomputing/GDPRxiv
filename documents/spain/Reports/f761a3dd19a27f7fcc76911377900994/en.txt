The inquiry raises the issue of the transfer, by the consulting entity, to the Ministry of Education and Vocational Training, of the telephone numbers of the households selected by the National Institute of Statistics to conduct the interviews that are part of the Programme for the International Assessment of Adult Competencies of the OECD (PIAAC, for its acronym in English), which is part of the current National Statistical Plan and whose management in Spain corresponds to the National Institute for Educational Evaluation (INEE) under the General Directorate of Evaluation and Territorial Cooperation of the aforementioned department, due to the impossibility, arising from the pandemic situation we are in and the need to follow the health protocols recommended by health authorities regarding social distancing and minimizing meetings in closed places, of carrying it out through the interviewer’s visit to the home for a face-to-face interview.  

The inquiry is accompanied by the documentation that had been requested by the CNMC and which includes a reasoned report on the legitimacy of the processing, signed by the General Director of Evaluation and Territorial Cooperation, a risk report and data protection impact assessment in accordance with the model approved by the AEPD, the Agreement of the Monitoring Commission of the Agreement for the development, application, and analysis of the international program for the assessment of adult competencies, regarding the protection of personal data, from December 2019, the Agreement for the processing of personal data derived from the execution of the contract, and the declarations of the company and individuals regarding statistical confidentiality.  

According to the aforementioned documentation, the PIAAC program is a multi-cycle study conducted every ten years, promoted by the OECD, included in the National Statistical Plan 2021-2024 with plan number 8667 and IOE number 42184, and its main objective is to obtain quality information about the level of key competencies in reading, mathematics, and adaptive problem-solving of the adult population between 16 and 65 years old, without special categories of personal data being processed.  

Currently, PIAAC is in its cycle 2, specifically preparing the pilot phase of the study focused on collecting information through two avenues: on one hand, a comprehensive background questionnaire, and on the other, the administration of cognitive tests to assess performance in reading, mathematics, and adaptive problem-solving. Both parts are conducted through a face-to-face interview at the home of a sample of households using laptops. The expected duration of the interview is estimated to be between 90 and 120 minutes. The personal data of the informants are encrypted and properly anonymized once the survey is applied. After the purification and cleaning of the databases, all personal information, including phone numbers and addresses of the informants, is deleted from the final databases.  

Furthermore, considering the difficulty of conducting the survey in a pandemic scenario like the current one, it is indicated that there is close collaboration with the Ministry of Health to develop a prevention plan for conducting interviews safely and that “In addition to this plan, health authorities have provided us with various suggestions such as prioritizing initial telephone contact” and that for this, it is essential to have the phone numbers of all households in the sample selected by the INE for the target population of the study, whether they have given their consent or not.  

Finally, it is stated that the use of these phone numbers would be limited exclusively to conducting the fieldwork of the study, so it would not be subject to additional processing, and they would be destroyed once the study report is published.  

I  
This Agency has already had the opportunity to express its opinion, on different occasions, regarding the appropriateness of the communication of telephone numbers by the CNMC to the statistical services of various bodies for conducting surveys included in the National Statistical Plan, highlighting that the relevant criterion for determining how this communication will proceed does not derive from the obligation to conduct the survey by the corresponding public body, but from the obligation of its completion by the affected parties.  

In this case, as the inquiry refers to the preparation of an official statistic, of mandatory completion, included in the National Statistical Plan and executed by the statistical services of the State, the criterion maintained by this Agency in its reports 75/2020 and 78/2020 applies.  

Thus, we stated in Report 78/2020:

The inquiry refers to the preparation of an official statistic, of mandatory completion, included in the National Statistical Plan and executed by the state statistical services, for which it is necessary, given the restrictions arising from the COVID-19 pandemic, to obtain the phone numbers of the respondents in advance from the CNMC, which implies, as indicated in this Agency's Report 49/2020, the communication of personal data for a purpose different from that for which they were obtained. Since it involves the communication of data between administrative bodies, it must first comply with the provisions of its specific regulations, which, in this case, is constituted by Law 12/1989, of May 9, on Public Statistical Function, whose Article 23 includes, within the State Statistical Services, in addition to the National Institute of Statistics (INE) and the Superior Council of Statistics, “the units of the different ministerial departments and any other public entities dependent on it to which that function has been entrusted,” attributing in its Article 33 to the statistical services of the ministerial departments, among other competencies, in its paragraph f), “The preparation and execution of the statistical projects entrusted to them in the National Statistical Plan.”

On the other hand, the aforementioned law, in its article 7.1, states that “Statistics for which data is required on a mandatory basis shall be established by Law,” as reflected in the fourth additional provision of Law 4/1990, of June 29, on the General State Budgets for 1990, which includes the statistics that are mandatory to complete, among which the second additional provision of Law 13/1996, of December 30, on Fiscal, Administrative, and Social Order Measures included “The statistics that are part of the National Statistical Plan and specifically according to article 45.2 of Law 12/1989, of May 9, on the Public Statistical Function, those whose realization is mandatory for the Spanish State by requirement of European Union regulations. Likewise, the statistics that may be carried out under the protection of article 8.3 of the aforementioned Law.”

Furthermore, regarding its compliance with the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation, article 25 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the Guarantee of Digital Rights states the following:

Article 25. Data processing in the field of the public statistical function.
1. The processing of personal data carried out by the bodies assigned the competencies related to the exercise of the public statistical function shall be subject to the provisions of its specific legislation, as well as to Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and this organic law.
2. The communication of data to the competent bodies in statistical matters shall only be understood as covered under article 6.1 e) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 in cases where the statistics for which the information is required is mandated by a European Union law or is included in the legally provided statistical programming instruments.

In accordance with the provisions of article 11.2 of Law 12/1989, of May 9, on the Public Statistical Function, the data referred to in articles 9 and 10 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 shall be strictly voluntary to provide and, consequently, may only be collected with the express consent of the affected individuals.

3. The competent bodies for the exercise of the public statistical function may deny requests from affected individuals to exercise the rights established in articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 when the data is protected by the guarantees of statistical confidentiality provided for in state or regional legislation.

Therefore, in accordance with the provisions of the specific regulations, it turns out that, in the present case, we are faced with the assumption of a mandatory completion statistic as it is included in the National Statistical Plan approved by Royal Decree 410/2016, of October 31, which includes among the information that must be provided, as indicated in the inquiry, the telephone number as a quality control instrument for the actions carried out, so that “This variable thus becomes not only an initial contact instrument with the informant but is also part of the content of the questionnaire.”

And since it is a mandatory completion statistic, there is a legal obligation for the citizens previously selected in the survey sample to provide the requested information, being classified in Article 50 of Law 12/1989 as a minor infraction “The submission or delay in sending data when it has not caused serious harm to the service, and there is an obligation to provide it,” as a serious infraction “The non-submission or delay in sending the required data when serious harm to the service occurs, and there is an obligation to provide it,” and as a very serious infraction “The notorious, habitual resistance or with the allegation of false excuses in sending the required data when there is an obligation to provide it.”

It is precisely the obligation to complete the survey that allows us to differentiate this case from those in which it is voluntary, such as those conducted by the Center for Sociological Research, since, involving the processing of special categories of data, Article 5 of Law 39/1995, of December 19, on the Organization of the Center for Sociological Research establishes the principle of voluntariness of responses, which requires that, in the case of intending to communicate telephone numbers by the CNMC to the CIS, additional guarantees must be adopted, as analyzed in Report 49/2020.

Therefore, in the present case, the communication of the data of the subscribers to the telephone service by the CNMC for the preparation of an official statistic, of mandatory completion, included in the National Statistical Plan and executed by the state statistical services would find its basis in Article 6.1.e) of the GDPR (“the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller”).

In any case, the data controller must comply with the principles established in Article 5 of the GDPR, indicating c. Jorge Juan 6
