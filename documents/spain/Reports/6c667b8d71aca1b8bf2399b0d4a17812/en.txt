Legal Department  
N/REF: 0075/2020  

The inquiry raises the issue of the transfer, by the consulting entity, to the Bank of Spain, of the telephone numbers of the households selected by the National Statistics Institute to conduct the Financial Survey of Families, due to the impossibility, arising from the pandemic situation we are in and the need to follow the health protocols recommended by health authorities regarding social distancing and minimizing meetings in closed places, of carrying it out through the interviewer visiting the household for a face-to-face interview. It also justifies the impossibility of conducting the interview on paper sent by mail, due to the duration and complexity of the interview (an average duration of one hour and fifteen minutes) and the fact that it is a long questionnaire, with numerous branches and checks that are triggered in case of inconsistencies between responses to certain questions, with ample and well-known scientific evidence showing that in long and complex surveys, the role of the interviewer is fundamental.  

As indicated in the inquiry, the Financial Survey of Families (EFF) has been conducted by the Bank of Spain since 2002 and every three years, in order to obtain detailed information about the incomes, assets, debts, and expenditures of Spanish households for statistical purposes. Given the relevance of this information, the EFF is part of the National Statistical Plan (Royal Decree 410/2016, of October 31, approving the National Statistical Plan 2017-2020, Plan No. 7914). Furthermore, the EFF is part of a system of surveys in each country of the Eurosystem coordinated by the European Central Bank to measure the financial position of households in the Eurosystem (the Household Finance and Consumption Survey).  

The inquiry is accompanied by the report issued by the data protection delegate of the Bank of Spain.

Referring to the inquiry regarding the preparation of an official statistic, which is mandatory to complete, included in the National Statistical Plan and executed by the state statistical services, the criterion maintained by this Agency in its recent report 78/2020 applies:  

The inquiry refers to the preparation of an official statistic, which is mandatory to complete, included in the National Statistical Plan and executed by the state statistical services, for which it is necessary, given the restrictions arising from the COVID-19 pandemic, to obtain the telephone numbers of the respondents in advance by the CNMC, which implies, as indicated in this Agency's Report 49/2020, the communication of personal data for a purpose different from that for which they were obtained. Since it is a communication of data between administrative bodies, it must first comply with what is provided in its specific regulations, which, in this case, is constituted by Law 12/1989, of May 9, on the Public Statistical Function, whose article 23 includes, within the State Statistical Services, in addition to the INE and the Superior Council of Statistics, “the units of the different ministerial departments and any other public entities dependent on them to which that function has been entrusted,” attributing in its article 33 to the statistical services of the ministerial departments, among other competencies, in its letter f), “The preparation and execution of the statistical projects entrusted to them in the National Statistical Plan.”  

On the other hand, the aforementioned law, in its article 7.1, states that “Statistics for which data is required to be provided mandatorily shall be established by Law,” being included in the fourth additional provision of Law 4/1990, of June 29, on the General State Budgets for 1990, the statistics of mandatory completion among which the second additional provision of Law 13/1996, of December 30, on Fiscal, Administrative, and Social Order Measures included “The statistics that are part of the National Statistical Plan and specifically according to article 45.2 of Law 12/1989, of May 9, on the Public Statistical Function, those whose realization is mandatory for the Spanish State by requirement of European Union regulations. Likewise, the statistics that may be carried out under the provisions of article 8.3 of the aforementioned Law.”

Article 25. Data processing in the context of public statistical function.
1. The processing of personal data carried out by the bodies entrusted with the competencies related to the exercise of the public statistical function shall be subject to the provisions of their specific legislation, as well as to Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and this organic law.
2. The communication of data to the competent statistical authorities shall only be understood as covered by Article 6.1 e) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 in cases where the statistics for which the information is required is mandated by a European Union law or is included in the legally established statistical programming instruments. In accordance with the provisions of Article 11.2 of Law 12/1989, of May 9, on the Public Statistical Function, the data referred to in Articles 9 and 10 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 shall be strictly voluntary and, consequently, may only be collected with the express consent of the affected individuals.
3. The competent bodies for the exercise of the public statistical function may deny requests from affected individuals to exercise the rights established in Articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 when the data is protected by the guarantees of statistical confidentiality provided for in state or regional legislation. Therefore, in accordance with the specific regulations, it is determined that, in this case, we are faced with a situation of mandatory statistics as it is included in the National Statistical Plan approved by Royal Decree 410/2016, of October 31, which includes among the information that must be provided, as indicated in the consultation, the telephone number as a quality control instrument for the actions carried out, so that “This variable thus becomes not only an initial contact instrument with the informant but is also part of the content of the questionnaire.” And since it is a mandatory statistics, there is a legal obligation for the citizens previously selected in the survey sample to provide the requested information, which is classified in Article 50 of Law 12/1989 as a minor infraction “The submission or delay in sending data when it has not caused serious harm to the service, and there is an obligation to provide it,” as a serious infraction “The non-submission or delay in sending the required data when it causes serious harm to the service, and there is an obligation to provide it,” and as a very serious infraction “The non-submission or delay in sending the required data when it causes severe harm to the service, and there is an obligation to provide it.”

It is precisely the obligation to complete the survey that allows for the differentiation of this case from others where participation is voluntary, such as those conducted by the Center for Sociological Research, since, involving the processing of special categories of data, Article 5 of Law 39/1995, of December 19, on the Organization of the Center for Sociological Research, establishes the principle of voluntariness of responses, which requires that, in the event that the CNMC intends to communicate telephone numbers to the CIS, additional guarantees must be adopted, as analyzed in Report 49/2020.
Therefore, in the present case, the communication of subscriber data to the telephone service by the CNMC for the preparation of an official statistic, which is mandatory to complete, included in the National Statistical Plan and executed by the State statistical services, would find its basis in Article 6.1.e) of the GDPR (“the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller”).
In any case, the data controller must comply with the principles established in Article 5 of the GDPR, noting in the consultation that the use of these phones would be limited exclusively to conducting the fieldwork of the Survey, so that it would not be subject to additional processing, adhering to the principles of purpose limitation and data minimization, and that the principle of storage limitation would also be guaranteed by destroying the provided telephone numbers once the quality approval of the report has been given.
Additionally, the other guarantees inherent to the exercise of statistical functions must be included, such as adherence to statistical confidentiality or the dissociation of the data from the moment the survey is conducted, separating the responses given by the respondents from the telephone numbers, as well as the other appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure respect for the rights and freedoms of the affected individuals and the adequacy of the processing to the regulations on personal data protection that the Ministry of Culture, as the data controller, must adopt, in accordance with the principle of proactive responsibility.
For all these reasons, this Agency understands that the communication of telephone numbers by the CNMC to the statistical services of the Ministry of Culture, under the terms provided in this report, is covered by the GDPR as long as, as already indicated in Report 70/2015, the data is used solely for the purpose of conducting the statistics for which it is requested.
All this without prejudice to the fact that, based on the consultation regarding the restrictions arising from the COVID-19 pandemic and as previously mentioned in Report 49/2020, it being recommended in section 28 of the 2020 Annual Program of the National Statistical Plan 2017-2020, the promotion of multichannel data collection in household surveys through electronic and telephone means, taking into account the evolution of society in the use of the Internet and the effectiveness of telephone data collection, this Agency considers it necessary that, in the event that the generalization of surveys through electronic and telephone means is anticipated, the corresponding legislative modification be promoted to ensure legal certainty and the adequacy of the corresponding personal data processing to the GDPR and the doctrine of the Constitutional Court, by establishing the specific guarantees deemed appropriate.
Therefore, the same criterion must be applied to the present case as identical circumstances exist, and it must be concluded that the communication of telephone numbers by the CNMC to the statistical services of the Bank of Spain, under the terms provided in this report, is covered by the GDPR as long as, as already indicated in Report 70/2015, the data is used solely for the purpose of conducting the statistics for which it is requested.
Furthermore, it should be emphasized the necessity to dissociate the data from the moment the survey is conducted, separating the responses given by the respondents from the telephone numbers, which must be deleted once the quality approval of the report has been given.
