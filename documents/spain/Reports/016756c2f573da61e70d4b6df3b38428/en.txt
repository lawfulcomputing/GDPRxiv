Legal Advisory Office  
N/REF: 0028/2022  

The inquiry raises whether the request for information made by the Court of Auditors to political parties to control their accounting legality is in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (hereinafter GDPR) and with Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter LOPDGDD).  

The inquirer states that, as a political party, it has received a request for information from the Court of Auditors regarding, on one hand, (i) the verification of the origin of the funds received in bank accounts opened exclusively for the deposit of contributions from public officials and membership fees, for which a sample of bank statements of both contributions and fees corresponding to certain territorial offices is requested, and in the case of direct debit remittances, a detailed account of the same, including the name, surname, and ID number of the members; and on the other hand, (ii) to verify the correct accounting registration of the payroll and tax withholdings and social security contributions of a sample of the political party's workers, which includes personal data of the party's employees.  

Considering the above, the inquirer asks whether such a request is in accordance with the principle of proportionality and whether it aligns with the financial and accounting control purpose of the Court of Auditors, as it may affect, in addition to the right to data protection, other fundamental rights such as privacy and ideological freedom, to the extent that it contains special categories of data (personal data of members, employees, and individuals linked to the political party).  

Furthermore, the inquiry extends to the requests that political parties, as legal entities, receive for their activities related to Article 31 bis of Organic Law 10/1995, of November 23, of the Penal Code, in the development of the Compliance area, and the ability to access personal data, backgrounds, and reports that may also affect the privacy of individuals (e.g., Articles 18.1 CE and 18.4 CE).  

The last aspect of the inquiry focuses on how to provide information to the Court of Auditors in the exercise of its auditing function regarding the accounting legality of the political party, as Article 19 of Organic Law 8/2007, of July 4, on the financing of political parties (hereinafter LOFPP), under the heading "Duty of collaboration," in its first section refers to "submission" in general terms, while in the second section it refers to "making available," when aware of data, reports, or backgrounds that affect the privacy of individuals.  

The inquirer believes that the provision should be interpreted as a whole and that it should not deliver a copy of the requested information but rather make it available to the Court of Auditors at the designated office or dependency for verification or cross-checking.  

I  
Having outlined the terms of the inquiry, it should be noted that for the processing of personal data to be in accordance with the GDPR, several elements of the data protection system must be fulfilled, including the principles of data protection contained in Article 5.1 of the GDPR, which states that personal data shall be:  
a) processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject ("lawfulness, fairness, and transparency");  
b) collected for specified, legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes; according to Article 89, paragraph 1, further processing of personal data for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes, or statistical purposes shall not be considered incompatible with the initial purposes ("purpose limitation");  
c) adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed ("data minimization");  
d) accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; all reasonable steps must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate with respect to the purposes for which they are processed are erased or rectified without delay ("accuracy");  
e) kept in a form which permits identification of the data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes of the processing of personal data; personal data may be stored for longer periods as long as they are processed.

f) treated in such a way as to ensure an adequate level of security for personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, through the application of appropriate technical or organizational measures ("integrity and confidentiality").

The principle of lawfulness is the one that determines under what circumstances or conditions processing must be considered lawful. 
In general, the circumstances or legal bases that legitimize the processing of personal data are found in Article 6 of the GDPR, and regarding special categories of data, in Article 9.2 of the GDPR, which outlines the circumstances under which the general prohibition on processing such data established in paragraph 1 does not apply, that is, it is exempted.
This means that in order to process these latter data, coverage must be found in Article 9.2 GDPR, and once the general prohibition is exempted, one must refer to the circumstances of Article 6 GDPR to legitimize the processing in question. V
Another aspect that must be analyzed to respond to the inquiry raised is the type of personal data that will be subject to processing when fulfilling the request made by the Court of Auditors, and, if applicable, the necessity or not of meeting any additional requirement to ensure the lawfulness of such processing.
From the reading and documentation attached to the inquiry, it is inferred that the information requested by the Court of Auditors is, on one hand, related to the source of membership fees, which necessarily implies the identification of the members of the political party, and on the other hand, refers to a sample of the workers of the political formation.
What is relevant here will be to determine whether we are dealing with the processing of data that reveals political opinions.
Regarding political opinions and their legal nature from the perspective of data protection law, the Council of Europe’s Convention No. 108, dated January 28, 1981, for the protection of individuals with regard to the automated processing of personal data includes in the "special categories of data" personal data "that reveal [...] political opinions" (Article 6), which "shall not be processed unless domestic law provides appropriate safeguards," admitting as the only exception to the previous rule the case where "it constitutes a necessary measure in a democratic society" for the protection of state security, public safety, the monetary interests of the state, the repression of criminal offenses, or for the protection of the concerned individual and the rights and freedoms of others.  
In the GDPR, Article 9 indicates what special categories of data are, with a special protection regime, based on a general prohibition of processing and a system of exceptions in its paragraph 2. 
Specifically, the provision states in its paragraph 1 that:

However, that assertion cannot be maintained regarding the other information requested by the Court of Auditors in its request, which is the sample of workers from the party. Here, what is relevant is the employment relationship between the party as employer, with economic-financial obligations arising from such status, and the workers. 
It defies any logic to consider that political opinions can be revealed merely by working in a political party (the same cannot be said when we talk about collaborators and volunteers not bound by an employment relationship, from which precisely that "voluntariness to help and collaborate with the party" can infer a certain ideological positioning). 
Delving into this aspect, it should be indicated that under no circumstances could having one political opinion or another be a requirement for being hired by the employer, since in that hypothetical case, it would also mean that a person is not hired or is excluded from a selection process for not professing the employer's ideology, which would obviously violate, in addition to ordinary legality, other fundamental rights such as those provided in Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution.

Having made this clarification, the public interest of the actions of the Court of Auditors regarding the financial oversight of political parties is delineated in the aforementioned Organic Law 8/2007, of July 4, on the financing of political parties (LOFPP), which in its Explanatory Memorandum states that:
In this new regulation, it is intended that it be definitively assumed that the exercise of popular sovereignty requires that the political control of the institutions elected at the polls ultimately corresponds to the citizen, from which it reveals the indispensable need to establish guarantees and more means so that the financing system does not incorporate elements of distortion between the popular will and the exercise of political power.
The freedom of political parties in the exercise of their powers would be harmed if a model of total liberalization were allowed as a financing formula, since, if so, the influence that contributions from a particular source of financing could have on a specific political decision would always be questionable, breaking the function of political parties as institutions that channel the formation of popular will.
The financing of political parties must correspond to a mixed system that includes, on one hand, contributions from citizens and, on the other, resources from public authorities in proportion to their representativeness as a means of guaranteeing the independence of the system, but also its sufficiency. Private contributions must come from natural or legal persons that do not contract with public administrations, be public, and not exceed reasonable and realistic limits.
Therefore, it will be the responsibility of the Court of Auditors, in general, to control the legality of the use of public funds to ensure good financial management, informing of any case of irregularity in doing so, and especially, the control of legality over the origin of the funds with which political formations are financed.

Likewise, and only in these cases, political parties may fulfill the obligation referred to in paragraph one of this article by making the information available to the Court at the location or office they designate.

As can be seen, two mechanisms are established that serve to adopt guarantees and measures to ensure the least interference in the right: on one hand, the duty of secrecy or professional confidentiality, and on the other, the provision of documentation instead of submission. 

Furthermore, it should be noted the reference made by the regulation to the administrative or criminal liabilities that may correspond in case of violating the express obligation of confidentiality. In this regard, it is worth indicating that the administrative responsibilities of the officials of the Court of Auditors (Article 37.1 of Organic Law 2/1982, of May 12, on the Court of Auditors) are regulated in a norm with the rank of law, such as Royal Legislative Decree 5/2015, of October 30, which approves the consolidated text of the Basic Statute of Public Employees, and on the other hand, criminal responsibilities are regulated in Organic Law 10/1995, of November 23, of the Penal Code.

Finally, another aspect that is analyzed later in this report and that must be taken into account here is the prior knowledge of the holders of the special categories of data subject to processing, regarding the possibility of carrying out the processing consisting of the communication of their data. 

That is to say, the laws regulating the actions of the Court of Auditors and political parties establish the duties of collaboration of these with said body, which leads affiliates or "donors" to foresee the possibility that certain data processing may take place, as provided for in the laws (in the Organic Law of political parties (Article 13.2), the formal and personal obligations arising from the financial control carried out by the Court of Auditors are expressly assumed). Ultimately, they do not see their expectation of privacy or predictability of processing compromised.

From the above, it is concluded that the identification of party affiliates through the control of the origin of contributions and donations by the Court of Auditors constitutes a processing of special categories of data that is legitimized under Articles 9.2 g) and 6.1 e) of the GDPR.

Indeed, they must be aware of the existence of the laws applicable to these formations, among which the Organic Law 6/2002, of June 27, on Political Parties, is now of interest, which in its article 13.2 expressly assumes "the formal and personal obligations regarding the accreditation of purposes and compliance with the requirements set forth in the aforementioned regulations concerning the control of the public funds they receive," and Organic Law 8/2007, of July 4, on the financing of political parties and the consequences that arise from its application, among which is the knowledge by the competent authorities in the exercise of their functions and under certain assumptions, of their names and surnames and NIF or equivalent. 
Consequently, there is no breach of the predictability of processing, and with the information provided in the consultation, there are no elements that allow us to affirm that the communication is made arbitrarily. 
IX 
Another aspect cited by the requester is the adequacy of the information request made by the Court of Auditors to the provisions of article 19 of the LOTFF and the need to interpret the provision as a whole, to which it indicates: 
One. Political parties shall be obliged to send any documents, records, data, and justifications required by the Court of Auditors for the fulfillment of its auditing function. 
Two. When, in the exercise of the auditing function, the authorities or officials of the Court of Auditors become aware of data, reports, or records that affect the privacy of individuals, they shall be obliged to the strictest and complete confidentiality regarding them, incurring, if they fail to comply with this obligation, the administrative or criminal responsibilities that may correspond. 
Likewise, and only in these cases, political parties may comply with the obligation referred to in paragraph one of this article by making the information available to the Court at the location or dependency designated by them. 
The requester proposes that the last paragraph of the provision should apply, and instead of "sending" the requested information, it should "make it available" for verification. Well, it should be indicated that this is a procedural issue regarding how to carry out the functions of the Court of Auditors, subject to judicial control and that, therefore, this Agency is not competent to rule on this matter.
