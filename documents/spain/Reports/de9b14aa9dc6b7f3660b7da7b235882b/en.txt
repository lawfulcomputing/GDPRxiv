I

Before proceeding to analyze the text submitted for report, it is necessary to point out that, given the legal basis of the report that will be issued immediately and its mandatory nature, in accordance with the provisions of the regulations just mentioned, it should be indicated in the Explanatory Memorandum of the regulation that it has been submitted to the prior report of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.

On the other hand, it must be taken into account that the current regime regarding the protection of personal data has undergone a profound reform as a result of the approval of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation). The Regulation, which came into force on May 25, 2016, will be fully applicable, according to its Article 99.2, from May 25, 2018, thus being the regulation to be considered in matters of data protection by virtue of the principle of primacy of Union law and the direct effect of Regulations. Therefore, this report will analyze the Project submitted for report in light of the Regulation, as it will be the presumably applicable regulation at the time of its final approval.

II

That said, the Project submitted for report aims, according to its Article 1, to develop Law 13/2011, of May 27, on the regulation of gambling, regarding "the conditions under which entities holding licenses to carry out gambling activities included in the scope of the aforementioned Law may carry out advertising, sponsorship, promotion, or any other form of commercial communication of their activity" and "the policies for responsible gambling and consumer protection"; that is, the Project aims to develop what is established in Articles 7 and 8 of Law 13/2011, which regulate the two previously defined matters.

For this reason, the structure of the Project, apart from its preliminary title, is distinguished into two clearly defined Titles, with Title II developing the provisions of Article 7 of the Law and Title III developing its Article 8.

Entering into the provisions of Title II, the regime of commercial communications of gambling activities is established, starting from the principle, enshrined by Article 5.1, that "commercial communications shall be governed by the provisions of Law 13/2011, of May 27, this royal decree, and the rest of the regulations developing the aforementioned Law. Likewise, the general regulations in force regarding advertising, consumer and user protection, and unfair commercial practices shall apply, as well as the sectoral regulations applicable according to the medium or support used for dissemination, and, in particular, those regulating audiovisual communication services and information society and electronic commerce services."

In this regard, it should be noted that Article 7.2 a) of Law 13/2011 states that "The conditions to be included in the respective licenses for the authorization of advertising activities and their limits shall be established by regulation, particularly regarding (...) The sending of advertising or promotional communications by email or by any other equivalent electronic communication means, which shall only be possible if previously authorized by the recipient, in accordance with the provisions of the first paragraph of Article 21 of Law 34/2002, of July 11, on Information Society Services and Electronic Commerce."

On the other hand, the activity of gambling operators will be subject to the provisions of the regulations on the protection of personal data, thus ensuring the rights of the affected parties, whether or not they are users, in general and, in particular, regarding the receipt of commercial communications through any channel by any means.

Therefore, it would be advisable to add a final clause to Article 5.1 of the project stating that the sending of communications shall be carried out "in any case with full respect for the current regulations on the protection of personal data."

Precisely, Article 14 establishes the specific rules applicable to communications carried out through the various communication channels, although it refers only to those carried out through audiovisual communication services or through information society services.

Regarding the latter, it is stated in Article 14.3 a) that “Gaming operators who market games whose participation is subject to prior user registration may not send any type of commercial communication by email or equivalent means directed to registered users whose registration in the General Registry of Access Prohibitions to Gaming is made known to the operator by the authority responsible for gaming regulation or who have exercised their right to self-exclusion prior to the two days preceding the aforementioned sending of the commercial communication.”

Logically, this restrictive rule must be considered an addition to what is established in Article 21 of Law 34/2002, to which Article 7.2 a) of Law 13/2011 refers.

It should also be noted that the Regulation of Organic Law 15/1999 already regulated in its Article 49 the so-called advertising exclusion files, establishing in its section 4 that “those who intend to carry out treatment related to advertising or commercial prospecting activities must first consult the common files that may affect their actions, in order to avoid processing the data of those affected who have expressed their opposition or refusal to such treatment.”

This means that the carrying out of commercial communications, whether through information society services or through means other than such services and audiovisual communication systems, should in no case be carried out to those who have requested their inclusion in the exclusion systems just mentioned, unless the operator has the specific consent of the user for such commercial communications to be sent to them.

In this regard, it should also be taken into account that the Organic Law Project for the Protection of Personal Data is currently being processed by the General Courts, which aims to adapt Spanish law to the General Data Protection Regulation. This Project refers in its Article 23 to advertising exclusion systems, stating the following:

“1. The processing of personal data aimed at preventing the sending of commercial communications to those who have expressed their refusal or opposition to receiving them shall be lawful.

For this purpose, information systems, general or sectoral, may be created, in which only the essential data to identify the affected individuals will be included. These systems may also include preference services, through which the affected individuals limit the receipt of commercial communications to those coming from certain companies.

2. The entities responsible for the advertising exclusion systems shall communicate their creation, their general or sectoral nature, as well as the manner in which the affected individuals can join them and, where appropriate, assert their preferences to the Spanish Data Protection Agency.

The Spanish Data Protection Agency shall publish a list of such systems that have been communicated to it, incorporating the information mentioned in the previous paragraph.

3. When an affected individual expresses to a responsible party their wish for their data not to be processed for the sending of commercial communications, they must be informed of the existing advertising exclusion systems, and may refer to the information published by the Spanish Data Protection Agency.

4. Those who intend to carry out commercial communications must first consult the advertising exclusion systems that may affect their actions, excluding from processing the data of those affected who have expressed their opposition or refusal to it. For these purposes, to consider the previous obligation fulfilled, it will be sufficient to consult the advertising exclusion systems included in the list published by the Spanish Data Protection Agency.”

Well, from what has been indicated, two important consequences arise in the Project now submitted for report:

- On the one hand, it should be noted that the sending of commercial communications through electronic means would only be possible with the consent of the interested party, as prescribed by Article 21 of Law 34/2002 and recalled by Article 7.2 a) of Law 13/2011.

- On the other hand, in all types of commercial communications directed to a specific user, it must be verified prior to their execution whether such user is registered in the advertising exclusion systems, in order not to carry out the communication, through any channel, in the event of being in them, unless the specific operator has the explicit consent of the recipient for the communication to take place.

III 

Within the provisions related to responsible gaming policies 
and user protection, Article 24 refers to the regulation of 
awareness mechanisms regarding the duty of operators to collaborate 
with the administration in raising awareness and promoting responsible gaming, 
stating that “For the purposes of promoting or addressing statistical studies 
on responsible gaming and gaming patterns, the authority responsible for 
gaming regulation may require the operator's collaboration to 
communicate to registered users the existence of questionnaires about their 
experience and gaming habits, as well as to facilitate access to them. 
The response by the player will be voluntary and anonymous in any case, 
without prejudice to the possibility of subjecting access to the 
questionnaires to identification in order to preserve the integrity of the 
data obtained.” 

In this regard, it should be noted that Article 2113 of Law 13/2011 
assigns to the National Gaming Commission, whose competencies are 
developed by the General Directorate of Gaming Regulation, the function of 
“promoting and conducting studies and research work on gaming, 
as well as its incidence or impact on society,” and must also, 
according to Article 21.9, “ensure that the interests of participants and 
vulnerable groups are protected, as well as compliance with the laws, 
regulations, and principles that govern them, to defend public order and 
prevent unauthorized gaming.” 

This means that the conduct of the studies referred to in the 
provision would find its support in the competencies just reproduced, 
so the required collaboration is covered by the gaming regulatory framework. 

However, the provision indicates that the responses by players to 
the questionnaires referred to in the provision will be “voluntary and 
anonymous,” although it will be possible to re-identify those who respond to 
the questionnaire in order to preserve the integrity of the data obtained; 
that is, the responses given by the players.

In relation to this point, it must be based on the principle of minimization, contained in Article 5.1 c) of the General Data Protection Regulation, according to which the data shall be “adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed.”

In this sense, the provision included in the last clause would prevent a true anonymization of the data concerning the individuals who have responded to the questionnaires, since full anonymization cannot exist when the very regulation that provides for the surveys to be answered anonymously also establishes that re-identification of the respondents will be possible.

Taking into account the principle of anonymity enshrined in the provision itself as well as the principle of data minimization, which should prevent a possible re-identification of the affected individuals, it should, in our opinion, be modified the last clause of Article 24 of the Draft submitted for report, removing the expression “without prejudice to the possibility of subjecting access to the questionnaires to identification in order to preserve the integrity of the data obtained” and adding a new clause to the provision stating that “Operators must adopt the technical and organizational measures that guarantee the integrity of the responses provided by users to the questionnaires.”

IV

Finally, it must be analyzed what is provided in Article 26 of the Draft subject to this report, according to which “Operators must establish mechanisms and protocols that allow for the detection of risk behaviors of registered users.” To this end, the provision adds, “objective criteria or indicators that reveal patterns of activity such as, for example, the volume, frequency, and variability of participations or deposits will be taken into account, without prejudice to other quantitative or qualitative elements that may also prove relevant according to the mechanics of the different games or the experience of the operator.”

Section 2 states that “upon detecting a user who may be exhibiting risk behavior, and without prejudice to other possible measures adopted within the framework of their corporate social responsibility and which must be included in an action protocol, the operator will communicate this to them via email or equivalent means. The message will contain information regarding the participations and spending of the player in the recent period determined by the operator and the possible existence, if applicable, of changes in the player’s patterns of gaming or spending behavior, as well as recommendations to, at a minimum, access the gaming area.”

Finally, regarding this report, section 3 provides that “before January 31 of each year, the operator must communicate to the authority responsible for regulating gaming the updated version of the basic description of the mechanisms and protocols implemented that allow for the detection of risky behaviors, the action protocol in the event of detection of such behaviors, the total number of users with detected risky behavior during the previous year according to the established mechanisms, as well as the actions taken and the follow-up and effect of the same,” without prejudice to the fact that, as provided in section 4, “the authority responsible for regulating gaming may develop, by resolution, the specific mechanisms to which the detection of risky behaviors shall be subject, as well as the content of the protocols that operators and, where applicable, the authority itself must adopt towards users, once such behaviors are detected.”

As a starting point, it should be noted that Article 8.1 of Law 13/2011 states that “gaming operators must develop a plan of measures regarding the mitigation of the possible harmful effects that gaming may produce on individuals and incorporate the basic rules of responsible gaming policy,” including in its subsection a) as one of its objectives, with respect to consumer protection, “to pay due attention to at-risk groups.”

This means that the necessary treatments for compliance with the provisions established in the aforementioned precept, which gaming operators must carry out, are covered by the provisions of Article 6.1 c) of the General Data Protection Regulation, which establishes that the processing of personal data will be lawful “if it is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation applicable to the data controller.”

However, from what is established in Article 26, it follows that operators must develop systems that allow for the detection of patterns for the identification of users in at-risk situations, based on different parameters, of which the Project only cites a few examples. Logically, for the detection of the group in such a situation, operators must apply the protocol to all their users, in order to know to whom to direct the communications referred to in Article 26.2 of the Project.

In this way, the processing provided for in Article 26.1 may involve the massive use of data for the detection of factors that allow for correlations with the risk situations to be prevented, which may imply the

the use of "big data" techniques that would also be applied, as mentioned,  
to the entire group of players. In this way, the massive processing could also generate another type of profiles that could be used by the operator for purposes other than those established in Article 26 of the Project in relation to Article 8 of Law 13/2011.  

Article 5.1 b) of the General Data Protection Regulation establishes that data must be “collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes, and not further processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes; in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1, further processing of personal data for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific and historical research purposes, or statistical purposes shall not be considered incompatible with the initial purposes.”  

The use of data obtained from the massive processing of data for the prevention of risk situations for purposes aimed at promoting the operator's activity, even not only from a commercial point of view, must thus be considered an incompatible purpose with that which justifies the processing, based on the legal obligation provided in Article 8.1 of Law 13/2011. To this end, it cannot be considered that there is a link between the aforementioned obligation and the widespread profiling for different purposes that could be considered a “reasonable expectation” for the purposes derived from the aforementioned Article 5.1 b).  

For this reason, it is deemed necessary that the Project submitted for report expressly provides that the processing of personal data of users resulting from the application of the protocols mentioned in Article 26.1 may only aim at the detection of groups in risk situations, and the patterns used for compliance with such protocols cannot be applied for different purposes.  

V  

Finally, the Project provides in its Article 26.3 that data regarding the total number of users with risky behavior detected according to the mechanisms mentioned in paragraph 1 will be communicated to the competent authority in gaming regulation.  

The aggregated transmission of the aforementioned data would not constitute a transfer of personal data, being necessary to point out at this point that Law 13/2011 does not grant in its Article 21 to the competent authority for gaming regulation powers for the processing of identifying data of such groups unless access is carried out within the framework of the investigative powers granted by Article 24 of the Law, whose paragraph 1 provides that “In order to guarantee the provisions of this Law and the provisions that complement it, the National Gaming Commission shall be responsible for the auditing, monitoring, inspection, and control of all aspects and administrative, economic, procedural, technical, computer, telematic, and documentation standards related to the development of the activities provided for in this Law,” adding the second paragraph of Article 24.3 that “the National Gaming Commission shall have access to the personal data collected in the user account of the participants, respecting at all times the provisions of Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13, on Data Protection and its implementing regulations.”  

In the aforementioned cases, access to the data of those in risk situations, in order, for example, to audit the implementation of the mechanisms and protocols provided in Article 26.1 of the Project would be possible as long as it occurs within the scope of the mentioned inspection actions and not as a result of a possible mass communication of the data, as there is no legal basis that would allow the regulatory authority to process such information.
