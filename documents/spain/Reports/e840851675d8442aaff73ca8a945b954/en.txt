The inquiry raises the question of whether, from the perspective of Regulation 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, regarding the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data (GDPR) and the Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and guarantee of digital rights (LOPDGDD), and taking into account the Judgment of the Supreme Court No. 476/2020 of September 25, the content of the right of access to the medical history includes information about who has accessed it.  

I  
The information referred to in the medical history must be considered health data (Article 4.15 GDPR), and therefore falls within the special categories of data whose processing is prohibited by paragraph 1 of Article 9 of the GDPR, unless one of the exceptions provided for in paragraph 2 applies.  
In general terms and in relation to the subject of the inquiry concerning the field of healthcare and medical diagnosis where the medical history serves its main function, the provision in letter h) would apply, according to which the processing is necessary for the purposes of preventive or occupational medicine, assessment of the worker's capacity, medical diagnosis, provision of healthcare or social treatment, or management of health and social care systems and services, based on Union law or the laws of the Member States or by virtue of a contract with a healthcare professional and without prejudice to the conditions and guarantees provided for in paragraph 3; without prejudice to other circumstances that may exempt the general prohibition of processing that may arise in other cases.  
For its part, the LOPDGDD in its Article 9.2 provides the following:  
“The data processing contemplated in letters g), h) and i) of Article 9.2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 based on Spanish law must be covered by a norm with the rank of law, which may establish additional requirements regarding its security and confidentiality.”  
In particular, such a norm may cover the processing of data in the health field when required by the management of public and private health and social care systems and services, or the execution of an insurance contract of which the affected party is a part.”  
And in its additional provision 17, in paragraph 1 it indicates that the data processing related to health and regulated in the laws it lists, including, in relation to the inquiry raised, Law 41/2002, of November 14, which regulates the autonomy of the patient and rights and obligations regarding information and clinical documentation (hereinafter LAP), is covered by letters g), h), i) and j) of Article 9.2 of the GDPR.

1. The patient has the right of access, with the reservations indicated
in paragraph 3 of this article, to the documentation of the medical history and to
obtain a copy of the data contained therein. Health centers shall regulate the procedure that guarantees the observance of these rights.
2. The patient's right of access to the medical history may also be exercised by duly accredited representation.
3. In this regard, it should be noted that there are autonomous regulations that provide that the content of the right to access the clinical history also includes knowing who has accessed it.

1. Every person has the right to the confidentiality of all information related to data concerning their health and stays in public or private health centers. IV
That said, the reiterated criterion of this Agency is that the content of the right to access the clinical history does not include knowing the information about who has accessed it, as it neither forms part of the clinical history nor refers to personal data of third parties, as referenced in Reports 165/2005 and 171/2008 that the consulting party itself cites in its writing.
What follows is to analyze the content and scope of the Judgment of the Supreme Court No. 476/2020 of September 25 (the Judgment or STS 476/2020 hereinafter), for the purpose of determining whether it affects the criterion upheld so far and to what extent it may represent an expansion of the content of the right of access, in the sense of understanding that it includes information about who has accessed the clinical history.
First of all, it should be noted that the STS 476/2020 corresponds to the criminal order and, as far as it concerns here, aims to analyze the adequacy to the legal system of one of the pieces of evidence that served to convict the accused of a crime of discovery and revelation of secrets, under article 197.2 of the Penal Code in force at the time of the events.

In particular, the Judgment analyzes whether the complainant's knowledge of who accessed their medical history – which served to determine the existence of the typical and unlawful conduct described in Article 197.2 of the Penal Code (CP) – is lawful, and if not, to what extent it can invalidate the judicial process due to the acquisition of illicit evidence.

The facts of the Judgment can be summarized as follows: two individuals unrelated to the patient's care processes accessed their medical history, thereby violating not only the Law on Patient Autonomy (LAP) but also the Organic Law on Data Protection (LOPD), ultimately incurring the conduct described in Article 197.2 CP. 

The affected party and complainant is aware of who accessed their medical history because a third party with access to that information provided it to them, thus occurring outside the "regulatory channel" (sic). This information serves, among other things, as evidence to convict the accused, and what is disputed in the appeal is the "legality" of that evidence.

Based on this premise, it should be noted what the Judgment states:
(...) It is true that Article 18.3 of Law 41/2002 provides that the right to information "cannot be exercised to the detriment of the right of third parties to the confidentiality of the data contained therein collected in the therapeutic interest of the patient, nor to the detriment of the right of the professionals involved in its preparation, who may oppose the right of access the reservation of their subjective notes," but it is not established that these limits were exceeded by the complainant, as it is not alleged that access was made to information that could affect third parties or professionals, such as the "subjective notes" that practitioners sometimes record in medical histories.

As noted in the first-instance judgment, the fact that the patient accessed their medical history is of no relevance. The issue raised here is different. What is criticized in the appeals is that the complainant accessed the records of the individuals who had consulted or viewed their medical history, and it is argued that there is no right to knowledge of that data. (...)

Focusing the legal debate, the Judgment analyzes whether the obtaining of evidence that could be classified as illicit entails its automatic exclusion, and to what extent it affects the essential content of the right to effective judicial protection and to a process with all guarantees (Article 24 of the Spanish Constitution - CE), for which it cites the doctrine of the Constitutional Court stating that:

In any case, the exclusion of such evidence is not automatic but requires determining whether its admission entails a breach of the procedural guarantees of Article 24.2 CE, which necessitates a balancing judgment aimed at ensuring the balance and equality of the parties and making possible the integrity of the process in question, as a fair and equitable process. And it is precisely in the balancing judgment made by the Court where it affirms the issues that the consultant believes may modify the criteria maintained so far by this Agency. 

Specifically, when the Judgment conducts the balancing judgment of the case submitted to cassational review, it indicates the following:
(...) It is hardly possible to invoke that right by someone, like the appellant, who accesses a computer system violating access rules to obtain reserved information about another person, and when, moreover, their conduct constitutes a crime.

It is true that the complainant also accessed the information from the system outside the legally or regulatory prescribed channels, but that irregularity is not comparable and, certainly, lacks constitutional relevance. The complainant accessed their own personal data, which appeared in the database by legal provision and to which they had a right to know. The irregularity of their conduct was limited to not having used the regulatory channel provided. In contrast, the convicted individuals accessed the system not only outside the legally prescribed channels but to obtain information belonging to another, which they were not permitted to know, committing a crime. The described behaviors are not comparable, and in the case of the appellants, there was no infringement of their right to data protection. The absence of constitutional injury would be sufficient by itself to reject the nullity of the evidence requested in the appeal. (...)

Therefore, taking into account the context and purpose served by the reasoning of the Judgment, it cannot be stated that access to information about who accessed the medical history is being legitimized.

Rather, in the specific case and from the perspective of the right to effective judicial protection provided in Article 24 of the CE, its impact on the specific judicial process and its collision with the right to data protection (Article 18.4 CE) of the accused, what the Supreme Court does is assess to what extent the irregular access made by the complainant infringes the rights of the accused in the process, to conclude that the evidence was not used.

regulatory channel, and that there is no constitutional injury to the rights of the accused. 
This issue is far from asserting that within the content of the right to access medical records, there is also the right to know who has accessed them. What the Judgment states is that such irregularity does not invalidate the evidence obtained and does not affect the process.
In short, the Judgment analyzes a specific case from the perspective of the legality of the evidence obtained and its impact on the judicial process and the rights of the accused, reaching the indicated conclusion. This does not mean that the Judgment is legitimizing or "legalizing," from the perspective of the LAP or the LOPDGDD, that the right to access medical records should also include knowing who has accessed them. 
V
In accordance with the above and to respond to the inquiry raised, the criteria established in Reports 165/2005 and 171/2008 must be maintained, and therefore, it should not be understood that the information regarding whether there have been accesses and the identity of the persons who have accessed is included within the right to access medical records under Article 18 of the LAP, unless expressly provided for in the applicable regional regulations or other norms.
