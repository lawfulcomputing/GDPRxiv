As stated in the Regulatory Impact Analysis Report (MAIN) submitted along with the Draft Royal Decree, Law 28/2022, of December 21, promoting the ecosystem of emerging companies, provides in its provisions for the creation of controlled testing environments for limited periods of time with the aim of assessing the utility, viability, and impact of technological innovations. Specifically, Article 16.1 establishes that “[p]ublic authorities shall promote, through regulation, the creation of controlled environments, for limited periods of time, to evaluate the utility, viability, and impact of technological innovations applied to regulated activities, the offering or provision of new goods or services, new forms of provision or delivery of the same, or alternative formulas for their supervision and control by the competent authorities,” specifying in subsection 4 of that provision the principles to which the creation and development of controlled testing environments must adhere. Likewise, Annex I of the Draft Royal Decree states that the European Commission adopted a proposal for a Regulation establishing harmonized rules on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Regulation) on April 21, 2021. This proposal from the Commission aims to ensure that artificial intelligence systems marketed in the Union's market and used therein are safe and comply with existing legislation regarding fundamental rights and the values of the Union, to guarantee legal certainty to facilitate investment and innovation in artificial intelligence, and to improve governance and the effective enforcement of existing legislation regarding fundamental rights and security, as well as to facilitate the development of a single market for legal, safe, and reliable artificial intelligence applications and to avoid market fragmentation, with Annex II establishing a list of areas of artificial intelligence systems considered to be of specific high risk.  

I  
The Draft Royal Decree “establishing a controlled testing environment for the trial of compliance with the proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council establishing harmonized rules on artificial intelligence” aims, as stated in its Article 1 “Object” subsection 1, to verify the design, validation, and monitoring requirements that must be established in the development of artificial intelligence systems that may pose risks to the safety, health, and fundamental rights of individuals:  

Article 1. Object  
1. This Royal Decree aims to create an experience to verify the requirements in which different entities selected through a public call participate, in which, as a trial, some artificial intelligence systems that may pose risks to the safety, health, and fundamental rights of individuals are selected, with the objective of conducting the experience that may subsequently facilitate all European organizations in implementing the principles that will govern the design, validation, and monitoring of artificial intelligence systems, and that will help mitigate such risks.  

As established in Article 5 “Eligibility Requirements for Access” of the Draft Royal Decree, participation in the controlled testing environment is open to providers of artificial intelligence systems (hereinafter “AI systems”) and, as participating users, legal entities that make use of a high-risk artificial intelligence system, general-purpose systems, or foundational models, provided that the provider of such systems also accesses the controlled testing environment:  

Article 5. Eligibility Requirements for Access.  
1. Participation in the controlled testing environment is open to those AI providers and users residing in Spain or having a permanent establishment in Spain, or being part of an association of entities, where the representative of the association or sole attorney, being a member of it, is the applicant entity and whose domicile or main establishment is necessarily located in Spanish territory for the purposes of Article 9 of Legislative Royal Decree 1/2010, of July 2, which approves the consolidated text of the Capital Companies Act.  
2. Private legal entities and public sector entities in Spain that make use of a high-risk artificial intelligence system, general-purpose systems, or foundational models may access this environment as participating users, provided that the AI provider of that system also accesses the environment.  

Therefore, the controlled testing environment will relate to “AI systems” and also to the use of AI systems in treatments of certain users.

Regarding the processing of personal data, Article 3 of the Draft Decree establishes in its section 3 the definition of "artificial intelligence system":
“Artificial intelligence system”: a system designed to operate with a certain level of autonomy and that, based on input data provided by machines or by individuals, infers how to achieve a set of established objectives using machine learning strategies or those based on logic and knowledge, and generates output information, such as content (generative artificial intelligence systems), predictions, recommendations, or decisions, that influence the environments with which it interacts.
It should be noted that an AI system will consist of an AI algorithm and other elements that will allow for the effective implementation and operation of the algorithm, and that may condition the operational parameters of the system in multiple aspects. In short, through incoming data, and by applying the system's algorithm, it obtains output information, with the aim—he adds—of achieving a set of established objectives using machine learning strategies or those based on logic and knowledge.
On the other hand, Article 2, section 1, of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation or GDPR) establishes the material scope of this regulation in "the processing of personal data," not in systems, technologies, or technical infrastructures:
1.   This Regulation applies to the total or partial automated processing of personal data, as well as to the non-automated processing of personal data contained in or intended to be included in a filing system.
A processing operation will be defined, as established by Article 24, section 1 of the GDPR, by its nature, scope, context, and purposes:
Taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is in compliance with this Regulation. Such measures shall be reviewed and updated as necessary.

The nature of a processing operation implies the way in which such processing is effectively implemented. The implementation of a processing operation can be divided into different processing operations, as established by Article 4.2 of the GDPR:
“processing”: any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination, or any other form of enabling access, comparison, or interconnection, limitation, erasure, or destruction;
The processing operations may, in turn, be manual or automated, in whole or in part. Automated implementation can be carried out using different systems, such as mobile systems, local storage systems, cloud systems, encryption systems, video surveillance systems, and/or AI systems.
In the case at hand, and as explained in the article published by the AEPD "Artificial Intelligence: System vs. Processing, Means vs. Purpose," April 1, 2023, a single processing operation may include one or several AI systems in a specific implementation of that processing. Therefore, an AI system itself is not a processing operation, but, in some cases and not always, it could be part of a processing operation.
III
An AI system may not be part of a processing of personal data either in its design/development, in its distribution by a provider, in its operation within the framework of a processing operation, or in its evolution.
For example, an AI system operating in an industrial production environment, such as on an assembly line, in which personal data have not been used for its development and that has no interaction or decision-making in relation to human operators, would not be involved in a processing operation within the material scope of the GDPR (hereinafter GDPR processing).
However, there are various GDPR processing operations in which one or several AI systems could be involved, as explained in the guide.

First, an AI system may be involved in a GDPR processing activity whose purpose is the design and development of an automated system. In the event that the decision has been made to develop such an automated system using AI technology, and that such AI technology requires the use of personal data for its development (e.g., a machine learning AI system or certain rule-based systems), we would be in the scenario of a GDPR processing activity. It should be noted that in the decision of how to develop such an automated system, alternatives to AI or AI that does not require personal data could have been chosen.
Second, an AI system could contain data of identified or identifiable individuals. This circumstance does not always occur in an AI system, nor is it exclusive to AI systems, but it could happen. In that case, the distribution by a provider of an AI system with such characteristics could involve the communication of personal data (that is, processing of personal data) when there are identifiable individuals' data that can be extracted from the AI system.
Third, a processing activity may implement one or several of its operations using one or several AI systems to automatically process personal data, to make decisions regarding natural persons, or to profile an individual. For example, when in a personnel recruitment process an AI system is used in the selection operations to implement a preliminary filter of the most relevant resumes, this will involve the processing of personal data and automated decision-making with legal effects or that could significantly affect similarly. It would then be a choice of the data controller to add or not qualified human oversight in such processing.
Finally, a fourth processing activity may occur when some AI systems have the characteristic of being able to evolve during the execution of the processing by the controller using personal data from the processing. Regarding the evolution processing of AI systems, the following cases could arise: either the processing is carried out by a third party to fulfill its own purposes, or by that third party to fulfill the purposes of the controller, or by the controller itself for its own purposes.

IV
The Draft Royal Decree establishes in Annex II a list of areas of high-risk AI systems, a concept that should be nuanced in relation to the definition of high risk in the GDPR. 
Regarding high risk, Article 35 of the GDPR “Data Protection Impact Assessment” (DPIA) establishes that the data controller must determine whether a processing operation is of high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, in particular, if new technologies are used. The existence of high risk in a processing operation is determined non-exhaustively in sections 35.3 and the lists of 35.4 of Article 35 of the GDPR, in the cases of Article 32, section 2, or Recital 75 of the GDPR, in the cases of Article 28.2 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), in the cases and examples of Guidelines WP248 of the Article 29 Working Party (now European Data Protection Committee - EDPB), in the specific regulations that require a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA), in the specific cases and conditions described in the guidelines published by the EDPB for specific processing operations, in the specific cases and conditions described in the codes of conduct in accordance with Article 40, and in the certification mechanisms in accordance with Article 42 of the GDPR. 
In summary, the risk classification established in the Draft Royal Decree complements, but does not replace, the risk assessment for personal data processing established in the GDPR. It complements it in the sense that it determines the high risk of those processing operations that use such types of systems. Specifically, in the various processing operations involving an AI system, risk management must be carried out considering not only the nature of some of its operations (one or several AI systems), but also the risk to the rights and freedoms that the combined use of all systems implementing the processing entails, the scope or extent of such processing, the context in which it takes place, and the purposes, as well as the uncertainty that the use of new technologies may introduce.
V
In the Draft Royal Decree, Article 5 “Eligibility Requirements for Access,” previously referenced, establishes the participation of different agents, such as AI providers and users, both private legal entities and members of the public sector. 
In the proposals submitted, the legal positions of each of the participants regarding personal data processing must be clearly defined, whether as controllers, joint controllers, processors, or sub-processors, as established by the GDPR, whose observance is not displaced but rather reinforced by the Draft Royal Decree (art. 16, Personal Data Protection). 
1. Both participating AI providers and participating users in the controlled testing environment shall respect the applicable data protection provisions. The regime for the protection of personal data in the actions carried out within the framework of this controlled testing environment is that provided for in both Regulation (EU) 679/2016 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (GDPR), and in Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), with all personal data processed in the private systems of the participating AI providers or, where applicable, of the participating users remaining subject to this protection to verify the requirements included under such protection. 
2. Acceptance of participation in the controlled testing environment will imply recognition of compliance with data protection legislation.
VI
In the Draft Royal Decree, in Article 1 “Object,” section 1, previously referenced, it is established that the objective is to extract experience regarding the principles that will later be applied at the European level for the design, validation, and monitoring of AI systems:
…the objective of conducting the experience that may subsequently facilitate all European organizations in implementing the principles that will govern the design, validation, and monitoring of artificial intelligence systems, and that will help mitigate such risks. 
On the other hand, in Article 4 “Legal Regime,” in section 2, it is established that AI systems are not exempt from compliance with their specific legislation, not from the design stage, but once placed on the market. Furthermore, as long as it has not been previously introduced into that market:

In the event that an artificial intelligence system is regulated by specific sector legislation and has not been introduced to the market or put into service prior to the request for its participation in the controlled testing environment, its participation therein does not exempt it from the requirement that the artificial intelligence system must pass the conformity assessment in accordance with the specific legislation once the artificial intelligence system is placed on the market. 
In this case, it should be noted that the provisions of the Royal Decree cannot displace the obligations of compliance with the GDPR and the LOPDGDD in the processing involving the AI system. In particular, personal data processing must be compliant from the design stage, which implies "before" its market launch. That is, it cannot be understood that an AI system that "has not yet been introduced to the market" can comply with the personal data processing regulations "once it is placed on the market," since the GDPR is mandatory legislation "from the design of the AI system." 
As participation in the controlled testing environment does not exclude the obligation to comply with data protection regulations, for reasons of legal certainty, there should be an explicit mention of Article 16 of the Draft Royal Decree, modified in accordance with the observations included in this report, and even its explicit inclusion in Article 4.2 of the Draft, or in a specific subsequent section. 
VII
In Article 7 of the Draft Royal Decree "Request for Access to the Controlled Testing Environment," it establishes in its section 3 the obligation to submit a responsible declaration regarding compliance with the regulations related to the protection of personal data:
3. The applications submitted must be accompanied by: a technical report that must include the content provided in Annex II; a responsible declaration certifying compliance with the regulations related to the Protection of Personal Data; and any documentation established in the call for applications. 
Unlike the technical report, which is included in Annex II of the Royal Decree, the responsible declaration does not appear therein. For legal certainty, it is suggested that the text of this declaration should appear in an Annex of the Royal Decree along with a descriptive Annex of the documentation evidencing the principle of proactive responsibility in the same way as the technical report. This annex should clearly reflect, among other things, the clear identification of the processing activities and the responsible/processor roles in relation to design/development, distribution, operation, and evolution, the legitimations of the processing activities, and, in the case of high-risk processing according to the GDPR, the favorable overcoming of the impact assessment for data protection (EIPD).

In Article 12, "Development of Tests by Participants," of the Draft Royal Decree, in section 2, it is established:
The adaptation of these artificial intelligence systems to comply with the requirements does not imply potential risks regarding the protection of consumers, users, and third parties who may be affected. 
As currently drafted, this section could be understood to imply that the Royal Decree conducts an a priori risk assessment ("does not imply risks"), unless it should be interpreted to mean that the adaptation of such AI systems should not imply risks. If this is the latter case, the wording should be clarified. If this is not the case, and as stated at the beginning of this report, the Royal Decree could not conduct an a priori risk assessment for the rights and freedoms established by the GDPR of a processing in which an AI system is (or will be) involved. It is the responsibility of the data controllers, in accordance with the GDPR, to analyze the risks and evaluate the impacts regarding data protection (Articles 24, 25, 32, 35, etc. GDPR) by adopting the necessary measures and even demonstrating compliance with this Regulation of the measures planned to address the risks, taking into account the rights and legitimate interests of the data subjects and other affected persons (Article 35.7.d) GDPR).

The principle of lawfulness of processing (Article 5.1.a) GDPR), the principle of proactive responsibility (Article 5.2 of the GDPR), and the principle of data protection by design (Article 25.1 of the GDPR) establish, as already mentioned, that compliance with the GDPR in the processing of personal data must be established and guaranteed before the processing begins. 

On the other hand, the principle of data minimization (Article 5.1.c of the GDPR) and the principle of storage limitation (Article 5.1.e of the GDPR) establish that data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed, and maintained in a way that allows for the identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes of processing personal data; personal data may be retained for longer periods provided that they are processed solely for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes, or statistical purposes, in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1 GDPR, without prejudice to the application of appropriate technical and organizational measures imposed by this Regulation to protect the rights and freedoms of the data subject.

In this case, the adequacy of the personal data used in the processing, for example, for the development processing through training of an AI system, must be evaluated prior to the commencement of processing, and if applicable, adequately certified by third parties. If it is not possible in any exceptional case, which must be properly justified prior to the start of the project, it must be determined that a processing operation has not been implemented that by design prevents such evaluation. This, in its case, could imply a breach of data protection regulations. Otherwise, it would be necessary to establish, among other things, the legitimacy of that retention, the proper management of the risk to rights and freedoms, and the application of privacy measures such as selecting a significant sample, if possible anonymized, with specific retention periods, or with synthetic data.

Furthermore, within the framework of the GDPR, it will need to be evaluated whether the operation of the AI system in an AI processing preserves the principle of accuracy (Article 5.1.d of the GDPR) of the decisions, profiles, or inferred values. In this case, what was stated in the previous section applies.

Therefore, the wording of the article should be clarified in the sense that all personal data processing carried out within the controlled testing environment must comply with data protection regulations, particularly the GDPR and the LOPDGDD, and the applicable sectoral regulations. Moreover, such compliance must be ensured prior to the execution of personal data processing.

Article 29 of the Project “Participation of private legal entities and public sector entities as users of an artificial intelligence system” establishes in section 2:
The private legal entity or public sector entity, when acting as participating users, must collaborate by implementing the measures specified in the guidelines provided by the competent authority for monitoring after the implementation of the artificial intelligence system.
To the extent that the processing involving the AI system deals with personal data, decisions about individuals, or profiling, the legal position under the GDPR of the user in relation to the developer, provider of the system, or evolver of the system must be defined. This circumstance should be reflected in this section, also making reference to what has been observed regarding the Responsible Declaration and the accreditation of what has been declared. Regarding the evolution of the AI system, the roles are more complex; we could enter a scenario of responsible-responsible communication or responsible-processor situations, in which case the provisions of Article 28 must be specifically taken into account.
XII
In Annex II “List of specific high-risk artificial intelligence system areas,” a set of AI systems that the Royal Decree establishes as high risk is enumerated.
As noted in section IV of this report, the risk classification established in the Draft Royal Decree complements but does not displace the risk assessment for personal data processing established in the GDPR. Specifically, in the various personal data processing operations involving an AI system, risk management must be carried out considering not only the nature of some of its operations (one or several AI systems) but also the risk to the rights and freedoms that the joint use of all systems implementing the processing entails, the scope or extent of such processing, the context in which it unfolds, and the purposes, as well as the uncertainty that the use of new technologies may introduce. To the extent that the processing is of high risk according to the GDPR, overcoming the data protection impact assessment (DPIA) prior to the execution of such processing will be necessary.
All of this is without prejudice to the fact that some of the high-risk systems that could participate in the controlled testing environment, such as those described in section 7.a “artificial intelligence systems intended for use by competent public authorities or on their behalf as polygraphs and similar tools, or to detect the emotional state of an individual;” in which there is no restriction on their use for purposes such as, for example, criminal investigation, or section 8.a “artificial intelligence systems intended for use by a judicial authority or on its behalf for the interpretation of facts or law to apply the law to a specific set of facts” should have a prior analysis of the lawfulness of the processing.
XIII
In Annex IV of the draft Royal Decree, within the “Technical Documentation to be submitted upon completion of the implementation of the requirements,” point 3 establishes:
Detailed information on the supervision, functioning, and control of the artificial intelligence system, particularly regarding: its capabilities and performance limitations, including the degrees of accuracy for specific individuals or groups of individuals for whom the system is intended to be used and the overall level of accuracy expected in relation to its intended purpose; the sources of risk to health and safety, fundamental rights, and discrimination in view of the intended purpose of the artificial intelligence system; the necessary human oversight measures in accordance with Article 11 and the guidelines provided by the competent authority for this purpose, including the technical measures established to facilitate the interpretation of the output information from the artificial intelligence systems by the users of the system; the specifications regarding input data as appropriate;
Regarding personal data processing, the aspects mentioned in this point should be established based on the impact they may have on the user’s processing, or on the intended purposes and specific operational contexts for which the system is validated. In particular, performance metrics, which must be selected according to the purposes and operational contexts and evaluated concerning the risk they may pose to the rights and freedoms of citizens within the framework of personal data processing carried out by a user.
