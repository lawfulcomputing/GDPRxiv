I  
As indicated in its Explanatory Memorandum, the Project submitted for report is approved in development of the provisions of Article 25.1 of Organic Law 4/2015, of March 30, which states that “Individuals or legal entities engaged in activities relevant to public safety, such as lodging, transportation of persons, commercial access to public telephone or telematic services through establishments open to the public, trade or repair of used items, rental or dismantling of motor vehicles, buying and selling of jewelry and metals, whether precious or not, objects or works of art, security locksmithing, metal waste management centers, wholesale establishments for scrap or waste products, or the sale of hazardous chemical products to individuals, shall be subject to the obligations of documentary registration and information under the terms established by the applicable provisions.”  

Thus, the text subject to this report regulates the regime of registration and communication of certain data related to individuals or legal entities that carry out the activities mentioned in its heading as well as the users of such activities, such as travelers in the case of lodging establishments or tenants and drivers in the case of vehicle rentals.  

To this end, regarding lodging activities, the obligation is established for travelers over the age of fourteen to sign an entry report, which will be registered by those who carry out such activities professionally, proceeding to store it, along with the remaining information contained in Annex I of the Project, by telematic means as well as its subsequent submission to the “competent authorities,” as provided in Article 6. This obligation of registration and communication will also be required of tour operators and digital platforms engaged in intermediation, for a fee, in these types of activities. The registration obligation is excluded in the case of those who carry out the activity on a non-professional basis, who must nonetheless communicate the data under the same terms.  

Similarly, the obligations of documentary registration and communication are established for those who carry out vehicle rental activities.

II
Having made these considerations, the study of the text under review must proceed, analyzing the existence of a legitimization for the processing of data that must be carried out by the obligated subjects, as well as compliance with the principles established in Article 5.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation in such processing. Subsequently, it will also be necessary to analyze what is provided in Article 7, since the data processing referred to therein would be subject, according to its own wording and the purposes described in the provision, to what is established in Directive 2016/680, taking into account what is indicated by the fourth transitional provision of the Organic Law Project on Data Protection currently under parliamentary processing, as it results in applicable regulations for processing, as long as the transposition of the aforementioned Directive has not occurred, the one in force prior to May 25, 2018.

Regarding the legal basis for the processing of personal data by the obligated subjects, as already indicated, the Project under review highlights its nature as a development regulation of Organic Law 4/2015. In this sense, Article 6.1 c) of the General Data Protection Regulation states that "Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met: (...) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject."

Article 6.3 adds that "The basis for the processing indicated in paragraph 1, letters c) and e), must be established by: a) Union law, or b) the law of the Member States that applies to the controller," adding that "The purpose of the processing must be determined in that legal basis (...). This legal basis may contain specific provisions to adapt the application of the rules of this Regulation, among others: the general conditions governing the lawfulness of processing by the controller; the types of data subject to processing; the affected data subjects; the entities to which data may be communicated."

For this reason, it is necessary to develop the necessary control tools over these activities.”

Thus, the purpose that would justify the imposition of the legal obligation for the processing and communication of personal data established in the Draft would be the preservation of public safety and the prevention, detection, and, where appropriate, prosecution of criminal behaviors, especially concerning the fight against terrorism and organized crime.

III
The existence of an adequate legal basis for processing does not, however, prevent the need to analyze whether the processing and communication of the data referred to in the Project adequately complies with the principles of data protection enshrined in Article 5.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation. In this regard, it should be particularly noted that Article 5.1 c) enshrines the principle of minimization in the processing of personal data, stating that the data must be “adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed.” Similarly, Article 5.1 e) enshrines the principle of limitation of the retention period of the data, establishing that the data will be “kept in a manner that allows the identification of the interested parties for no longer than is necessary for the purposes of processing the personal data.”
Regarding this latter principle, the Project establishes a retention period of three years, which is similar to that previously established for accommodation establishments. Thus, taking into account the legality of the processing already analyzed with the aforementioned retention periods, it can be understood that the processing would respect the principle of retention.

IV

As a first consideration, it should be noted that the Project provides for the communication of data to the "competent authorities," the scope of which is unclear, given that Article 7 seems to limit this condition to the Secretary of State for Security. In this regard, it is not possible to link the concept of "competent authorities" with that established in Directive (EU) 2016/680, as it has not yet been transposed and would also include judicial bodies and the Public Prosecutor's Office, according to its Article 3.7. Thus, Article 7.1 does not seem to consider these latter bodies as recipients of the data but rather as authorized to access the data previously provided to the "competent authorities."

Therefore, it would be necessary to clarify in Article 6 of the Project who the recipients of the communications established therein are, delimiting whether reference is being made to the Ministry of the Interior, as seems to be inferred from Article 7.1, to the Security Forces and Corps, which are considered authorized for access but do not appear to be regarded as "competent authorities" for the purposes of receiving the information, or whether this concept also includes judicial bodies and the Public Prosecutor's Office.

In this same sense, taking into account the nature and purposes of the processing and the specific reference that Article 7.3 of the Project makes to the development regulations of Directive (EU) 2016/680, there are no apparent reasons that would justify access to the data, in general, by the Ombudsman, given that he cannot be considered a "competent authority" for the purposes provided in Article 3.7 of the aforementioned Directive.

On the other hand, Article 7.2 indicates that an essential objective of the creation of these files is "the possibility of interconnection with other police databases in order to improve effectiveness in the prevention and investigation of terrorism-related crimes or those related to serious organized crime." In this regard, it must be taken into account that it will be necessary to have the proper authorization for the aforementioned interconnection to take place within the scope of a specific investigation, without the possibility of establishing a general data interconnection system that does not comply with the data protection principles enshrined in Article 4 of Organic Law 15/1999, applicable to the processing subject to Directive (EU) 2016/680 until a transposition norm of the same into Spanish law is adopted, as well as in Article 4 of the Directive itself.

Finally, as already anticipated, it would be necessary to amend paragraph 3 of Article 7, given that at present there is no specific norm for "the protection of personal data by the competent authorities for the purposes of preventing, investigating, detecting, or prosecuting criminal offenses or enforcing criminal sanctions," since the aforementioned transposition of Directive (EU) 2016/680 has not taken place. 

For this reason, and given that in relation to the processing subject to the Directive there has been no displacement of internal law norms as a result of the full application of the General Data Protection Regulation, it must be considered that what is established in Organic Law 15/1999 remains fully in force until that transposition is carried out, as the fourth transitional provision of the Organic Law Project on Data Protection reminds us.
