Legal Office

The inquiry is based on what this Agency indicated in its Report 11/2019 (N/Ref. 2995/2019) and requests to know the opinion of this Legal Office regarding whether the designation of more than one data protection officer is mandatory within the Ministry of Defense, taking into account the existence, within its structure, of different educational and health centers, to which Article 34 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights, would refer, considering that the requester believes that the designation of a specific data protection officer in the area of the General Health Inspection, with competence in the two military hospitals, in the ten forensic examination units, and in the Institute of Preventive Medicine of Defense, in addition to the current one who would continue to have competencies in the rest of the Ministry of Defense, could be sufficient.  

I  
As this Agency has repeatedly pointed out and is stated in the Preamble of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), “the greatest novelty presented by Regulation (EU) 2016/679 is the evolution from a model fundamentally based on compliance control to one that rests on the principle of active responsibility, which requires a prior assessment by the controller or processor of the risk that the processing of personal data may generate, in order to adopt the appropriate measures based on that assessment.”  

A fundamental role within this new model of active responsibility established in the General Data Protection Regulation will be played by the data protection officer (DPO), which the General Regulation governs in Articles 37 to 39. In particular, Article 37.1 a) imposes the mandatory designation of a DPO in cases where “the processing is carried out by a public authority or body, except for courts acting in their judicial capacity,” a circumstance that applies to the Ministry of Defense.  

However, Article 37.1 of the GDPR also contemplates other cases in which the appointment of a DPO must proceed, considering the type of activities carried out by the controller (or processor):  
b) the main activities of the controller or processor consist of processing operations that, by their nature, scope, and/or purposes, require regular and systematic monitoring of data subjects on a large scale, or  
c) the main activities of the controller or processor consist of large-scale processing of special categories of personal data under Article 9 and data related to criminal convictions and offenses referred to in Article 10.  

This provision is complemented by what is established in Article 34.1 of the LOPDGDD, which specifies certain cases in which the designation of a DPO is mandatory, among which, as indicated in the inquiry, are “educational centers that offer teachings at any of the levels established in the legislation regulating the right to education, as well as public and private universities” and “health centers legally required to maintain patients' medical records.”  

Therefore, the obligation to designate a DPO may arise from the public nature of the controller or from the characteristics of the processing activities, presenting, in this case, the uniqueness of both circumstances occurring in the consulting entity, as it is a Public Administration that carries out processing activities that, when considered in themselves, would require the mandatory designation of a DPO. This is the case, for example, with the hospitals integrated into the military health network, whose main activity is to provide healthcare, which requires the large-scale processing of data related to the health of their patients collected in medical records, making the designation of a DPO mandatory according to the aforementioned Article 34.1 l) of the LOPDGDD, or in the case of the different educational centers integrated into the structure of the Armed Forces, to which Article 34.1 b) of the same would apply.  

In this regard, in Report 11/2019 cited in the inquiry, a similar, although not identical, issue was raised to that which is the subject of this report, as it concerned a case in which a single DPO had been designated for all the bodies of the same regional Public Administration, and it was concluded that “Article 34 of the LOPDGD establishes the specific appointment of DPOs in the case of professional colleges and their general councils, of educational centers that offer teachings at any of the levels established in the legislation regulating the right to education, as well as public and private universities, and of health centers legally required to maintain patients' medical records.”

II
For the proper resolution of the issue raised, it must be based on the important functions that Article 39.1 of the GDPR assigns to the DPO:
1. The data protection officer shall have at least the following functions:
   a) to inform and advise the controller or the processor and the employees involved in the processing of the obligations that they have under this Regulation and other data protection provisions of the Union or of the Member States;
   b) to oversee compliance with the provisions of this Regulation, other data protection provisions of the Union or of the Member States, and the policies of the controller or the processor regarding the protection of personal data, including the allocation of responsibilities, awareness-raising and training of staff involved in processing operations, and the corresponding audits;
   c) to provide advice requested regarding the data protection impact assessment and to monitor its application in accordance with Article 35;
   d) to cooperate with the supervisory authority;
   e) to act as a contact point for the supervisory authority on issues related to processing, including the prior consultation referred to in Article 36, and to consult, where appropriate, on any other matter.

These are, therefore, advisory and supervisory functions aimed at ensuring the proper compliance with the regulations on personal data protection, indicating that Article 39.2 states that “The data protection officer shall perform their tasks with due regard to the risks associated with processing operations, taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing.” Furthermore, Article 38.1 clearly establishes that “The controller and the processor shall ensure that the data protection officer is involved properly and in a timely manner in all issues related to the protection of personal data.”

In addition to the important advisory functions assigned to the DPD, including cases where it is necessary to conduct an impact assessment due to high-risk processing, and specifying the supervisory functions, Article 36 of the LOPDGDD provides that “The delegate may inspect the procedures related to the subject of this organic law and issue recommendations within the scope of their competencies,” that “In the exercise of their functions, the data protection delegate shall have access to personal data and processing processes, and the data controller or processor may not oppose this access by citing any duty of confidentiality or secrecy, including that provided for in Article 5 of this organic law,” and that “When the data protection delegate perceives the existence of a relevant breach in data protection, they shall document it and communicate it immediately to the management and administration bodies of the data controller or processor.”

Furthermore, regarding relationships with this Agency, it should be noted that it is the responsibility of the DPD, in accordance with Article 39.1.d) of the GDPR, to cooperate with the supervisory authority, with particular emphasis on the regulation that Article 37 of the LOPDGDD establishes regarding the intervention of the data protection delegate in the event of a complaint to the data protection authorities:

1. When the data controller or processor has designated a data protection delegate, the affected party may, prior to submitting a complaint against them to the Spanish Data Protection Agency or, where applicable, to the regional data protection authorities, address the data protection delegate of the entity against which the complaint is made. In this case, the data protection delegate shall communicate to the affected party the decision that has been made within a maximum period of two months from the receipt of the complaint.

2. When the affected party submits a complaint to the Spanish Data Protection Agency or, where applicable, to the regional data protection authorities, those authorities may forward the complaint to the data protection delegate so that they may respond within one month. If, after this period, the data protection delegate has not communicated to the competent data protection authority the response given to the complaint, that authority shall continue the procedure in accordance with the provisions established in Title VIII of this organic law and its implementing regulations.

3. The procedure before the Spanish Data Protection Agency shall be that established in Title VIII of this organic law and its implementing regulations. Likewise, the autonomous communities shall regulate the corresponding procedure before their regional data protection authorities.

On the other hand, Article 39.1.e) of the GDPR also establishes as functions of the DPO “to act as a contact point for the supervisory authority on issues related to processing, including the prior consultation referred to in Article 36, and to carry out consultations, where appropriate, on any other matter.” Precisely, in light of the new model established in the GDPR and the functions assigned to the DPO regarding advising the controller and conducting consultations with the AEPD, it is a reiterated criterion of this Legal Office that “if the data controller has doubts about the legal basis that may determine the lawfulness of a particular processing, they must consult their data protection officer in cases where, as in the present case, their appointment is mandatory, who must provide the necessary advice. Only in the event that the data protection officer has legal doubts about the matter submitted for their consideration that cannot be resolved with the criteria already informed by the AEPD or due to being new issues arising from the application of the new legal framework for the protection of personal data and that have a general scope in which a report that contributes to legal certainty is advisable, may the said officer elevate a consultation to this Legal Office, accompanying such consultation with their own report in which the issues subject to consultation are analyzed in detail and with justification.”
For the proper fulfillment of these duties, the GDPR requires certain training requirements for the DPO, and that they be provided with the necessary resources.
Regarding the training requirements, Article 37.5 states that “The data protection officer shall be appointed based on their professional qualities and, in particular, their specialized knowledge of law and practice in the field of data protection and their ability to perform the functions indicated in Article 39.”
For its part, Article 35 of the LOPDGDD adds that “Compliance with the requirements established in Article 37.5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 for the appointment of the data protection officer, whether a natural or legal person, may be demonstrated, among other means, through voluntary certification mechanisms that will particularly take into account the obtaining of a university degree that certifies specialized knowledge in law and practice in the field of data protection.”

The required level of knowledge is not strictly defined but must be commensurate with the sensitivity, complexity, and volume of the data that an organization processes. For example, when the data processing activity is particularly complex or involves a large amount of sensitive data, the DPO may need a higher level of knowledge and support. There is also a difference depending on whether the organization systematically transfers personal data outside the European Union or if such transfers are occasional. Thus, the DPO must be chosen carefully, duly considering the data protection issues that arise within the organization. 
 Professional qualities 
Although Article 37, paragraph 5, does not specify the professional qualities that should be taken into account when appointing the DPO, an important factor is that they have knowledge of national and European legislation and practices in the field of data protection and a deep understanding of the GDPR. It is also useful for supervisory authorities to promote adequate and periodic training for DPOs. 
Knowledge of the business sector and the organization of the data controller is also beneficial. Likewise, the DPO must have a good understanding of the processing operations carried out, as well as the information systems and the security and data protection needs of the data controller. 
In the case of a public authority or body, the DPO must also possess a solid knowledge of the rules and administrative procedures of the organization. 
 Ability to perform their functions 
The ability of the DPO to perform their functions should be interpreted both in reference to their personal qualities and knowledge as well as to their position within the organization. Personal qualities should include, for example, integrity and a high level of professional ethics; the primary concern of the DPO should be to enable compliance with the GDPR. The DPO plays a fundamental role in promoting a culture of data protection within the organization and contributes to the implementation of essential elements of the GDPR, such as the principles related to data processing, the rights of data subjects, data protection by design and by default, the record of processing activities, the

On the other hand, the need to provide the DPO with the necessary resources for the performance of their functions is established as an obligation of the controller in Article 38.2 of the GDPR: “The controller and the processor shall support the data protection officer in the performance of the tasks mentioned in Article 39, providing the necessary resources for the performance of those tasks and access to personal data and processing operations, and for the maintenance of their specialized knowledge.”

In this regard, the Guidelines of the Article 29 Working Party state the following:
Article 38, paragraph 2, of the GDPR provides that the organization shall support its DPO “by providing the necessary resources for the performance of [their] tasks and access to personal data and processing operations, and for the maintenance of their specialized knowledge.” 

The following aspects should be particularly taken into account:
1. Active support for the work of the DPO by senior management (at the board level).
2. Sufficient time for the DPO to fulfill their functions, which is particularly important when a part-time internal DPO is appointed or when the external DPO carries out data protection in addition to other obligations. Otherwise, the conflict between priorities could lead to neglect of the DPO's obligations. It is essential to have enough time dedicated to the tasks of the DPO. It is advisable to establish a percentage of time for the DPO's own work when it is not carried out full-time. It is also advisable to determine the time necessary to perform the work, the appropriate level of priority for the DPO's functions, and for the DPO (or the organization) to draft a work plan.
3. Adequate support in terms of financial resources, infrastructure (premises, facilities, equipment), and personnel, as required.
4. Official communication of the DPO's appointment to all staff to ensure that their existence and function are known within the organization.
5. Necessary access to other services, such as human resources, legal department, IT, security, etc., so that DPOs can receive essential support, contributions, and information from these services.
6. Continuous training. DPOs should be given the opportunity to stay up to date with developments in the field of data protection. The aim should be to constantly improve the level of knowledge of DPOs, and they should be encouraged to participate in data protection training courses and other forms of professional development, such as participation in private forums, workshops, etc.

 Depending on the size and structure of the organization, it may be necessary to establish a DPO team (a DPO and their staff). In such cases, the internal structure of the team and the tasks and responsibilities of each of its members must be clearly defined. Similarly, when the DPO function is performed by an external service provider, a group of individuals working for that entity may effectively carry out the DPO functions as a team, under the responsibility of a designated primary contact for the client. In general, the more complex or sensitive the processing operations are, the more resources should be allocated to the DPO. The data protection function must be carried out effectively and provided with sufficient resources for the processing being conducted.
III
Once the significance of the functions assigned to the DPO by the GDPR and the training and resource requirements that must be provided to them have been analyzed, it is appropriate to examine the issue raised in the consultation, which focuses on the need to appoint more than one DPO within the Ministry of Defense. To do this, one must start from what is stated in Article 37.3 of the GDPR, which states that “When the controller or processor is a public authority or body, a single data protection officer may be designated for several of these authorities or bodies, taking into account their organizational structure and size.” This is, therefore, the same rule provided in the previous section of the provision for private entities, stating that “A corporate group may appoint a single data protection officer, provided that they are easily accessible from each establishment.”
This issue is analyzed in the Guidelines on Data Protection Officers of the Article 29 Working Party, whose section 2.3 refers to the appointment of a single DPO for multiple organizations:
2.3. Appointment of a single DPO for multiple organizations.
 Article 37, paragraph 2, allows a corporate group to appoint a single DPO, provided that they “are easily accessible from each establishment.” The notion of accessibility refers to the DPO's tasks as a point of contact with respect to data subjects and the supervisory authority, but also internally within the organization, considering that one of those tasks is “to inform and advise the controller or processor and the employees who are involved in processing of their obligations under this Regulation.”

In order to ensure that the DPO, whether internal or external, is accessible, it is important to ensure that their contact details are available in accordance with the requirements of the GDPR. The DPO, with the help of a team if necessary, must be able to communicate effectively with the stakeholders and cooperate with the relevant supervisory authorities. This also means that such communication must take place in the language or languages used by the supervisory authorities and the affected stakeholders. The availability of a DPO (whether physically in the same facilities as an employee, online, or through other secure means of communication) is essential to ensure that stakeholders can contact the DPO.

In accordance with Article 37, paragraph 3, a single DPO may be designated for several authorities or public bodies, taking into account their organizational structure and size. The same considerations apply regarding resources and communications. Since the DPO is responsible for a variety of tasks, the controller or processor must ensure that a single DPO, with the help of a team if necessary, can carry out these tasks effectively despite being designated by several authorities and public bodies.

Therefore, what is essential is not the number of DPOs, nor even that they are part of the organization of the controller (paragraph 6 of Article 37 provides that "the data protection officer may be part of the staff of the controller or the processor or perform their functions under a service contract") but rather that they meet the training and independence requirements that allow them to adequately perform the functions assigned to them by the GDPR, taking into account that, as noted in Recital 97 of the GDPR, "The level of specialized knowledge required should be determined, in particular, by reference to the data processing operations carried out and the protection required for the personal data processed by the controller or processor."

Therefore, since the designation of a DPO in a Public Administration is an eminently organizational matter, in the terms indicated in the aforementioned Report 11/2019, and as long as their independence is adequately guaranteed, what is relevant is that the functions assigned to the DPO can be carried out effectively, also taking into account the criterion of availability, which is fundamental to ensure that stakeholders can easily contact the DPO (according to Article 38.4 of the GDPR, "the stakeholders may contact the data protection officer regarding all issues related to...").

Notwithstanding the above, the report itself states that, according to the General Health Inspection, "the Ministry of Defense is legally obligated to maintain clinical records at two hospital centers, one in Madrid and one in Zaragoza, and 10 expert assessment units that would be included within the concept: 'medical recognition centers (healthcare providers without hospitalization)' which provide the technical, expert, and administrative support required by the Expert Boards to issue their reports. Additionally, the Institute of Preventive Medicine of Defense safeguards clinical documentation related to the vaccination history of Armed Forces personnel. All of them are under the organic dependency of the General Health Inspection." Hence, the DPD of the Ministry of Defense suggests that "Notwithstanding the above, it is also true that due to the nature and scope of the operations involving the processing of special categories of personal data, which are primarily carried out in the two military hospitals and to a lesser extent in the other healthcare centers, it may advise the existence of a specific Data Protection Officer in the field of healthcare centers, but not a specific one for each of them, rather a single DPD in the field of the General Health Inspection of Defense, responsible for the processing of such data within the Ministry of Defense, given the functional and organic dependency, as well as the functions attributed to it, in the Royal Decree establishing the structure of this department."
This Agency shares the Ministry of Defense's view, considering the special nature of the data being processed (health data, included within the special categories of data according to Article 9 of the GDPR), the number of processing activities and affected individuals, especially in the case of military hospitals, which provide specialized healthcare services to users of the Public Health System under an agreement, the inherent risk of such processing that requires conducting an impact assessment, the applicable special regulations, as occurs, for example, regarding access to clinical records, the high number of claims raised in this area, etc.
Therefore, it is deemed appropriate to designate a specific DPD in this area, corresponding to the responsible party to assess whether a single DPD is sufficient for all bodies integrated into the General Health Inspection or, especially in the case of hospitals, a DPD per center, considering the previously mentioned requirements for adequacy of resources.
Regarding the maintenance of a single DPD with responsibilities in the rest of the Ministry of Defense, including the numerous military educational centers that depend on it, this is a decision of the responsible party that this Agency considers acceptable, provided that the requirements for training, adequacy of resources, availability, etc., established by the GDPR are met, and that it is ensured that the DPD participates adequately and in a timely manner in all matters related to the protection of personal data. For these purposes, it is appropriate to seek the advice of the DPD itself, as has been done by the Ministry of Defense, with its opinion being favorable to this possibility.
IV
In light of what has been indicated in this report, and aside from what has been requested in this consultation, this Agency must emphasize once again the importance of the DPD figure in the new model established by the GDPR, which pivots on the basis of the proactive responsibility of the responsible party. According to this, in cases where it is mandatory or deemed appropriate on a voluntary basis, it is the responsibility of the responsible party to assess the appropriateness of designating one or more DPDs, as well as whether the DPD should belong to its own structure, ensuring at all times their independence and availability. Likewise, it must ensure that the DPD meets the appropriate training requirements and is provided with the necessary personal and material resources for the effective performance of the functions assigned to them, that they participate adequately and in a timely manner in all matters related to the protection of personal data, and that they report to the highest hierarchical level, adequately documenting, according to the aforementioned principle of proactive responsibility, all decisions made in this regard, to be able to demonstrate it at the request of the supervisory authorities.
In this way, it will be guaranteed that the appointment of the DPD has not been made merely formally and that they effectively fulfill the functions assigned to them by the GDPR, being the first interested party in such effectiveness the responsible party itself, who will be the one to respond, and not the DPD, in case of non-compliance with the GDPR.
