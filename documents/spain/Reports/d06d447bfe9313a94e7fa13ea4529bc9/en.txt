Before proceeding to analyze the text submitted for report, it is necessary to point out that, given the legal basis of the report that will be issued immediately and its mandatory nature, in accordance with the provisions of the aforementioned regulations, it should be indicated in the Exposition of Motives of the regulation that it has been submitted to the prior report of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.

I  
The project submitted aims to develop Law 13/2011, of May 27, on the regulation of gambling, regarding:

a) The conditions under which entities holding enabling titles to carry out gambling activities included within the scope of Law 13/2011, of May 27, may carry out activities of advertising, sponsorship, promotion, or any other form of commercial communication of their activity.  
b) The conditions under which responsible gambling policies and consumer protection policies must be developed by entities holding enabling titles to carry out gambling activities included within the scope of Law 13/2011, of May 27.

A previous version of this draft Royal Decree was reported by this Agency on April 27, 2018. That report already took into account Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation, GDPR), as it is the regulation presumably applicable at the time of its final approval. Likewise, the text of the Draft Organic Law on the Protection of Personal Data, then under processing in the General Courts, was also taken into consideration.

The observations made in the Report of April 27, 2018 have been fully incorporated into the new text that is now being reported.

However, a clarification must be made regarding the observation related to the regulation of commercial communications via email or other equivalent means, contained in section 1 of the current article 24 of the project. In this regard, this Agency pointed out that “it should be noted that the sending of commercial communications through electronic means would only be possible with the consent of the interested party, as prescribed by article 21 of Law 34/2002 and reiterated by article 7.2.a) of Law 13/2011.”

In light of this observation, the aforementioned article 24.1 of the project states the following:

“1. The sending of commercial communications via email or other equivalent electronic communication means may only be carried out in accordance with the provisions of article 21 of Law 34/2002, of July 11, on Information Society Services and Electronic Commerce.”

However, this generic reference to article 21 of Law 34/2002 does not correspond with what this Agency has stated nor with what is provided in article 7.2.a) of Law 13/2011, which requires, in any case, the consent of the interested party:

“a) The sending of advertising or promotional communications by email or by any other equivalent electronic communication means, which will only be possible if it has been previously authorized by its recipient, in accordance with the provisions of the first section of article 21 of Law 34/2002, of July 11, on Information Society Services and Electronic Commerce.”

The express reference to the consent of the interested party, and the reference to the first section of article 21 of Law 34/2002 is important, as the second section of the same article establishes an exception to the principle of the consent of the affected party:

“2. The provisions of the previous section shall not apply when there is a prior contractual relationship, provided that the provider has lawfully obtained the contact details of the recipient and uses them to send commercial communications regarding products or services of its own company that are similar to those initially contracted with the customer.”

This exception is not applicable under the provisions of article 7.2.a) of Law 13/2011, previously transcribed, which requires, in any case, prior authorization from the recipient, in accordance with the provisions of the first section of article 21 of Law 34/2002.

Therefore, the wording of section 1 of article 24 must be modified, proposing the following text: 
 
 
“According to the provisions of article 7.2.a) of Law 13/2011, of May 27, the sending of advertising or promotional communications by email or by any other equivalent means of electronic communication shall only be possible if it has been previously authorized by the recipient, in accordance with the provisions of the first paragraph of article 21 of Law 34/2002, of July 11, on Information Society Services and Electronic Commerce.” 
 
II 
 
On the other hand, the new text submitted provides for a broader and more detailed regulation of commercial communications related to gaming activities, due, among other reasons, to the need to ensure an adequate level of protection for the most vulnerable groups. In this regard, the Explanatory Memorandum states the following: 
 
“Thirdly, it is evident that there is a growing and sustained social sensitivity generated by the notable increase in advertising investment in state-level gaming activities in recent years and the consequent proliferation of commercial communications associated with these types of activities. Likewise, social demand requires the implementation of preventive measures, awareness-raising, control, and intervention regarding the serious consequences that gambling and betting consumption may entail for certain individuals. 
This social demand, fully shared by the majority of the parliamentary spectrum, aims to achieve an adequate level of protection for the most vulnerable groups such as minors, young people, and individuals who may be experiencing a gambling problem. 
Therefore, from the state sphere, it is considered a priority to reinforce the enforceability and material scope of the currently applicable framework to the advertising activities of all state-level gaming operators and to the actions regarding responsible gaming carried out by these entities.” 
 
 
Considering the new developments introduced in the new text submitted, and regarding its impact on the processing of personal data, it should be emphasized what is stated in section 2 of article 24 regarding commercial communications via email or other equivalent means:

“2. Gaming operators that market games whose participation is subject to prior user registration may not send any type of commercial communication via email or other equivalent electronic communication means directed to: 
a) Registered users whose registration in the General Registry of Access Bans to Gaming is made known to the operator by the authority responsible for gaming regulation. 
b) Users who have exercised their right to self-exclusion. 
c) Users who have been classified as at-risk players by the operator, in accordance with the systems and protocols for detecting risk behaviors provided for in article 34. 
The prohibitions set forth in this section will take effect from 00:00 hours on the second day after the day on which the registration in the General Registry of Access Bans to Gaming, the self-exclusion, or the classification of a participant as a player with risk behavior becomes effective.” 
 
This obligation of the operators is in addition to the one already provided for, in general terms, in article 16, regarding consultation with advertising exclusion systems, which has been introduced at the proposal of this Agency: 
 
“Article 16. Advertising Exclusion Systems. 
Prior to carrying out commercial communications directed to a specific user, whether through information society services or through means other than such services, it must be verified whether the user is registered in the advertising exclusion systems in order not to carry out the commercial communication, unless the specific operator has the explicit consent of the recipient for sending it.” 
  
 
From the new wording contained in section 2 of article 24, the introduction of a new prohibition on sending commercial communications stands out, as contemplated in letter c) regarding users who have been classified as at-risk players by the operator, in accordance with the systems and protocols for detecting risk behaviors provided for in article 34. 
 
Regarding the processing of personal data under the systems and protocols for detecting risk behaviors, the Report of April 27, 2018 stated the following: 
 
“As a starting point, it should be noted that article 8.1 of Law 13/2011 provides that ‘gaming operators must develop a plan of measures regarding the mitigation of the possible harmful effects that gaming may produce on individuals and incorporate the basic rules...

of responsible gaming policy”, including in its section a) as one of its objectives, with regard to the protection of consumers, “to pay due attention to at-risk groups.” 
This means that the necessary treatments for compliance with the provisions established in the aforementioned precept, which must be carried out by gaming operators, are covered by the provisions of Article 6.1 c) of the General Data Protection Regulation, which establishes that the processing of personal data will be lawful if “it is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject.” 

Therefore, the processing of personal data for the aforementioned purposes finds its legal basis in the provisions of Article 8.1 of Law 13/2011, having incorporated in the current text the observation indicated by this Agency, to prevent such processing from being used for different purposes, by adding a final paragraph to section 1 of Article 34 of the following tenor: 

“The processing of personal data of players resulting from the application of the mechanisms and protocols provided for in this section shall only aim at the detection of players engaging in risky behavior and the implementation of the measures contained in this precept.”
