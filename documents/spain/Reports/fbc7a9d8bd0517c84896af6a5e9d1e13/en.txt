The consultation raises the question of what the status should be, as responsible party or data processor, in accordance with Regulation 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (GDPR) and the Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), concerning the participants in the management of the business related to Collective Investment Institutions (CIIs hereinafter).  

Specifically, it highlights the complex system of relationships and structures that, in practice, occur in the management of the CII business, indicating, in summary, the following:  

The CIIs are regulated by Law 35/2003, of November 4, on Collective Investment Institutions (LIIC hereinafter) and aim to raise funds, assets, or rights from the public to manage and invest them in assets, rights, securities, or other instruments, whether financial or not, provided that the return for the investor is established based on collective results (Article 1 LIIC), that is to say, as indicated in the Explanatory Memorandum of the law, they channel the participation of Spanish households in the capital markets.  

They are constituted through an agreement between the Management Company of the Institution (SGIIC hereinafter) that is responsible for management and representation, exercising dominion powers without being the owner of the fund, and ultimately making all decisions regarding the common assets (Article 40 LIIC), and the entity that is considered the Depository, which is mainly responsible for safeguarding the assets, as well as overseeing the management carried out by the SGIIC (Article 57 LIIC).  

Once constituted, the CIIs receive contributions from investors who become participants or co-owners of the common assets, managed by the SGIIC and safeguarded by the depository. This management carried out by the SGIIC involves the processing of personal data of the natural persons who are participants in the CII.  

The SGIIC carries out marketing actions to encourage investment in the CII, either directly or indirectly through third parties.  

In the first case, the investor is approached using its personnel or through agents. The latter assume the role of data processor, as service providers to the SGIIC, representing it in the marketing of the CII.  

As for indirect marketing, it is carried out through entities authorized to provide intermediation services, referred to as “receipt and transmission of orders” (RTO). These entities can be both credit institutions and investment service companies (ESI hereinafter), which are mainly regulated in the consolidated text of the Securities Market Law (LMV hereinafter) and in Royal Decree 217/2008, of February 15. Credit institutions can be considered as such when they provide the service of receipt and transmission of orders.  

Likewise, the SGIIC contracts with distribution platforms for distribution services. This will be a distribution and intermediation contract for the subscription and redemption of shares and/or participations of the CIIs.  

In these contracts with the distribution platform, the authority to transmit subscription and redemption orders for fund participations is granted, and additionally, it is allowed for the platform to enter into sub-distribution and intermediation agreements (with the ESIs) for the provision of investment services.  

The following aspects should be highlighted from the contract between the SGIIC and the platform:  
- Object of the contract: the SGIIC designates the platform as distributor and/or intermediary of the participations of the CIIs. The SGIIC authorizes the platform to distribute and/or act as an intermediary in the subscription of the participations of the CII, acting on behalf and for the account of its clients.  
- Remuneration: the platform will receive remuneration from the SGIIC in consideration for its mediation work in the subscription of participations of the CIIs.  
- Operation:  
  ▪ The SGIIC allows the platform to provide services directly or through the formalization of sub-distribution contracts with other authorized entities to act as intermediaries in the placement of shares and participations (ESI).

The ESI transmits the orders of its clients to the platform, without directly approaching the SGIIC. The platform channels the order to the IIC and facilitates communication with the SGIIC for executing the investment, receiving confirmation of the investment and, normally, all necessary information related to the client's participation.
In practice, most SGIICs have contracted distribution services with distribution platforms, and therefore, the ESI must use these platforms to provide their services. The platforms manage the vast majority of data traffic related to investment orders. This activity of the platforms is regulated by two contracts: the distribution contract with the SGIIC and the sub-distribution contract with the ESI.
The platform provides services to the ESI in the development of the activity that the ESI contracts with its clients, and also to the SGIIC, which is clearly the final responsible party for the data, for which it is entrusted with the marketing, processing the data of the investors to facilitate both the investment or divestment in the name of the investors.
Except for those exceptional cases, the platform has no direct relationship with the participant nor any decision-making capacity regarding the investment; it only channels the order, always acting under the criteria of another entity (the SGIIC or the ESI).

I
Taking the above into account, the inquiry focuses on how to configure the contractual relationship between the SGIIC and the distribution platform from the perspective of data protection, and the consideration that the ESI must have.
Three questions arise: Are the distribution platforms processors of the SGIIC? Can an entity be a processor for two different controllers pursuing complementary purposes with the same objective, regarding the same personal data? Is it necessary to qualify the distribution platform as a processor for each controller that converges and therefore, regulate in each service contract the obligations of Article 28 GDPR?
II

(…)

It also refers -in paragraph 8 of its article 4- to the data processor or processor as “the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that processes personal data on behalf of the data controller.” 
For its part, the LOPDGDD refers in its article 33 paragraphs 1 and 2 to the data processor, providing that:
“Article 33. Data Processor.
1. Access by a data processor to personal data that is necessary for the provision of a service to the controller shall not be considered a communication of data as long as the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, this organic law, and its implementing regulations are complied with.
2. A person acting in their own name and without it being established that they are acting on behalf of another, who establishes relationships with the data subjects, shall be considered a data controller and not a processor, even if there is a contract or legal act with the content set forth in article 28.3 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679. This provision shall not apply to data processing assignments made within the framework of public sector procurement legislation. 
A person who, while acting as a processor, uses the data for their own purposes shall also be considered a data controller.
In general, the role of the data processor responds to the need to address phenomena such as the outsourcing of services by companies and other entities, so that in those cases where the data controller entrusts a third party with the provision of a service that requires access to personal data by this third party, such access cannot be considered a transfer of data by the controller to the service provider, but rather the processing of the data is carried out by the processor on behalf of and for the account of the controller as if the controller were carrying it out themselves.
However, in practice, more complex situations may arise depending on the different functions of the actors and the processing itself, as occurs in the present consultation, and it is necessary to refer to the interpretative criteria established by the European Data Protection Committee (EDPC hereinafter) in Guidelines 7/2020 of September 2, 2020 “On the concepts of controller and processor in the GDPR,” from which the following sections should be highlighted:
12. The concepts of controller and processor are functional concepts: their purpose is to assign responsibilities according to the actual functions of the parties. This implies that the legal status of an actor as a “controller” or “processor” must be determined in principle by their actual activities in a specific situation, and not by the formal designation of an actor as a “controller” or “processor” (for example, in a contract).
21 (…) In most situations, the “determining body” can be easily and clearly identified by reference to certain legal or factual circumstances from which “influence” can normally be inferred, unless other elements indicate otherwise. Two categories of situations can be distinguished: 1) control derived from legal provisions; and (2) control derived from factual influence. (…)
22 (…) There are cases where control can be deduced from an explicit legal competence, for example, when the controller or the specific criteria for their designation are designated by national or Union law (…) the legislator has designated as the controller the entity that has a genuine capacity to exercise control.
23 The law will establish a task or impose on someone the obligation to collect and process certain data. In those cases, the purpose of the processing is usually determined by law. The controller will normally be the one designated by law to carry out this purpose, this public task (…) More generally, the law may also impose on public or private entities the obligation to retain or provide certain data. These entities would normally be considered controllers with respect to the processing necessary to fulfill this obligation.
25. The need for a factual assessment also means that the role of a data controller does not derive from the nature of an entity that is processing data, but from its specific activities in a specific context. In other words, the same entity may act simultaneously as the controller for certain processing operations and as the processor for others, and the qualification as controller or processor must be evaluated with respect to each specific data processing activity.

In accordance with the above and in light of the relationships evident in the inquiry, the SGIIC in marketing may hire mediating entities, which are the distribution platforms.
Indeed, through the distribution contract, the platforms channel the orders of clients who wish to be part of the Collective Investment Institution (IIC), which they receive from the marketing entities with which the SGIIC has reached the corresponding agreements, such as ESIs.
Therefore, the first conclusion reached is that the SGIIC will be considered responsible for the processing, and the mediating entity, the distribution platform, will act as the data processor, since the distribution service involves the processing of personal data and is carried out on behalf of the former. The distribution platform cannot make decisions about the processing but only receives instructions from the SGIIC in the sense that it is the SGIIC that has determined the why and for what purpose of the processing (the purpose of the processing).
That is to say, the processing of data is a consequence of the marketing activity that the SGIIC carries out, for which it uses, among others, the services of the distribution platform.
The SGIIC determines the how and why of the processing, and the platform carries out actions that must be considered processing of personal data in accordance with the definition in Article 4.2 of the GDPR, which considers as such: any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or modification, extraction, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination, or any other form of enabling access, comparison, or interconnection, limitation, deletion, or destruction;

Investment service companies are those companies whose main activity consists of providing investment services, on a professional basis, to third parties regarding the financial instruments indicated in Article 2.

In relation to the factual elements to be analyzed, the consultation states the following regarding the ESI:
“The ESI, in turn, promotes investment in the IIC among its clients, without assuming any type of representation of the SGIIC, that is, they operate in the reception and transmission of investment orders, on behalf of their client, the new investor who is interested in investing in the IIC that the ESI has promoted. 
The service provided by the ESI can mainly be summarized as prior investment advice, through personalized recommendations, and secondly, once a decision has been made by the potential investor, the ESI will provide the service of ‘reception and transmission of orders’ (RTO) for the execution by the SGIIC of the subscriptions and redemptions of the fund shares. However, it may also happen that the prior step of advising is not taken, and the potential investor directly instructs the ESI to process the investment order. 
For this service, the ESI is obliged by current regulations to collect personal information from the investors who are its clients: identifying information of the client, tax data, information about their financial knowledge and preferences. 
In short, the ESI provides services to the investor, ‘investment services’ and maintains a contractual relationship with them.”
Taking the above into account, the ESIs will be, in the first instance, responsible for the processing of the clients to whom they provide the investment service, which may or may not include advice, and in any case, the execution of the investment order. 
And regarding the distribution platform and its relationship with the ESI, it should be considered as the data processor of the ESI, since derived from the contractual relationship that binds them (referred to as a sub-distribution contract), it provides a service for channeling and transmitting the investment order that involves the processing of personal data. 
That is to say, the ESI, in order to carry out the investment service, the service it provides to its clients when they want to invest in the IIC, needs the distribution platform to send the purchase order to the SGIIC. 
In this way, the distribution platform will be considered as the data processor, both of the SGIIC and of the ESI, as it is the link between both in the transmission of subscription orders and ultimately serves for processing in the benefit of both.

V
On the other hand, regarding the relationship between the SGIIC and the ESI, it cannot be established that we are dealing with a data processing assignment, but rather that both entities are two independent data controllers, since although the data they process is the same and their activity ultimately converges in the participation of clients in the IIC, they carry out two independent and successive processes, which serve the same reality but from different perspectives. 
That of the SGIIC as the “seller” of the shares and the ESI (on behalf of its clients) as the “buyer” of the shares. As indicated in the consultation, these are independent processes that pursue complementary purposes with the same objective. The SGIIC determines the how and the why, regarding the initiative to develop marketing actions, and on the other hand, the ESI also determines the how and the why, when it decides to provide the investment service to its clients. 
Both processes contribute to the same end, the acquisition of shares in the IIC, but from different perspectives. 
Moreover, the existence of a sub-distribution and intermediation contract between the platform and the ESI does not make the ESI a data processor of either the SGIIC or the distribution platform. 
The ESI does not process data on behalf of the SGIIC, nor does it follow its instructions in the service of “reception and transmission of orders,” but rather it is the ESI that, on behalf of its clients, decides whether or not to invest in the IIC. 
Nor does it establish relationships with its clients on behalf of the SGIIC, since the contractual relationship between the clients of the ESI and the ESI is a contract for investment services, both being the parties to that contract. 
It is another matter that once the investment order has been given, there is an investment contract between the client of the ESI and the SGIIC. 
For the same reasons, the ESI cannot be considered a sub-processor of the platform.

VI
Therefore, of the possibilities raised by the inquiry, the appropriate option from the perspective of data protection is to consider, on one hand, the SGIIC as the data controller of the investors' data, from the moment the subscription order is executed. From the perspective of the data subject, there will be an investment contract and therefore the SGIIC is responsible for the processing of that data.
On the other hand, the ESI or sub-distributor should be considered as the data processor responsible for the processing of the investors' data derived from the investment service. From the perspective of the data subject, there will be an investment services contract and therefore the ESI is responsible for the processing of that data.
The distribution platform should be considered as a data processor for the SGIIC and also as a data processor for the sub-distributor or the ESI. It is a separate entity from those contractually linked by a contract (distribution contract or sub-distribution contract) whose object derives from the provision of a service that involves data processing (automated procedures (...) such as collection, recording, (...), communication by transmission, dissemination, or any other form of enabling access, comparison, or interconnection), regarding which it makes no decisions but merely follows instructions from both, and which also does not establish direct relationships with the data subjects, who only have a legal relationship with the SGIIC through the investment contract, and with the ESI through the investment services contract.
