SUBJECT: Public information regarding personnel who occupy or have occupied a temporary position in the General Administration of the State and application of Article 19.3 of the LTAIBG

Law 19/2013, of December 9, on transparency, access to public information, and good governance (LTAIBG), in its Article 38.2.a) assigns to the President of the Transparency and Good Governance Council (CTBG) the function of “adopting uniform interpretative criteria for the obligations contained in this Law.”

Similarly, Royal Decree 919/2014, of October 31, which approves the Statute of the Transparency and Good Governance Council, states that “the President of the Council is the competent body to adopt uniform interpretative criteria for the obligations contained in Law 19/2013, of December 9, in the regulation that develops said Law or in the Statute.”

For its part, the fifth additional provision of the aforementioned LTAIBG establishes the following: “The Transparency and Good Governance Council and the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will jointly adopt the application criteria, within their scope of action, of the rules contained in Article 15 of this Law, particularly regarding the weighting of public interest in access to information and the guarantee of the rights of the interested parties whose data is contained therein, in accordance with the provisions of this Law and Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13.”

In virtue of this, the Presidency of the Transparency and Good Governance Council and the Directorate of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection (AEPD) jointly adopt the following JOINT INTERPRETIVE CRITERION:

1. BACKGROUND

On May 22, 2018, the Director General of Public Governance of the Ministry of Finance and Public Function sent a letter requesting a report from the Spanish Agency for Data Protection (AEPD) and the Transparency and Good Governance Council (CTBG) regarding the procedure for allegations concerning the communication of public information related to personnel who occupy or have occupied a temporary position in the General Administration of the State.

The purpose of the aforementioned request for a report is as follows:

“First: In cases of requests for information through the right of access (Chapter III of Title I LTAIBG):

1. The specific reasons that may be alleged by the affected parties to oppose the communication of their data (whether they are limited to those expressed in previous paragraphs or may be others) and that discourage the provision of the information, and how such allegations should be weighed in light of what is established in Article 15.3 LTAIBG.

2. The possibility of opposition by the interested party to access to the information in the event of appointment and termination as temporary personnel before the entry into force of Law 19/2013 of December 19.

3. The course of action in the event that in the prior hearing the affected temporary personnel do not make any allegations within the time granted for that purpose or it is not possible to contact the interested party.

4. The need to request the consent of the interested party again when a hearing procedure has been carried out on the same issue following another access request submitted in a short period of time by a different requester.

5. The possibility of formalizing the consent in such a way that it serves for future requests for access to information, and how to carry out such formalization.

Second: in the event that proactive publication of the information on the Transparency Portal of the AGE is desired (Chapter II of Title I LTAIBG):

2. LEGAL GROUNDS  

2.1.  
PRELIMINARY CONSIDERATIONS  

It should be noted, as was done in previous interpretative criteria, that these must be understood in a sufficiently flexible and generic manner in their application to the different specific cases that may arise, since the analysis of the concurrent circumstances in the case is decisive for the application of the criteria.  

Furthermore, it must be taken into account that the competence to resolve and evaluate each specific case corresponds exclusively to the competent units for processing the request or, where applicable, to the Information Units created by the LTAIBG or their equivalents, as they are the ones that have all the necessary data and elements of judgment to resolve the case.  

2.2.  
PERSONAL DATA: LEGAL REGIME AND NEW APPLICABLE REGULATIONS  

The publication of information containing personal data and access to this type of information in response to a request for information under the LTAIBG are cases of personal data processing, as defined in Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation, GDPR). Thus, its Article 4.2 establishes that "processing" is “any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure or destruction.”

Previously, Article 4.1 of the GDPR defines "personal data" as any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("the data subject"); an identifiable natural person is considered to be any person whose identity can be determined, directly or indirectly, in particular by means of an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific elements of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that person;  

On the other hand, the above is confirmed in Article 86 of the GDPR, which aims to reconcile the right to the protection of personal data with the public's access to official documents.  

“Article 86. Processing and public access to official documents  

The personal data in official documents held by any public authority or public body or a private entity for the performance of a task in the public interest may be communicated by such authority, body, or entity in accordance with Union law or the applicable laws of the Member States in order to reconcile public access to official documents with the right to the protection of personal data under this Regulation.”  

In this regard, Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), in its second additional provision—Protection of data and transparency and access to public information—provides the following:  

“Active publicity and access to public information regulated by Title I of Law 19/2013, of December 9, on transparency, access to public information, and good governance, as well as the obligations of active publicity established by regional legislation, shall be subject, when the information contains personal data, to the provisions of Articles 5.3 and 15 of Law 19/2013, in Regulation (EU) 2016/679, and in this organic law.”

2.3.  
PUBLICATION OF INFORMATION ON PUBLIC EMPLOYEES' REMUNERATIONS: CRITERIA AND APPLICABLE REGULATIONS  

In the case of remuneration data of public employees, the joint criterion CI/001/2015 prepared by the AEPD and the CTBG in June 2015 already clarified that we were faced with a situation where the balancing referred to in section 3 of art. 15 of the LTAIBG was necessary.  

This balancing was resolved by providing certain guidelines or criteria that, in essence and without the intention of being exhaustive, as they are specified in the aforementioned criterion, addressed i) the method of job provision, as well as ii) the specific position of the public employee, or of the person who has entrusted them, especially in the case of temporary personnel, in the decision-making process of the public organization in which they perform their functions.  

2.4.  
SPECIAL CONSIDERATION OF THE HEARING PROCESS  

Having noted the above, and to focus on the issue addressed in this joint interpretative criterion, the questions now raised center on the development of the hearing process provided for in art. 19.3 of the LTAIBG, which would allow for understanding the circumstances of each specific case that would enable the adaptation of the general lines provided by the 2015 criterion to specific situations.  

Art. 19.3 of the LTAIBG states the following:  

“If the requested information could affect the rights or interests of duly identified third parties, they shall be granted a period of fifteen days to make any allegations they deem appropriate. The applicant must be informed of this circumstance, as well as of the suspension of the deadline for issuing a resolution until the allegations have been received or the deadline for their submission has elapsed.”

Related to the same, section 2 of article 22 of the LTAIBG, regarding the formalization of access, states that  

“If there has been opposition from a third party, access will only take place when, having been granted such access, the period for filing a contentious-administrative appeal has elapsed without it being formalized or resolved confirming the right to receive the information.”  

From the joint reading of both provisions and the aforementioned article 15, the following can be concluded:  

When access to the requested information may affect the rights or interests of third parties—being the right to the protection of personal data one of them, but not the only possible one—the managers of information requests must proceed to open a hearing process of fifteen days for the affected third parties regarding the information being requested. This process must be carried out in the days immediately following the receipt of the information request.  

Notwithstanding the above, it should be noted that article 15.2 of the LTAIBG already provides that, in the case of merely identifying data related to the organization, functioning, or public activity of the body, the general rule is the publicity of such information, but without excluding the carrying out of the hearing process in order to be aware of the existence of circumstances or situations that should be taken into account when deciding on access.  

Such third parties must be duly identified (article 19.3 LTAIBG), in such a way that the hearing process does not become impracticable, but at the same time, the practice of it does not hinder the right of access to public information.  

The deadline for issuing the resolution is suspended during the practice of the hearing process (article 19.3, second clause, LTAIBG) until the allegations have been received or the deadline for their submission has elapsed. The suspension of the deadline for resolution must be notified to the claimant to avoid them considering that their request has been denied by silence.  

The suspension of the deadline for issuing a resolution remains in effect until the allegations from the interested third parties have been received—in the event that such allegations are received before the expiration of the deadline granted for this purpose—or until the deadline granted for the formulation of allegations has elapsed. That is to say, once the fifteen-day period indicated in article 19.3 LTAIBG has elapsed without the contacted interested parties having submitted allegations.

2.5.  
CONCLUSIONS  

Applying the previous considerations to the issues raised in the request made by the DG of Public Governance, the following is noted as conclusions:  

I. The specific reasons that may be alleged by the affected parties to oppose the communication of their data (whether they are limited to those expressed in previous paragraphs or may be others) and that advise against the provision of the information, and how such allegations should be weighed in light of what is established in art. 15.3 LTAIBG.  

As indicated in the background collected in the request for a report, the interpretative criterion CI/001/2015 outlines the circumstances that must be taken into account when conducting this weighing. However, it should be preliminarily noted that i) the criteria indicated by art. 15.3 itself must also be considered in the weighing, and ii) the weighing must be carried out, in any case, by the competent body to respond to the information request as long as it has all the necessary elements of judgment for this.  

Having established the above, and in relation to the specific case raised, it should be noted that the criterion already approved in 2015 expressly stated that attention must be paid to the possible situation of special protection of the data subject, indicating, by way of mere example, a situation of gender-based violence or terrorist threat. These do not have to be the only reasons that the interested party may allege, as the law does not limit the reasons that may be put forward. In both cases cited as examples, it should be noted that the superior good that is intended to be protected is the physical integrity of the affected party, but there may be other equally worthy of protection (such as those indicated in section d) of art. 15.3 LTAIBGH, that is, that the personal data contained in the information to be revealed affect their privacy, their security, or refer to minors). In this regard, it is not possible to determine a priori the circumstances that, when raised by the interested parties, would lead to the conclusion that the right to the protection of personal data prevails over the right to public information. This is so because, in addition to the difficulty, if not impossibility, of establishing a priori circumstances that may occur in practice, the opposite would distort the specific case call made by the norm in the application of the limits to access.

First of all, Article 8 of the LTAIBG does not contain a closed list of economic, budgetary, and statistical information that the subjects obligated by the law must make public; rather, it is a non-exhaustive list. This is confirmed by the expression “at a minimum” contained in paragraph 1 of Article 8 when referring to the information of this nature that obligated subjects must make public. Consequently, and in accordance with what is established in Article 5.1 of the LTAIBG, obligated subjects must periodically publish all information whose knowledge is “relevant” to ensure the transparency of their activity. If the obligated subject concerned considers that the remuneration information regarding temporary personnel is “relevant” for these purposes, the LTAIBG would not prevent its publication.

Moreover, taking into account what was stated by the Supreme Court in its ruling of December 16, 2019, and linking it with the joint criterion CI/001/2015 issued between both signing entities, given that temporary personnel is intrinsically linked to personnel referred to as senior officials—since, among other circumstances, they automatically cease with the termination of the position of the person to whom they provide trust or advisory functions—(Article 12.3 of the Consolidated Text of the Basic Statute of Public Employees, approved by Royal Legislative Decree 5/2015, of October 30 (TRLEBEP)), it seems reasonable to think that their transparency regime must also be connected to the transparency regime established for the senior officials who appointed them. Therefore, it is estimated that since the annual remuneration received and the compensation received, if applicable, upon leaving the position by the senior officials and top managers of the entities included within the scope of application of the LTAIBG must be made public in accordance with Article 8.1.f) of this regulation, this rule should be applied analogously to the remuneration of the temporary personnel appointed by those senior officials.

This is further supported by what is established in Article 12 of the TRLEBEP.

Note that paragraph 1 of this Article 12 establishes that temporary personnel will be compensated from the budgetary credits allocated for that purpose. And the final clause of paragraph two of this article establishes that the maximum number of temporary personnel in each public administration and the remuneration conditions will be public.

While the TRLEBEP refers to the public service laws that are enacted to develop the Statute for determining the number of temporary personnel in each public administration, the same does not occur regarding the remuneration conditions, since the TRLEBEP establishes, directly and immediately, without the need for further development, that they will be public.
