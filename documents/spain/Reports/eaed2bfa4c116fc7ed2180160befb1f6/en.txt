The inquiry raises whether the request made by the Ministry of Universities to IMSERSO, consisting of the communication to the consulting Institute of a database with the identifiers of the individuals included in its SIIU - Integrated University Information System, which does not include the disability variable, is compliant with data protection regulations. The objective is for IMSERSO itself to cross-reference this data with that contained in the State Database of Persons with Disability Assessment, subsequently sending the obtained information for each identifier regarding their degree of disability to the requesting Subdirectorate.  

According to the consulting Institute, the Subdirectorate of Research University Activity of the Ministry of Universities has informed them that, based on the obtained data, which includes an indication of whether the person has a recognized disability at a degree equal to or greater than 33 percent, certain data cross-references would be carried out, enabling a procedure to publish only aggregated tables with the information - anonymized data - without, at any time, proceeding to publish identifying data related to identified or identifiable individuals, but only such aggregated tables.  

As a preliminary matter, it is important to note that at the time of formulating the inquiry, it must be based on the new regime established by Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, regarding the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, which repeals Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation, GDPR) and in Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights - LOPDGDD.  

Indeed, as indicated in the Explanatory Memorandum of Law 3/2018, “the greatest novelty presented by Regulation (EU) 2016/679 is the evolution from a model fundamentally based on compliance control to one that rests on the principle of active responsibility, which requires a prior assessment by the data controller or the data processor of the risk that the processing of personal data may generate, in order to adopt the appropriate measures based on that assessment.”

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation -GDPR-), and Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and guarantee of digital rights -LOPDGDD- constitute the legal framework of reference in Spain that affects the protection of personal data. These regulations govern the principles and foundations to which the collection and processing of personal data by any public or private entity carrying out personal data processing in the exercise of its activity must adhere.
Among other definitions, Article 4 of the GDPR refers to “personal data” as any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (“the data subject”); an identifiable natural person is considered to be any person whose identity can be determined, directly or indirectly, in particular by means of an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific elements of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that person. And “processing” as any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination, or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.
In relation to the so-called “health data,” Article 4.15 of the GDPR defines them as personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, which reveal information about their health status.

And, as has been specifically outlined, for the purposes of the subject matter of this inquiry, in relation to special categories of personal data, Article 9.2 j) of the GDPR refers to Article 89.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation itself, which operates under the terms analyzed below.

According to Article 9.2 j) of the GDPR, processing for statistical purposes may be carried out when the processing is necessary, in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1, on the basis of Union law or the law of the Member States, being -in addition- proportional to the objective pursued, essentially respecting the right to data protection and establishing adequate and specific measures to protect the interests and fundamental rights of the data subject. For these purposes, Member States may maintain or introduce additional conditions, including limitations, regarding the processing of genetic data, biometric data, or data concerning health - pursuant to Article 9.4 of the GDPR-.

On its part, Law 12/1989, of May 9, on the Public Statistical Function, establishes in its article 8 that the National Statistical Plan is the main organizing instrument of the statistical activity of the General State Administration and will have a validity of four years. Royal Decree 1110/2020, of December 15, which approves the National Statistical Plan 2021-2024 (BOE 30/12/2020), develops its provisions for that period, including the statistics for state purposes that must be carried out in the four-year period by the statistical services of the General State Administration or any other entities dependent on it. 
From the Ministry of Universities, the statistical publication of "University Performance Indicators" is carried out, with the number IOE: 44052 (Inventory of Statistical Operations), currently included in the National Statistical Plan 2021-24 with the code "8674 University Indicators". As observed in the current IOE Sheet: 44052, to which these "indicators" are incorporated, https://www.ine.es/dyngs/IOE/es/operacion.htm?id=1259946001133, it is indicated that (i) "the objective of this operation is to monitor students and the studies they undertake by presenting data related to academic performance in relation to credits, the renewal of students in different degrees, dropout and change of studies, the time spent until graduation, the grades of the records, and the transition rates from Bachelor's to Master's," that (ii) the "study variables" refer to "Performance, grade of the record, dropout from study, change of study, suitability, duration of studies, renewal of the degree, transition from Bachelor's to Master's," and that (iii) the "classification variables" must contain the "Type and modality of the university, university, and field of study." 
Well then, from the analysis of the variables and objectives included in the "University Performance Indicators," published by the competent body as a specification of its activity in relation to its participation in the National Statistical Plan, it is not inferred that, regarding the corresponding period of 2021-2024, the normative realization of statistics encompassing health data related to identified or identifiable individuals, specifically regarding the type and degree of their disability, has been contemplated. 
Consequently, the consulting Institute must adhere to the provisions of Chapter II of Title I of the aforementioned Law 12/1989, of May 9, which regulates the data collection regime for these purposes, and, specifically, in accordance with its article 11.2: 
"In any case, the data that may reveal ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or ideological beliefs, and, in general, any circumstances that may affect personal or family privacy, will be strictly voluntary to provide and, consequently, may only be collected with the express consent of the interested parties." 
In accordance with all of the above, and considering the transcribed regulations, the request made by the Ministry of Universities to IMSERSO, for the purpose of crossing information with personal data subject to this report, is not in compliance with data protection regulations.
