Upon reviewing your request for a report, submitted to this Legal Office, regarding the Draft Law amending the consolidated text of the General Law for the defense of consumers and users and other complementary laws, approved by Royal Legislative Decree 1/2007, of November 16, requested from this Spanish Agency for Data Protection in accordance with the provisions of articles 37 h) of the Organic Law, of December 13, on the Protection of Personal Data, and 5 b) of the Statute of the Agency, approved by Royal Decree 428/1993, of March 26, I am obliged to inform you of the following:

The Draft submitted for report aims to transpose into Spanish law Directive 2015/2302 of the European Parliament and of the Council of November 25, 2015, concerning package travel and linked travel arrangements, which amends Regulation (EC) No 2006/2004 and Directive 2011/83/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council and repeals Directive 90/314/EEC of the Council.

To this end, a new Book Four is introduced in the consolidated text of the Law for the defense of consumers and users, regulating package travel and linked travel arrangements, in which the regulatory standards for the content of these contracts and the binding nature of the pre-contractual relationship, possible modifications of the contract, causes for its termination, execution of the contract, and existing guarantees in favor of the traveler in cases where the contract is not consummated are incorporated. Linked travel services are also regulated, and Annexes are introduced in the consolidated text determining the standardized information that must be provided to travelers for the conclusion of contracts through a layered information system in which, along with a main legend, a hyperlink will be available detailing the rights of the consumer.

As a starting point, it should be noted that the text submitted for report does not introduce any particularity regarding the protection of personal data, which is logical, since the processing carried out by the various parties involved in the contractual relationship; that is, both the organizer and the retailer, as well as third parties to whom the travelers' data must be communicated for the effective realization of the trip, will be fully subject to the provisions contained in said regulations.

In this regard, it should be taken into account that the regulatory regime of the fundamental right to the protection of personal data is undergoing profound modification following the approval of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation), which will be fully applicable on May 25, 2018, as established by its article 99.2.

The conclusion reached, regarding the full subjection to the data protection norms of the various agents involved in the contract, is reaffirmed by Directive 2015/2302 itself when its recital 49 states that “This Directive is understood without prejudice to the rules on the protection of personal data established in Directive 95/46/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council (3) and to the Union rules on private international law, in particular Regulation (EC) No 593/2008 of the European Parliament and of the Council.”

Article 153.1 of the Consolidated Text, as amended by the Draft, provides that “before the traveler is bound by any package travel contract or corresponding offer, the organizer and also the retailer, when the package travel is sold through the latter, shall provide the traveler with the form containing the standardized information relating to the package travel set out in Annex II, A or B, and the following information that is applicable to the package travel.”

In turn, Article 153.2 also imposes information duties on the organizers and businesses to whom the data is transmitted “to the extent relevant for the respective travel services they offer,” adding that “the organizer shall also simultaneously provide the standardized information by means of the form set out in Annex II.C.”

Article 5.1 of Organic Law 15/1999 imposes on the data controller, which in this case will fall on the organizer and, where applicable, the retailer, the obligation to inform the data subject from whom the data is collected of the details mentioned therein.

At the same time, Article 13.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation regulates the right of the data subject to be informed about:

a) the identity and contact details of the controller and, where applicable, of their representative;

b) the contact details of the data protection officer, where applicable;

c) the purposes of the processing for which the personal data are intended and the legal basis for the processing;

The Draft Organic Law on the Protection of Personal Data, which adapts the Spanish legal system to the aforementioned General Data Protection Regulation, approved by the Council of Ministers on November 10, 2017, also refers to the duty to inform the data subject in its Article 11, establishing in its first paragraph that “When personal data is obtained from the affected party through electronic communication networks or in the context of providing an information society service, as well as in those other cases expressly established by law or when authorized by the Spanish Data Protection Agency, the data controller may fulfill the information duty established in Article 13 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 by providing the affected party with the basic information referred to in the following paragraph and indicating an electronic address or other means that allows easy and immediate access to the remaining information.” This basic information must include, according to paragraph 2, at least:  

a) The identity of the data controller and, if applicable, of its representative.  

b) The purpose of the processing.  

c) The manner in which the affected party may exercise the rights established in Articles 15 to 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.  

Furthermore, “if the data obtained from the affected party are to be processed for profiling purposes, the basic information shall also include this circumstance. In this case, the affected party must be informed of their right to object to the adoption of automated individual decisions that could produce legal effects concerning them or significantly affect them, when this right applies in accordance with the provisions of Article 22 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.”  

As already indicated, from the wording of the Draft submitted for report and from what is indicated in Recital 49 of Directive 2015/2302, it follows that the subjects under the regime of the Draft will be obliged to comply with the provisions of the data protection regulations, among which is, at the time of data collection, the one referring to the duty to inform the data subject in the terms currently provided by Organic Law 15/1999 and, after its full application on May 25, 2018, by the General Data Protection Regulation.  

This requires that the Draft submitted for report takes this obligation into account, establishing in its Article 153.1, as information that the organizer and the retailer must provide to the data subject, in addition to that established in the corresponding Annexes, the information required by the aforementioned regulations.
