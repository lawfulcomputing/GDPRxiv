Legal Office  
Entry Ref. 212884/2019  
The inquiry raises the question of whether the access of the Staff Board of the Peripheral Services of Andalusia in the province of Almería to the documentation referred to as “productivity supplement lists” of the civil servants of the General Administration of the Junta de Andalucía in the province of Almería complies with the provisions of data protection regulations.  
According to the inquiry, the consulting Staff Board considers this documentation essential for the exercise of its functions as a representative body of civil servants within its territorial scope, and for the proper compliance with the provisions of Royal Legislative Decree 5/2015, of October 30 - Basic Statute of Public Employees (TREBEP) - and Organic Law 11/1985, of August 2, on Trade Union Freedom - LOLS.  
As stated by the consulting entity - accompanying documentation that supports this - it has addressed the General Secretariat for Public Administration of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Public Administration of the Junta de Andalucía, requesting a copy of the aforementioned “list,” having received a negative response to its request, issued by that body based on the Report of this Agency dated May 27, 2009 (Report 241/2009).  
In its writing, the consulting Staff Board refers to judgment No. 211/2013, dated 07/19/2013, of the Central Administrative Court No. 7 of Madrid, issued in the Summary Procedure 0000518/2013, whose Legal Foundation two reproduces in its instance, and which, in relation to the claim made in the proceedings, rules, condemning the appealed body “to provide the requesting union with the information requested regarding the amounts paid as productivity.”  
Additionally, the consulting Staff Board partially transcribes the Judgment of the Supreme Court - STS - Social Chamber, number 111/2018, of February 7, 2018, issued in the cassation appeal 78/2017, whose ruling dismisses the cassation appeal filed by the Ministry of Finance and Public Administrations (General Directorate of Public Function) against the judgment of the Social Chamber of the National Court, dated December 21, 2016 (proceedings 312/2016).

According to the inquiry, this judgment, in its Legal Foundation Three, point 4, paragraphs 2 and 3, has determined the following:  
“(…) the need to identify the workers occupying each of the positions listed in the RTP is not trivial, as personal elements are related to aspects such as training, qualifications, and specialization, also being necessary to delineate the circumstances of vacancies, their coverage, order of priorities, systems of substitution and promotion, etc. In short, it seems evident that the fulfillment of those functions of the union representations justifies access to such data and, therefore, there is no undue access to personal data that contravenes the right to the protection of such data.”  

I  
In the field of the public sector, in which the issue now raised is situated, this Agency has repeatedly expressed its position, and among others, the reports 241/2009, of May 27, and 137/2010, of April 7, can be cited.  
As mentioned by the consulting entity itself, this Agency in its report 241/2009 modified the previously held criterion regarding the access of workers' representatives to the “productivity lists” of public employees, maintaining that:  
“Prior to the entry into force of Law 7/2007, of April 12, on the Basic Statute of Public Employees, this Spanish Agency for Data Protection had considered that the disclosure of the aforementioned information was covered by article 11.2 a) of Organic Law 15/1999, as previously cited, in relation to the last paragraph of article 23.3 c) of Law 30/1984, of August 2, on Measures for the Reform of Public Function, according to which ‘in any case, the amounts received by each civil servant for this concept shall be public knowledge to the other civil servants of the interested Department or Agency as well as to the union representatives.’”  
Likewise, after the entry into force of Law 7/2007, this Agency pointed out on one occasion that “although the aforementioned provision has been repealed by Law 7/2007, of April 12, on the Basic Statute of Public Employees, it should be noted that, in accordance with the provisions of its final provision four, section 2, the regulatory provisions of the remuneration rights of public employees, contained in Chapter III of Title III of the Statute ‘will take effect from the entry into force of the Public Function Laws that are enacted in development of this Statute,’ so it will be necessary to pay attention to what is provided in Law 30/1984.”  
In summary, after the entry into force of Law 7/2007, the resolution of the issue now analyzed was based on the determination of the validity of what is provided in article 23.3 c) of Law 30/1984. Consequently, the

Well, the issue regarding the validity of the aforementioned article 23.3 c) of Law 30/1984 as enabling the disclosure to union representatives of the individualized data corresponding to the productivity supplement of public employees has been thoroughly examined by the State Attorney General's Office - Legal Service Directorate of the State in Opinion dated January 26, 2009, in which it is indicated, first of all, the following:
“(...) in a first approach to Chapter IV of Title III of the EBEP (related to the right to collective bargaining, representation, and institutional participation and the right to assembly), a Chapter that, as has been seen, is directly applicable, more specifically, to its article 40 (regarding the “Functions and legitimacy of the representation bodies”), it is observed, after a careful reading of the same, that, unlike its closest legislative predecessor (article 9 of the repealed Law 9/1987, of June 12, on Representation Bodies, Determination of Working Conditions, and Participation of Personnel in the Service of Public Administrations), said provision does not attribute to the Personnel Boards and Delegates of Personnel, as applicable, the specific function of being aware of the amounts received by each official as a productivity supplement.
Indeed, a simple comparison of the functions assigned to those representation bodies in the cited articles reveals the clear intention of the legislator to maintain the functions that article 9 of Law 9/1987 attributed to them, except for the function of being aware of the amounts received by each official as a productivity supplement, which is replaced in article 40 of the EBEP by other very generic ones, such as that provided for in section 1.a) of this provision – “to receive information on personnel policy, as well as on data regarding the evolution of remuneration...” – or that provided for in section 1.f) – “to collaborate with the corresponding Administration to achieve the establishment of any measures that ensure the maintenance and increase of productivity.” 
It is therefore clear that there is an incompatibility, at this point, between the regulation contained in article 9 of the repealed Law 9/1987 (which came to specify the provision contained in the last paragraph, last clause, of article 23.3.c) of Law 30/1984) and that contained in article 40 of the current EBEP, a provision that is part of Chapter IV of Title III of this latter legal text and, therefore, of direct application, as established by Instruction one of the Resolution of June 21, 2007.
Once the verification just indicated has been carried out, the Opinion analyzes the validity of the third paragraph of article 23.3 c) of Law 30/1984, similar to article 82.3 c) of Law 2/1987 now analyzed, taking into account the provisions of the repealing provision and the fourth final provision of Law 7/2007, reaching the conclusion that the detected incompatibility.

In the present case, and regarding the issue submitted for report by this Center, related to the knowledge by union representatives of the amounts received by each official as a productivity supplement, provided for in article 23.3.c), last paragraph, of Law 30/1984, it is immediately evident that this latter provision is in clear contradiction with the provisions of article 40 of the EBEP, part of Chapter IV, Title III of this legal text which, as has been seen, is directly applicable, so the aforementioned provision contained in article 23.3.c), last paragraph, of Law 30/1984 must be understood as repealed by article 40 of the EBEP, without prejudice to the remaining provisions of article 23.3 of Law 30/1984 regarding complementary remuneration (given that regarding these latter provisions, there is no rule in the EBEP that is incompatible with them).
In summary, and in accordance with the provisions of the sole derogatory provision and final provision four of the EBEP and in Instruction one of the Resolution of June 21, 2007, of the General Secretariat for Public Administration, the last clause of article 23.3.c) of Law 30/1984, of Measures for the Reform of Public Function, which was specified in article 9.4.c) of Law 9/1987, must be understood as repealed by article 40 of the EBEP, from whose literal wording it is inferred that the function attributed by the previous regulations to the union representatives of public employees, consisting of being informed and heard about the amounts received by each official for productivity supplement, has disappeared.
Currently, there is no legal basis for the transfer to union representatives of data regarding the amounts received by officials for productivity supplement without their consent – article 11.2.a) of Organic Law 15/1999, on Data Protection – it is undeniable, as correctly pointed out in its draft report by the consulting State Attorney, that it is not possible to provide any data related to those amounts to union representatives without the prior consent of the interested officials, thereby fully restoring the validity of the provision contained in article 11.1 of Organic Law 15/1999, according to which "personal data subject to processing may only be communicated to a third party for the fulfillment of purposes directly related to the legitimate functions of the transferor and the transferee, with the prior consent of the interested party."

Thus, it is concluded in the Opinion that “Law 7/2007, of April 12, on the Basic Statute of Public Employees has rendered ineffective, since its entry into force, the obligation that weighed on the Departments or State Bodies to inform the union representatives of public officials about the amounts received by each of them as a productivity supplement, and the last paragraph of article 23.3.c) of Law 30/1984, of August 2, on Measures for the Reform of Public Function, and article 9.4.c) of Law 9/1987, of June 12, on Representation Bodies, determination of working conditions and participation of personnel in the service of Public Administrations, must be understood as repealed, in light of the clear provisions regarding this matter in article 40 of Law 7/2007, all of which is due to the combined effect and the interpretation made in the body of this report of the sole derogatory provision and the fourth final provision of Law 7/2007 and the first instruction of the Instructions of June 5, 2007, for the application of the Basic Statute of Public Employees in the scope of the General Administration of the State and its Public Bodies.
Therefore, “there is currently no legal basis for the transfer to union representatives of the data regarding the amounts received by officials as a productivity supplement without their consent, so it is not possible to provide any data related to those amounts to them without the prior consent of the interested officials, thus fully restoring the validity of the provision contained in article 11.1 of Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13, on the Protection of Personal Data, according to which “personal data subject to processing may only be communicated to a third party for the fulfillment of purposes directly related to the legitimate functions of the transferor and the transferee, with the prior consent of the interested party.”

Well, as has been indicated, the issue underlying the analysis of the appropriateness of the transfer to the representatives of public employees of the amounts individually received as a productivity supplement focuses on determining whether the rules that provided for such publicity are still in force after the entry into force of Law 7/2007. Consequently, the solution to this issue does not depend on a mere interpretation of the regulatory norms of the fundamental right to the

In light of all that has been indicated, it can be concluded. Modifying the criterion previously upheld by this Agency, that the transfer to union representatives of the data concerning the individualized perceptions of public employees regarding productivity supplements and bonuses is not covered by article 11.1 a) of Organic Law 15/1999, and it is necessary for such data transfer to take place with the consent of the affected parties.
II
In summary, both in the transcribed report - no. 241/2009, of May 27 - and in other subsequent reports that this Agency has issued on the matter, it has been determined that the intended transfer of the "productivity lists" to the representatives of the workers is inadequate according to the provisions of data protection regulations. 
Now, for the purposes of the consultation in question, it is necessary to analyze whether, with the entry into force and practical application of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, which repeals Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation -GDPR-), any different interpretation or any nuance is imposed in relation to the thesis repeatedly maintained by this Agency in recent years, stemming from the interpretation carried out by the State Attorney General's Office - Directorate of the State Legal Service.

In this regard, the new regulatory framework applicable in the field of data protection contemplates different scenarios that may give rise to the processing of personal data. 
Specifically, according to article 6 - "Lawfulness of processing" - of REGULATION (EU) 2016/679 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL of April 27, 2016 (GDPR), concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, among others, such processing is lawful and legitimate when:
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes; 
b) the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract; 
c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject; 
d) the processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person; 
e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller; 
f) the processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular where the data subject is a child.” 
Regarding the meaning of the expression “legal obligation” contained in article 6.1.c) GDPR, this expression corresponds, in the Spanish data protection regulation, to “obligation established in a norm with the rank of law.” Article 53.1 of the Constitution (CE) establishes that [t]he rights and freedoms recognized in Chapter Two of this Title bind all public authorities. Only by law, which in any case must respect its essential content, may the exercise of such rights and freedoms be regulated, which shall be protected in accordance with the provisions of article 161.1.a). The fundamental right to the protection of personal data is contained in article 18.4 CE, and therefore the necessity of a law to limit it is applicable.

Consequently, as stated in numerous reports from this Agency -in which legal provisions of similar content are analyzed-, from articles 40 of Royal Legislative Decree 5/2015, of October 30, which approves the consolidated text of the Basic Statute of Public Employees -TREBEP- and 10.3 of Organic Law 11/1985, of August 2, on Trade Union Freedom -LOLS-, the existence of the necessary regulatory coverage for the indiscriminate transfer of the "productivity lists" of workers to the Personnel Boards is not derived. 
Thus, in accordance with our reiterated opinion, the function of monitoring and protecting working conditions, attributed to the Personnel Boards by article 40 of the TREBEP and by article 10.2 of the LOLS, can be carried out without the need to proceed with a massive transfer of the "productivity lists" of the personnel providing their services in the corresponding Body or Dependency. Only in the event that the monitoring or control refers to a specific individual who has raised the corresponding complaint before the Personnel Board, will it be possible to transfer the specific data of that person.
In CONCLUSION:
 For the case submitted for consultation, even with the application of the GDPR, this Agency does not derive the existence of a legal norm with the rank of formal law, capable of being incorporated into the provisions of letter c) of article 6.1 of the GDPR, understanding that such data processing is not necessary for the fulfillment of a legal obligation imposed by law on the Personnel Boards. Consequently, the possible indiscriminate transfer of the "productivity lists" of the affected workers in favor of the Personnel Boards is not covered.
 This Agency maintains that the criterion supported in the Opinion of January 26, 2009, from the State Attorney General's Office - Legal Service of the State, remains fully in force, given the repeal of the enabling norms for the previously existing transfer, by the Basic Statute of Public Employees. Consequently, for the communication of the amounts received as a productivity supplement to the representative bodies of public employees, the consent of the affected individuals will be necessary. 
 In any case, in our opinion, the control function of the representatives of the workers will be fully satisfied, through the transfer to the Personnel Board of duly dissociated information. Thus, when such personal information is previously subjected to a dissociation procedure, the provisions of said Regulation -GDPR- will not apply, and its provisions regarding the "anonymized" information subject to processing will lapse.  
III
Notwithstanding the above, and analyzed the issue in light of the provisions of article 6.1 f) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, regarding the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data (General Data Protection Regulation), it can be considered that the legal authorization for workers of the same Department to access the data related to the productivity supplement received by the other members thereof finds its support in the provisions of article 6.1 f) of the Regulation.
According to the literal wording of this provision, such processing will be possible if it "is necessary for the satisfaction of legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, provided that such interests do not override the interests or the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require the protection of personal data."
Thus, it is necessary to determine to what extent the legitimate interest of the Personnel Boards may prevail over the right to data protection of each of them concerning the amount of the productivity supplement, which will undoubtedly affect the measures that must be taken to ensure that access to it does not lead to subsequent processing that may be contrary to the provisions of data protection legislation or generate situations that may jeopardize their rights. Obviously, these situations will be attributable to whoever actually commits the breach, but at the same time, it is the responsibility of the controller to establish the guarantees that minimize the possibility of their occurrence, so that the communication can be supported by the aforementioned article 6.1 f).
However, in the case of the representatives of the workers, it is not perceived that the processing of the personal data whose transfer is intended -contained in the "productivity lists"- is necessary for the satisfaction of legitimate interests -as per letter f) of article 6.1 of the GDPR- pursued by the Personnel Boards, which should prevail over the interests or the fundamental rights and freedoms of the affected workers, regarding whom the corresponding legal protection is imposed.

In CONCLUSION:
 The GDPR itself establishes the basic criteria to consider for detecting legitimate interests that allow for the assessment of the balancing rule set forth in Article 6.1 f). Applying these criteria to the processing of data for the purposes of union representation, it must be concluded that the aforementioned legitimate interest does not exist in the case under consultation - related to the transfer of the "productivity list," where the interests, rights, and fundamental freedoms of the data subjects prevail, requiring the protection of their personal data.
 However, it should be considered that there is a concurrence of legitimate interests so that, individually, the workers of the same Department may access the data regarding the productivity supplement received by the other members of that department, which finds its support in the provisions of Article 6.1 f) of the Regulation.
As noted in our legal report 137/2010, dated April 7, in cases of access by public employees, such access must be facilitated in a manner that is least harmful to the rights of the other affected parties. This leads to the conclusion that a general exposure of the data is the most suitable means, avoiding its publication on a network, even if private, and even more so its inclusion in a computer file, even if it could not be manipulated. This would be the formula that would best guarantee the balance of rights referred to in Article 6.1 f) of the General Data Protection Regulation.
