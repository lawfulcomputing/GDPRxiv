I  
The draft Ministerial Order submitted for report aims to complete the regulatory development of section 2 of article 71 of Law 18/2014, of October 15, approving urgent measures for growth, competitiveness, and efficiency, which enables, within the framework of the National Energy Efficiency Obligations System (hereinafter, SNOEE), the establishment of a mechanism for the accreditation of energy savings through the presentation of Energy Savings Certificates.  
This accreditation system has been created by Royal Decree 36/2023, of January 24, which establishes a System of Energy Savings Certificates (CAE) and offers an alternative to the obligated subjects of the SNOEE to comply with part of their annual final energy savings obligations, as opposed to the sole route of financial contributions to the National Energy Efficiency Fund existing until now.  
The projected Order develops, among other things, the figure of the delegated subject and the conditions that must be met both for its accreditation and to be able to assume by delegation part of the savings objectives of the obligated subjects, being able to develop or promote the implementation of actions that generate savings in final energy consumption.  
On the other hand, as one of the key components of the system and with the aim of achieving greater agility in the issuance of CAE, the role of the Energy Savings Verifier, an entity accredited by ENAC for the verification of energy savings achieved through the execution of actions in the field of energy efficiency, is detailed.  
Finally, the organization and management of the National CAE Registry is developed, which guarantees the validity of the CAE issued in the national territory and the traceability of the operations carried out with them by their holders.  
Regarding the matter of personal data protection, the regulation to which the draft Order submitted for consultation must comply is Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, which repeals Directive 95/46/EC (GDPR hereinafter) and Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and guarantee of digital rights (LOPDGDD hereinafter).  

II  
The first aspect to be analyzed is related to the subjective scope and the material scope of application of the GDPR and the LOPDGDD applied to the processing of personal data derived from the approval and execution of the draft Ministerial Order submitted for report.  
It is worth noting that the GDPR extends its protection, as established in its article 1.2, to the fundamental rights and freedoms of natural persons and, in particular, their right to the protection of personal data, defined in its article 4.1 as “any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘the data subject’); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific elements of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.”  
And regarding the processing itself, article 4.2 of the GDPR defines “processing” as any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination, or any other form of making available.

In Recital 14 of the GDPR, it is stated that (…) this Regulation does not regulate the processing of personal data relating to legal entities and in particular to companies constituted as legal entities, including the name and form of the legal entity and its contact details. (…)

Taking the above into account, it is observed in the text of the Order submitted for report that the recipients of the same are primarily legal entities and that, residually, personal data of individuals considered individually may be subject to processing, in the sense that such processing would occur not by virtue of being representatives or proxies of those.

The determination of legal entities as the main recipients of the regulation is already noted in the preamble, indicating in section III that:

On the other hand, due to the fact that the potential interested parties and economic operators who may participate in the certification system and procedures regulated in this order are legal entities, in accordance with the provisions of Article 14.2 of Law 39/2015, of October 1, the electronic processing of CAEs will be mandatory in all phases of the procedure.

This ministerial order shall apply to the entire CAE System, including the agents participating in it and, in particular, to those who have the status of delegated subject or autonomous manager, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 9 and 17, respectively, of Royal Decree 36/2023, of January 24.

Thus, regarding the delegated subject, the Order submitted for report coincides with the definition of "delegated subject" established in Article 9 of Royal Decree 36/2023, providing in its Article 3.1 that:

A delegated subject is any legal entity of a public or private nature that can assume, wholly or partially, the delegation of obtaining new annual energy savings from one or several obligated subjects and that has been previously accredited as such by the National Coordinator of the CAE System.

From the definition of the delegated subject, the need to address the figure of the obligated subject is also derived, as it is one of the actors in the delegation, and Royal Decree 36/2023 itself considers as such in its Article 2:

c) Obligated subject: The companies marketing gas and electricity, wholesale operators of petroleum products, and wholesale operators of liquefied petroleum gases, as established in Article 69 of Law 18/2014, of October 15.

The Order also addresses the figure of the Energy Savings Verifier in its Article 8, and its definition is also found in Article 2.k of the aforementioned Royal Decree, which defines it as:

k) Energy savings verifier: An entity accredited by the National Accreditation Entity (ENAC) that may be freely chosen by the obligated subject or the delegated subject from among those providing this service and that will be responsible for verifying the energy savings obtained from the execution of one or several energy efficiency actions, as well as ensuring that the documentation provided by the obligated subjects and/or the delegated subjects in the CAE file complies with all information requirements.

Article 5 of the GDPR sets forth the “Principles relating to processing” as follows:  
1. Personal data shall be:  
a) processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject (“lawfulness, fairness, and transparency”);  
b) collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes, and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes; in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1, further processing of personal data for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes, or statistical purposes shall not be considered incompatible with the initial purposes (“purpose limitation”);  
c) adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (“data minimization”);  
d) accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; all reasonable steps must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate with respect to the purposes for which they are processed are erased or rectified without delay (“accuracy”);  
e) kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes of the processing of personal data; personal data may be stored for longer periods insofar as they are processed solely for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes, or statistical purposes, in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1, without prejudice to the application of appropriate technical and organizational measures as required by this Regulation to protect the rights and freedoms of the data subject (“storage limitation”);  
f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage.

IV
Regarding the principle of lawfulness, reference must be made to what is indicated in Article 6 of the GDPR, specifically to the provisions of paragraph 1 letters c) and e), which state:
1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:
c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;
e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;
Recital 45 of the GDPR states that "When carried out in compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject, or if necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority, the processing must have a basis in Union or Member State law."
The LOPDGDD establishes in its Article 8 under the heading "Processing of data by legal obligation, public interest, or exercise of public powers" the following:
1. The processing of personal data may only be considered based on compliance with a legal obligation enforceable against the controller, in the terms provided in Article 6.1.c) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, when such is provided for by a rule of European Union law or a rule with the rank of law, which may determine the general conditions of processing and the types of data subject to it as well as the transfers that may arise as a consequence of compliance with the legal obligation. Such a rule may also impose special conditions on processing, such as the adoption of additional security measures or others established in Chapter IV of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.
2. The processing of personal data may only be considered based on the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority conferred on the controller, in the terms provided in Article 6.1 e) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, when it derives from a competence attributed by a rule with the rank of law.
Considering the above, and for the purposes of determining whether the legal basis for the processing derived from the approval and execution of the Order submitted for report is that provided in paragraph c) or that provided in paragraph e) of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, or if both apply, it is appropriate to cite what is included in Report 74/2019 where the need to delineate both concepts was highlighted, as failing to do so would imply confusing, in almost all cases of Administration action, both legal bases, concluding that
Therefore, the legal basis provided in letter c) of Article 6.1 of the GDPR will apply in those cases where a rule with the rank of law imposes a specific obligation on the Administration to give, do, or refrain from doing, which involves the processing of personal data, and different from the generic legal duty of the Administration to exercise the powers that the legal system attributes to it to serve the public interest objectively (Article 103 of the Constitution).

Individual mention deserves the processing of personal data carried out by the figure of the so-called "Energy Savings Verifier" regulated in Article 2.k of the aforementioned Royal Decree and in Article 9 of the draft order, as it is an entity external to the public administration that carries out a processing of personal data, with the purpose of verifying annual energy savings and issuing a favorable opinion that will be necessary to obtain the CAE. 
Indeed, it should be noted that, although its action is provided for in the draft order, it is an independent data processing – prior and distinct – from that carried out by the competent administration and which is also subject to data protection regulations. 
That is to say, there will be essential aspects of the processing carried out by this actor that differ from that carried out by the organs of the competent public administration and that will be decisive when, for example, establishing the information referred to in Article 13 of the GDPR, or the way to act in situations that may arise of very diverse nature such as what happens with the information subject to processing in the event that the issuance of the opinion is unfavorable, etc. 
The only indication regarding the fulfillment of its duties is in section 6 of the aforementioned Article 9 which states that: 
The Energy Savings Verifier will act and perform its duties in accordance with the procedures and technical documentation published in this regard by ENAC. 
Therefore, it is deemed appropriate to include some reference to the obligations that pertain to it in terms of data protection in the provision.

Regarding compliance with the remaining principles set out in Article 5 of the GDPR, special reference must be made, in addition to the principles of lawfulness and transparency, to the principles of purpose limitation and data minimization, as outlined in letters b) and c), which indicate that “personal data shall be:
b) collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes, and shall not be further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes; in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1, further processing of personal data for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes, or statistical purposes shall not be considered incompatible with the initial purposes (‘purpose limitation’);

As previously indicated, in the processing of the accreditation procedure as a delegated subject (Articles 3 and following), in the processing of the CAE issuance procedure (Articles 7 and following), and in the management of the National CAE Registry (Articles 14 and following), personal data of the representatives of legal entities that fall under the application of this order will be processed, and in some cases, personal data of natural persons, which is why the regulations on the protection of personal data apply.
Now, to make a judgment of adequacy regarding the principles of minimization and purpose limitation in relation to the information necessary for the processing of the indicated administrative actions, it should be noted that these are matters that respond to the technical judgment of the competent administrations, which precludes a thorough analysis in this regard by this Agency.
It should only be recalled what is indicated in Recital 39 regarding the principles of purpose limitation and minimization, according to which personal data should only be processed if the purpose of the processing cannot reasonably be achieved by other means.

identifying the processing of personal data and the necessary guarantees that
should be incorporated into the present regulatory text.
The above is reinforced by what is indicated in the aforementioned Royal Decree
311/2022, of May 3, (Article 2.1), which not only establishes which
specific measures must be established in each case but also imposes the necessity/obligation to carry out the aforementioned risk analysis and
Impact Assessment in terms of personal data protection (Article 3),
with the measures resulting from this analysis and impact assessment prevailing.
Article 3 of the aforementioned Royal Decree establishes:
Article 3. Information systems that process personal data.
1. When an information system processes personal data, the provisions of
Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, regarding the protection
of natural persons in relation to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, and repealing
Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation) and in
Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data
Protection and guarantee of digital rights, (…) as well as the
criteria established by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection (…) without prejudice to the requirements established in this royal
decree.
2. In these cases, the data controller or processor,
advised by the data protection officer, shall conduct a risk analysis
in accordance with Article 24 of the General Data Protection
Regulation and, in the cases of Article 35, an impact assessment on data protection.
3. In any case, the measures to be implemented as a
result of the risk analysis and, where appropriate, the impact assessment referred to in the previous paragraph shall prevail if they are more stringent than those provided for in this royal decree.
From the draft regulation, it follows, on the one hand, that the processing of personal data in the CAE System will be carried out through an electronic platform and that the information will be stored in a Public Registry, and on the other,
that there is a processing of personal data that will be carried out by an entity external to the competent administration, which is the Savings Verifier for the fulfillment of its duties.
Therefore, it would be appropriate to conduct at least the risk analysis, and if applicable, the impact assessment on data protection.
