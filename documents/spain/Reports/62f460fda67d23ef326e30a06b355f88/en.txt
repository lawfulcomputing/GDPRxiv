After examining your request for a report, submitted to this Legal Office, regarding the Draft Royal Decree amending the regulation of public hydraulic domain (RDPH) approved by Royal Decree 849/1986, of April 11, and the regulation of public water administration approved by Royal Decree 927/1988, of July 29, requested from this Spanish Agency for Data Protection (AEPD) in accordance with the provisions of Article 47 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the protection of personal data and the guarantee of digital rights (LOPDGDD), in relation to Article 57.1, letter c), of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data, and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation), and 5 b) of the Statute of the Agency, approved by Royal Decree 389/2021, of June 1, I am obliged to inform you of the following:  

The primary objective of the project, as stated in its Explanatory Memorandum and the Regulatory Impact Analysis Report, is to achieve better regulation of the public hydraulic domain, and its protection, use, and management, while also updating Royal Decree 927/1988, of July 29, which approves the Regulation of Public Water Administration and Hydrological Planning, in development of Titles II and III of the Water Law.  

Specifically, it aims to conserve and protect the good status of inland waters, both in their quantitative and chemical aspects, including all necessary measures for compliance, sustainability, and guarantee of the good status of groundwater, without forgetting the inclusion of associated aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems, groundwater, and wetlands.  

In terms of personal data protection, the additional regulation is not substantial compared to that already contained in the text of the regulation, and is in fact limited, because the new regulation does not aim to modify existing treatments or add new ones. When any section of the norm that may refer to personal data is touched upon— which, as stated, is limited and tangential— the new regulation does not add anything new to what already existed previously, so the legal bases, principles, and foundations of the treatments remain as they are, with adequate regulation from the perspective of personal data protection.

This Agency had the opportunity to express its opinion in Report 290/2012 regarding the amendment of the Regulation on Public Hydraulic Domain, which primarily concerned the regime of the Water Register, leading to Royal Decree 670/2013, of September 6, in which, apart from an observation regarding the data controller concerning the Water Register, the opinion of this Agency was favorable. Subsequently, through Report 488/2015, the Project Order regulating the IT structure of the Water Register and the central water database was fully favorably reported.

Well, in the reform now presented for Report, the modifications that should be noted regarding the protection of personal data are merely accessory to the main objective of this reform (the strengthening of the protection of the public domain) and do not present any alteration of the general regime prior to the reform. The personal data that would be processed in some way will be processed in the Water Register, Private Water Catalog, and Discharge Census, all of which are covered by the consolidated text of the Water Law (LA), as a mission of public interest (art. 80 LA), and the RDPH already contained a regulation on this (arts. 189 and ss. RDPH) that this Agency favorably reported in the cited Reports. The modifications in these sections of the RDPH in the project are irrelevant for these purposes.

Article 254 of the RDPH regulates the so-called Authorized Discharge Census. This provision refers to Article 15 of the LA, which states that all natural or legal persons have the right to access information regarding water, under the terms provided in the law regulating the right to information in environmental matters (currently Law 27/2006, of July 18), with special reference to discharges. This Agency considers that this mission of public interest may constitute the legal basis for the processing of personal data that must be included, if applicable, in the Discharge Census, provided, of course, that the principles of Article 5 of the GDPR are complied with, and in particular the principle of minimization, so that such data is only adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which it is processed. This Agency considers that the project is careful with the processing of personal data, and this can be observed in this matter of the discharge census in Annex VII, when regulating the personal data that will be registered for this purpose establishes, regarding the natural persons who are the holders of the discharge:
a) Holder and location of the discharge. Data of the holder of the discharge and location of each discharge point. When the holder of the discharge is a natural person who does not operate in commercial traffic, the name will be replaced by "Natural Person" without including any personal data (surnames, NIF, etc.).

This regulation, on the other hand, is not new, as it was added to Annex VII of the RDPH by Royal Decree 638/2016, of December 9. The same precaution should be taken (that this public interest mission may constitute the legal basis for the processing of personal data, provided, of course, that the principles of Article 5 of the GDPR are met, and in particular the principle of minimization, so that such data are only adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed) regarding the register of companies discharging waste (Article 268 of the RDPH) concerning the personal data that, if applicable, must be reflected therein.

The Fourth Transitional Provision of the Regulation establishes that 
The basin organizations will proceed to incorporate the information from the registrations in the electronic Water Register, completing its content with respect to what is established in Article 193 and subsequent articles of this Regulation. This completion of information shall not, in any case, imply any alteration either of the nature or of the content of the right previously registered in the General Register of Public Water Uses or in the movable sheets of the Water Register, and must be in accordance with what is established in the resolution granting the concession or the right to the private use of water and the applicable regulations at that time. For these purposes, the basin organizations will ex officio collect the necessary information and documentation.

This Agency does not fully understand the meaning of the expression "complete" contained in the provision. If "complete" is understood to mean "transfer" or "incorporate" the content of the registrations that already exist in "paper format" to electronic format, that expression would be unnecessary, as it is already expressed in the first part of the sentence (the use of the verb "incorporate"). If, on the other hand, it refers to the electronic format having, in some way, a broader content than it already had in paper format, so that what is "completed" is a "broader" content than the incorporation of the data contained in the original Water Register, this Agency expresses its disagreement as it would involve a broader processing of data than established in the regulation. This is mentioned, of course, exclusively as it refers to the processing of personal data, without this mention being extensible to other types of data not relating to natural persons, to whom the concept of personal data (Article 4.1) of the GDPR applies.

Another objective of the draft Royal Decree is the digitization of the water cycle, with a significant component of simplification and streamlining of administrative procedures, which is why the substitution of publications in the official provincial bulletins and in the announcement board of the municipalities by publications in the BOE and on the corresponding basin organization's website is established. In this regard, the Fourth Final Provision of the regulation contains a general statement of the principle, which is complemented by specific provisions, such as Article 109, referring to the publication of the "announcement note" in the BOE or on the website. It is worth recalling again the precaution that the personal identification data of the applicants or interested parties (see Article 109.2 of the RDPH) must, in all cases, be the minimum necessary for the purpose for which those personal data are processed.

Consequently, this Agency gives a favorable opinion on the Draft Royal Decree amending the regulation of the public hydraulic domain (RDPH) approved by Royal Decree 849/1986, of April 11, and the regulation of public water administration approved by Royal Decree 927/1988, of July 29.
