The consultation raises the adequacy to Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data (GDPR hereinafter) and to Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and guarantee of digital rights (LOPDGDD hereinafter) of the implementation of the program ACCESS TO DOCUMENTARY FUNDS AND WORKS (ATOPO) of the Provincial Council of Pontevedra.  

I  
According to the consultant, the execution of the ATOPO program involves the publication—and therefore the access of citizens—to information from various sources, such as repositories of collections and documentary, bibliographic, cartographic, and audiovisual funds deposited in the Museum of Pontevedra, in the Documentary and Bibliographic Heritage Service, and even coming (through the corresponding agreements) from municipal archives and other public or private institutions in the province.  
It is also intended to facilitate universal, direct access without identification or authorization, prior to defining the limits and conditions of other applicable regulations, among which is the protection of personal data.  
The consultation focuses on the following aspects:  
a) The processing of personal data of deceased individuals;  
b) The integration and application of Article 12 and subsequent articles of Law 19/2013, of December 9, on transparency, access to public information, and good governance (LTAIBG) in relation to the GDPR and LOPDGDD, and in relation to the deadlines established in Law 16/1985, of June 25, on Spanish Historical Heritage (LPHE) and Law 7/2014, of September 26, on archives and documents of Galicia;  
c) The publication of images in general and the publication of images of minors.  

II  
As a starting point, it is necessary to refer to the definition of processing to determine whether the execution of the ATOPO program can be considered as such.  
Article 4.2 of the GDPR, when defining data processing, includes (…) consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination, or any other form of enabling access, comparison, or interconnection (…).  

Therefore, once the existence of processing is considered, it is necessary to analyze what is established in Article 6 of the GDPR, which determines the cases in which the processing of personal data is considered lawful:  
1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following conditions is met:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party or for the implementation at the request of the data subject of pre-contractual measures;  
c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject;  
d) the processing is necessary to protect vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller;  
f) the processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, provided that such interests do not override the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in letter f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the exercise of their functions.  

The consultant indicates, in summary, that the execution of the ATOPO program is part of a legal mandate found in (i) Directive 2003/98/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council (and its transposition through Law 37/2007, of November 16, on the re-use of public sector information), (ii) in the various transparency laws at the state and regional level, and (iii) in the regulations governing access to documents and archives.  
Based on this consideration, the processing involved in the implementation of the ATOPO program could be legitimized under the cases provided for in letters c) and e) of paragraph 1 of Article 6, referring to compliance with a legal obligation or the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of public powers. In this regard, paragraphs 2 and 3 of the aforementioned article establish the following:  
2. Member States may maintain or introduce more specific provisions in order to adapt the application of the rules of this Regulation with respect to processing in compliance with paragraph 1, letters.

VII
In accordance with the above and as a response to the question raised by the consultant, regarding whether the cited regulations (limits and deadlines) prevail or what is indicated in article 12 of the LTAIBG, which states that All individuals have the right to access public information, under the terms provided in article 105.b) of the Spanish Constitution, developed by this Law, the answer must be that while the aforementioned article 12 contains a general formulation of a right, the limits and deadlines contained in the laws and regulations cited in this report cannot be overlooked.
That is to say, it is not acceptable, as proposed by the consultant, that due to the difficulties in executing the ATOPO program that involve compliance with deadlines and consent requirements, the application of article 12 of the LTAIBG be established ad libitum, disregarding the safeguards established in the transparency law itself and in the other applicable regulatory bodies.
It should also be noted that the processing of personal data associated with the execution of the ATOPO program, as well as the limits and safeguards that the applicable provisions establish, finds its legal basis not in section c) of article 6.1 GDPR, since we are not dealing with a "legal mandate" as the norms cited in the consultation serve to promote – not obligate – the reuse of public information, so the legal basis is found in section e) referring to the processing necessary for the fulfillment of a mission carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of public powers conferred to the data controller;
In this regard, article 8.2 of the LOPDGDD establishes: 2. The processing of personal data may only be considered based on the fulfillment of a mission carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of public powers conferred to the controller, under the terms provided in article 6.1 e) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, when it derives from a competence attributed by a norm with the rank of law.
In this case, it is the various transparency laws (state and regional), the LPHE and its implementing regulations, and Law 37/2007 on the reuse of public sector information, that attribute the competence to the consultant as an entity subject to the subjective scope of application of the same.

From the reading of STC 292/2000, we can affirm that the content of the right to data protection encompasses, on one hand, a set of powers of the right holder, and on the other, a series of legal obligations that fall on third parties who will process personal data. As powers, we can cite the intended effects in this consultation, such as the right of access, and among the legal obligations or duties, we can mention that the processing of data must find its legal basis in some provision of Article 6 of the GDPR, and otherwise classify it as unlawful.

From the above, it can be concluded that neither Directive 2003/98/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council nor Law 37/2007, of November 16, on the reuse of public sector information legitimizes by itself the dissemination of personal data nor diminishes the guarantees established by the GDPR and the LOPDGDD, as the processing is governed by this regulation.
Article 12 of the LTAIBG must be interpreted in relation to the limits and guarantees established in the law itself regarding the obtaining of consent and the passage of time.
In accessing information, whether through the LPH or through the LTAIBG, both open and universal formulas, as well as individualized requests, must coexist depending on the specific cases, without the practical difficulties that may arise being opposable to what is provided in these laws.
The decision on the reuse of public sector information is conditioned by the limitations included in the LTAIBG, as they affect the processing of personal data, as required by Article 3.4 of Law 37/2007. 
These limitations must be weighed differently depending on whether the data is subject to active publicity or is related to the individual exercise of the right of access to public information. In the processing of the latter, the procedure for allegations provided in Article 19.3 LTAIBG and the consequences of the opposition of the interested party must be taken into account, whether as an impediment to publication or as an additional element to consider in the weighing process.
To decide whether personal data is provided for reuse purposes, it is necessary to examine the risks to the interested parties and the measures that can minimize them through a data protection impact assessment, with the most appropriate alternative to allow the reuse of public information containing personal data being to proceed with its anonymization, so that it is excluded from the application of personal data protection regulations.
Anonymization requires the assessment of certain risks, such as the possibility that the reuser may reidentify individuals, which can be complemented by legally binding commitments through the granting of specific licenses.
