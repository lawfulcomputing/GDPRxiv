The consultation proposes a model information and informed consent sheet, called “Guide for the Proper Preparation of a Patient Information and Informed Consent Sheet (HIP(CI)” for participation in clinical trials with medications, in order to analyze whether it complies with the principle of information and transparency in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, regarding the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data (GDPR hereinafter).  

I  
As a starting point, it should be noted that the text proposed by the consultant in the model information and informed consent sheet is presented taking into account Opinion 3/2019 of the European Data Protection Board regarding the “Questions and Answers on the Interaction between the Clinical Trials Regulation (CTR) and the GDPR,” approved on January 23, 2019 (Opinion 3/2019 EDPB hereinafter), so that in addition to the analysis of the principle of transparency and its implications and the provisions of Articles 12 to 14 of the GDPR, the consultation must be addressed considering the aforementioned opinion and its consequences on the processing of personal data of participants in clinical trials.  
Taking the above into account, to provide a solution to the consultation raised to this Legal Office, the analysis that follows has considered the issues related to: how to provide the information (Articles 11 and 12 of the GDPR), secondly, what information must be provided (Article 13 and, where applicable, Article 14), and finally, what the content of the information offered is, being fundamental the type of processing to be carried out in each case that will determine the legal bases that legitimize it, and it is in this aspect where Opinion 3/2019 EDPB will have the greatest impact.

III
Before analyzing the adequacy of the text proposed by AEMPS to the GDPR and LOPDGDD, it should be noted that the proposed clauses and their explanatory notes refer, on one hand, to coded data and uncoded data, and on the other hand, to identifiable data and non-identifiable data. 
It should also be added that the possibility of re-identification in exceptional cases is indicated, from which it can be inferred that we are not dealing with anonymous or dissociated data, but rather that a technique of pseudonymization is being carried out.
The GDPR defines in its Article 4, paragraph 5, that "pseudonymization" means the processing of personal data in such a way that the data can no longer be attributed to a specific data subject without the use of additional information, provided that such additional information is kept separately and is subject to technical and organizational measures intended to ensure that the personal data is not attributed to an identified or identifiable natural person;
And in Recital 26 it establishes that the principles of data protection must apply to all information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. Pseudonymized personal data, which could be attributed to a natural person through the use of additional information, must be considered information about an identifiable natural person. To determine whether a natural person is identifiable, all means, such as singling out, that could reasonably be used by the data controller or any other person to identify the natural person directly or indirectly must be taken into account. To determine whether there is a reasonable likelihood that means will be used to identify a natural person, all objective factors, such as the costs and time required for identification, must be considered, taking into account both the technology available at the time of processing and technological advancements. Therefore, the principles of data protection should not apply to anonymous information, that is, information that does not relate to an identified or identifiable natural person, nor to data that has been rendered anonymous in such a way that the data subject is no longer identifiable, or ceases to be so. Consequently, this Regulation does not affect the processing of such anonymous information, including for statistical or research purposes.
In accordance with the above, the processing of data (including coded data) inferred from the clause proposed by AEMPS must comply with the provisions of the GDPR and the LOPDGDD.

The GDPR outlines the necessity of clearly establishing the map of participants in any data processing, in order to accurately determine the allocation of responsibilities in accordance with the aforementioned regulation. This regulation aims to ensure that no scenarios of action fall outside its scope of application, in order to provide supervisory authorities with the necessary elements to carry out their function and ultimately to offer European citizens the protection that their personal data deserves. Therefore, any activity involving the processing of personal data will be attributable to some entity that meets the requirements of the various categories provided by the GDPR.

In accordance with the above, the GDPR generally establishes (without prejudice to the figures of the recipient and third party) three scenarios of participants in the processing of personal data: the data controller, the joint controllers, and the data processor.

The GDPR defines in its Article 4.7 the figure of the data controller or controller as “the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; if the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the controller or the specific criteria for its appointment may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.”

Alongside this figure, the GDPR defines in its Article 26 the joint controller, determining the legal regime to which it must be subjected: “1. When two or more controllers jointly determine the purposes and means of processing, they shall be considered joint controllers. The joint controllers shall transparently and by mutual agreement determine their respective responsibilities in complying with the obligations imposed by this Regulation, in particular regarding the exercise of the rights of the data subject and their respective obligations to provide information referred to in Articles 13 and 14, unless, and to the extent that, their respective responsibilities are governed by the law of the Union or of the Member States applicable to them. Such an agreement may designate a contact point for data subjects.

2. The agreement referred to in paragraph 1 shall duly reflect the respective functions and relationships of the joint controllers in relation to the data subjects. The essential aspects of the agreement shall be made available to the data subject.

3. Regardless of the terms of the agreement referred to in paragraph 1, data subjects may exercise the rights conferred on them by this Regulation against, and in relation to, each of the controllers.”

From the above, it can be concluded that the text proposed by AEMPS, in general terms, should be adapted to the provisions of Articles 12 and 13 of the GDPR.
To comply with the principles of transparency, fairness, and purpose set forth in Article 5 of the GDPR, and taking into account what is indicated in Recitals 39 and 60, information regarding the processing of personal data should be provided independently from the rest of the information related to the clinical trial, even in a separate document.
However, in adapting the way of providing information to the specific context of the processing and considering what is indicated in Article 11 of the LOPDGDD, the possibility of using a layered information system should be assessed, which may exceed the information provided in the first layer of the minimum content proposed by the provision (taking into account the characteristics and context of the processing such as limitations on rights, possible third-party assignees, specific legal bases, etc.).
It is also important to comply with the systematic approach provided in Article 13 of the GDPR as it offers an order of presentation that facilitates the understanding by the recipient of the information, thus fulfilling the way of providing it concisely, transparently, intelligibly, and in an easily accessible manner, with clear and simple language, as indicated in Article 12 of the GDPR.
Furthermore, the scope of action of the Sponsor and the Center and their responsibilities is not clearly established; it should be identified in clear and simple language regarding which treatments each is responsible for, as well as the existence of a process for the coding or pseudonymization of the participants' data, so that the data subject knows in advance which data each will process and for what purpose.
It should be made clear to the participant in the clinical trial that the Sponsor does not access identifiable data, that these have undergone a pseudonymization process, if applicable, carried out by a third party unrelated to the clinical research.
In relation to pseudonymized data, it must be clearly and separately established those cases in which reversal will be possible, and since this involves processing data that undoubtedly affects the content of the data subject's rights, the applicable legal basis for such processing must be indicated if it differs from those already informed (for example, it could be the location of a participant under Article 9.2 c) of the GDPR, with the aim of protecting the vital interests of the affected individual).
