The inquiry refers to the request received by the National Commission of Markets and Competition, originating from the Government Delegation Against Gender Violence of the Secretariat of Equality and Against Gender Violence of the Ministry of Equality, through which it is requested that the mobile and landline phone numbers of a sample of women be communicated in order to conduct the European Survey on Gender Violence by telephone, which is part of the current National Statistical Plan.  

In this regard, the Ministry of Equality justifies its request in the current pandemic scenario, stating that it would possibly need to conduct the surveys by telephone due to the material impossibility of carrying them out in person arising from circumstances such as limitations on provincial and interprovincial travel, possible refusal to enter homes, or the need to ensure safety distances.  

Additionally, the report from its Data Protection Delegate is attached, which outlines the main characteristics of the EEVG, the legal bases that would support the communication of the data, and includes a series of aspects related to the security measures adopted.  

Finally, the Ministry of Equality adds that the telephone interview will only be conducted if the pandemic makes it strictly necessary and if the response rate for personal interviews is low, although it adds that the phone numbers will also be used to encourage the online interview or to obtain an appointment for the in-person interview, clarifying that the phone numbers of those subscribers who have exercised their right to object to being included in the phone listings would not be included.  

I  
This inquiry raises, once again, the possible communication of personal data to the phone numbers of subscribers to the telephone service, which are in the possession of the CNMV for a specific purpose, with the aim of being processed for a different purpose to facilitate the conduct of surveys included in the National Statistical Plan, considering the limitations arising from the current pandemic scenario.

I
As this Agency has repeatedly pointed out in successive reports issued during the state of alarm, this declaration does not imply the suspension of the fundamental right to data protection, without prejudice to the fact that it may be affected as a consequence of the causal event that led to the declaration, with the GDPR itself providing the appropriate channels that allow for a response to the health crisis situation while respecting the fundamental right to the protection of personal data. Therefore, any processing of personal data that must be carried out as a result of the pandemic and the declaration of the state of alarm must respect the fundamental right to the protection of personal data, in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, which allows, as we have seen, the establishment of specific rules regarding the exercise of that right, in line with the GDPR itself, as well as the doctrine developed by the Constitutional Court in interpreting Article 18.4 of our Constitution, particularly concerning the principle of proportionality.
Based on the above, addressing the strict terms of the consultation, and referring to the communication of the telephone numbers held by the CNMC, it is necessary to first analyze the legal basis under which the CNMC can proceed with the processing of personal data of subscribers to electronic communications services, as well as the specific purpose for which such data is collected and processed.

Second, the phone numbers of those individuals who have exercised their right not to appear in publicly accessible directories will not be included in the list provided by the CNMC.

IV
Finally, considering the urgency with which this report is requested given the proximity of the electoral processes to which the consultation refers, which would require adopting exceptional measures, it must be avoided, in any case, that such exceptional measures can, without further ado, be generalized once this period has ended, and in light of the possibility of using other alternative measures, as already indicated in Report 36/2020, a criterion of prudence must prevail that allows for a calm analysis of their implications and, in any case, regarding the competencies of this Agency, a rigorous study of the risks to the rights and freedoms that these treatments entail and the necessary guarantees to protect the right to personal data protection, adhering to the principle of proactive responsibility. 
Therefore, adhering to the principle of prudence already pointed out, as well as to what has been expressed by the consultant regarding the Annual Program 2020 of the National Statistical Plan 2017-2020, whose section 28 recommends promoting multichannel data collection in household surveys through electronic and telephone means, taking into account the evolution of society in the use of the Internet and the effectiveness of telephone data collection, this Agency considers it necessary that, in the event that the generalization of surveys through electronic and telephone means is foreseen, the corresponding legislative modification that guarantees legal security and the adequacy of the corresponding personal data processing to the GDPR and the doctrine of the Constitutional Court be promoted, by establishing the specific guarantees deemed appropriate.

Therefore, in accordance with the provisions of the specific regulations, it is evident that, in this case, we are faced with a situation of mandatory completion statistics as it is included in the National Statistical Plan approved by Royal Decree 410/2016, of October 31, which specifies that among the information to be provided, as indicated in the inquiry, is the telephone number as a quality control instrument for the actions carried out, such that “This variable thus becomes not only an initial contact instrument with the informant but is also part of the content of the questionnaire.”

And since it is a mandatory completion statistic, there is a legal obligation for the citizens previously selected in the survey sample to provide the requested information, which is classified in Article 50 of Law 12/1989 as a minor infraction “The submission or delay in sending data when it has not caused serious harm to the service, and there is an obligation to provide it,” as a serious infraction “The non-submission or delay in sending the required data when it causes serious harm to the service, and there is an obligation to provide it,” and as a very serious infraction “The notorious, habitual resistance or with the allegation of false excuses in sending the required data when there is an obligation to provide it.”

It is precisely the obligation to complete the survey that allows us to differentiate this case from others where participation is voluntary, such as those conducted by the Center for Sociological Research, since, involving the processing of special categories of data, Article 5 of Law 39/1995, of December 19, on the Organization of the Center for Sociological Research establishes the principle of voluntariness of responses, which requires that, in the event of intending to communicate telephone numbers by the CNMC to the CIS, additional guarantees must be adopted, as analyzed in Report 49/2020.

In the present case, as indicated in the report issued by the DPD, the survey involves the processing of personal data included within the special categories of data, which, in any case, will only be provided voluntarily. This voluntariness results from the provisions of Article 11.2 of Law 12/1989, of May 9, on Public Statistical Function, which states that "In any case, the data that may reveal ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or ideological beliefs, and, in general, any circumstances that may affect personal or family privacy shall be strictly voluntary and, consequently, may only be collected with the express consent of the interested parties."

Therefore, since it is a survey to be completed voluntarily by the affected parties, for the communication of the telephone numbers of the subscribers held by the CNMC to the CIS to proceed, additional guarantees must be adopted, in addition to the generic ones derived from the regulations on Public Statistical Function, including statistical confidentiality, as well as from the Code of Good Practice for European statistics. In this regard, according to the DPD's report and the consultation document itself, these additional guarantees would consist of limiting the sample so that the sample of telephone numbers does not reach 6% of the population during the initial sample collection period; conducting strict controls for anonymization and dissociation of data, so that the special categories of data will be maintained in a segregated file from the file of telephone numbers and other identifying data from the moment of the survey, making it practically impossible to associate that data with its owner, and securely deleting it once it is no longer necessary for the same purpose or for subsequent quality control work, without allowing any other use. It is also specifically provided that the numbers of those individuals who have exercised their right to object to being included in the telephone listings will not be included, and this guarantee should be understood to mean that they will not be provided by the CNMC.
