After examining your request for a report, submitted to this Legal Office, regarding the code of conduct for data processing in advertising activities presented by the Association for the Self-Regulation of Commercial Communication (“AUTOCONTROL”), I am pleased to inform you of the following:  

I  
This Legal Office has repeatedly highlighted, in the various inquiries submitted for its report, the paradigm shift that has resulted from the full application of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, regarding the protection of natural persons in relation to the processing of their personal data and the free movement of such data, as it is based on the principle of “accountability” or proactive responsibility, as stated in the Explanatory Memorandum of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the Guarantee of Digital Rights: “the greatest novelty presented by Regulation (EU) 2016/679 is the evolution from a model fundamentally based on compliance control to one that rests on the principle of active responsibility, which requires a prior assessment by the data controller or processor of the risk that the processing of personal data may generate, in order to adopt the appropriate measures based on that assessment.”  

Consequently, under this principle, the data controller must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is in compliance with the Regulation.  

Among the various instruments provided by the GDPR itself to ensure compliance with this principle are codes of conduct, which were already foreseen in previous regulations as a self-regulation mechanism aimed at facilitating compliance with the regulations on the protection of personal data. In this regard, the Article 29 Working Party, in its working document on the examination procedure for community codes of conduct, approved on September 10, 1998 (WP13), had already defined the essential elements of such codes, emphasizing that the codes should have sufficient clarity and internal coherence and provide sufficient added value, in terms of being sufficiently focused on the specific issues and problems of data protection in the organization or sector for which they are intended to be applied and offer solutions.

This regulation must be complemented by the provisions of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and the guarantee of digital rights, which dedicates Article 38 to codes of conduct, also referring to them as an element for identifying higher risks of processing in Article 28.2.h). The role of codes of conduct and their connection to the principle of proactive responsibility, as a means to demonstrate compliance with the GDPR, leads to the necessity of reviewing all previously approved codes, as outlined in the Guidelines of the European Data Protection Committee 1/2019 on codes of conduct and monitoring bodies regulated by Regulation 2016/679 (Guidelines 1/2019 on Codes of Conduct and Monitoring Bodies under Regulation 2016/679). It also highlights the benefits they can provide to supervisory authorities by allowing them to gain a better understanding and knowledge of the data processing activities of a specific industry, profession, or other sector. These guidelines identify some of the areas where codes of conduct can be useful, such as fair and transparent processing, legitimate interests, data security and protection by design and by default, and obligations of the data controller, noting that codes can have a broader or narrower scope depending on the particular sector, without the need to cover compliance with all legislation. Additionally, it identifies elements of their content that can help give them added value, such as the establishment of detailed guidelines for specific processing activities, best practices, practical solutions to problems identified in a specific sector, consideration of public concerns in general or perceived within the sector itself, etc.

Furthermore, the aforementioned Guidelines detail the criteria that must be taken into account by the supervisory authorities for the approval of the codes:
1) That it meets a particular need of that sector or processing activity, with the proposed solutions being beneficial not only for the controllers but also for the data subjects.
2) That it facilitates the application of the GDPR by identifying specific needs (for example, adapting the terminology of the sector).
3) That it specifies the application of the GDPR, focusing on sector-specific issues and providing added value, without merely reproducing the provisions of the GDPR.
4) That it provides effective mechanisms for monitoring compliance with the Code, both in terms of structures and procedures, with the existence of an accredited supervisory body being mandatory, except in the case of authorities and public bodies.

According to Article 40 of the GDPR, codes of conduct aim to contribute to the correct application of the GDPR, and must be substantiated in the documentation provided, in accordance with Guidelines 1/2019, justifying and establishing the basis for the approval of the code, describing the suitability of the proposed safeguards and mechanisms. 
This necessity is justified, as stated in the report of the General Subdirectorate of the General Data Protection Registry, by the growth of digital advertising, as a consequence of rapid technological evolution and the dynamism that characterizes the advertising sector itself, and the obligation to demonstrate proactive responsibility in the processing of personal data, contributing to compliance with the GDPR "by establishing, fundamentally, an extrajudicial procedure for resolving disputes between the units adhering to the Code and the interested parties."
b) Legitimacy, representativeness, and requirements of the application:
Article 40.1 of the GDPR, in its paragraph 2, recognizes legitimacy to promote codes of conduct to "associations and other representative bodies of categories of controllers or processors": According to Guidelines 1/2019, to assess representativeness, one may consider, among other criteria, the number or percentage of potential controllers or processors representative of a specific sector of activity that may adhere to the code and the experience of the representative body in relation to the specific sector and the processing activities that are the subject of the code. 
According to its Statutes, AUTOCONTROL is established as a voluntary non-profit association (Article 1) that aims to contribute to ensuring that commercial communication constitutes a particularly useful instrument in the economic process, safeguarding respect for advertising ethics and the rights of the recipients of commercial communications, excluding the defense of professional interests (Article 5), with public or private companies, whether advertisers, agencies, media, or other services related to commercial communication, as well as private entities and associations, whether business or professional, interested in the self-regulation of commercial communication being able to affiliate with it (Article 8).
As stated in the preamble of the code and reflected in the report of the General Subdirectorate of the General Data Protection Registry, "AUTOCONTROL is the representative association of the advertising sector in Spain to the extent that it is composed of the main advertisers, advertising agencies, media, and professional associations related to advertising activity (its members represent approximately 70% of advertising investment in Spain). 
Therefore, the promoting association of the code meets the representativeness requirements established by the GDPR. 
On the other hand, the formal requirements that the application must meet have also been fulfilled, in accordance with the provisions of Article 145.2 of the Regulation implementing Organic Law 15/1999, as they are included in it, as indicated by the report of the General Subdirectorate of the General Data Protection Registry:
- Code of Conduct for the processing of data in advertising activity
- Powers of representation of the General Director of AUTOCONTROL
- Regulations of the Advertising Jury
- Statutes of AUTOCONTROL
- Certification of the agreements adopted in the governing bodies of AUTOCONTROL, regarding the subscription of the code of conduct
- List of members of AUTOCONTROL
c) Scope of material application.
The code of conduct clearly and precisely identifies the processing of personal data to which it will apply, limited to data processing for advertising purposes, which it defines as "any processing (as defined in the GDPR) derived from or related to a communication made in the exercise of a commercial, industrial, craft, or professional activity, with the aim of promoting, directly or indirectly, the hiring of movable or immovable goods, services, rights, and obligations," including, by way of example, the following:
- Sending commercial communications.
- Promotions carried out to collect personal data for use for advertising purposes.
- Use of cookies and equivalent technologies for managing advertising spaces or conducting behavioral advertising.
- Creation of profiles for advertising purposes.
d) Scope of territorial application and competent supervisory authority.
The code limits its application "only to the processing carried out in Spanish territory by the adhering entities."
Legal Office
For these purposes, it should be noted that, according to the Statutes of AUTOCONTROL, “companies, entities, or associations that meet the requirements established in these Statutes and exist in Spain or in any other State” may affiliate with it. 
Based on the above, the territorial delimitation contained in the code of conduct does not strictly coincide with the territorial scope of application of personal data protection regulations, which does not depend on the location where the processing is materially carried out, but rather, essentially, on the fact that the processing is carried out in the context of the activities of a data controller or processor located in Spanish territory. 
This implies that it is possible for the processing to be fully developed materially outside Spanish territory but to occur in the context of the activity of an associated entity located in this territory, for example, by having contracted a processor from its group located in a third state. 
Furthermore, Article 3 of the General Data Protection Regulation includes other criteria for territorial application, such as the case where the processing relates to data subjects residing in the Union and is carried out by a controller or processor not established in the Union, “when the processing activities are related to a) the offering of goods or services to such data subjects in the Union, regardless of whether payment is required from them, or b) the monitoring of their behavior, insofar as this takes place in the Union.” 
Therefore, the territorial scope of the code should be modified to align it with the regime contained in Article 3 of the GDPR, without prejudice to maintaining its national character and the competence of the AEPD as long as its application is limited to processing activities carried out in the context of the activities of a controller or processor located in Spanish territory or that affect data subjects residing in Spain. 
d) Consultation with stakeholders: 
The Code has been submitted to the involved parties (entities in the advertising sector) in a General Assembly meeting held on June 7, 2018, and in Board meetings on July 12 and October 18, 2018.

Regarding the content of the code of conduct, the GDPR has identified certain areas that it may cover in Article 40.2: 
2. Associations and other representative bodies of categories of controllers or processors may develop codes of conduct or modify or expand such codes in order to specify the application of this Regulation, particularly regarding: 
a) fair and transparent processing; 
b) the legitimate interests pursued by controllers in specific contexts; 
c) the collection of personal data; 
d) the pseudonymization of personal data; 
e) the information provided to the public and to data subjects; 
f) the exercise of the rights of data subjects; 
g) the information provided to children and their protection, as well as the manner of obtaining consent from those holding parental responsibility or guardianship over the child; 
h) the measures and procedures referred to in Articles 24 and 25 and the measures to ensure the security of processing referred to in Article 32; 
i) the notification of personal data security breaches to supervisory authorities and the communication of such breaches to data subjects; 
j) the transfer of personal data to third countries or international organizations; or 
k) extrajudicial procedures and other dispute resolution procedures that allow for resolving disputes between controllers and data subjects regarding processing, without prejudice to the rights of data subjects under Articles 77 and 79. 
At this point, it should be emphasized that the GDPR does not identify a minimum content for codes of conduct, but rather it is an enumerative list of subjects that they may address and does not exhaust all the cases that may arise to facilitate the application of the GDPR, unlike what was done in the 1999 LOPD Development Regulation, which required that model codes include the content referred to in Article 73 of the LOPD Regulation, which involved the entire lifecycle of personal data, a supervision procedure, and a list of adherents, in accordance with Articles 75 and 76.

b) Extrajudicial procedure for the resolution of disputes 
As mentioned at the beginning of this report, the most relevant aspect of the code is the establishment of a mediation procedure to resolve conflicts that arise in advertising matters between the interested parties and the adhering entities, whether they are data controllers or data processors, as nothing prevents claims from being made against both obligated subjects, nor does the code specify this, so the conflict resolution system must apply to both. 

The procedure established is structured in 2 instances, a first mediation instance, which is carried out by the Mediation Unit of AUTOCONTROL, or through the data protection officer if the adhering entity has designated one and accepts mediation. It also provides that the control authority may, pursuant to the provisions of Article 65.4 of the LPDPGDD, refer to it for processing the claims it receives from affected parties against any of the entities adhering to the code of conduct. 
A second instance is for cases where either no agreement has been reached in mediation, either by the mediator or by the DPO, or the affected party considers the outcome of the mediation unsatisfactory, due to lack of agreement or disagreement with the proposed solution, which is resolved by the Advertising Jury. 
The procedure includes criteria for the admissibility of the claim, including that it is not being processed in an administrative and judicial procedure, and establishes a mechanism to rectify any defects that the claim may have. 
The procedure before the Advertising Jury is governed by the regulations established in its Regulation, which responds to the principles of contradiction, impartiality, transparency, efficiency, speed, and fairness.

According to what is stated in the aforementioned report, the content of this code of conduct provides sufficient added value to comply with the GDPR and sufficient guarantees, particularly regarding what constitutes its essential content, the establishment of an extrajudicial procedure for conflicts that may arise regarding personal data protection between the entities adhering to the code and the interested parties. 

It also adds value in other aspects duly analyzed in the report, such as the application of the principle of data protection by design and by default and the application, as the basis for processing, of consent and legitimate interest, providing some criteria for its appreciation, with examples and additional guarantees, that align with the criteria expressed by the EDPB and this Agency.

However, considering the impact that personal data protection has in the advertising field, derived from the increase in new technologies and the growth of digital advertising based on the profiles of those affected, as noted in the code itself to justify its necessity, it would be desirable for a continuous review of it to adapt it to the evolution of these technologies and to expand this part of its content, which would also allow for an increase in the actions of the Advertising Jury as the supervisory body of the code, since, as recalled by the Judgment of the Provincial Court of Madrid (Section 28) No. 208/2014 of June 27, "to the extent that the Advertising Jury is a self-control or self-discipline body, its resolutions must be based on the conduct rules approved by the association and not on legal norms."

b) CONFLICT OF INTERESTS
26.- The exercise of the functions of the supervisory body must not give rise to conflicts of interest.
27.- The members of the Advertising Jury are subject to a regime aimed at avoiding conflicts of interest:
• Members of the Advertising Jury must not have maintained any employment, commercial, organizational, or other type of relationship with the parties involved in a litigation in which they participate.
• Their mandate is for a fixed period of time and they cannot be removed without just cause.
• They must not receive instructions from any of the parties involved in a litigation in which they participate, nor from their representatives, nor maintain or have maintained any relationship with them during the three years prior.
• The remuneration of the members of the Advertising Jury shall not be related to the outcome of the procedure.
• Article 1.2 of the Advertising Jury Regulations establishes the obligation of its members to abstain in case of a conflict of interest.
• Article 14 of the Regulations of the Jury establishes the causes that give rise to the recusal and abstention of its members, which concludes with any other that may generate a conflict of interest or may raise doubts about their independence and impartiality.
• The parties to the litigation and the bodies of AUTOCONTROL may exercise recusal, and if deemed appropriate, a substitute is designated from among the members of the corresponding Section of the Jury.
28.- The characteristics of the procedure for appointing the members of the Advertising Jury, the regime of incompatibilities for performing their function and the remuneration they receive, the terms and guarantees of their mandate, the obligation to abstain in any circumstance that may give rise to a conflict of interest, and the possibility of recusal for the same reasons, as well as the substitution of the member who abstains or is subject to recusal, allow, in accordance with the accreditation criteria of the AEPD, to consider that the Advertising Jury is subject to measures and procedures that ensure its impartiality and the absence of conflicts of interest.

h) LEGAL CONDITION
60.- The supervisory body is not responsible for breaches of the GDPR committed by the members of the code, but it can be sanctioned for failing to fulfill its supervisory functions or for not taking appropriate measures when the rules of the code of conduct are violated, so it must provide all necessary information to assume responsibility in case of non-compliance, as provided in Article 41.4 of the GDPR, and face the administrative fines referred to in Article 83.4.c) of the GDPR.
61.- Regarding this requirement, it is worth clarifying that although the Spanish version of Article 83.4.c) of the GDPR refers to the breach of "The obligations of the supervisory authority under Article 41, paragraph 4.", it is evidently a translation error since, according to the definitions in Article 4 of the GDPR, the supervisory authority is "the independent public authority established by a Member State in accordance with the provisions of Article 51", and it is not referring to the supervisory authority, which holds the sanctioning power, being able to sanction itself, but to the supervisory body of the codes of conduct. In the English version of the GDPR, Article 83.4.c) states the following: "c) The obligations of the monitoring body pursuant to Article 41 (4)", and the EDPB Guidelines make it clear by stating that among the requirements that monitoring bodies must meet is to demonstrate that they have the appropriate status to be subject to sanctions in accordance with Article 83.4.c) of the GDPR. For its part, the LOPDPGDD in its Article 70 states that accredited entities supervising codes of conduct are subject to the sanctioning regime, and among the sanctionable infringements listed in Article 73 are the following: "ab) The performance of functions that Article 41 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 reserves for the supervisory bodies of codes of conduct without having been previously accredited by the competent data protection authority.
