The inquiry raises the question of whether, in accordance with the background described therein, the publication of the individual productivity of the civil servants of the consulting body is compliant with data protection regulations.  

As stated, this publication arises from the right of access recognized in Article 23.3.c) of Law 30/1984, of August 2, and should be materialized through the physical display of the lists, in accordance with the criteria set forth in various reports from the Agency itself. However, the possibility is raised as to whether the aforementioned information can or cannot be published on the corporate intranet of the consulting body.  

In any case, it is emphasized that -given the current situation caused by COVID-19 and considering that the staff of the consulting entity provides services in a non-presential manner- the publication of the list of individual productivity on the aforementioned corporate intranet should proceed, at least as an exceptional measure. Finally, for this case, it is questioned under what conditions the publication should be carried out and what data should be subject to publication.  

I  
Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, regarding the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation -GDPR-), and Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights -LOPDGDD- constitute the legal framework of reference in Spain that affects the protection of personal data. These regulations govern the principles and foundations to which the collection and processing of personal data by any public or private entity that carries out personal data processing in the exercise of its activity must adhere.  

It should be noted that data protection regulations contemplate different legal bases for legitimization that may lead to the processing of personal data. According to Article 6 -“Lawfulness of processing”- of the GDPR, such processing is lawful, and therefore legitimate when:  
“1. Processing shall be lawful only if at least one of the following applies:  
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;  
b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;  
c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject; (the bold is ours)  
d) processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;  
e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;  
f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular when the data subject is a child.  
The provision in point f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.”  

The issues raised in the inquiry have been analyzed by the Agency in several reports -including reports No. 123/2017, 137/2010, 183/2018, and 36/2019-, in which the advisability of reconciling the right of civil servants to know the allocation of the productivity supplement with their right to the protection of their personal data is noted, avoiding the public exposure of the information, but without limiting access to it.  

In any case, regarding the publicity of the productivity of public officials, this Agency has considered it necessary that access to the data does not lead to subsequent processing that may be contrary to the provisions of data protection legislation or generate situations that may jeopardize the rights of employees. This could be achieved, for example, by establishing systems that, while ensuring free access to the information and the transparency required by Article 23.3 c), do not allow the reproduction of the data.  

The aforementioned reports emphasize that from this provision derives a specific legal obligation regarding public knowledge -in favor of other civil servants of a specific administrative body- of the amounts received by each civil servant of that body as a productivity supplement. Conversely, such access should not occur for any other person -not a civil servant in the service of the consulting body- even in the event that they perform their professional work within their area of action, as occurs, for example, with non-civil servant public employees (labor personnel).

As inferred, in the case at hand, the processing is necessary for the fulfillment of a legal obligation applicable to the data controller, with the legal basis provided in letter c) of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, and such obligation being established in a norm with the rank of law, in accordance with Article 8 of the LPDPGDD.  
And this is because - as stated in our report 74/2019 - “the legal basis provided in letter c) of Article 6.1 of the GDPR applies in those cases where a norm with the rank of law imposes a specific obligation on the Administration to give, do, or not do, which involves the processing of personal data, and different from the generic legal duty of the Administration to exercise the powers that the legal system attributes to it to serve the public interest objectively (Article 103 of the Constitution).”  
II  
In its reports, the Agency has considered that, as a preferred means for proceeding with such publication, it should take place in a physically restricted access space in favor of those persons authorized for such access, and that necessary measures should be adopted to prevent public knowledge by the rest of the public employees and/or third parties, being carried out in a manner that implies the least interference with the rights and freedoms of the interested parties. This premise excludes the possibility of a generalized knowledge of said remuneration supplement, as could occur if it were published on the internet, where the risk would also increase as a result of possible indexing by search engines.  
However, as stated, Article 23.3 c) of Law 30/1984, of August 2, provides that “In any case, the amounts received by each official for this concept shall be public knowledge to the other officials of the interested Department or Agency (...),” without this legal provision being able to be overlooked due to the current situation caused by COVID-19, especially considering that the staff of the consulting entity is currently providing their services in a non-presential modality.  
From the above, it follows that the publication of personal data corresponding to the granting of the productivity supplement through its publication on the administrative intranet of the consulting body is in line with the provisions of section c) of Article 6.1 of the GDPR.  
However, this publication must be carried out in a private space of said intranet, so that only the official personnel can access the data, adopting the appropriate security measures to prevent the reproduction of the data as well as unauthorized access to it, having

Considering the provisions of Royal Decree 3/2010, of January 8, which regulates the National Security Framework in the field of Electronic Administration.
In summary, although, by virtue of the principle of proactivity - pursuant to Article 5.2 of the GDPR - and given that the consulting body holds the status of data controller in accordance with the GDPR, it is up to this body to assess the necessity of proceeding with the publication on its administrative intranet of the salary supplement related to the "productivity" of the officials, taking into account the criteria indicated, in our opinion - considering the current situation caused by COVID-19 and taking into account that the staff of the consulting body provides services in a non-presential manner - the option of publishing the productivity of the official staff on the administrative intranet is deemed balanced and compliant with data protection regulations.
In any case, as has been repeatedly stated, such access option will correspond solely to the official staff of that body, ensuring that the salary information is not found in an open space that can be consulted by anyone who does not meet that condition. To this end, the necessary organizational and technical measures must be adopted to prevent access to other personal data and the individual identification of each worker by unauthorized persons, ensuring that the confidentiality of such data is sufficiently guaranteed.
Furthermore, it should be noted that - once the current situation caused by COVID-19 has been overcome - the consulting body must evaluate the possibility of continuing to provide access to the personal information referred to in this report through its administrative intranet. In such a case, it must be ensured that the referred access is carried out under the same terms as previously mentioned and under appropriate security conditions. 
III
In all these cases, the publication of the data must comply with the purposes that justified its processing, and, as required by Article 5 of the GDPR, must be carried out with full respect for the principles of data minimization, limitation of the retention period, and integrity and confidentiality, so that the publication represents the least interference with the rights and freedoms of the data subjects:
“Article 5 Principles relating to processing
1. Personal data shall be:
a) processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject (“lawfulness, fairness, and transparency”);

In this regard, personal data may only be collected for processing, as well as subjected to such processing, when they are adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the specific, explicit, and legitimate purposes for which they have been obtained. Furthermore, in accordance with the principle of “minimization,” the publication of such remuneration data must be limited to the specific identification of the individuals affected by indicating their name and surname, and to the mention of the amount received and the time period to which such economic perception refers, without the publication of their ID number being permitted, in accordance with the provisions of paragraph 1, first sentence of the seventh additional provision of the LOPDGDD. Therefore, only in the case of a match of the name and surname, may four random digits of their national identity document be published.  
Precisely, in order to introduce the necessary guarantees in the processing of the ID by Public Administrations, the LOPDGDD introduced the aforementioned specific regulation, based on the interference that the joint knowledge of a person's name and surname and ID may pose to the fundamental right to the protection of personal data, in addition to the significant risk of identity theft that may occur.

Furthermore, the publication of personal data on the administrative intranet of the consulting body must comply with the strict fulfillment of the provisions of Article 5.1 b) of the GDPR, and personal data subject to processing may not be used for purposes incompatible with those for which the data were collected.  
In turn, in accordance with the provisions of Article 5.1 e) of the GDPR, the publication of such personal data on the aforementioned administrative intranet must be removed—proceeding to delete the published data—when they are no longer necessary or relevant for the purpose that motivated such publication.  
Finally, it must be emphasized the need for the competent bodies to promote the corresponding regulatory modification that guarantees the necessary legal security, required for the better interpretation and application of the mandate contained in Article 23.3.c) of Law 30/1984, of August 2.
