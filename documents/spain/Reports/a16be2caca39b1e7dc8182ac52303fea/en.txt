The inquiry, accompanied by the report issued by the data protection delegate of the ministry, raises the need for the National Commission on Markets and Competition (CNMC) to send the phone numbers of the individuals selected in the sample of the Sports Habits Survey 2020, conducted by the National Institute of Statistics (INE).  

The inquiry justifies its request on the grounds that it is a mandatory completion statistic, invoking what this Agency stated in its Report 70/2015, and that as a consequence of the social and health situation caused by the COVID-19 coronavirus pandemic and the restrictions arising from the measures adopted by the autonomous communities regarding public health, it is not possible to conduct the survey in person.  

It is also specified that the phone number is part of the information requested in the survey questionnaire as a quality control instrument for the actions carried out, that its use would be limited exclusively to conducting the fieldwork of the Survey, so that it would not be subject to additional processing, and it would be destroyed once the quality approval of the report is granted, ensuring, likewise, the confidentiality of the processing as it is subject to statistical secrecy under Law 12/1989, of May 9, on Public Statistical Function.  

The inquiry refers to the preparation of an official statistic, of mandatory completion, included in the National Statistical Plan and executed by the state statistical services, for which it is necessary, given the restrictions arising from the COVID-19 pandemic, to obtain the phone numbers of the respondents in advance from the CNMC, which implies, as indicated in this Agency's Report 49/2020, the communication of personal data for a purpose different from that for which it was obtained. Since it is a communication of data between administrative bodies, it must first comply with the provisions of its specific regulations, constituted, in this case, by Law 12/1989, of May 9, on Public Statistical Function, whose Article 23 includes, within the State Statistical Services, in addition to the INE and the Superior Council of Statistics, "the units of the different ministerial departments and any other public entities dependent on it to which that function has been entrusted."

Consequently, in accordance with the provisions of the specific regulations, it follows that, in the present case, we are faced with a mandatory completion statistic as it is included in the National Statistical Plan approved by Royal Decree 410/2016, of October 31, indicating among the information that must be provided, as stated in the inquiry, the telephone number as a quality control instrument for the actions carried out, so that “This variable thus becomes not only an initial contact instrument with the informant but is also part of the content of the questionnaire.”

And since it is a mandatory completion statistic, there is a legal obligation for the citizens previously selected in the survey sample to provide the requested information, classified in Article 50 of Law 12/1989 as a minor infraction: “The sending or delay in sending data when it has not caused serious harm to the service, and there is an obligation to provide it,” as a serious infraction: “The failure to send or delay in sending the required data when it causes serious harm to the service, and there is an obligation to provide it,” and as a very serious infraction: “The notable, habitual resistance, or with the allegation of false excuses in sending the required data, when there is an obligation to provide it.”

It is precisely the obligation to complete the survey that allows us to differentiate this case from others where participation is voluntary, such as those conducted by the Center for Sociological Research, since, involving the processing of special categories of data, Article 5 of Law 39/1995, of December 19, on the Organization of the Center for Sociological Research, establishes the principle of voluntariness of responses, which requires that, in the case of intending to communicate telephone numbers by the CNMC to the CIS, additional guarantees must be adopted, as analyzed in Report 49/2020.

Therefore, in the present case, the communication of the data of the subscribers to the telephone service by the CNMC for the preparation of an official statistic, of mandatory completion, included in the National Statistical Plan and executed by the State statistical services would find its basis in Article 6.1.e) of the GDPR (“the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller”).

In any case, the data controller must comply with the principles established in Article 5 of the GDPR, noting in the inquiry that the use of these phones would be limited exclusively to conducting the fieldwork of the Survey, so that it would not be subject to additional processing, adhering to the principles of purpose limitation and data minimization, and that the principle of storage limitation would also be guaranteed by destroying the provided telephone numbers once the quality approval of the report has been given.

Additionally, the other guarantees inherent to the exercise of the statistical function must be added, such as adherence to statistical confidentiality or the dissociation of the data from the moment the survey is conducted, separating the responses given by the respondents from the telephone numbers, as well as the other appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure respect for the rights and freedoms of the affected individuals and the adequacy of the processing to the regulations on personal data protection that the Ministry of Culture, as the data controller, must adopt, in accordance with the principle of proactive responsibility.

For all these reasons, this Agency understands that the communication of telephone numbers by the CNMC to the statistical services of the Ministry of Culture, under the terms provided in this report, is covered by the GDPR as long as, as already indicated in Report 70/2015, the data is used solely for the purpose of conducting the statistic for which it is requested.

All this without prejudice to the fact that, based on the inquiry regarding the restrictions arising from the COVID-19 pandemic and as previously mentioned in Report 49/2020, it is recommended in section 28 of the 2020 Annual Program of the National Statistical Plan 2017-2020, to promote multichannel data collection in household surveys through electronic and telephone means, taking into account the evolution of society in the use of the Internet and the effectiveness of telephone data collection, this Agency considers it necessary that, in the event that the generalization of surveys through electronic and telephone means is anticipated, the corresponding legislative modification be promoted to ensure legal certainty and the adequacy of the corresponding personal data processing to the GDPR and the doctrine of the Constitutional Court, by establishing the specific guarantees deemed appropriate.
