The inquiry raises different issues related to the publication of grades for subjects taught in undergraduate studies.

I

During the validity of Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13, on the Protection of Personal Data, this Agency repeatedly stated the necessity of obtaining the unequivocal consent of students in order to proceed with the publication of grades. In this regard, report 469/2002 on "Publication on bulletin boards of university students' grades" indicated the following:

"In general terms, it can be stated that the publication of the data referred to in the inquiry (both related to the name and surname of the students, as well as their National Identity Document number or their Personal File Number) constitutes a genuine transfer of personal data, defined by article 3 i) of Organic Law 15/1999, as 'any disclosure of data made to a person other than the data subject.'

Regarding transfers, article 11.1 of the same Law establishes as a general rule that 'the personal data subject to processing may only be communicated to a third party for the fulfillment of purposes directly related to the legitimate functions of the transferor and the transferee with the prior consent of the data subject.' Among the exceptions to the necessity of such consent included in article 11.2 is the case where the transfer is authorized by law.

In this sense, the obligation to notify the interested parties of administrative resolutions that affect their rights and interests is established in article 58 of Law 30/1992, of November 26, on the Legal Regime of Public Administrations and the Common Administrative Procedure.

In this provision and in the following article, the cases in which such notification will occur differently from personal notification, through the publication of the act on bulletin boards or announcements, are exhaustively regulated. Specifically, in article 59.6.b) of Law 30/1992, of November 26, it is established that publication will replace notification when it concerns acts that are part of a selection process.

Consequently, regarding the possibility of publishing lists of candidates with the results of a selection process, the provisions of the aforementioned articles 58 and 59 of Law 30/1992, of November 26, on the Legal Regime of Public Administrations and the Common Administrative Procedure will apply, making it possible to publish personal data, provided that the call expressly determines the place of publication, and governed by the principle of publicity derived from the application of the provisions in the mentioned precepts.

However, the case submitted for consultation, that is, the publication of students' grades on the bulletin boards of the consulting University, is not covered by what is established in the aforementioned precepts, since in this case we are not dealing with a selection process, but rather a form of communication and/or transfer of the grades corresponding to each subject, which are intended solely for the affected students, being recorded – in turn – in their academic file.

Consequently, the dissemination of such grades through the bulletin boards of the University will constitute an unauthorized transfer of personal data of the students not authorized by a norm with the rank of formal law. In that case, according to the regulation of the Organic Law (article 11.2.a), it would be necessary for each student to give their unequivocal consent to publish the grades, since this case does not constitute any of the legal exceptions to carry out transfers without consent.

Furthermore, since the aforementioned publication does not comply with the provisions of Organic Law 15/1999, it is unnecessary to analyze the appropriateness of including certain types of data (D.N.I, Personal File Number of the students, or any other) referred to by the inquirer in their writing, when the consent of the affected party has not been duly obtained."

This situation was modified by the twenty-first additional provision of Organic Law 4/2007, of April 12, amending Organic Law 6/2001, of December 21, on Universities, which establishes that "the consent of students will not be necessary for the publication of the results of tests related to the evaluation of their knowledge and competencies nor of the acts that are necessary for the proper execution and monitoring of such evaluation."

Thus, as this Agency already pointed out in its report 178/2014, "in this way, the legislator would be recognizing the possibility for university centers to appreciate the existence of a public interest in the widespread knowledge of the results of the aforementioned evaluations that...

At the time of issuing this report, the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, which repeals Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation, GDPR), must be adhered to, fully applicable since May 25, 2018, and to Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD). 

The aforementioned Regulation extends its protection, as established in its 
Article 1.2, to the fundamental rights and freedoms of natural persons and, in particular, their right to the protection of personal data, defined in its Article 4.1 as “any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘the data subject’); an identifiable natural person is one whose identity can be determined, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific elements of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.” 

Furthermore, Article 4.1 defines “processing” as “any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.” 

Therefore, the publication of students' grades, to the extent that it contains personal data relating to natural persons, is subject to the provisions of the GDPR. 

On the other hand, it is necessary to recall, as has been indicated on numerous occasions by this Agency, that the reform brought about by the General Data Protection Regulation regarding the regime contained in Organic Law 15/1999 requires a change in perspective concerning the principles that underpin the fundamental right to the protection of personal data and, in particular, the one that made the “principle of consent” the central axis of the right to data protection.

Indeed, while the Organic Law and the Regulation do not differ excessively regarding the enumeration of the legitimizing grounds for processing, there is a highly relevant modification in the way these grounds are presented in the applicable texts: thus, while from the wording of Organic Law 15/1999 it seemed to be deduced that the basic rule of legitimization was, in general, consent, with the remaining legitimizing grounds being exceptional in relation to consent, which as a general rule should govern processing, in the text of Article 6.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation, consent is presented as one of the six grounds for legitimization for processing without holding greater or lesser importance than the others enumerated in the regulation. 

Furthermore, the General Data Protection Regulation itself makes it clear that the consent of the data subject should not constitute the legal basis for processing in certain cases. Thus, Recital 42 states in its last sentence that “Consent should not be considered freely given when the data subject does not have genuine or free choice or cannot refuse or withdraw consent without detriment” and Recital 43 adds that “To ensure that consent has been given freely, it should not constitute a valid legal basis for the processing of personal data in a specific case where there is a clear imbalance between the data subject and the data controller, particularly when the controller is a public authority and it is therefore unlikely that consent has been given freely in all circumstances of that particular situation.” 

In this way, it is not appropriate to seek the consent of the data subject in any case where the processing is covered by any of the grounds included in letters b) to f) of Article 6.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation; that is to say when: 

b) the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or for the implementation at the request of the data subject of pre-contractual measures; 

c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject; 

d) the processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person; 

e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller;

f) the processing is necessary for the satisfaction of legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, provided that those interests do not prevail over the interests or the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject requiring the protection of personal data, particularly when the data subject is a child.

In this case, Article 1.1 of Organic Law 6/2001, of December 21, on Universities qualifies higher education as a public service, stating that “the University performs the public service of higher education through research, teaching, and study,” a qualification that is reiterated in Articles 27 bis 1.b) and 31.1.a). By virtue of university autonomy, it is the responsibility of the Universities to “verify the knowledge of students” (Article 2.2.f), reiterating Article 46.3 that “Universities shall establish the procedures for verifying the knowledge of students.”

Therefore, as noted in report 178/2014, the legislator has recognized the existence of a public interest, so the publication of university grades would find its legal basis in what is provided in Article 6.1.e) of the GDPR (the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller) derived from a competence attributed by a norm with the rank of law in accordance with Article 8.2 of the LOPDGDD.

On the other hand, even though the evaluation procedures are not competitive bidding procedures, the grades obtained will have an impact, as raised in the consultation, on the granting of honors limited to a number of students, as well as on the awarding of extraordinary prizes, so a legitimate interest of the students in the group in knowing the grades of their peers could also be appreciated, under the provisions of letter f) of Article 6.1. of the GDPR.

II

Therefore, since the publication of the grades obtained is lawful in accordance with the indications in the previous section, the principles set forth in Article 5 of the GDPR must be respected in all cases, especially those of purpose limitation, data minimization, storage limitation, integrity, and confidentiality, publishing in such a way that it entails the least interference with the rights and freedoms of the data subjects, which excludes the possibility of widespread knowledge of the grades, as could occur if they were published on the internet, where the risk would also increase due to possible indexing by search engines.

Therefore, it is considered a preferred means for proceeding with such publication that it be carried out on an intranet or virtual classroom where access is limited to teachers and group colleagues. In the event that this is not possible, it may be done on the notice boards of the center, provided that they are not located in common areas of the centers, that access to them is restricted to those individuals, and that necessary measures are taken to prevent public knowledge by those who have no interest in the same.

Regarding the data to be published, in accordance with the principle of minimization, it should be limited to the student's first and last name and the grade obtained. Only in the case that there are students with the same first and last names, the ID number must be published for them, applying what is provided in section 1, first paragraph of the seventh additional provision of the LOPDGDD. Therefore, only in the case of a match of the first and last names, four random digits from their national identity document, foreign identity number, passport, or equivalent document will be published, with this Agency recommending that the criteria contained in the document "Guidance for the provisional application of the seventh additional provision of the LOPDGDD," available at www.aepd.es, be followed.

As for the duration for which such publication must be maintained, the data must be "kept in a manner that allows the identification of the interested parties for no longer than necessary for the purposes of processing personal data" (Article 5.1.e). Therefore, in the case of provisional grades, as referred to in the inquiry, while the period for filing complaints is ongoing, and the definitive grades for the necessary time to ensure their knowledge by all interested parties.

In any case, by virtue of the university autonomy recognized in Article 27 of the Spanish Constitution and in Organic Law 6/2001, of December 21, on Universities, and with the consulting University holding the status of data controller under the GDPR, it will be the responsibility of the University to assess the necessity of proceeding with the publication of the grades and the manner in which it should be carried out, taking into account the criteria outlined in this report and the principle of proactive responsibility on which the current regulations on personal data protection are based.
