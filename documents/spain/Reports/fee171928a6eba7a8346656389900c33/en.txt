Upon examining your request for a report, submitted to this Legal Office, regarding the Draft instruction from the General Directorate of Registries and Notaries concerning the designation of the insolvency mediator and the communication of debtor data to reach an extrajudicial payment agreement, requested from this Spanish Agency for Data Protection in accordance with the provisions of articles 37 h) of the Organic Law, of December 13, on the Protection of Personal Data, and 5 b) of the Statute of the Agency, approved by Royal Decree 428/1993, of March 26, I am pleased to inform you of the following:

Before proceeding to analyze the text submitted for the report, it is necessary to point out that, given the legal basis of the report that will be provided immediately and its mandatory nature, pursuant to the provisions of the aforementioned regulations, it should be indicated in the Explanatory Memorandum of the regulation that it has been submitted to the prior report of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection.

**I**

The Draft submitted for the report aims, as indicated in its preamble, to establish the procedure for the designation of insolvency mediators in cases where the debtor initiates the procedure to reach an extrajudicial payment agreement under the terms established in Title X of Law 22/2003, of July 9, on Insolvency, as well as the communication to the mediator of the information related to the debtor that the latter will have provided in the form approved by Order JUS/2831/2015, of December 17, both prior to the acceptance of their designation and once it has occurred, and the communication of the aforementioned data to the insolvency registry and the scope of the publication that said registry will make of that information.

**II**

The first section of the Draft refers, in its first three paragraphs, to the initiation of the file as a consequence of the submission by the debtor of the form established in the aforementioned Order JUS/2831/2015, subsequently establishing the procedure for the designation of the mediator, in accordance with the requirements of article 233.1 of the Insolvency Law and the communication to the Official State Gazette of the designation, as well as the consequences of the mediator's failure to accept the position.

From the perspective of personal data protection regulations, the obtaining of information from individuals who hold the status of insolvency mediators is based on article 11.2 a) of Organic Law 15/1999 in connection with the aforementioned article 233.1 of the Insolvency Law, which clearly establishes that:

"The appointment of the insolvency mediator must fall on the natural or legal person sequentially corresponding from among those listed in the official list that will be published on the corresponding portal of the 'Official State Gazette', which will be provided by the Registry of Mediators and Mediation Institutions of the Ministry of Justice."

However, it would be necessary to clarify one of the mentions contained in the text of the first section of the Draft, as it indicates that the submission of the debtor's form "implies their consent for the processing of the included data."

Article 232.2 of the Insolvency Law provides that "The request shall be made using a standardized form signed by the debtor and shall include an inventory of the cash and liquid assets available, the goods and rights owned, and the expected regular income. It shall also be accompanied by a list of creditors, specifying their identity, address, and email address, indicating the amount and due date of the respective credits, which shall include a list of current contracts and a list of expected monthly expenses." The request necessarily implies the debtor's submission to the procedure, which also necessarily implies that the insolvency mediator can access information regarding their situation, given that the activities that the mediator must carry out necessarily involve knowledge of the information included in the application form.

This means that the debtor's consent is not required for the processing of their data within the framework of the extrajudicial procedure regulated by Title X of the Insolvency Law, as this processing is essential for the processing of the extrajudicial procedure provided for in the Law itself.

At the same time, it must be taken into account that the regulatory regime of the fundamental right to the protection of personal data is currently undergoing profound modification as a result of the approval of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation), which will be fully applicable on May 25, 2018.

Article 4.10 of the Regulation defines consent of the data subject as "any freely given, specific, informed, and unequivocal indication of the data subject's wishes."

III 

The second section of the Draft regulates the communication to the insolvency mediators of the debtor's data, differentiating between the moment after the designation but prior to the acceptance of the position, and the moment after such acceptance. 

In the first case, point 1 of the aforementioned section provides that only the identity data of the debtor and creditors will be provided to the mediator, regarding natural persons, including their names and surnames, NIF, address, and email, as well as their nationality in the case of foreigners and the marital economic regime. The estimation of the total amount of debts and the total value of the assets and rights listed in the application form will also be provided. 

Once the position has been accepted, all the data contained in the form will be provided to the insolvency mediator, as provided in point 2. 

The last paragraph of point 3 of the second section states that “The insolvency mediator, once they are communicated the debtor's data, is obliged to maintain the confidentiality of the same in accordance with Article 9 of Law 5/2012, of July 6, on mediation in civil and commercial matters.” 

In relation to this section, it must first be corroborated that, as previously indicated, the communication to the insolvency mediator of the data contained in the form is fully covered by Organic Law 15/1999, as it is necessary for the proper development of the procedure provided in Title X of the Insolvency Law. This provision would enable the two transfers, of different extent, that have been described previously. 

At the same time, the limitation of the data that will be subject to transfer prior to the acceptance of the position is, in our opinion, in accordance with the principle of proportionality (or, in the text of the new Regulation,

minimization) in the processing of personal data, as it is limited to the identifying data of debtors and creditors as well as the mere global estimation of debts and the value of the debtor's assets and rights.

In this regard, Article 4.1 of Organic Law 15/1999 states that “Personal data may only be collected for processing, as well as subjected to such processing, when they are adequate, relevant, and not excessive in relation to the scope and the specific, explicit, and legitimate purposes for which they have been obtained,” and Article 5.1 c) of the General Data Protection Regulation imposes that the data be “adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed.”

This proportionality derives from the fact that the information is communicated to the designated individual who has not accepted the position solely for the purpose of “verifying whether there is any cause for disqualification that prevents them from accepting the position, as well as (knowing) the information related to the basic characteristics of the insolvency situation.”

However, this provision should clearly be complemented by another that reflects the principle of purpose limitation in processing, currently enshrined in Article 4.2 of Organic Law 15/1999, which states that “Personal data subject to processing may not be used for purposes incompatible with those for which the data were collected.” This principle is also included in Article 5.1 b) of the General Data Protection Regulation.

Certainly, the last paragraph of point 3 of this section refers to the duty of confidentiality established in Article 9 of Law 5/2012, of July 6, on mediation in civil and commercial matters. However, in our opinion, this provision should be reinforced, not only by adding a reference in this paragraph to the duty of secrecy imposed by the current regulations on personal data protection but also by including a new paragraph that expressly enshrines the principle of purpose limitation in access to personal data by those who, having been appointed as administrators, do not accept the appointment, as well as by those who effectively carry out that function.

Therefore, it is proposed to add a point 4 to the second section of the Draft indicating the following:

“The data to which the insolvency mediator has accessed in accordance with section 2 may only be used and processed by them for the purposes expressly indicated in that section. The use of the data for any other purpose will not be possible.

Upon acceptance of the appointment, the insolvency mediator will only use and process the data to which they have accessed for the fulfillment of the functions established in Title X of Law 22/2003, of July 10, on Insolvency.”

IV

Finally, the third section of the Draft refers to the submission to the Public Insolvency Registry of data related to the existence of the procedure, reminding that Article 13.1 of Royal Decree 892/2013 limits this information to the identity data of the debtor, the dates of submission of the debtor's application, the opening of the procedure, and the acceptance of the mediator and the identity of the latter.

In view of this provision, the Draft incorporates two specific provisions aimed at limiting the information provided. Thus, first, it is stated that in no case will “the literal submission of notarial records, registrations, insolvency files of any kind, or others of the same nature, even as attachments to the submission of the data indicated in this Instruction” be admissible. It is indicated that “however, if they are submitted for statistical or similar purposes, in no case will they be subject to publication on the Insolvency Portal.”

Additionally, it is added that “the College of Property, Commercial, and Movable Goods Registrars of Spain must establish the necessary IT means so that the debtor's data published in the registry are only those referred to in section 1 of Article 13 of Royal Decree 892/2013, in compliance with the provisions of Articles 9 and 10 of Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13, on the Protection of Personal Data.”

As a starting point, the indicated rules are in line with the principles of proportionality and minimization of personal data, which have already been mentioned, limiting publication to the minimum essential data for the knowledge of the existence of a procedure aimed at achieving an extrajudicial agreement. However, it is necessary to make two comments regarding this section.

First, the express reference to Organic Law 15/1999 should be replaced with a general reference to personal data protection regulations, given that, as already indicated, it is about to be imminently modified as a result of the full application.
