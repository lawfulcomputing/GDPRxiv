The inquiry refers to a new request from the Center for Sociological Research (CIS) regarding the provision of certain telephone numbers of subscribers for the execution of the Health Barometer, which is part of the National Statistical Plan. However, due to the current pandemic situation, it is necessary to conduct this study by telephone, for which it requests the National Commission on Markets and Competition (CNMC) to authorize the transfer to the CIS of a sample of 312,000 random and anonymous telephone numbers (distributed randomly by province) for the execution of each of the three waves of the Health Barometer.  

As a consequence of the new request from the CIS, the CNMC requests a report on the following issues:  
- Whether the Health Barometer should be considered a mandatory survey for those affected, as it is expressly included in the PEN and conducted by an official statistical service, or if the nature of the study, whose questionnaire has previously included questions about possible hospital admissions or visits to health consultations, would imply the processing of personal data included within the special categories of data (such as health data) that can only be provided voluntarily.  
- If, taking the above into account, the communication by the CNMC to said body of the subscriber data provided by operators through the SGDA, who have not given their consent to be listed in subscriber directories or in services for consulting subscriber numbers, may comply with personal data protection regulations for the purposes stated by the CIS in this request, that is, to conduct the interviews that are part of the Health Barometer.

II

At the present time and as previously mentioned, this legislative amendment has occurred through the second final provision of Law 15/2021, of October 23, which has reworded Article 49.1 of the General Telecommunications Law, including among the cases in which there is an obligation to communicate the data appearing in subscriber telephone directories to official statistical services, in the following terms:
“[…] The National Commission on Markets and Competition must provide free of charge to the entities that will prepare subscriber telephone directories, to those providing telephone inquiry services on subscriber numbers, to those providing emergency call services, and to official statistical services for the preparation of surveys and the development of the statistical competencies conferred by law, the data provided by the operators, in accordance with the conditions established by royal decree.

The transfer of these data in favor of official statistical services must be based on the principles set forth in data protection regulations and, especially, on those of minimization and limitation of purpose.”

From the perspective of the fundamental right to the protection of personal data, the provision has undeniable significance, as these treatments will find their basis, after this amendment, in compliance with a legal obligation applicable to the data controller (Article 6.1.c) of the GDPR), so it will no longer be necessary to analyze the compatibility of the processing for a different purpose according to the criteria established in Article 6.4 of the GDPR, including the reasonable expectations of the affected individuals, as such processing is provided for in a norm with the rank of law. However, such processing must be carried out under the legally established terms, including compliance with the requirements that are expressly established by regulation, for which the provisions established in Royal Decree 424/2005, of April 15, which approves the Regulation on the conditions for the provision of electronic communications services, must be adhered to.

Regarding the regulation of telephone directories, Article 49.1 of the LGT establishes that:
“The preparation and marketing of subscriber guides to electronic communications services and the provision of information services about them shall be carried out under conditions of free competition.”
Furthermore, Article 48.3 establishes that:
“With respect to the protection of personal data and privacy in relation to subscriber guides, end users of electronic communications services shall have the following rights: a) To be included in the subscriber guides. b) To be informed free of charge about the inclusion of their data in the guides, as well as the purpose of the same, prior to such inclusion. c) To not be included in the guides or to request the omission of some of their data, to the extent that such data is relevant to the purpose of the guide stipulated by their provider.”
As for the duty to provide information for the free competition provision of telephone directory editing services or telephone inquiries, Article 49.1 of Law 9/2014 adds that:
“For this purpose, companies that assign telephone numbers to subscribers must respond to all reasonable requests for the provision of relevant information for the provision of information services about subscriber numbers and publicly accessible guides, in an approved format and under equitable, objective, cost-oriented, and non-discriminatory conditions, being subject to the provision of the aforementioned information and its subsequent use to the data protection regulations in force at any given time.”
For this reason, as this Agency has reiterated in different legal reports - including Report No. 0008/2020, of April 2, 2020 - “(…) the legislator explicitly specifies, in a norm with the rank of Law, the possibility that, in a subsequent regulatory development, operators may be required to provide the necessary data to fully comply with the rights/duties that it itself enshrines in the Law.”
Consequently, in the general context - to which the exercise of its competencies refers - the communication of data to the National Commission of Markets and Competition finds its foundation in the legal obligation to guarantee free competition in the market. For its part, the legal basis legitimizing the processing of the data thus obtained by the assignees of this information is rooted in the legitimate interest of these when they intend to provide the telephone directory service on subscriber numbers or information about subscriber numbers under conditions of free competition.
Within this specific regulatory framework, the successive communications of information containing personal data would be covered, under the regime in force since May 25, 2018, by paragraphs c) and f) of Article 6.1 of the aforementioned Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016 - GDPR and must comply with the regulatory standards that govern them: Royal Decree 424/2005, of April 15, which approves the Regulation on the conditions for the provision of electronic communications services, the universal service, and the protection of users, Order CTE/711/2002, of March 26, which establishes the conditions for the provision of the telephone inquiry service on subscriber numbers, and the Circular of the National Commission of Markets and Competition 1/2013, regarding the procedure for supplying subscriber data for the provision of guide services, telephone inquiries on subscriber numbers, and emergencies, amended by Circular 5/2014, of July 30.

Specifically, it must be taken into account what is provided in the second paragraph of Article 68.2 of Royal Decree 424/2005, which, in development of the right that subscribers have not to appear in directories or to request the omission of some of their data, as we have seen in Article 48.3 of the LGT, states that:
“The data concerning subscribers who have exercised their right not to appear in publicly accessible directories will only be provided to the entities that hold the emergency call service. For these purposes, emergency call services are understood to be those provided through the number 112 and any others determined by the Secretary of State for Telecommunications and the Information Society.”
Consequently, the primary purpose that legitimizes the communication of data to the CNMC is based on the legal obligation to guarantee free competition in the market and the legitimate interest of operators in providing directory services regarding subscriber numbers or information about subscriber numbers under conditions of free competition.
The regulatory regime for data protection in relation to subscriber directories for electronic communications services is set forth in Article 12 of Directive 2002/58/EC, which establishes the following:
“1. Member States shall ensure that subscribers are informed free of charge before being included in directories about the purposes of subscriber directories, whether printed or electronic, available to the public or accessible through information services about them, in which their personal data may be included, as well as any other possibility of use based on search functions incorporated in the electronic versions of the directory.
2. Member States shall ensure that subscribers have the opportunity to decide whether their personal data appears in a public directory, and if so, which of them, to the extent that such data is relevant to the purpose of the directory stipulated by its provider, and to verify, correct, or delete such data. Non-inclusion in a public subscriber directory, as well as the verification, correction, or deletion of personal data from a directory, shall not result in any charge.
3. Member States may require that for any purpose of a public directory other than the search for contact data of individuals based on their name and, if necessary, a minimum of other identifiers, the specific consent of subscribers is obtained.
4. Paragraphs 1 and 2 shall apply to subscribers who are natural persons. Member States shall also ensure, within the framework of Community law and applicable national legislation, sufficient protection of the legitimate interests of subscribers who are not natural persons regarding their inclusion in public directories.”
Consequently, in order to reconcile free competition in the market with the right to the protection of personal data of subscribers, the law includes in Article 48.3 the specific guarantees that ensure their proper protection in the terms provided in the aforementioned Directive 2002/58/EC, among which is the right to “not appear in directories or to request the omission of some of their data, to the extent that such data is relevant to the purpose of the directory stipulated by its provider.”
Furthermore, the regulatory framework is completed with the provisions of Articles 67 and 68 of the Regulation on the conditions for the provision of electronic communications services, the universal service, and the protection of users, approved by Royal Decree 424/2005, of April 15, clearly differentiating the matters related to the recognition of users' rights concerning the protection of their personal data in subscriber directories from the issues related to the provision of the service itself, with the former appearing in Article 67 and the latter in Article 68.

Therefore, our legal system establishes the obligation to provide location data to emergency services, based on this obligation, among other circumstances, on the need to protect the vital interest of the caller. 
Consequently, the legal basis for the communication of subscriber data to emergency services is found in letter d) of article 6.1. of the GDPR: “the processing is necessary to protect vital interests of the data subject or another natural person.” The concurrence of this legal basis presents unique connotations from the perspective of the legal regime of personal data protection, as reasoned in Report 17/2020: 
“Section 3 of article 6 GDPR does not establish the necessity for the basis of processing on the grounds of vital interest to be established by Union Law or the applicable Member State Law to the data controller, as this section refers exclusively to processing established for the fulfillment of a legal obligation, or for the fulfillment of a mission carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of public powers, both referred to in letters c) and e) of that article 6 GDPR, but not for the processing included in letter d).” 
IV 
Consequently, until the legal modification made by Law 15/2021, of October 23, article 49.1. LGTel only contemplated, on one hand, the communication of subscriber data to entities that will prepare subscriber telephone directories and to those providing telephone inquiry services about subscriber numbers, in order to guarantee free competition, subject, in any case, to the condition that the affected parties had not exercised their right not to appear in the directories; and, on the other hand, to emergency services, in order to ensure their proper management and the protection of the vital interests of the affected party or a third party, prevailing, in this case, such protection over the possible opposition of the affected party to appear in the directories. Consequently, while the general rule is the prevalence of the right not to appear in the directories, in the case of emergency calls, the vital interest prevails, and in this sense, the provision of article 68.2 of Royal Decree 424/2005, of April 15, should be understood.

Following the legal amendment, a new provision has been introduced regarding the communication of subscriber data: “to the official statistical services for the preparation of surveys and the development of the statistical competencies conferred by law.” In this case, such communication, grounded in a legal obligation, finds its basis in the exercise of competencies by the aforementioned services, thus legitimizing the processing of personal data by them under letter e) of Article 6.1 of the GDPR: “the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller.”  
Consequently, the communication of data would be based, with respect to the CNMC, on letter c) of Article 6.1 of the GDPR, and regarding the processing of data by the CIS, on letter e). In these cases, the GDPR contains specific provisions in paragraphs 2 and 3 of Article 6 itself:

Article 8. Processing of data by legal obligation, public interest, or exercise of public powers.  
1. The processing of personal data may only be considered based on the fulfillment of a legal obligation enforceable against the controller, as provided in Article 6.1.c) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, when a rule of European Union Law or a law of equivalent rank provides for it, which may determine the general conditions of processing and the types of data subject to it as well as the transfers that may arise as a consequence of the fulfillment of the legal obligation. Moreover, such law must in all cases respect the principle of proportionality, as recalled by the Judgment of the Constitutional Court 14/2003, of January 28:
“In other words, in accordance with a reiterated doctrine of this Court, the constitutionality of any restrictive measure of fundamental rights is determined by the strict observance of the principle of proportionality. For the purposes that are relevant here, it is enough to recall that, to verify whether a restrictive measure of a fundamental right passes the proportionality test, it is necessary to ascertain whether it meets the following three requirements or conditions: whether the measure is capable of achieving the proposed objective (suitability test); whether, furthermore, it is necessary, in the sense that there is no other more moderate measure to achieve such purpose with equal effectiveness (necessity test); and, finally, whether it is balanced or proportionate, deriving more benefits or advantages for the general interest than harms to other conflicting goods or values (strict proportionality test; SSTC 66/1995, of May 8, F. 5; 55/1996, of March 28, FF. 7, 8, and 9; 270/1996, of December 16, F. 4.e; 37/1998, of February 17, F. 8; 186/2000, of July 10, F. 6).”

V
As a consequence of all the above, this Agency considers that the modification made in Article 49.1 of the LGTel generally supports, due to the existence of a legal obligation, the communication of subscriber data to official statistical services, but without including in the communication the data of those subscribers who have exercised their right not to appear in the directories, as this would require the legislator to have expressly included it, adequately justifying such inclusion in the need to adjust the data provided to the previously identified samples.
Likewise, and in accordance with the cited regulations and jurisprudence, the corresponding specific guarantees resulting from the risk analysis should be included in the text. In this sense and without exhaustive intent, once the purpose of the processing is identified in the provision (the preparation of surveys and the development of the statistical competencies conferred by the Law), compliance with the principle of minimization would require identifying the specific data that must be communicated according to that purpose, as well as limiting them to those strictly necessary for the proper conduct of the survey, being able to establish the population percentages that may be considered not excessive; the principle of limitation of purpose would justify including the express prohibition of using them in surveys different from the one for which they have been requested, as well as for other different purposes; and the principle of limitation of the retention period would require including a period for their deletion.
