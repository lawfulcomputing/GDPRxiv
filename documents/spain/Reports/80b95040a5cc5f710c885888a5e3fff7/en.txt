The consultation is made as a consequence of the legal modification introduced in Article 49.1 in fine of Law 9/2014, of May 9, General Telecommunications Law, carried out by the recent Law 15/2021, of October 23, and the new requests submitted by the Centro de Investigaciones Sociológicas (CIS) in which the supply of telephone numbers of subscribers is requested for the realization of various studies included in the National Statistical Plan, as well as to carry out other studies and surveys within its competence, expressly stating that the CIS indicates that “These telephone numbers must be provided under the same conditions as to emergency services, as provided by Article 49 of the aforementioned Law, thus not excluding the telephone numbers of those individuals who have requested not to appear in telephone directories, as this exclusion does not apply under the new legislation.”  

In the consultation, considering the previous requests from the CIS and in accordance with the provisions of Article 68.2 of the Regulation on the conditions for the provision of electronic communications services, the universal service, and the protection of users, approved by Royal Decree 424/2005, of April 15, this Agency is requested, within the scope of its competencies, to inform the Comisión Nacional de los Mercados y la Competencia (CNMC) whether this legislative change modifies the criteria expressed by the AEPD up to this point regarding the requests from the CIS or other statistical bodies or services. In particular, the CNMC requests the AEPD's opinion on whether, after the modification of Article 49 LGTel, the communication by the CNMC to the CIS of data relating to the telephone numbers of subscribers provided by operators through the SGDA, who have not given their consent to appear in subscriber directories or in services for consulting subscriber numbers, is in accordance with personal data protection regulations for the purposes stated by the CIS, namely:  
- to prepare the various studies included in the National Statistical Plan (PEN) in its Annual Programs;  
- to conduct other studies and surveys of a mandatory nature for the CIS, such as pre-election and post-election surveys, which are not included in the PEN;  
- to carry out other necessary studies based on exceptional circumstances, such as those arising from the pandemic.

From the perspective of the fundamental right to the protection of personal data, the provision has undeniable significance, as such processing will find its basis, after this modification, in the fulfillment of a legal obligation applicable to the data controller (Article 6.1.c) of the GDPR), so it will no longer be necessary to analyze the compatibility of the processing for a different purpose according to the criteria established in Article 6.4. of the GDPR, including the reasonable expectation of the affected parties, since such processing is provided for in a norm with the force of law. However, such processing must be carried out under the terms legally established, including compliance with the requirements that are established by regulation, which is expressly stated, for which the provisions established in Royal Decree 424/2005, of April 15, which approves the Regulation on the conditions for the provision of electronic communications services, the universal service, and the protection of users, as well as in the Circular of the National Commission of Markets and Competition 1/2013, regarding the procedure for supplying subscriber data for the provision of directory services, telephone inquiries about subscriber numbers, and emergencies, modified by Circular 5/2014, of July 30. Consequently, until the legal amendment made by Law 15/2021, of October 23, article 49.1. LGTel only contemplated, on the one hand, the communication of subscriber data to entities that will prepare telephone directories of subscribers and to those providing telephone inquiry services about subscriber numbers, in order to ensure free competition, subject, in any case, to the condition that the affected parties had not exercised their right not to appear in the directories; and, on the other hand, to emergency services, in order to ensure their proper management and the protection of the vital interests of the affected party or a third party, with such protection prevailing in this case over the possible opposition of the affected party to appear in the directories. The data concerning subscribers who have exercised their right not to appear in publicly accessible directories shall only be provided to the entities responsible for the emergency service.

On one hand, it refers to the corresponding regulatory development, which among the guarantees provided includes, in the repeatedly cited article 68.2 of Royal Decree 424/2005, of April 15, that “The data concerning subscribers who have exercised their right not to appear in publicly accessible directories will only be provided to the entities responsible for the emergency call service.”
On the other hand, it includes a specific mention of the regulations on the protection of personal data by stating that “it must be based on the principles set forth in the data protection regulations and, in particular, on those of minimization and limitation of purpose.”
Notwithstanding this provision, it is a very generic provision, regarding which two observations must be made. The first is that the application of the principles set forth in the data protection regulations is directly applicable in accordance with the GDPR, regardless of the express mention included in the last paragraph of article 49.1 of the LGTel, in its new wording. The second is that, given its generality, they cannot be considered as specific guarantees, in the terms required by the Constitutional Court in its rulings 292/2000 of November 30 and 76/2019 of May 22.
Consequently, this Agency considers that such reference to the regulations on the protection of personal data should not be interpreted as an exclusion of a specific guarantee, the right not to appear in telephone directories, which is indeed recognized generally by law in its article 48.3, and that the special regulations themselves only exclude it concerning emergency services.

V
As a consequence of all the above, this Agency considers that the modification made in article 49.1 of the LGTel generally supports, due to the existence of a legal obligation, the communication of subscriber data to official statistical services, but without including in the communication the data of those subscribers who have exercised their right not to appear in the directories, as this would require that the legislator had expressly included it, adequately justifying such inclusion in the need to adapt the data provided to the previously identified samples.
Likewise, and in accordance with the cited regulations and jurisprudence, the corresponding specific guarantees resulting from the risk analysis should be included in the text. In this sense and without being exhaustive, having identified the purpose of the processing in the provision (the preparation of surveys and the development of the statistical competencies conferred by the Law), compliance with the principle of minimization would require identifying the specific data that must be communicated according to that purpose, as well as limiting them to those strictly necessary for the proper conduct of the survey, being able to establish the percentages of the population that may be considered not excessive; the principle of purpose limitation would justify including the express prohibition of using them in surveys different from the one for which they have been requested, as well as for other different purposes; and the principle of limitation of the retention period would require including a period for their deletion.
