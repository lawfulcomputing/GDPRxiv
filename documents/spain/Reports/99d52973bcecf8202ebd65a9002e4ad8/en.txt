All of this means that the impact of applying data protection standards must inspire the entirety of the regulation on clinical trials with medicines and the various actions and procedures developed under it, given the special incidence that it has for the different participants and particularly for the subjects of the trial in the rights guaranteed by data protection regulations.  

In this regard, it should not be forgotten that in the case we are dealing with, the data of the trial subjects being processed are specially protected data related to their health, so the specific rule contained in Article 7.3 of Organic Law 15/1999 applies directly. Thus, it will not be possible to justify the processing and transfer of the data on other grounds than those provided for in Articles 6 and 11 of the Organic Law, requiring the consent of the interested party or a specific legal authorization.  

Certainly, the Draft, like Royal Decree 223/2004, is based on the principle that processing will, in any case, be subject to the duly informed consent of the trial subject. However, it must be taken into account that this mere consent is not the only principle to be considered in the processing of data carried out within the framework of clinical trials.  

In particular, it is relevant at this point to consider the principles established in Article 4 of the Organic Law, being essential, first of all, to guarantee proportionality in the processing, so that according to Article 4.1, the data is adequate, relevant, and not excessive in relation to the purpose that justifies its processing, which entails two fundamental consequences: on the one hand, the volume of data subject to processing must be reduced to what is effectively indispensable for fulfilling the specific purposes of the trial and, in any case, the preservation of the health of the trial subjects; on the other hand, the number of persons or entities with access to such data must also be minimized.  

This second conclusion requires taking into account that in the preparation of the protocol, approval, development, and monitoring of the trial, a plurality of subjects are involved, both from the perspective of its execution and its oversight: thus, reference must be made in any case to the figures of the sponsor, the investigator, and the monitor, as well as to the persons contracted by the sponsor (commonly referred to as contract research organizations). Additionally, reference must be made to the role of the Ethics Committees for Research with Medicines, the Spanish Agency for Medicines and Health Products, the competent authorities of the Autonomous Communities, and the European Medicines Agency. Data protection regulations, and especially those that guarantee the aforementioned principle of proportionality, must be applied in all flows of data.

V
As previously noted, a second issue that should be taken into account is one that would allow for a clear determination of the role of each participant in the development of the clinical trial from the perspective of personal data protection regulations, in order to specify whether these participants are considered data controllers or data processors. It is particularly important to highlight that the status of data controller does not require actual access to the data, but rather the authority to make decisions regarding the purpose, content, and use of the processing. In a specific case, there may coexist a data controller, who carries out the effective processing of the data, making certain decisions about the processing, and a data processor, who generally decides on the existence and scope of that processing, as has been repeatedly pointed out by the jurisprudence of our Supreme Court.
At this point, the Type Code of Farmaindustria, to which reference has been made previously, is extremely clarifying, as it differentiates the cases in which the sponsor will or will not have access to the identifying data of the subjects of the trial. However, in all cases, it highlights the fact that it logically corresponds to the aforementioned sponsor to make decisions regarding the purpose, content, and use of the processing, even if they do not access the data. In any case, they are responsible for the processing related to the clinical trial, without prejudice to the fact that certain obligations established by Organic Law 15/1999 may not apply to them in specific cases, due to not having access to the data, such as the obligation to respond to requests for the exercise of rights or to directly obtain informed consent from the subjects of the trial for the processing of their data.

At the time of issuing this report and as a starting point, we must refer to what is stated in Recital 79 of the GDPR, which indicates that (…) The protection of the rights and freedoms of data subjects, as well as the responsibility of controllers and processors, including in relation to the supervision by the supervisory authorities and the measures adopted by them, requires a clear attribution of responsibilities under this Regulation, including cases where a controller determines the purposes and means of processing jointly with other controllers, or where processing is carried out on behalf of a controller (…).
The GDPR emphasizes the need to clearly establish the map of participants in any data processing, in order to accurately determine the attribution of responsibilities in accordance with the aforementioned regulation. This regulation aims to ensure that there are no situations of action outside its scope of application, in order to provide supervisory authorities with the necessary elements to carry out their function and ultimately to provide European citizens with the protection that their personal data deserves. Therefore, any activity involving the processing of personal data will be attributable to a subject that meets the requirements of the different categories provided by the GDPR.
The GDPR defines in its Article 4.7 the figure of the data controller as “the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; if the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the data controller or the specific criteria for its appointment may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.”
And in its Article 4.8, it defines the figure of the data processor as “the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that processes personal data on behalf of the data controller.”
In this regard, it should be noted that the figure of the data processor responds to the need to address phenomena such as the outsourcing of services by companies and other entities, so that in cases where the data controller entrusts a third party with the provision of a service that involves the processing of personal data, we will be dealing with processing carried out on behalf of the controller. 
This does not necessarily imply that the data subject to processing is owned by the controller, but rather that the processing operations, including, for example, collection, are attributed to the controller.

Therefore, from the perspective of the protection of personal data, in accordance with the GDPR and the prior criterion of this Agency, the monitor holds the status of “data processor,” as they process personal data on behalf of the sponsor, who has the obligation to carry out the monitoring, being the sole responsible for the choice of the monitor, for which “they will only choose a processor that offers sufficient guarantees” (Article 28.1 of the GDPR). In the exercise of their monitoring function, the monitor must follow only the instructions of the sponsor, “in accordance with the sponsor's standardized procedures” (Article 40.1.a) of the Royal Decree), and a contract as provided in Article 28.3 of the GDPR must be signed between the sponsor and the monitor.
Consequently, as the data processor of the sponsor, the legal basis applicable to the processing of personal data carried out by the monitor will be the same as that of the sponsor. In this case, since monitoring is an obligation imposed on the sponsor by Regulation (EU) 536/2014, it will be the one contemplated in Article 6.1.c) of the GDPR: “the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject.”

access pseudonymized data and the monitor accesses the identifying data of the patient's medical history. As we have seen, this access occurs in compliance with a legal obligation for monitoring by the sponsor, without prejudice to the fact that the sponsor does not have such data by virtue of measures adopted to comply with other principles of the GDPR, such as the purpose limitation (Article 5.1.b) and data minimization (Article 5.1.c), with the consequent impact on the adoption of security measures based on the different personal data processed. As we have seen, the GDPR does not consider access to data by the controller as a determining criterion for assessing the existence of a data processing agreement; rather, it requires that the processing operations, which include, for example, collection, be attributed to the controller.

Access to the personal data of the medical history by the monitor is done on behalf of the sponsor and not on behalf of the center responsible for that medical history. Likewise, the center does not participate in the selection of the monitor nor can it give instructions regarding the exercise of its function, and there is no legal relationship between the center and the monitor. The center's inability to give instructions to the monitor regarding the development of its function is highlighted by the AEMPS, which in its "Instructions Document of the Spanish Agency of Medicines and Medical Devices for the conduct of clinical trials in Spain," expressly states that "The necessary separation of functions provided for by legislation on clinical research, clearly distinguishing the research activities carried out in a specific center by the trial investigators from those of monitoring, cannot be conditioned by instructions from that center to the monitor regarding the monitoring of the trial." (Section 63, which will be referenced later).

Therefore, Regulation (EU) 536/2014 requires, as we have seen, as a condition for approving the clinical trial, that it be accredited, through a declaration from the sponsor, that the participating institutions will allow monitoring, so that prior to the authorization of the trial, the center must have committed to facilitating the necessary access to the medical history for monitoring tasks, by virtue of the corresponding agreement with the sponsor.

Consequently, the monitor's access to the medical history data is done on behalf of the sponsor and not the center, and the data processing agreement must be signed only with the sponsor and not with the center, without prejudice to the fact that the latter, as the controller of the medical history, must establish all necessary measures to ensure the security of personal data, such as a duty of confidentiality in accordance with Article 32 of the GDPR.

In this regard, it is worth recalling that section 6 of Article 41 of Royal Decree 1090/2015, although referring to research personnel, regulates a relevant aspect for the purposes of the consultation, related to the intervention of the healthcare center where the trial is conducted regarding access to the medical history of the trial subject, by establishing the following: 
“The contracted personnel must be authorized by the management of the healthcare center, specifying whether or not they have access to the medical history and personal data of the subjects included in the trial. This authorization can be formalized in two ways, through:
a) The signing of a contract, if they are personnel contracted by the center.
b) An independent access document if they are personnel contracted by third parties.”
Therefore, the Royal Decree itself that regulates clinical trials refers to an independent document separate from a contract to specifically authorize access to the medical history of personnel contracted by third parties, such as the monitor, who is hired by the sponsor as the data processor. The monitor must be able to access, unlike the sponsor who will only know pseudonymized data, the identifying data and medical history of the trial subjects in order to properly carry out their functions.
Thus, an authorization for such access in a specific document is appropriate without the need to distort the figure of the monitor as the data processor of the sponsor by artificially attributing to them the status of data processor of the center.
V
From the above, the following can be concluded:
The sponsor is responsible for the processing of data for the purpose of conducting clinical trials with medicines, having to make decisions regarding the purposes and means in accordance with the provisions of Regulation (EU) 536/2014 and Royal Decree 1090/2015 that have been outlined.
Provisions that are specifically applicable in relation to the references that this modality of research collects in Article 16.3 of the LAP when it contemplates the uses of the medical history for research purposes.
The monitor is a data processor for the performance of monitoring activities on behalf of the sponsor, in the terms delineated by the aforementioned clinical trial regulations, and must subscribe to the guarantees established in Article 28 of the GDPR.
The healthcare center is responsible for the processing of medical history data in the terms established in the LAP and regional regulations governing this matter for the purposes provided in said regulations; except for the uses of the same for health research purposes in the modality of clinical trials which, as noted, are governed by the terms provided in the Royal Decree on clinical trials with medicines. According to the provisions of this regulation, its intervention as the data processor is limited to the authorization for access to the medical history of the trial subject by personnel contracted by third parties, such as the monitor or the CRO. Access can be articulated through a document distinct from a contract and the figure of the data processor, in which specific guarantees for the performance of their functions are established.
In this regard, reference must be made, as mentioned in the consultation, to the document from AEMPS “Annex VIIIC. Revised instructions for updating the section on Personal Data Protection in the subject information sheet (HIP/CI) regarding Regulation (EU) No. 2016/679 General Data Protection Regulation,” (annexed to the AEMPS instruction document cited), which provides the following:
“Both the Center and the Sponsor are respectively responsible for the processing of their data and commit to comply with the applicable data protection regulations. The data collected for the study will be identified by a code, so that no information that could identify you is included, and only your study physician/collaborators will be able to relate such data to you and your medical history. Therefore, your identity will not be revealed to any other person except to health authorities, when required, or in cases of medical emergency. The Research Ethics Committees, representatives of the Health Authority in matters of inspection, and personnel authorized by the Sponsor, will only be able to access to verify personal data, the procedures of the clinical study, and compliance with good clinical practice standards (always maintaining the confidentiality of the information).”
As can be seen, the instruction, in addition to referring to the responsibility of the sponsor and the center which must be understood in the terms previously indicated by the Royal Decree on clinical trials with medicines, specifically includes information to the trial subject regarding access to their information by personnel authorized by the sponsor, among whom is the monitor.

“In the context of data protection, should the monitor have a data processing agreement with each center where they intervene, given that they may access identifying data and the medical history of the subjects in the trial?
