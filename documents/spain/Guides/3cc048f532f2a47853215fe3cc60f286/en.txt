3. ABOUT US  
3.1 Mission and Vision  
The Spanish Agency for Data Protection (hereinafter AEPD) is an independent public authority responsible for ensuring the privacy and data protection of citizens. Its basic objectives are, on one hand, to ensure and facilitate compliance with data protection regulations and, on the other, to promote among citizens the knowledge of their rights regarding the processing of their personal data and to support them in exercising those rights. It is headquartered in Madrid, and its scope of action extends throughout Spain.  
The AEPD has the following MISSION:  
• To disseminate and protect the rights of citizens, promoting among the public and organizations the value of data protection;  
• To ensure compliance with data protection legislation, promoting preventive measures and exercising sanctioning authority;  
• To drive proactive work that allows for the detection of the impact that new technological developments may have on the privacy of citizens, promoting a conception of privacy as an asset of public and private organizations, and as a distinguishing element of competitiveness in the market.  
This mission gives meaning to Social Responsibility at the AEPD, posing the challenge and the need to be an organization demanding in its procedures and management model, and ambitious in the impact of each of its projects and activities and in the social orientation of all its actions. This aims to shape and consolidate the Agency as an open and approachable body that strengthens and expands communication channels with all stakeholders, providing a comprehensive response to their needs.  
The VISION of the AEPD represents the place where the Agency wants to reach and the way it wants to be recognized by the recipients of its services. Specifically, the AEPD aims to be a close, proactive, and collaborative Agency that offers an effective response to both citizens and obligated subjects and that addresses new demands, challenges, and opportunities to deepen the effective guarantee of the fundamental right to data protection.

Organizational Structure  
3.2 Organizational Structure  
The AEPD is an independent administrative authority and interacts with the Government through the Ministry of Justice.  
In Spain, there are also regional data protection agencies in Catalonia, the Basque Country, and Andalusia, with a scope limited to data processing within their respective public, regional, and local bodies.  
The representation of the AEPD is currently held by its Director, but following the entry into force of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD), once the election process provided for in Article 48 is completed, it will correspond to the Presidency, which, with a five-year (renewable) mandate, will issue its resolutions, circulars, and guidelines. The Presidency of the Agency will be assisted by a Deputy to whom it may delegate its functions. Both will exercise their functions with full independence and objectivity and will not be subject to any instructions in their performance. The legislation regulating the exercise of high office in the General State Administration will apply to them.  
The Presidency will be advised by a Consultative Council which, in accordance with the aforementioned LOPDGDD, is composed of a deputy, a senator, a representative of the General Council of the Judiciary; a representative of the General State Administration with experience in the matter, proposed by the Minister of Justice; a representative from each Autonomous Community that has created a data protection authority in its territorial scope; an expert proposed by the Spanish Federation of Municipalities and Provinces; an expert proposed by the Council of Consumers and Users; two experts proposed by Business Organizations; a representative of data protection and privacy professionals, proposed by the state-level association with the largest number of members; a representative of the supervisory bodies or entities for extrajudicial conflict resolution; an expert proposed by the Conference of Rectors of Spanish Universities; a representative of the organizations that group the General, Superior, and Professional Councils of various regulated professions at the state level; a representative of information security professionals, proposed by the state-level association with the largest number of members; an expert in transparency and access to public information proposed by the Transparency and Good Governance Council, and two experts proposed by the most representative trade union organizations.  
The Director - or Presidency from the LOPDGDD - of the AEPD hierarchically oversees the General Subdirectorate of the General Data Protection Registry (which will change its name following the new Statute of the Agency, as the aforementioned Registry has been abolished with the entry into force of the GDPR), the General Subdirectorate of Data Inspection, the General Secretariat, and the Agency's Support Unit. The Support Unit currently includes the Legal Office, the International Area, the Communication Office, and the Unit for Evaluation and Technological Studies (UEET).  
Acts issued by the management (on behalf of the Agency) exhaust the administrative route, and against them, only an optional appeal for reconsideration can be filed before the AEPD itself or a contentious-administrative appeal before the National Court.

Regulatory Framework  
3.3 Regulatory Framework  
The Spanish Agency for Data Protection is governed by its specific regulations, which consist of the following provisions:  
• Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, regarding the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation).  
• Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and the guarantee of digital rights.  
• Royal Decree 428/1993, of March 26, approving the Statute of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection, in what is not repealed by Organic Law 3/2018 (first transitional provision).  
• Law 40/2015, of October 1, on the Legal Regime of the Public Sector, with the special provisions established in the Twentieth Additional Provision of Organic Law 3/2018.  
The AEPD is subject to Administrative Law both in the exercise of its powers and in its patrimonial and contracting regime. In particular, the legal regime applicable to the AEPD is:  
• Regarding the exercise of powers, it is governed by Law 39/2015, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations.  
• Regarding the patrimonial regime, Law 33/2003, on the Heritage of Public Administrations (5th Additional Provision), applies.  
• Regarding contracting, it is subject to Law 9/2017, on Public Sector Contracts, which transposes to the Spanish legal system the Directives of the European Parliament and of the Council 2014/23/EU and 2014/24/EU, of February 26, 2014 (article 3.1.c), with the exception of the Twentieth Additional Provision of Organic Law 3/2018.  
Concerning the personal and material resources available to the AEPD to carry out its functions, they are as follows:  
• Personnel: Officials from the different public administrations and labor personnel hired for this purpose, according to the nature of the functions assigned to each job position.  
• Budget: It has an integrated budget, with the necessary independence, in the General State Budgets. The Agency is subject to the provisions of Law 47/2003, of November 26, General Budgetary Law that are applicable to it.  
External control is exercised by the Court of Auditors and internal control by the General Intervention of the State (IGAE).

3.4 Strategy  
3.4.1 Description of Strategy and Priorities  
In order to adapt to the new European data protection framework, bring the Agency closer to those responsible and citizens, and modernize its management, a strategic plan was designed for the period 2015-2019 with the aim of systematically collecting the fundamental lines of its activity through a temporal programming and a definition of objectives that could be evaluated both within the organization and externally.  
After the completion of the strategic plan in 2019, whose main actions we will see later, a new stage begins at the AEPD with the approval in April of that same year of the Framework for Action on Social Responsibility fully aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) for the 2030 Agenda. This new framework is accompanied by an action plan with more than 100 initiatives for the next five years and represents a clear commitment from the Agency to stakeholders.  
Considering that the AEPD is a public body that safeguards a fundamental right, it is a firm commitment to demonstrate the Agency's commitment to Social Responsibility. This new model requires a change within the culture of the entity as it includes criteria of social and environmental responsibility when planning and measuring its impact. Although this entails greater complexity, the Agency has a highly professional and committed human team that embraces this new model with the hope and assurance of contributing to the construction of a better society.  
Throughout 2019, the AEPD continued to develop in its last year the Strategic Plan established for the period 2015-2019 by launching 113 initiatives structured around five major strategic axes, which in turn have been developed through different specific programs and almost entirely with the Agency's personnel resources.  
It is worth noting that the vast majority of the measures planned for the 2019 fiscal year had a multiannual nature, meaning they were a continuation of actions initiated in previous periods, being completed during the current year, which is why the evaluation of their compliance has been carried out within the framework of the final evaluation of the entire plan.  
However, there have also been "non-continuous" actions, meaning those that were initiated and executed in 2019. In this case, most of the initiatives are the guides and tools that were planned for the year 2019.  
From this work perspective with a social responsibility outlook and within this exercise, it is important to highlight the set of measures that the Agency has promoted regarding Gender Equality. Among them, the creation of the Priority Channel to urgently request the removal of violent or sensitive content on the internet stands out, which has received very favorable reception. This initiative has positioned the AEPD as a reference authority at the European and global level in combating this scourge of gender violence, and more broadly, the so-called "digital violence" that particularly affects women and young people in the school environment. But there have also been more measures in this area, such as the creation of a website (microsite) on gender violence; the approval of the AEPD Protocol on sexual harassment and recommendations to companies regarding obligations in cases of digital violence, or the announcement of a new award on Data Protection and Gender Violence, among others.  
The other major milestone of the 2019 fiscal year was the first anniversary, in May, of the effective application of the European Regulation, and in December, of the Organic Law on data protection (LO 3/2018), whose balance was presented at the event held on December 5 at the Spanish headquarters of the European Commission.  
In this regard, it is important to highlight, first and foremost, the initiatives launched to raise awareness among minors in their educational environment about the responsible use of the internet, collaborating with Educational Administrations in making available

Specifically, regarding claims submitted to the Agency, it first analyzes whether the claim has not been raised with the data controller (or their DPO), or if it was raised but the controller/DPO did not respond, or the response did not satisfy the claimant, in which case the claim is forwarded to the controller (to the attention of the DPO, if one has been appointed). In the transfer, the controller is required to respond to the claimant within one month and inform the AEPD of the response given, the causes that led to the problem, and any additional information that may be relevant to the case. When the report from the controller or their DPO is received, the Agency evaluates it to decide whether to admit or not admit the claim for processing.  
If it is deemed that the response is adequate (the claim has been addressed by the controller), the claim is inadmissible, but this is “without prejudice to the Agency, applying the investigative and corrective powers it holds, being able to carry out subsequent actions related to the data processing referred to in the claim.” If, after this period, such notification does not occur, it will be understood that the processing of the claim continues.

Appointment and Mandate  
In accordance with Article 48 of the LOPDGDD 3/2018, the Presidency of the AEPD and its Deputy shall be appointed by the Government, upon the proposal of the Ministry of Justice, from among individuals of recognized professional competence, particularly in the field of data protection.  
Two months prior to the expiration of the mandate or, in the other cases of cessation, when such has occurred, the Ministry of Justice shall order the publication in the BOE of the public call for candidates.  
Following an evaluation of the merit, capacity, competence, and suitability of the candidates, the Government shall submit to the Congress of Deputies a proposal for the Presidency and Deputy accompanied by a justificatory report that, after the mandatory hearing of the candidates, must be ratified by the Justice Commission in a public vote by a majority of three-fifths of its members in the first vote or, if this is not achieved, by an absolute majority in the second vote, which shall take place immediately after the first. In this latter case, the favorable votes must come from Deputies belonging to at least two different parliamentary groups.  
Their mandate shall be for five years and may be renewed for another period of equal duration.  
Cessation and Separation  
In accordance with the LOPDGDD, the Presidency and the Deputy shall only cease before the expiration of their mandate, at their own request or by separation agreed upon by the Council of Ministers, for:  
a) Serious breach of their obligations,  
b) Sudden incapacity to perform their function,  
c) Incompatibility, or  
d) Final conviction for a willful crime.  
In the cases provided for in letters a), b), and c), the ratification of the separation by the parliamentary majorities provided for in Article 48.3 shall be necessary.  
Independence  
In accordance with the LOPDGDD, the Presidency and the Deputy shall exercise their functions with full independence and objectivity and shall not be subject to any instructions in their performance. The legislation regulating the exercise of high office in the General State Administration shall apply to them.  
Article 59 of the GDPR establishes that each supervisory authority shall prepare an annual report (Memoria) of its activities, which may include a list of types of reported infringements and types of measures adopted in accordance with Article 58, paragraph 2. The reports shall be transmitted to the national Parliament (Constitutional Commission of the Congress of Deputies), to the Government, and to the other authorities designated under the law of the Member States (Autonomous Authorities of the Basque Country, Catalonia, and Andalusia, and the General Council of the Judiciary). They shall be made available to the public, the Commission, and the European Data Protection Board.  
The acts and provisions issued by the Presidency of the Agency conclude the administrative route, being subject to direct appeal before the Contentious-Administrative Chamber of the National Court.

4.5 Risk Management and Compliance  
4.5.1 Risk Management  
The existence of risk is inherent to any human activity and, therefore, its diversity is as varied as the activities carried out: labor, health, financial, environmental, information security, or risks in the processing of personal data. Risk analysis is a tool that allows for an objective assessment of the risks and the possible measures we can use to mitigate them.  
The risk-based approach in data protection is aimed at determining the technical and organizational security measures to protect personal data and to detect risks to the rights and freedoms of individuals.  
In addition, an adequate compliance policy in the public sector entails, first and foremost, conducting a risk assessment regarding integrity within the entity itself, in order to detect shortcomings in control and to establish effective mechanisms for supervision and quality control of the risk management system. It also involves evaluating the human resources system of the entity, through the establishment of appropriate processes for selection, training, and assessment of human capital.  
4.5.2 Compliance System  
The Agency's commitment to transparency, integrity, and accountability, as outlined in its Framework for Action on Social Responsibility and Sustainability 2019-2024, has materialized in the strategic objective of internally implementing mechanisms that facilitate the application of best practices and generate an organizational culture centered on values and commitments, aimed at improving its management and efficiency.  
This initiative contributes to strengthening the integrity of the public function performed by the AEPD and its commitment to social, economic, and environmental improvement.  
In this regard, the adoption of internal mechanisms that ensure best practices and enhance the control environment contributes to the achievement of the SDGs and the targets set in the 2030 Agenda, and especially to SDG 16, which seeks to create responsible, effective, and transparent institutions at all levels that are accountable (Target 6).

4.6.1 National Security Scheme

The purpose of the ENS is to create the necessary conditions of trust in the use of electronic means, through measures to guarantee the security of systems, data, communications, and electronic services, allowing citizens and Public Administrations to exercise rights and fulfill duties through these means.  
The AEPD works on information security in accordance with the ENS guidelines and in order to comply with the scheme.

The AEPD has been complying with the annual campaigns for completing the INES report since 2016, which allows it to identify the areas to work on in order to achieve improvements.

5.5.3 Carbon Footprint  
In 2019, the Agency worked specifically on a tool that would allow it to obtain a general overview of the impact that the AEPD has on climate change, in order to determine key reduction points that will contribute to designing its reduction strategy.  
To this end, it calculated, through an external company, its Carbon Footprint to quantify and report the Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emissions associated with its activities through the external company FactorCO2. The results have been verified through an audit conducted by the consulting firm TUVRheinland Iberica ICT, S.A.  
Below, we reflect the main parameters of the study:  
• Definition of emission flows  
• Official vehicle: emissions derived from the fuel used by the vehicle of the Director of AEPD. It is considered a personal vehicle and, therefore, direct emissions of the organization. These are obtained from the kilometers traveled by the vehicle.  
• Refrigerants: emissions derived from leaks occurring in air conditioning equipment. The maintenance manager has been consulted.  
• Electricity: emissions derived from the purchase and use of electricity throughout the AEPD building. These are obtained from electricity bills and the type of supplier.  
• Water consumption: emissions derived from water consumption throughout the building. These are obtained from water bills.  
• Office supplies: emissions derived from the consumption of common office materials (paper, toner). This has been obtained from annual orders.  
• Waste: emissions derived from the waste generated by employees. An estimate has been made based on the number of employees and in-office days annually.  
• Business travel: emissions derived from trips made (airplane, train, car, etc.). This has been obtained from the origin and destination of the trip and mode of transport.

• Data protection as a guarantee in the policies for preventing digital harassment in the workplace: Recommendations from the AEPD  
As noted, violence can manifest in various forms: physical assaults, threats, psychological violence, intimidation, harassment, or moral, sexual, or gender-based harassment. For almost all of these, new technologies and the widespread access to personal or even intimate information about individuals have become a very effective tool.  
Digital harassment in the workplace is the conduct of aggression or harassment towards a worker with the aim of humiliating them, and it can be severely detrimental to individuals' health.  
There is a significant amount of national and international legislation that has been addressing the need for states to adopt measures to prevent and combat moral or sexual harassment in the workplace for years, but to date, specific attention has not been paid to the necessity of combating these behaviors when they are carried out in violation of the worker's right to personal data protection in the digital realm.  
The company must promote working conditions that prevent sexual harassment and harassment based on sex, and establish specific procedures for its prevention and for addressing complaints or claims that may be made by those who have been subjected to it. For this purpose, measures such as the development and dissemination of codes of good practices, the implementation of informational campaigns, or training actions may be established.  
Likewise, worker representatives must contribute to preventing sexual harassment and harassment based on sex in the workplace by raising awareness among workers about it and informing the company's management of any behaviors or actions they become aware of that could foster such conduct.  
What happens when behaviors considered harassment are carried out through the unlawful processing of the victim's personal data that clearly affects their privacy?  
In a hyper-connected world where computer tools, mobile phones, social networks, or instant messaging services are our usual means of interaction, and where we often expose, as never before, a part...

For this reason, the Agency, from its dual role as a data protection authority and a workplace, has considered the need to formulate recommendations aimed at Companies and Public Administrations that allow for the prevention and work towards the eradication of behaviors that, through the unlawful use of personal data, constitute harassment of workers in any of its forms.  
In these recommendations, first of all, the behaviors that constitute cyberbullying are listed, especially when carried out through the use of personal data. It is specified that harassment via mobile phone among colleagues is a workplace risk and that the company cannot remain idle in the face of such behaviors.  
The worker who harasses may have civil, criminal, administrative liability for data protection violations, and labor liability for harassment. The company may violate the Occupational Risk Prevention Law and face fines of up to €187,000.  
It is emphasized that the commission of such acts does not only constitute an unlawful act by the perpetrator but also poses a risk to the organization that, having knowledge of these unlawful data processing activities, does not react with actions aimed at eradicating them.  
The inclusion of three types of measures in the action protocols against harassment and in equality plans is recommended:  
• An unequivocal declaration of the organization's commitment to data protection and gender equality.

Principles and methodology  
Principles  
This report is based on the following principles that define the content and quality of the report:  
Regarding report quality: ACCURACY, BALANCE, CLARITY, COMPARABILITY, RELIABILITY, TIMELINESS.  
For the Spanish Data Protection Agency, establishing priorities and understanding the issues that concern stakeholders the most and how they impact the organizational model, and vice versa, is one of the priority objectives. To this end, we convene an annual meeting with our main stakeholders to conduct the materiality analysis. Ultimately, we determine our priorities through the materiality analysis to ensure that the AEPD's sustainable strategy is aligned with their expectations.  
The result of the process conducted to review our priorities is a materiality matrix reflecting ten relevant issues, which are the focus of AEPD's efforts to develop and implement its sustainable strategy.  
To carry out the process of identifying priority issues and their scope, the recommendations of the Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) standards have been followed. In fact, materiality is one of the GRI Reporting Principles that AEPD has adhered to in the preparation of its Annual Report. This principle establishes that the Report should address those aspects that reflect the significant economic, environmental, and social effects of the organization; or that substantially influence the evaluations and decisions of stakeholders.  
Regarding the included stakeholders: Employees, Society and NGOs, Media, Suppliers and concessionary services, Foundations and CSR experts, Companies, Public Administrations.  
Methodology  
The preparation of the social responsibility report of the Spanish Data Protection Agency refers to the following methodological process:  
• Phase 1 - Identification of trends and relevant issues for the sector and society as a whole, considering their relationship with the Agency's strategic objectives, the social responsibility strategy, and the information demanded by stakeholders.  
• Phase 2 - Prioritization of the identified issues through a combination of internal and external analysis of their relevance (see materiality matrix - section 5.2.2).  
• Phase 3 - Review and validation of the material issues by the management board.
