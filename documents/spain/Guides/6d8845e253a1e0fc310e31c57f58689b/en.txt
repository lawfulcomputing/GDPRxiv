1. WHY IS THE DECALOGUE OF PRINCIPLES AND THE ASSOCIATED PROOF OF CONCEPTS BEING PROPOSED?  
Access to the Internet is no longer a completely free option but has become an obligation for the personal and economic development of citizens.  
Minors must be protected in all aspects. The fundamental rights of all Internet users must also be respected, regardless of their age. The protection of minors cannot be an excuse to violate fundamental rights. Fundamental rights, particularly data protection rights, cannot be used as an excuse not to protect minors.  
An age verification system can have a significant impact on individuals' privacy, their right to act, think, inform themselves, and educate themselves freely, and on the surveillance and monitoring of each of their actions.  
The AEPD is obliged to protect the fundamental rights of citizens concerning data protection.  
The principles establish how to reconcile the best interests of the minor with the fundamental rights of citizens, and the PoCs demonstrate that it is possible to implement them in real scenarios, proving their viability.  
2. WHAT IS THE MAIN ADVANTAGE OF THE PROPOSED PoCs?  
The main advantages are the comprehensive protection of minors, the guarantee of the fundamental rights of the interested parties, universality, real auditability and transparency, and the suitability of a method that generates trust for its widespread use.  
Another advantage is that they are systems that can be exported worldwide and that, at the same time, align with Spanish and European identity providers that guarantee identity as a universal right.  
3. WILL THE AGE VERIFICATION APP BE PROVIDED BY THE AEPD?  
The AEPD is a Data Protection Authority, that is, a supervisory authority. Among its functions is not to provide this type of solution or applications. The applications developed for the proof of concepts are only prototypes or demonstrators; they are not intended to be offered to the public.  
The verification app is not a new mechanism for providing identity. Identity is a right of citizens, which in Spain is guaranteed by the State through the Ministry of the Interior or other entities.  
The verification app must be provided by public and/or private entities with different models and motivations for doing so. And the AEPD has among its competencies to ensure that all data protection guarantees are met, and therefore the rights and freedoms of citizens.  
At least the National Mint and Stamp Factory (FNMT) has already committed to developing the age verification app for public use.  
4. DOES VERIFYING AGE MEAN THAT INTERNET SERVICES MUST KNOW THE IDENTITY OF THE PERSON ACCESSING CONTENT? OR AT LEAST KNOW THEIR AGE?  
The purpose of protecting minors is to prevent them from accessing inappropriate content, not for Internet providers to know the age of the individuals accessing content or even their identity. Although at first glance it seems that both are
Page: 5 of 13  
equivalents, the second scenario would involve an intrusive way of achieving the real purpose of the processing.  
The decalog, demonstrated with the PoC, establishes that it is not necessary for Internet content providers to know the identity or age of users. The regulation also does not establish that there is legitimacy to carry out that processing, either by content providers or by third parties, when it is not necessary for age verification.  
5. IF A MINOR OR AN ADULT ACCESSES CONTENT SERVED FROM A SERVER OUTSIDE OF EUROPE, DOES IT REVEAL THEIR IDENTITY OR AGE?  
With the principles outlined in the decalog, identity is treated independently of age verification. In the PoC, age verification processing is carried out in the apps installed on the device itself, without accessing external servers; therefore, no information would be revealed to external servers, whether inside or outside of Europe.  
6. DOES THE AEPD PROPOSE A NEW DIGITAL IDENTITY MANAGEMENT SYSTEM IN THE PoC?  
No. The AEPD shows in the PoC that it is possible to separate age verification from identity providers, so it is not necessary to create new digital identity systems. Existing identity providers for the physical or digital world are sufficient, promoting technological neutrality and a free market of options. Therefore, the solution proposed in the PoC is compatible with a scheme based on the European digital wallet defined in eIDAS2 or with national or universal identification means, such as the passport, which are already available.  
7. WITH THE PROPOSED PoC, MUST A PERSON DECLARE TO A THIRD ENTITY THEIR DESIRE TO ACCESS ADULT CONTENT?  
No. The proposed PoC unlink age verification from the declaration of the purpose of accessing adult content (which is done in the browser or in a specific application for accessing content from a particular provider, for example, from a social network). A process in which the person must identify themselves for the specific purpose of accessing adult content is not necessary. Nor is it necessary to declare to a third entity that they have that purpose.  
In this way, the decision to access adult content is managed exclusively within the user's device, where their status as a "person authorized to access" is processed, thereby creating trust and ensuring that the system meets the suitability principle, since if it were not widely used, it would not be useful.  
8. DOES THE SYSTEM PROPOSED IN THE PoC ALLOW FOR LINKING THE USER'S BROWSING BETWEEN DIFFERENT SERVICES?  
No, it does not allow that. The use of general-purpose certificates or biometric systems directly on the servers of the content provider or third entities would allow it, revealing information about the user and enabling profiling. All systems based on a third-party intermediary, for example, know the user's browsing and also link it with the verification of their identity, being very intrusive with the privacy of individuals.

But in the PoC, this type of linkage is not possible because it does not exit the device of the user to resolve the age verification, and furthermore, this verification does not involve the identification of the person.  
9. ARE SOLUTIONS ADOPTED IN OTHER COUNTRIES THAT ACT AS INTERMEDIARIES BETWEEN THE USER AND THE SERVICE THEY WANT TO ACCESS NOT VALID?  
Age verification solutions have been adopted that do not prevent the location of minors and the mass collection of data. Some of them are very intrusive to privacy and monetize the browsing data of users, profiling them, identifying them, and creating parallel digital identity systems.  
The most popular solutions based on trusted third parties acting as intermediaries between Internet users and the content they wish to access could pose serious risks to the rights and freedoms of all individuals on the Internet. In particular, some of them could involve the processing of minors' data with risks to them. The same applies to other solutions, already available on the market, that are based on different designs or architectures but also involve that type of risk. None of the solutions analyzed by the AEPD fully comply with the ten principles proposed, when it has been demonstrated that it is possible thanks to the PoC.  
The urgency in implementing age verification systems cannot be an excuse to expose minors to greater risks, violate fundamental rights, and build parallel identity management systems that do not preserve privacy and turn identity into a service when it is a right of citizenship.  
10. WHAT HAPPENS WHEN A MINOR DOES NOT HAVE A MECHANISM TO PROVIDE THEIR IDENTITY?  
The foundation of the principles and the solution proposed in the PoC is that the minor should be free from having to be identified or subjected to supervision. Therefore, there is no need to provide minors with identification mechanisms; they do not need them.  
It is the individuals authorized to access the content who must use the mechanisms they already have to verify their age.  
11. CAN AN ADULT SITE NOT KNOW THE IDENTITY OF THE USER?  
An adult site will have the legitimacy to know the identity of the user within the framework of a service contract with that person, as long as knowing certain aspects of their identity is essential to establish that contract, and only when necessary. Also when required by law.  
But this identification process is different from the age verification process that allows for the protection of a minor against inappropriate content offered on that site. They constitute two distinct treatments that must be independent.  
12. DOES THE ACCREDITATION OF THE USER'S AGE THROUGH THE USE OF CERTIFICATES, DIGITAL WALLETS, QR CODES, OR OTHER METHODS NOT IMPLY THE EXCLUSION OF CERTAIN PEOPLE FROM USING THE SOLUTION?  
The orientation of the principles is that the use of different identity providers should be allowed, that individuals can choose which is most suitable in their case, that these providers do not detect that access to an adult service is desired, and that they do not identify them before Internet services. All of this is based on the rights that individuals have.

In this way, the right to act on the Internet is not mediated by a limited set of private services, but is guaranteed as a right in the digital world.

13. WHEN COULD THE ENTIRE SYSTEM FOR PROTECTING MINORS FROM INAPPROPRIATE CONTENT BE FULLY OPERATIONAL?  
These systems should already be operational.  
Content and service providers, in collaboration with stakeholders in the Internet ecosystem and civil society, have a guide for complying with the principle of active responsibility of the GDPR and the application of data protection by default and by design.  
14. ARE THE MECHANISMS PRESENTED BY THE AEPD IN ITS PoC THE ONLY ONES ALLOWED?  
The PoC are a demonstration that there are ways to comply with the principles and that, therefore, the AEPD can demand compliance with these principles. A solution that follows what is indicated in the PoC while respecting the principles will be as valid as any other approach that complies with the same principles.  
Other mechanisms and solutions that comply with the principles outlined in the decalogue and that, therefore, guarantee compliance with data protection regulations, and thus the protection of the best interests of the child and the rights and freedoms of citizens, will be considered adequate from the AEPD's perspective.  
15. HAS IT BEEN CONSIDERED THAT ADDITIONAL DEVELOPMENT WOULD BE REQUIRED FOR THE PoC TO BECOME A SOLUTION THAT CAN BE USED ON THE INTERNET?  
The AEPD has been working on defining the principles outlined in the decalogue, the design, and the implementation of the PoC, with significant effort in dialogue with multiple stakeholders within the framework of a Working Group on Age Verification created in March 2023, which has included the National Commission of Markets and Competition, the National Mint and Stamp Factory, the Ministry of the Interior, and the current Ministry of Digital Transformation.  
The role of the AEPD, as the supervisory authority, is to pave the way for the entire ecosystem that makes up the Internet industry to assume the responsibility of implementing the definitive systems. All the material generated and made available to them will serve as guidance and orientation for them to apply the necessary resources.  
16. IN THE PROOF OF CONCEPT, THERE ARE ASPECTS THAT ARE PENDING DEFINITION OR IMPROVEMENT. WHEN WILL THE AGENCY DO THIS?  
The AEPD is a Data Protection Authority, that is, a supervisory authority. These PoC are a demonstration that the proposed principles can be met; they will not become final products, meaning that the AEPD does not intend to itself implement these.

17. EVERY TIME AN ADULT WANTS TO ACCESS CONTENT LABELED FOR ADULTS, WILL IT BE NECESSARY TO SCAN A QR CODE, ACCESS A DIGITAL WALLET, OR READ AN OFFICIAL DOCUMENT? WILL THESE TYPES OF PROCESSES HINDER NAVIGATION?  
The solutions proposed so far in the market, in which users must authenticate before a service of a third entity, via the Internet and without guarantees of response times, can indeed be limiting.  
The decalog does not make low-level proposals with technical design or implementation details, so it can be complied with solutions that require the user to register with their identity only once or with solutions that require verifying identity and/or age attributes much more frequently. Providers of age verification applications will likely try to strike a balance between the reliability of the solution and usability, as constantly requesting proof would indeed hinder navigation through adult content.  
18. ISN'T BIOMETRIC AUTHENTICATION ON THE MOBILE DEVICE ITSELF AN INACCURATE MECHANISM?  
Biometric authentication performed by the user on their own device without resorting to any external resources or servers is one of the options that a commercial solution should offer to provide the opportunity to verify that the user retrieving an age data from an official identity document is the owner of that document, by comparing the photograph retrieved from that document with a selfie taken in real-time. This use case has been included in the PoC to show that alternative mechanisms must be allowed simultaneously to ensure trust and digital non-discrimination; that is, it is one among all possible options.  
Nevertheless, biometric authentication on the resources of the mobile device itself, in a purely personal processing, can have sufficient performance for many situations and users. If this is not the case, it would be necessary to resort to another of the possible mechanisms.  
19. WHY IS AN ESTIMATIVE SYSTEM NOT CONSIDERED ADEQUATE IN THE DECALOG? ISN'T IT LESS INVASIVE TO PRIVACY?  
There are different reasons that make an estimative system inadequate from the perspective of data protection.  
Most of those currently on the market have been implemented in a way that violates most of the decalog: they allow the identification of minors, identify adults, collect browsing habits, profile users, hinder transparency and auditability, involve biases and unfounded limitations on the right to act, allow the detection of individuals belonging to other vulnerable groups, etc.  
On the other hand, estimative systems executed on the device itself (without sending data to external servers) would be far from meeting the requirement of "verifiable" age verification as mandated by current regulations, as they result in probabilistic systems.

20. HOW CAN USERS AVOID EXPLOITING VULNERABILITIES IN THE AGE VERIFICATION APP? OR END UP INSTALLING MALICIOUS VERSIONS OF THESE AGE VERIFICATION APPS? COULD A “FAKE” AGE VERIFICATION APPLICATION BE CREATED THAT WOULD ALWAYS ALLOW A MINOR TO PRESENT THEMSELVES AS AN ADULT?  
Of course, a governance mechanism for the global system, as in any system that must be implemented securely, must take into account that 100% security does not exist, especially on mobile devices. In particular, vulnerabilities or weaknesses that are sure to arise must be managed appropriately.  
Obviously, those providing these apps must maintain them adequately within the governance framework, with updates that address weaknesses or vulnerabilities discovered once they are in use. It must be considered that the governance framework for age verification systems and protection against access to inappropriate content, due to its impact, should be incorporated into the incident notification processes and the monitoring processes established under the NIS2 Directive.  
Regarding malicious apps, cryptographic mechanisms can be established to guarantee the legitimacy of age verification apps (or their origin), as is already done in the case of others that require trust (banking and payment apps, chat and messaging tools, etc.).  
21. HOW IS IT GUARANTEED THAT ALL DATA RELATED TO AGE VERIFICATION OR ACCESS TO ADULT CONTENT DOES NOT END UP IN THE HANDS OF A THIRD PARTY WHO ENDS UP MONITORING OR PROILING USERS? THROUGH THE DEVICE MANUFACTURER, THE OPERATING SYSTEM, OTHER APPS. PHONES ARE VERY UNSAFE.  
It is true that there are weaknesses and vulnerabilities in mobile phones that can expose users' privacy. We must continue to advance so that the privacy and security of devices are significantly increased.  
Otherwise, nothing currently being executed on devices will be private or secure. The proposed solution could guarantee the same levels of security and privacy as the apps that users currently trust for making payments, banking transactions, communications, access to content, etc. The same practices and recommendations currently followed for all these apps would be adhered to, at a minimum.  
22. WHO WILL LABEL ADULT CONTENT? CAN THESE LABELS BE TRUSTED, WHO AUDITS THEM? IS THIS LABELING MODEL SCALABLE? AND HOW DOES IT ADJUST TO THE RELIGION OR CULTURE OF EACH COUNTRY, FOR EXAMPLE?  
The AEPD has developed a technical note with a proposal for this labeling system based on Age.xml, which arises from the European project MIRACLE. This solution is scalable, can be adjusted to different cultures and religions, and is based on self-assessment (the labeling is done by the providers themselves), which could be controlled or nuanced by different committees and commissions formed by authorities, the industry, parent associations, etc.  
It is the system already used, for example, in Germany. Other labeling systems could be valid as long as they are compatible with the proposed ten principles.

23. WOULD YOU ALLOW A FAMILY TO IMPLEMENT SPECIFIC PROTECTION MEASURES IN THE CASE OF A MINOR WHO NEEDS SPECIAL PROTECTION?  
The universe of the Internet has allowed the expansion of new psychological problems in minors, such as obsessive behaviors, eating disorders, etc. The system proposed in the PoCs, by filtering content on the device, would allow content providers to include configurations in their apps so that families can incorporate additional protections over specific content, complementing (not necessarily replacing) parental control tools.  
24. IS THE RESPONSIBILITY OF CONTENT PROVIDERS THEN LIMITED TO LABELING THE CONTENT THEY OFFER?  
No, these providers must assume their responsibilities in the complete data processing that allows for the protection of minors against inappropriate content. In this processing, content labeling is an essential part, but content providers must also take their share of responsibility regarding age verification mechanisms or the enforcement of access policies. It is a shared responsibility in a complex ecosystem that involves other operators such as identity providers, content access app providers, browsers, age verification solution providers, etc. However, this complexity is not a reason to evade such responsibility. The governance model must ensure the assumption of responsibilities.  
25. IN THE SOLUTION PRESENTED IN THE PROOF OF CONCEPT, CONTENT PROVIDERS DO NOT HAVE TO PERFORM ANY TASK. DOES THE ENTIRE MINOR PROTECTION SYSTEM FALL ON USERS AND THE VERIFICATION APP?  
The operation of this system establishes requirements for service and content providers. On one hand, they must label their content appropriately or use a common format to determine that the entire site has age restrictions (online gaming, pornographic content platforms, etc.), with labels that are adequately interpretable by browsers or other content access applications. The same service and content providers, when offering their own content access applications, must implement protection mechanisms and consult the age verification app. Browsers must also implement protection mechanisms and consult the age verification app based on the content labeling they receive. The mentioned protection mechanisms must include content filtering on the device. All of them must implement governance measures to ensure transparency and auditability and to prevent app impersonation, exploitation of potential vulnerabilities, or access to services with unlabeled content, to mention a few examples.  
26. HOW CAN A CONTENT PROVIDER ASSUME ITS RESPONSIBILITY IN THE PROTECTION OF MINORS AGAINST INAPPROPRIATE CONTENT IF IT DOES NOT RECEIVE ANY INFORMATION ABOUT THE PERSON

WHO IS TRYING TO ACCESS YOUR CONTENT? SHOULD THEY NOT RECEIVE ANY ATTRIBUTE RELATED TO THEIR AGE?  
The PoCs developed by the AEPD demonstrate that it is possible to assume this responsibility without needing to receive any type of data about the user, thus complying with the ten principles proposed and avoiding the risks and threats identified in the age verification solutions currently in use. It is possible to carry out all processing without sending data outside the user's device and, therefore, it is not necessary for the content provider to receive any type of data about the person accessing their content. Similarly, the provider can assume their responsibilities without needing to execute any processing on their own servers or develop proprietary solutions.  
27. ON PLATFORMS THAT REQUIRE THE CREATION OF AN ACCOUNT FOR ACCESS TO CONTENT AND WHERE THERE IS CONTENT FOR MINORS AND ADULTS, WOULD THIS SOLUTION FOR AGE VERIFICATION DEVELOPED IN THE PoCs BE EQUALLY VALID?  
Yes, content suitable for all audiences would be accessed normally. And only in the case of content inappropriate for minors (principle 4) would verification processes be carried out to determine if a minor needs protection.  
28. HOW IS IT GUARANTEED THAT THOSE WHO EXERCISE PARENTAL AUTHORITY ARE THE ONES WHO EFFECTIVELY DECIDE WHAT THE MINOR CAN SEE AND WHAT THEY CANNOT?  
According to the ten principles that have been developed, through the governance mechanisms of the solutions (principle 10), the auditing of the execution of content filtering for adults, and active participation in content labeling systems (principle 8).  
29. IS IT NOT POSSIBLE THAT WHAT HAPPENS IN THE END IS THAT AN ADULT DOWNLOADS ALL THE CONTENT AND SERVES IT FROM THEIR OWN SERVER FROM WHERE THEY DISTRIBUTE IT PUBLICLY, WITHOUT LABELS OR LIMITATIONS? OR THAT SOME CONTENT PROVIDERS FOR ADULTS DO NOT LABEL IT AS SUCH? OR THAT A "PARALLEL" INTERNET IS GENERATED?  
At this point, governance mechanisms must be applied, along with the authentication tools of age verification apps and content access/browsers (the latter must incorporate protection mechanisms and consult the age verification app).  
Any solution that presents itself as perfect is not truthful and underestimates human imagination. Governance mechanisms must continuously monitor new vulnerabilities and weaknesses and react to them.

30. IS IT NOT POSSIBLE THAT BROWSERS OR APPLICATIONS FOR ACCESSING CONTENTS THAT BYPASS AGE FILTERING AND DO NOT VERIFY THE AGE OF USERS MAY EMERGE?  
At this point, governance mechanisms must be applied, along with the authentication tools of age verification apps and content access/browsers (the latter must incorporate protection mechanisms and consult the age verification app). It must be assumed that technology does not provide a complete guarantee, but rather requires continuous adaptation. Therefore, governance mechanisms must continuously monitor new vulnerabilities and react to them.  
There are technical solutions that allow content suitable for all audiences to be accessed from any browser or app, but those intended for adults can only be accessed from browsers or apps that perform age verification. Again, the existence of methods to circumvent protections does not justify failing to establish measures and attempting to make them as effective as possible.  
It must be considered that the governance framework of age verification systems and protection against access to inappropriate content, due to its impact, should be incorporated into the incident notification processes and the supervision processes established under the NIS2 Directive.  
31. WHAT HAPPENS IF A VPN IS USED TO ACCESS ADULT CONTENT?  
The system proposed in the PoC is more robust against the use of VPNs than others currently employed, as it does not rely on knowing from where the content request is made to the server.  
On one hand, policies are established and executed locally, as they are executed on the device, regardless of the server accessed or where the request originates.  
32. IS IT NOT POSSIBLE FOR A MINOR TO ACCESS INAPPROPRIATE CONTENT USING AN ADULT'S DEVICE?  
The accreditation, periodically or at each device restart, for example, that the person using the device is its owner and has the verified age, prevents this circumstance.  
It is always necessary to strike a balance between the reliability of the age verification solution and usability (proof cannot be requested constantly as this would hinder navigation through adult content).  
The existence of methods to circumvent protections does not justify failing to establish measures and attempting to make them as effective as possible.  
33. WOULD IT NOT BE SIMPLER TO BLOCK CONTENT ON THE SIM CARD AS IS DONE IN OTHER COUNTRIES?  
It is necessary to be cautious, as this type of solution could easily allow for the detection of minors, who would be registered in some way when acquiring the phone, activating the SIM, etc., as a minor.  
Moreover, reality shows us that many phones for minors are acquired without indicating that the use will be for a minor, or that phones from adults are inherited.  
In any case, any solution should comply with the ten principles to ensure compliance in terms of data protection and the rights and freedoms of citizens. Additionally, different solutions could be applied simultaneously for greater assurance.  
34. WHAT IS THE SCOPE OF THE PoCs, WOULD THEY BE VALID IN AN INTERNATIONAL CONTEXT OR ONLY IN SPAIN?  
They would be valid in an international context as they allow the person accessing content to verify their age through different mechanisms (QR code, digital wallet, official identity documents in physical format). It is sufficient that at least one of these mechanisms is available in the user's country of origin for them to be able to verify their age in some of the proposed ways, such as collecting passport data, which is recorded in a universal application format.  
35. IS THIS AN INITIATIVE SOLELY FROM SPAIN?  
This model has been proposed at the European level. By Spanish initiative, there is already a mandate from the plenary of the European Data Protection Committee for the Key Provisions group to work on age verification criteria, which will be raised in what is known as the "Berlin Group" of the Global Privacy Assembly.  
36. ARE THE PoCs COMPATIBLE WITH THE NEW REGULATION ON THE EUROPEAN DIGITAL IDENTITY, EIDAS2?  
Yes, they are. In fact, one of them, which allows access to content through an Android mobile phone, is based on a digital wallet, which could be the one developed by SGAD and FNMT in Spain in the future. Furthermore, the principles outlined in the ten principles are perfectly compatible (in terms of data minimization, putting the user at the center, and controlling with whom they share their data, etc.) with the spirit of the eIDAS2 regulation.  
37. DO THE PoCs RESPECT THE PRINCIPLE OF TECHNOLOGICAL NEUTRALITY?  
Both the PoCs and the principles they demonstrate are independent of device manufacturers, operating systems, identity providers, etc. The principles are completely neutral in this regard.  
Regarding the PoCs, three have been developed to show how these principles can be translated into real scenarios using different types of devices, from different manufacturers, with different operating systems, heterogeneous identity providers, etc. Any system that respects the principles is valid, regardless of the technologies or architectures selected for its implementation.
