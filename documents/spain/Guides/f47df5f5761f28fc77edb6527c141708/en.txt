When we give a mobile to our children, we provide them with the opportunity to access a wide variety of information, relationships, and content. Children and adolescents have been using technology since they were very young, but they are not always aware of the risks they may be exposed to. To do so, assess their level of maturity for their age. You can use a family contract to establish a commitment from both parties.
PLAN FOR THE 
ARRIVAL OF THE MOBILE
1
SUPERVISE AND SET 
RULES AND LIMITS
Make sure that the mobile does not take away time from other key activities in their development (sleeping, studying, playing offline, etc.). At first, you can use some parental control software, but don’t relax; the best supervision is in your hands. Keep in mind that excessive blocking can be counterproductive. Keep the possibility of unlocking content that is of interest and appropriate for their level of maturity open, and agree with them on filters, restrictions, and time limits.

BE CAREFUL WITH 
DATA ON SOCIAL MEDIA
The device most commonly used for gaming is the mobile. Video games offer entertainment, socialization, and learning. Most are no longer a solitary source of leisure but connected with other people, which brings both the advantages and risks of social networks, and therefore, the same precautions should be applied. Offer them games suitable for their age and maturity. Not all video games pose the same degree of exposure to inappropriate content (violence, sex, offensive language, etc.). Accompanying them in their digital life also means supervising what they play, how much time they dedicate to it, and how they behave.
By giving a mobile to our children, we grant them access to a world full of possibilities, with a wealth of information at their fingertips and open channels of communication online. Guide them in social networks, which ones they can use and which ones they cannot. Discuss the dangers they may encounter on the internet and social media and how to manage their data, which may be seen by more people than they expect. Set up their social media profile together so that not everyone can see what is published. Choose what profile information is public and which users can access it by establishing different levels of privacy. Likewise, for each post made on a social network, teach them how to configure who can see it. Keep in mind that minors under 14 years old need your consent to register. Ensure that only strictly necessary data is included to complete the registration and do not use their real name but a nickname or alias.

Who do they play with? What contacts do they have on their social networks? Who do they follow? Respecting their privacy, take an interest in these matters. Teach them not to share personal information or photographs with strangers, as this can lead to situations of cyberbullying, blackmail, or grooming.
It is also very important that they do not accept or add as contacts on any platform or service people they do not know in real life. Under no circumstances should they provide information about where they live or with whom, what school they attend, what team they play for, or any other information that would allow others to locate them in the real world. Actions and decisions on the internet also have consequences. Before posting on social media or forwarding photos, videos, or audio in which other people appear, they must ensure that the other person agrees to it. They should not spread information about other people online without their consent. They should also not create or disseminate false or malicious information. Explain to them the possible consequences of what is sent or published. What may seem like a fun idea today can have unpredictable consequences.

Teach them that they can tell you about any situation that has made them feel uncomfortable. Being available to help in a timely manner can prevent the situation from becoming more serious, especially if the content they have seen online involves danger, causes anxiety or fear, or if the content is sexual or violent. The AEPD has a priority channel for reporting the unauthorized publication of sensitive, sexual, or violent content (photographs, videos, audio, or information that identifies individuals) on the internet and requesting its urgent removal.
