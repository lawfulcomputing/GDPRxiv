INTRODUCTION  
DATA PROTECTION IN SPANISH SMES  

The validity of the application of the new European regulation on data protection and its impact on the activities of SMEs, along with the growing importance of data management in smaller companies, generates the need to understand the incidence of active data protection management, as well as the position and level of adaptation of companies to these changes. For this reason, the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD), with the support of the Spanish Confederation of Small and Medium Enterprises (CEPYME), has launched a survey aimed at these types of entities in order to obtain information on the current state of these issues, as well as to define any future actions that improve SMEs' management in terms of data protection.  
The starting point of the measurement carried out by the AEPD is the resources managed by SMEs, which are likely to generate legal obligations in the field of data protection.  
Based on this starting point, we have measured the actions already taken by SMEs, that is, the facts of behavior regarding data protection, as the first group of indicators of the state of the issue in the field of study. With this, we will be able to know what resources SMEs manage, what types of personal data they have regarding those resources, and what they are already doing in the field of protecting such data.  
However, the relevance of the current regulatory framework is undeniable, in the sense that companies may or may not be aware of both the Regulation and their obligations, as well as the resources that the AEPD has available for companies to complete that information.  
Experience and knowledge are the two forms of interaction that SMEs have in relation to the regulatory field, and both produce certain impacts that need to be measured and understood.  
SMEs, which manage resources with associated personal data, that have or have not done certain things to protect them, that know or do not know the new obligations, that have certain perceptions based on their experience and knowledge, that is, in their interaction with reality, and that share or do not share some basic ideas about data and its protection, are in a more or less positive disposition (attitude) to undertake certain actions relevant to data protection.  
In summary, as shown in the following graph, in terms of the effects that data protection regulation seeks to influence, a cyclical sequence of facts, perceptions, ideas, and attitudes among SMEs would occur, which will modify the facts to some extent and will produce changes throughout the system.  
Now, in the information cycle that has been constructed, it can also be formulated how to significantly influence to improve data protection behaviors in the field of SMEs. This is reflected by projecting action onto information.  

Finally, in the adopted approach, there are some hypotheses about certain factors that could imply variability in the measured indicators. This would be specifically analyzed in three factors:  
––  
Size, determined by the number of employees.  
––  
Activity, aggregated into large sectors.  
––  
Territory, which, due to sampling restrictions, will be treated in an aggregated manner, encompassing several Autonomous Communities.

Regarding concrete actions, although 5 out of 7 questioned actions have already been executed by the majority of companies, they carry an unequal weight:
––
Three of them have been executed by more than 80% of SMEs. These are:
•	Provides a consultancy service on personal data protection or plans to contract it in the short term
•	At the time, proceeded to notify the files to the AEPD
•	Has prepared the security document.
––
Two others have been executed by the majority of companies, but in proportions lower than 70%. These are:
•	Includes informative clauses in the forms (69%)
•	In contracts, clauses on data protection are included (58%)
––
Finally, there are two actions with minority execution, which does not reach 40%. These are:
•	Has anticipated or addressed requests for the exercise of individuals' rights (39%)
•	Use of the AEPD website (24%)

This section aims to determine, fundamentally, whether SMEs have knowledge of the Regulation and the new obligations it entails.

In general, the perception of data protection in SMEs, understood as the way these entities affected by regulatory change process its impact, is positive.
The survey verifies that the impact produced by the regulation and, especially, recent changes, is also positive:
––
Around 8 out of 10 SMEs perceive the data protection regulation as positive.
––
Almost 9 out of 10 believe that the Regulation is better than the previous regulation regarding data protection.
However, it should be noted that among the companies that do not view the regulation positively, it is noted that it does not adapt well to the resources available to SMEs and that the requirement to provide more resources for compliance, as well as the application of sanctions, could jeopardize the current situation of smaller companies, hindering the normal development of their activity.
The AEPD has resources that minimize this contingency. Such resources are rated positively:
––
The average rating of AEPD resources is 7, on a scale of 0 to 10.
Similarly, areas for improvement are also pointed out, referring to the density of content on the website, the clarity of the same, or the adaptation of the same to the needs and characteristics of SMEs.

CONCLUSIONS
In summary, from a global perspective of the results, regarding a reality dominated by three resources: clients, suppliers, and employees, the majority position of SMEs is positive in terms of action, perception, vision, and attitude regarding data protection, but not without some manifest or latent risks:

––
The small size of these organizations carries the risk that their perception of data protection may deteriorate due to costs, the resources required, the possibility of sanctions for non-compliance, and that, consequently, it may excessively interfere with their activity.

––
This risk is not compensated by the valuation that SMEs place on data as an important asset, a proposition that is gaining increasing prominence with digitalization, but which, in the realm of active data protection management, has not yet taken strong hold.

Therefore, extending knowledge, properly sizing the implementation tools and the implementation actions themselves, in accordance with the resources to be managed and the size of the companies, as well as extending and deepening the understanding of data as a source of value, are conditions that will undoubtedly favor the success of the GDPR, based on a distinctly positive perception and general idea of this regulation by SMEs.

Population: SMEs residing in Spain.
Stratification: by Autonomous Community (CCAA), by Size, and by Sector of activity.
Sample distribution: semi-proportional by CCAA and non-proportional for the “size x sector” cross-section, in order to have sufficient numbers in the strata of larger size, which have a low population weight. Proportionality has been restored for overall results in the data processing through weighting.
Sample size: the sample size is 1,106 units.
Sampling error: for a confidence level of 95.5%, in the most unfavorable case (p=q=50), the sampling error for overall data is + 3 percentage points.
Fieldwork dates: The fieldwork was conducted between April 26 and May 16. 
The fieldwork was executed by a team of 11 interviewers, coordinated by a team leader and under the coincidental quality control of 2 supervisors.
