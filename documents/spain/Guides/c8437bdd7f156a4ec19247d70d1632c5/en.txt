In light of this new regulatory framework, the Agency established in 2019 a Working Group on 'Minors, digital health and privacy' with the aim of studying and proposing initiatives and measures to protect minors in the digital realm, where the services provided and the content offered through social networks and equivalent services require the processing of personal data for their operation. The Working Group, which includes various stakeholders involved and committed to the protection and safeguarding of the best interests of minors, from both the public and private sectors, such as professional organizations and scientific societies, has been meeting since then to discuss issues affecting their protection and safety in the online world.  
The work of the Group has led to various initiatives, among which it is worth highlighting, in the area of digital education, the promotion and collaboration for the creation of the Aseguratic portal, which contains the most extensive repository of materials, resources, and tools (cataloged and organized by subject, age, source, and recipients: teachers, families, students) available on the website of the National Institute of Educational Technologies and Teacher Training (INTEF); and in the area of digital health, promoting collaboration and the development of tools to prevent and detect inappropriate or addictive use of ICT, such as the Family Digital Plan of the Spanish Pediatric Association. In this regard, it is particularly noteworthy the Decalogue with the criteria and requirements that age verification systems must meet for access to online content for adults, ensuring that they are effective and respectful of the data protection and privacy framework.  
The various actions taken by the Agency to protect individuals on the Internet have been publicly recognized in recent years, with 28 awards granted by public and private institutions and entities, both national and international.  

Strategy and lines of action: Minors, digital health and privacy  
The Spanish Agency for Data Protection (AEPD), an independent administrative authority that safeguards the rights and freedoms of individuals regarding the processing of their personal data, pays special attention to the protection of the rights of the most vulnerable individuals. Among them are minors: children and adolescents in full neurodevelopment and in the process of forming and acquiring the principles and values that will shape their personality, which require specific protection, as stated in the Convention on the Rights of the Child of 1989.  
The Agency established in 2015 priority action axes, including its own program for the protection and education of minors, which was fundamentally justified by the advancement of digital technologies and their impact on the processing of minors' data. Among other measures, the Agency launched a communication channel primarily aimed at educational centers, teachers, monitors, parents, and students; an inspection plan was carried out on cloud services in the educational field; alliances were established with various stakeholders involved in the protection and well-being of minors; materials, content, and resources were developed for families, teachers, and minors; and awards for good educational practices regarding the responsible use of the Internet and social networks were created.

II. A strengthened strategy for the effective protection of minors  
Strategy and lines of action: Minors, digital health, and privacy  
The actions deployed by the Agency, in collaboration with an increasing number of public and private actors, highlight the multidisciplinary challenge we face and also the diversity of measures that must be adopted in many areas of action, which require a coordinated effort from Public Administrations, the industry, and families. The work of the Agency has come to configure a sum of measures that align with the competencies of other bodies, creating a forum for reflection and sharing of initiatives aimed at protecting minors, enriched with contributions from very diverse fields.  
The definition of specific actions, their approval, and implementation consequently have different drivers and responsible parties, but the joint reflection is extremely constructive and necessary.  
From the perspective of the Agency's competence, its main objective, as an independent public authority responsible for safeguarding the privacy and data protection of individuals, is to protect minors from the inappropriate use of their personal information. The Working Group created has highlighted that protecting the data of minors is protecting their development.  
The digital world undoubtedly offers numerous advantages and benefits that need to be leveraged, but it also exposes minors, who have become active consumers of continuously evolving digital technologies, to a variety of risks with potential harmful effects that severely affect various areas, from health—whether physical, mental, psychosocial, or sexual, particularly due to digital violence—to neurodevelopment, learning, and the acquisition of cognitive skills, to family and social relationships, and to consumption habits, which are monitored and monetized.  
Urgent and rapid action is required to enable minors to take advantage of the opportunities offered by ICT in all areas: education, social, leisure, etc. We must contribute to preventing the risk situations that may arise from the increase in their consumption: inappropriate or addictive use, attention deficit, difficulties in disconnecting, access to adult content, especially pornography, advertising, biases, profiling, consumption involving the exchange of personal data, which are subject to monetization by the various online services heavily used by this group.  
This concerning reality, evidenced by numerous reports and studies conducted by different institutions and organizations, justifies and demands a new impetus to address the harmful effects of the digital environment, the inappropriate use of which has created a serious public health problem that needs to be tackled by all those individuals and entities that care for the well-being of minors. A revised strategy that provides the necessary resources to make the use of the Internet by minors a safe and healthy place.

1. Regulatory Framework  
The protection of minors in the digital world affects different areas, one of which is privacy and the processing of personal data that impacts all of them, requiring a comprehensive regulatory framework that provides adequate guarantees.  
The various areas in which harmful consequences arise from the inappropriate use of the Internet by minors lead to a dispersed regulation. The Additional Provision 19 of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDPGDD), states that the Government will submit a bill specifically aimed at guaranteeing the rights of minors in light of the impact of the Internet, in order to strengthen their security and combat the discrimination and violence they suffer through new technologies. The Observations of the Committee on the Rights of the Child regarding digital rights will also be taken into account.  
The Agency, through the Working Group on ‘Minors, Digital Health, and Privacy’:  
1.1. Will promote the adoption of complementary provisions and developments of the Comprehensive Protection Law for Childhood and Youth.  
1.2. Will actively participate in the development of Guidelines on minors by the European Data Protection Board within the framework of the General Data Protection Regulation.  
1.3. Will promote the regulation of the processing of neurodata and the corresponding neuro-rights, especially in the field of services aimed at minors (video games, virtual reality, metaverse).

2. Exercise of Powers  
The protection of minors in the digital environment, which has the right to data protection as a fundamental element and the Agency as an institution for its guarantee, affects, as noted, various institutions, bodies, and entities, which requires cooperation and collaboration among all of them to achieve a comprehensive framework that guarantees the rights and freedoms of minors on the internet, as stated in General Comment number 25 of the Committee on the Rights of the Child, and protects them from the risks of inappropriate use.  
The alliance and collaboration between bodies and institutions constitutes one of the areas in which the Agency will deploy its actions, with the main aim of promoting a comprehensive law for the protection of children and youth in the digital world.  
In exercising its area of action and the powers attributed to it, the Agency will prioritize certain lines of action:  

2.1. It will exercise its sanctioning powers, such as the temporary or definitive limitation of the processing of personal data, including the suspension of data processing from websites with pornographic content that violate data protection regulations.  

2.2. It will seek alliances with European authorities to exercise investigative powers over the social networks most widely used by minors.  

2.3. It will continue to promote the work of the 'Minors, Digital Health, and Privacy' Group, to which institutions and experts in the field will be invited, in order to collaborate in identifying regulatory measures; and will hold periodic meetings with the main providers of social networks and equivalent digital services to implement effective age verification systems for access to content and its monitoring.  

2.4. It will convey to other forums and international organizations (Asia-Pacific Privacy Network, African Data Protection Network, UNESCO, OECD, Ibero-American General Secretariat, ISO), with the aim of standardization, the criteria and requirements for the development and implementation of effective age verification systems that comply with data protection and privacy regulations.  

2.5. It will collaborate, within the existing institutional framework, in raising awareness about the privacy and security of minors on the Internet and the assistance that is channeled through various help lines such as the INHOPE network managed by INCIBE, or the support for cases of bullying.  

2.6. It will strengthen cooperation with the CNMC for the labeling of audiovisual content, the evaluation of the suitability of age verification systems, and the other measures provided for in Articles 89, 90, and 91 of the General Audiovisual Communication Law, and the study of its application to interactive video games.  

2.7. It will support audiovisual authorities in promoting codes of conduct for the protection of minors in audiovisual communication services and video-sharing platforms.  

2.8. It will collaborate with consumer authorities to adopt measures for the protection of minors in the online consumption sphere.

3. Digital education  
Article 84 of the LOPDPGDD provides, as a measure for the protection of minors on the Internet, that parents, guardians, curators, or legal representatives will ensure that minors make balanced and responsible use of digital devices and information society services in order to guarantee the proper development of their personality and preserve their dignity and fundamental rights. This provision is reaffirmed in Organic Law 8/2021, of June 4, on comprehensive protection of childhood and adolescence against violence (LOPIVI), which states that Public Administrations, within the scope of their respective competencies, will promote and guarantee specialized, initial, and ongoing training in fundamental rights of children and adolescents to professionals who have regular contact with minors, which will include specific training in safety and safe and responsible use of the Internet, including issues related to intensive use and the generation of behavioral disorders.  
Minors and their parents, in many cases, are unaware of the extensive exchange of personal data that occurs when using digital services. It is necessary to raise awareness and educate about the value of privacy and how to protect and share personal data to safeguard rights and freedoms and avoid harm and detriment.  
Therefore, it is essential to work with families on responsible and healthy use of the Internet, as the digital example set by parents is key in education.  
For its part, Article 92 of the LOPDPGDD states that educational centers and any natural or legal persons who carry out activities involving minors will guarantee the protection of the best interests of the child and their fundamental rights, especially the right to the protection of personal data, in the publication or dissemination of their personal data through information society services.  
In this area, the Agency:  
3.1. Will support and collaborate in the creation of schools and spaces for digital education and awareness for families in collaboration with the involved agents (Educational Administrations (INTEF), INCIBE, State School Council).

4. Digital health and wellbeing  
The problematic use of technology impacts all areas of people's health, particularly affecting minors who are in the full development of their personality, impacting their neurodevelopment and learning.  
Not all families have received information and, therefore, are not aware of the effects it has on sleep and rest, or those derived from decreased physical activity, visual fatigue, pain caused by poor posture, anxiety, decreased attention, impulsivity, social isolation, or the adoption of risky behaviors.  
In this area, the Agency:  
4.1. Will encourage and support initiatives aimed at researching the consequences of inappropriate or addictive use of social networks and equivalent digital services and will contribute to the dissemination of resources based on scientific evidence aimed at preventing, detecting, and treating situations arising from such addictive behaviors.  
4.2. Will collaborate and support health administrations and scientific societies in initiatives aimed at addressing the impact that inappropriate or addictive use of the Internet has on the health (physical, mental, psychosocial, sexual) and neurological development of minors.  
4.3. Will continue to support the Spanish Association of Pediatrics in the dissemination and use of the Family Digital Plan by families and health professionals.  
4.4. Will collaborate with the General Council of Official Colleges of Psychologists within the framework of the collaboration protocol signed to analyze proposals that contribute to protecting the wellbeing of minors in the digital environment.  
4.5. Will collaborate for the use in the school environment of the Healthy Child Protocol for the healthy use of ICT by students.  
4.6. Will collaborate with the General Council of Official Colleges of Physicians, within the framework of the signed collaboration protocol, to carry out actions that contribute to protecting the health of minors in the digital environment.  
4.7. Will support the inclusion in the program for substance or behavioral addictions of the addictive use of the Internet, social networks, and equivalent services.  
4.8. Will collaborate within the framework of the Ibero-American Network for Data Protection with the authorities of the Network in the working group created on Health and Digital Violence.
