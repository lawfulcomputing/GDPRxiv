THE DUTY TO INFORM AND OTHER MEASURES OF  
PROACTIVE RESPONSIBILITY IN MOBILE DEVICE APPS  

I.  
INTRODUCTION  
This technical note is aimed at entities involved in the development, distribution, and exploitation of mobile device apps, particularly those that play the role of data controllers or joint controllers in each of their areas of competence, as well as other agents involved in the mobile app ecosystem, such as application developers and library developers, among others.  
In general, the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) has various published resources to guide compliance with data protection obligations, such as the “Guide for Compliance with the Duty to Inform,” the “Decalogue for Adapting Internet Privacy Policies to the GDPR,” the “GDPR Guide for Data Controllers,” and the “Guidelines for Drafting Contracts Between Controllers and Processors.” The then Article 29 Working Party also published an “Opinion 02/2013 on Smart Device Applications.” This technical note aims to expand and complement these resources by establishing specific guidelines in the case of mobile device apps, developing certain specific aspects related to the duty to inform and other measures of proactive responsibility.  
These guidelines are derived from the conclusions of work carried out in a collaborative framework between the Polytechnic University of Madrid (UPM) and the AEPD on mobile device apps in the educational and welfare sectors, which are described in Annex I.  

II.  
SPECIFIC GUIDELINES FOR MOBILE DEVICE APPS  
Regarding the duty to inform, the analysis of the mobile device apps studied concludes that there are certain compliance aspects that require special attention from data controllers:  
1. The information provided to users about the processing of their personal data must comply with the requirements established in Articles 13 and 14 of the GDPR and Article 11 of the LOPDGDD, particularly concerning layered information, as outlined in the “Guide for Compliance with the Duty to Inform” and the “Decalogue for Adapting Internet Privacy Policies to the GDPR.”  
2. Such information, in the form of a privacy policy, must be available both within the application and in the app store. In this way, the user can consult it before installing the application or at any time during its use.  
3. Access to the privacy policy must be easily achievable from within the application and require the user to perform a number of interactions.

reduced, if possible to a maximum of two clicks as recommended by the GT29 in its guidelines on transparency.  
4. The data controller must clearly identify themselves in the privacy policy, and in cases where data controllers are established outside the EU and offer their applications to users in Europe, they must have appointed a representative in the EU and identify them in the privacy policy as well.  
5. The information about the processing must be complete and consistent both in the app store, if applicable, and in the application itself. There cannot be discrepancies between the two, and this also applies in the case of pre-installed applications on marketed devices.  
6. The language in which the privacy policies are described must be appropriate for the target user of the application, taking into account their age and level of knowledge; furthermore, the language used must also be considered. For example, applications available in Spanish and therefore aimed at Spanish-speaking users must provide the privacy policy in Spanish, without prejudice to the possibility of it being displayed in other languages. These aspects are especially relevant in the case of applications aimed at minors.  
7. Privacy policies must be concrete and specific about the processing of personal data being carried out. To avoid "information fatigue," it is necessary to avoid providing generic, non-specific information about the application. The privacy policy should avoid, for example, describing a set of applications or other services of the organization such as its website.  
8. The privacy policy of the app must provide the user with all the information about the processing of personal data that it intends to carry out, precise information about which data and processing are necessary for the basic functioning of the application, which are optional, and all relevant additional information regarding the processing that will be carried out with the data. Additionally, the privacy policy should indicate the permissions that the application may request (directly or through third-party libraries) for access to data and resources, for what processing and purpose those permissions are requested, and to what extent (reading, writing, etc.). For example, it must inform whether the application will process the data only when it is running due to user action in the foreground or if it also needs to access it when running in the background. Users should also be provided with information on how they can manage the permissions granted to the application so that they can decide at all times whether to grant or revoke such permissions, or under what conditions they grant them.  
9. When the legitimacy for the processing of personal data through the app is consent, such consent must be requested in a granular manner, that is, selectively and independently for the different processing and purposes. The installation and use of the app cannot be conditioned on obtaining consent for processing that is not necessary to provide the defined service.  
10. Ambiguous or empty clauses should not be used, such as, for example, "any data may be collected or disseminated, or retained indefinitely" or "we collect your data in order to improve your user experience."

11. Specific information regarding the data retention periods and the final destination of the data once those periods have ended must be included.  
12. In the same vein, specific information related to the logic applied in the profiling and automated decision-making processes must be included, or a link to consult such information, such as that used for personalizing advertisements.  
13. The definition of the purposes of processing and their legal bases must be clear and precise, as well as the personal data collected for each of those purposes.  
14. It is important to provide users with information about their rights regarding data protection and to provide mechanisms and procedures for exercising those rights effectively.  
15. If applicable, information about the existence of international data transfers must be provided in a concrete and specific manner.  
Data controllers who commission the development, production, and/or operation of applications to third parties with access to personal data must ensure compliance with the requirements established in the GDPR for each of the parties. For these cases, the "GDPR Guide for Data Controllers" and "Guidelines for Drafting Contracts between Controllers and Processors" are particularly useful.  
The following requirements should be highlighted:  
16. The processing must be regulated by a contract or legal link that establishes the object, duration, nature, purpose of the processing, type of personal data, categories of data subjects, and the obligations and rights of the controller.  
17. The contract for the commission will specifically stipulate that the processor will process personal data only in accordance with the documented instructions of the controller, meaning that the processor must not introduce other personal data processing activities into the application that the controller may be unaware of, such as those that may be introduced by including third-party libraries for advertising, analytics, or other purposes.  
18. The contract for the commission will stipulate that the processor will take the measures indicated by the controller regarding the security of the processing, specifically including good development practices and taking into account privacy by design and by default from the very conception of the application.  
In particular, the following practices should be given special consideration:  
19. Providing granularity in the management of access permissions to protected resources of the system in accordance with what is established in the privacy policy. An example is limiting access permissions to a resource, such as the images folder, instead of granting a generic access permission to the device's storage.

20. Respect user preferences regarding privacy, for example, concerning ad personalization, avoiding, where applicable, access even to advertising identifiers.  
21. Avoid access to unique global identifiers along with the device's advertising identifier, which would allow for assignments that could nullify user protection measures such as changing their advertising identifier.  
22. Avoid the dissemination of personal data to analytics and advertising services from the moment the application is launched, without the user having been able to make any use or adjustment.  
23. Ensure that there is no dissemination of personal data without the knowledge of the data controller, concerning communications initiated from third-party libraries used by the developer to enhance the application's functionality or monetize it.  
24. Avoid the transfer of personal data to unspecified or unreported recipients in the privacy policy, who have the role of data controllers or joint controllers.  
25. Avoid unreported international data transfers in the privacy policy.  
26. Using advanced methods for encrypting communications (e.g., certificate-pinning) provides an additional guarantee for user privacy to be considered depending on the characteristics of data processing.  

**III. CONCLUSIONS**  
Transparency in the processing of personal data by mobile apps is a crucial aspect for compliance with data protection regulations, that is, for the protection of the rights and freedoms of citizens.  
The AEPD has made available to data controllers two resources to facilitate compliance with the transparency and information requirements of the GDPR, one generic, such as the “Guide for Compliance with the Duty to Inform,” and one specific for internet applications: the “Ten-Point Guide for Adapting Internet Privacy Policies to the GDPR.”  
This technical note extends the previous guides and aims to address specific aspects of the mobile app environment where special care has been identified as necessary.  
To this end, it should be emphasized that information to the user must be provided in clear and simple language, concisely, transparently, intelligibly, easily accessible, and tailored to the interested party or potential user of the application. Therefore, it is essential to consider the audience to whom the application is directed when drafting the informative clauses of the privacy policy.  
Certain sensors and data storage of the mobile device are a potential source of personal data that applications or third-party libraries included in the applications may access. The operating system of the mobile device protects access to these resources through permissions with different levels of protection.  
While the device shows the user a notification requesting their authorization to access such resources, in many cases, the information displayed is not sufficient in the context of the GDPR, nor is the granularity of the permission specified correctly, as it must include, among other information, the purpose of processing those data. The need to access such resources must be properly informed in the application's privacy policy, so that the user can decide whether or not to grant authorization to the application to access such resources.  
Secondly, guidelines are also developed for data controllers who commission the development, production, and/or operation of applications to third parties with access to personal data, ensuring compliance with the requirements established in the GDPR for each of the parties. These guidelines complement the “GDPR Guide for Data Controllers” and “Guidelines for Drafting Contracts Between Controllers and Processors.”  
The data controller must ensure compliance with all active accountability requirements set forth in the GDPR, also ensuring that the processor only processes the data according to their instructions and taking measures to ensure that this is the case.

ANNEX I: ORIGIN AND METHODOLOGY  
This technical note has been developed within the framework of the work on privacy and data protection under the Strategic Plan of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection for the period 2015-2019. The first is aimed at the processing of personal data carried out in apps for mobile devices used in compulsory education environments, while the second focuses on apps for mobile devices for monitoring physical activity, well-being, and health. These works have been carried out by the Polytechnic University of Madrid, under the direction and coordination of this Agency.  
The Strategic Plan of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection 2015-2019 establishes in its strategic axis number 2 “Innovation and data protection: a factor of trust and quality assurance” a line of action through which the Evaluation and Technological Studies Unit is created, among whose objectives is the preparation of studies and reports on technological initiatives and projects (point 2.5 of the aforementioned plan).  
In this regard, the AEPD considers the need to carry out proactive work among those responsible for products and services that process personal data, especially the need to conduct studies on services of the so-called connected society, among which are the works subject to this report, in which the data used are particularly sensitive due to the source subject, as they involve minors in the study directed at applications for use in compulsory education environments and, on the other hand, due to the nature of the data since in the study on applications for monitoring physical activity, the data has a focus close to that of health.  
At the same time, it aims to promote collaboration between this Agency and the university-research environment to encourage technical research aimed at fostering privacy and data protection.  
The work has involved carrying out technical research tasks aimed at:  
• Detecting information flows in apps for mobile devices in general,  
• Designing standard procedures that allow for systematic, orderly, and comparable privacy assessments in applications,  
• Evaluating a representative subset of applications useful in the educational environment and related to health and well-being,  
• Evaluating the privacy policies of applications concerning the ten-point adaptation to the GDPR published by the Agency.  
The ultimate goal is to detect those practices that are most harmful to the privacy of users of both groups of applications, in order to provide solutions or alternatives for developers, promoters, and users of such applications.  
This report aims to highlight the main conclusions obtained from both studies, conclusions that could well be extended to other types of apps for mobile devices.  
In the framework of these works, a total of 20 apps for mobile devices for Android terminals have been analyzed, 10 applications from each of the domains of interest, education and well-being.

The criteria applied for the selection of the applications have been:  
• Applications with the highest number of downloads in Google Play Store within each of the study domains, aimed at Spanish-speaking audiences.  
• Ensuring a homogeneous distribution of applications developed and published by small developers, but which have a great reception among users, and applications developed and published by large tech companies.  
• Ensuring a homogeneous distribution between paid applications and free applications.  
In the case of applications for monitoring physical activity that work with an external device such as an activity bracelet, the analysis was conducted on the application connected to the activity bracelet.  
The methodology of these works includes a technical analysis of the information flows of each application using static and dynamic analysis techniques in contrast with an analysis of the privacy policies of the application. Therefore, for each application, a double analysis has been carried out; on one hand, the privacy policies of each application are contrasted with the ten-point privacy policy published by the Agency, and on the other hand, the discrepancies that may appear between what is stated in the privacy policies and what is observed in the static and dynamic analysis are contrasted.
