EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
The purpose of this document is to inform about the use of cookies and similar technologies 
on Public Administration web portals, particularly regarding the use of web analytics services.  
It is directed at data controllers in the Public Sector and their data protection delegates. 
The text analyzes the applicability of the LSSI and data protection regulations, as well as 
the obligations imposed on the controller, depending on the consideration of the service as 
an information society service and whether there is processing of personal data. 
It also presents some typical cases of third-party services commonly used on Public 
Administration websites and their implications concerning the analyzed regulations. 
Finally, options for the use of web analytics services are highlighted, which, when configured 
with the appropriate guarantees, do not involve the processing of personal data and therefore 
do not require consent on Public Administration pages that are not considered information 
society services. 
 
Keywords: Public administrations, DPD, cookies, consent, information, 
web analytics, third parties, LSSI, GDPR, data protection, obligations, services, 
terminal equipment, users.

I. OBJECTIVE AND TARGET AUDIENCE 
This document is directed at data controllers of Public Administrations and their data protection delegates (DPD). It details the obligations of Public Administrations regarding the use of cookies on their portals and, in general, the techniques for using information from users' terminal equipment, particularly the use of web analytics tools.  
This document does not intend to replicate or repeat what is established in the AEPD Guide on the use of cookies, but rather to specify the particular case of the use of these technologies in Internet services of Public Administrations. 
II. INTRODUCTION 
Law 34/2002, of July 11, on information society services and electronic commerce (LSSI) complements the guarantees of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) concerning information society services. 
According to Article 95 of the GDPR, this regulation cannot impose additional obligations for processing in areas where there are specific obligations in the LSSI. 
However, the LSSI is linked to the legal concepts of the GDPR that do not have specific regulation within it, as is the case, for example, with informed consent. 
The LSSI applies to providers of information society services established in Spain. It defines information society services as “any service normally provided for a fee, at a distance, by electronic means and at the individual request of the recipient,” emphasizing that it also includes services not paid for by their recipients to the extent that they constitute an economic activity for the service provider. 
Therefore, regarding the use of cookies by providers of information society services, the LSSI is the applicable special law, regardless of whether the cookies process personal data or not.  
However, in the case of information society services where the use of information related to the use of cookies is employed within the framework of personal data processing, the provisions of the GDPR and the LOPDGDD apply to everything not regulated in the LSSI, such as aspects related to the application of the principle of proactive responsibility, the hiring of data processors, international transfers, or others. 
However, if the entity owning the website is not considered a provider of an information society service, if the information obtained through the use of cookies is used within the framework of personal data processing, the GDPR and the LOPDGDD will apply, even if the LSSI does not.  
Finally, it should be noted that the use of cookies does not imply personal data processing in all cases. 
A Public Administration, by having a web portal or providing services via the Internet, will not always be considered a provider of information society services and, consequently, as a subject obliged under the LSSI. This is the case for Public Administration websites where no services are provided that involve an economic activity.

III. APPLICABLE REGULATIONS  
A Public Administration may have different channels on the Internet enabled through different Internet domains, channels on which specific services can be implemented. The applicability of the LSSI and data protection regulations must be analyzed for each of those domains, channels, and services separately. It could be the case that for one domain or channel or service a regulation applies, and for another domain or channel or service, due to not being considered a provider of information society services and not processing personal data, data protection regulations and the LSSI do not apply.  
A.  
APPLICABILITY OF THE LSSI1  
A Public Administration that offers services on the Internet, for example, through a web portal, by merely having it would not necessarily be considered a provider of information society services and, consequently, as a subject obliged by the LSSI.  
Certain typical activities of Public Administrations, such as the electronic management of tax collection, information about third-party services (for example, mere information on a Town Hall's website about existing rural houses in the municipal area), or the collection of fees for a public service are considered public or general interest activities and, therefore, distinct from the "economic activity" referred to by the LSSI.  
In the resolution of the file (E/08710/2015), resulting from a complaint against a town hall for using cookies on its website without displaying a notice about them, it was established that the LSSI did not apply, since there was no type of activity on the town council's website that could categorize it as a provider of information society services.  
The Legal Report 0083/2014 from the Legal Office regarding the inquiry raised by a Public University also serves as an example, in which it is established that, in practice, public universities, to the extent that they do not carry out economic activities through electronic communications – with the websites serving solely to assist in the provision of the public service of higher education – will not be providers of services of

the information society, so they would not be subject to the obligations under Article 22.2 of the LSSI.  
However, if a domain or channel belonging to a Public Administration provides any type of service that involves an economic activity, such as the sale of books, tickets, etc., the LSSI would apply to those specific domains, channels, or services where such economic activity is carried out, regardless of whether personal data is involved or not. For example, regarding the aforementioned report 0083/2014, when the activity of a Public University’s website does have an economic nature (for example, the sale of books or the offering of proprietary degrees with their corresponding prices), the LSSI will be applicable.  
It is also important to consider the case of a website portal of a Public Administration that allows the use of third-party cookies in exchange for the possibility of including third-party web services on that portal. In that case, such activity could constitute an economic activity as long as those tools or applications are executed embedded within the Public Administration's own domain or channel. This can occur with some web analytics services, captchas or human verification services, videos, maps, and, in general, third-party services that include cookies or similar technologies that may carry out data processing for their own purposes and thus obtain a benefit. In those cases, the Public Administration will be a service provider of the information society, and the LSSI will apply to the website portal where they are included.  
In the report from the legal department of the aforementioned Agency, it is indicated that the incorporation of third-party services with buttons to share content on social networks, or web analytics that involve the use of persistent third-party cookies would imply the application of the LSSI.  
As an example, the sanctioning procedure (PS/00208/2021) considers a regional public transport consortium that creates a Friends of Public Transport Club, which offers information about ideas, leisure plans, discounts, and contests from the different collaborators of the consortium, such as financial entities, businesses, and other entities that carry out economic activities, as a service provider of the information society and therefore subject to the LSSI.  
B.  
APPLICABILITY OF DATA PROTECTION REGULATIONS  
In the event that the information obtained through the use of cookies is employed in the processing of personal data, the provisions of the GDPR and the LOPDGDD will apply, such as those related to the application of the principle of proactive responsibility for data processors, international transfers, rights of data subjects, and others.  
Regarding the processing of personal data, web analytics services are particularly sensitive when they have implications for the processing of personal data, such as contributing to the tracking of user navigation, profiling, supporting automated decisions, etc.  
Likewise, when web analytics tools or other third-party services that use cookies or similar technologies involve connections and transfers of personal data to servers outside the European Economic Area, they will be carrying out international data transfers, and in such cases, all provisions and guarantees provided in Chapter V of the GDPR for international transfers will apply.  

This circumstance is common in services that are promoted as free.

In the event that the Public Administration is also a provider of information society services, and therefore the LSSI applies as a special law, according to Article 95 of the GDPR, no additional obligations will be imposed by the data protection regulations. That is to say, the provisions of the LSSI relating to the legitimacy of processing, consent, and information to the data subjects will apply.  
IV. OBLIGATIONS IMPOSED BY THE REGULATIONS  
A.  
WEBSITE OF A PUBLIC ADMINISTRATION THAT IS NOT CONSIDERED AN INFORMATION SOCIETY SERVICE AND DOES NOT PROCESS PERSONAL DATA THROUGH COOKIES  
A Public Administration that is not considered a provider of information society services is not subject to the LSSI.  
If, in addition, it opts to use cookies without processing personal data or does not use any technology classified as cookies, such activity will not fall within the material scope of either the GDPR or the LSSI (as far as the AEPD is concerned).  
Therefore, it will not be mandatory to obtain consent for the use of cookies, nor to inform the data subjects through cookie or privacy policies, nor to comply with any other obligation. Incorporating unnecessary information policies may confuse and lead to information fatigue for the user.  
B.  
WEBSITE OF A PUBLIC ADMINISTRATION THAT IS NOT CONSIDERED AN INFORMATION SOCIETY SERVICE BUT DOES PROCESS PERSONAL DATA THROUGH THE USE OF COOKIES  
A Public Administration that is not considered a provider of information society services is not subject to the LSSI.  
If it uses information obtained through cookies for the processing of personal data (for example, cookies with unique identifiers), such processing will be subject to the GDPR and the LOPDGDD. This implies compliance with all obligations under the data protection regulations, among others, having a legal basis for processing, informing the data subjects in the privacy policy (not in a cookie policy), offering and enabling the exercise of rights for the data subjects, and establishing the necessary safeguards in the event of international data transfers, without prejudice to other mandatory requirements that may apply to such processing.  
C.  
WEBSITE OF A PUBLIC ADMINISTRATION THAT IS CONSIDERED AN INFORMATION SOCIETY SERVICE  
In the case of a website of a Public Administration that is considered an information society service and uses cookies, there are no particularities regarding any other information society service merely because it is a Public Administration.

V. TYPICAL CASES  
A.  
THIRD-PARTY SERVICES ON WEBSITES  
On the websites of Public Administrations, it may be necessary to use third-party services, such as captchas, videos, or maps.  
When these services, or similar ones, involve the use of cookies and/or the processing of personal data as previously indicated, the data controller must analyze the obligations that may arise from the LSSI and/or the data protection regulations.  
If, instead of embedding these third-party services on their website, they are included as links that redirect to third-party services, the Public Administration will not be considered a subject obliged by either of the two regulations. That is to say, it will not have, among other things, the obligation to inform or to have a legal basis for data processing.  
The responsible Public Administration should inform the user, in advance, that they will be accessing a service outside of the website of that Public Administration with its own processing and data protection policies, so that the user can decide whether or not to access such links.  
This information could be included in an informational banner (not a consent banner), prominently and visibly, so that the risks of the link can be appreciated at the moment the user is about to access those third-party links and in the privacy policy.  
It should be noted that the above applies as long as access to such third-party services is not imperative for providing the services of the Public Administration to citizens, in which case the regulatory obligations and responsibilities that may be required in each case would apply.  
The responsible party must be diligent regarding the use and contracting of third-party services, selecting those that meet the requirements to comply with the obligations imposed by the regulations.  
B.  
OPTIONS FOR WEB ANALYTICS WITHOUT PROCESSING PERSONAL DATA  
Web analytics allows for the extraction of information about the use of a web portal. There are many different web analytics tools with various functionalities, configurations, and ways to use them. For example, not all web analytics tools require cookies to function, and not all web analytics tools process personal data.  
In most cases, having a tool that provides basic anonymized analytical information is more than sufficient to meet the needs of the
Page: 9 of 10  
organization. Using tools that include additional functionalities may involve the processing of personal data, incur in the assumptions of application of the LSSI, and introduce risks to user privacy. If the use of tools with other functionalities is considered, we recommend evaluating the necessity and proportionality concerning the purpose of web analytics being pursued.  
There are technical solutions that allow for a complete web analytics service without involving the processing of personal data or incurring in assumptions that the Administration is considered a provider of information society services.  
For example, there are tools available in the market5 that include the following characteristics:  
- The implementation is carried out on servers in Europe6 and under the responsibility of the data controller, which avoids possible international data transfers and the potential tracking of users' navigation on the Public Administration's website and other websites by third parties.  
- They allow for the anonymization of IP (minimum truncation of 2 bytes) and any data that allows for the identification or tracking of users' navigation.  
- The visitor profile and similar options can be disabled, or such functionality is not present.  
- They allow for the collection of analytical information using techniques other than cookies. For example, although not exclusively, analytics based on the import of server logs7 or CDN.  
- It is possible to disable features such as UserID, Tag Manager, Heatmaps/Session recordings, and DeviceID.  
- They can provide all functionality without using techniques such as tracking pixels, fingerprinting, third-party tracking web beacons, or similar technologies.
