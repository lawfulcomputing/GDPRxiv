WHAT IS THE SO-CALLED 'ZERO COST'?

The term "zero cost" compliance with data protection regulations refers to the practice of offering complete compliance with this legislation at a very low price—or even for free. A service for compliance with specific regulations requires, to achieve a correct result, a detailed individual study of the entity, the types of processing carried out, the IT systems, and the document management systems, as well as a training program for the employees of the entity. Offers that may involve fraud are typically issued by consultancies and companies, primarily targeting SMEs and self-employed individuals, using the fear of sanctions established in the General Data Protection Regulation.

HOW CAN I DISTINGUISH THESE PRACTICES AND WHAT ARE THE RISKS?

Some distinguishing features of these fraudulent practices are as follows:

1. Compliance in form, but not in substance
The "Compliance with data protection regulations" is offered, but in some cases, the intended compliance may be achieved by providing the client with pre-filled forms that supposedly allow them to "complete the file." It should be emphasized that compliance with the GDPR and the LOPDPGDD is not merely formal compliance; it involves reviewing, designing, and applying data protection principles to the specific circumstances of each company. Non-compliance with data protection regulations by the data controller or processor constitutes an infringement, for which the AEPD could initiate the appropriate sanctioning procedure and impose a penalty.

2. Unnecessary services, aggressive practices, and unfair competition
There is an attempt to instill the belief that entities, SMEs, and self-employed individuals must hire a third party for compliance services, implying that doing it with their own means would involve an unbearable effort or cost. This advertising can be misleading, as it offers services that are sometimes unnecessary, disguising them as training when they are not. As part of the advertised service, the provision of a Data Protection Officer may be included, leading to the belief that their appointment is always mandatory, when having a DPO is not obligatory in all cases (art. 34 LOPDGDD). Furthermore, the decision on the best way to achieve compliance should be made based on the type of entity, depending on the data processing they carry out and the human and material resources available.

Additionally, the providers may use institutional signs (for example, the logo of the AEPD or accredited certification entities) to create the impression that they have the endorsement of public bodies. For the purposes set forth in Article 8 of Law 3/1991, of January 10, on Unfair Competition, it is considered "aggressive practice" to act with the intention of impersonating the AEPD in communications to data controllers and processors, generating the appearance that they are acting in collaboration with the AEPD, conducting commercial practices that restrict the decision-making power of the recipients by referencing the possible imposition of sanctions for non-compliance with data protection regulations, or offering documentation intended to create an appearance of compliance with the regulations as a complement to carrying out training actions without having undertaken the necessary actions to verify that such compliance actually occurs. In the event of such aggressive practices, those affected may exercise the appropriate actions before the commercial courts. Furthermore, if the providers are advertising services at a price significantly lower than market prices, this could constitute unfair competition. The authority to investigate behaviors that may violate Law 15/2007, of July 3, on the Defense of Competition, and, where appropriate, sanction possible infringements, would fall to the National Commission of Markets and Competition.

3. Use of funds allocated to training programs
The hiring of the service for compliance with data protection regulations at zero cost, financed through public funds via social security contribution reductions for professional training for employment, may lead to infringements that will be sanctioned by the Labor and Social Security Inspectorate with fines ranging from 626 euros to 187,515 euros, without prejudice to considering, in each case, an infringement for each company and for each training action, the solidarity of the various parties involved in the organization and execution of the training in the return of the amounts unduly obtained and the accessory sanctions that may apply in each case.

4. Tax infringement
Regarding compliance with tax obligations by companies, both by the service provider and the client, training activities aimed at employees are exempt from Value Added Tax (VAT), while the rate applicable to a compliance service with specific legislation would be 21%. WHAT RECOMMENDATIONS DOES THE AGENCY OFFER TO SMEs AND SELF-EMPLOYED INDIVIDUALS?

Before hiring, inform yourself to understand your obligations and what is the safest way for your company to comply with them.

It is advisable for SMEs and self-employed individuals who wish or need to hire services for compliance with data protection regulations to ensure that the services offered by consultants or companies do not engage in the previously mentioned practices.

If you intend to adapt on your own, the AEPD provides several information channels for those responsible (INFORMA Channel) and for citizens (Citizen Attention) and a free assistance tool (FACILITA_RGPD) aimed at companies that process personal data of low risk.

FACILITA_RGPD is aimed at companies that handle low-risk personal data, for example, personal data of customers, suppliers, or human resources. With just three screens of very specific questions, FACILITA allows the user to assess their situation regarding the processing of personal data they carry out: whether they meet the requirements to use the tool or if they need to conduct a risk analysis. FACILITA generates various documents tailored to the specific company, informative clauses that must be included in their personal data collection forms, contractual clauses to be attached to processing agreements, the record of processing activities, and an annex with indicative minimum security measures.
