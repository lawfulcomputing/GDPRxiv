Introduction  

REGULATION (EU) 2016/679 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL, of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation, or GDPR), applicable since May 25, 2018, introduces a series of obligations for data controllers and processors as a novelty compared to the Directive 95/46/EC, such as the principle of accountability, which requires not only compliance with the GDPR but also the ability to demonstrate such compliance.  

In order to contribute to its proper application, the GDPR promotes the development of voluntary compliance mechanisms, such as codes of conduct in its Article 40, which can be used by data controllers and processors as elements to demonstrate that the GDPR is being applied effectively.  

Article 41 of the GDPR stipulates that compliance with a code of conduct may be supervised by a body that has an adequate level of expertise in relation to the subject matter of the code and is accredited for this purpose by the competent supervisory authority (hereinafter the AEPD, referring to the Spanish Agency for Data Protection), and establishes the general requirements to be met (Article 41.2 of the GDPR).  

Before proceeding with the accreditation of a supervisory body, the AEPD presented the draft requirements for the accreditation of supervisory bodies to the European Data Protection Board (hereinafter EDPB) (Article 41, paragraph 3, of the GDPR). In this regard, the EDPB issued its Opinion 1/2020 on the AEPD's draft accreditation requirements for a supervisory body of the code of conduct, in accordance with Article 41 of the GDPR.  

This document establishes the criteria to be applied for the accreditation of supervisory bodies of codes of conduct. In its drafting, the AEPD has taken into account the provisions of Articles 41 and 57 of the GDPR, the EDPB Guidelines 1/2019 on codes of conduct and supervisory bodies under the GDPR (hereinafter the Guidelines), the EDPB Opinion 1/2020 on the AEPD's draft criteria for the accreditation of supervisory bodies of the code of conduct, in accordance with Article 41 of the GDPR, and the relevant opinions of the EDPB.

The GDPR establishes in its Article 41, paragraph 2, the general principles or requirements that supervisory bodies must comply with:

(a) demonstrate their independence and expertise in relation to the subject matter of the code to the satisfaction of the competent supervisory authority;  
(b) establish procedures that allow them to assess the suitability of the controllers and processors concerned to apply the code, supervise compliance with its provisions, and periodically review its application;  
(c) establish procedures and structures to handle complaints regarding violations of the code or how the code has been, or is being, applied by a controller or processor, and to make such procedures and structures transparent to stakeholders and the public; and  
(d) satisfactorily demonstrate to the competent supervisory authority that their functions and duties do not give rise to a conflict of interest.

Accreditation Criteria

1. Independence  
Supervisory bodies will carry out their supervisory functions independently and autonomously, without the influence of the promoter of the code, the members of the code, or the profession, industry, or sector to which it refers. The independence of a supervisory body can be established through a set of formal rules and procedures for its designation and operation.

Supervisory bodies, whether external or internal, will be structured and managed in such a way that their independence and impartiality are not compromised. The promoter of the code will demonstrate that the supervisory body, whether external or internal, included in the code project is independent in the performance of its supervisory functions over the members of the code and the profession they represent, the industry, or the sector to which the code applies, and from the promoter of the code.

The supervisory body, or the individuals responsible for decision-making within the supervisory body, must not have any dependency of any kind (organizational, economic, professional, or personal) on the entity being supervised.

Internal supervisory bodies may be configured as an independent committee or be part of an independent department of the promoter of the code. In the case that an internal supervisory body is proposed, it must have proper separation of personnel, independent management, accountability, and function from other areas of the organization. This can be achieved in various ways, for example, through the use of effective organizational and informational barriers and independent reporting management structures for the association and the supervisory body.1

1.1.  
The independence of the supervisory body can be demonstrated through, among others:

• the statutes or regulations of the supervisory body, including its structure and composition, the procedure for appointing its members, the duration of their term, sufficient to ensure their independence, and provisions regarding the appointment of substitutes in case of a conflict of interest;  
• the description of sufficient material and human resources to carry out its work and ensure its independence;  
• protective measures against interference by the controllers and processors being supervised, or by any other body;  
• protection against dismissal or sanction, whether direct or indirect, for the exercise of their functions;  
• a documented and publicly available policy of the obligations of the supervisory body, including the rules, procedures, functions, and responsibilities.

1.2.  
In addition to the criteria established in the previous point, internal bodies will be subject to additional and specific verification of their independence and must demonstrate the application of complementary measures that ensure that the independence of their supervisory activities is not at stake, among others through:

• the provision of different information channels;  
• the separation of management and the performance of activities;  
• the separation of the reporting line;  
• the use of a specific name or logo different from the name or logo used by the association, organization, or promoter of the code.

1.3.

**2. Conflict of Interest**

The supervisory body will demonstrate that the exercise of its supervisory functions does not give rise to a conflict of interest.

The supervisory body will be responsible for the impartiality of its supervisory activities and will not be subject to direct or indirect sanctions related to the performance of its functions, nor will it allow commercial, financial, or other pressures that may compromise its impartiality.

2.1. The supervisory body will adopt measures and procedures, mandatory and applicable to all its members, to ensure that there is no conflict of interest, including, among others:

• measures and procedures that allow for the identification of cases or situations that may give rise to a conflict of interest and that enable the supervisory body to avoid and, where appropriate, manage and resolve them;  
• the individuals in the supervisory bodies who make decisions must commit, in writing, or, where appropriate, by electronic means, or in any other manner that is binding, to report possible conflicts of interest and to follow the established procedures in order to avoid such conflicts;  
• absence of instructions, guidelines, or suggestions in the performance of their functions;  
• determining the abstention of individuals in the supervisory body responsible for making decisions in the event of a conflict of interest;  
• allowing the recusal of individuals responsible for decision-making in the event of a conflict of interest and establishing the reasons that would justify it;  
• rules for substitution in the event of recusal.

**3. Experience**

The supervisory body must be able to demonstrate the specialized knowledge and experience necessary to effectively carry out the supervisory activities of the code. These aspects will be evaluated by the AEPD, in accordance with the level of experience required in the code itself. Supervisory bodies must have the necessary knowledge and experience regarding legislation and practice in data protection in the sector, as well as the capacity to supervise and ensure compliance effectively.

In order to ensure that the appropriate competencies are possessed to meet the specific obligations and responsibilities for effective supervision of the conduct codes, the experience requirements will be defined taking into account various factors, such as the sector of activity to which the conduct code refers, its size, the volume and nature of the data to be processed, the risks of the processing activities, and the different interests involved.

To evaluate compliance with the expertise requirements of the supervisory body, all its members will be considered as a whole.

3.1. The requirements of expertise, knowledge, and necessary experience may be demonstrated, among other means, through:

• Information about the professional background of the decision-makers;  
• voluntary certification mechanisms, in particular, holding a university degree that provides evidence of specialized knowledge in law, in the conduct code sector, and/or in practice in the field of data protection;  
• certifications of experience in the field of data protection and/or in the area of the conduct code;  
• A documented process of initial evaluation and continuous supervision of all personnel involved in the supervision process;  
• training and access to training courses for the members of the supervisory body responsible for decision-making.

4. Procedures and Structure  

The supervisory body will have adequate procedures, structures, and resources to carry out the supervision process, including the assessment of the suitability of the data controllers or processors to apply the code, monitor compliance, and conduct periodic reviews of their operations.  

4.1 The supervisory body must demonstrate that it has the appropriate structure and procedures to carry out its functions by describing:  

• the procedure for verifying the suitability of the data controllers and processors applying for adherence to the code to ensure they can comply;  
• the details of the compliance assessment processes of the conduct code that will take into account, among other factors, the sectoral scope of application of the code, the number and size of the members, the risks arising from data processing, or the complaints received, and include:  
  o the type of evaluation to be conducted, such as random audits, periodic inspections, report submissions, completion of questionnaires;  
  o the method and definition of the requirements for its execution in view of the characteristics and purpose of the evaluated conduct code;  
  o the frequency with which they are carried out;  
  o the documentation of the results and their consequences: reports to be issued and their recipients.  
• the procedure for investigating, identifying, and managing non-compliance with the code, as well as additional controls to ensure that appropriate measures are taken to remedy the non-compliances.

5. Transparent Management of Complaints  

The supervisory body will establish procedures for the independent, impartial, and transparent management of complaints and will have an adequate structure and sufficient resources.  

The supervisory body must demonstrate that it has a documented, effective, impartial, and transparent procedure for the reception, evaluation, monitoring, recording, and resolution of complaints within a reasonable timeframe.  

5.1 Information about the complaint processing procedure must be accessible and written in language easily understandable by the interested parties.  

5.2 The procedures, which must be transparent and easily accessible to the interested parties, must comply with the following principles and requirements:  

• simplicity and accessibility, preferably by electronic means;  
• traceability, allowing interested parties to know the status of the complaint processing;  
• adversarial procedure to safeguard the rights of the parties. Once the complaint is received, the alleged facts and potential infringements will be sent to the adherent, giving them a reasonable period to submit their corresponding allegations, which shall not exceed one (1) month;  
• promptness. The procedure must be resolved within a reasonable timeframe not exceeding three (3) months, which may be extended for one (1) month, taking into account the size of the entity under investigation and the difficulty of the investigation;  
• the scaling of corrective measures, including suspension or exclusion from the code, allowing adjustments according to the severity of the non-compliance and being deterrent, in accordance with the list of corrective measures established in the conduct code.  
• notification to the interested parties of the actions and resolutions adopted;  
• publicity of the resolutions adopted;  
• statistical data will be published periodically with the results of the supervisory activities, the number of complaints received, the types of infringements, and the corrective measures adopted.  
• no charge for submitting complaints regarding non-compliance with the conduct code by data controllers and processors.

**6. Communication with the AEPD**

6.1 The supervisory body will inform the AEPD annually of the activities carried out, which will include both the measures and actions taken to verify compliance with the code, and their results, as well as the complaints received and their outcomes.

6.2 When a substantial change occurs in the supervisory body that could affect its independence, expertise, or generate conflicts of interest, it will be communicated without unjustified delay to the AEPD, in order to initiate, if necessary, a new accreditation procedure. These changes could affect, but are not limited to:

• its legal, commercial, ownership, or organizational status;  
• the structure, management, and competent personnel;  
• the resources and the direction of the institution or its registered office; and  
• any other circumstances that could affect the supervisory body's ability to meet the accreditation requirements.

6.3 The supervisory body must have, in any case, a procedure to communicate without unjustified delay to the AEPD the decisions regarding cases of non-compliance with the code that involve temporary suspension and exclusion from adherence to the code. The communication will contain the reasons for the decision, as well as the criteria on which it is based. In the event of lifting the suspension, the supervisory body will communicate the criteria supporting its decision.

**7. Code Review Mechanisms**

The supervisory body plays a key role in reviewing conduct codes, for which it must demonstrate that it has documented procedures to assess the effectiveness of the conduct code and ensure that it remains relevant for its members and continues to comply with the application of the GDPR, or, otherwise, that it should be reviewed and adapted based on the conclusions of the evaluations carried out.

7.1 The supervisory body must have mechanisms that allow the promoter to understand the need to review it to ensure that it remains appropriate to contribute to the proper application of the GDPR. The supervisory body will inform the code promoter and recommend that the relevant parts of the code be reviewed in accordance with the evaluation.

**8. Legal Status**

The supervisory body will not be liable for any non-compliance with the GDPR committed by the members of the code. The supervisory body can only be sanctioned for failing to fulfill its supervisory functions or for not taking appropriate measures when the rules of the conduct code are violated.

8.1 Supervisory bodies must identify themselves and provide a description of their legal status that includes all necessary information to determine that they have adequate standing to exercise their functions and to assume responsibility in case of non-compliance, as provided for in Article 41, paragraph 4, of the GDPR, and to address the administrative fines referred to in Article 83, paragraph 4, letter c), of the GDPR.

8.2 In addition to the headquarters of the supervisory body, which must be located in the territory of the European Economic Area (EEA), when practical, the names of its representatives will be added, and if different, the names of the persons responsible for its oversight. Their purpose is to determine who is responsible for the actions of the supervisory body and to identify the responsible department against which actions can be taken in case of non-compliance with its obligations.

8.3 The supervisory body must provide a description of the resources it has to be considered legally responsible for all its supervisory activities and demonstrate that it has sufficient resources to fulfill its supervisory functions (for example, the effectiveness of administrative fines, etc.).
