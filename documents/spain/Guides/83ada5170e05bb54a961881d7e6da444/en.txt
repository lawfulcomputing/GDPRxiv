ORGANIC LAW 3/2018, OF DECEMBER 5, ON PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION AND 
GUARANTEE OF DIGITAL RIGHTS  
NEW OBLIGATIONS FOR THE PUBLIC SECTOR  
 
1. Publication of the Record of Processing Activities of the Public Sector Body or Agency  
Public sector bodies and agencies are required to publish on their website an inventory of the personal data processing activities they carry out, identifying who processes the data, for what purpose, and what legal basis legitimizes that processing. 
 
2. Obligation to Inform Citizens about the Exercise of Their Rights  
Public sector bodies and agencies are required to include clear and precise information on their website aimed at citizens regarding the exercise of the rights of access, rectification, deletion, the right to restrict processing, as well as portability and opposition. 
 
3. Authority to Verify Citizens' Personal Data  
Public sector bodies and agencies may verify, without needing to request the consent of the interested party, the accuracy of the personal data provided by citizens that is held by public sector bodies and agencies. 
 
4. New Regulation on the Submission of Documentation by Citizens: Amendment of Article 28 of Law 39/2015  
Law 30/1992 already recognized the right of citizens not to submit to administrative procedures the documents held by the Administration or that had been prepared by it. The legal basis for the processing of personal data by the Administration was the consent of the citizen, which was understood to be tacitly granted if the interested party did not expressly oppose it.  
Both the General Data Protection Regulation and the new Organic Law eliminate the need to obtain consent, not even tacit, from the citizen, by establishing compliance with a mission in the public interest or, in particular, the exercise of public powers as the main legitimizing legal basis for the processing of personal data by public sector bodies and agencies.  
Furthermore, the new wording provided by the Organic Law to Article 28 of Law 39/2015 recognizes the right of the interested party to oppose public sector bodies and agencies consulting or requesting the aforementioned documents, but in that case, the citizen must necessarily provide them so that the Administration can ascertain that the requirements established by the regulation are met. Otherwise, they will not be able to consider their request, precisely because the required conditions would not have been demonstrated.  
In any case, this right of opposition does not apply in cases of verification or inspection powers. 
 
5. Notification of Administrative Acts: Identification of Citizens  
The new Law prohibits the joint use of surnames, first name, and full number of the official identification document of individuals in those administrative acts that are to be published or notified by means of announcements.  
From the entry into force of the Organic Law:  
• When an administrative act is to be published, the person will be identified by their first name and surnames, adding four random numerical digits from their official identification document.  
• When it comes to notifications by means of announcements, the person will be identified exclusively by the number of their identification document.  
In both cases, when the person lacks an identification document, they will be identified only by their first name and surnames. 
 
6. Communication of Personal Data of Citizens to Private Entities  
Public sector bodies and agencies may communicate the personal data of citizens to private entities that request it:  
a) either when they have the consent of the citizens.  
b) or when they find that the requesting private entity has a legitimate interest that prevails over the rights and interests of the concerned citizens. 
 
7. Appointment of a Data Protection Officer (DPO) and Notification of the Appointment to the AEPD  
Public sector bodies and agencies are obliged to appoint a Data Protection Officer who has the necessary qualifications, to provide them with the means necessary for the exercise of their functions, and to notify the appointment to the AEPD for inclusion in the public register of Data Protection Officers.  
The Data Protection Officer is not personally liable for any potential violations of data protection laws committed by their organization solely by virtue of this appointment. 
 
8. Involvement of the Data Protection Officer in the Resolution of Complaints in the Public Sector  
The Data Protection Officer of the public sector body or agency must receive complaints directed to them by citizens when they choose this route before filing a complaint with the AEPD, and will communicate the decision made to the citizen within a maximum period of two months.  
Additionally, the Data Protection Officer must receive complaints that the AEPD decides to forward to them prior to the initiation of a sanctioning procedure. The Officer must communicate the decision made to the citizen and to the AEPD within a maximum period of one month.

9. Greater transparency of sanctions imposed on the Public Sector  
Infringements committed by the bodies and agencies of the Public Sector will be sanctioned with a warning accompanied by corrective measures and will not incur any economic penalty.  
The sanctioning resolution of the AEPD will identify the responsible official for the infringement, will be notified to the infringer, their hierarchical superior, the Ombudsman, and will be published on the AEPD website and in the corresponding official gazette.  
The sanctioning resolution may propose to the body or agency the initiation of disciplinary actions, the resolution of which must be communicated by the body or agency of the Public Sector to the AEPD.  
If the infringements are attributable to authorities and executives of the Public Sector and it is evidenced that there are technical reports or recommendations that have not been attended to by them, the sanctioning resolution will include a reprimand with the identification of the responsible official and will be published in the corresponding official gazette.  

10. Processing of personal data in the notification of security incidents  
Public authorities, computer emergency response teams (CERT), computer security incident response teams (CSIRT), providers of electronic communication networks and services, and providers of security technologies and services may process personal data contained in security incident notifications exclusively for the time and extent necessary for their analysis, detection, protection, and response, always adopting appropriate and proportionate security measures to the level of risk.  

11. Personnel records of the public sector: legitimization of processing  
The new Organic Law establishes that the legitimizing basis for the processing of personal data carried out by personnel records of the public sector is the exercise of public powers.  
These records may process personal data that are strictly necessary for the fulfillment of their purposes related to criminal offenses and convictions, as well as administrative infringements and sanctions, of which they must be informed in an express, clear, and unequivocal manner.  

12. Rights of public employees: greater privacy  
The Organic Law guarantees the right to privacy of public employees in the workplace against the use of video surveillance and sound recording devices, as well as against the use of digital devices and geolocation systems.

13. Adaptation to the Organic Law of data processing service contracts  
Data processing service contracts between bodies and agencies of the Public Sector (as controllers) and other bodies or agencies of the public sector or third parties (as processors) signed before May 25, 2018, will remain in effect until a maximum of May 25, 2022.  

14. Processing of personal data by public service concessionaires  
The bodies and agencies of the Public Sector will maintain control over the personal data of users of public services even after the validity of the service concession contract has ended.  
In the Public Sector, a service concessionaire, responsible for processing personal data, never becomes a controller even if it establishes relationships with the individuals whose data it has accessed by virtue of providing the service.  

15. Education for digitalization  
The Government must submit, within one year from the entry into force of the Organic Law, a draft law aimed at ensuring a safe and appropriate use of digital media.  
The Autonomous Communities will have the same period to include in the curricula the necessary content to ensure the full integration of students into the digital society and to ensure adequate training for all teaching staff.  

16. Processing of personal data in health research  
The new Organic Law relaxes the processing of data for health research:  
• it expands the purposes for which consent for processing can be granted,  
• it includes the possibility of reusing information for which consent has already been previously granted,  
• it recognizes the use of pseudonymized data as an option to facilitate health research, including guarantees to prevent the re-identification of affected individuals,  
• it regulates the guarantees of this processing, including the involvement of Research Ethics Committees or, failing that, the Data Protection Officer or an expert in personal data protection.
