The development of the Internet and Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) has led to the emergence of various forms of violence, facilitated by the widespread and intensive use of mobile devices and the Internet, social networks, and services such as instant messaging or geolocation, which have served as channels for their proliferation.  
The characteristics of ICT have given rise to new threats, stemming, among others, from the speed at which information spreads in this environment, the ability to access information through search engines, and the difficulties in its removal. The ease of viral dissemination and the persistence of information in the online environment entail new risk situations, such as access to and disclosure without consent of sensitive information, intimate photographs or videos; surveillance and monitoring of online activities; damage to reputation, etc., with behaviors known as "sextortion" or online sexual harassment being particularly harmful.  
Although online violence affects the entire population, women are particularly affected by these phenomena, suffering as a consequence physical, psychological, and economic harm. In its 2014 EU-wide survey, the EU Agency for Fundamental Rights revealed that 23% of women reported having experienced online harassment or abuse at least once in their lives, and one in ten had been a victim of online violence.  
With the effective application of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) on May 25, 2018, the aim is to address the new challenges that the rapid technological evolution and globalization have posed for the protection of personal data, derived from the significant increase in the magnitude of its collection and exchange. In this regard, the GDPR expands the rights of data subjects, highlighting the right to erasure, whereby anyone who has made personal data public and is obliged to delete it will also be required to inform the data controllers about the data subject's request for those controllers to delete any links to such data, or copies or replicas of such data.  
On the other hand, in the workplace, these forms of digital violence also occur and are reproduced, often accompanying—and sometimes causing—conduct constituting workplace harassment and sexual harassment or harassment based on sex. These behaviors affect the physical, mental, and emotional health of workers, making it the employer's obligation to combat them, as the guarantor of the health and safety of their workers.  

**CONDUCTS CONSTITUTING CYBERBULLYING**  
We refer to a series of conducts: workplace harassment, sexual harassment, harassment based on sex, harassment based on sexual orientation, or harassment based on gender identity and expression when these are carried out through digital means by recording, disseminating, or publishing.

The aforementioned forms of harassment can occur between individuals of the same or different hierarchical levels, whether or not they have a dependent relationship within the organizational structure of the entity or workplace, as long as there is a position of power.  
This document contains recommendations aimed at preventing and combating the aforementioned behaviors that occur in the digital realm, especially through the use of personal data in behaviors constituting harassment (cyberbullying or digital harassment).  
It is understood that behaviors constituting harassment will occur both when degrading content that violates the dignity of the affected person is sent to that person, and when such information, especially videos or images, is disseminated, if its nature can be inferred as an attack on the dignity and sexual freedom of individuals.  
It is essential to keep in mind that engaging in these behaviors not only constitutes an illicit act, but also the organization’s knowledge of these illicit data processing activities will be considered unlawful if it does not react with actions aimed at eradicating them, as it contravenes risk prevention regulations.  
In the case of workers subject to the ordinary labor regime, the Workers' Statute considers verbal or physical offenses and harassment in its various forms as contractual breaches.  
In the case of public employees, behaviors constituting workplace harassment or sexual harassment or harassment based on sex are considered very serious disciplinary offenses, under Article 95 of the Basic Statute of Public Employees.  
It should also be noted that these behaviors may also constitute a criminal offense.  

MECHANISMS FOR ADDRESSING HARASSMENT SITUATIONS  
In private companies and in Public Administrations, these behaviors have frequently been addressed through mechanisms for the prevention of workplace harassment and sexual harassment and harassment based on sex, which we will refer to in this document with the broad term 'harassment prevention policies.'  
Law 31/1995, of November 8, on the Prevention of Occupational Risks, establishes the obligation of companies to guarantee the safety and health of employees in aspects related to work, considering that workplace harassment constitutes not only a psychosocial risk for the worker but a true attack on safety and health at work.  
The mechanisms for preventing sexual harassment and harassment based on sex derive from Organic Law 3/2007, of March 22, for the effective equality of women and men, so that companies with fifty or more employees must approve an equality plan, the minimum content of which must include prevention against sexual harassment and harassment based on sex.

In the public sphere, Public Administrations outline their policies for the prevention and eradication of harassment in Protocols of action against workplace harassment and against sexual harassment and harassment based on sex. These documents include the obligation of all public actors to adapt the provisions contained therein to their respective areas and specialties.  
Both in private companies and in the realm of Public Administrations, the development of new technologies and the emergence of new manifestations of workplace harassment and sexual harassment advise addressing this dimension by incorporating specific measures aimed at safeguarding the fundamental right to data protection into their protocols or prevention mechanisms.  
Therefore, in compliance with the functions assigned to it by Regulation (EU) 2016/679, and in particular its Article 57, the Spanish Agency for Data Protection makes a series of recommendations or provisions to be included in prevention policies, specifically aimed at eradicating workplace harassment and sexual harassment or harassment based on sex when harassment behaviors occur through the processing of personal data that affect the privacy and intimacy of the affected individuals.  

HOW CAN DATA PROTECTION BE INCORPORATED INTO HARASSMENT PREVENTION POLICIES? RECOMMENDATIONS  
Various measures can be implemented, to be included in the Protocols of action against harassment and in the Equality Plans:  
1. COMMITMENT STATEMENT TO PREVENT AND ERADICATE DIGITAL HARASSMENT  
Include the commitment of the company or public entity to data protection and gender equality. The entity will commit to ensuring respect in the processing of personal data according to the principles set forth in the data protection regulations, specifically in Article 5 of the General Data Protection Regulation, as well as to the gender perspective analysis of the data processing it carries out.  
Likewise, the rejection of the company or public entity regarding the use of personal data when its purpose is to engage in sexual harassment and harassment based on sex will be reflected, as well as any use of such data that is contrary to the right to privacy and intimacy, or that constitutes an action aimed at disrespecting, degrading, or humiliating its employees.  
Especially concerning sexual harassment, it is about raising awareness of the relationship between gender equality and data protection and expressing a strong rejection of those behaviors that underpin harassment behaviors through the use of personal information related to individuals' sexual lives.  
The entity thus acquires the commitment to prevent, raise awareness, and act in cases of cyberbullying that it becomes aware of, whether this is:

• workplace harassment, cases of dissemination of derogatory content about the person with the aim of creating a hostile or humiliating environment for the employee,  
• whether sexual, cases of sexual harassment behaviors through social networks and cases of dissemination of videos and images related to the sexual life of individuals.  
In this way, through the commitment declaration, awareness is raised about the risks posed by the Internet and ICT regarding this type of behavior, and a culture of respect for individuals' privacy and awareness in the use of personal data is promoted.  
An example of this declaration could be:  
“(Name of the organization) expresses its deep rejection of workplace harassment and sexual harassment or harassment based on sex and its commitment to the prevention and eradication of these behaviors. (Name of the organization) firmly opposes the use of personal data that involves unlawful data processing, which could undermine the right to privacy and intimacy of employees.  
(Name of the organization) is firmly committed to the protection of personal data, necessary for safeguarding the fundamental right to honor and personal and family intimacy of individuals. Consequently, (name of the organization) will respect the principles of Article 5 of the General Data Protection Regulation and will minimize the processing of personal data of its employees that may increase the risk of behaviors constituting harassment. In any case, it will take into account the possible gender impact of the processing it carries out.”  
2. MEASURES AIMED AT PREVENTING CYBERBULLYING  
Entities could adopt measures to prevent behaviors within the workplace that constitute sexual or workplace harassment through unlawful processing of their employees' data by other employees. Specifically, within the duty to ensure occupational safety and health, it is understood as the obligation to train staff regarding behaviors constituting workplace harassment and sexual harassment or harassment based on sex, as well as in the appropriate and respectful use of social networks and ICT concerning the fundamental right to privacy protection.  
In this regard, information is presented as a fundamental tool. Entities could:  
1. Include in their harassment prevention policies a description of inappropriate behaviors in the use of new technologies, so that employees become aware of the risks that these may pose to privacy, and of the behaviors that may lead to a situation of workplace harassment or sexual harassment or harassment based on sex. In this way, companies can inform about the nature of certain behaviors as offenses or crimes, as well as publish examples of behaviors carried out via the Internet and social networks that constitute cases of harassment. The aim is for employees to be clear about the criminal and administrative consequences, where applicable, of the aforementioned behaviors.

As an example, the following behaviors constitute harassment through unlawful processing of personal data:  
- The recording of degrading images that affect the privacy of workers, particularly—but not limited to—when these affect the sexual life and freedom of individuals.  
- The dissemination of images or videos of a sexual nature among workers, whether it involves sending by someone who has received them legitimately – the so-called ‘revenge porn’ – or mere dissemination on social networks or communication platforms (Telegram, WhatsApp) of images or videos of third parties.  
- The publication or dissemination of messages or audiovisual content (images, memes, etc.) aimed at undermining the dignity of a worker and creating a humiliating or hostile environment, especially—but not limited to—when these involve insinuations regarding the worker's sexual life.  
- The publication or dissemination of derogatory comments, offensive jokes, or demeaning remarks about a worker's professional worth on instant messaging networks or social media, whether they have a sexual nature (constituting sexual harassment), relate to the worker's sex (harassment based on sex) or their sexual orientation or identity, or have a general nature (workplace harassment).  
- The sending of offensive messages or sexual insinuations made through instant messaging networks or social media.  
- The dissemination of insults or false rumors using instant messaging networks or social media.  

2. Provide information regarding possible mechanisms for reacting to personal data processing that may constitute a harassment situation: information about content removal mechanisms from major internet platforms, privacy policies of major operators, or the priority channel made available to the public by the Spanish Data Protection Agency, among others.  
Within this informative work, it would be extremely useful to organize training courses related to the appropriate use of social networks and the Internet, the identification of harassment situations in these media, and the fight against these situations.  

From the perspective of the company or organization, it should be taken into account the transversal nature of harassment prevention in the organizational measures of the entity and, specifically, in its data protection policies. The aim is for the entity to minimize data processing, especially when it comes to specially protected data that could lead, in case of misuse, to situations of harassment or discrimination.

3. MEASURES AIMED AT ERADICATING CYBERBULLYING  
Consequently with its declaration of commitment against these behaviors, and in accordance with current regulations on occupational risk prevention and gender equality, there arises for companies and public entities the duty to implement measures to eradicate situations of harassment when they occur.  
1. On one hand, organizations have the duty to collaborate with the competent authorities for the eradication of these situations: the AEPD (Spanish Data Protection Agency), the FCSE (State Security Forces and Corps), and the judicial authorities. Likewise, they have the duty to report when they become aware of situations of cyberbullying in cases of gender-based violence.  
2. Furthermore, entities have the duty to activate the action mechanisms provided in their harassment prevention policies, initiating the relevant disciplinary actions against employees who engage in such behaviors and informing them of the possible legal consequences and responsibilities they may incur. Specifically:  
• Responsibility in data protection: the dissemination of data of a natural person (in contents such as images, audios, or videos that allow identification), published on different internet services without consent, is considered a violation of personal data protection regulations. The AEPD is competent to sanction these behaviors with fines that, in the most serious cases, can reach 20,000,000 euros.  
• Responsibility in the labor field: Employees who engage in these behaviors will incur disciplinary responsibility which, in the case of very serious offenses, may lead to dismissal. Likewise, for civil service personnel, these behaviors are classified as serious or very serious disciplinary offenses that could be sanctioned with transfers, suspension of duties, or even removal from service.  
Additionally, any action or omission by the employer that causes harm to employees, either by failing to adequately assess occupational risk, including so-called psychosocial risks, or by not adopting appropriate preventive measures, constitutes a serious infringement punishable by fines that can reach 6,250 euros.  
Specifically, sexual harassment in the workplace, regardless of who is responsible, constitutes a very serious infringement, for which the employer may be sanctioned with fines that can reach 187,515 euros.  
• Criminal responsibility: The dissemination and transfer to third parties of images or videos without consent, even if obtained with the consent of the affected person in any location out of the sight of third parties, when they undermine the privacy of a natural person, may constitute various crimes.  
All these crimes against moral integrity, discovery of secrets, or even sexual harassment if they are a means to obtain sexual favors, can be punished with prison sentences.

• Civil liability: compensation must be provided for the damages caused to the affected person, both of a patrimonial nature and of a moral nature.  

3. Finally, it would be advisable to foresee special channels in these action mechanisms for cases where harassment is carried out through unlawful processing of personal data.  
In this regard, entities can publicize and disseminate the channel established by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection to enforce the right to the deletion of especially sensitive personal data.  
When the AEPD considers that the processing of personal data may entail a serious detriment to the right to personal data protection, it may order the controllers or processors of the data to block the data and cease its processing and, in the event of non-compliance with this mandate, proceed to immobilize it. When a complaint has been filed regarding the lack of attention by a controller to the rights established in Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR, the AEPD may decide at any time, even prior to the initiation of sanctioning proceedings, through a reasoned resolution and prior hearing of the data processor, the obligation to attend to the requested right, without prejudice to the continuation of the procedure.  
For exceptionally sensitive cases (dissemination of sexual or violent content), the AEPD has made available to the public a qualified reporting channel, through which it aims to simplify the affected citizen's access to the precautionary measures provided for in Article 69 of the LOPDPGDD (blocking of data) against the unlawful dissemination on the Internet of sensitive data, so that immediate removal of the content constituting harassment from social networks and the Internet is achieved.  
It is advisable to include in the harassment reporting models references or information regarding the role of the AEPD when the harassment occurs through unlawful processing of personal data, so that the affected person is aware of the possibility of requesting the protection of their rights, filing a complaint that leads to sanctioning proceedings, or requesting urgent precautionary measures.  
Given the novelty of the aforementioned rights protection channel, it would be advisable for companies and public entities to make a special effort to publicize this mechanism among their staff, even through the publication on their intranets of a link to the web form. Likewise, a link to this channel could be included in the entities' protocols, or the possibility could be foreseen for the person responsible for taking measures to stop the harassment situation - or the confidential advisor, in the case of protocols for action against sexual harassment in the ministries and public agencies - to direct the affected worker to the aforementioned channel.
