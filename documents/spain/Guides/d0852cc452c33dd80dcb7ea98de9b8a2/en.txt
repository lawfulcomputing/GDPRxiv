INTRODUCTION  
The Internet plays a fundamental role in society and is one of the few sectors of the economy that continues to grow, positioning itself as a pioneer in the local and European economy. The Internet currently contributes 3.8% of the GDP of the European Union and, at the national level, represented 7% in 2018, contributing 81.6 billion euros directly to the Spanish GDP.  
A large part of the investment in the Internet comes from the online advertising sector. A study by the independent financial research company IHS Markit shows that digital advertising contributes 526 billion to the annual GDP of the EU. As is known, one of the ways in which advertising on the Internet is carried out is through cookies. Cookies are tools that play an essential role in providing numerous information society services that concentrate the largest advertising investment, facilitate user navigation, and offer advertising based on browsing habits.  
The importance of online advertising is reflected in investment figures, which grew by 10.6% compared to the previous year. For the first time in 2019, investment in the Internet consolidated its position in first place with an investment of 3.15 billion euros, surpassing television. This represents 46.3% of all investment made in media.  
Beyond the direct and indirect contributions to the economy, digital advertising also provides significant added value to citizens in their role as consumers, as it provides them with content at a reduced price or for free. In other words, the revenues derived from online advertising make possible the great value that consumers obtain from the online services they use. This surplus is about 40 euros per month per household, more than what the family pays for broadband access at home.  
We must also consider that advertising has a multiplier effect throughout the economy through direct and indirect jobs, increased sales, etc.  
According to the study "The economic contribution of advertising in Europe," prepared by Deloitte, in the labor field, it also represents an advantageous figure for the European economy, as there are 5,762,573 jobs in the EU supported by advertising, which accounts for 2.6% of employment in the EU (considering jobs directly impacted by advertising and jobs compatible with the activity chain).

At the same time, it must be kept in mind that the use of data storage and retrieval devices on users' terminal equipment has significant implications regarding their privacy. A cookie is one of those widely used devices, which is why we will generically refer to these devices as cookies hereinafter.  
Citizens, politicians, and consumer organizations, among others, have highlighted the need to ensure effective protection of privacy. This interest has also been reflected in various documents made public by data protection authorities; among others, in the Opinions of the Article 29 Working Party (hereinafter, WP29), currently replaced by the European Data Protection Board (hereinafter, EDPB). The industry, for its part, is also aware that digital development hinges on ensuring users' trust in the network and, therefore, in this area, on ensuring that the use of cookies is carried out while always respecting users' privacy.  
Gaining users' trust implies that they are aware of the value their browsing habits hold for the Internet ecosystem and that they can assess the advantages associated with the information they provide to Internet service providers, while also knowing how to manage the acceptance or rejection of such advantages.  
Indeed, through the use of cookies, service providers obtain data related to users that can subsequently be used for the provision of specific services, for serving advertisements, or as a basis for the development of improvements or new products and services, sometimes free of charge. This circumstance determines the need to implement a system in which the user is fully aware of the use of those devices and the purpose of their use, ultimately being informed about the destination of the data being used and the implications this system has on their privacy. Therefore, community and national regulations require the obtaining of informed consent for the use of certain data in order to ensure that users are aware of the use of their data and the purposes for which it is used.  
When determining the level of detail in the information that must be provided about cookies, as well as the manner in which consent for their use must be obtained, there should be a clear reference regarding the level of understanding of users in relation to them. In this regard, while general knowledge of the internet is increasingly widespread and the average consumer has greater technical knowledge about how cookies work and the available options for their management, a higher level of information is still required when it is directed at those users who have lower technical knowledge or use the Internet less regularly.  
In summary, the digital industry and, in particular, the digital advertising sector and those who represent it are aware of the importance of privacy on the Internet and the need to adopt information and consent formulas that meet the requirements of transparency and control demanded by regulations, adapting it to the current level of user knowledge; all while protecting the significant investment in this sector of activity, but also demonstrating the industry's interest in implementing a transparent system in this area.

Scope of the Regulations  
1. SCOPE OF THE REGULATIONS  
The second section of Article 22 of the LSSI establishes:  
“Service providers may use data storage and retrieval devices on the terminal equipment of recipients, provided that they have given their consent after being provided with clear and complete information about their use, in particular, regarding the purposes of data processing, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13, on the Protection of Personal Data.  
When technically possible and effective, the recipient's consent to accept the processing of data may be provided through the appropriate settings of the browser or other applications.  
The above shall not prevent the possible storage or technical access solely for the purpose of carrying out the transmission of a communication over an electronic communications network or, to the extent strictly necessary, for the provision of a service of the information society expressly requested by the recipient.”  
Consequently, the guidelines of this guide will be useful for those cases to which the second section of Article 22 of the LSSI applies in accordance with the provisions of Chapter II of the LSSI itself, titled “Scope of Application.”  
In particular, it is important to specify that, in accordance with the transcribed provision, it applies to any “data storage and retrieval devices” on any “terminal equipment of the recipients” and that the annex of the aforementioned LSSI defines the “Recipient of the service or recipient” as the “natural or legal person who uses, whether or not for professional reasons, a service of the information society.”  
Thus, Article 22 of the LSSI and this guide refer to the use of cookies and similar technologies used (such as local shared objects or flash cookies, web beacons or bugs, etc.) to store and retrieve data from a terminal equipment (for example, a computer, a mobile phone, or a tablet) of a natural or legal person who uses, whether or not for professional reasons, a service of the information society. The aforementioned regulation also applies to the use of fingerprinting techniques, that is, to the techniques for taking the digital fingerprint of the device.

Regarding the cookie management or configuration systems referred to later in this guide, when multi-purpose cookies with two or more different purposes and not exempt from the scope of application of Article 22.2 of the LSSI are used, it must be ensured that these cookies are only used if all the purposes they group are accepted, that is, if a cookie serves two purposes, but the user only accepts one of them, the cookie should not be used, unless the management system used allows for differentiated treatment of the different purposes of these multi-purpose cookies, so that it is possible that if the user accepts one of its purposes and not others, the cookie only operates with the accepted purpose.

2. TERMINOLOGY AND DEFINITIONS  
For the purposes of this guide, it is useful to first define a series of concepts.  
2.1. Cookies and Similar Technologies  
The LSSI is applicable to cookies understood in the sense indicated at the beginning of this guide, that is, as any type of data storage and retrieval device used on a user's terminal equipment for the purpose of storing information and retrieving information already stored, as established in Article 22.2 of the LSSI.  
Cookies allow the storage on the user's terminal of data amounts ranging from a few kilobytes to several megabytes. Below is a classification of cookies based on a series of categories. However, it is necessary to keep in mind that the same cookie may be included in more than one category.  
2.1.1. Types of Cookies According to the Entity Managing Them  
Depending on who the entity is that manages the equipment or domain from which the cookies are sent and processes the data obtained, we can distinguish:  
a) First-party cookies: These are those sent to the user's terminal from a device or domain managed by the publisher itself and from which the service requested by the user is provided.  
b) Third-party cookies: These are those sent to the user's terminal from a device or domain not managed by the publisher, but by another entity that processes the data obtained through the cookies.  
In the case that cookies are served from a device or domain managed by the publisher itself, but the information collected through these is managed by a third party, they cannot be considered as first-party cookies if the third party uses them for its own purposes (for example, improving the services it provides or providing advertising services on behalf of other entities).  
2.1.2. Types of Cookies According to Their Purpose  
There are many purposes for the use of cookies. Depending on the purpose for which the data obtained through cookies is processed, some of the purposes may be:  
a) Technical cookies: These are those that allow the user to navigate through a website, platform, or application and use the different options or services that exist therein, including those that the publisher uses to enable the management and operation of the website and enable its functions and services, such as, for example, controlling traffic and data communication, identifying the session, accessing restricted access parts, remembering the elements that make up an order, carrying out the purchase process of an order, managing payment, controlling fraud linked to the security of the service, making the request for registration or participation in an event, counting visits for the purpose of billing software licenses with which the website operates.

2.1.3. Types of Cookies According to the Duration They Remain Active  
According to the duration they remain active on the terminal equipment, we can distinguish:  
a) Session Cookies: These are designed to collect and store data while the user accesses a web page. They are usually used to store information that is only relevant to retain for the provision of the service requested by the user on a single occasion (for example, a list of purchased products) and disappear at the end of the session.  
b) Persistent Cookies: These are the ones in which the data remains stored on the terminal and can be accessed and processed for a defined period set by the cookie's owner, which can range from a few minutes to several years.  
In this regard, it should be specifically assessed whether the use of persistent cookies is necessary, as privacy risks could be reduced by using session cookies. In any case, when persistent cookies are installed, it is recommended to minimize their duration according to the purpose of their use.  
For these purposes, Opinion 4/2012 of the Article 29 Working Party indicated that for a cookie to be exempt from the obligation of informed consent, its expiration must be related to its purpose. Therefore, session cookies are much more likely to be considered exempt than persistent cookies.  
2.2. Data  
This is the information obtained through the user's terminal equipment via the data storage and retrieval device (cookies or others).  
Data will be personal when it concerns information about identified or identifiable natural persons, as defined in Article 4 of the GDPR.  
2.3. Terminal Equipment  
This is the device from which the user accesses the service, such as a personal computer, mobile phone, tablet, etc., and from which the information is obtained.  
2.4. Information Society Service  
This is any service provided at the individual request of a user, whether for a fee or not, at a distance and by electronic means, as long as, for the purposes of this guide, it constitutes an economic activity for the publisher whose provision leads to the use of cookies. For example: the provision of a service for accessing information through a web page or a mobile application created by a magazine or newspaper, a financial service, an entertainment service, an e-commerce service from an online store, etc.

2.5. Web Page  
Information society services can be provided by the publisher through a platform, software application, or a website that the user accesses from their terminal equipment.  
Cookies or storage devices have traditionally been associated with web pages. Throughout this text, the term web page will be used to encompass all these possible means.  
2.6. Advertising Space  
This is a place where the publisher anticipates, when programming the web page, that the advertising of the products, image, or services of advertisers will appear. There is a wide variety of advertising spaces or formats; from those integrated into the application itself, such as banners, skyscrapers, page scrapers, buttons, and text links, to the so-called floating ads, such as pop-ups, layers, and transit interstitials.

3. OBLIGATIONS  
The legal obligations imposed by the regulations are twofold, namely: the obligation of transparency and the obligation to obtain consent.  
Prior to the study by the involved entities of the most appropriate solutions to comply with the aforementioned obligations (in accordance with the nature of their activity, the business model they develop, and the scope of their responsibility), it is recommended that they carry out a review of the cookies used, either internally or with the advice of associations or specialized entities. This review will aim to identify the cookies being used, analyzing whether they are first-party or third-party cookies, session or persistent cookies, and specifying their function in order to decide whether they fall within the scope of application of Article 22.2.  
Considering the possible changes that may occur in the management and use of cookies, it is advisable to periodically conduct this review to update the available information about them.  
In any case, it is reminded that the solutions implemented to comply with the obligations of Article 22.2 of the LSSI must be technologically neutral and, therefore, must be solutions that the majority of browsers recognize.  
3.1. Transparency  
3.1.1. What information must be provided  
The second paragraph of Article 22 of the LSSI establishes that clear and complete information must be provided to users regarding the use of data storage and retrieval devices and, in particular, regarding the purposes of data processing. This information must be provided, as indicated, in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, which requires that the processing of users' data be carried out transparently for them.  
Consequently, the information about cookies provided at the time of requesting consent must be sufficiently complete to allow users to understand their purposes and the use that will be made of them.  
The cookie policy must include the following information21:  
a) Definition and generic function of cookies.  
EXAMPLE:  
What are cookies? This website uses cookies and/or similar technologies that store and retrieve information when you browse. In general, these technologies can serve very diverse purposes, such as recognizing you as a user, obtaining information about your browsing habits, or customizing the way content is displayed. The specific uses we make of these technologies are described below.

3.1.2. How this information should be displayed  
3.1.2.1. Information Requirements  
Among the transparency requirements, the following rules stand out, applicable to the provision of information to users regarding the use of cookies:  
a) The information or communication must be concise, transparent, and intelligible.  
The information must be succinct to avoid information fatigue and must use clear and simple language so that it can be understood by an average user.  
The GT29, in its Opinion 15/2011, recommended taking into account the type of average user to whom the website is directed and adapting the language and content of the messages to their technical level. In the same vein, in its guidelines on transparency, the GT29 states that the information "must be comprehensible to the average member of the target audience."

The way users are informed should leverage the experience gained through these methods.

3.1.2.2. Layered Information  
In its guidelines on transparency, the GT29 recommends the use of layered privacy statements or notices, that is, containing information in layers, so that the user can go to those aspects of the statement or notice that are of greater interest to them, thus avoiding information fatigue, and without prejudice to the fact that all information is available in one place or in a complete document that can be easily accessed if the interested party wishes to consult it in its entirety.  
This system may consist of displaying essential information in a first layer when accessing the page or application, and completing it in a second layer through a page that offers more detailed and specific information about cookies.  
In the first layer, which for clarity may be identified under a commonly used term (such as, for example, “cookies”), the following information would be included:  
a) Identification of the publisher responsible for the website. The corporate name will not be necessary, as long as their complete identifying data appear in other sections of the website (legal notice, privacy policy, etc.) and their identity can be clearly inferred from the website itself (for example, when the domain itself corresponds to the name of the publisher or the brand with which they identify themselves to the public, or when such name or brand is clearly displayed on the website).  
b) Identification of the purposes of the cookies that will be used.  
c) Information on whether the cookies are first-party (from the website operator) or also from third parties associated with it, without the need to identify the third parties in this first layer.

Regarding the moment when consent must be obtained for the use and, where appropriate, installation of cookies, it should be recalled that Article 22 of the LSSI states:  
“Service providers may use data storage and retrieval devices on the terminal equipment of recipients, provided that they have given their consent after being provided with clear and complete information about their use, in particular, about the purposes of data processing, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13, on the Protection of Personal Data.”  
Consequently, the use of the cookie may take place when the user has the required information about cookies and the method of obtaining consent, and such consent is provided in accordance with the indicated procedures.  
In this sense, the use of cookies should be accompanied by informed consent from users for such use, so that recipients have the opportunity to review the information and decide whether or not to accept the use of these devices.

3.2.6. Obtaining Consent for the Use of Cookies When a Publisher Provides Services Through Different Pages  
A single publisher that provides different services through different domains may, through a single webpage, inform and obtain consent for the use of cookies that are sent from the other domains that it owns and offer similar content or features, due to the provision of services requested by the user, provided that it is informed, in addition to what is indicated above in the section on information, about which webpages or domains it owns from which the cookies will be sent, the type of cookies, and the purposes for which they are processed and consent is obtained from the user.  
In the event that the pages through which a publisher provides services offer content or have characteristics that are not similar (for example: some of the pages contain adult content), it will be necessary to adopt additional precautions.  
3.2.7. Changes in the Use of Cookies  
As a general rule, as long as consent has been obtained validly, it will not be necessary to obtain it each time a user revisits the same webpage from which the service is provided.  
In any case, it is evident that if the purposes for using cookies or the third parties that use cookies change after consent has been obtained, it will be necessary to update the cookie policy and allow users to make a new decision.  
3.2.8. Updating Consent  
The EDPB, in its guidelines on consent, recommends as best practice the renewal of consent at appropriate intervals. This Agency considers it good practice that the validity of the consent given by a user for the use of a specific cookie does not last longer than 24 months and that during this time the selection made by the user regarding their preferences is preserved, without requiring new consent each time they visit the relevant page.  
3.2.9. Withdrawal of Consent for the Use of Cookies  
Users must be able to withdraw the consent previously granted at any time. To this end, the publisher must ensure that it provides information to users in its cookie policy on how they can withdraw consent and delete cookies.  
The user must be able to revoke consent easily. The system offered for withdrawing consent must be as easy as that used when it was granted. It will be considered that such ease exists, for example, when the user has easy and permanent access to the cookie management or configuration system.

3.2.10. Possibility of Denying Access to the Service in Case of Rejection of Cookies  
Following the EDPB guidelines on consent, for consent to be given freely, access to services and functionalities must not be conditioned on the user's acceptance of the use of cookies. Therefore, so-called "cookie walls" that do not offer an alternative to consent cannot be used, as explained below. This criterion is particularly important in cases where denying access would prevent the exercise of a legally recognized right by the user, as, for example, access to a website is the only means provided to the user to exercise such a right.  
There may be certain cases where the refusal to accept the use of cookies prevents access to the website or the total or partial use of the service, as long as the user is adequately informed about this and an alternative access to the service is offered without the need to accept the use of cookies.  
As established in the EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent, the services of both alternatives must be genuinely equivalent, and it will not be valid for the equivalent service to be offered by an entity unrelated to the publisher.  
4. RESPONSIBILITY OF THE PARTIES IN THE USE OF COOKIES  
The LSSI does not define who is responsible for complying with the obligation to provide information about cookies and obtain consent for their use.  
It is necessary for the parties involved in the use of cookies to collaborate to ensure compliance with the established legal requirements.  
Basically, in the management of cookies and in the processing of the data obtained, two situations are distinguished as described below based on the purpose for which the data obtained through the use of cookies is processed.  
4.1. The Publisher or Third Parties Use Cookies for Purposes Exempt from the Obligations to Inform and Obtain Consent  
In cases where a publisher offers users a service and all the cookies used from its webpage are used exclusively for the purposes for which consent is not required, as listed above, whether they are first-party or third-party cookies, it will not be necessary to inform about their use or obtain consent.

However, in the event that you use third-party cookies for the provision of the service requested by the user, you must contractually establish with those entities with which you contract directly that those entities do not process the data for any purpose other than providing the service to the user, since otherwise, it would be necessary to inform about those other purposes and obtain consent.  
4.2. The publisher or third parties use cookies for purposes not exempt from the obligations to inform and obtain consent  
In this case, it can be differentiated whether own or third-party cookies are used.  
In cases where the publisher, through the use of its own cookies, processes data for any of the purposes that are not exempt from the obligation to inform and obtain consent, it will be necessary to inform about the purposes for which the data will be processed and obtain the user's consent, through one of the forms established in this guide.  
Likewise, when third-party cookies are used for any of the non-exempt purposes, both the publisher and the other entities involved in the management of the cookies will have the responsibility to ensure that users are clearly informed about the cookies and the purposes for which they will be used and to obtain the required consent.  
In this regard, when the publisher uses a CMP that creates an environment in which the third parties participating in it can fulfill the duties of information and obtaining consent, the third parties will be individually and directly responsible for their compliance.  
Generally, third-party cookies store information on the user's terminal equipment or use such information because the publisher, when designing the website, platform, or application, anticipated the possibility of including third-party content that uses them or because it uses third-party software that requires them.  
Therefore, publishers, when they are not the owners of the cookies being used, must ensure that the interested parties receive the necessary information and that mechanisms are enabled to allow their consent, for example, through contractual obligations or guarantees that bind the third-party owner of the cookies or through the installation of platforms for consent management.  
Thus, when information about third-party cookies is offered through a link to the third party's website, the publisher must ensure that the links are not broken, being the third party's obligation to ensure that the information is not outdated and that it is also offered in Spanish or in the co-official language used on the publisher's website.  
In this way, in such cases and in order to facilitate and ensure compliance with the obligations established in this area, when third-party cookies are used, the contracts entered into between publishers and third parties should include one or more clauses ensuring that the required information will be provided to users and that the means through which valid consent for the use of cookies and for its revocation can be obtained will be articulated, as well as the consequences of the revocation of consent for the publisher and, especially, for the third parties that obtained it through the publisher.

It should be noted that in practice, when a CMP is not used, it is more difficult for entities that use third-party cookies, which do not have a direct relationship with the user, to comply with the aforementioned requirements, and that users will tend to direct their concerns to the entity they can identify and with which they have a relationship, that is, to the publisher.  
It should also be taken into account that the holders of the cookies, to the extent that they determine the purposes and means of processing, are responsible for the personal information collected by them and for subsequent data processing that is carried out.  
Thus, advertisers, publishers, agencies, advertising networks, and other intervening agents will be responsible for processing when they use their own cookies and when, using third-party cookies, they participate in determining the purposes and means of processing, even if this is carried out through a data processor, such as when an advertiser hires a media agency to carry out the processing under their direction and in accordance with their instructions.  
Those agents who limit their actions to following the instructions of the data controller will be considered data processors. The figure of the data processor is defined in Article 4.8 of the GDPR and is regulated in Article 28.  
In principle, each data controller will be responsible for the specific processing they carry out. Even in cases where different data controllers are involved, each of them will assume their respective responsibility.  
However, in those cases where the entities referred to in the previous paragraphs (advertisers, agencies, advertising networks, publishers, and other agents) jointly determine the purposes and means of processing, they will be considered joint controllers and must comply with the provisions of Article 26 of the GDPR. That said, the existence of joint responsibility will not necessarily translate into an equivalent responsibility of the different entities involved in the processing of personal data, but rather the responsibility of each entity will depend on the involvement they have in the specific processing. Therefore, the level of responsibility of each of them must be assessed on a case-by-case basis, taking into account all relevant circumstances based on their respective responsibilities assumed in determining the means and purposes of processing.  
In this regard, the scope of the obligations of information and obtaining consent by the publisher regarding third-party cookies is limited to the processing for which they are responsible, including, where applicable, joint responsibility in ensuring that the user is aware of and consents to the use by identified third parties of their cookies and the purposes of the associated data processing. These obligations do not extend, however, to the subsequent phases of processing in which the publisher does not intervene, which will be the exclusive responsibility of the third party.  
For example, responsibility would not extend to data processing carried out subsequently by third parties for the purpose of enabling them to offer audience creation services, profiles, or similar (developed from the processing of data obtained through cookies), nor can responsibility be presumed in the creation of such audiences or profiles simply because they are used.  
In any case, the administrative responsibility required before the control authorities for compliance with the obligations arising from the use of cookies corresponds to each obligated party and is not transferable contractually.

II. Selling Agent:  
Publisher or Platform  
Any entity providing information society services that owns a website accessible to a user and for which cookies are used. For example, this would be the case of the owner of a carpentry shop or a fish market that has a website through which it simply offers information related to the services it provides or has an online store through which commercial transactions can be made or has created a mobile application for which the use of cookies may be necessary for its operation.  

Some publishers, also known as platforms, in addition to providing a service to users, offer, acting as platforms, either by themselves or with the help of one or more third parties, advertising spaces to advertisers for whose management the use of cookies is necessary. This would be, for example, the case of a magazine or a media outlet that provides users with an information service through a webpage or a mobile application and, in turn, offers alongside this information advertising about the services, products, or image of one or more advertisers.  

In light of this definition, one can observe the dual role or function that a publisher can develop. It acts as a provider of a service to the user, for whose provision the use of cookies may be necessary. When acting as a platform, it can also offer advertisers advertising spaces that will be managed either by itself or by one or more entities simultaneously, using technologies that may require the use of cookies for their operation.

• Advertising networks.  
• Supply side platform (SSP).  
• Agencies and/or advertisers through direct agreements accessing their ad servers.

• Supply side platform (SSP).  
• Advertisers, agencies, and their ad servers or their DMP.  
• Ad exchanges (places where supply and demand meet to carry out commercial transactions of buying and selling, where advertisers and platforms/publishers come into contact).

III. Advertiser  
It is the entity whose products, services, or image are advertised through the advertising spaces available to the publishers on their pages or other applications from which they provide services to users. In this sense, it acts as a requester of advertising spaces.  
Frequently, the publisher also tends to act as an advertiser. In these cases, the publisher acts as an advertiser from the moment it uses the advertising spaces made available by other publishers to promote the service it provides to users.  
This would be the case, for example, when the owner of a carpentry shop or a fish market, when also acting as the publisher of its own website from which it provides the service, acts as an advertiser by contracting the advertising dissemination of its products or services directly with another publisher, publishers, or, indirectly, with an advertising network.  
IV. Buying Agents  
They are the agents interested in the dissemination of advertising. They determine the total budget they will use in an advertising campaign, define the target audience, and provide the creative materials.  
In reference to programmatic buying, they are the ones who stipulate the minimum bid price they are willing to pay per impression.

• through a media agency  
• directly dealing with SSPs

V. Intermediaries in the Model  
a) Consent Management Platform (CMP)  
A CMP is a tool that is installed on the publisher's support, website, or application, and allows any party responsible for the use of cookies to fulfill its duties of information and consent collection.  
For a CMP to be valid, it is necessary that it allows the entities that use it, both publishers and other agents, to comply with the requirements established by the regulations in the terms set out in this guide.  
In particular, a coordinated and coherent environment must be created in which, through the articulation of agreements or contractual frameworks, publishers and third parties are obliged to:  
• Comply with transparency requirements towards users.  
• Obtain valid consent from users.  
• Respect the users' consent options and allow the management of those consents, including their revocation.  
These platforms must be subject to audits or reviews that validate compliance with the aforementioned requirements.  
b) Advertising Agencies and Media Agencies  
They are entities responsible for the design and execution of advertising, as well as the creation, preparation, or programming of advertising campaigns for advertisers, acting on behalf of and for the account of these in the purchase of advertising spaces. In this sense, they can also be considered as requesters of advertising spaces for advertisers.

c) Advertising Networks  
These are a set of entities that, acting on behalf of and directly or indirectly representing one or more publishers, offer, also directly to advertisers or indirectly through other demanders, such as advertising agencies, the possibility of obtaining advertising spaces or some type of concrete result such as clicks, sales, or registrations, through the management and processing of data obtained from the use of cookies downloaded or stored on the users' terminal devices when they access the services provided by the publisher.  
The purpose of the work of these entities is to carry out, in the best possible way, the management of the inventory of advertising spaces, the ownership of which corresponds to the publishers, so that it converges with the demand of the advertisers (they sell the advertising space of the publishers).  
It is the publisher who decides whether all or part of the inventory they have is managed exclusively by one entity or by several entities simultaneously, depending on the criteria they establish.  
These types of entities usually perform an aggregation of the offer of advertising spaces and inventories from various publishers. They may also purchase inventory from different publishers for the advertising campaigns carried out by advertising agencies.  
Some of these entities collect, through the cookies they manage, information about the browsing habits of users who access the services or pages offered by any of the publishers they represent. The data is collected with the aim of making the dissemination of the advertisers' advertising pieces as efficient as possible. To do this, they analyze users' habits on the Internet in order to offer them the most suitable advertising related to their browsing profile.  
These types of entities act on the supply side, as suppliers of spaces, in the name and representation, directly or indirectly, of one or more publishers.  
Although these types of entities show their clients the data obtained as a result of the use of cookies in an aggregated manner, it will be necessary to inform and obtain the consent of users for the collection of such data and its aggregation and subsequent processing in order to manage the offer of the publishers' spaces. Generally, this will be carried out from the website of the publishers that use this type of first-party or third-party cookies.  
To manage these advertising spaces as effectively as possible, different types of equipment, technologies, management programs, or applications, such as ad servers, ad exchanges, etc., are used, which process the obtained data in real time, so that the advertisers' advertising pieces are automatically included in the publishers' advertising spaces based on the criteria established in each case.  
Finally, it is also necessary to point out that it is common for this management work to be fully or partially subcontracted to other companies specialized in developing the necessary actions for managing advertising spaces.

Advertisers also have trading desks.

VI. The Auction Model  
a) Supply Side Platform (SSP)  
These are advertising technology platforms that connect inventory to various ad exchanges.  
b) Ad Exchange  
An ad exchange is the place where supply and demand meet to conduct commercial transactions of buying and selling, where advertisers and publishers come into contact. Publishers offer and manage their advertising spaces, and advertisers create and manage their campaigns. Ad exchanges provide a technological platform that facilitates the automation of price-based auctions and real-time purchases, known as RTB (real-time bidding). Thus, the principle of operation of an ad exchange is real-time bidding. Some, for example, connect advertising networks, agencies, and third-party demand-side platforms with a wide global inventory in real time. Ad exchanges install cookies on the user, and some encrypt or hash them when sending them to the associated DSPs to prevent cookies set by advertiser networks from leaking to third parties.  
c) Demand Side Platform (DSP)  
A DSP is a bidding technology that allows advertisers and/or media agencies to purchase inventory across different ad exchanges. DSPs, also known as bidders:  
• Evaluate the value of each impression.  
• Optimize the bid price using audience data.  
• Bid for the inventory.  
In this way, the purchase is made impression by impression, known in terminology as "real-time bidding." A DSP has a unique interface that allows bidding, optimizing, and reporting. The DSP cannot access the user's cookies until it contacts the user, and it cannot do so without winning the auction for the space, which is why ad exchanges and advertisers use cookie matching or cookie syncing.  

VII. Analysis and Measurement Companies  
These are entities that measure and/or analyze user navigation on a publisher's website and their behavior with any of its elements or with advertising, through the analysis of data obtained using cookies. Generally, for the provision of this type of service, what are called "third-party cookies" are used, that is, cookies that are sent to the user's terminal equipment not from a device or domain managed and controlled by the publisher but from a device or domain managed by the entity conducting the data analysis.
