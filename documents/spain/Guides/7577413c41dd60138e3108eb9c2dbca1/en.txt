INTRODUCTION  
The Government's Digital Agenda aims to enhance future industries so that Spain remains at the forefront of innovation and participates in initiatives that allow for the detection of future trends, the generation of companies in these sectors, and the promotion of talent and entrepreneurship.  
In this regard, a specific line of action is highlighted: the enhancement and use of cloud computing as "a key mechanism to ensure the competitiveness of our companies," especially SMEs, as well as facilitating its use by the Administration.  
In this framework, it should not be forgotten that cloud computing services have specific implications for the protection of personal data for which the client contracting such services is responsible. These implications require an assessment of the best way to incorporate the guarantees contemplated in data protection regulations, modulating them to adapt to their specific characteristics.  
The Spanish Agency for Data Protection has taken the initiative to develop guidelines that facilitate, in an educational manner, compliance.  
To this end, it has prepared two documents directed, respectively, at clients contracting cloud computing services and at the providers offering them.  
The first document, structured as a practical guide, is more extensive and detailed given the greater lack of knowledge that clients, especially SMEs and professionals, may have in this area.  

5  
This document, directed at the providers of these services, is more concise, attempting to avoid reiterations with the previous one, while offering complementary information through references to it.  
The guidelines aim to reiterate, especially to large corporations that massively offer these services and to those who offer adhesion contracts to their potential clients, that they must adapt to the regime of guarantees that data protection regulations attribute to citizens.  
With this, the Agency aims to promote a preventive policy for compliance with the law, emphasizing that clients of cloud computing services should consider, when selecting their provider, those whose offerings minimize the risks of non-compliance.  
THE PARTIES INVOLVED IN THE CONTRACTING OF ‘CLOUD COMPUTING’ SERVICES AND THE APPLICABLE LAW  
The application of data protection regulations to the offering of cloud computing services must start from the identification of the legal position occupied, respectively, by the provider of such services and the clients with whom they contract.  
The aforementioned regulations distinguish two distinct parties: the data controller and the data processor.  
The former is the person, professional, or entity that decides on the purpose, content, and use of the processing.  
Consequently, the client contracting cloud computing services, when making decisions about the contracting of such services, the maintenance or not of their own information systems, the type of cloud, and the type of services contracted, as well as the choice of provider based on the conditions offered, continues to maintain the status of data controller for the data to which the aforementioned services will apply. This responsibility, arising from the application of the law, cannot be altered contractually.  
On the other hand, the provider of cloud computing services that involve access to personal data, even if it is a large corporation that holds a dominant position over its clients, will be a service provider, that is, a data processor in the terminology of the aforementioned regulations.  
This initial approach regarding the legal position occupied by the client and the service provider has as its main consequence the determination of the applicable law, which will be that of the client.
6  
Therefore, if the clients/data controllers have an establishment in the European Union or if, without being established in the Union, they process personal data of individuals residing therein for the offering of goods or services or for monitoring their behavior, the legal relationship with the service provider shall be governed by Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (General Data Protection Regulation or GDPR). The determination of the applicable law is, furthermore, a matter that is not available for the parties.  
DILIGENCE AND TRANSPARENCY IN CONTRACTING  
The client contracting a cloud computing service provider has a legal obligation of diligence to, according to Article 28.1 of the GDPR, choose "only a processor that offers sufficient guarantees to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures, so that the processing will be in compliance with the requirements of this Regulation and will guarantee the protection of the rights of the data subject."  
This duty of diligence will translate, given the specific characteristics of these services, into a range of information requirements directed at the service provider aimed at understanding the guarantees it offers for the protection of the personal data for which it remains responsible.  
Such information will be essential for deciding on the type of cloud and the kind of services contracted and, specifically, to discern which one or ones offer adequate guarantees and to choose among them.  
The fulfillment of this duty of diligence must be matched by a corresponding diligence on the part of the cloud computing service provider in providing information, particularly regarding the mechanisms that ensure compliance with the obligations arising from data protection regulations, in order to be considered a transparent provider, as established in Article 28.3(h): "the processor shall make available to the controller all the information necessary to demonstrate compliance with the obligations set out in this article, as well as to allow and contribute to audits, including inspections, by the controller or another auditor authorized by the controller," as well as in the final paragraph of the same article, "the processor shall immediately inform the controller if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes this Regulation or other Union or Member State data protection provisions."  

7  
Transparency is, therefore, an essential principle that must govern the relationships between the parties, especially in cases where the service provider occupies a preeminent position over the clients. This circumstance will be common when the latter are SMEs, micro-enterprises, professionals, or public administrations without a large organizational structure.  
To this end, the Guide for clients contracting cloud computing services includes a range of questions regarding the required guarantees that must be addressed by the provider marketing them, to which this document refers (Guide available at www.aepd.es). In particular, the GDPR establishes in Article 28.1.5 that "The adherence of the processor to a code of conduct approved pursuant to Article 40 or to a certification mechanism approved pursuant to Article 42 may be used as an element to demonstrate the existence of the sufficient guarantees referred to in paragraphs 1 and 4 of this article."  
It is for this reason that the Spanish Agency for Data Protection will particularly value diligence and transparency in the contracting of these services.  
GUARANTEES TO BE INCORPORATED INTO THE CONTRACT  
Data protection regulations require, in cases of service provision, the conclusion of a contract whose content allows for the verification of the incorporation of the guarantees required by Article 28 of the GDPR. This article includes a broader set of contractual clauses in its paragraph 3 than that of the LOPD, so it is necessary to proceed with the adaptation of the contracts.  
In the event that third-party subcontractors are involved in the provision of the offered service, it is necessary to adopt additional guarantees (which have been ratified by the Supreme Court Judgment of July 15, 2010, F.D. Tenth).  
These guarantees refer to the following aspects:  
–– The identification of the services and the company to be subcontracted, informing the client of this (including the country in which it provides its services if international data transfers are anticipated).  
–– That the client can make decisions as a consequence of the involvement of subcontractors.  
–– The conclusion of a contract between the cloud computing service provider and the subcontractors with guarantees equivalent to those included in the contract with the client.
8  
However, given the specific characteristics of cloud computing services, the required guarantees can be modulated to adapt them to the requirements of the GDPR (for some examples on this issue, you can access the Guide for clients contracting cloud computing services, available at www.aepd.es. The solutions outlined should be taken as mere examples, and other solutions that offer the same guarantees may be adopted).  
These guarantees must also be provided by those companies that act as partners of other cloud computing providers, in any of the roles of reseller, cloud service aggregators, cloud builders, application providers, etc., and that provide services by contracting directly with clients.  
Portability, that is, the effective possibility for personal data to be returned to the client or for the client to indicate that it should be transferred to a new service provider they have selected, at the time the service provision ends, is a guarantee that must be particularly taken into account, regardless of the right to portability recognized by the GDPR for individuals.  
Therefore, the contract must include specific solutions to guarantee that portability, adapted to the different modalities of cloud and the type of services offered.  
The guarantees regarding portability in terms of personal data protection are independent of those arising from the termination of service provision under private law, although the contractual conditions in this area should not make that impossible.  

INTERNATIONAL DATA TRANSFERS  
The provision of cloud computing services will often involve cross-border data flows to third countries that imply an international data transfer. Data flows that occur within the framework of the European Economic Area (the EU member states plus Iceland, Norway, and Liechtenstein) do not have this consideration.  
When international data transfers occur, they must be carried out with adequate guarantees. Particularly important, given the specific characteristics of cloud computing services, is to establish mechanisms to ensure that subcontracting carried out in this context of international transfers is managed smoothly, while ensuring that the responsible client has sufficient information about the subcontractors, or potential subcontractors, and maintains the ability to make decisions. (For more information, you can consult the Guide for clients contracting cloud computing services)

EXERCISE OF THE RIGHTS OF ACCESS, RECTIFICATION, DELETION,  
LIMITATION OF PROCESSING, PORTABILITY, OPPOSITION, AND  
AUTOMATED INDIVIDUAL DECISIONS  
The client contracting cloud computing services, while remaining the data controller, is obliged to facilitate the exercise of the rights recognized in Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR for the data subjects within the legal deadlines.  
For this, it may require the collaboration of the cloud computing service provider.  
Therefore, the provider of these services must foresee that this collaboration may be necessary, provide information in this regard, and, when requested, make it effective diligently.  

CONTRACTING WITH PUBLIC ADMINISTRATIONS  
If the cloud computing service provider intends to offer services to public administrations, it must keep in mind that they are subject to additional legal obligations, so it must configure its services in a way that allows for compliance.  
In particular, it must be reiterated that the data controller has a specific duty of diligence to contract a data processor that complies with the guarantees provided in the GDPR, so the necessary information must be provided.  
In this regard, it should be noted that Law 9/2017, of November 8, on Public Sector Contracts, which transposes the European Parliament and Council Directives 2014/23/EU and 2014/24/EU of February 26, 2014, includes in its Twenty-Fifth Additional Provision the guarantees applicable to the provision of services involving the processing of personal data, including cases of subcontracting.  
Other regulations that must be considered are Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations, and Law 40/2015, of October 1, on the Legal Regime of the Public Sector.  
Additionally, it is necessary to offer services adapted to the National Security Scheme and the National Interoperability Scheme (Royal Decrees 3/2010 and 4/2010, of January 8).  
Finally, if there is a possibility that, when carrying out international data transfers, authorities from third countries may request access to the information, this possibility and the options that the Administration contracting the services may adopt regarding it must be communicated (for more information on the specifics in public administrations, see the Guide for clients contracting cloud computing services, available at www.aepd.es).  

10  
To conclude this document, the Spanish Data Protection Agency recommends that cloud computing service providers take into account the following points:  
–– Review their contracts considering the criteria outlined in this document.  
–– Adapt them to ensure compliance.  
–– Inform their clients, if they do not comply, of the need to adopt these guarantees.  
–– Responsibility for non-compliance with the law may be demanded from both the client and the service provider.
