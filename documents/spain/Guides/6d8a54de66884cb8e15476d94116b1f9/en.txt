GUIDELINES FOR THE APPLICATION OF THE EIGHTH ADDITIONAL PROVISION AND THE TWELFTH FINAL PROVISION OF THE LOPDGDD  

Law 39/2015, of October 1, on the Common Administrative Procedure of Public Administrations, establishes in its article 28 that the delivery of documentation within the framework of the processing of an administrative procedure is both a right and a duty.  

On one hand, according to paragraph 1 of the aforementioned article, interested parties have the duty to provide the data and documents required by Public Administrations in accordance with the applicable regulations. On the other hand, as stated in paragraph 2, the interested party has the right not to provide information that, either because it has been generated by Public Administrations or because it has been previously submitted, is already in their possession. In this way, the principle of "once only" is applied, which is key in the development of electronic administration and aims to eliminate unnecessary administrative burdens that arise when users must supply the same information more than once to different public sector bodies.  

In response to this need for interoperability and communication between Public Administrations, the data verification and consultation services of intermediary platforms, or other electronic systems enabled for this purpose, allow any body to consult or verify the data that a citizen needs to prove in the processing of a procedure, without the need to request the submission of the corresponding certificates or supporting documents, thereby making effective the right of the citizen recognized in article 28.2 of Law 39/2015.  

In the original wording of the aforementioned article and in relation to this right not to provide documents that are already in the possession of another administration, in order to enable the consultation of documentation between administrations, the interested party had to express their consent, which could be understood as granted as long as there was no express opposition or a special applicable law did not require express consent. That is to say, the consultation of documentation between administrations was enabled through consent that could be understood as granted tacitly.  

However, this possibility contradicted Regulation (EU) 2016/679, General Data Protection Regulation (hereinafter, GDPR), which defines consent as “any freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes by which they, by a statement or by a clear affirmative action, signify agreement to the processing of personal data relating to them,” and which, consequently, nullifies any possibility of basing processing on the existence of tacit consent.  

The entry into force of Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (hereinafter, LOPDGDD), whose purpose is to adapt the Spanish legal system to the GDPR and to complete its provisions, has made it necessary to amend the wording of different laws.

Currently, Article 28 of Law 39/2015, amended by the LOPDGDD,  
maintains the principle of one-time submission by guaranteeing the right of interested parties to “not submit documents that are already in the possession of the acting administration or have been prepared by any other administration,” although, from the perspective of data protection, the true significance of the amendment lies in the elimination of the need to obtain the consent of the interested party, whether tacit or express, allowing for the “consultation or collection of such documents unless the interested party opposes it” and without the possibility of opposition “when the submission of the document is required in the context of the exercise of sanctioning or inspection powers.”  
The Spanish Agency for Data Protection has received multiple inquiries regarding  
the interpretation of the new wording provided by the Twelfth Final Provision of the LOPDGDD, both concerning the need to obtain consent for consultation, through data intermediation platforms or electronic systems enabled for that purpose, of the data previously submitted by the interested parties and that are already in the possession of Public Administrations, as well as regarding the need to obtain, or not, in the context of the procedure being processed, the express opposition to such consultation.  
In order to establish a practical criterion to follow when defining the  
request and data collection forms used by transferors and transferees of verification and consultation services, these guidelines incorporate the criteria already expressed in legal reports 108/2018, 155/2018, and 175/2018 in this regard.  
That is to say, in the case of the processing of data arising from the relationships of citizens with the Administration, which may include operations of communication, consultation, and verification of data between administrations, the legal basis for the processing must be sought in sections c) (compliance with a legal obligation) and e) (performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of public powers) of Article 6.1 of the GDPR. In this way, it is assumed that, between the Administration, as the controller, and the citizen, as the data subject, there exists a situation of imbalance in which it is unlikely that consent has been given freely in all possible circumstances, which, consequently, invalidates consent as a legal basis legitimizing the processing.  
Only in cases where a special applicable law requires express consent, both legal bases (compliance with a legal obligation and performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of public powers) would be displaced by consent given under the conditions required by Article 7 of the Regulation.  
This same consideration must be taken into account in the event that the  
processing operations previously described affect special categories of data, making it necessary to lift the prohibition on processing established in section 1 of Article 9 of the GDPR, that the consultation of the data is necessary for reasons of an essential public interest, defined in a law and that includes specific guarantees in accordance with what is described in section 9.2 g of the regulation.  
As a consequence of the above, the management of the right of opposition referred to in Article 28 of Law 39/2015, in its sections 2 and 3, must be understood as the right that the interested party has, in accordance with Article 21 of the GDPR, to oppose, for reasons related to their particular situation, the processing of their data.

In any case, the exercise of the right of opposition must be accompanied by an expression of its cause so that the responsible party can weigh the alleged reasons, as an opposition in absolute terms would not be valid and could be understood as the revocation of consent that has not been granted, given that this is not the legal basis for the processing. Furthermore, in the event that the citizen expresses a motivated opposition, they must necessarily provide the documents to which they oppose consultation so that the acting administration can ascertain that the requirements established in the corresponding procedure are met; otherwise, their request cannot be granted.  
This interpretation translates, in practical terms, to the fact that, when collecting data from citizens linked to a specific procedure through the various forms available on the electronic offices of the Public Administrations, the acting administration will carry out the processing in compliance with a legal obligation, a mission of public interest, or in the exercise of public powers. The acting administration must inform the administered party, in application of the principle of transparency, about the data that will be consulted for the resolution of the procedure in question as well as the possibility of exercising their rights in the field of data protection, including the right of opposition, providing information regarding the channels to do so, but without the necessity or obligation to include a box or mechanism that allows the interested party to exercise the right of opposition ad nutum in an absolute manner and without motivated justification. Likewise, and in accordance with the eighth additional provision of the LOPDGDD, the acting administration is empowered, in the exercise of its competencies, to carry out the verifications that are necessary to verify the accuracy of the data provided by the citizen in the requests made.  
Only in cases where, as a result of the type of procedure, the consultation, transfer, or communication of data of a tax nature or of any other type whose specific legislation regulates the need for express consent from the interested party may be necessary, will it be necessary to include a clause in which the interested party authorizes their consultation by the acting administration to the data transferring administration responsible for these.
