PRESENTATION  
This guide is part of the set of initiatives adopted by the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) aimed at facilitating knowledge and compliance with data protection regulations for companies, especially SMEs.  
The group of property managers has particular characteristics that justify special attention to their activities. When property managers are involved in managing the affairs of homeowners' associations, they assume advisory and consultancy roles that also extend to the area of data protection concerning the data managed by the associations. At the same time, it is noteworthy that property managers acting on behalf of homeowners' associations generally play the role of data processors concerning the data of the homeowners.  
Therefore, property managers have this dual role of material managers of the processing activities they carry out in the context of providing their services to the associations and as advisors to these associations regarding all matters related to the protection of the affected personal data.  
These data processing activities by homeowners' associations affect the majority of citizens in Spain. Moreover, they are processing activities with very similar characteristics in most cases, which does not exclude the possibility of less conventional processing situations or that, depending on specific circumstances, some processing may have a certain complexity.  
For all these reasons, the Spanish Agency has understood that publishing a guide focused on data protection in homeowners' associations directed at property managers could contribute to facilitating their tasks by providing them with information that is tailored to their needs.

The guide addresses, first of all, general issues of data protection regulations that also apply to data processing carried out by homeowners' associations. In this sense, sections are included that are dedicated to the definitions of basic concepts such as personal data, or to the way of organizing the relationships between the homeowners' association, as the data controller, and the property manager, as the processor.  
On the other hand, the guide considers specific treatments that, as mentioned earlier, are often common to all homeowners' associations: information about owners with outstanding payments, video surveillance, or data processing of employees.  
Furthermore, since May 25, 2018, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) of the European Union is applicable, which introduces substantial changes to the regulations applicable in Spain. In some aspects, the GDPR contains important novelties. Some of them will affect the processing carried out in the context of homeowners' associations, such as the elimination of the obligation to notify files to public data protection registers, without prejudice to the establishment of a record of processing activities.  
Others, however, given the characteristics of these treatments, do not seem to have a general impact in this area. This will occur, for example, in obligations such as designating a data protection officer or conducting data protection impact assessments. These measures are reserved by the Regulation for entities that carry out processing that involves a certain level of risk to the rights and freedoms of data subjects, a circumstance that does not seem to occur in the usual processing within homeowners' associations.  
The guide contains references to some of these novelties, mainly regarding the record of processing activities, the right to information, or the obligations to be fulfilled.  
To assist both public and private controllers in adapting to the GDPR, the Spanish Data Protection Agency has published numerous guides and guidelines in this regard. All of them are accessible on the Agency's website and can provide useful support as a complement to this Guide.

1.  
Legitimacy for Data Processing  
Property managers carry out multiple personal data processing activities when acting on behalf of homeowners' associations. From the perspective of personal data protection regulations, they are legitimized to process and dispose of the data of co-owners that are necessary for the ordinary management of community affairs, as they act in relation to the communities they serve as data processors.  
Likewise, homeowners' associations regarding the processing of data of the members are legitimized, for the purposes of the grounds set forth in the GDPR, in compliance with a legal obligation in accordance with the provisions of the Horizontal Property Law (LPH).

2.  
Identification of Data Processing  
In their ordinary management, a homeowners' association may use different types of personal data to carry out various processing activities. The most common processing by a homeowners' association is that which incorporates information with the personal data of the individuals who are part of the community itself.  
Generally, the following personal data are subject to processing: names of the owners, contact phone numbers, postal addresses, ID numbers, and email addresses. This processing is commonly referred to as "management of the homeowners' association" or "owners," and the community uses it for its accounting, tax, and administrative management, ensuring compliance by the owners with the obligations imposed by the LPH and the exercise of the rights that correspond to the co-owners in the community.  
The most common data processing activities of homeowners' associations are carried out with the "purpose" of "management" of the community. They use them for various legally and contractually assumed "functions," as well as to facilitate the exercise of their rights to the members themselves.  
Data protection regulations do not prevent the homeowners' association from also having other personal data of the co-owners, provided that such data are adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purposes of that community.  
Finally, on many occasions, homeowners' associations approve the installation of camera or video camera systems, which result in the processing of images of identified or identifiable individuals. In this case, there would be a processing that can be referred to as "video surveillance" or "security cameras," whose main purpose is the security and control of access and/or monitoring of the common facilities and elements of the property.

4.1  
R  
esponsibility  
The purpose of the data processing carried out by homeowners' associations is their management, ensuring compliance by the owners with the obligations imposed by the LPH, as well as guaranteeing the proper exercise of the rights that correspond to the community members and to "third parties" in the community.  
The aim pursued by the data processing carried out by homeowners' associations is to ensure the proper functioning of the community.  
The responsibility of the processing lies with the homeowners' association itself.  
The property manager of a specific homeowners' association only carries out the activities assigned to them by the LPH. Many of these functions involve the processing of personal data of identified or identifiable individuals, who may be the owners subject to the horizontal property regime, but also other neighbors, non-owners of the managed property, or third parties who may be affected by the actions of the community or any of the owners.  
The responsibility for the processing carried out for the proper management and functioning of the homeowners' association under the horizontal property regime lies with the homeowners' association itself.

4.2 Data Processor  
In the horizontal property regime, the property manager, who manages one or several communities on behalf of the data controller, acts as a "data processor."  
To ensure that the relationship between the community of property owners - data controller - and the property manager - data processor - complies with data protection regulations, the following conditions referred to in Article 28 of the GDPR must be met:  
- That access to the data by the property manager is carried out solely for the purpose of providing a service to the data controller, and that this service relationship is contractually established.  
- The contractual relationship must be documented in writing or in another form that allows for the verification of its establishment and content.  
- It must be expressly stated that the property manager will only process the data according to the instructions of the community of property owners, and that they will not apply or use the data for any purpose other than that specified in the contract, nor will they communicate it, even for its preservation, to other persons.  
- The property manager must limit the use of the data to the scope of the functions of the community of property owners that they have been designated.  
- Both the property manager and any person working for them must respect confidentiality in the processing of personal data.  
- It is understood that the property manager acts in accordance with the instructions of the data controller when, in the performance of their duties, they process the data of the owners contained in the files they safeguard.  
- At the end of their relationship, the property manager must return to the community of property owners all supports and/or documents containing personal data subject to data processing.  
- However, the property manager may retain the personal data subject to processing as long as responsibilities may arise from their relationship with the community of property owners.

5.  
Obligations of Property Managers  
Derived from Their Specific Activity  
in the Field of Homeowners' Associations  

The main obligations established by data protection regulations fall on the data controller; however, property managers, both in their role of advising and representing homeowners' associations and acting as "data processors," must facilitate compliance and participate in it.  

The property manager must provide the associations with all the information they need to adapt their processing of personal data to data protection regulations. This information and advice must refer both to the internal and ordinary activities of the association and to those related to the contracting of works, goods, and/or services from third parties.  

The GDPR is characterized by proactivity, such that controllers will apply appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing of personal data is carried out in accordance with the regulation. Such measures will be reviewed and updated when necessary.  
•  
The principle of proactive responsibility is described in the GDPR as the need for the data controller to apply appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is compliant with the Regulation.  
•  
In practical terms, this principle requires organizations to analyze what data they process, for what purposes, and what types of processing operations they carry out.  
•  
From this knowledge, they must explicitly determine how they will apply the measures provided for by the GDPR, ensuring that those measures are adequate to comply with it and that they can demonstrate this to interested parties and supervisory authorities. In summary, this principle demands a conscious, diligent, and proactive attitude from homeowners' associations and property managers regarding all personal data processing they carry out.

Data Retention. While one of the principles previously indicated that the GDPR establishes is related to the limitation of the retention period, the European regulation, in regulating the right to erasure, determines a series of exceptions in which such erasure would not proceed. Among these exceptions are:  
•  
Compliance with a legal obligation requiring the processing of data imposed by Union or Member State law applicable to the data controller.  
•  
For the formulation, exercise, or defense of claims.  
Therefore, data protection regulations allow for the retention of personal data for the time during which some type of liability arising from a legal relationship or obligation, or from the execution of a contract or the application of pre-contractual measures requested by the data subject, may be required.  
The property manager may only retain personal data that is strictly necessary during the limitation period set by tax, accounting, social, or civil regulations.  
Once this period has expired, the data must be destroyed. They may only be retained if they are dissociated beforehand or if, exceptionally, considering the historical, statistical, or scientific values in accordance with specific legislation, it is decided to maintain certain data in full.

Analysis of Specific Cases  
7.1 Non-Payment of Community Fees by Owners  
In general, the publication on the notice board of the property of the identity of the debtor owners and/or the overdue and unpaid fees owed to the community is not covered by data protection regulations.  

However, the Horizontal Property Law, in its aim to enable communities to legitimately collect what is owed to them by co-owners, provides the possibility of publicizing through the calling of the Owners' Meeting the identity of those individuals who are not up to date with the payment of all debts owed to the community. This publicity entails the knowledge by all co-owners of the identity of the debtors, as well as the amount of their debt, without the need to obtain their consent.  

At the same time, the LPH establishes that among the obligations of each owner is the one to communicate to the person performing the functions of secretary of the community, by any means that allows for proof of receipt, their address in Spain for the purposes of summons and notifications related to the community. In the absence of this communication, the address for summons and notifications will be considered to be the apartment or premises belonging to the community, with full legal effects for those delivered to the occupant thereof.  

If an attempt to summon or notify the owner is impossible at the location provided in the previous paragraph, it will be understood to have been carried out by placing the corresponding communication on the community notice board, or in a visible place of general use designated for this purpose, with diligence stating the date and reasons for proceeding with this form of notification, signed by the person performing the functions of secretary of the community, with the approval of the president. Notification carried out in this manner will produce full legal effects within three calendar days.

As a general rule, the publication on the community notice board of a list of owners who are not up to date with their payments is not covered by data protection regulations.  
However, the Horizontal Property Law (LPH) allows for the disclosure of the identity of debtors and their debts to the community, incorporating such data into the notice for the meeting.  
Exceptionally, when there is no record of the address of any owner, due to their failure to provide this information to the community, the notice for the owners' meeting may be published on the community notice board—or in another visible general-use location designated for this purpose. In this case, the reasons for such publication must be stated alongside the published document, with a record being made by the person performing the secretary's duties.  
The publication of the list of owners with overdue and outstanding payments, through individualized and periodic communication sent to each of them, is also not covered by data protection regulations. Even in cases where there is no express mention of the personal identification of the debtor neighbor(s), only indicating the data related to the property, such communication is still not compliant with data protection regulations, as this data or reference is always associated with that of the property owner, thus it is undoubtedly personal data, referring to identifiable natural persons.  
The fact that personal communications to the owners periodically include the community's accounting and a list of owners who are not up to date with their payments implies a transfer or disclosure of data to a generality of people, which may be excessive, considering that the legal authorization for the communication of the aforementioned data provided in the LPH only justifies its inclusion in the Notice for the Owners' Meeting and in the minutes that result from it.  
However, if the aforementioned publication were to be carried out in compliance with an express agreement adopted by the Owners' Meeting, we would be faced with a data transfer with the prior consent of the interested parties, which in principle would not violate the regulations on personal data protection.

Access and Obtaining Copies of Community Documentation by Owners of Properties in Horizontal Property Regime  
This type of access and obtaining copies by co-owners responds to the purpose of knowing and verifying the correct management of the community, potentially granting access to very varied information, such as that related to salaries, professional fees, invoices, and contracts.  
In the documentation of the community of property owners, numerous pieces of information can be found that include personal data, such as identifying and contact details of the owners, their bank account numbers, participation coefficients, individual consumption, income made by the owners, or debts they maintain with the community, voting intent in the adoption of agreements, etc.  
Any personal data related to employees that the community of property owners may have will also be considered as such, as will the data related to professional fees paid by the community.  
Focusing exclusively on the processing of personal data contained in the documents used by the community itself, these can encompass a wide range of personal information related, even, to the private and family life of the affected individuals, as well as any type of activity carried out by a person, such as that related to their labor relations or their economic or social activity.  
The LPH itself enables various communications of personal data, which for the purposes of the GDPR would be legitimized based on compliance with a legal obligation.  
Thus, Article 16.2) regulates how the meetings should be convened, stipulating that the notice will contain a list of owners who are not up to date with the payment of debts owed to the community and will warn of the deprivation of the right to vote if the situations provided for in Article 15.2 occur.

Article 19 of the LPH provides for the forwarding of minutes to the owners, indicating in its second paragraph the mentions that must be included, among which are various types of personal data.  
From the perspective of personal data protection, it should be noted that, without prejudice to the possibility of making these communications, the principles of the GDPR must also be taken into account, especially the principle related to data minimization.  
Consequently, the communication of data must be limited to those that - in each case - are “adequate, relevant, and limited” for the fulfillment of the purpose that legitimizes access to them, which in these cases refers to the good governance of the community and its control.  
Thus, by way of example, the communication of directories containing the addresses of the owners or their bank account numbers does not meet the suitability requirement, as they do not contribute in any way to the purpose of controlling the proper administration of the community of owners.  
Similarly, regarding the payroll of the community employees, it must be taken into account that along with the information related to their remuneration, other data will appear, such as the tax address, the bank account where payments are made, and even specially protected data related to health or ideology, as well as the deduction, if applicable, of the union fee for union members. This data is not relevant to the purpose of controlling the management of the community of owners, so the display of such directories or payrolls will be contrary to the principle of proportionality and, consequently, will lead to a violation of data protection regulations, without prejudice to the obligation to inform the owners of the remuneration paid to employees, with the appropriate breakdown of remuneration concepts.  
Article 20 e) of the LPH states that it is the administrator's responsibility to act, where appropriate, as secretary of the Board and to safeguard the documentation of the community for the owners' access. This article must be interpreted in accordance with data protection regulations, so it does not allow generalized access to all documentation held in the community's files that may contain personal data, but only to those data that are strictly pertinent, adequate, and limited in relation to the purposes for which they are processed. Consequently, outside of the cases where the LPH expressly requires communication to other owners of certain personal data, it must be examined in each case whether access to the documents complies with the principle of proportionality, being suitable, necessary, and balanced to achieve the intended purpose, as stated by the Constitutional Court. Otherwise, direct access to the document will not be permitted.

7.3  
E  
Display of the Minutes Book of the Community of Property Owners  
to Financial Entities  
The minutes book of the Board of Owners of a community under horizontal property regime records the agreements of said Board, with the minutes of each meeting needing to express the content referred to in the LPH, which includes the identification data of the corresponding owners and the apartment in which they reside, with the custody of such minutes books corresponding to the administrator.  
The question arises as to whether it is compliant with data protection regulations for the legal representative of the community to display the minutes book before a banking entity to formalize the opening of a current account or for the annual change of signatures of the community's officers.  
Logically, the opening and management of a current account with the financial entity necessitates that it be aware of the identification data of the person assuming that legal representation and of any changes that occur annually (or when they are removed from their position before this period), as well as the personal data of those officers of the community to whom the president grants the signing authority, whether independent or joint, for operations related to said account.  
However, such data can be communicated through the corresponding certification issued by the administrator of the community of property owners or another legal power of attorney document, for which the accuracy and truthfulness will be the responsibility of said governing bodies before the financial entity.  
The certification issued by the administrator will be sufficient documentation to formalize the opening of a current account or for the annual change of signatures of the community's officers before the financial entity.

Schematic installation of cameras and/or video cameras in communities of property owners is governed by the following requirements and conditions:  
Legitimacy for Installation  
For the installation of cameras in common areas, the agreement of the Board of Owners will be necessary, which will be reflected in the minutes of said Board.  
It is recommended that the agreement reflect some of the characteristics of the video surveillance system, as well as the number of cameras or the area covered by them.  
The legitimacy for the processing of personal data (images and, where applicable, voices) would be in the public interest, as stated in Article 6.1 e) of the GDPR.  

Record of Processing Activities  
The record of processing activities related to the video cameras must be configured.  
Right to Information  
One or more signs will be installed at the various accesses to the monitored area, in a visible location, informing that access is to a video-monitored area. The sign will clearly indicate that it is a video-monitored area, the identity of the data controller, and to whom and where to address to exercise the rights provided by data protection regulations. The AEPD has a model sign available.  
Additionally, the remaining information required by data protection legislation, which we have referred to in this Guide when analyzing compliance with the right to information in the collection of personal data, will be made available to the affected parties. This information may be available at the concierge, reception, offices, bulletin boards, or accessible via the internet.

7.5 Other Activities  
Administrators also carry out part of their activities in other situations different from those previously described in relation to the horizontal property regime, such as those related to the management of rentals and other real estate income belonging to their clients.  
In these cases, the owners of the homes and business premises are not the owners, neighbors of a community, but they are also not entrepreneurs; they are individuals who obtain such income through the aforementioned management, which is occasionally carried out by a property manager, raising the type of obligations that correspond to each of the subjects referred to in this activity.  
n When the personal data of the affected individuals are processed by the administrator, deciding on the object, content, and use of such processing, they become the data controller.  
n Unlike what happens with the Horizontal Property Regime, in which the administrator manages one or several communities on behalf of the controller acting as a data processor, in this case, the condition of data controller will lie with the administrator themselves or, where applicable, with the acting real estate agent.  
n Therefore, it will be necessary for the property manager, as the data controller, to have a contractual basis for carrying out the data processing and, in turn, to comply with the right to information in the terms contained in the data protection regulations.  
n Correspondingly to their responsibility, the administrator will also have the obligations related to the registration of processing activities.

Some FAQs  
n  
Can contact details of property owners be communicated to an external service in emergency situations, or in the event of incidents (for example, flooding or pipe breaks), without the consent of the interested parties?  
Yes, when communicated to a property manager responsible for managing the community, such a situation can be understood as a legitimate interest on the part of the community to resolve the incident as quickly as possible in order to avoid damage.  
n  
Can the email address used by an owner to report a malfunction be retained and used for their relationships arising from community management aside from the specific malfunction?  
If the email address has been provided by the owner, it can be used by the manager for managing their relationships with the community. Any other use, such as advertising or marketing, would not be legitimized.  
n  
Can communications be made to owners via instant messaging systems (WhatsApp)?  
If provided by the owner, it could be used within the framework of community management without the need for consent. However, it cannot be used to include the owner in a group without their express, informed, and explicit consent.  
n  
Is it necessary to obtain new consent under the GDPR?  
The processing of owners' data by the community is based on the fulfillment of legal relationships and obligations under horizontal property legislation, and by the property manager under the treatment manager contract.  
If processing were to be carried out based on consent given implicitly, express consent would need to be obtained or it would need to be assessed whether any of the remaining legitimization grounds contemplated in Article 6.1 of the GDPR apply.  
n  
Can personal data be published on the community notice board?  
The cases in which the display of personal data related to matters arising from community management is authorized are specified in Article 9.h) of the LPH. The notice board should not be placed in a location easily accessible to any person.

ANNEX  
n  
Personal data: any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (the data subject); an identifiable natural person is considered to be any person whose identity can be determined, directly or indirectly, in particular by means of an identifier, such as a name, identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more elements specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that person.  
n  
Processing: any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether by automated means or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or any other form of enabling access, alignment, combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.  
n  
Data controller or controller: the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; if the law of the Union or of the Member States determines the purposes and means of the processing, the controller or the specific criteria for their appointment may be established by the law of the Union or of the Member States.  
n  
Processor or processor: the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that processes personal data on behalf of the data controller.  
n  
Third party: a natural or legal person, public authority, service, or body other than the data subject, the data controller, the processor, and the persons authorized to process personal data under the direct authority of the data controller or the processor.  
n  
Consent of the data subject: any freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes by which they, by a statement or by a clear affirmative action, signify agreement to the processing of personal data concerning them.
