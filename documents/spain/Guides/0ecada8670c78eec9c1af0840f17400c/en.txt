The Internet plays a fundamental role in society and is one of the few sectors of the economy that continues to grow, positioning itself as a pioneer in the local and European economy. The Internet currently contributes 3.8% of the GDP of the European Union and, at the national level, represented 7% in 2018, contributing 81.6 billion euros directly to the Spanish GDP.  
A large part of the investment in the Internet comes from the online advertising sector. A study by the independent financial research company IHS Markit shows that digital advertising contributes 526 billion to the annual GDP of the EU. As is well known, one of the ways in which advertising on the Internet is carried out is through cookies. Cookies are tools that play an essential role in providing numerous information society services that concentrate the largest advertising investment, facilitate user navigation, and offer advertising based on browsing habits.  
The importance of digital advertising is reflected in investment figures, which grew by 10.6% compared to the previous year (2021-2022). Since 2019, investment in the Internet has consolidated, surpassing television, in the first position of advertising investment. With a total investment in 2022 of 4.533 billion euros, it accounts for 60% of all investment made in media.  
Beyond direct and indirect contributions to the economy, digital advertising also provides significant added value to citizens in their role as consumers, as it provides them with content at a reduced price or for free. In other words, the revenues derived from online advertising make possible the great value that consumers obtain from the online services they use. This surplus is about 40 euros per month per household, more than what the family pays for broadband access at home.  

We must also consider that advertising has a multiplier effect throughout the economy through direct and indirect jobs, increased sales, etc.  
According to the study "The Economic Contribution of Advertising in Europe," prepared by Deloitte, it also represents a favorable data point for the European economy in the labor field, as there are 5,762,573 jobs in the EU supported by advertising, which represents 2.6% of employment in the EU (considering jobs directly impacted by advertising and jobs compatible with the activity chain).  
At the same time, it must be noted that the use of data storage and retrieval devices on users' terminal equipment has significant implications regarding their privacy. The cookie is one of those widely used devices, as it is a common tool for tracking and managing user preferences and behaviors.

The second paragraph of Article 22 of the LSSI establishes:  
“Service providers may use storage and data retrieval devices on the terminal equipment of the recipients, provided that they have given their consent after being provided with clear and complete information about their use, in particular, regarding the purposes of data processing, in accordance with the provisions of Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13, on the Protection of Personal Data.  
When technically possible and effective, the recipient's consent to accept data processing may be provided through the appropriate settings of the browser or other applications.  
The above shall not prevent the possible storage or technical access solely for the purpose of carrying out the transmission of a communication over an electronic communications network or, to the extent that it is strictly necessary, for the provision of a service of the information society expressly requested by the recipient.”  
Consequently, the guidelines in this guide will be useful for those cases to which the second paragraph of Article 22 of the LSSI applies in accordance with the provisions established in Chapter II of the LSSI itself, titled “Scope of Application.”  
In particular, it is important to clarify that, in accordance with the transcribed provision, it applies to any “storage and data retrieval devices” on any “terminal equipment of the recipients” and that the annex of the aforementioned LSSI defines the “Recipient of the service or recipient” as the “natural or legal person who uses, whether or not for professional reasons, a service of the information society.”  
Thus, Article 22 of the LSSI and this guide refer to the use of cookies and similar technologies used (such as local shared objects or flash cookies, web beacons or bugs, etc.) to store and retrieve data from a terminal equipment (for example, a computer, a mobile phone, or a tablet) of a natural or legal person who uses, whether or not for professional reasons, a service of the information society. The aforementioned regulation also applies to the use of fingerprinting techniques, that is, to the techniques for taking the digital fingerprint of the device.  
This guide contains some practical guidelines on how information should be provided to comply with the requirement of informed consent, as the aforementioned Article 22.2 of the LSSI links the obtaining of consent to the information provided to the user, so that the consent that, if applicable, the user gives is informed. This Article 22.2 refers to Organic Law 15/1999, of December 13, on the protection of personal data.

In this regard, the GT29, in its Opinion 4/2012, interpreted that among the exempted cookies would be those aimed at:  

◤ "User input" cookies.  

◤ Authentication or user identification cookies (session only).  

◤ User security cookies.  

◤ Multimedia player session cookies.  

◤ Session cookies for load balancing.  

◤ User interface customization cookies.  

◤ Certain plug-in cookies for exchanging social content.  
Thus, it can be understood that these cookies are excluded from the scope of Article 22.2 of the LSSI, and therefore, it would not be necessary to inform or obtain consent for their use. On the contrary, it will be necessary to inform and obtain consent for the use of any other type of cookies, both first-party and third-party, session or persistent, that do not fall outside the scope of Article 22.2 of the LSSI and for which the guidelines of this guide will be useful.  
That said, for reasons of transparency, it is recommended to inform, at least in general terms, about those cookies excluded from the scope of Article 22.2 of the LSSI, either in the cookie policy or in the privacy policy itself (example: “This website uses cookies that allow the functioning and provision of the services offered on it”).  
In any case, it should be taken into account that the same cookie can have more than one purpose (multipurpose cookies), so there is a possibility that a cookie is exempt from the scope of Article 22.2 of the LSSI for one or more of its purposes and not for others, with the latter being subject to the scope of application of that provision. This should, in the words of the GT29, “encourage website owners to use a different cookie for each purpose.”  
Regarding the cookie management or configuration systems referred to later in this guide, when using multipurpose cookies with two or more different purposes that are not exempt from the scope of Article 22.2 of the LSSI, it must be ensured that these cookies are only used if all the purposes they group are accepted; that is, if a cookie serves two purposes, but the user only accepts one of them, the cookie should not be used, unless the management system used allows for differentiated treatment of the different purposes of these multipurpose cookies, so that it is possible for the cookie to operate only with the accepted purpose if the user accepts one of its purposes and not others.

2.1 COOKIES AND SIMILAR TECHNOLOGIES  
For the purposes of this guide, it is useful to first define a series of concepts.  
Terminology and definitions  
The LSSI applies to cookies understood in the sense indicated at the beginning of this guide, that is, as any type of device for storing and retrieving data that is used on a user's terminal equipment with the purpose of storing information and retrieving information already stored, as established in Article 22.2 of the LSSI.  
Cookies allow the storage on the user's terminal of amounts of data ranging from a few kilobytes to several megabytes.  
Below, a classification of cookies is made based on a series of categories. However, it is necessary to keep in mind that the same cookie may be included in more than one category.  
2.1.1. Types of cookies according to the entity that manages them  
According to who the entity is that manages the equipment or domain from which the cookies are sent and processes the data obtained, we can distinguish:  
a) First-party cookies: these are those for which the editor is responsible and that, generally, are sent to the user's terminal equipment from a device or domain managed by the editor itself and from which the service requested by the user is provided.  
b) Third-party cookies: these are those for which a different entity from the editor is responsible and that, generally, are sent to the user's terminal equipment from a device or domain that is not managed by the editor, but by another entity that processes the data obtained through the cookies.  
In the case that cookies are served from a device or domain managed by the editor itself, but the information collected through them is managed by a third party, they cannot be considered as first-party cookies if the third party uses them for its own purposes (for example, improving the services it provides or providing advertising services on behalf of other entities).  
2.1.2. Types of cookies according to their purpose  
There are many purposes for the use of cookies. Depending on the purpose for which the data obtained through cookies is processed, some of the purposes may be:  
a) Technical cookies: these are those that allow the user to navigate through a website, platform, or application and use the different options or services that exist therein, including those that the editor uses to enable the management and operation of the website and enable its functions and services, such as, for example, controlling traffic and data communication, identifying the session, accessing restricted access parts, remembering the items that make up an order, carrying out the purchase process of an order, managing payment, controlling fraud linked to the security of the service, making a request for registration or participation in an event, counting visits for the purpose of billing software licenses with which

functions the service (website, platform, or application), use security elements during browsing, store content for the dissemination of videos or sound, enable dynamic content (for example, loading animation of text or image), or share content through social networks. Also belonging to this category, due to their technical nature, are those cookies that allow the management, in the most efficient way possible, of the advertising spaces that, as an additional design or "layout" element of the service offered to the user, the publisher has included on a webpage, application, or platform based on criteria such as the edited content, without collecting information from users for purposes other than personalizing that advertising content or other content. Technical cookies will be exempt from compliance with the obligations established in Article 22.2 of the LSSI when they allow the provision of the service requested by the user, as is the case with the cookies listed in the previous paragraphs. However, if these cookies are also used for non-exempt purposes (for example, for behavioral advertising purposes), they will be subject to such obligations.  

b) Preference or personalization cookies: These are those that allow remembering information so that the user accesses the service with certain characteristics that can differentiate their experience from that of other users, such as, for example, the language, the number of results to display when the user performs a search, the appearance or content of the service based on the type of browser through which the user accesses the service or the region from which they access the service, etc. If it is the user themselves who chooses those characteristics (for example, if they select the language of a website by clicking on the icon of the corresponding country's flag, the currency they wish to use in transactions, the font size, or the color contrast between the background and the content to improve readability), the cookies will be exempt from the obligations of Article 22.2 of the LSSI as they are considered a service expressly requested by the user, provided that the cookies adhere exclusively to the selected purpose. These preference cookies that affect the user interface do not necessarily have to be session cookies, as requiring the user to determine their preferences for these purposes each time they visit a website or access an application can be annoying and cause fatigue. However, for these cookies to be considered exempt, their use must be limited to what is necessary for their purpose, and the information that may be derived from the user's selection cannot be used for other purposes (for example, for the personalization of advertising content) or to create a user profile.  

c) Analysis or measurement cookies: These are those that allow the responsible party to track and analyze the behavior of users of the websites to which they are linked, including the quantification of the impacts of advertisements. The information collected through this type of cookies is used in measuring the activity of the websites, application, or platform, in order to introduce improvements based on the analysis of the usage data that users of the service generate. Regarding the processing of data collected through analysis cookies, the GT29 stated that, although they are not exempt from the duty to obtain informed consent for their use, it is unlikely that they represent a risk to users' privacy as long as they are first-party cookies, that process aggregated data for strictly statistical purposes, that information about their uses is provided, and that the possibility for users to express their refusal to their use is included.  

d) Behavioral advertising cookies: These are those that store information about the behavior of users obtained from

2.1.3. Types of cookies according to the duration they remain active  
According to the duration they remain active on the terminal equipment, we can distinguish:  
a) Session cookies: these are designed to collect and store data while the user accesses a webpage. They are usually used to store information that is only relevant to retain for the provision of the service requested by the user in a single session (for example, a list of purchased products) and disappear at the end of the session.  
b) Persistent cookies: these are those in which the data remains stored on the terminal and can be accessed and processed for a defined period by the cookie's controller, which can range from a few minutes to several years.  
In this regard, it should be specifically assessed whether the use of persistent cookies is necessary, as privacy risks could be reduced by using session cookies. In any case, when persistent cookies are installed, it is recommended to minimize their duration based on the purpose of their use.  
For these purposes, Opinion 4/2012 of the GT29 indicated that for a cookie to be exempt from the obligation of informed consent, its expiration must be related to its purpose. Therefore, session cookies are much more likely to be considered exempt than persistent cookies.

OBLIGATIONS  
3.  
The legal obligations imposed by the regulations are twofold, namely: the obligation of transparency and the obligation to obtain consent.  
Prior to the study by the participating entities of the most appropriate solutions to comply with the aforementioned obligations (in accordance with the nature of their activity, the business model they develop, and the scope of their responsibility), it is recommended that they carry out a review of the cookies used, either internally or with the advice of associations or specialized entities. This review will aim to identify the cookies being used, analyzing whether they are first-party or third-party cookies, session or persistent cookies, and specifying their function in order to decide whether they fall within the scope of application of Article 22.2.  
Considering the possible changes that may occur in the management and use of cookies, it is advisable to periodically conduct this review in order to update the available information about them.  
In any case, it is reminded that the solutions implemented to comply with the obligations of Article 22.2 of the LSSI must be technologically neutral and, therefore, must be solutions that most browsers recognize.  

3.1 TRANSPARENCY  
3.1.1. What information must be provided  
The second section of Article 22 of the LSSI establishes that clear and complete information must be provided to users regarding the use of data storage and retrieval devices, and in particular, regarding the purposes of data processing. This information must be provided, as indicated, in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR, which requires that the processing of users' data be carried out transparently for them.  
Consequently, the information about cookies provided at the time of requesting consent must be sufficiently complete to allow users to understand their purposes and the use that will be made of them.  
The cookie policy must include the following information22:  
a) Definition and generic function of cookies.

3.1.2.2. Layered Information  
In its guidelines on transparency, the GT29 recommends the use of tiered privacy statements or notices, that is, containing information in layers, allowing the user to go to those aspects of the statement or notice that are of greater interest to them, thus avoiding information fatigue, and without prejudice to the fact that all information is available in one place or in a complete document that can be easily accessed if the interested party wishes to consult it in its entirety.  
This system may consist of displaying essential information in a first layer when accessing the page or application, and completing it in a second layer through a page that offers more detailed and specific information about cookies.

a) Identification of the publisher responsible for the website. b) Identification of the purposes of the cookies that will be used.

3.2.6. Obtaining consent for the use of cookies when a publisher provides services through different pages  
The same publisher providing different services through different domains may, through a single webpage, inform and obtain consent for the use of cookies that are sent from the other domains that it owns and offer similar content or features, due to the provision of services requested by the user, provided that, in addition to what is stated above in the section on information, it is informed about which webpages or domains it owns from which the cookies will be sent, the type of cookies, and the purposes for which they are processed, and consent from the user is obtained.  
In the event that the pages through which a publisher provides services offer content or have characteristics that are not similar (for example: some of the pages contain adult content), it will be necessary to adopt additional precautions.

This Agency considers it good practice that the validity of the consent given by a user for the use of a specific cookie does not exceed 24 months and that during this time the selection made by the user regarding their preferences is preserved, without requiring new consent each time they visit the relevant page.  

3.2.9. Withdrawal of Consent for the Use of Cookies  
Users must be able to withdraw the consent previously granted at any time. To this end, the publisher must ensure that it provides information to users in its cookie policy on how they can withdraw consent and delete cookies. The user must be able to revoke consent easily. The system offered for withdrawing consent must be as easy as the one used when consent was given. It will be considered that such ease exists, for example, when the user has simple and permanent access to the cookie management or configuration system.  

3.2.10. Possibility of Denying Access to the Service in Case of Rejection of Cookies  
Following the EDPB guidelines on consent, for consent to be given freely, access to services and functionalities must not be conditioned on the user's acceptance of the use of cookies. Therefore, so-called "cookie walls" that do not offer an alternative to consent cannot be used, as explained below. This criterion is particularly important in cases where denial of access would prevent the exercise of a legally recognized right by the user, as, for example, access to a website may be the only means provided to the user to exercise such a right.  

There may be certain cases where the refusal to accept the use of cookies prevents access to the website or the total or partial use of the service, provided that the user is adequately informed about this and an alternative is offered, not necessarily free of charge, for accessing the service without the need to accept the use of cookies. According to the EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent, the services of both alternatives must be genuinely equivalent, and it will not be valid for the equivalent service to be offered by an entity unrelated to the publisher.  

Regarding the free access to internet services, the question has been raised about under what circumstances and conditions the "consent or remuneration" models (also popularly called "pay or okay") related to behavioral advertising can be applied by large online platforms in a way that constitutes valid consent and, in particular, freely given, so as to legitimize the processing by such large online platforms of the personal data of the interested parties (the users).

In this regard, the European Data Protection Committee (EDPC) has approved Opinion 8/2024, which exclusively affects the offering by large online platforms of access to the service without behavioral advertising through the payment of a fee. Among the large online platforms referred to in the opinion, examples include major social networks. Furthermore, it is expected that during the first half of 2025, the EDPC will publish a General Application Guide on the validity of consent in "pay or okay" models.  
Opinion 8/2024 states that, in most cases, it will not be possible for large online platforms to meet the requirements to understand that consent has been validly granted by the data subjects if they only offer users a binary choice between consenting to the processing of personal data for behavioral advertising purposes and paying a fee to avoid such processing of personal data.  
The EDPC adds that offering (only) a payment alternative to the service that includes processing for behavioral advertising purposes should not be the default option for data controllers, indicating that, when developing the alternative to the version of the service with personal data processing for behavioral advertising, large online platforms must consider providing data subjects with an "equivalent alternative" that does not involve paying a fee.  
If data controllers choose to charge a fee for access to the "equivalent alternative," they must also consider the possibility of offering another free alternative, without behavioral advertising, which can initially be considered equivalent.  
This alternative, the EDPC adds, could consist of general or contextual advertising. That is, advertising that requires the processing of less or no personal data from the recipient, such as, for example, being based on a selection of topics chosen by the subject from a list of subjects of interest provided by the platform (Cf. paragraph 75 of Opinion 8/2024).  
This option derives from the principle of data minimization, which obliges data controllers to ensure that only the data necessary for advertising activities will be processed. The offering of this free alternative is a particularly important factor when assessing whether the consent granted for behavioral advertising would be considered valid and whether there has been any detriment to the data subject.

4.1 THE PUBLISHER OR THIRD PARTIES USE COOKIES FOR PURPOSES EXEMPT FROM THE OBLIGATIONS TO INFORM AND OBTAIN CONSENT  
The LSSI does not define who is responsible for fulfilling the obligation to provide information about cookies and obtain consent for their use.  
It is necessary for the parties involved in the use of cookies to collaborate to ensure compliance with the established legal requirements.  
Basically, in the management of cookies and in the processing of the data obtained, two situations are distinguished below based on the purpose for which the data obtained through the use of cookies is processed.  
Responsibility of the parties in the use of cookies  
In those cases where a publisher offers users a service and all the cookies used from their website are used exclusively for purposes for which it is not necessary to obtain consent as listed above, whether they are first-party or third-party cookies, it will not be necessary to inform about their use or to obtain consent.  
However, in the case that third-party cookies are used for the provision of the service requested by the user, a contractual agreement must be established with those entities with which they contract directly, ensuring that those entities do not process the data for any other purpose than providing the service to the user, since otherwise, it would be necessary to inform about those other purposes and obtain consent.  
In this case, it can be differentiated whether first-party or third-party cookies are used.  
In cases where the publisher, through the use of first-party cookies, processes the data for any of the purposes that are not exempt from the obligation to inform and obtain consent, it will be necessary to inform about the purposes for which the data will be processed and obtain the user's consent, through one of the forms established in this guide.  
Likewise, when third-party cookies are used for any of the non-exempt purposes, both the publisher and the other entities involved in the management of the cookies will have the responsibility to ensure that users are clearly informed about the cookies and the purposes for which they will be used and to obtain the requisite consent.

Responsibility of the parties in the use of cookies  
In this regard, third parties will be individually and directly responsible for their compliance. Generally, third-party cookies store information on the user's terminal equipment or use such information because the publisher, when designing the website, platform, or application, anticipated the possibility of including third-party content that uses them or because it employs third-party software that requires them. Therefore, publishers, when they are not the owners of the cookies being used, must ensure that the interested parties receive the necessary information and that mechanisms are enabled to allow their consent, for example, through contractual obligations or guarantees that bind the third-party owner of the cookies or through the installation of platforms for consent management. Thus, when information about third-party cookies is offered through a link to the third party's website, the publisher must ensure that the links are not broken, with the third party being obligated to ensure that the information is not outdated and that it is also provided in Spanish or in the co-official language used on the publisher's website. In this way, in such cases and with the aim of facilitating and ensuring compliance with the obligations established in this area, when third-party cookies are used, the contracts entered into between publishers and third parties should include one or more clauses ensuring that users will be provided with the required information and that the means through which valid consent for the use of cookies and for its revocation can be obtained will be articulated, as well as the consequences of the revocation of consent for the publisher and, especially, for the third parties who obtained it through the publisher. It should be noted that in practice, when a CMP is not used, it is more difficult for entities that use third-party cookies, which do not have a direct relationship with the user, to comply with the above requirements, and that users will tend to direct their concerns to the entity they can identify and with which they have a relationship, that is, the publisher. It should also be taken into account that the owners of the cookies, to the extent that they determine the purposes and means of processing, are responsible for the personal information collected by them and for subsequent data processing that is carried out. Thus, advertisers, publishers, agencies, advertising networks, and other intervening agents will be responsible for processing when they use their own cookies and when, using third-party cookies, they participate in determining the purposes and means of processing, even if this is carried out through a data processor, such as when an advertiser hires a media agency to carry out the processing under its direction and in accordance with its instructions. Those agents who limit their actions to following the instructions of the data controller will be considered data processors. The figure of the data processor is defined in Article 4.8 of the GDPR and is regulated in Article 28. In principle, each data controller will be responsible for the specific processing it carries out. Even in cases where different data controllers are involved, each will assume its respective responsibility. However, in cases where the entities referred to in the previous paragraphs (advertisers, agencies, advertising networks, publishers, and other agents) jointly determine the purposes and means of processing, they will be considered joint controllers of the processing and must comply with the provisions of Article 26 of the GDPR. That said, the existence of joint responsibility will not necessarily translate into equivalent responsibility of the different entities involved in the processing of personal data, but rather the responsibility of each entity will depend on the involvement it has in the specific processing. Therefore, the level of responsibility of each of them must be evaluated on a case-by-case basis, taking into account all relevant circumstances based on their respective responsibilities assumed in determining the means and purposes of processing. In this sense, the scope of the obligations of information and obtaining consent by the publisher regarding third-party cookies is limited to the processing for which it is responsible, including, where applicable, joint responsibility in ensuring that the user is aware of and consents to the use by identified third parties of their cookies and the purposes of the associated data processing. Such obligations do not extend, however, to the subsequent phases of processing in which the publisher does not intervene, which will be the exclusive responsibility of the third party. For example, responsibility would not extend to data processing subsequently carried out by third parties for the purpose of enabling them to offer audience creation services, profiles, or similar (developed from the processing of data obtained through cookies), nor can responsibility be presumed in the creation of such audiences or profiles simply because they are used. In any case, the administrative responsibility required before the supervisory authorities for compliance with the obligations arising from the use of cookies corresponds to each obligated party and is not transferable contractually.

A CMP is a tool installed on the publisher's support, website, or application, and allows any responsible party for the use of cookies to fulfill their duties of information and consent collection.

These are entities responsible for the design and execution of advertising, as well as the creation, preparation, or programming of advertising campaigns for advertisers, acting on behalf of and for the account of these in the purchase of advertising spaces. In this sense, they can also be considered as demanders of advertising spaces for advertisers.  
c) Advertising networks or ad networks  
These are a set of entities that, acting on behalf and direct or indirect representation of one or several publishers, offer, also directly to advertisers or indirectly through other demanders, such as advertising agencies, the possibility of obtaining advertising spaces or some type of specific result such as clicks, sales, or registrations, through the management and processing of data obtained from the use of cookies downloaded or stored on the users' terminal devices when they access the services provided by the publisher.  
The purpose of the work of these entities is to carry out, in the best possible way, the management of the inventory of advertising spaces, the ownership of which corresponds to the publishers, so that it converges with the demand of the advertisers (they sell the advertising space of the publishers).
