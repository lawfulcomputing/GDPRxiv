Recommendations for Protecting Personal Data in Mobility and Teleworking Situations  

The organization, as the data controller, may decide that certain activities of its business are carried out in mobility and teleworking situations. This decision may be part of the management strategy, either general or partial for specific areas or activities (for example, personnel who travel frequently), or it may be motivated by exceptional situations or even force majeure.  

If in the first case prior planning is required, in the second case, urgent circumstances may compel the implementation of provisional solutions. When this occurs, it is mandatory, in parallel and especially when the situation is prolonged, to reflect on and adjust the implementation of teleworking. It is important to consider that the resilience of the State, the continuity of business processes, and the rights and freedoms of the data subjects whose data is being processed depend on this.  

The organization and the personnel involved in teleworking actions must take into account the following recommendations.  

RECOMMENDATIONS ADDRESSED TO DATA CONTROLLERS  

Below is a set of recommendations for the data controller that must be adapted to the specific situation of their business object:  

1. Define a data protection policy for mobility situations  
   • Based on the entity's data protection and information security policy, and forming part of it, it is necessary to define a specific policy for mobility situations that addresses the specific needs and particular risks introduced by accessing corporate resources from spaces that are not under the organization's control.  
   • This policy must determine what forms of remote access are permitted, what types of devices are valid for each form of access, and the level of access allowed based on the defined mobility profiles. Responsibilities and obligations assumed by employees must also be defined.  
   • It is necessary to provide functional guides adapted to train employees, derived from these policies, which include at least the information presented in the section “Recommendations addressed to personnel participating in processing operations” of this same document.  
   • Personnel must also be informed of the main threats they may face when working outside the organization and the possible consequences that may arise if these guidelines are breached, both for the data subjects and for the employee.

• These guidelines must identify a contact point to report any incident affecting personal data, as well as the appropriate channels and formats for making such communication.  
• Staff must sign a telework agreement that includes the commitments made while performing their tasks in a mobility situation.  

2. Choose reliable solutions and service providers with guarantees1  
• It is necessary to avoid using telework applications and solutions that do not offer guarantees and that may lead to the exposure of personal data of staff, interested parties, and corporate services of the organization, particularly through email and messaging services.  
• It is essential to resort to providers and processors that offer proven solutions and sufficient guarantees that, in the same sense, prevent the exposure of personal data of staff, interested parties, and corporate services of the organization.  
• If they access personal data, they will be considered data processors, and the relationship will be governed by a contract or other legal act that binds the processor to the controller. This contract must establish the object, duration, nature, and purpose of the processing, the type of personal data and categories of data subjects, and the obligations and rights of the controller, in accordance with the terms established in Article 28.3 of the GDPR.  

3. Restrict access to information  
• Access profiles or levels to resources and information must be configured based on the roles of each employee, in an even more restrictive manner than those granted for access from the internal network.  
• Additionally, further access restrictions must be applied based on the type of device used to access the information (secured corporate laptops, external personal devices, and mobile devices such as smartphones or tablets) and also depending on the location from which access is made.  

4. Periodically configure the equipment and devices used in mobility situations  
• Remote access servers must be reviewed, and it must be ensured that they are correctly updated and configured to guarantee compliance with the information protection policy for mobility situations established by the organization, as well as the control of defined access profiles.  
• Corporate equipment used as clients must:  
  o be updated at the application and operating system level,  
  o have unnecessary services disabled,

5. Monitor access to the corporate network from the outside  
• Monitoring systems must be established to identify abnormal behavior patterns in the network traffic conducted within the framework of the remote access and mobility solution with the aim of preventing the spread of malware through the corporate network and unauthorized access and use of resources.  
• Security breaches affecting personal data must be communicated to the Control Authority and/or to the affected parties, with the purpose of creating a resilient teleworking environment.  
• Staff must be informed, in the information protection policy for mobility situations, about the existence and scope of these monitoring and supervision activities.  
• If monitoring activities are also used to verify compliance with the employees' labor obligations, the data controller must inform, in advance, clearly, expressly, and concisely, the employees and, where applicable, their representatives, of the measure adopted within the framework of the control functions provided for in the Workers' Statute that must be exercised within its legal framework and with the inherent limits thereof.  
• The monitoring mechanisms implemented in the context of remote access to corporate resources in mobility and teleworking situations must respect the digital rights established in the LOPDGDD, in particular, the right to privacy and use of digital devices and the right to digital disconnection in the workplace.  
• The configuration defined for remote access to resources must be reviewed periodically to ensure that it has not been altered or deactivated without authorization and remains updated and adapted to a continuously evolving external risk environment.

6. Manage data protection and security rationally  
• The measures and guarantees established in the defined policies must be based on a risk analysis that evaluates the proportionality between the benefits to be obtained from remote access and the potential impact of compromising access to personal information.  
• The policy must include internal procedures for provisioning and auditing remote access client devices, administration and monitoring procedures for the infrastructure, services provided by data processors, and how the policy is reviewed and updated according to existing risks.  
• The resources that can be accessed must be limited based on the risk assessment regarding the loss of the client device and the exposure or unauthorized access to the information handled.  
• Applications and remote access solutions must be planned and evaluated considering the principles of privacy by design and by default throughout all stages of the solution's deployment: from defining requirements and needs to the withdrawal of the solution or any of its components.  

RECOMMENDATIONS ADDRESSED TO PERSONNEL PARTICIPATING IN PROCESSING OPERATIONS  
Recommendations for personnel must be included in the telework policy of the responsible party, referenced in the telework agreement, and adjusted to the specific situation of the tasks to be performed. A guide on the content of these recommendations is as follows:  
1. Respect the information protection policy in defined mobility situations by the responsible party  
• The measures and recommendations outlined in the guides and the data protection and information security policy in defined mobility situations by the organization must be observed, as well as the rest of the rules and procedures that develop them, especially concerning the confidentiality duty of the employee regarding the personal data to which they had access in the performance of their work functions.  
2. Protect the device used in mobility and access to it  
• The employee must define and use robust access passwords that are different from those used to access personal email accounts, social networks, and other types of applications used in their personal life.  
• Applications or software that have not been previously authorized by the organization must not be downloaded or installed.

• It is advisable to avoid connecting devices to the corporate network from public places, as well as connecting to unsecured open WIFI networks.  
• The defined authentication mechanisms (certificates, passwords, tokens, two-factor systems, etc.) must be kept protected to validate against the organization's remote access control systems.  
• If a corporate device is available, it should not be used for personal purposes, avoiding access to social networks, personal email, websites with claims and impactful advertising, as well as other sites that may contain viruses or facilitate the execution of harmful code.  
• If the device used to establish the remote connection is personal, personal activity should be avoided simultaneously with professional activity, and independent profiles should be defined for each type of task.  
• The antivirus system installed on the device must be operational and updated.  
• The legitimacy of received emails must always be verified, checking that the originating email domain is valid and known, and being wary of downloading attachments with unusual extensions or establishing connections through links included in the body of the email that present any abnormal pattern.  
• If manageable by the employee, it is advisable to deactivate WIFI, Bluetooth, and similar connections that are not being used.  
• Once the workday in a mobile situation is concluded, the remote access session must be disconnected, and access to the device should be turned off or locked.  

3. **Ensure the protection of the information being handled**  
• Both in public places and in domestic environments, it is mandatory to take the necessary precautions to ensure the confidentiality of the information being managed.  
• If paper is regularly generated and worked with, during mobility situations it is important to minimize or avoid the entry and exit of documentation in this format and to take extreme precautions to prevent unauthorized access by third parties.  
• Information on paper, including drafts, cannot be discarded without ensuring that it is adequately destroyed. If possible, do not throw whole papers or pieces into trash bins in hotels, public places, or domestic garbage that someone could access and retrieve personal information from.  
• It is advisable to take extreme precautions to prevent unauthorized access to personal information, both one’s own and that of third parties, being handled, by not leaving any information storage media in plain sight in the place where teleworking is conducted and by locking device sessions when they are unattended.  
• The screen should be kept out of sight from third parties. If working regularly from public places, it is advisable to use a privacy filter for the screen.  
• As much as possible, it is advisable to prevent conversations from being overheard by third parties by using, for example, headphones or moving to a space where the employee is not accompanied.  

4. **Store information in the designated network spaces**  
• It is advisable to avoid storing information generated during mobility situations locally on the device used, preferring to use the shared or cloud storage resources provided by the organization.  
• If the use of personal devices is allowed, under no circumstances should unauthorized applications in the entity's policy for sharing information (cloud file hosting services, personal emails, instant messaging, etc.) be used.  
• The corporate backup policy defined for each device should not be blocked or disabled.  
• It is advisable to periodically review and delete any residual information that may remain stored on the device, such as temporary browser files or document downloads.  

5. **If there is suspicion that the information may have been compromised, immediately report the security breach**  
• Any anomaly that may affect the security of the information and the personal data processed must be reported to the responsible party without delay and as soon as possible, through the defined channels for this purpose.  
• In the event of any issue that may arise in the context of mobility situations and that may represent a risk to the protection of information and access to corporate resources, the employee must consult with the Data Protection Officer and the information security officer, or the designated responsible profiles, providing them with all relevant information of which they are aware.
