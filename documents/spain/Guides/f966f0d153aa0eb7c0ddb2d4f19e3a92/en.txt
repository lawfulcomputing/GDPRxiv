EXECUTIVE SUMMARY  
The use of new technologies has been applied across all sectors, allowing for the contracting of essential services for citizens. We are talking about sectors such as telecommunications operators, energy marketers, financial entities, insurance, etc., which carry out part of their contracts using the network and without the simultaneous physical presence of the parties involved in the contracting.  
The Spanish Agency for Data Protection, as the Control Authority, has among its functions the promotion of public awareness and understanding of the risks, standards, guarantees, and rights related to the processing of personal data, as well as raising awareness among those responsible for and in charge of data processing about their obligations. On the other hand, it has always been aware that the greatest problem posed by the use of digital means is the potential for identity theft and the negative consequences for citizens, as well as the need for companies to be able to verify the services contracted when these are carried out remotely and therefore without the presence of the contractors.  
It is known that there are other risks associated with electronic contracting, such as credential theft, for example through phishing, and the impersonation of the service provider. Nevertheless, within the Strategic Plan of the Agency, this Official Inspection Plan has been undertaken with the aim of analyzing data processing in the sectors of telecommunications operators and energy marketers, its compliance with data protection regulations always viewed from the perspective of verifying the identity of the contractor and the services contracted.  
At all times, emphasis has been placed on verifying the procedures used in these sectors to identify the interested parties and thereby avoid, as much as possible, identity theft and the consequent fraud. Special attention has also been paid to how companies verified the contracting of their services.  
Therefore, actions have been taken in entities that are implementing innovative projects to overcome these obstacles. The procedures analyzed have been: electronic handwritten signature, facial recognition, online risk analysis products, certified electronic notification, and evidentiary proof of digital events.  
It is noteworthy to indicate that during the course of this Plan, the application of data protection regulations has positively evolved in many aspects and has meant a substantial change in the way data processing is approached in both sectors, as due to the volume of data they manage, they have found it necessary to carry out impact assessments of already implemented procedures and promote mechanisms to ensure the privacy and security of information with the new vision adopted following Regulation EU 2016/679, General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and Organic Law 3/2018, of December 5, on Personal Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD).

OBJECTIVES
The main objectives of this inspection plan are as follows:
1. To verify compliance with the legal obligations established in the applicable regulations regarding distance contracting in telecommunications and energy sectors.
2. To assess the effectiveness of the measures implemented by operators and marketers to protect consumer rights and personal data.
3. To identify potential risks and areas for improvement in the practices of telecommunications operators and energy marketers.
4. To promote transparency and accountability in the distance contracting processes.

6

SCOPE
This inspection plan will cover:
1. Telecommunications operators providing services through distance contracting.
2. Energy marketers engaging in distance contracts with consumers.
3. All relevant documentation and practices related to the contracting processes, including advertising, information provided to consumers, and the management of personal data.

7

METHODOLOGY
The inspection will be carried out through the following steps:
1. Collection of information: Gathering data from operators and marketers regarding their distance contracting practices.
2. Analysis of documentation: Reviewing contracts, terms and conditions, and privacy policies to ensure compliance with legal requirements.
3. Interviews: Conducting interviews with key personnel involved in the contracting processes to understand their practices and challenges.
4. Reporting: Preparing a detailed report on the findings of the inspection, including recommendations for improvement.

8

CONCLUSION
The implementation of this inspection plan is essential to ensure that distance contracting practices in the telecommunications and energy sectors are conducted in compliance with legal standards and that consumer rights are adequately protected. The continuous evolution of technology necessitates ongoing oversight and adaptation of legal frameworks to safeguard the interests of consumers in this rapidly changing environment.

GENERAL ASPECTS  
TELECOMMUNICATION OPERATORS  
Most of the large telephone operators have different commercial brands that offer different types of tariffs and are aimed at different customer segments. In some cases, all commercial brands operate under the same Tax Identification Number (NIF), while in others, they have different ones but are owned by the parent operator. On the other hand, each of the commercial brands has its own information system and commercial strategy.  
When contracting, it is the interested parties themselves who provide their personal and billing data. In cases of telephone channels, these are usually recorded directly as a measure of proof of the contract, and in cases of portability from another company, they do so through a third entity (with which they have a service provision contract as data processors) in order to verify the provided data, as stipulated in the current regulations (Circulars 1/2008 and 1/2009 of the CMT, now CNMC). The teleoperator informs about the Privacy Policy using scripts, requests consent for sending commercial communications, and usually refers to the website for further details on the privacy policy.  
In contracts made electronically, the interested parties provide their data using the websites of the operator they wish to contract with. In cases of portability, this information is recorded in a computer system pending the consent of the operator owning the line (donor operator), at which point the interested party becomes a customer of the new operator.  
The websites provide information about the Privacy Policy and request consent on various aspects: receiving commercial communications, receiving commercial communications from third parties, communication to Group companies, or use for making automated decisions, among others.  
All operators have mobile applications that allow for various types of actions, from contracting value-added products, new mobile terminals, new telephone lines, tariff changes… to merely informational applications. In general, new contracting is not allowed through this channel without being a customer beforehand.

Accreditation of the identity of the contractor  
In the stores of some telephone operators, the mobile terminal acquired can be delivered to customers who have contracted through telephone or telematic means, in which case the presentation of an official identity document (DNI, NIE, passport) and a banking document that proves that the account on which charges will be made is owned by the contractor is requested.  
Deliveries are also made using the Postal Service, which verifies the customer's DNI using a scanning system to collect a copy.  
Telephone contracts are usually recorded, and in cases of portability, a third entity performs verification by third parties.  
Telecommunications companies are engaged in new projects that allow for the accreditation of the potential customer's identity prior to the formalization of the contract via the Internet. We are talking about innovative projects such as facial recognition through the sending of an SMS message with a link to an application that will allow the customer to send an image of their DNI and a proof of image of their face consisting of a real-time photograph of the customer's face, so that the photograph will be biometrically analyzed and compared with that of the DNI to ensure that it is the holder of this document who is requesting the contract.  
Another project, already being implemented by many operators, is aimed at verifying the customer's identity and the ownership of the current account in the Internet contracting process and prior authorization from the account holder (backed by the European PSD2 Payment Services Regulation) that will allow third parties to access customers' bank accounts and initiate payments on their behalf.  
Accreditation of distance contracting  
Telecommunications companies are also implementing projects that allow for the accreditation of the services contracted and the consents granted by the customer at the time of contracting.  
One of the services that are beginning to be implemented corresponds to the use of a trusted third party that certifies that the customer, through the web, has contracted and accepted the data processing, generating a file with the collected data and digitally signed, also encrypting all content, so that any modification would result in a new file.  
In cases of portability, SMS messages are usually sent to the phone that is to be ported before activating the order, which, as an additional control measure, must be responded to by accepting the request. In the case of using certified electronic notification, a trusted third party certifies the delivery of the message.  
In general, the IP address and the date and time when the event occurred are stored.

Verification of the customer's identity in subsequent processes  
In the Customer Area of the website, customers identify and authenticate themselves through a user code and password. Since a customer may have more than one line contracted, the user code can be either the line number (access only to the data associated with the queried line) or the ID number (access to all data associated with the lines contracted by the user).  
In the Customer Service departments (telephone channel) of the different companies, customers are usually identified by full name, ID number, and a third random piece of information such as the address to which invoices are sent or bank account number, although many companies provide a password (at the customer's request) that must be indicated to proceed with access to the data.  
Alerts  
Some companies in the sector have identified different circumstances that may be susceptible to fraud in contracting (high concentration of orders in certain areas, same IP addresses…) and have implemented procedures that allow for alerts regarding these anomalies and determine the probability of fraud, in which case, the contracts are usually verified by specialists and additional documentation may be requested from the customer.  
Also, in the case of non-payments, random fraud tests are conducted. Upon reasonable suspicion of fraud or identity theft, any debt that may have been incurred is canceled, and cases that may be related are investigated.  
ENERGY MARKETERS  
There are multiple services offered by energy marketing companies. However, the most relevant concerning data protection regulations correspond to:  
• new contracts in which the customer has no service with any other marketing company,  
• change of marketer in which the customer has an active contract with another company,  
• change of account holder in the case of an active contract where the holder is modified while maintaining the contract with the same marketing company.  
In addition to these services, all marketers offer modifications to the active contract: tariff changes, power modifications… and also provide the customer with the option to contract maintenance services associated with energy.  
Companies have different channels for making contracts. The most common correspond to: in-person contracting, contracting requests through websites and mobile apps, and telephone requests, whether initiated by the interested party or by the entity's call center or third-party companies with service provision contracts.

**Verification of the identity of the contractor**  
In telematic channels and, in general, customers already registered with an energy company who request new contracts identify themselves with a user code and password through the Customer Area of the website or, if they do not have identification, they are asked for their ID and another identifying piece of information (usually the contract number or bank account number). Interested parties in contracting who are not customers of the company are asked for personal, contact, banking, and supply point data (CUP – which uniquely identifies a supply point) and the address of the same.  
Through the Customer Service Departments (telephone channel) for contracting services, the contractor who is already a customer is generally identified using name, ID, and supply point address data, and in the case of non-customers, personal, contact, banking, and supply point and address data are also requested.  
All companies require documents to formalize the contracting, basically a copy of the ID and a copy of a bank receipt (or a photograph in the case of orders made with mobile applications). The companies send the interested party the direct debit document (SEPA form) by the system they have chosen (email, postal...) which must be returned signed.  
The audited companies do not allow contracting with interested parties who will not be the holders of the contract, except for authorized individuals.  
The ID of the potential customer is used in inquiries to common credit information systems.

using special devices for this purpose (digitizing tablets) that capture biometric data of the signer during the signing process, associating it with the signed electronic document.  
It is implemented in practically all banking entities and in other environments where documents in electronic format must guarantee authenticity, integrity, and non-repudiation. It has legal validity.  
The biometric data captured during the signing process relates to the pressure of the pen, the speed and acceleration of writing, the graph, the pen's movement, and the recording of the signing process. As basic requirements to have full legal guarantee, in addition to the confirmation of authorship through the aforementioned biometric data, non-repudiation must be considered through the storage of time stamps, type of device, etc.  
Biometric Facial Recognition  
Biometric facial recognition is a computer procedure for identifying a person in a digital image (facial recognition) by analyzing the facial characteristics extracted from an image or a frame of a video, generating a facial pattern or fingerprint in such a way that it serves for comparison. The stored biometric information (pattern) allows for partial reconstruction of the original biometric information. This reconstruction can sometimes have sufficient fidelity for another biometric system to recognize it, as it is possible to create a composite image, for example. The fidelity of the reconstruction depends on the amount of biometric information collected.  
Generally, the entities that use this procedure usually include in their own information systems the algorithm that allows generating the pattern, thus being responsible for carrying out the initial registration of the individuals' data.  
In the registration process, a photograph or video is requested along with a photocopy of the ID for subsequent human verification. With the biometric data extracted from the image, the pattern is generated and stored along with the previous documentation.  
Through the entity's website or app, the front and back of the ID are captured along with a video of the person or a selfie taken with a digital camera or mobile phone. Both images are compared, and the pattern is generated with a reliability index defined by the company, related to the risk assumed by the company itself. The pattern is stored along with the rest of the data provided by the interested party, encrypted in the entity's information systems (own or contracted).  
In case it is necessary to verify the identity of the person, a new complete procedure is carried out including a copy of the ID and an image of the interested party, generating a new pattern that is compared with the existing one, obtaining a percentage of similarity.

**Identification of Holders and Online Risk Analysis**  
With the entry into force of the second European Payment Services Directive (Payment Service Providers Directive - PSD2) in January 2018, which obliges financial entities to allow third-party companies access to their digital platforms provided they have the customer's consent, various solutions have emerged in the market that enable the identification of bank account holders and the execution of risk analyses at the moment a contract or a financing request is made through telematic means.  
Access to customer data is performed by the interested party themselves entering their credentials (username and password) without the involved entities knowing this information. Once access is granted, the banking entity provides the established data (from the identification of the holder - name, ID number, address… - to account movements, risk ratings, average balances… including data from common credit information systems, in many cases) that will allow the company to identify the holder and perform scoring if applicable.  
As seen in the operation of the sector that uses these types of procedures, a large amount of information is provided to the entities involved in the process, some of which are located in the European Union. Being a very specific topic and with a short implementation period, the Spanish Agency for Data Protection does not make any assessment in this report.  

**Certified Electronic Notification**  
Electronic notification is an electronic communication that certifies the content of the text, the sender, the recipient, the date and time of sending and delivery. The communication can be sent using email or as an SMS message.  
With the information from the notification, a certificate is generated that is associated with the recipient's data.  
The documents or text messages sent along with the obtained certificate are kept by the entity providing the electronic notification service, and in many cases, a notarial deposit is used as an additional security measure.  
For the submission of documentation, email is used, and documents in “pdf” format are generated, creating a hash (the result of applying a cryptographic algorithm to a series of data that uniquely represents a specific unencrypted text - original document - and has no inverse function) and the date and time are recorded, safeguarding the result (hash along with date and time). In many cases, an external provider (certification entity) is responsible for certifying and safeguarding the electronic proof (document and its hash), thus making the document unalterable.  
The recipient receives an email with a link to access the documentation, which includes a transaction identifier that uniquely identifies the document, and the recipient must accept the notification and access the documents for download, in which case a hash is generated with the notification information, again recording the date and time.  
Both the sender and the recipients receive the certificate of the notification made.

**Proof of Digital Events**  
There are entities in the market that provide companies with services aimed at obtaining proof of digital events used, among others, to certify contracts made through telematic means without the presence of any of the parties.  
The procedure is similar to certified electronic notification services, although the process begins with sending the contract conditions to the recipient, who, if in agreement, must sign, as they are informed that it will have contractual character. With all the documentation sent, the signature of the interested party, and the exchange process with the recipient, a hash is generated that uniquely identifies this process and does not allow alteration. The entity safeguards all the documentation along with the hash and the certificate, which is also sent to the sender and the recipient. Generally, it is also kept in notarial deposits with a reference to it.

**CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS**  
It should be noted that among the objectives pursued in the Audit Plan was the study of the procedures that the audited companies have implemented, and in some cases were in the process of implementing, the mechanisms to ensure the new vision adopted as a result of the General Data Protection Regulation. **TRANSPARENCY AND INFORMATION**  
All the investigated companies have user information on their websites adapted to the stipulations in Articles 13 and 14 of the Regulation and Article 11 of the LOPDPGDD in their Privacy Policy.  
• In business groups, there are companies that include information about all the entities in the group with the contact details of each one; however, some companies only include information for one responsible party.  
• The entities include the contact details of the Data Protection Officer (DPO). However, it has been detected that one of the telecommunications operators does not provide the contact address of the DPO, but only informs about the registered office of the company. It has also been noted in some cases that when accessing by the trade name, there is no information about the company's DPO, making it necessary to go to the page of the responsible party for the trade name to find such information.

DATA RETENTION  
•  
In the energy marketing sector, it is reported that data is maintained as long as the contractual relationship lasts and there is a legal obligation, in which case it will be kept blocked and will be exclusively available to the competent authorities.  
However, it has been detected that some entities, once the contractual relationship has ended, retain the data for 5 years after the existing debts and payment obligations have expired. Only one entity uses a retention period of between 5 and 15 years depending on the start date of the contractual relationship.  
Regarding information related to the sending of commercial communications, some companies retain the data of their former customers indefinitely for this purpose as long as they have not exercised their right to deletion.  
Companies that use recording with external providers usually have a scheduled periodicity for the cancellation of recordings by the service provider.  
In cases of requests from new customers that do not result in a contract for any reason, entities usually cancel the request/order and delete the provided data. If a recording is interrupted and the customer cannot be contacted to continue the process, the data provided up to that point is deleted.  
•  
In the telecommunications operator sector, the data of potential customers who have given their consent to be contacted is not deleted. Only some companies have procedures in place for this information to be periodically deleted (in one of the entities investigated, the period is three months) as long as they do not exercise their right to deletion or opposition beforehand.  
In general, customer data is not deleted unless requested by them, remaining in the files in a deactivated state.  
When a customer requests the cancellation of their data, it is blocked if there are no billing incidents.  
Traffic and location data are retained for one year.  
Companies that use recording with external providers usually have a scheduled periodicity for the cancellation of recordings.  
Some companies have procedures to permanently delete the data of customers who have requested deletion after a reasonable period has elapsed to resolve any incidents that may arise (one of the entities investigated proceeds to delete after 90 days of the request if no incidents have occurred).

SERVICES  

Make sure to access the website of the company with which you wish to contract the service.  
To do this, check that it is identified with the details of the responsible party, its registered office, and a simple and quick procedure for contact. This information must be available on the website itself (legal notice/privacy policy) in clear and concise language.  

Pay attention to the privacy policy.  
If you use the telephone channel, make sure they provide you with basic information and a reference to how to obtain additional information.  
Find out if your data will be communicated to third companies.  
Find out how they will use your data.  
They must provide you with information about the legal basis that allows the company to use your personal data.  

Remember that to receive commercial communications, there must be an existing commercial relationship and that the offered products must be similar to those contracted.  
Ensure that there is a simple and free procedure for you to oppose their receipt. In other cases, they must request your consent through unchecked boxes. Keep in mind that there are advertising exclusion systems where you can express your wish for your data not to be processed for sending you commercial communications.  

Find out about the criteria the company has for the retention of your data once the contractual relationship has ended.  
Inquire about the procedure to exercise your rights.  
The new regulations have included the right to limit processing, which allows you to oppose the deletion of the provided data, but disables the company from using it. And the right to data portability, which requires that the collected data be sent to a third company when technically possible.  

Use secure passwords based on combinations of numbers, letters, and special characters. It is very useful to remember passwords that are based on combined information that only you know, without using names, birthdays, or other similar easily discoverable information. It is very important to change passwords periodically.  

Log out after completing the process you are performing both on the website and in mobile applications.  
On mobile devices, use additional security measures.  
You can set a time lock and an access password. Many devices have fingerprint authentication or facial recognition that you can enable.  

Remember that the Spanish Agency for Data Protection is the competent authority for claims regarding data protection.
