ADMINISTRATIVE, DISCIPLINARY, CIVIL, AND CRIMINAL  
CONSEQUENCES OF THE DISSEMINATION OF SENSITIVE CONTENT  

1. For Companies  
Labor Inspection and Social Security  
The Law on Offenses and Sanctions in the Social Order (LISOS) classifies as a very serious administrative offense “The acts of the employer that are contrary to the respect for the privacy and due consideration of the dignity of workers.” Sanctioning procedures in the social order could be directed against companies.  
• Very serious offenses: fines from 6,251 to 187,515 euros (art. 40.1.c, LISOS)  

“Sexual harassment, when it occurs within the scope of the powers of business management, regardless of who the active subject is” (art. 8.13 LISOS)  

Example: a female worker subjected to a possible situation of sexual harassment reports it to the human resources management of the company. Despite the alert received, the company does not take any protective and preventive measures, and in particular does not activate the harassment protocol or specific procedure for processing the complaints and claims received (Article 48 of Organic Law 3/2007, for the effective equality of women and men)  
“harassment based on sexual orientation or based on sex, when it occurs within the scope of the powers of business management, regardless of who the active subject is, provided that, known by the employer, he has not adopted the necessary measures to prevent it” (art. 8.13 bis, LISOS)  
Example: two male workers in a company are subjected to harassment by their colleagues, including those in positions of responsibility, for maintaining a romantic relationship that is disseminated through images. The company management is aware of the situation and does not take measures to prevent it.  

Occupational Risk Prevention  
Any action or omission by the employer in this matter that causes harm to workers may constitute an offense; either for failing to adequately assess occupational risk, including so-called psychosocial risks, or for failing to develop the adoption of appropriate preventive measures, or for failing to carry out adequate health monitoring.

• Serious offenses: fines from 626 to 6,250 euros (art. 40.1.b, LISOS)  
“Failing to carry out risk assessments and, where appropriate, their updates and revisions, as well as periodic controls of working conditions and the activities of workers that are necessary, or failing to carry out those preventive activities that the results of the assessments made necessary, with the scope and content established in the regulations on occupational risk prevention” (Article 12.1 b)  
Example: a company has a risk assessment that has not been reviewed for three years. In recent months, there have been repeated absences of workers from the same department, who are subjected to mockery through a company workers' chat. In this chat, private conversations and photographs are leaked. The company, being aware of the situation, does not conduct a review of the psychosocial risk assessment, considering the initial assessment valid. As a consequence of the absence of an updated assessment, the company also does not adopt the necessary preventive measures.

2.2 Administrative Responsibility  
Spanish Agency for Data Protection  
The dissemination of especially sensitive data of a natural person (in content such as images, audios, or videos of a sexual or violent nature that allow identification), published on different internet services without consent is considered an unlawful processing of personal data.  
Example: A businessman is informed, by a complaint from the works council, that a supervisor, without the knowledge of the company management, had installed a hidden television camera in the women's locker rooms at the workplace, through which he recorded images while the workers were present and subsequently distributed them to some employees of the company. The Human Resources department agrees to initiate a disciplinary file, and after confirming the facts as true and that they are considered a very serious offense in the applicable collective agreement, agrees to the disciplinary dismissal of the worker, which is one of the sanctions that can be imposed, also according to the agreement, in the case of such a serious offense.

Administrative, disciplinary, civil, and criminal consequences of the dissemination of sensitive content  
This conduct is classified as a crime in Article 197.7 of the Penal Code: “Anyone who, without the authorization of the affected person, disseminates, reveals, or transfers to third parties images or audiovisual recordings of that person obtained with their consent in a home or in any other place out of the sight of third parties, when the disclosure severely undermines the personal privacy of that person, shall be punished with a prison sentence of three months to one year or a fine of six to twelve months.  
The penalty shall be imposed at its upper half when the acts have been committed by the spouse or by a person who is or has been connected to them by an analogous relationship of affection, even without cohabitation, the victim is a minor or a person with a disability in need of special protection, or the acts have been committed with a profit-making purpose.”  
Example: A group of students from a school assaults a disabled minor, and one of them records the assault and publishes it on the internet. The images are replicated by another student on the wall of a profile created on a social network.  
2.4 Liability for damages  
Citizens may have to compensate the victim for the damages arising from the unlawful conduct, both material and moral. In the case of minors, their parents or guardians, foster parents, and legal or de facto guardians would be jointly liable with them.  
Likewise, they are obliged to respond in the same manner for the material and moral damages caused when they occur by minors under 14 years of age, before the civil jurisdiction.

Publication in a public forum with contact details could constitute a crime against moral integrity, as provided in Article 173.1: “Anyone who inflicts degrading treatment on another person, severely undermining their moral integrity, shall be punished with a prison sentence of six months to two years.”
