ORGANIC LAW 3/2018, OF DECEMBER 5, ON PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION AND 
GUARANTEE OF DIGITAL RIGHTS 
NOVELTIES FOR THE PRIVATE SECTOR 
1. Obligation to inform citizens about the processing of their data and 
about the exercise of their rights 
Organizations are required to inform citizens clearly and simply about the most important aspects of the processing of their data, identifying who processes the data, on what legal basis, for what purpose, and how to exercise the rights of access, rectification, deletion, limitation of processing, portability, opposition, and automated decision-making, including profiling. 
Organizations may not deny the exercise of these rights if the citizen wishes to exercise them in a manner different from that offered. 
Organizations that process basic data may use the free tool FACILITA for their adaptation to the General Data Protection Regulation. 
2. Designation of a Data Protection Officer (DPO) and communication of the 
designation to the Spanish Agency for Data Protection (AEPD) 
The Law requires organizations whose main activities consist of processing that requires regular and systematic observation of citizens on a large scale, or large-scale processing of special categories of personal data, or data related to criminal convictions and offenses, to designate a Data Protection Officer who has the necessary qualifications, to ensure they have the means necessary to perform their functions, and to notify the designation to the AEPD for inclusion in the public Register of Data Protection Officers. 
In other cases, the designation of a Data Protection Officer will be voluntary. 
The Data Protection Officer is not personally liable for any potential violations of data protection committed by their organization simply by virtue of this fact. 
3. Involvement of the Data Protection Officer in the resolution of 
complaints 
The Data Protection Officer must receive complaints directed to them by citizens when they choose this route before filing a complaint with the AEPD, and will communicate the decision made to the individual within a maximum period of two months. 
Additionally, the Officer must receive complaints that the AEPD decides to forward to them prior to the initiation of a sanctioning procedure. The Officer must communicate the decision made to the AEPD within a maximum period of one month. 

In this way, generally speaking, if the Data Protection Officer manages to have the responsible party resolve the complaint through either of these two avenues, and without prejudice to the interested party subsequently addressing the AEPD, a sanctioning procedure would not be initiated. 

4. The legal bases that legitimize the processing of personal data of 
citizens by organizations 
The General Data Protection Regulation and the Organic Law outline several legal bases that legitimize the processing of personal data by private organizations: prior contractual relationship that includes the processing, consent of the citizen, or legitimate interest that prevails over the rights of individuals, among others. 
Therefore, currently, it is not necessary for the individual to consent to the processing of their personal data if there is another legal basis that legitimizes the processing. 
In cases where the citizen's consent is necessary due to the absence of another legitimizing basis, the Law establishes that it must be a manifestation of free, specific, informed, and unequivocal will by which a person accepts the processing of their personal data, either through a declaration or a clear affirmative action. Tacit or implied consent is excluded. 
Furthermore, when it is intended that the citizen's consent legitimizes processing for a variety of purposes, it must be specifically and unequivocally stated that such consent is granted for all of them. 
A contract or the provision of a service may not be denied on the grounds that the person does not consent to the processing of their personal data for purposes unrelated to that contract or the provision of that service. 

5. Processing of data of minors 
In the case of personal data of minors, when the processing of data is legitimized by consent, the organization must obtain the consent of the minor when they are at least 14 years old; and that of the parents or their legal representatives if the minor is under 14 years old. 

6. Limitation of advertising activity: the "Robinson lists" 
Entities that are going to carry out an advertising campaign must consult the "Robinson lists" in advance to avoid sending advertising to all citizens who have registered in them. 
This consultation will not be necessary in the case of citizens who have given their consent to receive advertising from an entity, whether they did so before or after registering in that list.

7. Employee Rights: Greater Privacy  
The Organic Law guarantees the right to privacy of employees in the workplace against the use of video surveillance devices and sound recording, as well as against the use of digital devices and geolocation systems, of which they must be informed in an express, clear, and unequivocal manner.  

8. Professional Contact Data: Legitimization of Its Processing  
The Law allows the use of personal contact data of individuals providing services in an entity, as well as of professionals (lawyers, doctors, etc.) and individual entrepreneurs, provided that they are processed for the purpose of maintaining contact with the entity in which they provide their services or for establishing contact for professional or business purposes.  

9. Internal Reporting Systems: Exemption from Criminal Liability for Organizations  
The Law includes internal reporting systems, including anonymous ones, as a mechanism for members of an organization to report the commission of infractions within it that may be contrary to the applicable general or sectoral regulations.  

10. Inclusion in Creditworthiness Information Systems (“Delinquent Files”)  
Citizens may be included in creditworthiness information systems when they have a debt of more than 50 euros with any service provider (the previous law did not establish any minimum amount).  
Citizens may not remain registered in these systems for more than 5 years, counted from the due date of the payment obligation (the previous law established a period of 6 years).  
The Law establishes that these information systems may be consulted:  
• when the person consulting has a contractual relationship with the individual and this relationship involves the payment of a specific amount,  
• when the individual has requested financing, deferred payment, or periodic billing.  
If, as a result of the consultation, the request for the contract is denied or the contract is not concluded, the person who consulted must inform the affected individual of the result of the consultation.

11. Updates on Video Surveillance  
• Capturing Public Spaces. Images of public spaces may only be captured when it is essential to preserve the safety of individuals and property as well as their facilities.  
However, capturing images of public spaces may extend further when necessary to ensure the safety of strategic assets or infrastructure related to transportation, without in any case allowing the capture of images from within a private residence.  
• Data Deletion. Data must be deleted within a maximum period of one month from its capture, unless it must be retained to prove the commission of acts that threaten the integrity of individuals, property, or facilities. In such cases, the images must be made available to the competent authority within a maximum period of 72 hours from the time the existence of the recording is known.  
• Duty to Inform. The duty to inform in the case of video surveillance will be fulfilled by placing an informative device in a sufficiently visible location that identifies, at a minimum, the existence of the processing, the identity of the responsible party, and the possibility of exercising rights. A connection code or internet address to this information may also be included in the informative device. The model of the video surveillance sign created by the Spanish Agency for Data Protection can be consulted at this link. In any case, the data controller must keep the information specified in point 1 of this document available to citizens.  

12. Commercial Operations  
The Law establishes that data processing, including prior communication, resulting from the development of any structural modification operation of companies or the contribution or transfer of business or business activity branch, will be presumed lawful, provided that the processing is necessary for the successful completion of the operation and guarantees, when appropriate, the continuity of service provision.  
In the event that the operation is not concluded, the acquiring entity must immediately proceed to delete the data, without the obligation to block being applicable.  

13. The Obligation to Block Personal Data After Exercising the Rights of Rectification or Deletion  
The data controller, when proceeding with the rectification or deletion of data, is obliged to block them; that is, to identify and reserve them using techniques that prevent their processing, including visualization.  
When blocking such data is not technically possible or is uneconomical, a secure copy of the information will be made so that

Digital evidence, or evidence of another nature, must be provided to verify the authenticity of the information, the date of the blocking, and the non-manipulation of the data.  
The blocking of personal data will only allow its availability to judges and courts, the Public Prosecutor's Office, or competent public administrations (such as the AEPD), for the enforcement of any potential liabilities arising from the processing and only for the statute of limitations period of such liabilities. After this period, the data must be destroyed.  

14. Processing of personal data in the notification of security incidents  
Public authorities, computer emergency response teams (CERT), computer security incident response teams (CSIRT), providers of electronic communications networks and services, and providers of security technologies and services may process the personal data contained in security incident notifications exclusively for the time and extent necessary for their analysis, detection, protection, and response, always adopting appropriate and proportionate security measures relative to the level of risk.  

15. Adaptation to the Organic Law on data processing service contracts  
Contracts for the processing of personal data between organizations (as data controllers) and third parties (as data processors) signed before May 25, 2018, will remain in force until a maximum of May 25, 2022.  

16. Processing of personal data in health research  
The new Organic Law relaxes the processing of data for health research:  
• it expands the purposes for which consent for processing can be granted,  
• it includes the possibility of reusing information for which consent has already been provided,  
• it includes the use of pseudonymized data as an option to facilitate health research, including guarantees to prevent the re-identification of affected individuals,  
• it regulates the guarantees of this processing, including the involvement of Research Ethics Committees or, failing that, the Data Protection Officer or an expert in personal data protection.

17. Modification of Law 3/1991, of January 10, on Unfair Competition: aggressive practices  
The Law on Unfair Competition identifies the impersonation of the Spanish Agency for Data Protection or its functions and the advice known as “adaptation to the Regulation at zero cost” as an aggressive practice, in order to limit the advice offered by companies with substandard services.
