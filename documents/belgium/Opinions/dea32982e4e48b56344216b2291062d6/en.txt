Opinion 155/2018 - 2/6

I. SUBJECT OF THE REQUEST FOR OPINION

1. The royal decree concerning the execution of the code of registration, mortgage, and registry rights and the maintenance of records in the registries of courts and tribunals (hereinafter "royal decree") aims to implement the new Articles 288 and 288bis of the Code of registration, mortgage, and registry rights, as these articles are worded after the law of October 14, 2018, amending the Code of registration, mortgage, and registry rights in order to reform registry fees. The applicant indicates that since 1968, this royal decree has not been systematically adapted to the evolution of legislation. This evolution has led to the elimination of many registry fees and also brought changes to the competencies and names of certain courts and tribunals and certain administrative services of the Federal Public Service Finance addressed by this decree. Furthermore, the registers and books, the maintenance of which in the registries is prescribed by the decree, are no longer kept on paper but maintained electronically.

II. SUBSTANTIVE EXAMINATION

Data Controller in the sense of Article 4, 2 (7) of the GDPR

2. The data controller is not explicitly identified in the text of the royal decree itself. In response to a request for additional information, the applicant specifies that it is the SPF Justice that is responsible for transmitting the lists of the registry fees that have become due to the SPF Finance and that the General Administration of Collection and Recovery (AGPR) is responsible for receiving the data.

3. The attribution of the role of data controller to an institution based on whether it is an "exporter" or "importer" of data creates confusion because the role of data controller is assigned for a "processing" of personal data as a whole, and if the organization determines the means and purposes of this processing, it must be qualified as a "data controller." In the present case, if the SPF Justice and the AGPR jointly determine the means and purposes of the processing, they must be qualified as joint data controllers and comply with the obligations that arise from this qualification (Article 26 GDPR). In order to ensure a clear distribution of responsibilities and the effective exercise of the rights of the data subjects, it is necessary to delineate the roles and responsibilities of the institutions involved in the processing of data.

Purpose and Legality

4. Article 288 of the Code of Registration, Mortgage, and Registry Fees forms the basis for the regulation via royal decree deliberated in the Council of Ministers for the collection and recovery of registration fees by the Federal Public Service Finance directly from the final debtors of these fees. Until the entry into force of the law of October 14, 2018, amending the Code of Registration, Mortgage, and Registry Fees, registration fees were paid by the applicant to the clerk in the form of a provision at the time of enrollment. Following the shift of the due date of registration fees to the time of the judgment or ruling, these fees will no longer be collected at the opening of the case but at its conclusion. Consequently, the clerk no longer has an active role in the collection of registration fees, except for providing data in the application from which the Federal Public Service Justice will collect the necessary data to enable the Federal Public Service Finance to collect the fees.

5. The authority considers that the processing established by the royal decree has a specific, explicit, and legitimate purpose in accordance with Article 5, 1(b) of the GDPR.

Proportionality

6. Article 3 of the royal decree states that the SPF Justice transmits, via an electronic flow, the lists with the registration fees that have become due to the SPF Finance within three working days following the day they became due. In order for the SPF Finance to ensure the collection and recovery of registration fees, these lists contain the following information: a unique reference code per case; the court or tribunal where the case has been registered; the date on which the registration fee became due; the case number; the identification of the debtors by mentioning, if available, their national number or, failing that, their identification number at the Crossroads Bank for Social Security when it concerns natural persons or their identification number at the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises when it concerns legal persons; the amount of the tax debt of each with a unique identification code for each of these debts; an indication for each debtor whether or not they benefit from legal aid as referred to in Article 664 of the Judicial Code; the total amount of registration fees that are due due to the case.

7. The Data Protection Authority draws attention to the fact that the use of the national register number requires authorization as provided by the law of August 8, 1983, organizing a national register of natural persons.

8. The applicant additionally indicates that the data "indication for each debtor whether or not they benefit from legal aid" allows informing the AGPR that the fees to be paid should not be recovered immediately but only if the person benefiting from legal aid is subsequently able to pay them in accordance with Articles 664 and 693 of the Judicial Code.

9. Article 3 states that the lists communicated to the SPF Finance mention "at least" the data previously enumerated. The Authority reminds that the personal data processed must be listed exhaustively so that it can assess the proportionality of the data in relation to the purposes of the processing.

10. The Authority deems that the data listed in Article 3 of the royal decree are relevant and not excessive in relation to the purposes of the processing.

Data Retention Period

11. The Authority reminds that the principle of data retention limitation requires that personal data not be retained for a period exceeding that of the realization of the purposes for which they are processed (Article 5(1)(e) GDPR). The royal decree should explicitly mention the duration for which the data is retained by the institutions involved.

Transparency  

12. The data controller must inform the data subjects about the processing concerning them in accordance with Article 12 of the GDPR. If the data is collected directly from the individual, the information listed in Article 13 must be provided; if the data is collected indirectly, the information listed in Article 14 of the GDPR must be provided.  

Rights of the data subjects  

13. The Authority acknowledges that the Royal Decree does not provide for any exceptions to the exercise of the rights set out in Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR; therefore, the modalities for exercising the rights provided for in Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR must be established. These modalities will depend on the role assumed by the different institutions as requested in point 3 of this notice.  

Security  

14. All personal data from the documents held by the registries (records of Article 14; personal data related to current accounts mentioned in Article 15, etc.) must be securely retained (in accordance with Article 28) and/or exchanged (in the cases provided for in Article 3 of the communication by the SPF Justice of the lists with the rights to be registered that have become enforceable at the SPF Finance, in Article 9 of the exchanges between the clerk and the receiver, in Article 31 of the communication of the records of Article 14, of the acts passed by them, or before them as well as the minutes of the rulings, judgments, orders, and all other acts of which they are custodians upon request from the agents of the general administration of heritage documentation) in a secure manner both physically and electronically (for example, access management, limiting data transmissions to what is strictly necessary, data encryption). Some of the processed data potentially belongs to the category of personal data related to criminal convictions and offenses (Art. 10, GDPR) and must be subject to enhanced security measures.  

15. Article 28 of the Royal Decree provides that "the official of the general administration of heritage documentation responsible for monitoring the registries has access to the data of the electronic database used for maintaining the records." The APD reminds that access must be logged, meaning that a logging system must be established to know which official consulted which data, at what time, from which person, for what purpose, and under which authorization.

FOR THESE REASONS,  

The Authority,  

issues a favorable opinion on the Royal Decree concerning the execution of the code of registration rights, mortgage, and registry and the maintenance of records in the registries of courts and tribunals, provided that the following points are integrated complementarily:  
• Point 3, it is necessary to explicitly indicate who is the data controller concerned as provided by the Royal Decree;  
• Point 7, the applicant must make a request to use the National Register number;  
• Point 9, the personal data processed must be exhaustively listed so that the Authority can assess the proportionality of the data in relation to the purposes of the processing;  
• Point 11, the Royal Decree should explicitly mention the duration for which the data is retained by the institutions involved;  
• Point 12, the data controller must inform the data subjects about the processing concerning them in accordance with Article 12 of the GDPR;  
• Point 13, the modalities for exercising the rights provided for in Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR must be established;  
• Point 14, personal data must be retained and exchanged securely;  
• Point 15, a logging system for the access of the official of the general administration of heritage documentation responsible for monitoring the registries must be established.
