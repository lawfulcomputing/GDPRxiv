I. 
SUBJECT OF THE REQUEST AND CONTEXT

1. 
The Walloon Minister of Local Authorities, Housing, and Sports Infrastructure consults the Authority for an opinion regarding a draft decree of the Walloon Government organizing the merger of the missions assigned to the Walloon Housing Company (SWL), the Walloon Social Credit Company (SWCS), and the Center for Sustainable Housing Studies (CEHD) within the Walloon Agency for Sustainable Housing (2nd reading) [the project]. This draft has been the subject of an opinion from the Council of State, opinion No. 65.477/4 of March 28, 2019 [the opinion of the Council of State].

2. 
Constituting a first step in the reform of the housing sector in Wallonia, the project organizes the "merger"/"gathering," within a new Walloon agency, the Walloon Agency for Sustainable Housing [the Agency], of three entities: the Walloon Housing Company [the SWL], a public limited company ("more than 99% in the hands of the Region," according to the rectifying note to the Government in the first reading) subject to the laws and regulations applicable to public limited companies for everything that is not expressly provided otherwise by the Walloon Code of Housing and Sustainable Habitat [the CWLHD]1; the Walloon Social Credit Company [SWCS], which is a legal entity under public law constituted in the form of a public limited company ("more than 99% in the hands of the Region," according to the same note), also subject to the laws and regulations applicable to public limited companies for everything that is not expressly provided in the CWLHD2, as well as to the specific rules applicable to mortgage credit institutions; and finally, the Center for Sustainable Housing [CEHD], a non-profit association whose eighteen members are representatives of ministers (including the Minister responsible for Housing) and various institutions (such as the SWL, the SWCS, the Higher Council of Housing, and representatives of the universities of the Wallonia-Brussels Federation).

3. 
The rectifying note to the Government in the first reading situates the undertaken reform, in summary, within the objectives of simplification, transparency, and performance of the regional policy declaration. Three objectives are more specifically expressed: that of better readability of the housing sector for the public and investors, including the desire for a more precise identification of the normative and operational actors and control in the sector; that of effective governance of this sector, including the objective of harmonizing the procedures and conditions for granting credits, resulting in a lower cost for the Region; and that of the efficiency of existing resources (human and otherwise).

II.  
EXAMINATION OF THE PROJECT

II.1. Principles of transparency and legality enshrined in Article 22 of the Constitution and Article 8 of the ECHR, and Article 27 of the project

4.  
Article 27 of the project reinstates Article 91 of the CWLHD3 and provides that “[t]he Agency is authorized to receive, collect, hold, and transmit data and information necessary for the pursuit of its missions in compliance with the applicable legal and regulatory provisions in this matter.”

5.  
Insofar as this provision concerns the processing of personal data4, the opinion of the Council of State5 clearly recalls the following: “[t]he provision under examination thus authorizes the Walloon Agency for Sustainable Housing to process personal data. Under Article 22 of the Constitution, the organization of personal data processing is subject to compliance with the principle of legality. Therefore, the legislator must itself establish the essential elements of such a system, namely the purposes of the processing as well as the cases and conditions under which personal data is processed. Interference with the exercise of the right to respect for private life must be defined in clear and sufficiently precise terms that allow for a predictable understanding of the circumstances in which the legislator authorizes such interference. Any interference with the right to respect for private life must, moreover, be based on reasonable justification and be proportionate to the objectives pursued by the legislator.”

6.  
The Authority agrees with the position expressed by the Council of State as just cited, and has already reiterated several times6 that, under the principles of transparency and legality enshrined in Articles 8 of the ECHR and 22 of the Constitution, a decree must clearly specify under what circumstances the processing of personal data is authorized7, and consequently determine what data is processed, the persons concerned, the conditions and purposes of said processing, and the duration.

II.6. Subsequent data processing (articles 5, 1., b), and 6, 4., of the GDPR) and principles of Belgian and Walloon e-government 

37.  
In practical terms, the merger of SWL, SWCS, and CEHD within the new Agency will result in the concentration at the level of a single data controller (without prejudice to the conclusions to be drawn in accordance with the above, points 30 and following), of data originally processed by SWL, SWCS, and CEHD, which are in principle three distinct data controllers. For the processing of these previously collected data, which the Agency will inherit, it will be the responsibility of the data controller to ensure, if it is contemplated to process them for the realization of a mission other than that for which they were collected, compliance with Article 6, 4., of the GDPR, and to implement, where appropriate, the necessary technical and organizational measures in accordance with Articles 24 and 32 of the GDPR (and, if necessary, separating the processing activities of the different departments of the Agency). This exercise will be carried out, where appropriate, within the framework of a data protection impact assessment (see below, point no. 41).  

38.  
The subsequent processing carried out by the CEHD based on the data collected by the Agency in the context of its various missions generally pursues a purpose of statistics and research compatible with the initial data processing, in accordance with recitals no. 50 and Articles 5, 1., b), 6, 4., and 89 of the GDPR, provided that, in this case, they are based on a legal basis compliant with the principles of transparency and legality previously recalled (see above, points 4-10 and 21-29). Conversely, this will generally not be feasible (use by the credit and real estate departments of the Agency of the data processed by the CEHD), subject, however, to the concrete realization of the analysis just mentioned (see above, point no. 37).

II.7. Data Protection Impact Assessment (Article 35 of the GDPR)  

41.  
Finally, the Authority invites the applicant to consider the necessity or not of conducting a data protection impact assessment [DPIA]. The processing of personal data related to the Agency's missions may be considered as posing a high risk to the rights and freedoms of the individuals concerned. It indeed pertains to the assessment of the economic situation of the individuals concerned (see above, point no. 14), it seems also to involve data concerning health (disability – vulnerable individuals are therefore potentially affected) (see above, point no. 11), the data will be processed on a large scale (it concerns the landscape of (social) housing and social credit in Wallonia) and it clearly falls within the implementation of the right to housing (see above, point no. 9), having in this context a determining impact on the possibility for the individuals concerned to access social credit or a lease contract.  

42.  
That being clarified, the CPVP has, however, recognized following Group 29, and the Authority agrees, that for "existing processing, a DPIA is generally required only if the risks to the rights and freedoms of individuals change after May 25, 2018, for example because a new technology is employed or because personal data is used for another purpose." It is clear in this case that the Agency's missions are already being carried out by the SWL, the SWCS, and the CEHD. However, the merger of these entities into a single Agency raises questions, as illustrated. In this context, therefore, the applicant will assess the necessity or not of conducting a data protection impact assessment.
