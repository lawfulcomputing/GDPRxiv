I. OBJECT OF THE REQUEST FOR OPINION

1. The Minister of the Middle Classes, Independents, SMEs, Agriculture, and Social Integration (hereinafter the requester) seeks the opinion of the Authority regarding amendments made to the draft law (hereinafter the draft law) establishing the Information Security Committee and amending various laws concerning the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (hereinafter the General Data Protection Regulation or GDPR).

2. The amendments aim to provide several control and inspection services in the economic and social sectors with the possibility of limiting a number of rights generally held by data subjects in the context of the protection of their personal data, in accordance with Article 23 of the GDPR.

3. The Authority has issued a first opinion, within a shortened timeframe, regarding the first part of the amendments (related to the amendment of the law of February 4, 2000, establishing the Federal Agency for the Safety of the Food Chain). This opinion addresses the amendments aimed at limiting the rights of data subjects in the context of social protection legislation.

II. EXAMINATION OF THE REQUEST FOR OPINION

2.1. General

4. Article 23 of the GDPR allows Member States to provide, within certain determined limits and for specific objectives, exceptions to the rights of data subjects. The specific objectives for which this is possible are listed in Article 23.1 of the GDPR; these include objectives of public interest of the Union or a Member State, notably a significant economic or financial interest of the Union or a Member State, including in the monetary, budgetary, and fiscal areas, public health, and social security, particularly a mission of control, inspection, or regulation related, even occasionally, to the exercise of public authority.
Notice 64/2018 - 3/13  
5. Any legislative measure providing limitations on the rights of the data subject must at least contain specific provisions relating to the elements listed in Article 23.2 of the GDPR, namely:  
- the purposes of the processing or categories of processing,  
- the categories of personal data,  
- the extent of the limitations introduced,  
- the safeguards intended to prevent abuse or unlawful access or transfer,  
- the identification of the data controller(s) (or categories of data controllers),  
- the retention periods,  
- the risks to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects, and  
- the right of the data subjects to be informed of the limitation.  

6. In order to determine the scope of the margin of assessment that the legislator has in this context, it is important to recall the case law of the Court of Justice regarding Article 13 of Directive 95/46/EC, which provided a similar exception basis. In the Smaranda Bara ruling, the Court confirmed that these exceptions could only be established by "legislative measures"1. Subsequently, the Court clarified that Member States could adopt these exceptions only to the extent that they are "necessary"2. Given the unchanged intention of the European legislator to ensure a high level of protection3, this means that exceptions to the rights of data subjects must remain within the limits of what is strictly necessary4. The necessity and proportionality of the measures concerned must therefore be interpreted restrictively.  

7. In order to meet the imperative of necessity, it is the responsibility of the legislator to demonstrate that there is no less intrusive measure than the limitation of the rights of the data subjects to achieve the intended objective. In this regard, the Authority refers to the explanatory note from its Irish counterpart regarding Article 23 of the GDPR: “The proposed measure should be supported by evidence describing the problem to be solved by the measure, and indicating why existing or less intrusive measures cannot sufficiently address the problem” (our translation of the original English: “A proposed measure should be supported by evidence describing the problem to be addressed by the measure, how it will be addressed by the measure, and why existing or less intrusive measures cannot sufficiently address it.” - Irish Data Protection Authority, “Limiting Data Subject Rights and the Application of Article 23 of the General Data Protection Regulation”, June 2018, available at the following webpage: https://www.dataprotection.ie/docs/EN/19-06-2018-Limiting-Data-Subject-Rights-and-the-Application-of-Article-23-of-the-GDPR/m/1746.htm).  

2.2. Limitations in the context of social prevention  

8. The amendments introduced in the draft law on social protection modify the Organic Law of July 8, 1976, concerning public social assistance centers, the Law of May 26, 2002, regarding the right to social integration, the Law of April 2, 1965, concerning the provision of assistance granted by public social assistance centers, and the program law of December 22, 2008. The amendments aim to limit the rights of data subjects to receive copies of personal data or to object to the processing of their personal data within the framework of the aforementioned legislations to the extent that they organize control and inspection missions related to social protection.  

 Limitation of the right to obtain a copy of personal data  

9. The proposed amendments stipulate that the obligation to provide a copy of the data is "excluded when it concerns the processing of personal data that is necessary for the social investigation for which the CPAS is responsible for processing, and this for the entire duration of the social investigation" (new Article 90 of the Organic Law of July 8, 1976, concerning public social assistance centers, amendment no. 4). The reason invoked to demonstrate the absolute necessity of this limitation is that the obligation to provide a copy to the data subjects "would harm the effectiveness of the work carried out both by the social workers of the public social action centers in processing the files and that provided by the staff of the competent federal administration; this obligation would also harm the proper conduct of the inspection by the inspection service of compliance with the regulations and the grants awarded" (justification for the new Article 90 of the Organic Law concerning the CPAS). Furthermore, it is specified that "as long as a decision has not been made by the competent federal administration or by the public social action center, the person has no interest in requesting a copy of their file, especially since the protection of the personal data of the data subject is guaranteed by their right of access to the file (at any time), by their right to transparency, information, and rectification" (justification for the new Article 90 of the Organic Law concerning the CPAS).  

10. This justification is not convincing. It is not demonstrated how providing a copy of the processed personal data would harm the proper conduct of an inspection or the effectiveness.

If the request for limitation is motivated by concerns related to the workload of the relevant services, the Authority reminds, for all useful purposes, that Directive 95/46 of October 24, 1995, on the protection of individuals with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (hereinafter referred to as Directive 95/46), as interpreted by case law, already imposed on the data controller the obligation to "provide" the data subject with a "complete overview" of the personal data "in an intelligible form, that is to say, a form that allows the requester to become aware of such data and to verify that it is accurate and processed in accordance with this directive […]" (Article 125, transposed into Belgian law at Article 10 § 1 b of the Privacy Act of December 8, 1992, in conjunction with CJEU, July 17, 2014, C-141/12 AND C-372/12, Ministry of Immigration v. M, see the ruling's dispositif). Subject to further clarification by the European Data Protection Committee (EDPC) and, where applicable, by the Courts and tribunals, it is not certain that the scope of the right to obtain a "copy" of the data introduced in Article 15 of the GDPR should be interpreted differently. The Authority further reminds that the right to a copy of personal data provided for in Article 15 of the GDPR is not without limits: the data controller may refuse to comply with a request that is manifestly unfounded or excessive (Article 12 GDPR); the data controller may also ask the data subject to specify their request when the requested data is numerous (Recital 63 GDPR). 

11. In any case, the restriction of the right to obtain a copy of personal data, as requested by the applicant, is not justified by the need to keep this data confidential during the investigation by social protection services, given that the data subject has the right to access their file at any time, as the applicant recalls in their justifications (justification of the new Article 90 of the organic law relating to CPAS, cited in full above, § 10). 

12. Furthermore, to the extent that the data subject can access their file anyway, would the absence of providing a copy not risk creating discrimination?

13. The Authority does not follow the applicant's argument regarding the Charter of the Social Insured, invoked by the applicant to demonstrate the uselessness of the right to copy, according to its opinion ("the legislation and the Charter of the Social Insured guarantee the right to be heard and to understand the decision," see for example the justification for amendment No. 4). This Charter indeed provides that "social security institutions are required to provide the social insured who requests it in writing, with all useful information regarding their rights and obligations and to communicate on their own initiative to the social insured any additional information necessary for the examination of their request or for the maintenance of their rights [...]" (Article 3 of the law of April 11, 1995, aimed at establishing the "charter" of the social insured). The existence of a procedure aimed at ensuring a certain transparency regarding the rights and obligations of the social insured does not constitute a sufficient reason to limit the rights provided by the GDPR, according to the conditions set out in Article 23 of the GDPR.  

14. The Authority further expresses all reservations regarding the argument invoked by the applicant that "the interest in receiving a copy of the data held by the competent federal authority and public social action centers is manifested when a position is taken by the public social action centers (possibility of contestation, etc.)" (justification for amendment No. 4). The Authority recalls in this regard that the right to receive a copy of the processed data is a fundamental right provided for in the GDPR without the data subject having to justify the existence of a decision based on the relevant data in order to claim it. The Authority believes that there is no need to add such a limiting condition to the right to copy provided for in Article 15 of the GDPR, according to which the interest of the data subject does not constitute a condition for the admissibility of the request for a copy. 15. The Authority further considers that there is no need to limit requests for copies of personal data to cases where an administrative decision has been made based on that data, especially in the context where such a limitation of access is not provided as such in Belgian legislation regarding administrative publicity. It should be noted that the GDPR allows Member States to enact or maintain specific legislation concerning public access to official documents, including documents containing personal data, based on the legislation provided by Union law or the law of the Member State to which the public authority or public body belongs (Article 86 in conjunction with Recital 154 GDPR).

16. Belgian legislation regarding administrative publicity provides access to administrative documents containing personal data under more restrictive conditions than those provided for access in the GDPR (see the law of April 11, 1994, regarding administrative publicity and its regional equivalents, such as the Brussels Region Ordinance of March 30, 1995, regarding administrative publicity). Without making a determination here on the compatibility of this specific legislation with the GDPR9, the Authority believes that it is up to social prevention services to rely on these provisions, if necessary, to restrict access to their administrative documents when these services act as an administrative authority. Thus, for example, a limitation is possible when access to unfinished decisions is requested or when access could violate the confidentiality of the deliberations of the authority responsible for data processing (see Article 6 § 2 al. 3 of the aforementioned law of April 11, 1994).

17. The Authority concludes that the present request for limitation of the right to copy is excessive and is not, based on the information provided by the applicant, validly founded on one of the justifications permitted by Article 23 of the GDPR, namely, the needs of a control or inspection mission related to the exercise of public authority.  Limitation of the right to object to the processing of personal data  

19. It is provided that the right to object may be limited when it comes to the processing of personal data for which the CPAS or the competent federal administration is responsible, during the period in which these services carry out their legal missions (new Article 92 of the Organic Law of July 8, 1976 regarding public social assistance centers, introduced by amendment No. 6).  

20. The first justification invoked is the necessity for the CPAS to assess the extent of the needs of the individuals concerned and the need to "demonstrate proactivity, and, based on the personal data previously collected, to offer them the most relevant assistance that was not specifically the subject of their initial request" (justification of amendment No. 6). The second justification invoked is as follows: "the fact that any person can object whenever they wish to the processing of their personal data would seriously harm the proper conduct of the social investigation and the follow-up of cases by social workers, as well as the control and inspection of compliance with regulations and the use of subsidies by the competent federal authority."  

21. Before concluding on the merits of this limitation, the Authority wishes to remind that the right to object as provided in the GDPR is not absolute: the individual concerned must justify "reasons relating to their particular situation" to oppose a data processing; the

The data controller may also continue to process the data if it "proves that there are legitimate and compelling reasons for the processing that prevail over the interests and rights and freedoms of the data subject, or for the establishment, exercise, or defense of legal rights" (Article 12 GDPR).

22. The Authority hears the arguments of the applicant and considers it justified to introduce in Belgian law a limitation on the right of opposition of the data subjects when the data in question is processed in the context of the legal missions of the data controller (CPAS or competent federal body), provided, of course, that this limitation only applies to processing carried out within the framework of its legal missions. Indeed, any processing of personal data carried out by the CPAS or the competent social protection body outside the scope of its legal missions should, in the Authority's opinion, be subject to a right of opposition from the social assistance applicant.

23. The Authority considers it desirable to better frame the justification provided by the applicant regarding their wish for social workers to be able to "demonstrate proactivity" based on the "personal data collected previously" and to propose assistance not requested by the data subject (justification of amendment no. 6, cited in full above). The Authority recommends that the legislator demonstrate in its justification that such proactive processing of data falls within the legal missions of the data controller, possibly by referring to the definition of social investigation in Article 1 of the law of December 1, 2013, which states that this investigation aims to allow the center to collect the "necessary information to arrive at an accurate diagnosis of the existence and extent of the need for assistance and to propose the most appropriate means to address it."

24. The Authority emphasizes the importance of the principle of proportionality in data processing within the framework of the social investigation, namely, the "individual investigation allowing the CPAS to collect the necessary information to arrive at an accurate diagnosis of the existence and extent of the need for assistance and to propose appropriate means to address it" (Article 2 of the circular concerning the minimum conditions of the social investigation required under the law of May 26, 2002, relating to the right to social integration and in the context of social assistance granted by the CPAS and reimbursed by the State in accordance with the provisions of the law of April 2, 1965). Thus, for example, the home visit conducted as part of the social investigation must be carried out "with respect for the privacy of the assistance applicant."

- Regarding the scope of limitations:
 this scope is not specified in relation to the needs of the missions of the concerned bodies (for example, to the extent that the exercise of rights would harm the needs for control, investigation, or preparatory acts);

- Regarding the guarantees aimed at preventing abuse or unlawful access or transmission:
 the DPO records the factual or legal grounds on which their decision is based; the Authority considers it preferable to specify that this information is made available to the competent supervisory authority, similar to other provisions limiting rights introduced in the context of the draft law (see Opinion No. 34/2018 of April 11, 2018, p. 12);

- Regarding the determination of data controllers: the CPAS or the competent federal administration regarding social assistance are designated as "data controllers" (see for example new article 92, amendment No. 6). To the extent that these entities are in fact joint controllers within the meaning of Article 26 of the GDPR, the Authority recommends specifying this in any related provision, by referencing the role of the CPAS and the competent federal administration as "joint data controllers"; the Authority also recommends specifying which competent federal administration acts as a joint data controller (for example, the Royal Decree of December 1, 2013, concerning the minimum conditions for social investigation indicates that "the Minister of Justice and the Secretary of State for Asylum and Migration, for Social Integration and the Fight against Poverty" are responsible for its execution).

- Regarding retention periods: it is provided that "without prejudice to the necessary retention for processing within the framework of judicial proceedings, personal data resulting from the derogation referred to in paragraph 1 shall not be retained longer than necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed, with a maximum retention period of 10 years, starting from the date on which the data subject no longer meets the conditions for granting social assistance, unless the law provides that a provision may only apply a limited number of times in the life of the beneficiary, such as for example, the installation grant [...]" (see for example new article 96, amendment No. 11);

- Regarding risks to the rights and freedoms of data subjects:
 the Data Protection Officer or DPO informs the data subject of the possibility of filing a complaint with the competent supervisory authority or of bringing a judicial remedy;
 the DPO records the factual or legal grounds on which the decision is based; in this regard as well, the Authority considers it preferable to specify that this information is made available to the competent supervisory authority, similar to other provisions limiting rights introduced in the context of the draft law (see Opinion No. 34/2018 of April 11, 2018, p. 12);

- Regarding the right of the data subject to be informed of the limitation:
 as soon as possible, and in any case within one month from the receipt of the request (+ 2 months in case of complex or numerous requests);
 the DPO informs the data subject in writing of any refusal or limitation of their right to object;
 UNLESS the communication risks compromising one of the legal missions of the CPAS or the competent federal administration (see for example new article 92, amendment No. 6).

26. The Authority takes note of the specific provisions that the amendments provide to frame the possibility of limiting the right of objection of data subjects.

27. The Authority concludes that the derogations to the rights proposed in the aforementioned amendments (6, 12, 18, 21, 24) meet the test of Article 23 of the GDPR, provided that the categories of personal data concerned are specified, the extent of the limitation is specified with reference to the missions of the data controller(s), it is specified that the CPAS and the competent federal administration are joint data controllers, and it is specified which competent federal administration this is. It would also be preferable to specify that when the DPO records the factual or legal grounds on which the decision is based, they keep this information available to the competent supervisory authority.
