I. OBJECT OF THE REQUEST FOR OPINION

1. The Authority received on August 8 a request for an opinion from the Federal Public Service Foreign Affairs, Foreign Trade and Development Cooperation regarding the draft law amending the law of December 21, 2013, establishing the consular code and the law of February 10, 2015, concerning the automated processing of personal data necessary for Belgian passports and travel documents.

2. This draft law aims, among other things, to adapt the Consular Code to harmonize the conditions under which the Minister of Foreign Affairs can refuse, revoke, or invalidate the identity card of Belgians registered in consular registers abroad with those under which he can perform these same administrative acts concerning the passports of these same Belgians. In doing so, it also somewhat adjusts the provisions of the same Code relating to the refusal of issuance, revocation, or invalidation of passports issued to Belgians, stateless persons, and refugees recognized in Belgium. The spirit of the draft law is to prevent a person who falls within the categories referred to in the proposed Article 39/1 from being able to travel freely abroad. The draft law also provides a legal basis for the database currently named "Passban" which centralizes the identities of individuals reported to the Minister of Foreign Affairs for refusal of issuance of an identity card or passport or for revocation or invalidation of their identity card or passport.

II. SUBSTANTIVE EXAMINATION

3. The measures provided for in the draft law have an impact on the fundamental right to privacy as well as on the freedom of movement of the individuals concerned. They must therefore pass the triple test of legality, proportionality, and legitimacy under Article 8, §2 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR) and Article 2 of Protocol No. 4 to the ECHR.

4. The current Article 39 of the Consular Code provides that the Minister of Foreign Affairs must give his formal consent to the issuance of an identity card for any person who is subject to an arrest warrant, a search warrant, a judicial measure depriving liberty, or a measure of provisional or conditional release accompanied by a prohibition on traveling abroad.
Notice 99/2018 - 3/12  
Article 5 of the draft law provides that the Minister of Foreign Affairs may henceforth refuse, withdraw, or invalidate an identity card of a Belgian abroad under certain conditions.  

5. Article 39/1, paragraph 1 of the draft aims to determine the categories of persons who must be reported by the Public Prosecutor and the police services to the Minister of Foreign Affairs, specifying that it concerns Belgians who are subject to a criminal investigation for an offense referred to in Articles 198, 199, or 199bis of the Penal Code or a judicial measure limiting freedom with a prohibition on leaving the territory, a Belgian or European or international arrest warrant, or a report for the purpose of arrest.  

6. Given that the Minister of Foreign Affairs is only competent in matters of issuing identity cards for Belgians registered in consular registers abroad, the communications referred to in the draft Article 39/1 can only concern these Belgians. It is necessary to correct the wording of this provision accordingly.  

7. The Authority also notes that the draft law expands the types of criminal investigations that will lead to the withdrawal, refusal, and invalidation of identity cards and passports, in that individuals subject to a criminal investigation for the offenses referred to in Articles 198 and 199 of the Penal Code are now included, and not only those subject to a criminal investigation for the offenses referred to in Article 199bis of the Penal Code (cf. current Article 62 3° of the Consular Code). In light of the purpose of the draft law, which is to prevent individuals presenting particular risks from traveling freely abroad, the Authority questions whether the criminal investigations referred to should not be more targeted than what the draft law currently proposes by referencing Articles 198 to 199bis of the Penal Code. It can be accepted that individuals who are subject to a criminal investigation for counterfeiting or falsification of passports, for using a falsified passport, for using a false name in a passport, or for fraudulent use of a passport present a particular risk that necessitates preventing them from using a passport during the investigation. That said, this does not seem to be the case for all offenses referred to in Articles 198 to 199bis of the Penal Code. For example, one can refer to cases of individuals who accept passports or other documents from a third party for the purpose of passport issuance fraudulently, or cases of individuals who do not comply within a set timeframe with a decision to withdraw a passport. In the absence of specific justification on this matter in the explanatory memorandum, it is up to the author of the draft law to limit the types of criminal investigations referred to those that are necessary in light of the purpose of the draft law.

8. The Authority also notes that the conditions under which the judicial data communications referred to will take place appear vague, given that the draft article 39/1 refers to the "cases provided for in the directives of the College of Public Prosecutors" and that these are not necessarily all public. In order to meet the quality criteria for laws governing the processing of personal data, it is necessary that the discretion available to the Public Prosecutor's Office in the referred communications be determined by or under the law. If the legislator fails to specify that it is in the presence of risk indicators that the individuals concerned by the referred criminal investigations will attempt to evade justice by traveling abroad that the reports in the "Passban" database must be made, it should be clarified that the directives of the College of Public Prosecutors specifying the circumstances under which these sensitive data communications will take place must have a public character (for example, by being published on a website or preferably in the Belgian Official Gazette). The explanatory memorandum regarding article 14 of the draft law refers to the circular of April 29, 2016, from the Ministers of Justice, Interior, Foreign Affairs, and Defense concerning the transmission of information to the Federal Public Service Foreign Affairs for the application of the rules regarding the refusal to issue and the withdrawal of travel documents. The latter can be consulted on the Public Prosecutor's website (https://www.om-mp.be/fr/savoir-plus/circulaires), but it is far from accessible to the citizen.  

9. Draft article 39/1, paragraph 2 provides for another data communication for the reporting of individuals in Passban in these terms: "the Belgian administrative authorities (in the Dutch version of the text, the qualifier 'competent' is added) communicate on their own initiative the identity of Belgians who evidently present a substantial risk or threat to public order or public safety." Given that this provision creates an obligation to communicate data, it is necessary to precisely determine the authorities that are accountable. First of all, it is not actually about "authorities" but rather about "bodies, services, or organizations" (see for example the terms in articles 44/11/3bis and 44/11/3ter of the police function law). The "authorities" are indeed politically responsible authorities such as administrative and judicial police authorities (public prosecutor, mayor, Minister of the Interior, etc.). According to the explanatory memorandum, this concerns the OCAM as well.

The following wording could be chosen: "In order to enable the Minister of Foreign Affairs to adopt the administrative acts referred to in Articles 39/2 to 39/3 and to carry out the data processing referred to in Chapter 7bis of the law of February 10, 2015, concerning the automated processing of personal data necessary for Belgian passports and travel documents."

11. Article 39/2, §1 mentions the cases in which the Minister of Foreign Affairs will automatically refuse to issue the identity card (namely, when the applicant is subject to a judicial measure depriving them of liberty referred to in Article 39/1 of the draft, under criminal investigation for offenses referred to in Articles 198 to 199bis of the Penal Code, or if they have provided inaccurate data regarding their nationality or identity) and §2, the cases in which they may refuse (reasoned opinion from a competent authority (or "service," cf. remark above regarding the notion of authority) that the applicant poses a substantial risk or threat to public order or public safety). As with the remark made on Article 39/1 of the draft, it is necessary to specify the competent authorities (in reality "services") referred to. For the rest, reference is made to the previous remark on the necessity of only referring to criminal investigations necessary in light of the principle of proportionality.

12. Article 39/3, paragraph 3 of the draft provides for the possibility for the FPS Foreign Affairs to request from the competent authorities (or "services") an investigation against a Belgian abroad who requests their identity card, which will also be usefully improved in the same sense. In accordance with the principle of proportionality, this type of investigation should only be required in the presence of signs of risk or substantial threat to public order or public safety. These provisions should be supplemented in this regard.

13. Article 39/4 of the draft provides for the situations in which the refusal to issue an identity card is lifted. The Authority questions the wording of paragraph 2, which states that, despite the fact that a judicial measure depriving the applicant of an identity card has ended, the

The Minister of Foreign Affairs may request an opinion from OCAM or other security services to verify whether the applicant does not still pose a manifest risk or substantial threat to public order or public security. The legitimacy and necessity of such a measure concerning individuals whose judicial detention ordered by the competent authorities has ended is questionable. A justification for the necessity of such a measure must be included in the statement of reasons unless it is omitted. In any case, in accordance with the principle of legality, it is necessary to determine in the law the criteria that should guide the Minister in his request for consultation.

14. The draft law does not provide any obligation for the Public Prosecutor's Office or the clerks of the Judiciary or even OCAM, intelligence services, or state security services to ensure the "de-listing" of the persons concerned with the competent services of the SPF Foreign Affairs for the lifting of the refusal to issue the identity card referred to in article 39/4 of the draft. This should be addressed unless it is already provided for in other legal provisions.

15. Article 6 of the draft law adapts the provisions of the Consular Code relating to the refusal to issue, withdrawal, and invalidation of passports for Belgians, stateless persons, and refugees. Insofar as these provisions mirror those relating to the identity card analyzed above, reference is made to the aforementioned remarks.

16. Article 7 of the draft law inserts a new article 65/3 into the Consular Code to allow the Minister of Foreign Affairs to issue a temporary travel document with limited territorial validity and duration in the event of a refusal to issue an identity card or passport, with the prior agreement of the competent authorities (or services). Paragraph 2 of this provision also provides for the granting of a territorially limited temporary travel document in the event of invalidation or withdrawal of an identity card. According to the statement of reasons, these temporary documents will be necessary for the repatriation of the interested parties so that they can make themselves available to the Belgian authorities or will allow the interested parties to avoid discrimination due to the invalidation of their identity card.

17. Article 9 of the draft adapts the aforementioned law of 2015 to the changes that have occurred in the competencies of the provinces; the latter are currently no longer competent in matters of passport issuance. Following this reform, the staff members of the Provinces can no longer legitimately have access to the data referred to.

18. Article 10 of the draft adds a category of staff members with a right of access to the automated processing of the SPF Foreign Affairs established for the production of

passports, namely, the "staff of the common counter of the SPF of Foreign Affairs and the Interior responsible for the issuance of passports and travel documents in super urgent procedures, individually authorized by the Ministers of Foreign Affairs or the Interior or by the personnel designated by them for this purpose." The Authority recommends that it be specified that these are the staff members whose function within this counter requires access to this data. 19. Article 14 of the draft law inserts a chapter 7bis "automated processing of personal data for the purpose of refusal to issue, withdrawal, and invalidation of identity cards, passports, and Belgian travel documents" into the aforementioned law of February 10, 2015, in order to make some adaptations to this law and to provide a legal basis for the "Passban" database, which allows for the centralization of the names of individuals reported by the authorities for the purpose of refusal, withdrawal, or invalidation of their identity card, passport, or Belgian travel document (cf. p. 32 of the explanatory memorandum of the draft law).

20. The proposed Article 27/1 designates the Minister of Foreign Affairs as the "data controller for the automated processing of personal data for the purpose of refusal, issuance, withdrawal, and invalidation of identity cards, passports, and Belgian travel documents." To meet the requirement of a determined and explicit purpose for the processing of personal data as provided in Article 5.1.b of the GDPR, the wording of this provision deserves to be improved to better describe the intended processing and its purpose, for example in these terms: "data controller of the database established, on the one hand, to record individuals registered in the Belgian consular registers reported by the authorities, bodies, services, or competent organizations for the purpose of refusal of issuance, withdrawal, or invalidation of their identity card, passport, or Belgian travel document and, on the other hand, to carry out the necessary data processing for the adoption of these administrative decisions, their lifting, the issuance of temporary travel documents, as well as the realization of statistics on the adoption of these administrative acts."

21. The proposed Article 27/2 determines the list of "personal data recorded in the automated processing referred to in Article 27/1" as well as the categories of individuals listed in the "Passban" database. The notion of "automated processing referred to in Article 27/1" will be usefully replaced by the notion of "database referred to in Article 27/1." Furthermore, the categories of data (name, first names, date of birth, national register identification number, place of residence, nationality or status of statelessness or refugee, case number, reason for reporting, date of reporting, date of withdrawal from the "Passban" database, authority that reported the individual, date of refusal of the identity card or passport, status of

file, competent prosecutor's office, date of transfer of the file to the prosecutor's office, date of response from the competent prosecutor's office) appear relevant in light of the purpose of the database.

22. Article 27/3 in draft provides for two periods of data retention in the "Passban" database.

23. Paragraph 1 of Article 27/3 in draft provides that all data will be retained until the moment the refusal to issue travel documents concerning the person is lifted; which appears proportional in light of the purpose of the database.

24. Paragraph 2 of this same provision in draft, however, provides that data relating to the reasons for the report, the date of the report, the date of removal from the "Passban" database, the identification of the authority (or service) that reported the person, the date of refusal of the identity card or passport, the status of the file, the competent prosecutor's office as well as the date of the response from the prosecutor's office will be retained for 10 years from the date on which the refusal to issue the passport or travel document is lifted concerning the person. It follows from the explanatory memorandum that this will allow for responses to parliamentary questions regarding the use of the "Passban" database. The retention of data for statistical purposes is permissible in light of the need to conduct oversight on the use of the database. It is nonetheless appropriate to specify in Article 27/3, paragraph 2 in draft, the purpose for which this data is retained for a longer period. The performance of statistical processing must comply with Article 89 of the GDPR and the principle of minimization. In order to guard against the risk of easy "re-identification" of the individuals concerned, only the reporting periods or the durations of the administrative acts in question should be retained if the statistical purpose can be achieved based solely on this information. The author of the draft bill will adapt Article 27/3 paragraph 2 accordingly. Furthermore, only staff members responsible for carrying out these statistics will have access to this data during the specified time period. For additional information, the Authority refers to the obligations imposed by Title 4 of the aforementioned law of July 30, 2018, which determines the conditions under which data processing for statistical purposes can be carried out.

Only members of the personnel of the SPF Foreign Affairs or its subcontractors who need access to the "Passban" database for the exercise of their function participating in the adoption of administrative acts of refusal to issue, withdrawal, and invalidation of identity cards and/or passports may have such access. Instead of referring to the personnel "responsible for the enforcement of regulations regarding identity cards, passports, and travel documents," it is appropriate to refer to these personnel members.

27. The description of the second category of personnel mentioned lacks clarity. According to the explanatory memorandum, it concerns the IT personnel responsible for the development and maintenance of the "Passban" database. This wording is preferable. Article 24/4 2° will be adapted accordingly.

28. Finally, it should also be specified that the personnel responsible for producing statistics on the use of the "Passban" database will also have access to the reduced data as provided for in Article 27/3.

29. In view of the categories of data contained in the "Passban" database, Article 10, §2 of the law of July 30, 2018, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data is applicable. It imposes the establishment by the Minister of Foreign Affairs and, where applicable, his subcontractor of a list of categories of persons having access to the "Passban" database with a description of their function related to their access to this database. This list must be able to be provided to the Authority upon first request. Under this same provision, the draft law will subject these personnel members to a legal obligation of confidentiality regarding the personal data they become aware of in the exercise of their function.

30. Given the high risk to the rights and freedoms of the individuals concerned that the processing referred to in Chapter 7bis of the aforementioned 2015 law may entail, it is the responsibility of the Minister of Foreign Affairs to conduct a data protection impact assessment before its implementation. If this analysis indicates that specific modalities must frame this processing, they should be included in the law.

31. For the same reasons and in view of the nature of the data processed, the "Passban" database as well as the data communications feeding it must be framed by high security measures (strong authentication, user and access rights management system, etc.).

32. Article 15 of the draft law adapts Article 28 of the aforementioned law of February 10, 2015, concerning the obligation to inform the individuals concerned about the data processing carried out in the context of passport issuance. The processing related to the use of the "Passban" database is exempt from this information obligation given, according to the explanatory memorandum, the impossibility of informing about the registration in "Passban" at the time of the passport application.

33. The Authority notes that Article 28 of the aforementioned law should be deleted in view of its inconsistency with Article 13 of the GDPR, which determines the subject of the information obligation on the part of the data controller who collects data directly from the individual concerned and does so in a more comprehensive manner. An exemption from the information obligation for the occasional data processing referred to in the future Chapter 7bis of the law (except, where applicable, for statistical data processing) should not be provided as it is already contained in the limitations on the rights of the individuals concerned provided for in Chapter III of Title I of the aforementioned law of July 30, 2018. That said, this remark is made without prejudice to the general information regarding the existence of this database and the reporting criteria that must be provided, under Article 13 of the GDPR, to individuals who request a passport or identity card from the services of the Minister of Foreign Affairs.

FOR THESE REASONS,

the Authority issues an unfavorable opinion on the draft law for the following reasons:  
1. No limitation of the measure of withdrawal, refusal, or invalidation of identity card or passport to only those individuals whose types of criminal investigation to which they are subject reveal that they present a particular risk of evading justice while traveling abroad (cons. 7);  
2. Lack of clear, transparent, and accessible precision for the citizen regarding the hypotheses and circumstances in which the authorities, bodies, services, or competent organizations must report individuals in "Passban" (cons. 8);  

According to point 23 of the guidelines on transparency under the GDPR of the "Article 29" Working Party adopted on 29/11/2018, revised on 11/04/2018, and taken up by the European Data Protection Committee, "there is no difference between the status of the information to be provided under paragraph 1 and paragraph 2 of Articles 13 and 14, respectively. Furthermore, the Authority is of the opinion that the following corrections should also be made to the draft law:  
1. Limit draft Article 39/1 to identity cards issued to Belgians registered in the consular registers (cons. 6);  
2. Clarification in draft Article 39/1 of the purpose of the obligation to communicate data (cons. 10);  
3. Framework for data communications from the competent authorities, bodies, services, or organizations with a view to lifting the measures of refusal, withdrawal, and invalidation of identity cards and passports (cons. 14);  
4. Adaptation of the provisions of the consular code modified by Article 6 of the draft law in accordance with the comments made on the provisions relating to the refusal, withdrawal, and invalidation of the identity card (cons. 15);  
5. Adaptation of the wording of Articles 10 to 13 of the draft law so that the designated staff members who have access to the data in question are only those who perform a function that requires it (cons. 18);  
6. Reformulation of draft Articles 27/1 and 27/2 for a clearer description of the data processing referred to and their purposes (cons. 20 and 21);  
7. Clarification of the purpose of processing for which the data from "Passban" will be retained for 10 years after the lifting of the refusal to issue the passport or identity card and limitation of the data retained to only those necessary for this statistical purpose (cons. 24);
