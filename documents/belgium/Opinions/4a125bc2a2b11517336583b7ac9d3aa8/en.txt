Opinion 15/2018 - 2/11
Preliminary Remark

The Commission draws attention to the fact that new European regulations regarding the protection of personal data have recently been enacted: the Regulation on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data and the Police and Justice Directive. These texts were published in the official journal of the European Union on May 4, 2016[1].

The Regulation, commonly referred to as GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation), came into effect twenty days after its publication, that is, on May 24, 2016, and is automatically applicable two years later, that is, on May 25, 2018. The Police and Justice Directive must be transposed into national legislation no later than May 6, 2018.

For the Regulation, this means that from May 24, 2016, and during the two-year implementation period, Member States have, on the one hand, a positive obligation to take all necessary implementing measures, and on the other hand, a negative obligation, known as the "duty of abstention." This latter obligation implies the prohibition of enacting national legislation that would seriously undermine the intended outcome of the Regulation. Similar principles also apply to the Directive.

It is therefore recommended to potentially anticipate these texts from now on. It is primarily the responsibility of the applicant(s) for the opinion to take this into account in their proposals or projects. In this opinion, the Commission has already ensured, as far as possible and subject to any subsequent additional viewpoints, compliance with the aforementioned negative obligation.

Notice 15/2018 - 3/11

I.  
SUBJECT OF THE REQUEST FOR OPINION  

1. The Minister of Finance requests the opinion of the Commission for the Protection of Privacy (hereinafter "Commission") on the excerpt from the draft law concerning the organization of a central contact point for bank accounts and financial contracts and extending access to the central file of seizure notices, delegation, assignment, collective debt settlement, and protest (hereinafter "the draft").  

Context  

2. The draft aims to establish a new legal framework for the central contact point for bank accounts and financial contracts (hereinafter "CCP"), which was established within the National Bank of Belgium (NBB) under the law of April 14, 2011, containing various provisions (articles 2 to 13).  

3. Initially, the use of the CCP is limited to combating tax fraud:  

•  
"When the agent designated by the minister, referred to in paragraph 2, subsection 3, has found that the investigation referred to in paragraph 2 has revealed one or more indications of tax fraud, he may request from the central contact point the available data related to this taxpayer" (Article 322§3 of the Income Tax Code 1992);  
•  
"It is useful to recall that for a long time, access to banking information has been strictly regulated for the tax authorities, notably by the joint reading of Articles 318 (investigations with the financial institutions themselves) and 322, CIR 92." (Report to the King - Royal Decree of July 17, 2013, concerning the operation of the central contact point referred to in Article 322, § 3, of the Income Tax Code 1992 (hereinafter "Royal Decree of July 17, 2013"));  
•  
"The data provided to applicants by the National Bank of Belgium may only be used either to determine the amount of the taxable income of the client or to establish the client's asset situation to ensure the recovery of taxes and withholdings due in principal and additional amounts, tax increases, administrative fines, interest, and costs" (Article 19 of the Royal Decree of July 17, 2013).  

4. However, this purely fiscal purpose has been extended by the program law of July 1, 2016, whose Article 123 states: "Article 46quater, § 1, of the Code of Criminal Procedure, inserted by the law of January 6, 2003, and amended by the law of December 27, 2005, is supplemented by a paragraph worded as follows: To allow the measures referred to in paragraph 1, the public prosecutor may, in cases of violations of Articles (...) of the law of January 11, 1993, concerning the prevention of the use of the financial system for the purposes of money laundering and the financing of terrorism, by specific and justified request, seek information from the Central Contact Point of the National Bank of Belgium referred to in Article 322, § 3, paragraph 1, of the Income Tax Code 1992."  

5. As the applicant indicates: "Due to its purely fiscal nature, the CCP currently offers very little interesting information to the institutions and individuals authorized to request information from the CCP under the program law of July 1, 2016, namely the public prosecutors, investigating judges, and courts and tribunals ruling in criminal matters, the Financial Information Processing Unit (hereinafter "CTIF"), and notaries acting in the context of inheritance declarations." The applicant therefore emphasizes "the importance of extracting the CCP from this fiscal context and providing it with a specific organic legal framework."  

6. The applicant considers that the CCP represents the ideal tool capable of assuming the role of a centralized automated mechanism that member states must establish following the proposal for a directive from the European Parliament and the Council amending Directive (EU) 2015/849 on the prevention of the use of the financial system for the purposes of money laundering or the financing of terrorism and amending Directive 2009/101/EC (hereinafter "Directive (EU) 2015/849").  

7. The main modifications introduced by the draft concern:  

•  
an expansion of the purposes for which the CCP was established;  
•  
the extension of the scope ratione personae by modifying the definition of "information debtor";  
•  
the obligation for information debtors to provide new information;  
•  
the increase in the frequency with which this information must be communicated;  
•  
a change in the modalities for transferring information and an increase in the sanctions incurred by information debtors in the event of failure to communicate data to the CCP.

II.  
SUBSTANTIVE EXAMINATION  

A. Data Controller and Subcontractors  

8. In the current form of the draft bill, it is not possible to determine whether the PCC/BNB acts as the data controller or the role of other actors. It is unacceptable for the draft bill to exempt certain actors from their responsibilities (Article 11 of the draft bill1) without determining which actor will be responsible in the event of a violation of privacy law and the GDPR. It is essential to clarify the roles and responsibilities of each of the actors involved (PCC/BNB; information obligors; information requesters).  

B. Principle of Purpose and Legitimacy  

9. The purposes of the PCC are not clearly indicated in the draft bill. Given the significant consequences of this text on privacy, it is essential to precisely indicate the purposes for which the PCC is established.  

10. The legal bases on which the processing is based must also be specified.  

11. Furthermore, as long as the royal decree designating the centralizing organizations (Explanatory Memorandum - Article 2) responsible for centralizing information requests from the PCC has not been adopted, it is not possible to provide an opinion on compliance with the principles of purpose and legitimacy. It is therefore important that the centralizing organizations are designated in the draft bill.  

12. Article 8 of the draft bill places the exclusive responsibility on those authorized to receive the information to ensure that: “anyone who submits an information request to the PCC on their behalf is identified and legitimate before they can gain access to the PCC” and that “any information request submitted on their behalf to the PCC is legitimate and justified and complies with the purpose defined by the legislator.” The same provision also applies when centralizing organizations request access to the PCC (Article 8 §2, 1° and §3). It is important that the identification of the information requester is based on robust registration procedures. Registration procedures based on identity verification using eID fully meet the aforementioned requirements. The Commission also requires that the requester take the necessary measures to log the access requests to be able to monitor the requests for access to the PCC. The PCC must regularly ensure that access to the information of the PCC is legitimate and justified.  

13. The change in the PCC framework also calls for an increased duty of transparency. Individuals whose information may now be used in the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing must be informed by the information obligors of the possibility that their data may be used for these purposes. Furthermore, the Commission requests the establishment, following the model of Article 6 §3 of the law of July 19, 19912, of the possibility for individuals whose information is recorded within the PCC to know all authorities, agencies, and persons who have, in the past six months, consulted or updated their data, except for the administrative and judicial authorities responsible for the investigation and prosecution of offenses.  

14. Article 11 of the draft bill states that: “the BNB is authorized to use the data recorded in the PCC for scientific or statistical purposes or in the context of its public interest missions and tasks carried out in accordance with the law of February 22, 1998, establishing the organic statute of the BNB.” In accordance with the principle of “accountability” contained in Article 5, 2 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (hereinafter “GDPR”), the BNB, when exercising this possibility, must justify the specific legal bases on which these processes are based as well as their purposes. It should be noted that in accordance with Article 4, § 1, 2°, of the LVP (and Article 5, 1, b GDPR), personal data may not be further processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes, taking into account all relevant factors, including the reasonable expectations of the data subject and the applicable legal and regulatory provisions. Currently, further processing for historical, statistical, or scientific purposes is not considered incompatible when carried out in accordance with the conditions set by the royal decree of February 13, 2001, implementing the law of December 8, 1992, on the protection of privacy with respect to the processing of personal data (hereinafter “royal decree of February 13, 2001”). After May 25, 2018, account must be taken of the application choices of Article 89 of the GDPR made by the legislator (framework law in preparation).

15. The Commission notes that scientific and statistical research should, in principle and preferably, be conducted using anonymous data, in accordance with Article 4, § 1, 3° of the LVP and by analogy with Articles 3 and following of the Royal Decree of February 13, 2001. When the purpose of the research cannot be achieved using anonymous data, coded personal data may be processed. It is only as a last resort and to the extent that coded personal data would also not allow for the achievement of the intended purpose that non-coded personal data may be used.  

C. Principle of Proportionality  

16. Article 4, § 1, 3°, of the LVP (5,1, c GDPR) stipulates that personal data must be adequate, relevant, and not excessive in relation to the purposes for which they are obtained and for which they are subsequently processed. In choosing the processing methods that allow for the achievement of the intended purpose, the data controller should also ensure to opt for those that are least intrusive to the privacy of the individuals concerned. Any interference with the right to data protection of the individuals concerned must indeed be proportionate to the purposes of the processing for the data controller.  

17. As a preliminary remark, the Commission recalls that in its opinion No. 36/2011 of December 21, 2011, it questioned the proportionality of the annual notification obligation of the information debtor to the PCC, in light of Articles 8 of the ECHR and 22 of the Constitution. The Commission challenged the fact that "the notification measure goes well beyond what seemed necessary, since it is supposed to apply to all accounts, including those for which neither the holder, nor the representative or beneficiary is a taxpayer under Belgian law" and recommended instead of providing for the central registration of all bank accounts within the BNB to establish within the framework of the tax declaration "the obligation to declare all accounts in Belgium, as already exists for foreign accounts. This measure indeed presented less interference in privacy than the creation of a central database." This notification obligation remains unchanged in Article 4 of the draft bill.

18. The scope ratione personae is extended by Article 3 of the draft bill, which broadens it to the following categories of establishments: "payment institutions and electronic money institutions referred to in the law of December 21, 2009, concerning the status of payment institutions, access to the activity of payment service providers, the issuance of electronic money, and access to payment systems, as well as persons established in Belgium who, professionally, carry out cash purchase or sale operations of currencies in the form of cash or checks denominated in foreign currencies or by the use of a credit or payment card, referred to in Article 102, paragraph 2, of the law of October 25, 2016, concerning access to the activity of providing investment services and the status and control of portfolio management and investment advisory companies, and bpost regarding its postal financial services or the issuance of electronic money" and to "insurance companies referred to in Article 6 of the law of March 13, 2016, concerning the status and control of insurance and reinsurance companies."  

19. The applicant justifies this extension of the scope by arguing that these institutions and persons, referred to in Article 5, § 1, 3°, 5°, 6° and 7° of the law of September 18, 2017, concerning the prevention of money laundering and the financing of terrorism and the limitation of the use of cash, indeed hold valuable information regarding transactions that involve the payment or withdrawal of cash.  

20. Article 3, 4° reserves the possibility by royal decree deliberated in the Council of Ministers and after obtaining the opinion of the CTIF and the opinion of the BNB to extend the application of the draft bill to other institutions and persons "when their activities risk being used for the purposes of money laundering or the financing of terrorism or serious crime." The applicant justifies this possibility of extension by stating that "the channels used in the context of money laundering and the financing of terrorism are constantly and rapidly evolving (...). Indeed, the European Commission has already proposed to broaden the scope of the AML directive to also include virtual currency exchange platforms (such as 'bitcoins') and providers of electronic wallets for storing these virtual currencies." In the event of an extension of the application of the draft bill to other institutions and persons, the opinion of the Privacy Commission must also be sought.  

21. Article 14, 3°, j of the draft bill indicates that must be communicated: "any other agreement or transaction whose existence is relevant for the execution of its legal missions by a person authorized to receive the information. The King determines, by royal decree deliberated in the Council of Ministers and after the opinion of the Financial Information Processing Unit and the BNB, the list of agreements and transactions concerned." The applicant justifies this indeterminacy by stating that "the channels of terrorism financing and organized crime, as well as the techniques used for money laundering and tax fraud, are constantly and rapidly evolving, it is necessary for the King to be empowered to add other categories of agreements or transactions concluded by clients with information obligors to the scope 'ratione materiae' of the law." While the Commission understands the necessity to adapt the list of agreements and transactions to respond to the evolutions of crime, it emphasizes that the adoption of a royal decree does not guarantee any more speed; these amendments could be made within the draft bill itself to increase the readability and transparency of the matter for both information obligors and the concerned persons.  

Frequency  

22. The transmission of information by information obligors will no longer occur once a year, but relevant events related to bank accounts and contracts (opening, modification of the holder or the agent, closure) will be communicated continuously so that the data recorded in the PCC is always current and relevant.  

Use of the National Register number  

23. The Commission acknowledges the authorizations held by information obligors to access the National Register number.  

24. Article 12 of the draft bill allows information obligors to access the National Register number under certain conditions. The Commission requires that the consultation of the number be done indirectly through an institution such as the non-profit association IDENTIFIN.  

D. Retention period  

25. In accordance with Article 4, § 1, 5°, of the LVP, the data may not be retained in a form allowing the identification of the concerned persons for a period exceeding that necessary for the achievement of the purposes for which they are obtained or for which they are subsequently processed.

26. Article 5 of the draft bill provides that the King shall set, upon the proposal of the competent ministers and after consultation with the BNB, the retention period for the various authorized persons to receive the information, taking into account their purposes.  

27. It is essential to specify in this law the duration for which the data may be retained within the PCC.  

E. Principle of Security  

28. The principle of securing the processing of personal data, as provided in Article 16 of the LVP (Article 5, f GDPR), imposes on the data controller the obligation to take adequate technical and organizational measures to protect the personal data it processes and to guard against misuse of purpose. The adequacy of these security measures depends, on the one hand, on the state of the technology and the costs incurred, and on the other hand, on the nature of the data to be protected and the potential risks.  

29. Article 8 places the responsibility on the authorized persons to receive the information to ensure that: "all requests for information made in its name to the PCC can be traced." The same applies to centralizing organizations (Article 8 §2, 3° and §3, 2°). The PCC must implement a precise login of accesses so that, in the event of the concerned persons exercising their right of access, an adequate response (Who requested access to what and why?) is provided to them.  

FOR THESE REASONS,  

The Commission  

The Commission issues an unfavorable opinion on the extract of the draft law concerning the organization of a central contact point for bank accounts and financial contracts and concerning the extension of access to the central file of seizure notices, delegation, assignment, collective debt settlement, and protest, taking into account the following remarks:  

• specify the roles and responsibilities of each of the actors involved (point 8)  
• clearly indicate the purposes for which the PCC is established (point 9)  
• clarify the legal bases on which the processing is based (point 10)  
• list the centralizing organizations in the draft bill (point 11)
