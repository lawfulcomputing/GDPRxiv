I. OBJECT OF THE REQUEST FOR OPINION

1. The Minister of Social Affairs and Public Health (hereinafter referred to as "the requester") submits for the opinion of the Data Protection Authority (hereinafter referred to as "the Authority") a draft Royal Decree defining the rules and content regarding the registration to be carried out by ambulance services and their annual report.

2. The draft is part of the implementation of the law of July 8, 1964, concerning emergency medical assistance, particularly its Articles 1 and 10ter, which respectively provide that:

"Article 1. - The purpose of this law is the organization of emergency medical assistance. Emergency medical assistance is understood to mean the immediate provision of appropriate aid to all persons whose health condition, due to an accident or sudden illness or the sudden complication of an illness, requires urgent intervention following a call to the unified call system through which assistance, transport, and reception in an appropriate hospital service are ensured. The King determines the operating and management modalities of emergency medical assistance. He ensures that all stakeholders comply with the objectives of this law."

"Article 10ter. - The actors of emergency medical assistance referred to in Articles 4, 4bis, 5, 6, and 6bis, as well as the centers of the unified call system and the emergency medical assistance and health vigilance dispatching unit referred to in Article 207 of the program law of July 9, 2004, are obliged to keep a record of their service activities, in accordance with the provisions of Articles 5, paragraph 1, e), and 7, § 2, d), of the law of December 8, 1992, concerning the protection of privacy with respect to the processing of personal data, and to produce an annual report.

This record aims to improve the functioning of emergency medical assistance, to conduct studies, to implement administrative simplification and automation projects, and to enable the Emergency Medical Assistance Commissions referred to in the Royal Decree of August 10, 1998, establishing the Emergency Medical Assistance Commissions, and the National Council for Emergency Medical Assistance referred to in the Royal Decree of July 5, 1994, creating a National Council for Emergency Medical Assistance to fulfill their missions.

The King determines the modalities and content of this record and the annual report, after consulting the Sectoral Committee for Social Security and Health, Health Section, established within the Privacy Protection Commission.

The commercialization of the data from the record is prohibited."

3. Currently, the ambulance team fills out a paper trip form in triplicate during and after the journey, which includes data specific to the trip as well as data related to the patient (the copies are intended respectively for the ambulance service, the hospital, and, without mentioning the patient's identification data, for the SPF Public Health).

4. The draft Royal Decree aims to regulate the automated recording system for this data. This system, called "AMBUREG," allows ambulance services to record for each trip the data related to the trip, patient identification, and health status. The data is then made available to the hospital to which the patient is transported/hospitalized, and a selection of data is communicated to the SPF Public Health, INAMI, and FAMU (Emergency Medical Assistance Fund).

5. The Sectoral Committee for Social Security and Health authorized the processing referred to in the draft in its deliberation No. 15/088 of December 15, 2015. The Authority notes that this authorization was granted under the auspices of the law of December 8, 1992, concerning the protection of privacy with respect to the processing of personal data. As of the date of the Authority's referral, Regulation (EU) 2016/679 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL of April 27, 2016, concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (hereinafter referred to as "GDPR") has entered into force and is applicable.

6. The requester specifies that the draft has been submitted for opinion to the data protection officer of the SPF Public Health.

B. Analysis of the proposed provisions  

1. Objectives and principle of legitimacy  

8.  
As previously indicated, the draft AR aims to execute the law of July 8, 1964, particularly its articles 1 and 10ter. Article 22 of the draft states that "AMBUREG aims to improve the functioning of emergency medical assistance, notably by:  
1° providing the hospital with journey data, personal and medical data of the patient in the context of the patient's transfer;  
2° evaluating the functioning of the ambulance service, particularly in the area of providing adequate and immediate care to patients or accident victims;  
3° evaluating the performance of the various links in the emergency medical assistance chain, including regulation in the 112 centers;  
4° preparing a schedule of on-call services based on the geographical dispersion of ambulance interventions and response times;  
5° supporting a policy of subsidies for ambulance services;  
6° facilitating the missions of the Emergency Medical Assistance Fund."  

9.  
The Authority notes that these objectives comply with the requirements of article 10ter of the law of July 8, 1964, in that AMBUREG is a recording system aimed at improving the functioning of emergency medical assistance, conducting studies, implementing projects for administrative simplification, and automating the processing of the data thus recorded.  

10.  
The Authority also notes in article 24 of the draft that the recorded data, without distinction, and subject to anonymization, may be used by third parties for statistical and scientific studies.  

11.  
Article 18 of the draft provides that "at the request of a health professional from the emergency service of the General Directorate of Health of the Federal Public Service Public Health, (...) the legal representative of the ambulance service shall provide a printed copy of the data from an ambulance intervention available in the database of records." The Authority infers that this is a request within the framework of the control missions assigned to the Federal Public Service Public Health in the application of the law of July 8, 1964. Article 10bis of this law indeed provides that: "§ 1. Without prejudice to the competence of judicial police officers, hygiene inspectors from the Ministry of Social Affairs, Public Health and the Environment exercise supervision of the application of the provisions of this law and its implementing decrees. For the exercise of this supervision, hygiene inspectors have access at all times to hospitals, vehicles of emergency mobile services, emergency medical assistance call centers, ambulance services and their vehicles, as well as training centers for paramedics. They may request all necessary information for the exercise of the supervision referred to in paragraph 1, and be provided with all documents or electronic media they need in the context of their control mission. (...)".  

12.  
It invites the applicant to clarify this, both in relation to the draft article 18 and in the broader context of the implementing decree, in the same way that the data may thus be communicated upon request. Furthermore, the Authority reminds the applicant that it must apply the law of May 5, 2014, guaranteeing the principle of single data collection in the functioning of services and bodies that fall under or carry out certain missions for the authority and concerning the simplification and harmonization of electronic and paper forms, known as the "only once" law.  

13.  
Subject to the clarification made in point 12, the Authority finds that the objectives referred to in the draft are explicit, legitimate, and determined, in accordance with article 5, § 1, b) of the GDPR.  

2. Designation of the person(s) responsible for processing and subcontractors  

14.  
Article 5 of the draft provides that "The minister responsible for Public Health is the data controller for the processing of personal data within the meaning of article 4 of the GDPR, concerning the objectives and means set out in this decree. By delegation, the Director General of the General Directorate of Health Care of the Federal Public Service, Public Health, Food Chain Security, and Environment is the data controller."  

15.  
Article 6 in the draft provides that "The ambulance service is a subcontractor for the benefit of the data controller referred to in article 5 concerning the purpose and means set out in this decree."  

16.  
Finally, article 7 in the draft specifies that "the service provider non-profit organization [SMALS] is a subcontractor for the benefit of the data controller referred to in article 5."  

17.  
First of all, the Authority draws attention to the fact that in this case, it is not appropriate to designate a natural person as the data controller. Indeed, it will be a

authority and/or an organization exercising a legal mission, whose obligations as data controller will not be concretely assumed by a specific natural person. The Authority invites the applicant to prioritize, where applicable, the designation of a service responsible for the matters covered by the AR.  

18.  
The Authority reminds that the designation of the data controller is of great importance for the compliance with the obligations imposed on the data controller and for the exercise of rights by the data subject. Article 4, § 1, 7) of the GDPR defines the data controller as "the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing; where the purposes and means of such processing are determined by Union law or the law of a Member State, the data controller may be designated or the specific criteria for its designation may be provided for by Union law or the law of a Member State." It defines the processor as "the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body which processes personal data on behalf of the data controller" (Article 4, § 1, 8) of the GDPR).  

19.  
It may also happen, in certain situations, that actors involved in a series of processing operations may be considered joint controllers of the processing, in accordance with what is provided for in Article 26 of the GDPR.  

20.  
Article 10ter of the law of July 8, 1964 identifies as actors in emergency medical assistance, including those responsible for carrying out the registrations referred to by the law, the intervening physicians, licensed ambulance services, the intervention team, and hospitals. The applicant, in charge of the SPF Public Health, is responsible, under Article 1 of this same law, for ensuring that all actors comply with the law of July 8, 1964.  

21.  
Upon reading Article 10ter, ambulance services thus have a legal mission to collect and record the data necessary for the management of emergency medical assistance, and the applicant has the mission to monitor compliance with these legal obligations and to ensure the proper management of the emergency medical assistance service. Registrations had already taken place previously via paper forms. AMBUREG aims to automate them. In this way, it allows each actor referred to by the law to fully carry out their missions and to ensure the proper management of emergency medical assistance in a coordinated and effective manner.

In this eventuality, more than ever, it is important that clear information is provided to data subjects, specifying the different stages and actors of the processing. 3. The processed data  

3.1.  
The data concerned  

28.  
Article 4 of the draft states that "the Ambureg registration concerns the RegisterPartA data, the RegisterPartB data, and additional data related to an ambulance intervention that comes from the unified call system and can be accessed by the ambulance service. The Ambureg registration includes both mandatory and non-mandatory variables."  

29.  
The RegisterPartA data is defined in Article 1, paragraph 10 of the draft as "the parameter values of the set of variables relating to journey data, personal data, and medical data, provided in the annex of indication Y (1) or Y (1,M), including completed identity data." The RegisterPartB data is defined as "the parameter values of the set of variables such as journey assessment, provided in the annex of indication Y(2) or Y(2,M), including completed identity data."  

30.  
First of all, the Authority notes that the applicant differentiates between two types of personal data, namely medical data and other data. While it should be recalled that "medical" data are also personal data, the Authority believes it understands that the data referred to as "personal" in the draft text should probably pertain to the identification data of the person receiving urgent medical assistance. The Authority therefore invites the applicant to correct this point.  

31.  
Regarding "medical" data, the Authority draws the applicant's attention to the fact that Recital 35 of the GDPR broadly defines health-related data: "Personal data concerning health should include all data related to the health status of a data subject that reveal information about the past, present, or future physical or mental health of the data subject. This includes information about the natural person collected when that natural person registers to receive healthcare services or during the provision of those services within the meaning of Directive 2011/24/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council for the benefit of that natural person; a number, symbol, or specific element assigned to a natural person to uniquely identify them for health purposes; information obtained during the testing or examination of a body part or bodily substance, including from genetic data and biological samples; and any information concerning, for example, a disease, disability, health risk, medical history, clinical treatment, or physiological or biomedical condition of the data subject, regardless of its source, whether it comes from a doctor or another healthcare professional, a hospital, a medical device, or an in vitro diagnostic test."  

32.  
In light of the data processing referred to by the registration in the Ambureg system and the purposes pursued, the Authority advises the applicant not to maintain the distinction as made between personal data and medical data. Except for data strictly related to the usual identification of the person, and not related to the monitoring of their care within the framework of urgent medical assistance, any personal data processed in connection with a purpose directly or indirectly related to a person's health status should be considered health data.  

33.  
The Authority has been able to review the data that will be processed, as formulated in the annexes. While it is understandable that not all variables can be included in the text of the royal decree, it nevertheless invites the applicant to specify the personal data that will be processed or, at the very least, to further clarify the categories or types of personal data.

3.2.  
Anonymization and Pseudonymization  

34.  
Article 23 of the draft provides that “§ 1. The non-profit organization providing services shall transmit daily the anonymized data from the database of records to the General Directorate of Health Care of the Federal Public Service Public Health, Food Chain Safety and Environment. Anonymization is carried out as follows:  

a) all patient identity data is removed, including the patient's address and billing address;  
b) the street name and house number of the intervention address are removed;  
c) only the country code of intervention addresses abroad is retained;  
d) intervention addresses in the form of additional information are removed.  

§ 2. The data referred to in the first paragraph is stored in a secure database and retained for a maximum period of 30 years from the date of receipt, and processed by the Federal Public Service of Public Health, Food Chain Safety and Environment under the supervision of a health professional referred to in Article 18.  

§ 3. The anonymized data referred to in § 1 are the variables marked with a Y in the column “FPS SPSCAE” of the annex.”  

35.  
It appears from reading Article 24 of the draft that the applicant wishes to make anonymized data available to third parties for research purposes, for scientific and statistical studies.  

36.  
Data can only be considered anonymous on the condition that they cannot in any way allow for the direct or indirect identification of the individual concerned. The removal of the information mentioned above does not guarantee anonymization.  

37.  
Recital 26 of the GDPR specifies that “to determine whether a natural person is identifiable, all means reasonably likely to be used by the data controller or by any other person to identify the natural person directly or indirectly, such as targeting, should be taken into account. To establish whether means are reasonably likely to be used to identify a natural person, all objective factors should be considered, such as the cost of identification and the time required for it, taking into account the technologies available at the time of processing and their evolution. Therefore, the principles relating to the protection of data do not apply to anonymous information, namely information that does not concern an identified or identifiable natural person, nor to personal data rendered anonymous in such a way that the individual concerned is not or no longer identifiable.”  

38.  
Thus, if the data is truly rendered anonymous, the Authority emphasizes that the GDPR does not require that this information, even “of medical origin,” be processed under the supervision of a health professional, this requirement being limited to the processing of personal data related to health for the purposes of preventive medicine or occupational medicine, assessment of the worker's capacity to work, medical diagnoses, health or social care, or management of health care or social protection systems and services based on Union law, the law of a Member State, or under a contract concluded with a health professional.  

39.  
The Authority therefore invites the applicant to strictly limit the requirement for anonymization by indicating that any data allowing for the identification of a person must be removed.  

3.3.  
Patient Registration Bracelet  

40.  
Article 21 of the draft provides that “from a date set by the Minister responsible for Public Health, the ambulance service shall place a registration bracelet on a patient at each contact with a patient according to the guidelines set by the minister and shall record the unique number mentioned on the registration bracelet, both during registration and during deferred registration.”  

41.  
The applicant was able to indicate additionally that the use of the bracelet was made necessary to ensure the identification of certain patients who do not have identification documents on them, to avoid any risk of error between patients, and to ensure better follow-up from the moment the patient arrives at the hospital.  

42.  
The Authority welcomes the initiative but invites the applicant to clarify and frame the use of the bracelet and the identification number thus assigned to the patient.

4. Proportionality  

4.1. Principle of Minimization  

43.  
In general, considering the large number of data and variables involved in the project, the Authority reminds that any data processing is subject to the principle of minimization, which implies that the data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed.  

44.  
Each of the actors involved in the chain of processing operations may only process the data necessary for the accomplishment of their mission.  

45.  
The Authority therefore invites the applicant to specify which data and for what purpose(s) the ambulance services and hospitals may consult in the database of records, as provided respectively by Article 13 of the project for the former and Article 22 of the project for the latter.  

46.  
Article 25 of the project states that "§ 1. The service provider association [SMALS] transmits daily the personal data and journey data from the database of records to the Emergency Medical Aid Fund for the purpose of automating the payment of the intervention of the Emergency Medical Aid Fund. (…)".  

47.  
The Authority notes that the FAMU has a legal mission to intervene in the payment of costs resulting from a physician's intervention within the framework of emergency medical aid only if the recipient of care has not fulfilled their payment obligation. Article 8 of the law of July 8, 1964, indeed states that "the Fund is only obliged to do so if, after being informed by the physician of the amount of the costs, the recipient of care has not fulfilled their obligation within a period set by the King."  

48.  
Article 25 of the project as currently drafted does not comply with the principle of data minimization and therefore with the GDPR. While the FAMU is indeed legitimate in receiving and processing data related to individuals who have benefited from emergency medical aid, it is only for those individuals who have not paid within a determined period (at most 6 months). Therefore, it cannot be a matter of sending it daily all the data passing through AMBUREG.  

49.  
When questioned on this point, the applicant clarified that it could not be predicted in advance, at the time of a medical transfer, that invoices would remain unpaid and that the communication of data in this manner was carried out without the intervention of an employee and did not concern medical data, only identification data. He added that the solution of transferring only the intervention data related to interventions for which a reimbursement request is made would be more complex and prone to error due to the numerous adjustments required in the FAMU application software and that of SMALS.  

50.  
These clarifications are not convincing insofar as neither the complexity nor the technical adjustments mentioned can justify that processing becomes disproportionate. They also do not reassure regarding the measures implemented or to be implemented to ensure data security. The Authority refers in this regard to points 62 to 68 below.  

4.2.  
Retention Period  

51.  
The principle of limitation of the retention period established by Article 5, § 1, e) of the GDPR states that data may "be kept in a form which permits identification of the data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the data are processed; personal data may be stored for longer periods insofar as they will be processed solely for archiving purposes in the public interest, for scientific or historical research, or for statistical purposes in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1, provided that the appropriate technical and organizational measures required by this Regulation are implemented to safeguard the rights and freedoms of the data subject."  

52.  
The project does not provide for any clear retention period. Articles 13 and 22 mentioned above state the possibility for ambulance services and hospitals to consult the data "up to 10 years after the request of the designated person."  

53.  
The Authority draws the applicant's attention to the fact that the consultation period is not a retention period.  

54.  
Every data controller must provide, and be able to inform the data subjects about, the retention period of the data or, at the very least, provide the criteria used to determine this duration. This also stems from the requirements for data protection by default, as provided for in Article 25 of the GDPR.  

55.  
The project as currently drafted does not comply with the provisions of the GDPR in this regard.

5. Exercise of the rights of data subjects  

56.  
Article 12, § 3 of the draft states that "the correction of data is possible up to 30 days at the latest after the moment of the request by the data subject." The Authority reminds that Article 16 of the GDPR provides that "the data subject has the right to obtain from the controller, without undue delay, the rectification of inaccurate personal data concerning him or her. Taking into account the purposes of the processing, the data subject has the right to obtain the completion of incomplete personal data, including by providing a supplementary statement." The specification "without undue delay" means that the data subject can expect a response from the controller within a reasonable timeframe and, at the very least, as provided for by Article 12 of the GDPR, particularly its paragraph 3. This does not mean in any way that the controller can limit the rectification period in any way. In doing so, the Authority invites the applicant to clarify this point in its draft in order not to be in breach of the GDPR.  

57.  
Article 19 of the draft provides that "in the context of the right of access referred to in Article 15 of the GDPR, the ambulance service provider shall provide, at the request of the patient or at the request of the Director General referred to in Article 5, paragraph 2, to the patient or his or her legal representative a printout of the personal data and other data from the ambulance intervention during which there was contact with the patient, as they are available in the database of records."  

58.  
The Authority reminds that the right of access referred to in Article 15 of the GDPR is a right granted to the data subject. It cannot in any case be a matter for a Director General to exercise this right. If said Director wishes to access certain personal data in the context of the execution of the law of July 8, 1964, this must be specified and justified legitimately in the body of the draft. As it stands, Article 19 is not compliant with the GDPR.  

59.  
Article 20 of the draft stipulates that "in order to ensure that the exercise of the rights referred to in Articles 12 to 22 and Article 34 of the [GDPR] does not in any way hinder the achievement of the objective referred to in Article 2, the Minister responsible for public health may limit the exercise of these rights, with the exception of the right of access, during the first ten years following the entry into force of this decree."

The Authority understands that the draft thus refers to Article 23 of the GDPR, which allows, under certain conditions, to limit certain principles and rights provided therein. In addition to the fact that such a limitation can only be accepted by law in the strict sense, Article 23 only permits it if it "respects the essence of fundamental freedoms and rights and constitutes a necessary and proportionate measure in a democratic society to guarantee:  

a) national security;  
b) national defense;  
c) public security;  
d) the prevention and detection of criminal offenses, as well as investigations and prosecutions in this regard or the enforcement of criminal sanctions, including protection against threats to public security and the prevention of such threats;  
e) other important objectives of general public interest of the Union or a Member State, including a significant economic or financial interest of the Union or a Member State, including in the monetary, budgetary, and fiscal areas, public health, and social security;  
f) the protection of the independence of the judiciary and judicial proceedings;  
g) the prevention and detection of breaches of professional ethics in regulated professions, as well as investigations and prosecutions in this regard;  
h) a mission of control, inspection, or regulation related, even occasionally, to the exercise of public authority, in the cases referred to in points a) to e) and g);  
i) the protection of the data subject or the rights and freedoms of others;

61.  
In light of the above, the limitation provided for in Article 20 of the draft is in violation of Article 23 of the GDPR.

6. Data Security and Impact Analysis  

62.  
Article 5, f) of the GDPR states that data must be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security, "including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures." This is the principle of integrity and confidentiality.  

63.  
Article 24, § 1 and § 2 of the GDPR provides that "1. Taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing as well as the risks, varying in probability and severity, to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is performed in accordance with this regulation. These measures are reviewed and updated as necessary.  

2. Where proportionate to the processing activities, the measures referred to in paragraph 1 include the implementation of appropriate data protection policies by the data controller."  

64.  
Article 25, § 1 and § 2 of the GDPR states the following:  

"1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing as well as the risks, varying in probability and severity, that the processing poses to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller shall implement, both at the time of determining the means of processing and at the time of the processing itself, appropriate technical and organizational measures, such as pseudonymization, intended to implement the principles related to data protection, for example, data minimization, effectively and to attach the necessary safeguards to ensure that the requirements of this regulation are met and to protect the rights of the data subject.  

2. The data controller shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that, by default, only personal data that are necessary for each specific purpose of the processing are processed. This applies to the amount of personal data collected, the extent of their processing, the duration of their storage, and their accessibility. In particular, these measures ensure that, by default, personal data are not made accessible to an indefinite number of natural persons without the intervention of the data subject."  

Notice 67-2018- 17/19  
65.  
Given the sensitive nature and the number of data processed, the number of operators involved, and the systematization of the intended processing, the Authority invites the applicant to be particularly attentive to compliance with the aforementioned provisions and to ensure that appropriate means are put in place by each data controller and/or processor to guarantee data security, as provided for in Article 32 of the GDPR, which states that:  

"1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing as well as the risks, varying in probability and severity, to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, including, among other things, as necessary:  

a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data;  
b) the means to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) the means to restore the availability and access to personal data in a timely manner in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a procedure for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  

2. In assessing the appropriate level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing, resulting from the destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or unauthorized access to such data, whether accidental or unlawful."  

66.
