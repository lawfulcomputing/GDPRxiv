I. OBJECT OF THE REQUEST AND CONTEXT

1. The draft ordinance regarding the electronic data exchange platform for health data [the draft], from the Joint College of the Common Community Commission of Brussels-Capital [COCOM], submitted for opinion to the Authority, is situated within the context of the electronic exchange of health-related data among actors in this field, in other words: eHealth. This field is not new, and the former Commission for the Protection of Privacy [CPVP] has already had the opportunity to express itself on this matter.

2. Currently, the Brussels Health Network, "a network for sharing computerized medical data, which connects all Brussels and Belgian hospitals with extrahospital care providers, and allows for the cataloging of certain patient data (test results, medical reports, letters, etc.)," has as its "main mission [...] to strengthen communication among care providers, with a constant concern for improving the quality of care provided to patients."

3. It is the result of an "initiative by care providers united within the Brussels Association of Medical Telematics (non-profit organization Abrumet). This project was born from the desire for collaboration between hospitals and general medicine circles in the Brussels region (the Federation of Associations of Brussels General Practitioners and the Brusselse Huisartsen Kring), which are the founding members of the non-profit organization Abrumet. All Brussels hospitals are represented, across all sectors (public/private/university)."

4. Currently, the Brussels Health Network is a "hub" of the federal eHealth platform. It thus enables the decentralized exchange of patient-related data among care providers, notably by managing, at its level, a directory of references.

19.

II.3. Data Retention Period

36. Data must be retained for a period not exceeding that necessary for the purposes for which it is processed, in accordance with Article 5, § 1, e), of the GDPR. The author of the project must at least determine the essential elements of the data retention period27, in order to comply with the principles of predictability and legality mentioned earlier (see above, points 7-9).

37. However, the project is silent on this matter. It is therefore not compliant with the principles just recalled.

II.4. Lawfulness of Processing and Data Recipients

38. Article 6 of the GDPR exhaustively determines the circumstances under which the processing of personal data is lawful, and Article 9 of the GDPR equally exhaustively determines the circumstances under which the processing of special categories of personal data (including "data concerning health") is not prohibited. There is no doubt that health-related data, in particular, will be processed within the framework of the platform's activities28.

39. Consent. Article 7, § 4, of the project states that "the processing of personal data by the platform is based on the consent of the data subject." This very broad provision is unclear regarding the scope of consent. It is even less clear when related to the allocation of responsibilities (see below, points 52 and following) provided by the other paragraphs of Article 7 of the project, and the use of the term processing "by the platform": which processing is conditioned on the consent of the data subject, only those for which the platform is responsible, or also those for which it is intended to be a subcontractor under the draft ordinance? Given the framework of the project, the first solution would make little sense: thus, only the use of the national register number would be subject to the consent of the data subject. This is not compliant with the rules governing the use of this number. The explanatory memorandum of Article 7 does not clarify this point.

II.5. Roles and Responsibilities

52.  
Reminder of the principles. The objective of the broad definition of the concept of data controller is to ensure effective and comprehensive protection of the data subjects. According to the facts, joint processing responsibility may bind several actors, allowing the data subject to exercise their rights against each of them.

53.  
That being said, "the existence of joint responsibility does not necessarily translate into equivalent responsibility [... and, on the contrary, operators may be involved at different stages of this processing and to varying degrees, such that the level of responsibility of each of them must be assessed taking into account all relevant circumstances of the case." It is within "the framework of its responsibilities, competencies, and possibilities" that the co-controller will ensure the compliance of its activity with data protection rules.

III.  
CONCLUSION  

67.  
As it stands, within the limits of the analysis carried out by the Authority (which is not exhaustive: see above, points 34 and 55), the draft submitted for opinion is not precise enough and in such a way that it violates the combined articles 22 of the Constitution and 8 of the ECHR. The following elements are imprecise and require amending and deepening the draft: the purposes of the processing (see above, points 18-24); the data processed (see above, points 25-32); the duration of data retention (see above, points 36-37); and the recipients of the data (see above, points 48-51).  

68.  
The draft must also be significantly clarified regarding the basis it intends to provide for the processing of personal data that it allows/establishes and the scope it recognizes for consent in this context (see above, points 39-44).  

69.  
It also disregards the GDPR in the way it defines responsibilities with respect, at least, to certain processing activities established (purpose Article 4) (see above, points 52-66). Article 7 of the draft must be revised taking into account the applicable principles in this matter.  

70.  
Finally, the draft must be more clearly articulated with the regulation on the sharing of health data of the eHealth platform, the order of May 8, 2014 establishing and organizing a regional service integrator, and the GDPR (see above, points 10-17, points 39-44, and points 52-66).
