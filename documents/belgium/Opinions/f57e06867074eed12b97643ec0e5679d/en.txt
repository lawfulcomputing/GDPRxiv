Opinion 62/2018 - 2/14
I.
SUBJECT AND CONTEXT OF THE DRAFT

1.
The Minister of Security and the Interior (hereinafter the "applicant") requests the Authority's opinion on a draft law amending the law of October 2, 2017 regulating private and particular security regarding the processing of personal data (hereinafter the "draft").

2.
The draft aims to introduce limitations to the rights of the data subject concerning the processing of personal data carried out by the General Directorate of Security and Prevention of the Federal Public Service (FPS) Interior in the context of its legal missions of control and monitoring of this law.

3.
The draft is based on the possibility offered to Member States by Article 23 of the GDPR to provide for limitations to the rights of data subjects under certain conditions.

4.
The draft provides for the insertion of a subsection 4bis entitled "Limitations on the rights of the data subject when processing personal data" in the first section "Final provisions" of Chapter 8 "Final, repealing, and transitional provisions" of the law of October 2, 2017, after subsection 4 "Access to data" which provides access to the Central Criminal Record of persons responsible for ensuring the correct application of the law, designated by a deliberative order in the Council of Ministers after the opinion of the Commission for the Protection of Privacy.

5.
The Authority will analyze whether the conditions of Article 23 of the GDPR are met.

In its opinion No. 33/2018 of April 11, 2018, the Commission for the Protection of Privacy stated that "a proper implementation of Article 23 of the GDPR requires an adaptation of the applicable sectoral legislation so that exceptions are tailored to the needs, tasks, and missions of the services concerned, without excessively limiting the rights of the data subject" and that "the philosophy of Article 23 of the GDPR, read in conjunction with Article 22 of the Constitution, requires that these exceptions to the rights of data subjects be established by formal law."

7.
The Authority acknowledges the applicant's intention to introduce limitations to the rights of the data subject concerning the private and particular security sector in the law of October 2, 2017.

II.2. General Condition of Legality

8.
The draft Article 269/1, § 1, first paragraph of the law of October 2, 2017 states the following: "By way of derogation from Articles 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, and 21 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and provided that Article 14, § 5, b), c) or d) cannot be invoked in the specific case, the rights may be delayed, limited, or excluded regarding the processing of personal data carried out by the General Directorate of Security and Prevention of the FPS Interior in its capacity as a public authority responsible for missions of general interest in the field of control, inspection, or regulation that is linked, even occasionally, to the exercise of public authority, in the cases referred to in points c), d), and g) of Article 23.1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679."

9.
The explanatory memorandum explains that: "Subject to the consideration of certain conditions, Article 23 of the Regulation provides grounds for justification for a legal limitation of the rights provided for in Articles 12 to 22 and 34 (information, rectification, access, etc.) when the limitation is an indispensable and proportionate measure in a democratic society for the preservation of missions in the field of control, inspection, or regulation that is linked, even if occasionally, to the exercise of public authority in the cases referred to in points a to e) and point g) of the Regulation. In this case, reliance can be placed on three of the mentioned grounds for justification (...)."

10. Article 23.1 of the GDPR exhaustively lists a series of legitimacy bases that can justify limitations on the rights of the data subject.

11. The Authority notes that the applicant is relying in this case on point h) of Article 23.1, namely "a mission of control, inspection, or regulation related, even occasionally, to the exercise of public authority, in the cases referred to in points a) to e) and g)". The cases referred to are, in this instance, the preservation of public safety (point c), the prevention and detection of criminal offenses, as well as investigations and prosecutions in this regard or the enforcement of criminal sanctions, including protection against threats to public safety and the prevention of such threats (point d) and the prevention and detection of breaches of the ethics of regulated professions, as well as investigations and prosecutions in this regard (point g). 

12. The Authority recognizes that the General Directorate for Security and Prevention of the SPF Interior is responsible for monitoring the application of the law of October 2, 2017, granting authorizations, conducting security investigations, and imposing administrative sanctions. It is of the opinion that these missions fall within the missions as referred to in point h of Article 23.1 of the GDPR. 

13. Specifically, it emerges from the explanatory memorandum that the objective of the draft bill is to prevent an interested party from being informed of the existence of a file about them, which could endanger or disrupt a control, investigation, or preparatory actions. "It goes without saying that it is necessary to avoid a person who is the subject of, for example, an investigation from being able to impede, delay, or obstruct this investigation by invoking certain rights provided for in the GDPR (e.g., right to restriction of processing, right to be forgotten, etc.)." 

14. The Authority invites the applicant to formulate this objective in the text of the draft bill as it generally materializes the necessary and proportionate nature of the limitations.

II.3.1. Purposes of processing or categories of processing  

16. Paragraph 2 of the new Article 269/1, § 1 of the law of October 2, 2017 states that:  
“The processing referred to in paragraph 1 is that carried out:  
1° for the exercise of the missions listed in Articles 65 to 75 and/or for the processing of reports referred to in Articles 49, 54, and 205;  
2° in the context of the exercise of missions related to administrative procedures concerning the granting, renewal, refusal, suspension, withdrawal of authorizations, rights of a person to carry out activities as referred to in this law, permits, and identification cards provided for by this law;  
3° for the exercise of the missions listed in Articles 208 to 233 of this law;  
4° for the exercise of the missions listed in Articles 234 to 255 of this law.”  

17. The explanatory memorandum mentions in this regard that:  
“The objective of the processing in question or of the categories of processing must indeed be focused on the realization of their legal missions by the services responsible for control, inspection, or regulation. By control, both proactive and reactive controls are meant. This includes missions related to the preparation, organization, management, and follow-up of the investigations conducted, including procedures aimed at the potential application of an administrative fine or administrative sanction. It is clearly established that it specifically concerns:  
- the procedures within the framework of investigations on security conditions (security screenings);  
- the processing of reporting obligations, included in the law, which generally relate to the reporting of punishable acts committed by persons who fall under the scope of this law;  
- the administrative procedures related to the granting, renewal, refusal, suspension, withdrawal of authorizations, rights of a person to carry out activities as referred to in this law, the authorizations and identification cards provided for by this law;  
- the preparation, organization, management, and follow-up of field controls;  
- the administrative procedure and sanction procedures.”  

18. The Authority notes that the purposes of the processing for which rights are limited fall within the framework of the legal missions of control and monitoring of the law of October 2, 2017 by the General Directorate of Security and Prevention of the Federal Public Service Interior.  

19. That being said, regarding point 2°, the Authority requests the applicant to specify the legal provisions on which the processing that may be limited is based, similar to the other points.  

II.3.2. Categories of personal data  

20. The explanatory memorandum mentions that “To ensure that investigations and procedures can be conducted effectively, exceptions to the rights of the data subjects apply to all types of personal data that the services responsible for control, inspection, or regulation process in the exercise of their legal missions.” It specifies that “This may include judicial or administrative police information, such as information from police, security, or intelligence services, or from the public prosecutor's office, but also other purely administrative information or information provided by various public or private actors that may be relevant in light of the legally intended purposes of the investigation.”  

21. The Authority notes that the categories of data processed are not specified in the legal text itself and requests the applicant to fill this gap under penalty of not complying with the provisions of Article 23.2.b of the GDPR.  

22. It observes in this regard that data from authorities falling under the Police and Justice Directive and from intelligence and security services are already subject to limitations on the rights of the data subject under the draft law relating to the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data (hereinafter the “framework draft law”). The current draft law explicitly refers to this.
Notice 62/2018 - 7/14  
The draft bill and the explanatory memorandum of the current draft explicitly mention these limitations: “Regarding the nature of personal data, it should be noted that it is not only about police and judicial information and that a simple reference to the specific regulation (N.B.: sic) that must be provided in this matter in the national transposition regulation of the police/justice directive is not sufficient (...) Specific regulation is necessary.” It therefore invites the applicant to only provide limitations concerning data not covered by these provisions.

23. The Authority further reminds that for all other data that the General Directorate of Security and Prevention of the SPF Interior processes for other missions or purposes, it is obliged – like any other data controller – to comply with the provisions of Articles 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, and 21 of the GDPR.

II.3.3. Scope of the limitations introduced

24. As detailed in the explanatory memorandum, the rights of the data subject may be “delayed, limited, or excluded.” The limitations provided relate to the following articles:  
- Article 13: information to be provided when personal data is collected from the data subject;  
- Article 14: information to be provided when personal data has not been collected from the data subject;  
- Article 15: right of access of the data subject;  
- Article 16: right to rectification;  
- Article 18: right to restriction of processing;  
- Article 21: right to object to the processing of personal data.

25. The Authority notes immediately that the extent of the limitations imposed on the various rights is not specified by the applicant, contrary to what is required by Article 23.2.c of the GDPR. It therefore finds that a significant margin of maneuver is left to the data controller, which is not acceptable.

26. It refers the applicant, for illustrative purposes, to Articles 61, 63, 65, and 67 of the draft law establishing the information security committee and amending various laws concerning the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC. These articles incorporate provisions specifying the limitations based on the concerned rights.

27. The Authority also notes that the explanatory memorandum does not justify the relevance of the various limitations provided. It invites the applicant to provide this justification. For example, it does not see the relevance of excluding the information to be provided when personal data is collected from the data subject.

28. It is also surprised that the right to erasure is not mentioned by the applicant, while the explanatory memorandum suggests that the applicant's objective is, in particular, to limit this right (cf. point 13).

29. Furthermore, Article 269/1, § 2 of the draft stipulates that the limitation of the rights of the data subject applies:  
a) regarding investigations into security conditions and the processing of reports: indefinitely in time;  
b) in the context of the exercise of missions related to administrative procedures concerning the granting, renewal, refusal, suspension, withdrawal of authorizations, rights of a person to carry out activities as referred to in the law of October 2, 2017, permits and identification cards provided by this law: exclusively during the period preceding a final decision in the context of the mentioned administrative procedures;  
c) in the context of control and sanction procedures: exclusively during the period in which the interested party is directly or indirectly subject to an ongoing investigation, control, or inspection, as well as during the preparatory work related thereto; if the investigation file is subsequently transmitted to the sanctioning official, referred to in Article 238 of the law, to decide on the results of the investigation, the rights will only be restored once a final decision has been made in this regard.

30. It also provides specific provisions, notably in the case of transmission of the file to the judicial authority, and specifies that the information collected during the execution of duties prescribed by the judicial authority may only be communicated with its express authorization.
Notice 62/2018 - 9/14  
31. It follows from the explanatory memorandum that the unlimited nature of the limitation of rights regarding investigations into security conditions and the handling of reports is justified by the presence of information from the authorities referred to in Titles 2 and 3 of the draft framework law.

32. The Authority does not accept this reasoning since, as previously mentioned, limitations on the rights of the individual concerned regarding this information processed subsequently are already provided for in the draft framework law.

33. It therefore requests the applicant to focus on the other data and to specify an appropriate period for the limitations related to the rights concerning this data. The Authority refers by analogy to the regime provided for concerning investigations by social, fiscal, and economic inspection services.

II.3.4. Safeguards to prevent abuse or unlawful access or transfer

34. The Authority takes note of the wording of Article 5, paragraph 1 of the draft bill which states: "In the context of the application of Article 412, the General Directorate for Security and Prevention of the Ministry of the Interior (...) takes the necessary measures aimed at ensuring the prevention of abuse as well as unlawful access and transfer of personal data, including the appropriate technical and organizational measures referred to in Article 32 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679."

35. However, it considers that this specification is superfluous as it does not specify the safeguards taken and only makes a reference to the obligation of Article 32 of the GDPR.

36. It invites the applicant to specify these safeguards.

II.3.6. Retention Periods and Applicable Guarantees  

38. Article 269/2 of the draft law provides for a general retention period of a maximum of 10 years from the date of the last processing of new information concerning the individual, for personal data processed by the General Directorate for Security and Prevention of the Federal Public Service Interior in the context of its legal missions regarding enforcement and monitoring compliance with the law of October 2, 2017.  

39. The explanatory memorandum specifies that "A 10-year retention period was chosen because - with a few exceptions that are provided for a longer retention period (30 years) - this period is proposed as a retention period in the archive management plans still in force today and the selection lists that have been established in consultation with the Archives of the Kingdom. For control and inspection services, it is important that files, including personal data, are retained long enough because authorizations, permits, and consents are generally issued for 5 years, and certain companies or individuals are sometimes subjected to an investigation or control only within the framework of a 'renewal procedure.' Setting the period at 5 years is insufficiently long because investigations and controls initiated within the framework of such renewal procedures are certainly not always finalized by the expiration date of the authorization, and there may still be ongoing administrative or judicial procedures as a result of the investigations and controls. Experience teaches us that some individuals sometimes disappear for a number of years and resurface nonetheless a few years later. Also, in the context of determining 'recidivism' regarding violations of the law and the implementing decrees and the related consequences, it must be possible to go back several years in time."  

40. The Authority takes note of these explanations.

Draft Article 269/1, § 4 states that "The limitation of the rights of the individual concerned, provided for in this article, does not affect the specific rights that have been granted to the individual concerned by or under this law in the context of a specific dispute or administrative procedure."  

43. The Authority recognizes that the individual concerned has other rights in the context of procedures during which the rights arising from the GDPR may be limited. These rights are likely to provide guarantees to the individual concerned to defend themselves, particularly if erroneous data concerning them is processed.  

II.3.7. Risks to the Rights and Freedoms of the Individuals Concerned  

44. Article 5, paragraph 1 of the draft law mentions that "In the context of the application of Article 414, the General Directorate for Security and Prevention of the Federal Public Service Interior takes into account the risks to the rights and freedoms of the individuals concerned (...)."  

45. The Authority takes note of this.

48. The Authority considers that the information regarding the limitation as presented in the explanatory memorandum does not correspond to what is written in the draft law and to what is required by Article 23.2.h of the GDPR. It invites the applicant to rectify this point.  

49. The Commission also considers that the notion of "request concerning the communication of information to be provided" is too restrictive in relation to the different rights of the data subject whose limitation is contemplated. It also does not understand in paragraph 2 the reference made to paragraph 1 regarding the right(s) referred to.  

II.4. Final (and specific) provisions  

50. The Authority notes that Article 5, paragraph 2 of the draft law provides that the General Directorate for Security and Prevention of the Federal Public Service Interior and the Authority shall conclude a protocol regarding the practical modalities related to the exercise by the latter of the powers conferred upon it by Article 58.1, point e (access to data necessary for the exercise of its powers) and point f (access to premises) of the GDPR, since the Authority is likely to access very sensitive information, as well as data originating from an authority referred to in Title 2 or 3 of the framework law.  

51. The Authority takes note of this provision. It recalls the equivalent possibility provided for in Article 48, § 2 of the law of December 3, 2017 establishing the Data Protection Authority16 which mentions that "The Data Protection Authority may conclude protocols regarding the duty of confidentiality with third parties in order to guarantee the exchange of data necessary for the exercise of its tasks and competencies."  

52. Furthermore, paragraph 3 of Article 5 of the draft law states that the legal obligations as set out in the GDPR and the framework law do not affect the legality of acts that the General Directorate for Security and Prevention of the Federal Public Service Interior has carried out as data controller or as processor before the entry into force of the mentioned obligations.  

53. The Authority considers that this provision is superfluous regarding a basic legal principle. It further specifies that processing that would have begun under the previous legal framework but which are still current must comply with the provisions of the GDPR.  

III. CONCLUSION  

54. The Authority takes note of the limitations on the rights of the data subjects projected within the framework of the monitoring and oversight missions of the law of October 2, 2017 by the General Directorate for Security and Prevention of the Federal Public Service Interior.  

55. It views unfavorably the absence of specification of the categories of data concerned and precision regarding the extent of the limitations imposed on the different rights, contrary to what is required by Article 23.2 points b and c of the GDPR (points 21 and 25).  

56. It also makes certain remarks on other aspects of the draft law and invites the applicant to:  
- formulate the concrete objective of the proposed legislative measure aimed at limiting the scope of the rights of the data subject in the text of the draft law (point 14);  
- specify the legal provisions on which the processing that may be limited in the context of the exercise of missions related to administrative procedures concerning the granting, renewal, refusal, suspension, withdrawal of authorizations is based.
