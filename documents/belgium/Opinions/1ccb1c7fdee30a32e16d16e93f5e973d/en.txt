I. SUBJECT OF THE REQUEST AND CONTEXT

1. The Minister-President of the Walloon Government consults the Authority for an opinion regarding a draft decree amending the decree of December 4, 2003, concerning the establishment of the Walloon Institute for Evaluation, Foresight, and Statistics [IWEPS/the Institute] [the IWEPS decree]. This draft decree mainly concerns the field of public statistics and is linked to the sixth State reform.

2. "In order to meet the objectives of the commitment regarding trust in statistics [(see Article 11, paragraph 3, of Regulation 223/2009)] and to finalize the implementation of [the cooperation agreement], in Wallonia," the explanatory memorandum of the draft (point 3.2.) explains "that it is necessary, on the one hand, to bring the decree concerning the IWEPS into compliance with the principles of the European Statistics Code of Practice and, on the other hand, to establish the legal framework applicable to the development, production, and dissemination of official statistics in Wallonia."

3. This code, based on Regulation 223/2009 and specifying the principles referred to in Article 2 regarding the development, production, and dissemination of European statistics (professional independence, impartiality, objectivity, reliability, statistical confidentiality, and cost-effectiveness), falls within a European normative context, and requires, according to the terms of the explanatory memorandum of the draft (point 3.2.1.), the incorporation into regional legislation of the principles of independence and collection mandate. The latter principle (see Article 6 of the draft) provides for a clear and binding legal mandate empowering statistical authorities to collect information for the purposes of statistics.

EXAMINATION OF THE DRAFT  

II.1. Preliminary Considerations  

5.  
Principle of transparency and legality. Under the principles of transparency and legality enshrined in Articles 8 of the ECHR and 22 of the Constitution, a draft decree must clearly specify under what circumstances the processing of personal data is authorized, and consequently determine what data is processed, the individuals concerned, the conditions and purposes of said processing, the duration of data retention, and the individuals who have access. The Authority has already had the opportunity to recall these principles. When the basis for processing relies on a legal basis of national law, Article 6, 3. of the GDPR also specifically requires that the purposes be defined in this basis.

6.  
These principles must be applied taking into account the nature of statistics and legislation in this area. Indeed, the objective pursued by statistics is inherently relatively broad, and is intended to give rise to concrete applications as diverse as there are relevant areas of public policy (Walloon in this case). Unless this legitimate objective is jeopardized, legislation adopted in this area will consequently offer a certain flexibility through its terms. This will be particularly reflected in the broad definition of the purposes and data concerned. But in return, it will also enshrine the necessary guarantees to frame such flexibility.

7.  
Subsequent data processing and statistics. Although the draft does not explicitly refer to the processing of personal data, there is no doubt that it concerns such data processing (see below, points nos 22 and following). The Walloon statistics as envisaged in the draft is to be carried out, in principle, on the basis of indirect data collection from sources other than the individual concerned, and therefore constitutes subsequent processing (see below, points nos 22 and following). Distribution of competencies in the field of statistics. Title 4 of the LTD (articles 186 et seq.) contains a series of specific rules regarding processing for archival purposes in the public interest, for scientific or historical research purposes, or for statistical purposes in execution of Article 89, 2. and 3. of the GDPR. From the terms of its explanatory memorandum, this regime offers an alternative to the data controller, providing them with "two ways [...] to derogate from the rights of the data subjects" (enshrined in Articles 15, 16, 18, and 21 of the GDPR) (italics added by the Authority), namely: either apply the regime provided for in Articles 190 et seq. of the LTD, or comply with a code of conduct approved in accordance with Article 40 of the GDPR (Article 187 of the GDPR)7. In practice, in the field of statistics, it is this derogatory regime that will generally apply to the data controller.  

9.  
The Council of State recently recalled the following in its opinion issued during the legislative process related to the LCA (the references cited by the Council of State are omitted hereafter): the "Constitutional Court and the legislation section of the Council of State now judge that it is up to [...] each legislator, within the limits of their competencies, to concretize the fundamental rights defined by higher norms [...], in the matters that have been assigned to them [...]"8. Thus, "[i]f the federated entities, each concerning their own matters, adopt provisions for the processing of data within the framework of activities that fall under their competencies – knowing that Articles 6, paragraphs 2 and 3, and 9, paragraph 4, of the [GDPR] allow for the adoption of specific provisions –, they are empowered to create authorities to oversee these specific rules"9. That being said regarding the adoption of these specific provisions, the Council of State also recalled that federal law (now the LTD) must be taken into account by the federated entities and constitutes the minimum regulation for any matter10. In other words, a federated entity cannot implement rules that are less protective of the data subjects in terms of personal data processing (but may implement stricter rules).  

10.

Regarding these safeguards under Belgian law, concerning the distribution of competencies, the question arises as to whether the rules that impact in one way or another on privacy and the processing of personal data in the law of July 4, 1962, relating to public statistics itself, should also be seen as a minimum regulation binding the legislators of the federated entities in this matter.

12.  
In the spirit of the legislators, it seems that the answer is no, since they deemed it useful to provide in the cooperation agreement, among other things, that the provisions related to statistical confidentiality (which does not also, a priori, target all the rules impacting data protection) would apply in accordance with the provisions of the law of July 4, 1962, just mentioned (see for the surplus, regarding the safeguards enshrined in the cooperation agreement, infra, point no. 33, and Chapter V of the cooperation agreement).

13.  
The Authority is of the opinion that in the present context, the LTD could be considered as constituting by itself, this binding minimal regime for the federated entities. The latter would remain free to define their own normative framework for data protection for the rest (even if aligning it with the federal normative framework), in the field of statistics. Deciding otherwise would make the applicable normative framework even less readable and would harm the principle of transparency mentioned above as well as legal certainty. Such a position would be all the more problematic in this regard, as the logic would then command generalizing it to other matters subject to a partial transfer of competencies to the federated entities, which have an impact on the protection of natural persons concerning the processing of personal data. That being said, the question raised falls within the competencies of the Council of State and the Constitutional Court, which the applicant will follow, clarifying its possible choices to ensure legal certainty in its legal order.

II.2. Purposes

14.  
The purposes of the processing provided for in the draft must be determined, explicit, and legitimate (Articles 5, 1., b), and 6, 1., e), and 3., of the GDPR).

II.2.1. The Walloon Statistical System

15.  
The new Chapter V of the IWEPS decree proposed by the draft establishes the "Walloon Statistical System." As previously explained, this system aims to be decentralized (see above, point no. 4). It is composed of the IWEPS, the statistical authority of the Walloon Region, and the

"other producers of official statistics, namely the entities under the regional authority that develop, produce, and disseminate official statistics in accordance with this decree" (Article 12 of the draft, as it inserts Article 17/5, paragraph 2, into the IWEPS decree), and which "are professionally independent entities within their respective bodies for their activities related to the development, production, and dissemination of official statistics" (Article 13 of the draft, as it inserts Article 17/6, paragraph 2, into the IWEPS decree). Note: for the sake of readability, this last clarification should be drafted together with the first. These other producers of official statistics must be designated by the chief statistician of the IWEPS (Article 17/7, 5°, of the IWEPS decree as inserted by Article 14 of the draft). The IWEPS and the other producers of official statistics together constitute the producers of official statistics.

II.2.2. Statistical Mission and Strategic Advisory Mission

16.  
The IWEPS is explicitly designed to pursue two missions discussed below: a "statistical" mission and a "strategic advisory" mission, for the purpose of decision-making assistance.

17.  
Article 9, paragraph 2, subparagraph 2, inserted into the IWEPS decree by Article 3 of the draft, is ambiguous in that it provides that "In the context of its missions, the Institute is responsible for the collection, storage, and processing of data essential to the conduct of regional policy" (italics added by the Authority). Either this constitutes a third mission of the IWEPS, or it charges the IWEPS with the collection, etc., of the data necessary for the accomplishment of the two aforementioned missions. The Authority assumes that the second option is retained and invites the applicant to clarify its draft in this sense. If this were not the case, such a purpose (mission) would not be sufficiently precise to justify the processing of personal data.

18.  
The draft defines "use for statistical purposes" as "the exclusive use of data for the development and production of official statistics, statistical analyses, evaluative or prospective analyses, and statistical services, including all activities governed by this decree" (Article 1 of the draft, as it inserts Article 1, 13°, into the IWEPS decree). This definition should be linked to the definition of "federal" statistical confidentiality, which is applicable in this case through the cooperation agreement (see Article 42 of the cooperation agreement, and Article 1ter of the law of July 4, 1962, on public statistics, which states that statistical confidentiality "implies that any non-statistical use of the obtained data and any unlawful disclosure [are] prohibited"), more broadly, to the cooperation agreement which also, in a certain way, resorts to the notion of use for statistical purposes, and to Regulation 223/2009 (see its Articles 3, 8, and 20). In other words, use for statistical purposes is a concept that must be limited to the statistical mission of the IWEPS and consequently, to the purpose it constitutes.

19. The statistical mission of the IWEPS consists of "the development, production, and dissemination of regional statistics," this mission also covering "the conduct of fundamental and applied research that contributes to statistical work" (Article 3 of the draft, as it inserts Article 9, paragraph 1, subparagraph 3, into the IWEPS decree). It is exercised "mainly in the fields covered by economic, social, political, and environmental sciences" (Article 3 of the draft, as it inserts Article 9, paragraph 2, subparagraph 1, into the IWEPS decree). It emerges from the project that other producers of official statistics will handle personal data for the same statistical purpose as the IWEPS: Article 17/6, subparagraph 3 of the IWEPS decree, as proposed by Article 13 of the draft, provides that all producers of statistics "develop, produce, and disseminate the official statistics of the Walloon Region [...]".

20. In this context, the Authority understands from the economy of the project that the provision of statistical processing services, by the IWEPS and other producers of official statistics, based on the data they hold and at the request of third parties, also falls under this statistical mission (Article 17/12, paragraph 1, of the IWEPS decree proposed by Article 19 of the draft).

21. This is also the case, "outside the statistics program," for the provision of "data collection" services by producers of official statistics, at the request of an "international, national, regional, or local authority." (Note: regarding potential international cooperation, see the commentary on cross-border data flows, infra, point no. 38).

22. Finally, the IWEPS also has a "strategic advisory mission" consisting of "conducting, at the request of the Government or the Parliament, evaluations of public policies and foresight studies as well as short- and medium-term forecasts and contributions on current issues, thereby providing well-documented insights on matters of public interest and for decision-makers" (Article 3 of the draft, as it inserts Article 9, paragraph 1, subparagraph 4, into the IWEPS decree). The Authority is not sure of perceiving the link between this mission and the personal data that will be processed for the statistical mission of the IWEPS. This mission could seem to fall, to some extent (evaluation of public policies), within the statistical mission of the IWEPS, but this would not correspond to the choice made in the provision, as it concerns a separate mission. As currently drafted, the strategic purpose should be clarified. The Authority will assume that it should fall under the statistical mission of the IWEPS. Otherwise, it should be given distinct attention in the project, in light of the principles previously mentioned (see above, points nos. 5 and following).

II.3. Data Concerned and Recipients of the Data  

23.  
Regarding the data concerned, the draft bill does not explicitly target personal data, but such data is included in the broader concept of "confidential data" (see Article 1 of the draft bill, as it inserts Article 1, 5° into the IWEPS decree, referring to Article 3, 7 of Regulation 223/2009). The definitions of the concepts of statistical unit and identifier also indicate that personal data will be involved (Article 1 of the draft bill, as it inserts Article 1, 7° and 12° into the IWEPS decree).  

24.  
The draft bill generally provides that for the "execution of [its missions (those referred to in Article 9 of the draft bill, of the IWEPS decree, i.e., the statistical and strategic advisory missions)] the institute has access to all information, documents, and data, including confidential data, held by data providers as well as to scientific studies conducted by them or on their behalf, and it receives this information free of charge," "the Government making "available to the Institute the studies it conducts or has conducted on its behalf on these same subjects" (Article 5 of the draft bill, as it inserts Article 10/2 into the IWEPS decree, referring to Article 3, 7 of Regulation 223/2009).  

25.  
These data providers are "the units of public administration, local authorities and administrations, and private bodies entrusted with a public service mission, requested to provide official statistics producers with data collected mainly for administrative purposes" (see Article 1 of the draft bill, as it inserts Article 1, 6° into the IWEPS decree, also see the definition of public administration unit, in 12° of that last article).  

26.  
The data concerned are also those that official statistics producers may obtain from declarants under their collection mandate (see Article 17 of the draft bill, as it inserts Article 17/10 into the IWE decree), if they do not have the necessary data from the aforementioned providers.

If at first glance, this way of considering the data in question may seem questionable due to its breadth, regarding the purpose of public statistics (see above, point no. 6), the draft bill can nevertheless consider it in light of the programming process planned in the future Walloon statistical system. Thus, it is the five-year and annual statistical programs that will determine the strategic and operational aspects of the statistics to be produced. They are developed by the chief statistician (who heads the statistical authority, IWEPS), are subject to an opinion from the Users' Committee (a multi-party committee, see Article 17/8 of the IWEPS decree proposed by Article 15 of the draft bill), and ultimately, are adopted by the Government (Article 16 of the draft bill, as it inserts Article 17/9 into the IWEPS decree, particularly paragraph 4). The adoption of the annual statistical program (operational aspect) will establish the legal mandate for the binding collection of data by IWEPS and other official statistics producers, primarily from data providers, and secondarily from declarants (Article 17 of the draft bill, as it inserts Article 17/10 into the IWEPS decree, particularly paragraph 1). The provision rightly stipulates that only the data necessary for the execution of the programs must be provided (Article 17 of the draft bill, as it inserts Article 17/10 into the IWEPS decree, particularly paragraphs 2 and 3).

**II.4. Data Controller and Data Retention Period**

28. For the record, the data controller is the person, public authority, or any other body that determines alone or jointly with others the purposes and means of processing (Article 4, 7, of the GDPR). And when the purposes and means of processing are determined by national law, "the data controller may be designated or the specific criteria applicable to its designation may be provided by [national] law." In this regard, while Member States can specify the application of GDPR rules in particular areas where they legislate to ensure consistency and clarity of the normative framework applicable to data processing, they cannot derogate from the GDPR or depart from the definitions it establishes. If national law determines joint responsibility for processing, it may also determine the respective obligations of the joint data controllers (Article 26, 1., of the GDPR).

29. IWEPS, through the role of its chief statistician, is decisive in the creation of statistics and any potential processing of personal data involved. Indeed, among other things, the chief statistician is accountable to Parliament for the overall management of the statistical system, defines the standards and guidelines to be applied throughout the statistical system for the development, production, and dissemination of official statistics, coordinates the activities of the statistical system, designates other producers of official statistics, and above all, develops the five-year and annual statistical programs (see Articles 17/6, paragraph 3, and 17/7, 2° to 5°, inserted in the...
Notice 29/2019 - 10/13  
Decree IWEPS by Articles 13 and 14 of the draft bill), which respectively determine the strategic and operational aspects of the envisaged statistics (including the statistics to be published and the data to be collected) (see Article 17/9 inserted in the IWEPS decree by Article 16 of the draft bill, and also see above, point no. 26).

30.  
In light of these elements, the draft bill should provide that IWEPS and the producers of official statistics are responsible for the processing of personal data they carry out for statistical purposes. The applicant will identify for this purpose any potential joint liability in the production of the statistics, between IWEPS and other producers of official statistics. Regarding the latter, the Authority notes that as they are professionally independent entities within their respective bodies (see above, point no. 15), they could, if necessary, bear their own liability in terms of personal data processing, distinct from that of these bodies.

31.  
Concerning the duration of data retention, the Authority notes that Article 5, 1., e), of the GDPR offers some flexibility regarding statistics, providing that “personal data may be retained for longer periods insofar as they are processed exclusively for [notably] statistical purposes in accordance with Article 89, paragraph 1, provided that appropriate technical and organizational measures are implemented […] to safeguard the rights and freedoms of the data subject.” This is what the statistical programs will consequently need to determine, including the duration of data retention.

32.

II.5.2. Appropriate safeguards, technical and organizational measures

33.  
As previously mentioned, the LTD implements in Belgian law Article 89, 2. and 3., of the GDPR, and Article 89, 1., of the GDPR directly requires appropriate safeguards regarding processing for statistical purposes, which "ensure the implementation of technical and organizational measures, particularly to ensure compliance with the principle of data minimization," these measures may include "pseudonymization, insofar as these purposes can be achieved in this manner," and when the intended purpose can be achieved "by further processing that does not allow or no longer allows the identification of the data subjects," it is appropriate to proceed in this manner.

34.  
The cooperation agreement provides a series of guarantees for the statistical authority (IWEPS, in this case) – in this regard, the concept of producers of official statistics is indeed specific to the draft bill. Thus, IWEPS must guarantee "the rights of respondents and ensure compliance with statistical confidentiality," notably by "designating a data protection officer" and by "adopting a code of conduct defining the rules and guidelines imposed on service members regarding confidentiality, privacy protection, business secrecy, and data protection" (Article 36, 2°); the transmission of "confidential data" may only occur in the limited case where it is "necessary for the effectiveness of the development, production, and dissemination of official statistics, or to improve their quality" (Article 38); any further transmission will require the express authorization of the statistical authority that collected the data (Article 40); the transmitted data may only be used exclusively for "statistical purposes" and will be "accessible only to agents performing statistical tasks in their specific area of activity" (Article 41); finally, statistical authorities are bound to statistical confidentiality with respect to third parties, in accordance with the law of July 4, 1962, relating to public statistics.

The Authority supports such an approach but recommends that at the very least a general security objective be enshrined in the draft bill itself, without leaving total discretion to the Government in this matter, given the potential high risk already mentioned.

II.6. Cross-border data flows

39.  
The Authority draws the applicant's attention, regarding the possibility of international cooperation in statistics, to the regime of cross-border data flows established in the GDPR. The Authority limits itself to reminding that transfers of personal data to a third country outside the European Economic Area constitute processing that can only take place in accordance with Articles 44 and following of the GDPR.
