I. SUBJECT AND CONTEXT OF THE REQUEST FOR OPINION

1. On April 4, 2019, the Walloon Minister of Youth, Youth Aid, Justice Houses, Sports, and Promotion of Brussels (hereinafter, the requester) requested the Authority to issue an opinion regarding a draft order of the Government of the French Community amending the order of May 8, 2018 concerning adoption (hereinafter, the draft order).

2. The legal framework for adoption in Belgium has recently undergone a number of cascading adaptations at the federal and community levels, of which the draft order constitutes the latest step for the Walloon-Brussels Federation. The draft order aims to amend the order of May 8, 2018 regarding adoption (hereinafter, the adoption order) in order to implement the decree of the French Community amending the decree of March 31, 2004 regarding adoption (hereinafter, the adoption decree), adopted by the Parliament of the Walloon-Brussels Federation on March 27, 2019.

3. It appears from the note to the government that this wave of legislative changes aims in particular at harmonizing internal and international adoption procedures concerning the assessment of the suitability of prospective adoptive parents, the completion of certain steps in the procedure (conducting social investigations, informing birth parents, drafting reports...), strengthening certain guarantees in collaborations abroad, and harmonizing practices and transparency of procedures for equal treatment of adopters. These changes have an impact on the processing of personal data within the framework of adoption procedures. In particular, the amendment of the adoption order and its annexes (document templates) concerns a large number of personal data.

4. During this process, the opinion of the Privacy Protection Commission (hereinafter, the Commission), the legal predecessor of the Authority, was sought for the draft law concerning the simplification, harmonization, computerization, and modernization of civil law and civil procedure provisions as well as notarial law, and concerning various provisions in the field of justice. However, the opinion of the Authority was not sought regarding the draft decree amending the adoption decree, despite the opinion of the Council of State in this regard.

II. REVIEW OF THE REQUEST FOR OPINION  

1. Preliminary Remarks  

7.  
In its 1998 opinion, the Commission had already lamented the lack of sufficient guarantees regarding privacy protection in the provisions of the Civil Code related to adoption, in light of the principles of purpose, proportionality, and transparency. "Indeed, the context of international adoption, characterized in particular by a 'desire for a child' on the part of prospective adoptive parents, is likely to lead them to communicate, against their will, personal data concerning them, including medical data and very sensitive data, for fear that their adoption request will not be successful. The Commission therefore calls for a very restrictive application of the principle of purpose and, in particular, that the personal data subject to processing be limited to what is strictly necessary."  

8.  
Even though the legal framework for adoption has undergone several adaptations since then, the situation regarding privacy protection does not seem to have evolved significantly. Upon reviewing the draft decree, the decree related to adoption, and the regulation related to adoption, the Authority immediately regrets the lack of consideration for the protection of personal data of the individuals concerned, especially in such a sensitive area. Notably, none of the document templates attached to the draft decree include mentions ensuring transparency of processing and informing the individuals concerned. Similarly, none of the texts examined provide guarantees regarding data security and the rights of the individuals concerned.  

9.  
In accordance with the principle of accountability, the Authority invites the applicant to take appropriate organizational measures and to review the legal framework for adoption that falls under its competence, incorporating the necessary adjustments for the protection of personal data. This exercise should be carried out in consultation with the data protection officer, taking into account the applicable regional, federal, and international regulations, to verify their compliance with the GDPR, the LTD, Article 22 of the Constitution, Article 8 of the ECHR, and Article 16 of the International Convention on the Rights of the Child.  

2. Legitimacy and Purpose of Processing  

10.  
The purposes of processing personal data in the context of an adoption procedure can be deduced from the objectives of the decree related to adoption, and notably:  
- Promote quality support for birth parents considering placing their child for adoption;  
- Promote a qualitative assessment of the adoptability of children;  
- Adequately support the adoption of children with specific needs;  
- Ensure genuine co-responsibility with the countries of origin in international adoption situations;  
- Promote a quality assessment of the suitability of prospective adoptive parents;  
- Promote an examination of applications focused on the needs of adoptable children;  
- Promote individualized matching.  

11.  
These principles have been developed in an ethical charter approved by the government of the French Community, all in compliance with the Civil Code, the Judicial Code, and the Hague Convention.  

12.  
The authority considers these purposes to be determined, explicit, and legitimate in light of Article 5, 1, b) of the GDPR. The legal basis for processing is found in Article 6.1, c (execution of a legal obligation, on the part of the Accredited Adoption Organizations) and 6.1, e (execution of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority, for other stakeholders) of the GDPR.  

13.  
When the legal basis for processing is a legal obligation or a task carried out in the public interest, as is the case here, it is also necessary to take into account Article 6.3 of the GDPR which prescribes the essential elements of data processing to be included in the regulation:  
- The general conditions governing the lawfulness of processing by the data controller;  
- The types or categories of personal data that will be subject to processing;

3. Responsible(s) for Processing  

16.  
As a reminder, the data controller is defined as "the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing" (Article 4.7 of the GDPR). Article 4.7 of the GDPR states that when the processing and means of data processing are determined by regulation, as is the case here, the data controller may be designated by the regulation.  

17.  
According to the information available to the Authority, several actors are involved in managing an adoption request within the Federation Wallonia-Brussels.  

18.  
The Community Central Authority (CCA) is the competent authority for adoption for the Federation Wallonia-Brussels. Its main missions are:  
-  
the organization of preparation sessions;  
-  
the conduct of social investigations ordered by the Youth Court;  
-  
the support of adoption procedures in Belgium and abroad;  
-  
the oversight of Accredited Adoption Organizations;  
-  
cooperation with other competent Belgian authorities and with foreign authorities.  

19.  
The Accredited Adoption Organizations (AAOs) are services (legal entities under public or private law) acting as adoption intermediaries, with a mission of helping and protecting children and also supporting adoptive parenting. They must be accredited by the French Community under the adoption decree.  

20.  
The Family Court is the judicial authority competent in matters of the adoption of minors. It is particularly competent in the procedure for determining the suitability of prospective adoptive candidates and in the procedure for determining the adoptability of a child, for both domestic and international adoptions. The Public Prosecutor also intervenes to provide an opinion following the morality investigation it conducts on the adoptive candidates, notably by consulting their criminal records.  

21.  
Each of these entities can be classified as a data controller in the context of its missions during the adoption procedure and must ensure compliance with legislation regarding the protection of personal data. To avoid any ambiguity regarding the role of each and to facilitate the exercise of the rights of the concerned individual as outlined in Articles 12 to 22 of the GDPR, the Authority invites the applicant to indicate, in its draft order, who assumes the role of data controller (possibly jointly) and for which types of processing.  

4. Data Collected and Proportionality of Processing  

a) General Comments  

22.  
The adoption order and the draft order contain about twenty annexes including templates of documents to be used by data controllers in various situations and at different stages of the procedure: templates for reports, files, forms, agreements specific to different adoption situations… It notably includes sensitive data, such as: the consumption of alcohol and drugs and smoking by the biological parents of the child, their religious affiliation, the medical data of the child, particular points of attention regarding the child's psycho-emotional state, the identification and health status of the child's siblings, grandparents, and extended family of origin, the infertility of prospective adoptive candidates, their capacity.

of empathy, their "psychic flexibility," their management of stress and conflicts, their psycho-affective availability, the couple's mode of functioning, their financial situation14…  

23.  
In the context of an adoption procedure, the stakes are such that it is important to find, in the best interest of the child to be adopted, the adoptive parents who best meet their needs. The Authority acknowledges that this objective necessarily involves a thorough understanding of the child and the prospective adoptive parents, thus requiring the processing of personal data, including sensitive data.  

24.  
The best interest of the child constitutes the cornerstone of the Belgian legal framework and the Hague Convention on adoption. However, the Authority emphasizes that the best interest of the child does not justify in abstracto and by principle all intrusions into the private life of the child themselves, the prospective adoptive parents, and their respective families. It would even be contradictory to invoke the best interest of the child to process their personal data without appropriate guarantees for the protection of their privacy. Moreover, the Hague Convention specifically states that its purpose is "to establish safeguards to ensure that international adoptions take place in the best interests of the child and with respect for the fundamental rights recognized to them in international law"15. Furthermore, the right to respect for private life is a fundamental right enshrined in Article 16 of the Convention on the Rights of the Child.  

25.  
Similarly, the best interest of the child in the context of the adoption procedure does not lead to the annihilation of the right to the protection of personal data of the prospective adoptive parents. The only data that may be processed are those necessary to assess their suitability to adopt and their good match with the child and their needs, in accordance with the aforementioned ethical charter on adoption.  

26.  
The Authority recalls, in this regard, that the collection and processing of personal data of the child and the prospective adoptive parents must comply with the principles of proportionality and data minimization: only data that is adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purposes pursued may be processed (art. 5.1, c, of the GDPR). It is therefore necessary for the applicant to conduct a systematic analysis of each category of data required in the annexes of the adoption decree, in order to verify whether this data is necessary to achieve the purpose or purposes pursued16. For example, the child's health status and medical history are necessary elements to ensure their medical follow-up throughout their life. On the other hand, if it is

The Authority regrets not having been consulted on such a sensitive issue as the collection of biometric data related to the child to be adopted. It further regrets that the purpose of such data collection is not explained, nor its necessity demonstrated. The vague reference to the "difficulties experienced in recent years" cannot in any way suffice to justify such processing of sensitive data.  

31.  
It should also be recalled that biometric data falls within a particular category of personal data referred to in Article 9 of the GDPR, the processing of which is prohibited unless certain conditions are met. In this case, in the absence of more detailed explanations regarding the necessity of this collection in light of the purposes of processing and the appropriate safeguards for the fundamental rights and interests of the data subject, the Authority does not see on what basis such processing of biometric data could be considered lawful.  

32.  
The Authority further believes that the general reference to the use of "biometric data" is a priori too broad, as some methods (such as photography) are less intrusive than others (DNA). It is up to the legislator to determine the method(s) that organizations may use, in compliance with the principle of proportionality. Moreover, it is not demonstrated that the systematic collection of DNA would adequately combat the risks of identity falsification of the child to be adopted. Additional measures taken by the applicant to strengthen the reliability control of foreign partners may also provide guarantees in this regard (see points 53 and following). Assuming that the applicant can demonstrate that DNA is the best solution to the problem, the Authority believes that such control should be limited to cases where there is a genuine doubt about the child's identity and where there is no other reliable and less intrusive means of identification. The Authority therefore invites the applicant to develop a prior and thorough analysis of the proportionality of the proposed biometric identification measures and, where appropriate, to develop an appropriate legal framework for the collection and processing of such data19. It recommends conducting this prior analysis within the framework of the data protection impact assessment mentioned below.

c) Necessity to conduct a data protection impact assessment (DPIA)

33. According to Article 35.1 of the GDPR, "When a type of processing, in particular using new technologies, and taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the controller shall carry out, prior to the processing, an assessment of the impact of the envisaged processing operations on the protection of personal data."

34. In its Recommendation 01/2018, the Authority sets out 9 criteria to determine whether a processing operation may give rise to a high risk, among which are the sensitive or highly personal nature of the data processed and the vulnerability of the individuals concerned. "In most cases, a controller can assume that if a processing operation meets two criteria, a data protection impact assessment must be carried out."

35. Therefore, given the sensitive nature of the data processed (notably biometric data), as well as the vulnerable situation of the individuals concerned (birth parents, child to be adopted, and prospective adoptive parents), the Authority considers it necessary for the applicant to conduct such an impact assessment.

36. This impact assessment must be carried out by seeking advice from the data protection officer (Article 35.2 of the GDPR). It must contain at least the following elements (Article 35.7 of the GDPR):

a) "a systematic description of the envisaged processing operations and the purposes of the processing, including, where applicable, the legitimate interest pursued by the controller;  
b) an assessment of the necessity and proportionality of the processing operations in relation to the purposes;  
c) an assessment of the risks to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects in accordance with [Article 35.1]; and  
d) the measures envisaged to address the risks, including safeguards, security measures, and mechanisms to ensure the protection of personal data and to demonstrate compliance with this Regulation, taking into account the rights and legitimate interests of the data subjects and other affected persons."

5. Information Security

37. The Authority notes that the draft decree, the decree on adoption, and the adoption regulation do not mention any measures related to security, particularly regarding the confidentiality of personal data. It reminds in this regard of the obligation to implement "appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk," taking into account "the state of the art, the costs of implementation, the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks, which vary in probability and severity, to the rights and freedoms of natural persons" (Article 32.1 of the GDPR).

38. These measures may include, as necessary (Article 32.1 of the GDPR):  
a) the pseudonymization and encryption of personal data (for example, for DNA data);  
b) means to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services;  
c) means to restore the availability of personal data and access to it in a timely manner in the event of a physical or technical incident;  
d) a procedure for regularly testing, analyzing, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures.

39. Among the organizational measures, the Authority particularly suggests training and raising awareness of privacy compliance among the staff of the competent administration, as well as adoption agencies and social workers responsible for collecting and processing data. Thus, it is important to raise their awareness of their confidentiality obligations and the principle of proportionality. As a reminder, when collecting data during interviews and recording them in a report, only the data strictly necessary to achieve the intended purposes should be retained.

40. The Authority reiterates the sensitive nature of certain data in the file, the processing of which requires heightened vigilance. In particular, the categories of data referred to in Article 9 of the GDPR (including biometric data) require stricter security measures, in accordance with Article 9 of the LTD:  
- designate the categories of persons who have access to personal data (for all relevant instances), with a precise description of their role in relation to the processing of the data concerned;  
- keep a list of the categories of persons so designated available to the competent supervisory authority;

6. Rights of the Data Subjects  

41.  
From the outset, the Authority notes that the draft decree submitted for opinion, and in particular the models of documents (forms, agreements) attached to the draft decree, do not contain any informative mention regarding the protection of personal data and the exercise of rights for the data subjects. This situation should be corrected, in compliance with the principles of fairness and transparency and Articles 12 and following of the GDPR.  

42.  
Regarding the right of access, the Authority emphasizes that it is necessary to distinguish, on the one hand, the right of individuals to access their own personal data, and on the other hand, the right of the adopted child to access data about their origins.  

43.  
Article 15 of the GDPR enshrines the right of access for the data subject. In the context of an adoption, this right can be invoked by any person (biological parents and family, adopted child, prospective adoptive parents, adoptive parents...) concerning the data that pertains to them. Limitations to this right may be provided through legislative measures in accordance with Article 23 of the GDPR.  

44.  
Article 368-6 of the Civil Code, in its new version, governs the possibility for the adopted person, their legal representatives, or, in the event of the adopted person's death, their descendants to access information relating to the "origins of the adopted person, in particular those relating to the identity of their mother and father [biological], as well as the data necessary for monitoring their health situation, on the medical history of the adopted person and their [biological] family." It is not only a matter of the data subjects accessing data that concerns them, but also certain data relating to third parties, for specific purposes (access to origins and health care). Hence the need to specifically frame this right of access. The decree relating to adoption further provides that the right to consultation "concerns data relating to the origins of the adopted person, excluding data relating to the psychological and social aptitudes of the adoptive parents, and post-adoption follow-ups. This right is not open to the biological family or the siblings of the adopted person." The consultation must also be framed by a professional. The Authority believes that this consultation of data relating to a third party is based on legitimate purposes and is well defined. However, it regrets that the decree on adoption has not

7. Data Retention Period 

45.  
Article 49 of the adoption decree provides that the Central Competent Authority and adoption agencies shall retain "the information they hold regarding the origins of the adopted child, including information related to the identity of the mother and father as well as information related to the medical history of the adopted child and their family." This provision is based on Article 368-6 of the Civil Code, which imposes on the competent authorities the obligation to retain this data. The decree on adoption stipulates that these documents must be retained for "a minimum of fifty years." 

46.  
The Authority recalls the principle of data retention limitation provided by Article 5.1, e) of the GDPR, according to which personal data must be retained for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which they are processed. In this case, the purpose is to allow the adopted person (and their descendants after their death) to access information related to their origins and medical information that may influence their health and that of their descendants, in accordance with the access conditions set by law (see point 43). In these circumstances and in light of these purposes, the Authority considers it justified that the retention of this specific data be governed by a minimum indicative retention period. 

47.  
On the other hand, the Authority is surprised that this minimum period is half as long as the minimum retention period of 100 years provided by the royal decree of April 23, 2017, concerning the collection, retention, and access to information regarding the origins of the adopted person (Art. 1, § 2), enacted pursuant to Article 368-6 of the Civil Code. This contradiction should be corrected by aligning the community period with the period provided by the royal decree, in order to consistently guarantee the access rights of the persons concerned throughout Belgium. This retention period may indeed exceed the lifespan of the person concerned, so that the rules of the GDPR no longer apply to their personal data after their death.

8. Data Transfers in the Case of International Adoptions  

49.  
The legal framework for adoption in Belgium also regulates international adoptions. Under Article 360-2 of the Civil Code, international adoptions are considered to be situations where the child:  
“1° has been, is, or is to be moved from the country of origin to Belgium, either after being adopted in that country by a person or persons who usually reside in Belgium, or with a view to such an adoption in Belgium or in that country, or  
2° usually resides in Belgium and has been, is, or is to be moved to a foreign country, either after being adopted in Belgium by a person or persons who usually reside in that foreign country, or with a view to such an adoption in Belgium or in the foreign country, or  
3° resides in Belgium without being authorized to establish or stay there for more than three months, in order to be adopted by a person or persons who usually reside there.”  

50.  
These situations are also governed by the Hague Convention.  

51.  
The management of these adoption situations involves transfers of personal data from Belgium to competent adoption authorities located abroad, and vice versa.

a) Current Framework for Collaborations Abroad under the Adoption Decree  

53.  
Approved adoption agencies are authorized to collaborate with a number of countries of origin. For each concerned country, the French Community must approve a collaboration framework between the adoption agency and the local authorities or institutions hosting children in need of adoption. This authorization may only concern a specific region or one or more institutions in that country. The list of countries with which approved adoption agencies collaborate is published on the website of the Central Community Authority. Currently, all these countries are located outside the territory of the European Union.  

54.  
In accordance with the procedure provided for by the adoption decree, when an adoption agency wishes to initiate collaboration abroad, it notifies the administration of the French Community that has adoption in its responsibilities and attaches to its request:  
“1° a translation into French of the legislation on adoption in force in the country or territorial entity of the concerned country;  
2° the identification of the competent authorities in matters of adoption in the country or in the territorial entity of the concerned country as well as the identity of any institution, association, or person likely to collaborate with the agency, hereinafter referred to as ‘partners’;  
3° a collaboration framework in the country or in the territorial entity of the concerned country [see Annex 5 of the adoption decree];  
4° a completed questionnaire regarding the country or the territorial entity of the concerned country [see Annex 6 of the adoption decree];  
5° a mission report in the country or in the territorial entity of the concerned country;  
6° any other useful document.”  

55.  
The administration examines the collaboration request and verifies in particular “the guarantees provided by the authorities and [foreign] partners regarding compliance with the applicable law, the origins, adoptability, and the best interests of the child, and the fundamental rights recognized to him/her under Belgian law, the law of the country or territorial entity of the concerned country, and international law.”  

56.  
This general reference to the fundamental rights of the child naturally encompasses the right to the protection of privacy, notably enshrined in the International Convention on the Rights of the Child. 60.

c) Transfers of personal data from Belgium to third countries or to international organizations

61. In the context of international adoption, there will also be transfers of data from Belgium to countries located outside the territory of the European Union. This data may concern both the prospective adoptive parent and the child to be adopted, residing in Belgium. The draft decree modifies the decree on adoption in this regard to specify in the annex the content of the information to be sent abroad, through a model report on the adoptive parents sent to the country of origin of the child to be adopted34 and a model report on the adoptability of the child residing in Belgium for international adoption by adoptive parents residing abroad35. 62. In such cases, the Authority reminds that it is necessary to take into account Chapter V of the GDPR (Articles 44 to 50).

• Existence of an adequacy decision by the European Commission (Article 45 of the GDPR)

63. According to Article 45 of the GDPR, "A transfer of personal data to a third country or to an international organization may take place when the Commission has decided that the third country, a territory or one or more specific sectors within that third country, or the international organization in question ensures an adequate level of protection. Such a transfer does not require specific authorization."

64. Currently, according to the information published on the website of the Central Community Authority36, the countries with which the adoption agencies of the Walloon-Brussels Federation collaborate do not benefit from an adequacy decision37. Therefore, it is necessary to examine other possibilities.

• Existence of appropriate safeguards (Article 46 of the GDPR)

65. According to Article 46 of the GDPR, "In the absence of a decision [of adequacy by the European Commission], the controller or the processor may not transfer personal data to a third country or to an international organization unless it has provided appropriate safeguards and on condition that the data subjects have enforceable rights and effective legal remedies."

66. These appropriate safeguards may be provided by38:
- A legally binding and enforceable instrument between the competent authorities or public bodies;
- The use of standard contractual clauses for data protection, adopted by the Commission or by a supervisory authority and approved by the Commission39;
- A code of conduct drawn up in accordance with Article 40 of the GDPR;
- A certification mechanism approved in accordance with Article 42 of the GDPR.

67. Subject to the authorization of the Authority, appropriate safeguards may also be provided by40:
- "Contractual clauses between the controller or the processor and the controller, the processor, or the recipient of the personal data in the third country or the international organization; or
- Provisions to be included in administrative arrangements between public authorities or public bodies that provide for enforceable and effective rights for the data subjects."

68. In light of the above, the Authority believes that it would be appropriate to review the collaboration protocol with foreign countries regarding adoption in order to integrate appropriate safeguards for the protection of personal data.
