I. PRELIMINARY REMARK

1. The Commission draws attention to the fact that a new European regulation regarding the protection of personal data has recently been enacted: the Regulation on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data and the Police and Justice Directive. These texts were published in the Official Journal of the European Union on May 4, 2016[1].

2. The Regulation, commonly referred to as GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation), came into effect twenty days after its publication, that is, on May 24, 2016, and will automatically apply two years later, that is, on May 25, 2018. The Police and Justice Directive must be transposed into national legislation by no later than May 6, 2018.

3. For the Regulation, this means that from May 24, 2016, and during the two-year implementation period, Member States have, on the one hand, a positive obligation to take all necessary implementing measures, and on the other hand, a negative obligation, referred to as the "duty of abstention." This latter obligation implies the prohibition of enacting national legislation that would seriously undermine the intended outcome of the Regulation. Similar principles also apply to the Directive.

4. It is therefore recommended to potentially anticipate these texts from now on. It is primarily the responsibility of the applicant(s) for the opinion to take this into account in their proposals or projects. In this opinion, the Commission has already ensured, as far as possible and subject to any subsequent complementary viewpoints, compliance with the aforementioned negative obligation.

III. SUBSTANTIVE REVIEW 

6. Articles 22 and 23 of the cooperation agreement of May 23, 2013, between the Walloon Region and the French Community concerning the development of a joint initiative on data sharing and the joint management of this initiative1 indeed provide that the Walloon-Brussels Commission for the control of data exchanges must authorize "the transfers of data from authentic data sources or databases derived from authentic sources, unless this transfer is already subject to authorization from another sectoral committee established within the Commission for the protection of privacy." 

7. The Commission is not competent to authorize these flows. However, under Article 29 of the Privacy Act, the Commission may issue an opinion on any matter relating to the application of the fundamental principles of privacy protection. 

8. The Commission notes that the project for the implementation of a cadastre of educational and post-educational pathways has a legal basis, the Cooperation Agreement concluded on March 20, 2014, between the French Community, the Walloon Region, the Brussels-Capital Region, the French Community Commission, and the German-speaking Community organizing the implementation of a cadastre of educational and post-educational pathways2. 

9. This legal basis determines the purposes of the processing, the general conditions governing the lawfulness of the processing; the types of data subject to processing; the individuals concerned; the entities to which personal data may be communicated, the operations and procedures of processing...

10. The Commission recalls that the LVP provides the concerned individuals with a right to information, a right of access, a right to rectification and opposition, as well as the right not to be subject to an automated decision (Articles 9 to 12 LVP and Chapter III GDPR). These rights must be respected and exercised in accordance with the procedures set out in Articles 9 to 12 of the privacy law and Articles 28 to 35 of the Royal Decree of February 13, 2001.  

11. Regarding the principle of securing the processing of personal data, as provided in Article 16 of the privacy law, it imposes on the data controller the obligation to take adequate technical and organizational measures to protect the personal data it processes and to guard against purpose diversion. The adequacy of these security measures depends, on the one hand, on the state of the technology and the costs incurred, and on the other hand, on the nature of the data to be protected and the potential risks.  

12. The Commission notes that the cooperation agreement remains silent on this matter and takes the opportunity to emphasize the importance of an adequate information security policy. In this regard, I refer you to the "reference measures for security applicable to any processing of personal data."  

13. Furthermore, as stated in the preliminary remark, the GDPR came into force twenty days after its publication, on May 24, 2016, and will automatically apply two years later, on May 25, 2018.  

14. It is therefore recommended to anticipate its application from now on.  

15. In this regard, the GDPR establishes new obligations and principles that apply to data controllers, among others:  
• the obligation of accountability, the establishment of internal documentation, proportionality analysis of the processing;  
• the data protection impact assessment;
