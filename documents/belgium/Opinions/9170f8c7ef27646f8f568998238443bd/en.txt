I. OBJECT OF THE REQUEST AND CONTEXT

1. The draft ordinance establishing the payment circuit for family benefits [the draft] submitted for opinion by the competent Ministers of the College convened of the Common Community Commission of Brussels-Capital [Cocom], aims, following the sixth state reform, according to its explanatory memorandum, "to implement the new payment circuit for family benefits in the bilingual territory of Brussels-Capital in order to allow Cocom to take over the management and payment of family benefits." The former Commission for the Protection of Privacy [CPVP] has already had the opportunity to express itself in this area, regarding other federated entities.

2. This takeover of the management and payment of family benefits by Cocom will be effective as of January 1, 2020. The draft is directly linked to the rights to family allowances that will ultimately be defined under Brussels law for the territory of the Brussels-Capital region. Requested in this regard, the applicant communicated to the Authority a draft ordinance regulating the granting of family benefits [the draft family benefits]. However, this opinion does not address this latter text, which will only be mentioned for informational purposes, to the extent necessary to clarify the draft submitted for opinion. The cooperation agreement concluded in this matter must also be referenced, to which, at the time of writing this, the federal authority must still adhere [the cooperation agreement].

3. The draft provides for the establishment of a Brussels public operator (established within IRISCARE) taking over, with respect to Cocom, the rights and obligations of FAMIFED (the federal agency for family allowances), and provides for the approval by the College convened of four (private) family allowance funds that will succeed the federal funds in the Brussels region. These family allowance bodies will have the dual mission of establishing and paying Brussels family benefits. The public operator must also identify children for whom no rights are claimed, examine whether a right is due, and if applicable, pay the relevant family benefit accordingly. Various obligations, particularly of a financial nature, are also imposed on the family allowance funds, and rules govern the affiliation to the bodies.

II.  
EXAMINATION OF THE DRAFT  

5.  
Preliminary considerations. Under the principles of transparency and legality enshrined in Articles 8 of the ECHR and 22 of the Constitution, the draft must clearly specify under what circumstances the processing of personal data is authorized, and consequently determine what data is processed, the individuals concerned, the conditions and purposes of such processing, the duration of data retention, and the individuals who have access to it.  

II.1. Purposes and legal bases of the processing  

6.  
Purposes and legal bases of the processing. Essentially, three main groups of purposes focused on family benefits emerge from the draft and justify the processing of personal data. The purpose of identifying the beneficiary children, establishing their rights, and paying those rights to their beneficiaries, hereinafter referred to as the "purpose of executing family benefits," constitutes the primary purpose of the draft. Additionally, there is a purpose of controlling the implementation of the draft and the execution of family benefits, hereinafter referred to as the "purpose of control," and finally, a purpose of approving private family allowance funds, hereinafter referred to as the "purpose of approval."  

7.  
These three distinct purposes require their own analyses, and Article 34, § 1 of the draft cannot generally provide that "the processing of personal data takes place solely for the purpose of executing the missions of family allowance organizations," unless it contradicts the overall framework of the draft (note: it should probably be understood as referring to the processing by family allowance funds and the public operator, see below, points No. 8 and 20).

Purpose of executing family benefits. The purpose of the processing carried out by family allowance organizations, which are their missions, is clearly evident from the overall structure of the draft bill, although it could have been better expressed in the provisions. Violations "of the provisions of this ordinance are investigated, established, and sanctioned in accordance with the Social Penal Code" (Article 35, § 2, paragraph 1, of the draft bill), with the concerned agents having the powers referred to in Articles 23 to 42 of the Social Penal Code (Article 35 § 2, paragraph 2, of the draft bill).

II.2. Data Controllers  

19.  
As a reminder, the data controller is the person, public authority, or any other body that determines alone or jointly with others the purposes and means of processing (Article 4, 7, of the GDPR). And when the purposes and means of processing are determined by national law, "the data controller may be designated or the specific criteria applicable to its designation may be provided by [national] law." In this regard, while Member States can specify the application of GDPR rules in particular areas where they legislate to ensure consistency and clarity of the normative framework applicable to data processing, they cannot deviate from the GDPR or depart from the definitions it establishes. If national law determines joint responsibility for processing, it may also determine the respective obligations of the joint data controllers (Article 26, 1., of the GDPR).  

20.  
In this case, Article 34 of the draft has a certain ambiguity when it generally provides in its paragraph 2 that the data controllers are the family allowance bodies. What it may provide regarding the definition of the concept of data controller is that the family allowance bodies are responsible for the data they process for the purpose of executing family benefits (e.g.: "The family allowance bodies are responsible for the processing of personal data that they process in the
**Notice 166/2018 - 8/15**  
framework for the exercise of their missions referred to in articles…"). This article is indeed located in a  
**"Chapter 4. Obligations,"** which addresses the obligations of family allowance organizations.  

21.  
For the rest, the draft does not specify who is responsible for the processing carried out for control and approval purposes. A priori, the data processed in the context of control seems to fall under the responsibility of the Joint College and IRISCARE (the agents designated within their services being competent under Article 35, § 1, of the draft), just as those processed for approval purposes (the Joint College and the Management Board of Family Benefits of IRISCARE being competent to carry out this mission). It is necessary to clarify the provisions of the draft in order to specify who are the data controllers.  

22.  
In this clarification exercise, the applicant must also identify whether the Joint College, given the power it has regarding processing means established at the level of family allowance organizations (see infra, points 33), could be considered jointly responsible for the processing for the execution of family benefits.  

**II.3. Data processed, data subjects, and data recipients**  

23.  
Purpose of executing family benefits. Implicitly but certainly, the request for family benefits by the beneficiary (Article 27, § 2, of the draft) will contain personal data (not enumerated, however). Article 27, § 5, of the draft also refers to the "data necessary for determining rights to family allowances." Family allowance organizations must "use electronic data from authentic sources" (Article 32, 1°, of the draft), and must also refer to the National Register of Natural Persons to obtain the information referred to in Article 3, paragraphs 1 and 2, of the law of August 8, 1983 organizing a national register of natural persons (Article 33, § 1; § 2, allowing recourse to another source if the necessary information cannot be obtained from the National Register). That said, the provisions do not specify who the data subjects are concerning all or part of this data, even if this information can at least partially be inferred from the subject matter (note: several persons may be concerned, the beneficiary, the child, their father, for example). By also providing that the cost of certain medical assessments related to the granting of certain allowances is covered by IRISCARE (Article 38 of the draft), the draft allows us to understand that data related to health (defined in Article 4, 15, of the GDPR, and constituting special categories of data within the meaning of Article 9 of the GDPR) will also certainly be processed by the family allowance organizations themselves.  

24.  
The draft generally states, in Article 34, § 2, that "the data processed [by these organizations] are personal data necessary for [its] application […]" (Article
Notice 166/2018 - 9/15  
34, § 2, paragraph 2, of the draft). However, this provision merely reiterates the principle established in Article 5, 1., c), of the GDPR (data minimization principle). Should it be understood that it will ultimately be the responsibility of the data controller to determine which of the data mentioned in the previous point are necessary for the processing? In which case, the draft would lack precision.  

25.  
Legal uncertainty also arises from the absence of a formal legal reference, in the provisions of the draft, to the draft family benefits (which, for the record, is not subject to review by the Authority but has been communicated at its request). As has been pointed out, these two drafts are logically and legally linked. In terms of data protection, one of the consequences is as follows: the determination of the data necessary for the fulfillment of the mission of family benefits organizations can only be carried out with regard, at the very least, to the conditions for granting family benefits. Thus, for example, in order for the beneficiary to receive the correct allowance for the eligible child, it will be necessary to verify whether the child is unique, whether they are an orphan, whether the household's annual income does not exceed a certain amount, to determine the relationship between the beneficiary and the child, etc.  

26.  
In other words, in conclusion, particularly by referring to the relevant provisions of the draft family benefits, it is necessary to clarify the provisions of the draft so that the persons concerned (beneficiary, eligible child, others) and the data that must be processed regarding them are clearly identified, unless the requirements of transparency and legality established in Articles 8 of the ECHR and 22 of the Constitution are not met. In the current state of the draft, therefore, the Authority is not in a position to assess the proportionality of the data that will be processed in relation to the pursued purpose. Finally, the recipients of the data are clearly outlined in the context of the draft.  

27.  
If health-related data must be processed, in addition to Article 6, 1., c), of the GDPR which will constitute the legal basis for data processing, the applicant shall ensure that the processing meets one of the hypotheses of Article 9 of the GDPR, and to provide, if necessary, in their draft, the appropriate and specific measures, such as a confidentiality obligation, in order to guarantee the safeguarding of the fundamental rights and interests of the person concerned. In this case, Article 9, 2., g) (important public interest) or h) (management of social protection systems and services) could, in principle, prove relevant depending on the justification provided by the applicant, which will generally need to be sought in the draft family benefits.  

28.  
Purpose of control. In light of the imprecision of the purpose of the control mission established in the draft (see above, points 12-16), the Authority will limit itself to drawing the applicant's attention to the fact that only the data necessary for this purpose can be processed in this context.

Purpose of approval. The draft implicitly but certainly provides that personal data contained in the statutes of the applicant non-profit organizations (Article 5 of the draft) will be processed for the purpose of approval of the funds, as well as certain judicial data (Article 4, paragraph 1, 2°, of the draft, provides that the members of the board of directors of the approval applicant must not have been criminally convicted for non-compliance with social or tax legislation in the five years preceding the approval request). Regarding any potential criminal convictions, the assembled College and the Management Council (IRISCARE) being public authorities, Article 10 of the GDPR does not impose the establishment of specific appropriate safeguards in the draft. The recipients of the data are also clearly outlined in the economy of the draft.

II.5. Data Retention Period  

36.  
The author of the draft must at least determine the essential elements of the data retention period20, in order to comply with the principles of legality and transparency established in Articles 8 of the ECHR and 22 of the Constitution.  

37.  
The authority supports the approach taken in Article 34, § 2, paragraphs 3 and 4 of the draft, which aims at least at the purpose of executing family benefits, and invites the author of the draft to continue this approach, on the one hand, in cases where the limitation period has not been interrupted (probably, extensions of deadlines of five and seven years respectively), and on the other hand, regarding the data processed for control purposes in particular, and for approval as well.  

II.6. International Dimension  

38.  
In its request, the applicant refers to applicable bilateral agreements regarding family allowances21. When asked about this, the applicant confirmed the existence of several bilateral international agreements that he listed. He also explained that information exchanges were carried out, in this context, using paper forms.  

39.  
In this area, the Authority merely reminds, on the one hand, that the GDPR applies not only to electronic data exchanges but also to the non-automated processing of data contained or intended to be included in a file22, which will probably be the case here. On the other hand, transfers of data to a third country outside the European Economic Area constitute processing that can only take place in accordance with Articles 44 et seq. of the GDPR, compliance with which the Authority has not been in a position to evaluate.

points 23-27: regarding the purposes of executing family benefits, the recipients of the data are also clearly evident from the draft's economy;  
•  
points 30-32: the draft rightly pursues the objective of using authentic data sources, from the BCSS network and the National Register, but its explanatory memorandum at least should be clarified in this regard in order to clearly identify which data sources are being requested.  

However, other aspects of the draft call for an unfavorable opinion from the Authority, which invites the applicant to adapt it taking into account the following remarks:  
•  
points 11-16: the purpose of control is not sufficiently determined in such a way that the requirements established in Articles 8 of the ECHR, 22 of the Constitution, and 5, 1., b), of the GDPR are not met;  
•  
points 19 and 21-22: it is necessary to identify the possible presence of joint controllers and to designate who the controllers are for the purposes of control and approval;  
•  
points 23-28: the data processed for the purpose of executing family benefits are not determined by the draft; regarding the purpose of control, only the data necessary for this purpose may be processed, and one of the data recipients must be specified; in this regard, the draft does not therefore meet the requirements established in Articles 8 of the ECHR, 22 of the Constitution, and 5, 1., c), of the GDPR; furthermore, if it is intended to process data concerning health, the draft must take into account the provisions of Article 9 of the GDPR;  
•  
points 34-35: the explanatory memorandum does not mention any reflection on the necessity or not of conducting a data protection impact assessment, whereas the processing for the purposes of executing family benefits and control involves a high inherent risk for the individuals concerned, namely children (Article 35 of the GDPR);  
•  
points 36-37: while the draft rightly provides for retention periods for certain personal data, it only partially regulates this issue, which cannot suffice in light of the requirements of legality and transparency established in Articles 8 of the ECHR and 22 of the Constitution.  

The Authority finally reminds:
Avis 166/2018 - 15/15  
•  
on the one hand, that if a data protection impact assessment were to be carried out, it would be the responsibility of both the applicant, the College in session, and the data controllers within the limits of their respective responsibilities (points 34-35);  
•  
and on the other hand, that transfers of data to third countries outside the European Economic Area may only take place under the conditions provided for by the GDPR (points 38-39).
