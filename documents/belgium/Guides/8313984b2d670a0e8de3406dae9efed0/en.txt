Recommendation 02/2019 - 2/8

I. CONTEXT

1. The Authority received a joint request from the non-profit organization Vlaamse Vereniging van Steden en Gemeenten (Flemish Association of Cities and Municipalities, hereinafter "VVSG") and the non-profit organization Onderwijsvereniging van Steden en Gemeenten (Association of Education of Cities and Municipalities, hereinafter "OVSG").

2. Cities and municipalities have an identical set of regulatory tasks. This also applies to urban and municipal schools. In order to carry out their tasks, it is sometimes useful, or even necessary, to have access to personal data held by federal authorities. This requires the conclusion of a protocol between the data controller providing the data and the data controller receiving the data, in accordance with Article 20 of the LTD. This would concretely mean, for example, that when municipalities need tax information for a regulatory task, each Flemish municipality (of which there are more than 300) would have to negotiate and conclude a protocol with the Federal Public Service (FPS) Finance. The same applies to municipal schools. For the data controller providing the data, this represents a significant workload as it must consult with each municipality separately.

3. The VVSG and the OVSG have developed a procedure to establish general protocols. Such protocols have the advantage that, on the one hand, the data controller providing the data only has to deal with a single interlocutor and, on the other hand, the conditions for obtaining the data are the same for all data controllers in the target group.

4. The VVSG and the OVSG want to know if the Authority can validate such an initiative. They also wish to know if the Authority believes it is possible to conclude protocols in this manner with federal entities.

5. The Authority notes that this issue is not unique to municipalities and municipal schools but is applicable to all entities that perform tasks that are largely identical. Therefore, the Authority finds it useful to express its view on this issue through a recommendation.

II. ANALYSIS

A. General

6. The Authority assumes that the idea of concluding "general" protocols is inspired by the deliberations of sectoral committees that granted "general" authorizations. Entities that were part of the target group covered by such deliberations (for example, the target group "municipalities," the target group "hospitals") could, under certain conditions, request to adhere to this deliberation. The acceptance of this request for adherence constituted permission to use the authorization.

7. The Authority has no objection in principle to the conclusion of "model" protocols. The question that arises is whether this is legally possible and, if so, under what conditions.

8. The protocol referred to in Article 20 of the LTD is an agreement concluded between the data controller providing the data and the data controller receiving the data. Article 20, § 1 of the LTD refers to the conclusion of an "individual" protocol between 2 data controllers.

9. Article 20, § 2 of the LTD seems to confirm this. Under this provision, a protocol can only be concluded after consultation with both the data protection officer (hereinafter DPO) of the data provider and the DPO of the data recipient. These opinions are attached to the protocol. If one of the opinions is not followed, the reasons are included in the protocol.

10. Does this mean that "general" protocols are excluded? The Authority believes that if one simply relies on the wording of Article 20 of the LTD, "general" protocols are not excluded, provided that they are concluded in compliance with the legal requirements mentioned in Article 20, §§ 2 and 3 of the LTD.

B. In practice

B.1. Resolution through "model" protocols

11. Before addressing the issue of a "general" protocol, we examine whether the problem referred to in point 2 can still be largely resolved by concluding "individual" protocols based on a "model" protocol.

12.  
Upon request from a recipient data controller, the supplier data controller concludes a protocol regarding one or more purposes, for one or more data processing activities. When subsequently other recipient data controllers with the same or similar status express interest in concluding a protocol for the same purposes with the same data, the supplier data controller may convert the initial protocol into a "standard" protocol, on which its DPO provides an opinion, and which it then makes available to the interested data controllers. An alternative is that after consultation with a representative of a group of recipient data controllers or some of them, the supplier data controller establishes a "standard" protocol that it then makes available to the interested data controllers, after obtaining the opinion of its DPO. Both solutions fully comply with Article 20, §§ 2 and 3 of the LTD.  

13.  
A concerned recipient data controller may then:  

- verify to what extent compliance with the terms mentioned in the protocol is feasible for them;  
- seek the opinion of their DPO, as required by Article 20, § 2 of the LTD and by Article 39.1.a) of the GDPR.  

14.  
If the provisions of the "standard" protocol do not pose a problem for a recipient data controller and the opinion of their DPO is positive or at least not sufficiently unfavorable, the "standard" protocol may be signed, mentioning the reasons why the DPO's opinion was not followed, provided that the latter has raised objections.  

15.  
This approach has the advantage that:  

- a significant number of recipient data controllers can conclude and sign the "standard" protocol;  
- each recipient data controller is obliged to evaluate all the terms of the "standard" protocol, so as to clearly understand what they are committing to before signing.  

16.  
The fact that the supplier data controller proposes a "standard" protocol does not mean that it is take it or leave it. Belonging to a group of recipient data controllers with a similar status does not exclude specific particularities unique to a specific data controller.  

17.  
If, for example, following the opinion of their DPO, the recipient data controller finds that certain terms of the "standard" protocol are problematic, they may propose adaptations –

which will generally concern a limited number of elements of the protocol – to the supplier data controller. If the latter agrees to the proposed adaptations, the adapted protocol may be finalized in accordance with Article 20, §§ 2 and 3 of the LTD (again after the opinion of the concerned DPOs).  

18.  
Since only the elements of the protocol that require correction based on the specific particularities of the recipient data controller will need to be adapted, this will generally allow for a quick processing. Again, the concerned data controllers do not passively undergo the process. They participate voluntarily, which is positive in light of the accountability principle of Article 5.2 of the GDPR.  

19.  
In the absence of an agreement on the proposed adaptations, the case may be submitted to the Federal Authority Chamber of the Information Security Committee, pursuant to Article 35/1, § 1 of the law of August 12, 2012, relating to the creation and organization of a federal service integrator, which will handle it through deliberation that will establish the conditions under which the data transfer may occur.  

20.  
This means that the processing will take a little longer, but it will result in a decision tailored to the needs of both concerned parties.  

21.  
In conclusion, it can be stated that the situation where the supplier data controller chooses in certain cases to make a "standard" protocol available to a specific group of recipient data controllers is perfectly reconcilable with Article 20, §§ 2 and 3 of the LTD. This ensures an efficient processing of data transfers that does not come at the expense of the involvement of all data controllers. This approach does not exclude occasional corrections or a "standard" protocol adapted according to the specific particularities of a specific data controller. Depending on whether or not an agreement is reached on the adaptations, the investment in time and resources will be somewhat greater.  

B.2. Resolution by "general" protocols  

1. The principle agreement of the supplier data controller is required  

22.  
As already indicated in point 8, a protocol is in itself a convention. In the case of a "general" protocol, this means that the concerned supplier data controller agrees not to regulate the data transfer to a group of similar recipient data controllers through "individual" protocols but through a "general" protocol. When, for example, an association requests from a supplier data controller whether it is willing to regulate data exchange through a "general" protocol for its members, the supplier data controller is not
Recommendation 02/2019 - 6/8  
obliged to follow up on it. If the data processing provider wishes to consult separately with each of the data processing controllers who are recipients to settle the terms of the data transfer, it is their strict right to do so.

2. The "general" protocol, in fact a variant of the "standard" protocol?

23.  
A "general" protocol implies that a person/organization drafts, on behalf of a group of data processing controllers who are recipients, an agreement with the data processing provider. This person/organization must be duly authorized for this purpose, raising the question of the scope of this mandate: does it only involve the drafting of a protocol or does it also include the conclusion of the protocol?

24.  
The Authority believes that the mandate should be limited to negotiating the terms of the protocol with the data processing provider, thus developing a text that, after consultation with the DPO of the data processing provider, is submitted to all relevant data processing controllers for finalization, as provided for in Article 20, § 2 of the LTD. During the development of the protocol, the data processing provider has only one interlocutor, which simplifies matters, but the final conclusion must indeed take place in a "one-on-one" setting.

25.  
In practical terms, this means that from this stage onward, the same processes are followed as for the solution of the "standard" protocol, as outlined in points 13 to 20.

26.  
This aligns with Article 5.2 of the GDPR, which emphasizes the principle of accountability of the data processing controller. This means that the responsibility for compliance with the GDPR rests solely with the data processing controller, given that they make the decision regarding the processing. It is a responsibility that cannot be delegated to anyone else, either de jure or de facto. Consequently, the data processing controller must document their decisions regarding the processing of personal data in order to demonstrate compliance with the GDPR.

27.  
Points 24 and 25 illustrate that a mandate that goes further, thus extending to the conclusion of the protocol, makes little sense given the absence of any added value. In the event that a representative could conclude and sign a protocol for the data processing controllers who are recipients, this would mean, in light of Article 5.2 of the GDPR, that the mandate would need to be very detailed. As soon as the "general" protocol contains provisions that deviate from the mandate or that the latter does not cover, the representative will not be able to sign, and we revert to the process mentioned above in point 25.

28.  
The question arises as to whether such a uniform and detailed mandate for a large group of data processing controllers who are recipients is feasible. It is very likely that they will have differing opinions on a number of points governed by a protocol. To avoid this issue, the future representative could propose a sort of standard mandate. The data processing controllers who grant a mandate in this manner must be well aware that ultimately, they are the ones responsible for compliance with the GDPR, and not the representative. In any case, a standard mandate will not prevent the issue mentioned in point 27.

29.  
There is a significant requirement that cannot be ignored, regardless of the scope of the mandate: under Article 20, § 2 of the LTD, the DPO of the data processing controller who is a recipient cannot be sidelined. Their prior opinion before the conclusion of the protocol is mandatory. They must be able to exercise their advisory function independently. This means that they cannot be pressured or feel pressured to validate the submitted protocol without raising criticism ("this poses no problem for anyone," "this is still easy," etc.).

30.  
As soon as the DPO of a data processing controller who is a recipient makes a remark, the representative can no longer take any action. It then falls to the relevant data processing controller to decide on the next steps: ignore the remarks and sign anyway; initiate a discussion with the data processing provider to adapt the protocol; or, failing an agreement, submit the matter to the Federal Authority Chamber of the Information Security Committee.

B.3. Advertising

31.  
The concluded protocol is published on the websites of the data processing providers and recipients. The aim is to ensure broad visibility. This is an important element of transparency towards the data subject. For the data processing provider and a data processing controller who is a recipient, this illustrates their responsibility. In light of this situation, the Authority advises against the concerned data processing controllers limiting themselves to simply linking to a representative's website that aggregates the protocols of the target group. It happens too often that such links are not updated, and consequently, the protocols can no longer be accessed.

FOR THESE REASONS,  
the Authority,  

draws attention to the fact that, in accordance with the principle of accountability (Article 5.2 of the GDPR), a data processing controller cannot disclaim responsibility, regardless of the protocol concluded.

Recommendation 02/2019 - 8/8

Option 1: a "type" protocol

 The data controller supplier may, after consulting with its DPO, make a "type" protocol available to a target group of recipient data controllers;  
 An interested data controller collects the opinion of its DPO;  
 Effective publication of the protocol/the deliberation on the own website of the concerned data controllers (not via links to other websites);  

Option 2: a "general" protocol

 The data controller supplier is not obliged to agree to a proposal to draft a "general" protocol;  
 The mandate of the person/organization acting on behalf of the target group of recipient data controllers is limited to the development of a "general" protocol;  
 This "general" protocol is made available to a target group of recipient data controllers, after consulting with the DPO of the data controller supplier;  
 The recipient data controller collects the opinion of its DPO;  
 Effective publication of the protocol/the deliberation on the own website of the concerned data controllers (not via links to other websites).
