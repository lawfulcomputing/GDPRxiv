49. First of all, the violations of the processing of the complainant's personal data are constructed, at least partially, artificially by Noyb. 51. All of this indicates that the complainant is artificially creating their own interest (to take legal action) for the benefit of the representative.

The findings are summarized below by the Contentious Chamber in the form of a brief summary of what was established by the Inspection Service in order to outline the general lines of the report, without intending to provide a complete overview of all aggravating or mitigating elements of the report.

Finding 1: The personal data of the data subject is exported to the subcontractor (the second defendant) through the use of the "Google Analytics" feature on the website https://www.flair.be/:  
o  
The Inspection Service made these findings as part of a comparison based on technical findings (during the period following the aforementioned decision 21/2021 of the Contentious Chamber) for cookie parameters between August 9, 2022, and September 15, 2022; on this occasion, it was observed that several adaptations had been made to the cookie banner (such as the addition of a "reject all cookies" button) and that the "consent management platform" Didomi was being used.  
o  
Furthermore, the Inspection Service noted that even in the most recent situation, the "Google Advertising Products" and "Google Analytics" cookies are mentioned among the cookies with partners, and it is referenced that they are initiated by these partners through the "Transparency and Consent Framework" of the non-profit IAB Europe.  
o  
It notably appears from the processing register of the first defendant that "Google Analytics" is defined as "reporting and analyzing visitor and reading behavior (only aggregated anonymous statistics)" and that the data controller relies on legitimate interest for processing.  
o  
Regarding the Google Analytics tool, the first defendant states to the Inspection Service that in accordance with the terms of use of this tool, it "has no choice but to transmit personal data to the United States, if and to the extent that the data can be classified as personal data."  
o  
The Inspection Service refers to several decisions from foreign supervisory authorities that have denounced a similar issue concerning transfers to the second defendant in the United States of America, with the latter as the "data importer."
