I. Facts and procedure  
1.  
On January 10, 2020, the complainant filed a complaint with the Data Protection Authority against the defendant.  
2.  
The subject of the complaint concerns the defendant's use of personal data, including the content of payment transactions, for the creation of models for the "personalized discounts" service. The complainant asserts that the activation of the "personalized discounts" service occurs only after the data subject has given consent for this purpose, but that for the creation of the models on which this service is based and for which the defendant processes personal and financial data of customers, the defendant invokes its legitimate interest, whereas according to the complainant, consent is required for this purpose. The complainant states that he opposed the "tailored information" offered by the defendant. However, his opposition to the processing of personal data in the creation of models for "personalized discounts" was not taken into account. Furthermore, the complainant notes that for technical and organizational reasons, the opposition to the creation of models for "personalized discounts" using his data was only realized one month later. According to the complainant, this implies that the processes used by the defendant effectively prevent opposition.  
3.  
On January 14, 2020, the complaint was declared admissible by the Frontline Service based on Articles 58 and 60 of the LCA, and the complaint was forwarded to the Contentious Chamber under Article 62, § 1 of the LCA.  
4.  
On February 7, 2020, the Contentious Chamber decided, based on Article 95, § 1, 1° and Article 98 of the LCA, that the file could be examined on the merits and informed the parties concerned by registered mail of the provisions as stated in Article 95, § 2 and Article 98 of the LCA. They were also informed, pursuant to Article 99 of the LCA, of the deadlines for submitting their conclusions.  
The deadline for receiving the defendant's response conclusions was set for March 11, 2020, the deadline for the complainant's reply conclusions for March 26, 2020, and the deadline for the defendant's reply conclusions for April 10, 2020.  
5.  
On February 12, 2020, the complainant accepted all communications related to the case electronically, in accordance with Article 98 of the LCA.  
6.  
On February 17, 2020, the defendant requested a copy of the file (Art. 95, § 2, 3° of the LCA), which was transmitted to them on February 25, 2020. The defendant also accepted all communications related to the case electronically and expressed its intention to exercise the right to be heard, in accordance with Article 98 of the LCA.

7. On March 11, 2020, the Enforcement Chamber received the defendant's response in which he invokes a legitimate interest for the creation of data models for the "personalized discounts" service and argues that the creation of these models constitutes a compatible further processing. The defendant distinguishes between "tailored information" and "model creation" in terms of legal basis and the rights of the data subject, with "tailored information" being based on consent with the right of the data subject to withdraw that consent, and "model creation" being based on legitimate interest with the right of the data subject to object. Furthermore, the defendant asserts that the withdrawal of consent in the context of "tailored information" does not extend to the processing of personal data for the creation of models for "personalized discounts" based on legitimate interest. According to the defendant, the complaint is unfounded.  
8. On March 26, 2020, the Enforcement Chamber received the claimant's reply. The claimant argues that the processing of personal data in data models is carried out for a purpose entirely different from that for which the data was initially collected, namely for the execution of the contract that the defendant has with its clients for transaction processing. Therefore, he argues that processing for this new purpose constitutes incompatible processing and that the defendant cannot invoke legitimate interest for the creation of data models for direct marketing purposes for third parties. According to the claimant, there is also no clear distinction between the services "tailored information," "personalized discounts," and the creation of models for these services, such that the transparency and fairness of the processing are compromised. He asserts that the objection to the creation of models for "personalized discounts" is ignored, giving the user the illusion that this right has been exercised, whereas it later turns out that this is not the case. The claimant also finds problematic the duration between the privacy statement of September 1, 2019, and the practical follow-up to the objection, which could only be exercised after the notification of the "personalized discounts" service by letter dated September 21, 2019, adding the additional month required for the defendant to delete the claimant's data. The claimant asserts that during this period, the defendant can create valuable models with transaction data to subsequently offer direct marketing, such that the objection is implemented when the purpose of the processing has already been achieved, making it de facto impossible for the client to object to the processing of their data for this purpose.  
9. On April 10, 2020, the Enforcement Chamber received the defendant's reply. The defendant elaborates on the points as presented in the response. An additional point is raised: the absence of interest on the part of the claimant, according to the defendant, such that the complaint is inadmissible according to him and therefore not only unfounded. Furthermore, the defendant delineates the subject of the proceedings by indicating that the claimant has never activated the "personalized discounts" service and has only objected to the use of his data for the creation of models to offer personalized discounts, such that the legality of the processing in the context of the offering of personalized discounts is not on the agenda.

11. On April 3, 2023, the parties are informed that due to unforeseen circumstances, the hearing must be postponed to May 9, 2023.

15. The defendant primarily emphasizes the absence of any personal and current interest on the part of the claimant. The defendant believes he can deduce this from the fact that the right to object introduced by the claimant was granted before the filing of the complaint and that the claimant's personal data is therefore no longer being processed for the creation of models. According to the defendant, the claimant is not so much denouncing a violation of his own rights but rather, in the name of the general interest of other clients of the defendant, the practices of the defendant and does not aim to preserve his own rights but rather those of other clients.

20.  
Although the GDPR considers the "complaint" from the perspective of the data subject, imposing obligations on supervisory authorities when a person files a complaint (see Articles 57.1.f) and 77 of the GDPR), the GDPR does not prevent national law from allowing other persons than the data subjects to file a complaint with the national supervisory authority. The possibility of such a referral corresponds to the missions entrusted to supervisory authorities by the GDPR. In this regard and generally, each supervisory authority: ensures the monitoring of the application of the GDPR and compliance with it (Article 57.1.a) of the GDPR) and carries out any other mission related to the protection of personal data (Article 57.1.v) of the GDPR).2  
21.  
The Contentious Chamber considers in this regard that Article 58 of the LCA gives any person the possibility to file a complaint, provided that they have a sufficient interest, in accordance with the aforementioned provisions of the GDPR.  
22.  
The condition, however, is that the complainant justifies a sufficient interest. In this regard, the Contentious Chamber emphasizes that based on the documents in the file, it is an undisputed fact that the personal data of the complainant have been used by the defendant to create and train data models on which the "personalized discounts" service is based, which is precisely the subject of the complaint. The defendant's observation that the exercise of the right to object by the complainant has led to an adaptation of the data models so that the current models are no longer based on the complainant's data is, in this regard, absolutely irrelevant. The mere fact that the personal data of the complainant are no longer included in the dataset on which the models will be based and trained, and that the complainant's right to object was granted before the filing of the complaint, does not imply

c) Legal basis  
26. According to the complainant, the use of transaction data from the defendant's clients to create and train models intended to offer personalized discounts for third-party products and services must be considered as processing for a purpose other than the initial purpose of processing transactions, namely executing and recording payments. This leads the complainant to conclude that the defendant uses the transaction data obtained for the realization of the initial purpose for another incompatible purpose.  
27. The Contentious Chamber examines to what extent the defendant can use client transaction data to create data models on the basis of which personalized discounts are subsequently offered.  
28. The Contentious Chamber emphasizes that the processing of client transaction data to create data models constitutes processing for a new purpose, given that it does not appear from any document in the file that from the collection of transaction data, thus at the moment the client relationship begins, information was provided regarding this purpose. Article 13.1.c) of the GDPR indeed requires that before starting processing activities, the data subject be informed of the processing purposes for which the personal data is intended, including the legal basis. The creation of data models for commercial purposes constitutes a purpose that was not communicated to clients, including the complainant, at the beginning of the client relationship. It appears from the complaint that the complainant was not informed of this purpose at the start of the client relationship with the defendant, which the defendant does not dispute either. However, the defendant asserts that the use of transaction data in the data models should be considered as processing that is not incompatible with the initial purposes within the meaning of Article 5.1.b) of the GDPR.  
29. In the defendant's privacy statement, dated February 2, 2017, it is stated that the defendant also uses the transaction data of its clients to better understand its clients and to better serve them for all marketing purposes and commercial purposes, as outlined in the privacy statement. It explicitly refers to the purpose that the defendant pursues in order to operate as a business, as well as to the purpose of direct marketing for the defendant's own banking and insurance activities and also for the activities of the defendant's partners who offer products or services in the banking and insurance sector. On this basis, the Contentious Chamber can ascertain that the reuse of the data

The Contentious Chamber concludes that the complainant entrusted their personal data and transaction data to the defendant within the framework of their contractual relationship with the bank (namely the defendant) that they use, as a customer, for the settlement of their banking operations and that they could not reasonably expect at all that the bank would use this same data, without the complainant being able to oppose it, to create data models that exceed the defendant's banking and insurance activities and are purely intended to offer personalized discounts for third-party products and services.

36. The Dispute Chamber examines to what extent the legal bases as defined in Article 6.1 of the GDPR can be invoked by the defendant to legitimize the subsequent processing of personal data concerning the complainant.  
37.  
The defendant himself invokes legitimate interest, namely the legal basis as set out in Article 6.1(f) of the GDPR, which would allow him to process data that is the subject of the complaint, namely the creation of data models for the "personalized discounts" service.  
38. In accordance with Article 6.1(f) of the GDPR and the case law of the Court of Justice of the European Union (hereinafter "the Court"), three cumulative conditions must be met for a data controller to validly invoke this basis of legality, "namely, firstly, the pursuit of a legitimate interest by the data controller or by the third parties to whom the data are communicated, secondly, the necessity of processing the personal data for the realization of the legitimate interest pursued, and, thirdly, the condition that the rights and fundamental freedoms of the data subject do not prevail" (judgment "Rigas"9).  
39. In other words, in order to invoke the legal basis of "legitimate interest" in accordance with Article 6.1(f) of the GDPR, the data controller must demonstrate that:  
1) the interests pursued with the processing can be recognized as legitimate (the "purpose test");  
2) the intended processing is necessary to achieve those interests (the “necessity test”);  
and  
3) the balancing of these interests against the interests, freedoms, and fundamental rights of the data subjects weighs in favor of the data controller (the "balancing test").  
40. Regarding the first condition (the so-called "purpose test"), the Dispute Chamber considers that the creation of data models in order to offer personalized discounts to the defendant's customers for third-party products and services should be regarded as being carried out in pursuit of a legitimate interest. This allows the defendant to offer his customers a similar service, just like other banks that also grant discounts in the form of cash-backs. The creation of the data model for which the defendant uses the transaction data of customers, including that of the complainant, aims to offer personalized discounts, which makes

A final point raised by the complainant is that he had previously informed the defendant that he did not wish to receive "tailored information," but the facts giving rise to the complaint demonstrate that despite this, the defendant still used his personal data for the creation of models in order to offer personalized discounts from third parties. The defendant demonstrates in the conclusions and in the related documents that there is a clear distinction between, on the one hand, the creation of data models and, on the other hand, "tailored information," which consists of personalized advertising related to services in the banking and insurance sector. This is classic and personalized direct marketing for which the defendant requests prior consent (Article 6.1.a) of the GDPR), based on a transparent explanation of what "tailored information" precisely entails. It appears from the defense and the privacy statement of February 2, 2017, as well as that of February 1, 2019, that the defendant acted in accordance with Article 5.1.a) of the GDPR in conjunction with Article 12.1 of the GDPR. Regarding "tailored information," the complainant has withdrawn his initially given consent. The Contentious Chamber notes that the complainant cannot extend this withdrawal of consent regarding "tailored information" to the "creation of data models for personalized discounts from third parties," which, as stated above, concerns a different purpose and is lawful under the legitimate interest of the defendant, for which the complainant has a right of objection (Article 21 of the GDPR). The creation of data models is therefore a distinct processing operation from "tailored information," which is based on another legal basis, namely legitimate interest (Article 6.1.f) of the GDPR). The withdrawal of consent for "tailored information" cannot be equated with the exercise of the right to object to processing for another purpose, based on legitimate interest, to then conclude that the defendant does not respect the complainant's choice, contrary to what the complainant claims.
