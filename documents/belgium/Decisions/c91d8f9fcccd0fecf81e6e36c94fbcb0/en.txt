I.1.  
The Complaint  
2. On June 15, 2018, the complainants jointly filed a complaint with the Data Protection Authority (hereinafter "the DPA") against the defendant. At the time of the events described in the complaint, both complainants were employed at the school of the defendant.  
3. The subject of the complaint concerns two alleged violations of the provisions of the GDPR, claimed by the complainants, which would involve personal data of the complainants:  
-  
The first alleged violation concerns the publication on Smartschool of a series of minutes from the college, including two minutes from the College of Mayor and Aldermen dated February 12, 2015, and March 30, 2015, which concern the first complainant, in connection with an incident that led to his temporary suspension. The minutes contain incriminating statements from two teachers and a student regarding the first complainant named specifically. At a later stage, an appeal body is said to have annulled the decision of temporary suspension. However, the document containing this decision was not published on Smartschool.  
-  
The second alleged violation concerns the publication on Smartschool of a survey conducted among students regarding the second complainant. The student survey was completed by 48 students and was conducted on December 15, 2017. The publication of the student survey took place on January 22, 2018.  
4. Prior to the present complaint, the complainants exercised their rights, through a lawyer, by addressing the defendant and more specifically the school concerned of the defendant, by registered letter dated May 8, 2018. In this letter, the complainants expressed their grievances regarding the facts outlined above. The complainants also requested that the minutes of the first alleged violation concerning the first complainant be deleted within 5 working days. Furthermore, they put the defendant on notice for the damages suffered.  
5. The school of the defendant responded to this registered letter on May 22, 2018. It stated that the College of Mayor and Aldermen had acknowledged the complainants' letter in a session on May 16, 2018, and had then decided, without prejudice to all rights and without any

They first inquired whether the school director at the time was obliged by the organizing authority to post the minutes of the College of Mayor and Aldermen on Smartschool. II. Motivation  

II.1. The qualification of the defendant as the data controller  
35. Throughout the proceedings, the defendant has identified itself as the data controller and has also always been contacted in this capacity regarding the facts (notably by the Inspection Service). This applies to all processing activities carried out by
Decision on the merits 110/2021 - 10/22  
the school. However, this does not mean that it is entirely clear that the defendant, as a municipality, is in any case the data controller in all matters concerning the municipal school.  
36. In accordance with the legislation (specifically Article 4, paragraph 7 of the GDPR) and according to the consistent case law of the Court of Justice, it is indeed necessary to evaluate in practice whether a specific actor (which may be a natural person or a legal entity, but also a public authority, service, or another body) determines the purposes and means of processing personal data. The European Data Protection Board ("EDPB") has already clarified in this regard that the effective power of this actor over the processing of personal data can, in particular, be inferred from a legal provision, but also from an actual influence that the actor exerts on the processing activity(ies).  
37. Regarding the facts relevant to the scope of the complaint, it appears that the director of the defendant's school - after consultation or not within the school and with instructions that remained limited to the school's staff - decided on his own initiative to place certain documents he had received from the defendant on the Smartschool platform. In this sense, the director of the defendant's school acted without instruction to publish the documents from the defendant. Moreover, the director may have implicitly inferred from the content of the documents that the defendant would have preferred that these documents not be published on Smartschool.  
38. All of this does not imply that the defendant could bear no responsibility regarding the processing related to the facts that occurred within the framework of the complaint. Article 4, paragraph 7 of the GDPR indeed establishes that the purposes and means may be determined solely or jointly with others. This could also be the case if the director did not have an explicit mandate to publish the documents and acted on his own initiative, as the EDPB has also confirmed: "Consequently, an organization can still be responsible for processing, even if it does not make all decisions regarding the purposes and means." Indeed, the fact that an organization, as a defendant, has not given any general instruction or
Decision on the merits 110/2021 - 11/22  
concerning the integrity of the documents it transmitted could place (part of) the responsibility for processing in the hands of the defendant.  
39. Hic et nunc, however, it is not appropriate to analyze and determine the exact roles and responsibilities in this matter, as the Contentious Chamber will proceed to a dismissal of the elements within the framework of the complaint. Outside the framework of the complaint, however, findings have been made by the Inspection Service that explicitly concern the defendant as the data controller. This is why there is no doubt that the defendant acts as the data controller for these elements, including for the activities concerning its school.  
II.2. Regarding the findings within the framework of the complaint  
40. Based on the elements of the file of which it is aware and the powers conferred upon it by the legislator under Article 100, § 1 of the LCA, the Contentious Chamber decides on the course to be taken regarding the file; in this instance, the Contentious Chamber proceeds to dismiss part of the complaint, in accordance with Article 100, § 1, 5° of the LCA, based on the following reasoning.  
41. In the event of a dismissal, the Contentious Chamber must conduct a step-by-step examination and reasoning as specified below:  
-  
the absence of sufficient prospects for a conviction leads to a dismissal for technical reasons;  
-  
a conviction is technically feasible but prosecution is not desirable for reasons of public interest, leading to a dismissal for reasons of opportunity.  
If a dismissal is made on the basis of more than one reason, the reasons for dismissal (respectively a dismissal for technical reasons and a dismissal for reasons of opportunity) must be addressed in order of importance.  
42. In the present case, the Contentious Chamber considers that it is technically impossible to pursue certain elements of the file that are based on the complaint and decides to proceed with a dismissal for technical reasons based on the reasons outlined below.  
43. Based on the findings in the Inspection Service's investigation in this case (see annex), it appears that the Contentious Chamber is not competent ratione temporis, given that based on the facts and grievances raised in the complaint, it emerges that the complaint concerns processing that began before May 25, 2018, and that the processing in question also ended before this date.  

Decision on the merits 110/2021 - 12/22  
44. It is correct that the complaint was initially declared admissible by the Frontline Service of the APD and that an investigation by the Inspection Service also took place subsequently regarding the facts. Nevertheless, the Contentious Chamber has always maintained that it is not competent to conduct enforcement action for facts that occurred exclusively before May 25, 2018.  
45. For the Contentious Chamber to be competent, it is necessary for the GDPR to apply to the processing subject to the complaint. According to Article 4, paragraph 2 of the GDPR, the processing of personal data is: "any operation or set of operations performed or not by automated means and applied to personal data or sets of personal data, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure or destruction."  
46. The processing in this case and their date of incidence can be summarized as follows:  
-  
The first incident: a publication of personal data on Smartschool on November 23, 2017, at 10:58 AM. This is a publication of a series of minutes from the college, including two minutes dated February 12, 2015, and March 30, 2015, which concern the first complainant. The minutes of February 12, 2015, concern information about the disciplinary investigation that was initiated against the first complainant and contain incriminating statements. After the complainant requested the deletion of the minutes, they were removed from Smartschool on May 9, 2018;  
-  
The second incident: a publication of a survey conducted among students dated December 15, 2017, on Smartschool (publication date: January 22, 2018). The second complainant reportedly noticed the publication on the same day and immediately sent an email to the then-director. It appears from a communication between the Inspection Service and the complainants that the survey conducted among students that was published was deleted on the same day.  
47. The Contentious Chamber notes that the aforementioned processing took place before the entry into force of the GDPR on May 25, 2018. Furthermore, it emerges from the findings of the Inspection Service that the processing of personal data related to the two incidents did not occur after the entry into force of the GDPR on May 25, 2018. The publications were indeed removed from Smartschool on May 9, 2018, and January 22, 2018. Based on the inspection report and the documents, the Contentious Chamber cannot find that processing concerning the subject of the present complaint took place after May 25, 2018.

II.3.1. The designation of the DPO and the exercise of the DPO's missions as provided by law (Articles 37 and 39 of the GDPR)  

II.3.1.1.  
Regarding the designation of a data protection officer in accordance with Article 37 of the GDPR  

53. In its report, the Inspection Service highlighted several inaccuracies regarding the identity of the DPO:  
- The complainants' lawyer stated that no data protection officer had been designated;  
- Article 4.2.6 of the school regulation specified that the municipal administration had designated an additional consultant to act as an information security advisor and data protection officer. After June 2, 2019, this provision was removed from the regulation, without specifying the reason for the necessity of this removal;  
- There was also reference to an employee of company Z who was said to have been designated as DPO. Company Z provides, according to its website, "objective advice" through its DPOs.  
According to the Inspection Service, several inaccuracies remained regarding the designation of an employee of the external e-gov support service as DPO:  
- No decision of designation from the College of Mayors and Aldermen has been communicated, so that the DPA could verify from which date the defendant mandated company Z, whether this mission is temporary, and which tasks were or were not included in the service;  
- No registration code has been provided for the registration of the DPO;  
- The DPA has not received further information on the qualifications of the aforementioned employee of the e-gov support service as required by Article 37, paragraph 5 of the GDPR;  
- According to Article 37, paragraph 3 of the GDPR, a data controller may designate a DPO for several authorities and bodies. However, the GDPR requires that the data controller take into account the "organizational structure and size" in this context. The question arises as to whether, in this specific case, the DPO of a municipality with 15,000 to 20,000 inhabitants can also operate sufficiently independently and expertly as a DPO for both two municipal schools and the municipal administration. The processing is carried out
Decision on the merits 110/2021 - 15/22  
in the context of a community of schools with 3,000 to 4,000 students that exceeds the municipality, which can have consequences for the handling of incidents and the more efficient use of resources, including the allocation of teachers.  
54. Regarding the appointment of the DPO, the defendant refers to the awarding of the contract for the e-gov assistance service (document 5 of the defendant's file). Document 6 of the defendant concretely proves the appointment of a DPO for the defendant, as approved during a session of its Municipal Council on November 25, 2019. It appears from document 7 that the defendant submitted a request for the approval of an employee within the e-gov assistance service as a security advisor to the Vlaamse toezichtcommissie voor het elektronische bestuurlijke gegevensverkeer (hereinafter "VTC", Flemish Commission for the Control of Electronic Administrative Data Exchange).  
55. The Litigation Chamber refers to Article 37, paragraph 1, point a) of the GDPR, which states that data controllers are required to appoint a data protection officer when processing is carried out by a public authority or body, which is the case here. It can be inferred from the documents submitted by the defendant that it had not appointed a DPO before November 25, 2019. Given that there has been an obligation to appoint a DPO since the GDPR came into force (on May 25, 2018), it is therefore unacceptable that the defendant only appointed a DPO one year later.  
56. Regarding the publication of contact details and the communication of these details to the supervisory authority, the Litigation Chamber specifies that at the time of the Inspection Service's investigation, the defendant provided no evidence of a registration of the DPO with the Data Protection Authority (DPA).  
57. For all these reasons, the Litigation Chamber finds a violation of both Article 37, paragraph 1, point a) and Article 37, paragraph 7 of the GDPR, given that the mandatory appointment of the DPO was not carried out correctly (as evidenced by the absence of an appointment order or any other administrative decision in this regard) and was also not properly notified to the Data Protection Authority.

II.3.1.2.  
Regarding the tasks of the Data Protection Officer in accordance with Article 39 of the GDPR

58. The Contentious Chamber notes that there were several issues that persist regarding the Data Protection Officer (DPO) and (the possibility of exercising) the performance of certain tasks by this person.  
59. The essence of several issues that the Inspection Service has been able to observe seems to bring us back to the fact that the DPO - or at least the service that de facto carries out the tasks of the DPO - does not receive sufficient resources in this case to advise the data controller in order to take the necessary safeguards regarding the right to data protection that must be provided to assist and support the data subjects in their rights on the one hand, and to avoid violations of legislation related to data protection on the other hand.  
60. It emerges from the findings of the Inspection Service in this regard that the Inspection Service does not find or has not received any indication that the tasks more specifically as defined in Article 39, paragraph 1, points b), d), and e) of the GDPR are being properly performed. There is also no clear agreement, according to the Inspection Service, between the administration of the defendant and the external e-gov support service. Furthermore, it appears from the file that there has been no direct contact between the administration of the defendant and the external e-gov support service. There would have only been structural contacts with the IT manager of the defendant.  
61. Moreover - but without this having any impact on the assessment of the findings of the Inspection Service outside the framework of the complaint - it can be stated here that even the facts that occurred within the framework of the complaint reveal situations presenting a structural approach that is not optimal and concerns for data protection.  
62. Although during the hearing, the defendant rightly points out that the case has a long procedural delay, their statements - and those of their DPO - demonstrate, during the hearing, that the structural shortcomings at the level of data protection still persist:  
a. The number of hours that the external DPO has to perform the tasks required by the GDPR is completely insufficient to meet the requirements of the GDPR. According to their own statements, this amounts to 120 hours on an annual basis for the entire municipality and all its services.  
b. The DPO has (still) little or no contact with the municipal administration of the defendant, not to mention that the municipal administration does not consult him structurally or invite him to provide opinions. However, the defendant constitutes a public authority, to which the legislator explicitly imposes, in Article 37, paragraph 1, point a) of the GDPR, the obligation to designate a Data Protection Officer, indicating that the legislator believes that this function is essential.

crucial in the context of public services, and a DPO must have access to the highest level of management in order to properly carry out their duties.

II.3.2. Findings of the Inspection Service regarding the monitoring, documentation, and notification of incidents (Article 5, paragraph 2 and Articles 32 and 33 of the GDPR)  
65. The obligation for a data controller to implement security measures for certain processing activities—paying attention to the associated risks—is outlined in Article 32 of the GDPR. The obligation to record and notify incidents related to personal data is specified in Article 33 of the GDPR. The responsibility of a data controller is stated in Article 5, paragraph 2 of the GDPR.  
66. In its inspection report, the Inspection Service addresses how (the school of) the defendant handles the two incidents. Given that the Litigation Chamber is ratione temporis incompetent to rule on facts that occurred before May 25, 2018, it cannot rule on the resolution of the two incidents that took place before the GDPR came into effect.  
The Litigation Chamber is aware that the legal predecessor of the DPA (the Commission for the Protection of Privacy, hereinafter "CPVP") had provided, since June 10, 2014, a notification form for reporting personal data breaches (outside the telecommunications sector, as there was already a legal obligation to notify for that purpose). The CPVP had also stated in a press release dated June 10, 2014, that the use of this notification form was "more than advisable." However, the Litigation Chamber emphasizes the optional nature of the notification obligation prior to the entry into force of the GDPR.  
67. The Inspection Service further stated that it emerged from communications with the defendant that during a certain period after May 25, 2018, it was still not recording incidents. The Litigation Chamber considers itself competent to rule on this matter, given that the GDPR indeed applies to these facts. Proper recording of incidents is very important, especially in light of the size of the educational institution (3,500 to 4,000 students) but also in light of the risk-based approach under Article 32 of the GDPR. Indeed, in this case, it also involves the processing of sensitive personal data as defined in Article 9 of the GDPR (e.g., data concerning health (mental) and transgressive sexual behaviors, ...). The processing also concerns data of vulnerable individuals (namely minors) and involves data processing that entails an increased risk for the individuals concerned.

II.4. Action Plan  
75. Through this decision, the Litigation Chamber orders the defendant to draft an action plan to bring its processing activities into compliance with the GDPR. This action plan must be submitted to the Data Protection Authority within three months of the notification of this decision.  
76. The Litigation Chamber has noted several violations of the GDPR above. An action plan that brings the processing activities into compliance with the legislation, following this decision, must at least address the following aspects:  
a. Regarding the violations of Article 37, paragraph 1, point a) and Article 37, paragraph 7 of the GDPR: the manner in which the DPO is designated, how this designation is monitored, and the allocation of responsibility for notifying this DPO to the DPA;  
b. Regarding the violation of Article 39, paragraph 1 of the GDPR: the analysis of an adequate and appropriate framework for the DPO, concerning the exercise of their duties for the defendant, taking into account in particular the time budget of this DPO for the specific activities of the defendant and its services. Furthermore, it must also examine how adequate direct access can be granted to the DPO at the highest decision-making level, if necessary within the framework of reporting or advisory duties so that these duties can be carried out properly;  
c. Regarding the violation of Article 5, paragraph 2 of the GDPR: drafting internal policies to adequately grant access to the Data Protection Authority and its services when legally required, with particular attention to cooperation in the event of personal data breaches;  
d. Regarding the violation of Article 13 of the GDPR: drafting policies that serve to properly fulfill the information obligation, with structural policies regarding the periodic monitoring of statements (data protection or privacy) and other related documents, as well as ensuring the quality of the content of the statements and other documents.
