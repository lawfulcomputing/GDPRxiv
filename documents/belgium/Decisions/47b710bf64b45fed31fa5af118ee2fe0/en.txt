II. Conditions of the settlement and reasoning for rejected requests  
II.1. General considerations  
19.  
During the settlement procedure, no changes were made to the conditions for the settlement formulated in the settlement proposal; the conditions included in the settlement proposal apply by virtue of the settlement. They are not reiterated in this section and are displayed in the annex to this decision.  
20. The Dispute Chamber further clarifies that it does not consider the agreement to a settlement proposal as an admission, which could particularly be used as an aggravating circumstance in determining the sanction for future proceedings before the Dispute Chamber.  
21.  
The Dispute Chamber reaffirms that the documents submitted by the settling party are sufficient for what was requested under the first and second conditions, regarding the provision of a “clear document” that reflects the technical adjustments.  
22.  
The Dispute Chamber clarifies that, in light of the adequate procedural course, the settlement procedure has been divided into a settlement proposal on one hand, and a settlement decision on the other. This approach ensures that the procedural starting and ending points are clear for all parties involved, including the possibilities for appeal in these phases through the addition of the appeal clauses. The issuance of a settlement decision also facilitates the transparency of the settlement procedure, as the Dispute Chamber unequivocally opts for such transparency.  
23.  
Finally, the present decision is a prima facie decision taken by the Dispute Chamber in accordance with Article 95 of the WOG based on the complaint submitted by the complainant, in the context of the “preliminary procedure prior to the substantive decision” and is not a substantive decision of the Dispute Chamber in the sense of Article 100 of the WOG.  
II.2. The rejected requests of the complainant  
24.  
The complainant made several requests to the Dispute Chamber, which did not persuade the Dispute Chamber to amend the settlement proposal. The Dispute Chamber will proceed to publish this settlement decision on the website of the Data Protection Authority (see section III of this decision), as also requested by the complainant, which constitutes a separate decision that stands apart from the settlement under Article 95, §1, 8° WOG.  
25.  
The Dispute Chamber explains below why it does not address the other requests. It should be noted that the reasoning is included in the present decision, so that the motives clearly follow within the generally structured procedural economy.  
26.  
First, regarding the first condition in the settlement proposal, the complainant requests “to explicitly add that this condition entails that the ‘opt-out’ option must be provided at the same, first ‘level’ as the ‘agree and close’ option.”  
• The Dispute Chamber rejects this request for the following reason. It is not clear in what sense this request would yield a different concrete result on the disputed website than the current wording in the first condition. The Dispute Chamber considers that the proposed amendment would not bring about an improvement (cf. section c.1. of the settlement proposal) to the stipulated condition.  
27.  
Second, regarding the second condition, the complainant requests “to add that the ‘not less (visually) appealing’ representation includes, but is not limited to, that both the ‘opt-out’ option and the ‘agree and close’ option must have the same size, color, shape, contrast, and location of the button/option.”  
• The Dispute Chamber rejects this request for the following reason. It is not for the Dispute Chamber, nor for the complainant, to determine that buttons must have (exactly) the same properties. This choice belongs to the data controller. It is true that buttons with exactly the same properties clearly stand equal for granting consent. Conversely, it is not excluded that the data controller opts to present the “opt-out” option more attractively or innovates in the use of colors (which, for example, represent essentially equivalent options) that facilitate the data subject in making their choice. The more generally formulated condition in the settlement proposal regarding the “not less (visually) appealing” representation of the choices is therefore sufficient for the Dispute Chamber.  
28.  
Third, regarding the third condition, the complainant proposes the following text amendment:  
“The respondent commits to develop and implement, within a period of one month, a mechanism on the website of [...] that meets the requirements set forth in the aforementioned policy documents and seeks to achieve an equivalent effect; furthermore, the respondent commits to...

The respondent is required to ensure that the withdrawal of consent does not involve more steps (in terms of "clicks") than necessary for granting consent. A permanently visible and visually distinguishable option must be available on all pages of the website to reopen the cookie settings with one click. The technical adjustments made by the respondent in this regard will be documented in a clear document at the time of implementation and sent to the Dispute Chamber and the complainant. The Dispute Chamber rejects this request for the following reason. The complainant has not addressed the data controller based on the elements available in the administrative file regarding the alleged violation of their rights. The Dispute Chamber therefore considers it inappropriate here and now to propose such a condition, let alone to direct an “order” to the settling party. The complainant is, of course, free to address any data controller regarding which the complainant can exercise their rights within the conditions of the legislation.  
30. Fifthly, the complainant requests that an administrative fine be imposed via the settlement “in accordance with Article 83(1) GDPR.”  
•  
The Dispute Chamber rejects this request for the following reason. The imposition of an administrative fine is a power that can be exercised by the Dispute Chamber based on Article 100, §1, 13° WOG. In this sense, no administrative fine intended by the complainant can be imposed at this stage prior to the substantive examination under Article 95 WOG. It is correct that Article 107 WOG indicates that the legislator intended that with settlements—without clarifying whether this can indeed also be under Article 95 WOG—monetary amounts could also be collected, but this is not a requirement for the instrument of the settlement, as already stated by the Dispute Chamber in the settlement proposal. The settlement aims to reach a general solution for the grievances of the complainant without the need for punitive measures. The Dispute Chamber emphasizes the efficient course of the proceedings in this regard.

Since the procedure for the Dispute Chamber of the Data Protection Authority cannot be equated with the procedure of criminal law, "the settlement" as provided by the Belgian legislator under Article 95 §1 2° WOG cannot be equated with the "amicable settlement" from criminal law. The settlement in the sense of the WOG indeed has a sui generis character.

Furthermore, the Dispute Chamber specifies the precise facts framed in time and space that give rise to the settlement proposal (infra). Although the Dispute Chamber, as previously mentioned, does not make a ruling here and now on the existence of infringements, it must rely on the elements present in the file to proceed to the settlement proposal. These are the elements that the complainant experienced as harmful. The settlement proposal thus relates to certain facts within a specific time frame and within a certain (technical) context, whereby facts that exceed this time frame and context do not fall within the scope of the settlement. If a settlement is reached, it will pertain to the elements as presented in the grievances of the complaining party, specifically between the moment of filing the complaint and the date of the formal settlement decision. The contentious period concerns only that between the date of filing the complaint and the date of the formal settlement decision that follows this settlement proposal.

In general, the Dispute Chamber frames the settlement procedurally as follows:

1. The settlement under the WOG is a settlement between:
   - On the one hand, the Dispute Chamber, which commits to terminating the procedure, whereby it refrains from potentially imposing corrective measures,
   - And on the other hand, the defending party, which commits to paying a sum of money and/or complying with certain conditions.
   
2. The settlement fundamentally implies an acknowledgment of the facts by the settling party, but does not constitute an acknowledgment of a violation of the applicable legislation.

3. The scope of the settlement is in all cases limited to the facts and the time frame expressly mentioned in the settlement proposal, respectively the settlement.
