recalls that pursuant to Article 6.1 of the GDPR, the processing of personal data is only lawful if, and to the extent that, it is based on one of the lawful bases listed therein.  
47. This lawful basis must be identified prior to processing. There is no doubt that this prior identification results from Article 6.1 of the GDPR even if it is not explicitly stated therein. It arises from the combined reading of said Article 6.1 of the GDPR and Article 13.1 c) of the GDPR, which stipulates that the data controller is obliged to provide the data subject, at the time of obtaining the data concerning them, with all the information listed in Articles 13.1 and 13.2 of the GDPR, including "the purposes of the processing for which the personal data are intended as well as the legal basis for the processing" (Article 13.1 c) of the GDPR). The implementation of this obligation necessarily presupposes that the data controller has identified this legal basis in advance. This identification of the lawful basis prior to the operationalization of the processing contributes to the respect of the principles of fairness and transparency (Article 5.1 a), 13.1 c) and 14.1 c) of the GDPR) as well as to compliance with the principle of accountability and the obligations arising therefrom (Articles 5.2 and 24 of the GDPR).  
48. The Contentious Chamber also recalls that it is the responsibility of the data controller to identify a single lawful basis on which they base their processing. This requirement also contributes to the principles of fairness and transparency that they are obliged to implement (Article 5.1.a) of the GDPR). Different consequences arising from one or the other lawful basis, particularly in terms of rights for the data subjects, mean that it is not permissible for the data controller to invoke one or the other at their discretion. Generally speaking, by invoking distinct lawful bases, the data controller creates a certain ambiguity in terms of the exercise of the rights of the data subjects.

52. The Contentious Chamber recalls that reliance on the legal basis of Article 6.1. b) of the GDPR is subject to 3 conditions:  
-  
(1) a contractual or pre-contractual relationship exists between the data controller and the data subject;  
-  
(2) the contract must be valid under applicable law;  
-  
(3) the processing of personal data meets the necessity condition, in other words, the data processed is indeed necessary for the execution of the contract.

59. With regard specifically to Article 6.1.b) of the GDPR, the necessity of data processing is first assessed in light of the primary objective of the contract: the data controller must ensure that the provision of the product or service cannot occur in the absence of the implementation of the processing of the data in question. If, on the contrary, the objective of the contract can be achieved without the implementation of specific data processing, the necessity condition is not met.  
60. The necessity of the processing is also assessed in light of the expectations of each party regarding the objective of the contract: the data controller must not rely solely on its own viewpoint to evaluate its necessity in light of this objective; it must also take into account the perspective of the data subject, their understanding of the primary objective of the contract, or how the service has been promoted. The objective must be mutually understood as requiring the processing of the concerned data.  
61. It is therefore appropriate to interpret the necessity condition restrictively when analyzing the legal basis of the contract: it does not cover situations in which the processing is not truly necessary for its execution but rather imposed.
