I. Facts and procedure  
1.  
On March 10, 2020, the complainant filed a complaint with the Data Protection Authority against the defendant.  
2. The subject of the complaint concerns the online publication of the complainant's name, address, and photo on the defendant's website. The defendant obtained the personal data in question from the complainant during an on-site visit to the complainant's home following an energy renovation subsidized by the Province. The defendant, in his capacity as a politician, published the complainant's data on his personal website. The complainant has contacted the defendant several times requesting the deletion of the personal data concerning him. Although the defendant states that he will comply with the complainant's request, the situation remains unchanged, and the complainant's data can still be accessed online on the defendant's website.  
3. On March 12, 2020, the complaint was declared admissible by the First Line Service based on Articles 58 and 60 of the LCA, and the complaint was forwarded to the Contentious Chamber under Article 62, § 1 of the LCA.  
4. On February 15, 2021, the Contentious Chamber informed the parties of Decision 16/2021 of February 9, 2021, in which the Contentious Chamber ordered the defendant, under Article 58.2, c) of the GDPR and Article 95, § 1, 5° of the LCA, to comply with the request of the data subject to exercise his rights, specifically the right to erasure (Article 17.1 of the GDPR), and to delete the complainant's personal data within 14 days from the notification of the aforementioned Decision 16/2021.  
5. In the absence of any action taken by the defendant following Decision 16/2021, the Contentious Chamber decided, on March 10, 2021, based on Article 95, § 1, 1° and Article 98 of the LCA, that the case could be examined on the merits and informed the concerned parties by registered mail of the provisions as stated in Article 95, § 2 and Article 98 of the LCA. They were also informed, pursuant to Article 99 of the LCA, of the deadlines for submitting their conclusions.  
The deadline for receiving the defendant's response conclusions was set for April 21, 2021, the deadline for the complainant's reply conclusions for May 12, 2021, and the deadline for the defendant's reply conclusions for June 2, 2021.  
6. On May 5, 2021, the complainant indicated that he had not received any conclusions from the defendant and that, in the absence of such, he had no opportunity to respond, but that his data was still available online on the defendant's website.  
7. On May 6, 2021, the Contentious Chamber sent a request to the defendant to ascertain whether he wished to submit conclusions and that an additional deadline of one

15.

16.  
On January 22, 2024, the Contentious Chamber clarifies to the parties that the hearing scheduled for January 23, 2024, is maintained. The Contentious Chamber explains in this regard that once seized, it is authorized to examine compliance with the GDPR independently and to ensure its effective application, even though the complaint has been withdrawn by the complainant or has become moot.  
17. The oversight exercised by the Contentious Chamber is not primarily aimed at resolving disputes but constitutes one of the instruments available to the DPA to ensure compliance with data protection rules, in accordance with the provisions of European treaties, the GDPR, and the law of December 3, 2017, establishing the Data Protection Authority. If a complaint is filed and then referred for examination to the Contentious Chamber as an admissible complaint, the Contentious Chamber must assess whether the reported facts constitute a violation of one of the legal provisions that the DPA must monitor for compliance. This oversight also extends to the assessment of violations that have ceased at the time of examination by the Contentious Chamber.  
18. The hearing takes place on January 23, 2024. The parties, duly summoned, do not appear. Although the hearing of the Contentious Chamber was set to still provide the defendant with the opportunity to assert his defense rights, the Contentious Chamber can only note that the absence of the defendant means that he has chosen not to avail himself of this opportunity.  
19. On January 25, 2024, the minutes of the hearing are transmitted to the parties.  
20. On January 25, 2024, the Contentious Chamber also informs the defendant of its intention to impose an administrative fine as well as the amount of it, in order to give the defendant the opportunity to defend himself before the sanction is actually imposed. The defendant has the opportunity to respond no later than February 7, 2024.  
21. On February 8, 2024, the Contentious Chamber notes the absence of a response from the defendant regarding the intention to impose an administrative fine, as well as regarding the amount of it.  
II. Reasoning  
22.  
It emerges from all the facts that have occurred in this case that the defendant has had several opportunities to act in accordance with the GDPR, which, following a request made under Article 17.1 of the GDPR, obliges him to comply with the obligations incumbent upon him under Articles 12.3 and 12.4 of the GDPR. Initially, the defendant received several requests from the complainant to proceed with the deletion, but despite his promise to the complainant to remove his data from the website, the situation remained unchanged. Even when the Contentious Chamber subsequently ordered him to do so through Decision 16/2021 of February 9, 2021, no action was taken. The defendant therefore also did not seize this opportunity to give effect to the corrective measure of the Contentious Chamber and to proceed with the deletion of the data.

23. After the corrective measure imposed in Decision 16/2021 of February 9, 2021, was clearly ignored by the defendant and despite the order to proceed with the deletion, he nonetheless persisted in maintaining the publication of the complainant's data (despite being fully aware of the injunction as evidenced by the file1 and having never denied receiving the decision notifying him of it), the Contentious Chamber now decides to impose an administrative fine that is not intended to terminate a violation committed, but rather to rigorously enforce the rules of the GDPR.  
24. As clearly stated in recital 148 of the GDPR, the GDPR indeed emphasizes that for any serious violation – thus also upon the first finding of a violation – sanctions, including administrative fines, should be imposed in addition to or instead of appropriate measures2. In the recent ruling Deutsche Wohnen3, the Court of Justice of the European Union clarified that an administrative fine could be imposed if it is established that the data controller has committed, intentionally or negligently, a violation referred to in Article 83, paragraphs 4 to 6 inclusive of the GDPR.  
25.  
The Contentious Chamber demonstrates below that the violations of Articles 12.3 and 12.4 of the GDPR in conjunction with Article 17.1 of the GDPR as well as Article 31 of the GDPR committed by the defendant are in no way minor violations and that the fine does not constitute a disproportionate burden for an individual, as referred to in recital 148 of the GDPR, two cases in which one may refrain from imposing a fine. The fact that this is the first finding of a violation of the GDPR committed by the defendant does not in any way affect the Contentious Chamber's ability to impose an administrative fine. The Contentious Chamber imposes the administrative fine pursuant to Article 58.2.i) of the GDPR. The instrument of the administrative fine is therefore not intended to terminate the violations. To this end, the GDPR and the LCA provide for several corrective measures, including the injunctions cited in Article 100, § 1, 8° and 9° of the LCA.  
26.

27.  
First, the nature and severity of the violation are taken into consideration by the Contentious Chamber to justify the imposition of this sanction and its extent.  
28.  
In this regard, the Contentious Chamber notes that the provisions violated pertain to the very essence of the GDPR, namely the rights of data subjects under Articles 12 to 22 inclusive of the GDPR. Both the complainant's request and Decision 16/2021 of February 9, 2021, from the Contentious Chamber aimed to enforce the complainant's right to erasure by the defendant. The complainant's request for erasure was made several times to the defendant, and each time, despite the promise made to the complainant to delete the data, the defendant did not carry out this deletion.  
29.  
Decision 16/2021 of February 9, 2021, in which the Contentious Chamber ordered the defendant to proceed with the erasure, also went unheeded. Despite the binding nature of this decision, the defendant did not respond appropriately, despite the repeated requests from the complainant and the injunction from the Contentious Chamber. The defendant only proceeded with the erasure after the Contentious Chamber scheduled a hearing ex officio in accordance with Article 52 of the Rules of Procedure. The violations of the aforementioned articles give rise to the highest fines under Article 83, paragraph 5 of the GDPR. The rights of the data subject, including the right to erasure as provided in Article 17.1 of the GDPR, and the obligations arising for the data controller as established in Articles 12.3 and 12.4 of the GDPR, are essential elements of the GDPR, such that their disregard by the defendant must be considered serious. The same applies to the failure to comply with the obligation of cooperation as stated in Article 31 of the GDPR, given that the defendant simply ignored Decision 16/2021 ordering the execution by the complainant of the requested erasure, a decision that was therefore made in application of the fundamental rights of the data subject, specifically Article 17.1 of the GDPR, and that despite this decision from the Contentious Chamber, the publication of the complainant's data on the website was maintained.  
30. The duration of the violation is also taken into consideration. The Contentious Chamber notes that the complainant submitted their first request for the deletion of their personal data on the defendant's website on November 18, 2018. This request, however, went unanswered, so the complainant reiterated their request on August 13, 2019, and again on February 18, 2020.  
31.  
Given that the defendant also did not respond to these requests, the complainant filed a complaint with the Data Protection Authority, which, in its Decision 16/2021 of February 9, 2021, ordered the defendant to delete the personal data in question. Once again, no action was taken. The defendant also did not respond to the successive invitations from the Contentious Chamber allowing the parties, and thus also the defendant, to submit defenses.
