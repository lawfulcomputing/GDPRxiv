III.2. Regarding the alleged violation of Articles 12.1, 12.3, 12.4, and 15 of the GDPR  
III.2.1. Complainant's Position  
67. The complainant believes that, in accordance with Article 15.4 of the GDPR as interpreted by the CJEU in its judgment of May 4, 2023, it is possible to obtain a copy of entire documents when such a copy is essential for the data subject to exercise the rights they hold under the GDPR. It is essential for him to obtain a copy of the full content of the correspondence exchanged between the defendant and the Prosecutor in order to understand and, if necessary, contest the processing of his personal data.  
68. Furthermore, the complainant considers that the defendant is not required to provide any assistance to the information, firstly because access to the criminal file has been denied to him by the Prosecutor, and secondly because Article 93 of the Administrative Statute imposes the suspension of the disciplinary procedure as long as a criminal action is ongoing. The complainant also emphasizes that information, in accordance with Article 28bis, §1 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (hereinafter, "C.i.c."), refers to all elements of acts intended to investigate offenses, their authors, and evidence, and to gather elements useful for the exercise of public action. The letter sent by the defendant's lawyer to the Prosecutor does not correspond in any way to an investigative act. Thus, the exchanges of correspondence to which the complainant seeks access do not fall under the information and are not covered by its secrecy.  
III.2.2. Defendant's Position  
69. The defendant believes that the correspondence with the Prosecutor falls within the scope of Article 23 of the GDPR through Article 28quinquies, §1 of the C.i.c., which states that "except for exceptions provided by law, information is secret. Any person called to provide professional assistance to the information is bound to secrecy."  
70. In addition, the defendant argues that the right of access to a copy under Article 15.3 of the GDPR does not entail the right for the data subject to obtain a copy of the original document but only to obtain a copy of the data. She concludes that she is not obligated to provide a copy of the correspondence addressed to the Prosecutor and that the complainant's access request constitutes an attempt to divert the purposes of the right of access established in Article 15 of the GDPR in order to interfere in the judicial information and the disciplinary investigation conducted by the defendant. She adds that a copy of an excerpt from a document or an entire document can only be required when the contextualization of the processed data is necessary to ensure its intelligibility. In this sense, the present case constitutes the best evidence of the complainant's knowledge of the data contained in the correspondence with the Prosecutor.  
III.2.3. Position of the Contentious Chamber  
71. The Contentious Chamber will first assess whether the defendant can legitimately justify her refusal to comply with the complainant's access request based on Article 23 of the GDPR and, secondly, will evaluate the provisions of Article 15.3 of the GDPR and the complainant's right to obtain a copy.  
i. Article 15 of the GDPR  
72. Under Article 15.1 of the GDPR, the data subject has the right to obtain from the data controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning them is being processed. When such data is being processed, the data subject has the right to access such data as well as a series of information listed in Article 15.1.a) to h) of the GDPR.  
73. The Contentious Chamber recalls that the right of access constitutes an essential requirement of the right to data protection, as it serves as the "gateway" that enables the exercise of other rights conferred by the GDPR to the data subject.  
74. Under Article 12.1 of the GDPR, it is the responsibility of the data controller to take "appropriate measures to provide any information referred to in Articles 13 and 14 as well as to carry out any communication under Articles 15 to 22 and Article 34 regarding the processing to the data subject in a concise, transparent, understandable, and easily accessible manner, in clear and plain language [...]."  
ii. Article 23 of the GDPR
Decision on the Merits 02/2025 — 14/24  
75.  
Article 23 of the GDPR provides that, under Union law or the law of a Member State, the application of the rights listed in Articles 15 to 22 and 34 of the GDPR may be limited, subject to the conditions set out in this article. The provisions of Article 23 of the GDPR aim to create a balance between these rights and other legitimate interests in a democratic society. To this end, restrictions on the aforementioned rights are possible provided that the following conditions are met:  
-  
First: the derogation must be provided for by a legislative measure;

The derogation provided by a legislative measure  
76.  
Regarding the first condition, the legislative measure does not necessarily have to be a law in the formal sense but any legal norm that is sufficiently clear and precise, the application of which is predictable for any concerned party suffices38.  
77.  
In this case, the defendant invokes the derogation provided by the C.i.c., a law that provides for a limitation on the right of access through the secrecy of criminal information in its article 28quinquies.  
78.  
Article 28bis C.i.c. establishes a list of acts included in the information and derogating from the right of access, namely "all acts intended to investigate offenses, their authors, and the evidence, and to gather the elements useful for the exercise of public action."  
79.  
In this instance, the personal data that is the subject of the complainant's access request consists of the correspondence between the defendant and the Prosecutor.  
80. The Prosecutor's refusal to grant access to the ongoing investigation file to the defendant based on this article 28quinquies is an indication that this correspondence is not useful for the exercise of public action. On the contrary, it seems to resemble a request for information about the ongoing investigation file, which was refused based on the investigation secrecy of article 28quinquies of the C.i.c.  
81.  
The Contentious Chamber understands prima facie that nothing in the correspondence between the defendant and the Prosecutor pertains to the investigation and concludes that it does not constitute an act as understood by article 28bis of the C.i.c.  
82.  
As a result, the justification for the refusal of the complainant's access request does not satisfy the first condition of article 23 of the GDPR.  
83. Consequently, the Contentious Chamber does not find it necessary to continue its analysis of article 23 of the GDPR and concludes that the access request cannot be refused based on this article.  
iii. Article 15.3 of the GDPR  
84. As a preliminary matter, the Contentious Chamber recalls that a refusal to respond to the request for access to a copy must be based on the result of a balancing of interests exercise as provided for by article 15.4 of the GDPR and not on the grounds that the complainant has not demonstrated that the contextualization of the processed data is necessary to ensure its intelligibility. The Contentious Chamber considers the defendant's interpretation to be erroneous due to a misreading of the judgment of the CJEU39 on which it relies.

85. The GDPR provides under Article 15.3 that the data controller shall provide a copy of the personal data to the data subject. This right to a copy is the primary means of granting access to the processed data.  
86. In the judgment C-487/21 of May 4, 2023, the CJEU held that the right under Article 15.3 of the GDPR implies the right to obtain copies of excerpts from documents or even entire documents, or excerpts from databases that contain, among other things, the said data, if providing such a copy is essential to enable the data subject to effectively exercise the rights conferred upon them by this regulation, emphasizing that "the rights and freedoms of others must be taken into account in this regard" (the Contentious Chamber emphasizes).  
87. Since the right of access is not an absolute right, Article 15.4 of the GDPR provides that the right to obtain a copy must not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others. Therefore, the interpretation of Article 15.4 of the GDPR requires particular caution to avoid unjustifiably broadening the limitations permitted by Article 23 of the GDPR, particularly under Article 23.1.i) which also retains the limitation ground based on "the infringement of the rights and freedoms of others." These limitations are indeed only permitted, notably concerning the right of access, under strict conditions.  
88. Although it may appear to be formulated in absolute terms, Article 15.4 of the GDPR nonetheless requires a proportionate approach. A balancing with other fundamental rights must, in accordance with the principle of proportionality, be carried out on a case-by-case basis by the data controller seeking to rely on it.  
89. In this case, the Contentious Chamber notes that the defendant did not perform a balancing of rights that would justify its refusal to comply with the right to obtain a copy requested by the complainant.  
90. The defendant justifies this refusal solely on the basis of Article 23 of the GDPR, claiming that this request would allow the complainant to interfere with the judicial information and the disciplinary investigation conducted by the defendant. This refusal is found to be invalid, as analyzed above.

III.3.3. Position of the Disciplinary Chamber  
i. Principles of Security and Accountability  
102. The Disciplinary Chamber recalls that, as the data controller, the defendant is required to implement data protection principles and must be able to demonstrate that these are respected (principle of accountability – Article 5.2 of the GDPR).  
103. Based on Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR, personal data must be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security, "including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures."  
104. In this regard, consideration must be given to the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the likelihood and severity of the risks posed by the processing to the rights and freedoms of individuals. Furthermore, the Disciplinary Chamber emphasizes that the reasoned decision is a judicial data for which the defendant must pay particular attention.  
105. The Disciplinary Chamber reiterates that the defendant, still in its capacity as data controller, must implement all necessary measures for this purpose (Article 24 of the GDPR). The Disciplinary Chamber insists, as it has previously noted in earlier decisions against public authorities, that the public sector must generally serve as a model in the measures it adopts to ensure compliance with the fundamental right to the protection of personal data.  
106. Article 32.1 of the GDPR states that when assessing the appropriate level of security, particular account must be taken of the risks presented by the processing, resulting in particular from the destruction, loss, alteration, and unauthorized disclosure of personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, or from unauthorized access to such data, whether accidental or unlawful.  
107. The Disciplinary Chamber recalls that this Article 32 must be read in light of Articles 5.2 and 24 of the GDPR, which impose on the data controller the obligation to demonstrate compliance with Article 32 of the GDPR by taking appropriate technical and organizational measures in a transparent and traceable manner.
