Legal qualification and analysis * prima facie:  
a.2.

In itself, the complainant contests the accuracy of the information within the meaning of Article 5.1.d) of the GDPR. Since the information is related to her in the follow-up plan (at least indirectly because the complainant is the mother of the person subject to the follow-up plan), it may indeed concern personal data within the meaning of Article 4.1) of the GDPR.  
Although the contestation of the accuracy of personal data by the person to whom this personal data can be attributed does not ipso facto mean that the personal data is therefore inaccurate, the procedure has highlighted serious indications at least to doubt the accuracy of the specific personal data.  
More specifically, the school, or its collaborators, could not correctly determine (within a reasonable time) who had added the information to the follow-up plan.  
All of this could be considered in the context where the follow-up plan that led to the complaint and is the subject of this case is part of the "legally mandatory action plan," according to the defendant, who refers to Article 46 of the Decree on Basic Education of February 25, 1997 (M.B. of April 17, 1997). The defendant further explains that the subject of the complaint concerns a "follow-up plan" that falls within the framework of this legally mandatory action plan. The defendant emphasizes in this regard - rightly, according to the Disciplinary Chamber - that this follow-up plan is "not a static document" but "is adapted throughout the school year by the individuals who support the concerned student."  
When the complainant therefore approached the defendant to exercise her right under Article 16 of the GDPR, the defendant was then required, as the data controller, to seriously and adequately examine this request from the complainant as a data subject and, if necessary, to respond accordingly. The defendant states in this case that, in her opinion, it was this right that the complainant exercised when she requested the revision of certain aspects of the follow-up plan. The defendant states: "This was therefore correctly respected. The follow-up plan was properly adapted after the complainant's request in this regard."

Legal Qualification and Prima Facie Analysis:  
The complainant's grievances regarding this specific issue relate to the integrity of personal data within the meaning of Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR and to the security (of processing) of personal data within the meaning of Article 32 of the GDPR, read in conjunction with Articles 24 and 25 of the GDPR.  
It could be problematic that an employee of the defendant could not explain to the complainant how access management was conducted, giving her the (incorrect) impression that access management to the monitoring plans was non-existent, or at least poorly regulated or secured. In practice, however, the defendant has demonstrated throughout the proceedings that safeguards were indeed integrated to ensure adequate access management. In particular, an account on Schoolplatform is required with a unique password for all individuals who have access to the monitoring plan or who have 'write rights' to modify this monitoring plan. Furthermore, through a history of adaptations to the monitoring plan ("logging files"), the defendant demonstrates that only a limited number of individuals could make adaptations and had indeed done so. These included individuals who were part of the monitoring team for the complainant's son at the time of the events.  
In this regard, the defendant asserts in its conclusions that the processing of personal data occurring within the framework of the monitoring plan is indeed subject to appropriate technical and organizational measures "so that security is guaranteed." According to the Disciplinary Chamber, the grievances raised in the complaint must therefore be somewhat nuanced, as it is first inaccurate to say that "everyone" can have access.

III.5. Written and Verbal Exchanges with the Parties  

III.5.1. Written Exchanges  
The transaction proposal constitutes the starting point of the transaction procedure. However, the conditions of this proposal may be specified or adapted during the subsequent course of the transaction procedure, especially if these adaptations bring improvements in light of data protection legislation. Such requests do not automatically imply an extension of deadlines.  

The parties contact the Disputes Chamber in principle in writing when they deem it useful to make certain adaptations to the conditions of the transaction proposal. It is up to the

Contentious Chamber to adapt the settlement proposal based on this communication made by the parties.  

In this regard, a constructive attitude from the parties is encouraged. The Contentious Chamber expects that the requests will be reasonable and proportionate.  
When the requests reveal that the likelihood of reaching a settlement is low, or even non-existent, from the perspective of the Contentious Chamber, it may be decided to withdraw the settlement proposal.  

III.5.2. Verbal Exchanges  
The defendant may address a request for verbal explanations regarding the settlement proposal or other communications to the Contentious Chamber, the assessment of the relevance of such verbal exchanges being left to the Contentious Chamber29.  
It goes without saying that this is a favor, and the Contentious Chamber grants it and can adopt this choice only if it proves effective for the procedure.  

A record will be established and will generally only state the existence of this verbal explanatory exchange.  

The parties are free to participate in the verbal explanatory exchanges organized by the Contentious Chamber. The conversations are confidential. The content of the discussions cannot be disclosed to third parties.  

III.6. Deadline  
The defendant is required to respond to the present settlement proposal before 24/12/2024.  

III.7. Existence of Other Data Controllers and/or Subcontractors  
This settlement proposal is addressed solely to the defendant. No position is taken on whether and to what extent other parties are responsible for the potential violations that led to this settlement proposal.

III.8. Validation of the Settlement  
If the settlement proposal results in a formal settlement decision due to the explicit acceptance, within the aforementioned deadline, of the party to whom the settlement proposal is addressed, an appeal may be filed by the "aggrieved party"30.  

The final settlement does not affect the right of any individuals who have suffered harm to claim damages before a civil court based in particular on Article 82 of the GDPR.  

III.9. Appeal Against the Settlement Proposal  
Pursuant to Article 108, § 1 of the LCA, this decision on the settlement proposal may be appealed to the Market Court (Court of Appeal of Brussels) within thirty days from its notification, with the Data Protection Authority as the defendant.  
Such an appeal may be filed by means of a contradictory petition which must include the mentions listed in Article 1034ter of the Judicial Code31. The contradictory petition must be submitted to the registry of the Market Court in accordance with Article 1034quinquies of the Judicial Code32, or via the e-Deposit IT system of Justice (Article 32ter of the Judicial Code).
