I. Facts and procedure  
1.  
The subject of the complaint concerned the installation of two surveillance cameras that filmed the public road. Outside the scope of the complaint, this decision, taken after an investigation by the Inspection Service, also concerns the processing of personal data by the defendant on its website, given its activities as an erotic sauna.  
2.  
On October 14, 2019, the complainant filed a complaint with the Data Protection Authority against the defendant. The defendant's cameras were allegedly directed towards the public road, there was temporarily no pictogram indicating the existence of camera surveillance, no data of the data controller was completed on the previously present pictogram, and the complainant was never consulted regarding the installation of the cameras. The complainant therefore requested that the cameras be removed.  
3.  
The complaint included an email from the neighborhood officer with the following information:  
• The cameras were requested and installed in accordance with the legislation and were correctly registered with the police.  
• The cameras were not directed towards neighboring houses but rather towards "the sidewalk and the public road along [complainant's street] and towards the sidewalk at the corner of [complainant's street] and [street]."  
• A pictogram mentioning the address and phone number of the controller was indeed affixed.  
4.  
On October 29, 2019, the complaint was declared admissible by the First Line Service based on Articles 58 and 60 of the LCA, and the complaint was forwarded to the Contentious Chamber under Article 62, § 1 of the LCA.  
5.  
On November 8, 2019, the Contentious Chamber decided to request an investigation by the Inspection Service, pursuant to Articles 63, 2° and 94, 1° of the LCA. In accordance with Article 96, § 1 of the LCA, the request from the Contentious Chamber to conduct an investigation was transmitted to the Inspection Service, along with the complaint and the inventory of documents.  
6.  
On March 6, 2020, the investigation by the Inspection Service was concluded, the report was attached to the file, and it was transmitted by the inspector general to the president of the Contentious Chamber (Article 91, § 1 and § 2 of the LCA).  
The report contained findings related to the subject of the complaint and concluded that:  
1. Y was the data controller for the processing of personal data carried out through the installed cameras;

II. Motivation  
II.1. Description of the processing  
19.  
The initial complaint concerned the processing of images from two surveillance cameras installed on the exterior façade of the defendant's building for the purpose of "preventing further damage to our building and public properties." These two cameras were properly requested and declared to the local police. During the hearing, it was revealed that a third camera was installed in the access vestibule of the building, directed at the entrance of the building.  
20. An erotic sauna was operated in the building. The website of the erotic sauna included a privacy statement as well as a guestbook.  
II.2. The complaint and alleged violations  
21.  
Article 58 of the LCA states: "Any person may file a complaint or a written request, dated and signed, with the Data Protection Authority. [...]". In accordance with Article 60 of the LCA, a complaint is admissible when it:  
a. is drafted in one of the national languages;  
b. contains a statement of facts and the necessary indications to identify the processing to which it relates;  
c. falls within the jurisdiction of the Data Protection Authority.  
22.  
The Contentious Chamber has already issued the following considerations in a previous decision:  
"Although the GDPR considers the 'complaint' from the perspective of the data subject, imposing obligations on supervisory authorities when a person files a complaint (see Articles 57, 1.f) and 77 of the GDPR), the GDPR does not prevent national law from allowing other persons than the data subjects to file a complaint with the national supervisory authority. The possibility of such a referral corresponds to the missions assigned to supervisory authorities by the GDPR. In this regard and generally, each supervisory authority: ensures the enforcement of the GDPR and compliance with it (Article 57.1.a) of the GDPR) and carries out any other mission related to the protection of personal data (Article 57.1.v) of the GDPR)."

II.3. Determination of the Data Controller  
29.  
In accordance with Article 4(7) of the GDPR, the data controller is to be understood as "the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing."  
30. The Court of Justice has repeatedly interpreted the concept of "data controller" broadly in its case law to ensure effective and comprehensive protection of the data subjects.  
31.  
According to the EDPB Guidelines 07/2020, the quality of the relevant data controller(s) must be concretely assessed. Furthermore, according to these same guidelines, the concepts of "purposes" and "means" must be examined together in an inseparable manner, and it is necessary to establish who determines the ‘why’ (the purposes) and the ‘how’ (the means) of the processing.  
32.  
The Inspection Service found, based on the data in the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises, that the defendant was the data controller for the images from the building's cameras. During the hearing, it became apparent that the defendant considered itself to be the data controller for the images from the cameras.  
33. However, the defendant does not operate the erotic sauna itself. For this activity, it leases the building to the actual operators. Regarding the website and the guestbook, the defendant therefore believes that the operators of the erotic sauna should be considered the data controllers. In the conclusions following the hearing, during which additional questions were raised regarding the processing of personal data via the guestbook, the defendant explains that the privacy statement specifies that [C.S.], in this case the operator of the erotic sauna, is the ‘data processor’ for the personal data related to the website and the guestbook.

38. Based on these measures, in accordance with Article 100, § 1, 2° of the LCA, the Contentious Chamber orders the dismissal regarding the processing of images from the cameras. Article 4, § 1 of the LCA states: "The Data Protection Authority is responsible for monitoring compliance with the fundamental principles of personal data protection, within the framework of this law and laws containing provisions relating to the protection of personal data processing." At the time the cameras are unplugged, they no longer process personal data and the DPA ceases to have jurisdiction. The Contentious Chamber is not competent to order the removal of the unplugged cameras, as the complainant demands in her conclusions, since the cameras in question no longer process personal data.  
II.5. Regarding the register of processing activities  
40. The Inspection Service found that the data controller could only submit the register of processing activities related to images and not the register of processing activities as defined in Article 30 of the GDPR. For this reason, the Inspection Service finds a violation of Articles 30 and 31 of the GDPR.  
41. The defendant indicated a misunderstanding regarding the register of processing activities and clarified that this misunderstanding arose because she was convinced that no personal data was being processed.  
42. The Contentious Chamber notes that the defendant has two narratives. On one hand, she is convinced that she does not process any personal data and cannot or should not have a register of processing activities; on the other hand, she specifies in the privacy statement that the name (preferably an alias or a diminutive), the municipality, and the email address of the data subjects are processed so that they can be contacted by email and complete the online guestbook. The guestbook itself may also contain data that could lead to the identification of a client of the erotic sauna.  
43. The Contentious Chamber believes that the defendant has not maintained a register of processing activities and has thus violated Article 30 of the GDPR.

II.6. Regarding the privacy statement on the defendant's website  
44. In its investigation outside the scope of the complaint, the Inspection Service assessed the privacy statement on the defendant's website on November 13, 2019. Following its intervention, the Inspection Service found that the privacy statement had been adapted by the defendant. On February 27, 2020, the Inspection Service evaluated this new privacy statement.  
45. The inspection report established, on one hand, that the privacy statement was not transparent and understandable, as several terms were not explained and it was stated that only the customer section manager and the manager had access to the guestbook, while this guestbook is publicly accessible on the website. On the other hand, mandatory information under Article 13 or 14 of the GDPR was missing, notably concerning the identity of the data controller, the retention periods for personal data, the purposes of processing, the legal basis for processing, and the rights of the data subjects.  
46. The defendant emphasized the discretion that accompanies the nature of her activities and thus the limited processing of personal data. Furthermore, the privacy statement was adapted again on June 12, 2020. With this new adaptation, the defendant believed she had remedied the shortcomings mentioned in the inspection report.  
47. The Contentious Chamber notes that the privacy statement of June 12, 2020 includes the identity of the data controller (or its representative), retention periods, and the rights of the data subject. The manner in which a data subject can exercise their rights was also explained. However, the Contentious Chamber finds no mention of the legal basis on which the processing of personal data is based.  
48. The Contentious Chamber therefore concludes that the defendant has violated Article 13.1.c) of the GDPR due to the absence, in the privacy statement, of the legal bases for the processing of personal data by the defendant.  
II.7. Regarding the guestbook on the defendant's website  
49. In her summary conclusions, the defendant mentioned the existence of a guestbook on the website of her erotic sauna. During the hearing, the defendant stated that it was not possible to respond in the guestbook. The defendant's aim was to process as little personal data as possible. In her conclusions after the hearing, the defendant reviewed the measures taken in this regard concerning the guestbook. Clients

are encouraged to use an alias and not to include identity data in their message. When this does occur, the defendant may intervene by not publishing the message or by deleting it immediately.  
50. The Contentious Chamber notes that the website had a guestbook in which clients of the erotic sauna could leave messages. Given the nature of the defendant's activities, the personal data in this regard are data related to the behavior or sexual orientation of a person, as specified by the president of the Contentious Chamber during the hearing, referring to potential images from cameras of clients. Following the intervention of the Inspection Service, the email address of the client who posted a message was deleted; after the hearing, the website emphasized the use of an alias and the avoidance of personal data. The consultation of the public guestbook informs the Contentious Chamber that it is generally first names, initials, or aliases that are used, often in combination with the municipality and possibly with personal remarks that could lead to the specific client in question.  
51.  
After the hearing, the Contentious Chamber requested additional conclusions regarding this guestbook, particularly on the assurance that this guestbook complied with the principles of Articles 5 and 6 of the GDPR. The defendant's limited response contained no indication of a legal basis for this processing, nor any grounds for an exception to the processing prohibition of Article 9.1 of the GDPR. However, Article 5.2 of the GDPR clearly establishes that the defendant, as the data controller, must be able to demonstrate compliance with Article 5.1, specifically the lawfulness of processing under Article 5.1.a) of the GDPR. The Contentious Chamber finds that the defendant has violated Articles 5.1.a), 6.1, and 9.2 in conjunction with Article 5.2 of the GDPR.

Regarding the processing of personal data through surveillance cameras, the Contentious Chamber orders a dismissal since, following the measures taken by the data controller, the subject of the complaint no longer exists.  
54.  
It is up to the Contentious Chamber to decide on the most appropriate sanction in light of the violations of Articles 5.1.a), 6.1, and 9.2, in conjunction with Article 5.2, Article 13.1.c), and Article 30 of the GDPR and all the circumstances of the case at hand.  
55.  
The Contentious Chamber is mindful of the fact that the defendant has already made improvements to its data protection policy during the proceedings, that the defendant is actively trying to minimize the processing of personal data, and that this decision has been pending for four years.  
56.  
In these circumstances, the Contentious Chamber warns the defendant, pursuant to Article 100, § 1, 5° of the LCA, that:  
• given its activities, it can only lawfully process personal data based on a legal basis under Article 6.1 of the GDPR in conjunction with an exception under Article 9.2 of the GDPR and must be able to demonstrate this in accordance with Article 5.2 of the GDPR,  
• the legal basis for the processing must be clear to the data subject whose personal data it processes in accordance with Article 13.1.c) of the GDPR,  
• it must at all times maintain a complete record of processing activities and be able to present it to the DPA in accordance with Article 30 of the GDPR.
