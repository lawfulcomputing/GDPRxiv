II. Motivation  
II.1. Purpose of the decision  
18.  
The complaint concerns the fact that the complainant had to accept the general terms of the Z platform in order to create an account on the platform, on the one hand, and the fact that his personal data was processed while he wished to register his son, on the other hand. These aspects were addressed in detail during the hearing.  
19.  
The Inspection Service found various violations of the GDPR and applicable cookie legislation. In order to ensure effective enforcement of the complainant's rights, the Contentious Chamber decides not to address all the findings of the Inspection Service that exceed the subject of the complaint. However, this does not detract from the fact that it is the defendant's responsibility to take measures, if necessary, to fully comply with all obligations arising from the GDPR.  
II.2. Identification of the data controller  
20. The question arises as to who should be designated as the data controller for the processing activities on the Z platform.  
21.  
In its conclusions, the defendant argues that a distinction must be made between two relevant periods during which processing activities take place: on the one hand, from the creation of an account on Z as long as the account of the concerned person is not yet linked to a specific school or academy, and on the other hand, as soon as the data of the concerned person's account is linked to one or more schools/academies, so that the school or academy can, among other things, manage registrations, but can also communicate with parents and students.  
22.  
The defendant explains that for the first period of processing activities, therefore between the creation of the account and the linking to an academy, he is the data controller because he decides that if a person wants to access the Z platform, an account must be created. The defendant determines which data is requested, and he does not do this at the request of the school or academy. These processing activities are based on Article 6.1.b) of the GDPR, according to the defendant.  
23.  
As soon as the account is linked to the academy or school, therefore during the second period of processing activities, the defendant's position shifts from data controller to processor. Consequently, the defendant could not respond himself to the complainant's request for deletion since at that time he was not the data controller for these processing activities.
Decision on the merits 51/2025 — 5/19  
24.  
During the hearing, the defendant reiterates this viewpoint. The defendant specifies in this regard that a subcontracting contract has been concluded with the organizing authority of the school for 165 academies. There are slightly fewer than 165 subcontracting contracts because, for example, the academy of drawing and music in the same municipality falls under a single organizing authority, namely the municipality, and therefore, a single subcontracting contract suffices. The defendant also emphasizes that the database containing the registration data in the relationship between the school and the student is distinct from the database of platform Z (communication platform with students and parents of the academies), which serves only as a relay. The personal data for which the defendant is responsible for processing on one hand and subcontractor on the other can thus be separated.  
25.  
It is only for the login data to the account on platform Z that the defendant is responsible for processing. As soon as the data subject logs in to access an academy, the data subject provides the legally necessary registration data to register with an academy. For this data, it is the academy that is the data controller. The defendant also indicates that in the case of choosing a new school, the data subject must again complete a full registration of the student via the existing account. The defendant expressly states that there is no exchange of personal data between the academies. Registration is done by academy.  
26.  
The Contentious Chamber follows this explanation and concludes, in light of these elements, that the defendant is responsible for processing only for the first processing mentioned, namely the creation of the account. Once the account is linked to a school or an academy, that school or academy becomes the data controller and the defendant becomes a subcontractor.  
27.  
The complaint concerns the mandatory acceptance of the general terms and conditions on the defendant's platform to create an account, thus during the period when the defendant acts as the data controller since the account has not yet been linked to a school or an academy.  
II.3. Lawfulness of processing (Articles 5.1.a), 5.2, and 6.1 of the GDPR)  
28.  
The complainant criticizes the fact that he must accept the defendant's general terms and conditions to enroll his son in the academy. The Contentious Chamber will assess the lawfulness of this.  
29.  
The complainant states in his complaint and in his conclusions that he does not agree with the obligation to accept the defendant's general terms and conditions in order to enroll his son in the academy. He asserts that the enrollment of his son in an academy cannot be
Decision on the merits 51/2025 — 6/19  
conditional upon the mandatory transmission of his own personal data to another data controller other than the academy itself. Its purpose is indeed to enroll his son in the academy so that he can attend classes.  
30. Regarding the mandatory acceptance of the defendant's general terms for online registration, the Inspection Service finds that there is no question of a violation. A contract is indeed concluded between the data controller (in this case, the defendant) and the user of Z (in this case, the complainant), and the processing of personal data is necessary for this purpose.  
31.  
The defendant also argues that the acceptance of general terms as a condition for the conclusion of a contract does not constitute a violation of the GDPR.  
32.  
The Contentious Chamber notes, as indicated by the defendant and as also found by the Inspection Service, that the complainant accuses the defendant of requiring him to accept the defendant's general terms for the enrollment of his son in the academy of his choice, and that his own personal data is processed for this purpose by the defendant.  
33. The Contentious Chamber observes that the academy in which the complainant wishes to enroll his son, like all other academies, associations, and federations, must ensure the management of members as well as the processing of administration. They may choose to carry out these tasks themselves or to engage a specialized company such as that of the defendant for this purpose. The academy in which the complainant wishes to enroll his son uses the services of the defendant for these tasks, who has developed and manages the platform Z. The defendant thus offers its services in member management and administrative processing to academies that wish to use them. New members must first create an account on Z, which can then be linked to the academy (or academies) in which they wish to enroll. The defendant has also explained that it acts as the data controller for the creation of the account, as it determines the purpose and means of this processing (i.e., the creation of the account), which is also not disputed. Once the account is linked to an academy, the defendant acts as a processor on behalf of that academy, which then acts as the data controller.  
34. As explained by the defendant in its email of June 15, 2021, and in its conclusions, it is the data controller for the processing of personal data in the context of the creation of the account, before the account is linked to the relevant academy. It follows that if a data subject, as a new member of an academy, wishes to register online, and therefore creates an account on Z, they conclude a contract with the defendant. Article 6.1.b) of the GDPR provides a legal basis for the processing of personal data if "the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the
Decision on the merits 51/2025 — 7/19  
The data subject is a party or to the execution of pre-contractual measures taken at their request.1  
35. Contractual obligations towards a data subject may sometimes not be fulfilled without the data subject providing certain personal data.2 If the specific processing is part of the provision of the requested service, it is in the interest of both parties to process this data, as without it, the service cannot be provided and the contract cannot be executed. As required by Article 6.1(b) of the GDPR, the processing must be objectively necessary for the performance of a contract. This is the case in the present matter. It is indeed necessary to process the relevant personal data (i.e., identity data and contact details) when executing services related to member management. The processing of this data allows the defendant to carry out its member management activities so that the academy in question can identify the registered members and contact them via platform Z. In this case, the processing of personal data is part of the contract, given that without the processing of personal data, no account can be created to be subsequently linked to the academy in question. Furthermore, the Contentious Chamber emphasizes that the acceptance of general terms and conditions as a necessary condition for the conclusion of a contract, as in this case the use of Z, does not in itself constitute a violation of the GDPR.  
36. As also noted by the Inspection Service and as highlighted by the defendant, the Contentious Chamber draws attention to the possibility for any data subject to register ‘offline’, thus via the secretariat of the academy in question. In this way, registration via Z can be avoided if the data subject wishes.  
37.  
Regarding the complainant's accusation that their personal data is also processed while they wish to register their son, and thus would not have a direct link with the academy, the Contentious Chamber notes that the complainant is the father of the student. The Contentious Chamber assumes that at the time of registration, the son was a minor and therefore could not validly conclude a contract himself. Furthermore, the decision to enroll the son in the academy in question falls under the parental authority of the complainant. Consequently, the defendant must also process the personal data of the parent(s) of minor students in order to be able to execute its service.

II.4. Articles 12.1, 13.1, 13.2, 14.1, 14.2, 5.2, 24.1, and 25.1 of the GDPR  
II.4.1. Findings of the Inspection Service  
39. In execution of the principle of transparency of Article 5.1.a) of the GDPR, it is necessary, based on Article 12.1, Article 13.1, Article 14.1, and Article 14.2 of the GDPR, that the data controller, in this case the defendant, provides the data subjects with concise, transparent, and understandable information about the personal data being processed. As the data controller (for the creation of the account before linking to an academy), the defendant must comply with Articles 12, 13, and 14 of the GDPR.  
40. The Inspection Service finds that the defendant's privacy statement is not transparent and understandable for the data subjects, which is not in compliance with Article 12.1 of the GDPR. In this regard, the Inspection Service refers to the following elements: the privacy statement does not include a table of contents with links allowing data subjects to click to reach the relevant part of the text titled "Privacy Policy." This method is not transparent in the eyes of the Inspection Service and furthermore, it does not facilitate the rights of the data subjects. Next, the Inspection Service notes that the privacy statement is not clear for the data subjects, as it does not specify how the data subjects can ascertain what changes have been made and when they occurred. This also does not facilitate the rights of the data subjects. Finally, the Inspection Service emphasizes that the data subjects are not adequately informed about how they can file a complaint and what they can expect from such a process.  
41. The Inspection Service further finds that the defendant's privacy statement is also incomplete, as not all information that must be mentioned under Articles 13 and 14 of the GDPR is included. The Inspection Service arrives at this conclusion based on the following findings. First, the Inspection Service observes that the privacy statement does not contain information about who the actual recipients of the personal data are or the categories of these recipients, as required by Article 13.1.e) of the GDPR and Article 14.1.e) of the GDPR. According to the Inspection Report, the privacy statement also does not contain information on whether the data subject is obliged to provide the personal data or the possible consequences if such personal data is not provided (Article 13.2.e) of the GDPR). Second, the Inspection Report notes that the privacy statement incorrectly states that the data subject only has the right to file a complaint with the competent supervisory authority after first notifying the defendant, which would not be in compliance with Article 13.2.d) of the GDPR and Article 14.2.e) of the GDPR. Under Article 77.1 of the GDPR, the data subject may contact the supervisory authority in the Member State where they usually reside, where they work, or where the alleged violation has occurred. The Inspection Service therefore asserts that the reference to the website of the DPA can be maintained, but that it should also refer to other supervisory authorities.  
II.4.2. Position of the Complainant  
42. In its conclusions, the complainant asserts that the layered structure of the adapted general terms and conditions is not clear as it does not provide a clear overview of the personal data being processed.

The defendant assumes that the purpose of a table of contents could be to allow data subjects to navigate more quickly through the privacy statement. The defendant highlights that (its old) privacy statement does not require a table of contents.

II.4.4. Assessment of the Contentious Chamber  
47. The Contentious Chamber finds that the Inspection Service establishes a violation of Article 12.1 of the GDPR, because the privacy policy would contain irrelevant and inaccurate information, namely the absence of a table of contents, a lack of version management, and the absence of information on how data subjects can file a complaint and what they can expect from such a process.  
48. Article 12.1 of the GDPR requires that information be concise, transparent, understandable, and easily accessible, and that clear and plain language be used. The requirement that the provision of information to data subjects and communication with them must be done "in a concise, transparent, understandable, and easily accessible form" means that data controllers must present information/communications effectively and succinctly to avoid information fatigue. In an online context, the use of a layered privacy statement/communication will allow a data subject to navigate directly to the specific part of the privacy statement/communication they wish to read, instead of having to sift through long sections of text in search of specific topics.  
49. Regarding the absence of a table of contents, the Contentious Chamber notes that this is not a formal requirement of a privacy statement. It is always necessary to analyze on a case-by-case basis whether a privacy statement meets the requirements of Article 12.1 of the GDPR. In the present case, the Contentious Chamber finds that the former privacy statement consists of five pages and that intermediate headings and short, clear paragraphs were used, allowing the data subject to easily find the information sought. According to the Contentious Chamber, a table of contents would not provide essential added value concerning the requirements of Article 12.1 of the GDPR in this specific case.  
50. Furthermore, the Inspection Service found that the former privacy statement was unclear regarding potential changes and that there was no clear version management intended to facilitate the rights of data subjects. The Contentious Chamber recalls that the GDPR does not impose a requirement regarding when notifications (and methods) of changes to the provided information must be made.

II.5. Article 17 of the GDPR: Right to erasure  
61. Article 17.1 of the GDPR states that the data subject may obtain from the controller the erasure, without undue delay, of personal data concerning them and that the controller is obliged to erase such personal data without undue delay when the personal data are no longer necessary in relation to the purposes for which they were collected or otherwise processed.  
62. In accordance with Article 12.3 of the GDPR, the controller provides the data subject with information on the measures taken in response to a request made under Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR, without undue delay and in any event within one month of receiving the request. If necessary, this period may be extended by 2 months, taking into account the complexity and number of requests. The controller informs the data subject of this extension within one month of receiving the request. If the controller does not act on the request made by the data subject, they shall inform the data subject without delay and at the latest within one month of receiving the request of the reasons for their inaction (Article 12.4 GDPR).
Decision on the Merits 51/2025 — 16/19  
63. On June 7, 2021, the complainant requested the defendant to delete their personal data. On the same day, the defendant responded to the complainant's request and subsequently clarified this response on June 15, 2021. The defendant asserts that it responded to the complainant's request within the prescribed one-month period, in accordance with Article 12.4 of the GDPR, by indicating that it could not comply with the request, as it was not acting as the data controller for this processing. Regarding the management of registrations, the relevant academy is the data controller, given that the account is linked to the academy. The defendant specifies that its only role in the management of registrations is the processing of personal data at the request of the academy. Therefore, the defendant acts as a processor in this case. The defendant then informed the complainant that they should direct their deletion request to the academy so that the account could be dissociated. Once the account was dissociated, the defendant would have the ability to delete the personal data for which it is the data controller.  
64. The Contentious Chamber notes that Article 17.1 of the GDPR stipulates that the data controller must delete personal data at the request of the complainant. Since the defendant was not the data controller of personal data at the time of the request, the defendant cannot be accused of failing to proceed with the deletion. The defendant also informed the complainant within the one-month period prescribed by Article 12.4 of the GDPR regarding the reasons why it could not comply with the deletion request.  
65. The Contentious Chamber therefore concludes that there is no violation of Article 12.4 of the GDPR and Article 17.1 of the GDPR.  
66. The Contentious Chamber emphasizes, however, that it is the responsibility of the data controller to facilitate the exercise of the rights of the data subjects, in accordance with Article 12.2 of the GDPR, even when this right is exercised in relation to a processor. It is up to the data controller and the processor to include the necessary clauses in the processing agreement in accordance with Article 28.3.e) of the GDPR.  
67. During the hearing, the defendant explained that it had since adapted its working method. Now, in the event of a deletion request, the account is deleted from platform Z; however, the data held at the registration academy is retained because another legal basis applies in that case, such as the performance of a contract between the academy and the data subject (Article 6.1.b) of the GDPR), or as a statutory obligation to retain certain personal data of students for a specified period (Article 6.1.c) of the GDPR). The defendant informs the data subject that their account has been deleted and that if they wish to access it again in the future, a new account can be created, and a link with the students is then required again. Once an account has been deleted, it is completely removed. However, the data is still processed by the schools because the decree on part-time artistic education stipulates what must happen to this data and also prescribes a specific retention period.
