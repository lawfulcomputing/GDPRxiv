II. Rationale  
28. When the Contentious Chamber refers in this decision to the "defendant," it is exclusively referring to the defendant (and thus not to the body shop).  
II.1.1. Jurisdiction of the Contentious Chamber  
29.  
In the present case, the Contentious Chamber must note that in decision 136/2022, the wrong data controller was addressed, due to an error on the part of the Contentious Chamber, which involved contacting another company that has its own business number.  
30. The Contentious Chamber must emphasize in this instance that the plaintiff's initial mediation request was indeed directed at the defendant. The plaintiff's request dated June 21, 2022, was worded as follows: “I wish for your intervention for mediation with the Z2 garage […].”  
31.  
It follows that the defendant was indeed contacted in the mediation procedure. The email address used [...] is furthermore the email address that was used for the initial communications between the plaintiff and the defendant. This email address is also listed on the defendant's website. The lack of response to the mediation attempt is indeed attributable to the defendant. The subsequent conversion into a complaint also concerns the defendant.  

Decision on the Merits 58/2024 — 8/14  
32.  
Decision 136/2022 was then sent to the body shop. Since at that time, the email address on the body shop's website was identical to that of the defendant, this error went unnoticed during the sending of the decision. It was during the first phase of conclusions that this error was brought to the attention of the Contentious Chamber. Following this, the Contentious Chamber initiated a second phase of conclusions so that the defendant would also have the opportunity to submit its conclusions to the Contentious Chamber. The invitation to the hearing was then sent to the defendant. At this hearing, the defendant was represented by the same counsel as the body shop. During the hearing, the body shop was granted a dismissal.  
33. The defendant argues in its conclusions that the Contentious Chamber would not have jurisdiction over it. However, as specified above in points 30 and 31, it can be concluded that this is not accurate. The defendant is not a "third party" to the procedure, as it wrongly claims.  
34. In this regard - moreover - it can also be emphasized the status of the Contentious Chamber as an administrative jurisdiction, which implies that Article 811 of the Judicial Code does not apply. Submitting a complaint file cannot therefore be considered a procedure in the sense of Article 2 of the Judicial Code. The procedure before the Contentious Chamber is a sui generis administrative law procedure that monitors compliance with the GDPR and therefore, "the said ‘procedure’ when processing a complaint before the Contentious Chamber cannot simply be compared to the procedure in a judicial proceeding."  
35. Furthermore, it should be noted that no corrective measures are imposed on the body shop anymore. The initiation of the substantive procedure under Article 98 of the LCA indeed implies that decision 136/2022 is automatically replaced by the present decision on the merits. The injunction in decision 136/2022 indeed establishes that the Contentious Chamber imposes an injunction "subject to the introduction of a request by the data controller for a substantive decision in accordance with Articles 98 et seq. of the LCA."  
36. Both parties finally also request a condemnation of the other party for costs. However, the Contentious Chamber is not competent to condemn any party in the procedure for costs. Articles 95 and 100 of the LCA list exhaustively the corrective measures and sanctions that it can decide. The condemnation for costs is not among the measures listed in these articles, particularly in Article 100 of the LCA.

II.1.2. Admissibility of the Procedure on the Merits  
37.  
The complainant asserts that the procedure on the merits is not admissible, as the request for treatment on the merits in accordance with Articles 98 et seq. of the LCA was submitted late. The injunction in Decision 136/2022 indeed establishes that the Litigation Chamber imposes an injunction "subject to the submission of a request by the data controller for treatment on the merits in accordance with Articles 98 et seq. of the LCA." The decision itself does not therefore specify a deadline regarding the request for treatment on the merits.  
38. In practice, the Litigation Chamber generally grants a period of thirty days to the data controller to potentially request treatment of the case on the merits. The notification of the decision dates from September 27, 2022. The request for treatment on the merits dates from October 27, 2022.  
The Litigation Chamber considers that the request for treatment on the merits was not submitted late.

It is clear from practice that this is the case. The reading report in question was indeed only transmitted to the complainant after Z2 had given its authorization for this purpose.  
40. The Disciplinary Chamber therefore examines whether, in this case, there is not a joint responsibility for processing. The European Data Protection Board (hereinafter EDPB) defines an important criterion for establishing joint responsibility for processing as a situation in which processing would not be possible without the participation of both parties. This implies that the processing by each party is inseparable from that of the other party or is inextricably linked. Joint participation must include the determination of purposes, on the one hand, and the determination of means, on the other hand. The EDPB further specifies that "Thus, to assess the existence of joint controllers, it is necessary to examine whether the determination of the purposes and means that characterize a controller is decided by more than one party. The term 'jointly' should be interpreted as meaning 'together' or 'not alone', in various forms and combinations (...)."  
41.  
The Court of Justice has specified in this regard that "the existence of joint responsibility does not necessarily translate into equivalent responsibility, for the same processing of personal data, of the different actors. On the contrary, these actors may be involved at different stages of this processing and to different degrees, such that the level of responsibility of each of them must be assessed taking into account all relevant circumstances of the case."  
42.  
The Disciplinary Chamber therefore concludes that the defendant is a joint controller of processing with Z2. The defendant has some freedom to organize the processing activities themselves, but this is clearly done in consultation with Z2, which must therefore also be considered a joint controller of processing.  
43. The Disciplinary Chamber addresses itself exclusively to the defendant in this procedure. The complaint indeed exclusively targets the defendant. The Disciplinary Chamber also considers that if there is indeed joint responsibility for processing, it can address both entities for these actions. This follows in particular from Guidelines 07/2020 in which the EDPB states that "Supervisory authorities are

II.1.4. Complainant's Access Request  
44. On June 15, 2022, the defendant was summoned by the complainant's counsel to respond to the latter's access request. The defendant reacted on June 17, 2022, but negatively. Subsequently, the mediation procedure with the Frontline Service was initiated, to which the defendant did not respond.  
45. On September 26, 2022, decision 136/2022 was adopted against the body shop. This may obviously lead to some confusion, but the decision was nonetheless sent to the defendant's general email address, which implies that they could (also) have been informed of the injunction to comply with the complainant's access request. This decision mentions a compliance deadline of fourteen days.  
46. Ultimately, on November 2, 2022, more than a month after the decision was sent and five months after the initial access request, a diagnostic report was sent to the complainant.  
47. However, the Contentious Chamber does not take into account the fact that the defendant did not timely respond to the injunction contained in decision 136/2022, as this may indeed be due to a material error on the part of the Contentious Chamber, given that the injunction was addressed to the wrong controller of processing. However, this does not detract from the fact that the complainant's access request was, in any case, fulfilled too late and that, furthermore, there is uncertainty (without the Contentious Chamber having proof of this) regarding the adequacy of the report provided. It is still unknown whether it contains all the personal data of the complainant that was requested and extracted.
