48. The third condition implies that the interests or the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject must not override the legitimate interest of the data controller. The Court of Justice has held that this condition involves a balancing of the competing rights and interests at stake, which, in principle, depends on the circumstances of the specific case. Recital 47 also provides that the existing relationship between the data controller and the data subject affects the determination of the reasonable expectations of the data subject. For example, a data subject would not reasonably expect their data to be processed by a person with whom they have never had contact.

Article 6.1 GDPR further provides that the processing of personal data is lawful only if and to the extent that it is based on one of the legal bases set out in Article 6.1(a) to (f) GDPR. Furthermore, Z3 would have indicated that the data was collected in compliance with the applicable legal provisions, and more specifically on the basis of the consent of the data subjects.  
74.  
As already explained in point II.2.2 of this decision, a data controller cannot merely refer to the statements of an intermediary party or include a clause in the agreement concluded with that party stating that the latter has the obligation to provide data in accordance with data protection legislation. Each data controller is itself required to know, among other things, the origin of the data, how it was collected, on what legal basis, by whom, for what purposes, for how long, and for what processing. Without this information, the data controller cannot demonstrate, in accordance with Article 24.1 of the GDPR, that the processing is carried out in compliance with this regulation.  
75.  
Y2 does not demonstrate that the complainant could reasonably expect that his data would be transmitted by a data broker to a third party for a direct marketing campaign conducted by a commercial entity with which he had had no prior contact. The Contentious Chamber recalls that the interests and fundamental rights of the data subjects may, in particular, prevail in processing situations where the data subjects do not reasonably expect further processing (see recital 47 of the GDPR), as in the case at hand.  
76.  
In light of the above, the Contentious Chamber finds that Y2 has not demonstrated that Y1's interest outweighs the interests and fundamental rights of the data subjects. Given that the three-part test for data processing based on legitimate interest involves cumulative conditions, it is not necessary to examine whether the defendant meets the other two conditions of Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR. The Market Court has already ruled in this regard that if any one of the three elements of the three-part test is not met, the Contentious Chamber can rightly conclude that Article 6.1(f) of the GDPR cannot constitute a possible legal basis.19  
77. In these circumstances, it must be noted that Y2 has not demonstrated that Y1's interests outweigh the interests and fundamental rights of the data subjects, so that the processing cannot fall under Article 6.1(f) of the GDPR. Thus, it has violated the obligations imposed by Articles 5.1(a), 6.1, and 24 of the GDPR.  
II.3.2. Regarding the violation of Article 12.2-4 of the GDPR read in conjunction with Articles 15 and 17 of the GDPR  
78.  
The complainant exercised his rights of access and erasure of data by letter addressed to Y2 on October 10, 2022. Y2 forwarded this letter to Z3, which responded on October 12, 2022. On November 4, 2022, the complainant requested Y1, Z3, and Y2 to erase his data based on Article 17 of the GDPR, as well as information on the measures taken to achieve this. Y2 contacted Y1 and Z3 to ensure that they would respond to the complainant. Z3 responded on November 7, 2022, and Y1 responded on November 29, 2022.  
79.  
However, it appears from the case file that Y2 did not respond itself to the complainant's requests. This constitutes a violation of Article 12.3 of the GDPR, which states that "The data controller shall provide the data subject with information on the action taken in response to a request under Articles 15 to 22, without undue delay and in any event within one month of receipt of the request..."  
80. In these circumstances, it must be considered that Y2 has violated the obligations imposed by Article 12.3 of the GDPR read in conjunction with Articles 15 and 17 of the GDPR.
