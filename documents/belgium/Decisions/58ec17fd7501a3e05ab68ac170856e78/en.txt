II. Motivation  
II.1. Regarding the referral to the Contentious Chamber  
24.  
The Contentious Chamber recalls that its referral is defined based on the subject of the complaint filed, which may be broadened in support of the findings of the SI when it is seized. It adds that it may carry out certain investigations related to the grievances it is seized with in the context, for example, of its duty of diligence.  
25.  
In this case, the Contentious Chamber believes it does not need to exercise this prerogative to adopt a reasoned decision regarding the grievances raised by the complaint of February 6, 2020, filed by the complainant as investigated by the SI, and it adheres to the scope of its referral as defined by its letter of September 21, 2020, and the SI's investigation report.  
26.  
Consequently, the present decision of the Contentious Chamber will determine whether, as raised by the SI, the defendant has indeed committed  
-  
a violation of Articles 12.3 and 15 of the GDPR concerning the request made by the complainant on August 9 and 11, 2019 (Title II.2),  
-  
a violation of Article 32 of the GDPR regarding the system for extracting claims files by the insured (Title II.3), and  
-  
a violation of Article 33 of the GDPR regarding the absence of notification to the APD of the data breach that occurred during the sending to the complainant of a claims file related to another insured (Title II.4).  
It will also address the grievances arising from the deletion of the complainant's claims data from the CarAttest database and the sending of direct marketing emails (see point 17 above).  
All other grievances developed by the complainant, particularly through his reply conclusions, especially those not related to the processing of personal data, do not fall within the referral of the Contentious Chamber, notwithstanding the fact that the defendant has taken the trouble to respond to some of them.  
II.2. Regarding the alleged violation of Articles 12.3 and 15 of the GDPR  
II.2.1. The SI's viewpoint  
27.  
As mentioned in the procedural background, the SI notes that the defendant indicated during the investigation that the complainant's request of August 11, 2019, escaped its attention and that it had not identified it as a request for access rights within the meaning of Article 15 of the GDPR. The SI concludes that the defendant violated Articles 12.3 and 15 of the GDPR, not without noting that a response was provided to the request on January 21, 2020, following the complainant's reminder of January 15, 2020. The SI further considers that the fact that the complainant's access request of August 11, 2019, consists of a sentence included in the middle of a 4-page email stating numerous grievances—furthermore very diverse—constitutes a circumstance it qualifies as "mitigating" for the defendant.

II.2.3. Assessment by the Contentious Chamber  
35. Under Article 15.1 of the GDPR, the data subject has the right to obtain from the data controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning him or her is being processed (1st aspect of the right of access).  
36. When this is the case, the data subject has the right to obtain a series of information listed in Article 15.1 a) - h) and 15.2 of the GDPR relating to the processed data as well as access to it (2nd aspect of the right of access)8.  
37. The GDPR also provides in Article 15.3 that the data controller shall provide a copy of the personal data to the data subject. The provision of a copy is the primary means of granting access to the processed data.  
38. The data subject is not required to justify why he or she requests access to his or her personal data9.  
39. Article 15 of the GDPR must necessarily be read and applied in conjunction with Article 12 of the GDPR concerning the modalities for exercising the rights of data subjects.

a. Thus, under Article 12.2 of the GDPR, the data controller is required to facilitate the exercise of the rights conferred to the data subject under Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR.  
b. Under Article 12.3 of the GDPR, “the data controller provides the data subject with information on the measures taken in response to a request made under Articles 15 to 22, without undue delay and in any event within one month of receiving the request. 40. The Contentious Chamber considers, in support of the following reasoning, that the request made by the complainant in their email of August 11, 2019, constitutes a request to exercise their right of access within the meaning of Article 15 of the GDPR.10  
41. The Contentious Chamber notes at the outset that it was only before the Data Protection Authority (DPA) that the defendant indicated that it had not identified the complainant's request as a request for access to justify the lack of response within the timeframe of Article 12.3 of the GDPR (see below) that was being criticized. In its letter of January 21, 2020, the defendant indeed states that it did not respond to the complainant because the complainant had not attached their identity card to their request (point 10).  
42. Even if this request is, as the defendant points out, somewhat lost in an email related to a dispute between the complainant and the defendant regarding the latter's insurance activities and the contract between them, this sole circumstance does not disqualify it. The GDPR does not provide for any formal requirement for individuals requesting access to their data. To submit a request for access, it is sufficient for the applicants to specify that they wish to know the personal data concerning them processed by the data controller without even having to explicitly refer to the GDPR.11 Therefore, the data controller cannot refuse to respond to a request by invoking the absence of a specific reference to the right of access or to the GDPR, nor a fortiori to the absence of a reference to Article 15. Thus, while the Contentious Chamber did not uphold the arguments put forward by the defendant, who considered - once before the Contentious Chamber - that there had not been a properly submitted request to exercise rights, the Contentious Chamber nonetheless notes that certainly, the request was somewhat buried in a long email sent to a multitude of recipients, which may not have facilitated its identification. The Contentious Chamber also considers the commitment made by the defendant at the time to ensure that its staff was trained to identify requests for the exercise of rights under the GDPR.  
111. Regarding the sending of the claim form containing data of a third-party insured to the complainant (Article 32 of the GDPR – Title II.3) and the absence of notification of this data breach to the DPA (Article 33 of the GDPR - Title II.4), the Contentious Chamber also chooses to sanction these past shortcomings with a reprimand rather than a fine, given that it is an uncontested shortcoming that was quickly remedied concerning the violation of Article 32 of the GDPR and a shortcoming that can no longer be remedied concerning Article 33 of the GDPR. The Contentious Chamber also takes into account the period of the events in its choice and the absence at that time of the perspective that exists as of the date of this decision regarding the new obligation to notify data breaches to the DPA.  
112. Regarding the shortcomings related to the deletion of data from the CarAttest database and marketing emails (points 13, 17, and 26 above), the Contentious Chamber decides to dismiss them.
