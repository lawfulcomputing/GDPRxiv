I. Facts and Procedure  
1. On July 8, 2020, the complainant filed a complaint with the Data Protection Authority against the defendants.  
2. The facts underlying the complaint denounce the sending of correspondence related to the management of companies whose liquidation has been assigned to certain curators at their private addresses, rather than at their professional addresses.  
3. On July 10, 2020, the complaint was declared admissible by the First Line Service based on Articles 58 and 60 of the LCA, and the complaint was forwarded to the Contentious Chamber under Article 62, § 1 of the LCA.  
4. On August 7, 2020, in accordance with Article 96, § 1 of the LCA, the request from the Contentious Chamber to conduct an investigation was sent to the Inspection Service, along with the complaint and the inventory of documents.  
5. On August 30, 2021, the investigation by the Inspection Service was concluded, the report was attached to the file, and it was forwarded by the General Inspector to the President of the Contentious Chamber (Article 91, § 1 and § 2 of the LCA).

On January 10, 2022, the Contentious Chamber receives the response submissions from defendant 4. These submissions also contain defendant 4's reaction regarding the findings of the Inspection Service that go beyond the scope of the complaint. In summary, here is what defendant 4 defends:  
•  
He sends letters to the curators in order to fulfill his legal obligation, which consists of the collection and recovery of Walloon taxes when the company liable for the tax is in bankruptcy. This legal obligation arises from the decree of May 6, 1999, relating to the establishment, collection, and litigation regarding Walloon regional taxes;  
•  
In practice, and given the volume of letters he must send, defendant 4 has implemented an automated system for this purpose. The letters are generated by applications. These applications retrieve the contact data of the debtors via defendant 4's person signaling application, named "eSigna." The problem encountered in this case, which led to letters being sent to the private addresses of the curators instead of their professional addresses, stems from the fact that on the website of the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises (hereinafter "BCE"), the curators involved in this case were registered with their national registration number. Thus, the "BCE" website, in response to identification requests from the debtor of the "eSigna" software, responded with the national registration number of the concerned curator and not their business number. Furthermore, the letter was then automatically sent to the private address of the concerned curator and not their professional address;  
•  
He notes that an emergency procedure was implemented in March 2021 to manually address the problem mentioned below, pending the modification of his computer program. Subsequently, his IT service provider modified his computer program, notably by creating a "global correspondence list between the RN8 number of the agent, their business number, and their professional address." This list was then inserted into his "eSigna" application. Defendant 4 emphasizes that this work proved to be colossal and resolves the issue arising from the present case, given that eSigna automatically links the national registration number to the business number and then uses the professional address of the company's liable agent instead of the private address. The results are said to be conclusive, and the update work has continued for ongoing improvement.

42.  
Within the Belgian federal public sector, administrations are structured in interconnected networks, and the principle of unique data collection prevails.  
43.  
Among the administrations that largely constitute these networks, some are classified as "authentic data sources." Article 2, 6° of the law of August 15, 2012, concerning the creation and organization of a federal service integrator defines an authentic data source as a "database in which authentic data is stored." Authentic data is defined as data "collected and managed by an entity in a database and which serves as the unique and original data concerning the person or legal fact in question, so that other entities no longer need to collect the same data." Thus, the determination of the authentic nature of authentic data relates not so much to its nature as to the manner in which it "is collected, stored, and made accessible."37 Furthermore, it should be noted that the term "authentic" within the framework of the aforementioned law is an autonomous concept, and is therefore independent of the meaning that may be attributed to it by other legislative texts.  
44.  
Within each network, there exists a service integrator. Vertical service integrators are distinguished from horizontal service integrators.  
45.  
Article 2, 1° of the aforementioned law defines a service integrator as "an institution that, by or under a law, is responsible for the integration of services at a level of government or in a specific sector." This institution is generally public, but this is not necessarily always the case. The Belgian legislator had in mind39 the order of lawyers or the chamber of notaries, which constitute service integrators while being private institutions. Notwithstanding this, the mission pursued by service integrators always falls within the public interest.  
46.  
In summary, each piece of data is collected only once from citizens. Once the data is collected, no public institution can request the same data again.
