I. Facts and procedure  
1.  
On January 12, 2021, the complainant filed a complaint with the Data Protection Authority against the defendant, a municipality.  
2.  
The subject of the complaint concerns the issuance of a new identity card for which the defendant would take four fingerprints from each hand. Article 6, § 2, 8° of the law of July 19, 1991, however, states that only one fingerprint from each hand may be taken. The complainant asserts that after a request in this regard, no transparent information was provided by the city of (…) following the taking of the fingerprints, and that no legal basis could be provided for the mandatory taking of four fingerprints per hand. The complainant refers to the email correspondence of December 17, 2020, and December 22, 2020, with the defendant, as attached to the complaint. The complainant asserts not only that the number of fingerprints taken for the identity card exceeds that permitted by law, but that it is also possible that the defendant uses the other fingerprints for other purposes.  
3.  
On January 19, 2021, the complainant informed the First Line Service that when collecting the electronic identity card, eight fingers were again scanned to activate the identity card. According to the complainant, this implies that the defendant's assertion that the other fingerprints, except for the single fingerprint per hand prescribed by law, are destroyed is false.  
4.  
On January 29, 2021, the complaint was declared admissible by the First Line Service based on Articles 58 and 60 of the LCA, and the complaint was forwarded to the Contentious Chamber under Article 62, § 1 of the LCA.  
5.  
On June 16, 2023, the Contentious Chamber decided, based on Article 95, § 1, 1° and Article 98 of the LCA, that the file could be examined on the merits and informed the concerned parties by registered mail of the provisions as stated in Article 95, § 2 and Article 98 of the LCA. They were also informed, under Article 99 of the LCA, of the deadlines for submitting their conclusions.  
The deadline for receiving the defendant's response conclusions was set for August 11, 2023, the deadline for the complainant's reply conclusions for September 1, 2023, and the deadline for the defendant's reply conclusions for September 22, 2023.

16. Although the complainant does not specifically address the information he has obtained or not obtained during the collection of fingerprints, the Dispute Chamber nonetheless reminds that the defendant must provide the data subject with all information in accordance with Article 13 of the GDPR at the time of the first fingerprint collection. This means that the data controller must actively take steps to provide the information in question to the data subject. Under Article 12.1 of the GDPR, the written transmission of information or communications to data subjects is the standard means of communication. If written communication is conducted electronically, a privacy statement/layered communication may also be used. In any case, it is important to emphasize that all information addressed to a data subject must be accessible in one place or in a single document (paper or digital), easily accessible for the data subject if they wish to consult all the information intended for them.  
17. The Dispute Chamber emphasizes that the defendant not only has the obligation to provide the information that the data subject requests when exercising their right of access (Article 15 of the GDPR), which concerns personal data that is already processed by the data controller. The data controller also has the obligation to provide the information in accordance with Article 13 of the GDPR from the moment the personal data of the data subject is obtained – thus from the outset – in the case of collecting personal data from the data subject themselves. Proper information in the sense of Article 13 of the GDPR helps to avoid confusion on the part of the data subject and to anticipate requests for clarification from the data subject.

Among these fingerprints, at most one fingerprint per hand, the one with the best quality, is recorded. The fingerprint with the best quality is automatically detected by the software. Furthermore, it appears that even if several fingerprints per hand are processed, this method is dictated by the legal requirement that the fingerprint of the best quality must be integrated into the chip (Article 3, § 5, fifth paragraph of the Royal Decree of March 25, 2003) which justifies the taking of multiple fingerprints per hand in order to select the best and therefore a single fingerprint per hand, certainly in compliance with the order established in Article 3, § 5, fifth paragraph of the aforementioned Royal Decree of March 25, 2003.  
24. The crucial element in this regard is the temporary storage of fingerprints in the card producer's file for a maximum period of three months. This is in accordance with Article 6, § 2, fifth paragraph of the Law of July 19, 1991, concerning population registers, identity cards, foreigner cards, and residence documents which states that the digitized image of fingerprints may only be retained for the time necessary for the production and issuance of the identity card and, in any case, for a maximum period of 3 months, it being understood that after this 3-month period, the data must be destroyed and erased. This short imposed timeframe ensures that fingerprints are not retained beyond the necessary duration. The limited retention period contributes to data minimization given that after the selection of the best fingerprint per hand, the other fingerprints are deleted and subsequently, after the withdrawal of the eID by the concerned individual, the fingerprints stored on the RFID chip of the eID are also deleted from the card producer's file.  
25. The defendant demonstrates that taking multiple fingerprints constitutes a simple technical modality that allows the defendant to have the eID manufactured efficiently by the card producer within a relatively short timeframe with the necessary security guarantees by destroying all fingerprints after the legally defined three-month period.  
26. The fingerprints recorded on the RFID chip are indeed protected by the same security mechanism used for passports and residence permits.
