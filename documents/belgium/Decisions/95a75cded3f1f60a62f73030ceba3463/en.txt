35. The defendant cannot irreversibly prove (for example, based on statements or press releases from the complainants or Noyb) that the visits on February 10, 2023, were not made by the complainants or that there was a false declaration in the administration of evidence - this is at most a negligence on the part of the representative in the formulation and dating of certain pieces of evidence:  
a. The fact that, after the initial visit of the complainants on February 10, 2023, other individuals from Noyb would have collected evidence in preparation for the mandate and the complaint does not prove that the initial visits were not made by the complainants and/or that the personal data of the complainants were processed or not;  
b. The fact that other individuals took screenshots of other aspects of the cookie banners, or regarding the banner governing the withdrawal of consent, with evidence that other laptops were used to take these screenshots, does not prove that it was not the complainants who visited the websites of their own initiative on February 10, 2023, and decided to file a complaint;  
c. The fact that on the day of the visits by the complainants, namely February 10, 2023, cookies would have been refused – and not on other dates – and that it is thus established that no personal data of the complainants was processed and consequently, no complaint can be filed, is legally deficient on two points, as (i) one cannot require the complainants – to the extent that they indeed refused the cookies, as claimed by the defendant – to engage in the alleged infringing practice in order to file a complaint36 and (ii) it is the complainants who, according to their statements of February 10, 2023, observed the alleged infringing practice and "believed"37 at that moment that a violation of their personal data could be observed, which is why they mandated Noyb in accordance with Article 80.1 of the GDPR. For this latter aspect, the complainants rightly refer to Recital 142 of the GDPR preamble, which specifies that a data subject may mandate a representative as soon as they "believe" that their rights under the GDPR have been violated.  
36. 51. Based on the documents in the file, including the complaint form, the Contentious Chamber finds that the scope of the present case concerns the following alleged violations by the defendant:

Alleged violations of Article 10/2 of the Law of July 30, 2018, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data (M.B. 5/9/2018, hereinafter the "LTD"), in connection with Article 125, § 1, 1° of the Law of June 13, 2005, on electronic communications (M.B. 20/6/2005, hereinafter the "LCE"; this provision is mentioned in Article 10/2 of the LTD) and Article 6.1.a. of the General Data Protection Regulation (EU 2016/679, published 4/5/2016, hereinafter "GDPR"): the complainants assert in each of the four complaints regarding the four disputed websites that the consent requirements were not respected, notably due to the absence of a "reject" option at the first level of information in the cookie banner. In this regard, the alleged violations concern the lack of valid consent under the Belgian implementing provisions of the e-Privacy Directive (EU 2002/58) read in conjunction with the aforementioned provisions of the GDPR, concerning the processing of personal data carried out following the placement of cookies;  
2. Alleged violations of Articles 5.1.a. of the GDPR and 6.1.a. of the GDPR: the complainants assert in each of the four complaints that the use of different colors for the cookie banner in the context of granting consent would be misleading in this case. In this sense, the alleged violations concern compliance with the principles of lawfulness, fairness, and transparency, as well as the valid granting of consent. Given the manifest connection, the Contentious Chamber also raises ex officio in this context the consent requirements under Articles 10/2 of the LTD and 125, § 1, 1° of the LCE mentioned above – given the link with the placement of cookies and the consent requirements in these legal provisions. The defendant must therefore also include these articles in its defense in this regard;  
3. Alleged violations of Article 4(11) in conjunction with Article 7.3 of the GDPR: the complainants assert in each of their four complaints that in the context of the placement of cookies, it is not as easy to withdraw consent as it is to give it. In this sense, all complaints indicate that the data subjects must go through several steps on the disputed websites before they can withdraw any consent, after which the withdrawal of any consent given - for example, but not limited to, in cases where a data subject had previously accepted all cookies simultaneously - could only be done separately. Given the manifest connection, the Contentious Chamber also raises ex officio in this context the consent requirements under Articles 10/2 of the LTD and 125, § 1, 1° of the LCE mentioned above – given the link with the placement of cookies and the consent requirements in these legal provisions. The complaint regarding the website ‘7sur7’ also mentions in this context an alleged violation of Article 5.1 a). of the GDPR, as it would not be correct or transparent that the section regarding the cookie policy on the disputed website does not contain a button or link allowing the adjustment of cookie preferences, and that the mentioned section is also not available on the disputed website. The defendant must therefore also include these articles in its defense in this regard.  

III. Publication of the Decision  
52. The Contentious Chamber is in the habit – for reasons of transparency – of publishing decisions under Article 95, § 1 of the LCA, when these decisions contain warnings or injunctions (so-called "light decisions").  
53. Generally, the Contentious Chamber does not publish its decisions in the sense of Article 95, § 1, 1° of the LCA – when it concerns interim decisions to address the case on the merits – notably because they do not contain significant elements relevant to the public interest and are merely procedural advancement measures.  
54. However, in this very specific context, the Market Court has decided that a special obligation of motivation applies, requiring a comparison of the facts of the present case with another decision.  
55. Given that, precisely due to this particular obligation of motivation, the present decision contains relevant elements to be brought to the public's attention, the Contentious Chamber decides to publish this decision on its website.
