II.1.3. The use of the "Sidis Suite" database  
41.  
The detention files of foreign nationals are available both in physical form at the registry of the prison where the person concerned is detained, and also in digitized form within the "Sidis Suite" database. In the present case, the defendant consulted the complainant's detention file only in physical form, and it is on the basis of this premise that the Contentious Chamber conducted the legal examination under point II.1.2. of this decision. During the hearing, the defendant stated that it is the practice of the Office of Foreigners to consult the detention file of concerned individuals in both its physical and digitized versions. Furthermore, the defendant specified that the file accessible through Sidis Suite is similar to that accessible through the prison registry, except that in this database it is not possible to access information regarding the disciplinary history of the person concerned. Consequently, it appears important to the Contentious Chamber to also address some observations regarding the consultation of the detention file through Sidis Suite.  
42.  
Article 3, paragraph 1 of the law of May 5, 2019 defines Sidis Suite as "a computerized database […] in which the data necessary for the proper exercise of the legal missions of the penitentiary administration […] is processed."  
43. Article 7, § 1, 7° of the law of May 5, 2019 grants a right to read the data processed in Sidis Suite to the Office of Foreigners. The second paragraph of the same article provides that "The King shall determine, after consulting the competent control authority, the scope and modalities of this right to read and specifies by authority, body, or service for what specific purposes the data may be used."  
44. The royal decree intended to organize the scope and modalities of this right to read for the Office of Foreigners has still not been adopted to date. A draft of this royal decree has not yet been submitted for the opinion of the Data Protection Authority (APD).  
45. Furthermore, the Contentious Chamber notes that although Sidis Suite processes and collects a whole series of personal data, including the detention files of accused or convicted foreigners, without distinction regarding the regularity of their stay, the law of May 5, 2019 does not precisely outline the extent of the right to read these same data for the public authorities that are granted this right.  
46. Indeed, Article 7, § 1 of the law of May 5, 2019 indicates that public authorities benefiting from a right to read must be able to access the data necessary for the fulfillment of their legal missions. 53. The right to information constitutes, along with the right of access, two sides of the same principle: the principle of transparency. While the right of access represents the active aspect, requiring action from the data subject, the right to information represents the passive aspect, as the relevant information that must be communicated to the data subject does not require any action on their part; the data controller has a positive obligation to act in this regard.

II.3. Regarding the alleged breach of Articles 12.3 and 15.1 of the GDPR  
65. It appears from the case documents that the complainant exercised their right of access on August 18, 2023, and that the defendant did not respond at least until the time of the filing of the complaint. In its conclusions, the defendant stated that it had no observations to make in this regard.  
66. The right of access has three components. First, under Article 15.1 of the GDPR, the data subject has the right to obtain from the data controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning them is being processed. Second, when personal data is being processed, the data subject has the right to access the said personal data as well as a series of information listed in Article 15.1 a) – h). Third, under Article 15.3 of the GDPR, the data subject also has the right to obtain a copy of the personal data undergoing processing.  
67. Article 12 of the GDPR concerning the modalities for exercising their rights by data subjects provides in its third paragraph that the data controller shall provide information on the measures taken in response to the request made by the data subject, and this no later than one month from the request, unless a reasoned request for an extension is made.  
68. The Contentious Chamber recalls that the right of access constitutes a requirement of the right to the protection of personal data, as it serves as "the gateway" to the exercise of the other rights conferred by the GDPR to the data subject.  
69. In the present case, the violation of Articles 12.3 and 15 of the GDPR is established, given that, as described in point 65 of this decision, the defendant did not respond to the complainant's access request within the timeframe stipulated by Article 12.3 of the GDPR.
