25.  
It is undisputed that the data processing carried out by the defendant is subject to the GDPR. The conditions for application provided by the temporal, material, and territorial scope of the GDPR (Articles 2 and 3) are indeed met.  
26.  
The defendant has processed personal data relating to the complainant and his minor son. Article 4.1 of the GDPR defines the notion of "personal data"

According to the Article 29 Working Party, the notion of "any information" must be interpreted broadly. It may therefore include objective information or subjective information, that is to say, opinions or assessments regarding the data subject. The Contentious Chamber notes that the reports of the defendant factually recount the meetings that took place between the complainant and his son. These reports contain information relating to both of them, father and son, within the meaning of the above-mentioned Article 4.1. Even assuming that this information is classified as "subjective" in that it would be reported by a third party (the defendant), quod non, it would nonetheless remain as specified above "personal data" concerning the complainant and his son within the meaning of Article 4.1 of the GDPR.

II.4.2. Assessment of the Litigation Chamber  
96. The Litigation Chamber recalls that under Article 17.1 of the GDPR, the data subject has the right to obtain from the data controller the erasure, without undue delay, of personal data concerning them, on the one hand, and the data controller has the obligation to erase such personal data without undue delay when one of the following grounds applies, on the other hand:  
a) the personal data are no longer necessary for the purposes for which they were collected or otherwise processed;  
b) the data subject withdraws consent on which the processing is based, in accordance with Article 6(1)(a) or Article 9(2)(a), and there is no other legal basis for the processing;  
c) the data subject objects to the processing under Article 21(1), and there are no overriding legitimate grounds for the processing, or the data subject objects to the processing under Article 21(2);  
d) the personal data have been unlawfully processed;  
e) the personal data must be erased to comply with a legal obligation under Union law or the law of the Member State to which the data controller is subject;  
f) the personal data have been collected in the context of the offering of information society services referred to in Article 8(1).  
97. The Litigation Chamber decides, based on its previous findings, that in this case, the defendant cannot be reproached for a breach of Article 17.1(d) of the GDPR, which the plaintiff seems to invoke due to processing of erroneous data (see above title II.3.) or without a legal basis (see above – title II.2).  
98. However, the Litigation Chamber does not agree with the assertion made by the defendant in its summary conclusions that, since the processing would not be unlawful (Article 17.1(d) of the GDPR), no grounds of Article 17.1 of the GDPR could be invoked. In principle, other hypotheses could at least have been considered by the defendant, such as those provided for in Article 17.1(a) or Article 17(c) of the GDPR. However, the Litigation Chamber agrees with the argument that the defendant had, in any case, a ground for exemption from its possible obligation to erase under Article 17.3(b) of the GDPR.  
99. The Litigation Chamber notes in this regard that the defendant responded on June 8, 2022, to the request of June 1 from the plaintiff, thus respecting the one-month deadline prescribed by Article 12.4 of the GDPR applicable in the event of a refusal to comply with a request based on Article 17 of the GDPR. The defendant thus justified within the deadline that it could not proceed with the erasure given its obligation to archive and justify the subsidies received, both required by law (point 12). As it did in point 83 above, the Litigation Chamber recalls that, in addition to respecting the response deadline within the month and mentioning the reasons for refusal, Article 12.4 of the GDPR requires that the data controller inform the recipient of the said response of the possibility to lodge a complaint (claim – Article 77 of the GDPR) with a data protection authority and to bring a judicial remedy (Article 79 of the GDPR).  
100. Regarding Article 21/1 of the decree of October 13, 2016, invoked by the defendant, the Litigation Chamber notes, as raised by the plaintiff, that it was not applicable at the time of the facts. This article was indeed introduced, as mentioned by the defendant in footnote 17 of its conclusions, by Article 12 of the decree of January 1, 2017.

103. The Contentious Chamber concludes that the defendant was justified in invoking Article 17.3 b) of the GDPR and that, consequently, Article 17 of the GDPR was not violated.
