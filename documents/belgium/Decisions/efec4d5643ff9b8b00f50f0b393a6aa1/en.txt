I. Facts and Procedure  
I.1.  
The Four Complaints  
1.  
This case is based on four joint complaints from a single complainant regarding the defendant's cookie practices on four of its websites:  
a. The first complaint concerns the website ‘Gazet van Antwerpen’ (www.gva.be)  
b. The second complaint concerns the website ‘De Standaard’ (www.standaard.be)  
c. The third complaint concerns the website ‘Het Nieuwsblad’ (www.nieuwsblad.be)  
d. The fourth complaint concerns the website ‘Het Belang van Limburg’ (www.hbvl.be)  
2.  
The complainant is represented by Noyb – European Center for Digital Rights ("Noyb"), headquartered in Austria. II. Motivation  
II.1. Preliminary Points  
31. A first preliminary point concerns the reply conclusions of the complainant. The defendant asserts in its summary conclusions that the conclusions of the complainant should be excluded from the proceedings, on the one hand because they have not been signed (by the legal representative of Noyb) and on the other hand because the conclusions have not been drafted in accordance with Article 744 of the Judicial Code.  
32. The Contentious Chamber explains why the defendant's argument on this point is not legally admissible. In essence, the procedure before the Contentious Chamber is governed by the procedural provisions of the LCA. The Market Court has repeatedly stated that the Contentious Chamber is an administrative body and not a (formal) court. In this sense, one cannot simply assert that the provisions of the Judicial Code would apply fully and always as lex generalis in the procedure before the Contentious Chamber when the lex specialis of the LCA does not provide for such regulation.  
33. Furthermore, the Belgian legislator has explicitly defined in the LCA that the parties may submit conclusions. The legislator then left the APD to regulate how conclusions may be submitted – and, if necessary, to define this in the Internal Rules.  
34. It is in the letter from the APD dated February 5, 2024, that the parties were informed of the modalities for submitting conclusions. The invitation does not mention anywhere that the parties should submit their conclusions according to the formal modalities referred to by the defendant, nor does it refer to the Judicial Code. The Contentious Chamber cannot limit the conclusions of a party – this must also apply mutatis mutandis to the modalities of drafting and submitting its conclusions by a party when nothing is 'imposed' in advance on the parties. The complainant has complied with the deadlines for the conclusions regarding the submission of the document.

62.  
First of all, the drafting of the minutes by the Contentious Chamber and its sending to the parties is not a legal right, but merely an initiative of the APD to integrate the hearing into the administrative (formal) file and to formalize in the file the elements that were not raised in the respective conclusions.  
The Internal Rules state that a minute is drafted simply as a summary; the minute then explicitly indicates the following:  
"This minute aims solely to mention clarifications and additions made during the hearing, without reiterating the elements that were presented in the written conclusions of the parties." (the Contentious Chamber emphasizes in light of this decision)  
63. In this sense, the Contentious Chamber has taken note of everything that was said during the hearing. The defendant presented his arguments in depth (counting the table of contents and the list of documents, the summary conclusions total 117 pages) and in detail in his conclusions. The Contentious Chamber did not include in the minutes similar elements that were mentioned during the pleadings, and it merely recalled that the pleadings respectively concern "formal" and "substantive" elements – elements that are found in the summary conclusions and whose terms are literally similar. Each question or new substantive remark made during the hearing has indeed been included in the minutes.  
64. Secondly, the Contentious Chamber asserts that the purpose of the minutes is not to provide a comprehensive account of everything that was said during the hearing. A comprehensive account is not only irrelevant to the objective of the right to be heard in accordance with the law, but it is also undesirable for the proper functioning of the procedure for the Contentious Chamber and for its smooth conduct for the parties. The debates are indeed not reopened after the closure of the hearing, as expressly indicated in the minutes themselves. Under the principle of effectiveness, the GDPR must be applied usefully: unnecessary additions to the procedure are not only undesirable but are also illegal in accordance with this principle.

III.4. Execution by Provision  
207. In the context of execution by provision, the Contentious Chamber particularly considers the request and the arguments of the defendant in this matter:  
"10th plea (in the alternative): No execution by provision." The defendant invokes "particular reasons" in this regard and refers to the case law of the Court of Markets, according to which an effective remedy is only possible "when the applicant is not under pressure to pay an immediate fine and/or to comply immediately with the provisions of the contested decision." (Translator's note: free translation made by the Translation Service of the Data Protection Authority, in the absence of an official translation).  
208. The defendant therefore formulates – referring to the case law of the Court of Markets – the legitimate request to suspend the execution by provision in this case, as it puts pressure on the parties in the context (of the outcome) of a potential appeal procedure.  
209. The Contentious Chamber rejects the request to suspend the execution by provision for the following reasons.  
210. First, execution by provision is the standard situation for the national legislator. The European legislator has granted the supervisory authority the competence to take measures: it is therefore the authority that decides which (corrective) measure is most appropriate to – if necessary – encourage the defendant party to respond or to impose this measure on them.78  
211. The fact that an appeal is possible before a judicial body after any decision in this matter does not affect the powers of the authority. In light of the separation of powers, the judiciary must assess a posteriori whether the supervisory authority has acted within the legal framework and within the scope of its discretionary powers. When the judge exercises their own power to suspend execution, it is a decision falling within their evaluative competence.  
212. In view of the credibility of the powers that the European and national legislators have entrusted to the authority, the standard situation cannot be the suspension of the execution of decisions and measures taken by an authority as soon as a party requests it. Indeed, if this were the standard situation, it would undermine the entire intention of the legislator to act vigorously and effectively in a digital society. This does not correspond to the teleological conception of the powers granted to the authority under the GDPR.
