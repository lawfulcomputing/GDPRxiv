II.1. Regarding the alleged violation of the right of access (Article 15 of the GDPR)  
II.1.1. Regarding the content of the response provided to the right of access by the defendant  
24.  
The Contentious Chamber recalls that Article 4, 1) of the GDPR defines personal data as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (hereinafter referred to as 'data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more specific elements characteristic of their physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity; […]".  
25.  
Processing of personal data means "any operation or set of operations performed or not performed by automated means and applied to personal data or sets of personal data, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, communication by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure or destruction"3.

26.  
Pursuant to Article 15.1 of the GDPR, the data subject has the right to obtain from the data controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning them is being processed. When this is the case, the data subject has the right to access the said personal data as well as a series of information listed in Article 15.1 a) to h), such as the purpose of the processing of their data, the potential recipients of their data, and information regarding the existence of their rights, including the right to request rectification or erasure of their data or to lodge a complaint with the supervisory authority.  
27.  
The third paragraph of Article 15 specifies that "the data controller shall provide a copy of the personal data undergoing processing. [...]". This paragraph establishes a single right in conjunction with the first paragraph of the same article. Furthermore, it follows from the case law of the CJEU that this right implies the possibility for the data subject to obtain "the reproduction of extracts from documents or even entire documents or extracts from databases [...]" if this proves necessary. However, the interpretation of this right cannot be dissociated from that of Article 15.4, which provides that a balancing must be carried out with the rights and freedoms of others, where applicable.  
28.  
The Contentious Chamber recalls that the right of access constitutes an essential requirement of the right to data protection, as it serves as the "gateway" that enables the exercise of other rights conferred by the GDPR to the data subject.  
29.  
In the present case, it appears that the complainant exercised their right of access on January 25, 2022, to which the defendant responded on March 28, 2023, by providing the complainant with a partially anonymized version of the activity logs related to their contract covering the entire year of 2021. The defendant further states in its conclusions that the complainant had already posed similar questions to those in their access request during the mediation process (see point 4), which concluded on August 27, 2021, with the understanding that the Ombudsman considered that all useful information had been communicated to the complainant.  
30. In this regard, it must be clarified that in no way can a data controller be relieved of their obligation to respond to the exercise of the right of access solely on the grounds that the complainant already possessed the requested information prior to the exercise of said right. Article 12.5 does indeed provide that it may be refused to respond to the exercise of one of the rights referred to in Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR when the request of the data subject is manifestly unfounded or excessive; however, none of these

The Contentious Chamber further specifies that the CJEU distinguishes between the employee who acts according to the instructions and under the authority of their employer, and the employee who acts outside the respect of the instructions and authority of their employer – the former benefiting from a higher level of protection of their identity than the latter. However, this distinction has no bearing on the present case.  
38. Indeed, the Contentious Chamber notes that the complainant does not demonstrate, in his conclusions, any interest in obtaining the identity of the employees who mistakenly processed his personal data. Moreover, the complainant indicates in his conclusions that he is interested in knowing the purposes of the processing of his personal data, but not necessarily the identity of the employees who carried out this processing. During the hearing held on May 31, 2024, the complainant stated that he had no interest in accessing the purposes of the processing of his data, nor in the identity of the employees who carried it out.  
39. Consequently, the Contentious Chamber considers that the rights and freedoms of the employees of the defendant prevail over the complainant's right to access their identity.  
40. In any case, the Contentious Chamber recalls, emphasizing the reasoning put forth by the defendant, that the employees of the defendant cannot be considered as recipients within the meaning of Article 15.1.c) of the GDPR.  
41.  
Therefore, the initial request of the complainant – regarding the identity of the employees of the defendant – cannot be granted based on this provision.  
II.1.2. Regarding the modalities related to the exercise of the right of access (Articles 12.2, 12.3, and 15 of the GDPR)  
42.  
Pursuant to Article 12.1 of the GDPR, it is the responsibility of the data controller to take "appropriate measures to provide any information referred to in Articles 13 and 14 as well as

to proceed with any communication under Articles 15 to 22 and Article 34 regarding the processing of the data subject in a concise, transparent, understandable, and easily accessible manner, in clear and simple terms [...].  
43. Furthermore, it is the responsibility of the data controller to facilitate the exercise of the rights of the data subject (Article 12.2 of the GDPR) and to provide information on the measures taken following a request made under Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR, without undue delay and in any case within one month of receiving the request. Article 12.3 of the GDPR provides that this period may, if necessary, be extended by two months, taking into account the complexity and number of requests. In such a case, the data controller shall inform the data subject of this extension and the reasons for the delay within one month of receiving the request.  
44. In its Recital 59, the GDPR specifies that in order to facilitate the exercise by the data subject of the rights conferred upon them by the same Regulation, "The data controller should also provide means for requests to be made electronically, particularly where personal data is processed electronically." In this regard, the Contentious Chamber has previously had the opportunity to state that "in any event, the data subject should not be penalized in any way for not having sent their request to the correct address."12  
45. Furthermore, in its Guidelines 01/2022 on the right of access13, the European Data Protection Board ("EDPB") explains that this possibility of extending the response time constitutes an exception to the general rule and can only occur in certain circumstances exceptionally. Moreover, the EDPB notes that if a data controller often finds it necessary to extend this period, it could indicate a failure in their procedure for handling access requests.  
46. It follows from all of this that the defendant has the obligation to respond to the exercise of the complainant's right of access under Article 15 of the GDPR in accordance with the provisions of Article 12 of the GDPR, these two articles being intrinsically linked.  
47.

III.2. Regarding the imposition of an administrative fine  
60. Article 58.2 of the GDPR grants supervisory authorities the power to take one or more corrective measures against data controllers. In accordance with Article 58.2.i) of the GDPR, a supervisory authority may, depending on the circumstances of each case, impose an administrative fine in addition to or instead of the aforementioned corrective measures.  
61. In this regard, Article 83.1 of the GDPR requires that a fine imposed by a supervisory authority be, in each case, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. Article 83.2 of the GDPR sets out a number of criteria that must be duly taken into account in a specific case. Furthermore, the highest fine applies in accordance with Article 83.5.b) of the GDPR. Indeed, in the event of a violation of the rights of the concerned individual (in this case, the right of access guaranteed by Article 15 GDPR), the Contentious Chamber may impose an administrative fine of up to EUR 20,000,000 or, in the case of an enterprise, up to 4% of its total global annual turnover for the preceding financial year, with the higher amount being retained.

65. The effectiveness of the right of access is guaranteed by the provisions set forth in Article 12 of the GDPR, which is intrinsically linked to Article 15 of the GDPR, among others. Therefore, by only providing a satisfactory response to the complainant's access request 14 months after the date on which the complainant exercised their right of access, the defendant has committed a continuous violation of Article 15 of the GDPR for more than 14 months.  
66. Secondly, regarding the severity of the violation, the Contentious Chamber first notes that the defendant is a telecommunications company, and that the processing of personal data is therefore one of its core activities. Furthermore, as outlined in the above paragraphs, there has been a continuous infringement of Article 15 of the GDPR for 14 months, which constitutes the gateway to the exercise of other rights.  
67. Thirdly, concerning the duration of the violation, it has lasted for more than 14 months. Article 12.3 of the GDPR states that the data controller must respond "without undue delay and in any event within one month of receiving the request. If necessary, this period may be extended by two months, taking into account the complexity and number of requests." In this regard, the Contentious Chamber specifies, on the one hand, that the aforementioned extension of the deadline should only be used exceptionally – which otherwise could indicate the need for the data controller to develop its request handling procedures – and, on the other hand, that the data controller must respond to an access request within a period of less than one month if it is able to do so.  
68. Deliberate or negligent nature of the violation (Article 83, paragraph 2, point b) of the GDPR) – A distinction is made between violations caused by negligence and those caused deliberately. The deliberate nature of a violation involves meeting two conditions, namely the knowledge of a violation as well as the intention to cause it. Negligence is defined, conversely, by the absence of intentionality in the commission of the offense, although the principle of diligence has not been respected.  
69. The Contentious Chamber specifies that a high threshold is set to consider a violation as deliberate. Moreover, negligence can also be assessed by degrees.  
70. In this case, there is no indication of intent in the breach of Article 15 of the GDPR. However, given the financial and human resources of the defendant, as well as the fact that the processing of personal data constitutes one of the core activities of the defendant, the Contentious Chamber considers that the defendant has committed serious negligence. This is even more true as the inability to respond to an access request made by the complainant was manifested by two employees of the defendant.  
71. Categories of personal data concerned by the violation (Article 83, paragraph 2, point g) of the GDPR) – It does not appear from the case files that any data other than identification data has been processed by the data controller.  
72. Classification of the severity of the violation and setting an appropriate starting amount – The assessment of the above elements – namely the nature, severity, and duration of the violation, as well as the deliberate or negligent nature of the violation and the categories of personal data concerned – allows for determining the degree of severity of the violation as a whole. According to this assessment, the severity of the violation can be classified as "low," "medium," or "high."  
73. In this case, it should first be noted that the violation of Article 15 is among the violations listed in Article 83.5 of the GDPR, thus falling under the higher level of Article 83 of the same Regulation. Furthermore, it should be noted that the processing of personal data that prompted the complainant to exercise their right of access constitutes one of the core activities of the defendant. Additionally, the complainant received a response more than 14 months after exercising their right of access, and this during the exchange of conclusions in the present proceedings. However, it does not appear from the case that any data other than identification data was processed by the defendant. Moreover, the violation only concerns the complainant. Furthermore, the harm suffered by the complainant is low, given that they already had part of the answers they were requesting from the defendant and that they were not entitled to access other requested information, namely the identity of the employees who processed their personal data. In any case, the violation committed by the defendant stems from significant negligence on their part.  
74. In light of the elements presented above, the Contentious Chamber concludes that the established violation is of low severity. Therefore, for the calculation of the subsequent amount, a starting amount will be set between 0% and 10% of the maximum legal amount provided for in Article 83.5 of the GDPR.

III.2.3. The effectiveness, disproportionality, and deterrent nature of the fine  
84. A fine is deemed effective if it achieves its objectives, such as restoring compliance with the rules and sanctioning unlawful behavior. In this case, the fine aims to sanction the negligent and serious behavior of the defendant. Furthermore, it aims to deter other similar violations in the future. The prolonged violation of the complainant's fundamental rights, despite the complainant's access request and multiple reminders, demonstrates the need for a firm response from the Contentious Chamber. In this case, the defendant, with a turnover of more than (… euros), can bear a fine of 100,000 EUR (less than 0.01% of the retained turnover) without compromising its economic viability. The Contentious Chamber sought a final deterrent fine amount, in order to prevent the defendant from reoffending in the violation of GDPR rules. Additionally, it also seeks to deter other companies from committing similar violations.  
III.2.4. Defendant's reaction to the sanction form  
85. As a preliminary matter, the defendant argues the circumstances of the case at hand. It states that this is a one-time and isolated infringement, but not an infringement of a systemic nature. This violation would stem not from a deliberate intention or gross negligence, but from simple negligence. It adds that the number of individuals affected by the violation is limited to the complainant alone, who has suffered extremely limited harm – this being justified in particular by the fact that the GDPR violation committed only concerns simple identification data. In this regard, the defendant notes that although the right of access constitutes the "gateway" to the exercise of the other rights conferred by the GDPR, the complainant did not exercise these same rights after receiving a response to his access request on March 28, 2023. In any case, the defendant notes that it has corrected the violation it committed and has implemented additional internal measures, including training for its customer service to enable employees operating there to better recognize access requests, as well as to handle them better. Finally, the defendant points out that it receives an average of 150 access requests per year, and that, consequently, some requests may receive responses beyond the deadlines set by Article 12.3 of the GDPR due to the occurrence of human errors.
