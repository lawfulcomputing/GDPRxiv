2.3. Conclusion.
Based on the findings from the investigation, it is established that the Company, as the data controller, has failed to comply with the regulations regarding data protection, particularly concerning the exercise of the right of access, by not providing an adequate response to the request submitted by the complainant. This non-compliance constitutes a violation of the obligations set forth in the applicable data protection laws and regulations, specifically those outlined in the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

3. Sanctions.
In light of the violations identified, the Garante may impose administrative fines and other corrective measures as provided for under Articles 83 and 84 of the GDPR. The severity of the sanctions will be determined based on the nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, as well as any mitigating or aggravating factors that may be present.

4. Right to Appeal.
The Company has the right to appeal this decision before the competent judicial authority within the terms established by law. The appeal must be filed within the timeframe specified in the notification of this decision.

5. Final Provisions.
This document is issued in accordance with the provisions of the GDPR and other applicable laws on data protection. The Company is required to take immediate action to rectify the identified violations and ensure compliance with data protection obligations moving forward.

That said, it appears that the Company did not provide an adequate response to the complainant following the access request submitted by him: in the response of June 28, 2022, in fact, the Company, through its prevention and protection service officer, informed the complainant that it would proceed to delete the training certificates related to him since he “was no longer employed by the company.” This confirmed that it was in possession of the aforementioned certificates at the time of the request and, in any case, did not provide specific reasons as to why it could not issue the complainant a copy of the training certificates he had obtained and that it held.

On that occasion, the Company also did not inform the complainant, as required by Article 12, paragraph 4 of the Regulation, of the possibility, given the denial, to file a complaint with the Authority or appeal to the ordinary judicial authority. Only following the initiation of the investigation by the Authority did the Company clarify that it no longer possessed the requested certificates and took steps to recover them.

In particular, based on what was stated, the Company invited the companies that handle training for it to send the requested certificates to the complainant, and following this, as specified by the Company (which provided supporting documentation), it stated that “to our knowledge, the company Loxam Access s.r.l. The Company, therefore, violated Article 12 of the Regulation with reference to Article 15 of the Regulation. In particular, the Company did not provide an adequate response to the access request submitted by the complainant. This is true whether it was in possession of the training certificates at the time of the request (as seems to be indicated by the response of June 28, 2022, attached to the complaint) or whether it had already deleted them by that time (as indicated by the Company’s statement made to the Authority, see response of 18.4.2023, “on July 7, 2022, an email was received from the [complainant] stating that the certificates were not in possession because, following the termination of the relationship, believing that the company was no longer obliged to retain them as they contained sensitive data, they were deleted from the archive”).

In this regard, it is recalled that the training certificates of the worker contain personal data related to him, to which the interested party has the right to access pursuant to Article 15 of the Regulation. In this regard, the Guidelines 01/2022 on data subject rights – Right of access, EDPB, March 28, 2023, clarify that “In some cases, the personal data itself sets the requirements in what format the personal data should be provided” (see par. 5.2.5, point 155, unofficial translation “In some cases, the personal data itself defines the requirements of the format to be used”).

In any case, if the data controller cannot or does not believe it is obliged to comply with a request to exercise rights (including, therefore, those to exercise the right of access), it must communicate to the interested party the specific reasons for the denial as well as the possibility of filing a complaint with the Guarantor or appealing to the ordinary judicial authority pursuant to Article 12, paragraph 4 of the Regulation. In this case, the Company did not follow the aforementioned conduct. Furthermore, as confirmed by the actions taken by the company during the investigation, it was able to recover (at least in part) the certificates related to the interested party and subject to a specific access request.

**Reference to Article 12 of the Regulation in relation to Article 15 of the Regulation.**

The violation, ascertained in the terms set out in the reasoning, cannot be considered "minor," taking into account the nature, the severity of the violation itself, the degree of responsibility, and the manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the violation (Recital 148 of the Regulation).

The Authority also took into account the average level of severity of the violation in light of all relevant factors in the specific case, in particular the nature, severity, and duration of the violation, considering the nature, purpose, or aim of the processing in question as well as the number of affected individuals harmed by the damage and the level of damage they suffered.

The Authority also considered the criteria relating to the intentional or negligent nature of the violation and the categories of personal data affected by the violation, as well as the manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the violation (see Article 83, paragraph 2, and Recital 148 of the Regulation).

Therefore, in light of the corrective powers granted by Article 58, paragraph 2 of the Regulation, and considering the specific case, an administrative monetary penalty is imposed pursuant to Article 83 of the Regulation, commensurate with the circumstances of the specific case (Article 58, paragraph 2, letter i of the Regulation).

In this context, considering, in any case, that the conduct has exhausted its effects, given that the Company has declared that it has provided the interested party with the training certificates of which it managed to obtain a copy and has stated regarding "the DPI 3 Cat course that the company Siapa informed, via certified email on 21/03/2023, that there was no certificate in favor of the employee," the prerequisites for the adoption of further corrective measures under Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not exist.

**4. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative monetary penalty and ancillary sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i), and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code).**

As a result of the proceedings, it appears that C.I.EL. S.p.A. violated Article 12 of the Regulation in relation to Article 15 of the Regulation. For the violation of the aforementioned provisions, the application of the administrative monetary penalty provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, letter b) of the Regulation is foreseen, through the adoption of an injunction order (Article 18, Law 24.11.1981, No. 689).

It is deemed necessary to apply paragraph 3 of Article 83 of the Regulation, which states that "If, in relation to the same processing or related processing, a data controller [...] violates, with intent or negligence, various provisions of this regulation, the total amount of the administrative monetary penalty shall not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation," the total amount of the penalty is calculated so as not to exceed the maximum penalty provided for by the same Article 83, paragraph 5.

With reference to the elements listed in Article 83, paragraph 2 of the Regulation for the purpose of applying the administrative monetary penalty and its quantification, considering that the penalty must "in any case [be] effective, proportionate, and dissuasive" (Article 83, paragraph 1 of the Regulation), it is represented that, in this case, the following circumstances have been considered:

a) in relation to the nature of the violation, it concerned cases punished more severely under Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation (exercise of rights); regarding the severity of the violation, the nature of the processing concerning the exercise of rights was taken into account, in particular the right of access to data; concerning the duration of the violation, it was considered that only following the opening of the investigation did the interested party receive an appropriate response to the request (the response was ultimately provided just under eleven months after the submission of the request for the exercise of the right of access);

b) with reference to the intentional or negligent nature of the violation and the degree of responsibility of the controller, the negligent conduct of the Company, which did not provide an appropriate response to the request for the exercise of the right of access, was taken into consideration;

c) in favor of the controller, the cooperation of the Company with the Authority during the proceedings was considered.

It is also believed that the economic conditions of the offender, determined based on the revenues achieved by the Company concerning the ordinary financial statements for the year 2022, are relevant in this case, taking into account the aforementioned principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and dissuasiveness to which the Authority must adhere in determining the amount of the penalty (Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation). Lastly, the amount of penalties imposed in similar cases is taken into account.

In light of the above elements and the evaluations made, it is deemed appropriate, in this specific case, to impose on C.I.EL. S.p.A. the administrative penalty of payment of an amount equal to €10,000 (ten thousand).
