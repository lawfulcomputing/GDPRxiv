**2.2. Information provided by the Customs and Monopolies Agency**

In response to a request for information sent by the Authority pursuant to art. 157 of the Code, in a note dated March 13, 2024, the Customs and Monopolies Agency stated, in particular, that: 
“the establishment of a concession relationship presupposes […] a reliability assessment, a judgment that must concern the overall position of the applicant, regardless of filters, even legitimate, represented by companies of which the subject holds the position of partner or administrator. This assessment can only be made considering also elements (which include significant tax liabilities) pertaining to the subject requesting the issuance of a concession, in any capacity and in any role, therefore also in the capacity of legal representative of a legal entity (it must obviously be considered that liabilities not connected in any way to the work activity are excluded); 
“the investigation conducted in this case by the competent Monopolies Office - in strict adherence to the provisions of ADM circular no. 28 of July 22, 2021 implementing the ministerial decree of February 21, 2013, no. 38, 'Regulation governing the distribution and sale of tobacco products' as amended by ministerial decree of February 12, 2021, no. 5112 - takes into account the relevance that law December 22, 1957, no. 1293 recognizes to the requirements and subjective conditions of the natural person requesting (for themselves or, as in this case, in the name and on behalf of the interested economic operator of which they are the legal representative) such concession”; in particular, art. 2, paragraph 4-ter, of the aforementioned ministerial decree “(as well as other provisions of the same decree mentioned below [including art. 5, paragraph 4]) explicitly refers to the figure of the applicant, which in the case of a company, is identified in its legal representative”; 
the provisions of circular no. 28/2023, mentioned above, “always refer to the figure of the applicant (and, therefore, to the direct interlocutor of ADM) and not generically to the subject interested in the issuance or renewal, which in the case of special sales points can be a natural person or a legal person. The rationale of the provisions dictated by the repeatedly mentioned ministerial decree finds its reason in the nature of the fiduciary relationship that must exist between the public administration and the concessionaire and which, especially in the delicate sectors of application of excise duties - energy, alcohol, or tobacco - requires the tax administration to take into account all elements that, directly or indirectly, allow confirming the overall reliability of the concessionaire”; 
“nor has the principle of correctness been violated, as the personal data in question have not been processed in a prejudicial, discriminatory, unexpected, or misleading manner for the interested party but have rather been processed for determined, legitimate, and explicit purposes (investigative checks aimed at processing the renewal application for the sale of monopoly goods and checks on the veracity of the declarations made in this context).”
No wrongdoing or deception has been committed, as the Office has obtained tax data and information from the Revenue Agency - Collection as prescribed by circular no. 28/2021 adopted by this Agency on July 22, 2021, and duly published on the institutional website of the Agency; "the principle of data minimization has also been respected, as only the data essential for the described purposes have been processed, namely the data necessary to verify the existence of 'any tax violations and situations of arrears' attributable to the legal representative that could hinder the renewal of the concession as they are related to the work activity carried out"; "examining the statement regarding the tax liabilities of the appellant [and acquired from the Revenue Agency - Collection as part of the checks carried out], it is evident that its nature is that of a technical document in which, to indicate the creditor Entities of the various debt items, terms are used whose reading does not allow discerning to which type of tax they can be traced (e.g., 'Financial Administration - Regional Directorate of Valle d'Aosta - Territorial Office Aosta' and 'Financial Administration - Regional Directorate of Valle d'Aosta - Control Office'). The Office of Monopolies therefore found itself in the need to acquire further informational elements to verify whether these debt items towards the Treasury could be classified within those for which the sector regulation recognizes the value of an obstacle to the acceptance of the application or whether, instead, they were 'tax liabilities not related to the work activity carried out.' Given the repeated lack of response to requests aimed at obtaining the substitute declaration completed by the legal representative of the company in a personal capacity, the Office of Monopolies correctly proceeded to communicate the notice of rejection of the application, due to the potential obstacle represented by the debt position of the legal representative of the company, which was significantly above the minimum threshold indicated by the regulations"; "the sending of the aforementioned statement was [...] necessary to allow the exercise of the right to participate in the procedure so as to represent to the Office of Monopolies any informational element deemed useful to allow the continuation of the investigative activities and avert the rejection of the application"; "the aforementioned notice of rejection was notified by the competent Office of Monopolies to the certified email address of the company, declared by the same appellant, as the legal representative of the same, at the time of submitting the renewal application. This communication was certainly referable to the application submitted by XX and, therefore, the Office correctly (or rather, dutifully) used the company's PEC address, expressly indicated by the appellant as the sole reference for the communication related to the investigation following the renewal application. It is important to emphasize that during the investigative activities conducted in this case, the competent Office of Monopolies also used other communication channels (primarily non-certified email addresses) whenever the exchange of information did not pertain to personal data, while it exclusively used certified email when it had to report the existence of 'any tax violations and situations of arrears' attributable to the legal representative that could hinder the renewal of the concession as they are related to the work activity carried out. This was precisely to limit the circulation of the same data and protect - to the extent possible - the right to the confidentiality of the interested party."

3. The legislation on the protection of personal data and the applicable sector regulations  
In general, the data controller is required to comply with the principle of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency" as per Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a), of the Regulation.  
In particular, public entities may process personal data if the processing is necessary to comply with "a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject" or "for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of public authority vested in the data controller" (Article 6, paragraph 1, letters c) and e), of the Regulation).  
The legal basis for processing carried out in the public sector must in any case meet specific requirements, both in terms of the quality of the source, necessary contents, and appropriate and specific measures to protect the rights and freedoms of the data subjects, as well as in terms of the proportionality of the regulatory intervention concerning the legitimate objective to be pursued (Article 6, paragraphs 2 and 3, letter b), of the Regulation), in addition to having to be "suitable" also in light of the structure of the sources of the "constitutional order" of the Member State (see Recital 41 of the Regulation).  
In this context, national legislation has introduced more specific provisions to adapt the application of the rules of the Regulation, determining with greater precision the specific requirements for such processing and other measures aimed at ensuring its lawfulness and fairness (Article 6, paragraph 2, of the Regulation), and, in this context, has provided that the processing referred to in Article 6, paragraph 1, letters c) and e), of the Regulation, carried out pursuant to national regulatory provisions, is permitted only in the presence of an appropriate legal basis, consisting of a law, regulation, or general administrative act (Article 2-ter of the Code).  
As far as relevant for the case at hand, it is particularly noted that the sector legislation:  
- refers to the figure of the "concessionaire" where it regulates the methods of renewal of the concessions for the management of special sales (see Article 19, last paragraph, of Law December 22, 1957, no. 1293, concerning "Organization of distribution and sale services of monopoly goods");  
- provides that "for the purpose of assignment, pursuant to Articles 19 and following of Law December 22, 1957, no. 1293, of the management of the established sales outlets, […the] applicant must present a substitute declaration of notoriety drawn up pursuant to the decree of the President of the Republic December 28, 2000, no. 445, regarding the existence of any tax violations and situations of arrears towards the Treasury or towards the Collection Agent of an amount exceeding that provided for by Article 80, paragraph 4, of Legislative Decree April 18, 2016, no. 50, definitively established or resulting from judgments no longer appealable" (Article 2, paragraph 4-ter, of the Decree of the Ministry of Economy and Finance of February 21, 2013, no. 38 - "Regulation").
**Regulations on the Distribution and Sale of Tobacco Products**  
- Among the reasons for exclusion from participation in concession procedures concerning debt situations - such as, for example, those of a fiscal nature - relevance is attributed solely to events concerning the "economic operator" (Articles 2, paragraph 1, letter p), and 80, paragraph 4, of Legislative Decree No. 50 of April 18, 2016), limiting the reasons for exclusion related to legal representatives to the definitive conviction suffered for one of the specific crimes listed therein (Article 80, paragraphs 1-3, of Legislative Decree No. 50 of April 18, 2016).  
Finally, it is noted that the data controller is, in any case, required to comply with the principles of data protection and, in particular, among these, those of "data minimization" and "data protection by design" and "data protection by default" (Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter c), and 25 of the Regulation).  

**4. Outcome of the Investigative Activity**  
That said, and in light of what emerged from the preliminary investigation and the statements made by the data controller, it has been established that the Customs and Monopolies Agency has acquired and used, in the context of carrying out the controls provided for by Circular No. 28/2021 and in order to verify the recurrence of the requirements for the issuance to the company XX. of the renewal act of the concession referred to in Article 19 of Law No. 1293 of December 22, 1957, information concerning the debt position of Mr. XX, as a private citizen and legal representative of XX, subsequently communicating it to the certified email address of the company itself in the form attached to the resolution rejecting the request.  
As far as the aforementioned Circular No. 28/2021 is concerned, it emerged that it provided for this Agency the acquisition and use of information concerning the debt positions of the legal representatives of the applicant companies, which entailed processing aimed at preventing evasive activities of the regulatory system applicable to concessionaires, thus allowing, in the first instance, the collection and processing of "all elements that, directly or indirectly, allow for the confirmation of the overall reliability of the concessionaire" (see note of March 13, 2024). Only subsequently, following the conduct of the deemed appropriate checks, the aforementioned circular introduced a measure that allowed for the decision not to consider for the purposes of evaluating the request "tax liabilities not related to the work activity carried out" (see Circular No. 28/2021) - a measure that, moreover, in this case, was concretely disregarded and implemented only following the request for filing of the rejection by XX. with a note dated January 3, 2023.  
In this way, the aforementioned Circular No. 28/2021 provided indications regarding the systematic, aprioristic, and generalized acquisition of even inadequate, relevant, and limited personal data to what is necessary concerning the pursued processing purpose, such data being referable to debt situations incurred by the legal representative of the concessionaire as a private citizen and a separate center of attribution of obligations. This was done without taking into account the risks to the rights and freedoms of the interested party, including in particular the harm to their reputation caused by the disclosure to the executives and personnel in charge of the company of such information, which, as they pertain to the personal sphere of the individual and are not connected to the corporate domain, are attributable to the private life of the interested party, whose protection is guaranteed by the regulatory framework even at the supranational level (see Article 8, paragraph 2, of the European Convention on Human Rights and Article 7 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union and, for data protection aspects, Recital 1 of the Regulation).  
While noting that, in any case, in the legal system, administrative circulars can contain specifications and detailed provisions, in terms of personal data processing, only within the limits and areas established by laws or regulations (Article 2-ter of the Code), it is highlighted that the primary and secondary sector regulatory framework provides that checks regarding debt situations are carried out exclusively concerning concessionary enterprises (see Article 19, last paragraph, of Law No. 1293 of December 22, 1957; Article 2, paragraph 4-ter, of the Decree of the Ministry of Economy and Finance of February 21, 2013, No. 38).  
Conversely, it appears that, in this case, the Customs and Monopolies Agency has acquired and processed personal data related to debts incurred by the legal representative of XX, as a private citizen and a separate center of attribution of obligations, against a regulation that provides that such specific information should refer to the companies themselves (see Article 80 of Legislative Decree No. 50 of April 18, 2016). This was done in a manner not compliant with the principles of lawfulness, fairness, and transparency, "data minimization," data protection by design, and data protection by default, as well as in the absence of an adequate regulatory basis, in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and c), 6, and 25 of the Regulation and Article 2-ter of the Code.  
The above has also been acknowledged by the Customs and Monopolies Agency in the draft revision of the aforementioned Circular No. 28/2021, which then resulted in Circular No. 14/2023 of June 8, 2023, which "partially revised the assessments made in implementing Article 2, paragraph 4-ter of the ministerial decree of February 21, 2013, No. 38 through the adoption of Circular No. 28/2021."
[...] in fact eliminates the controls on the role of the legal representative [...] and limits them to entirely residual hypotheses in which the competent Offices of the monopolies must nonetheless correspond directly with the legal representative, without involving third parties” (cf. note of March 13, 2024). 

Nor does the circumstance that, finally, according to the Agency itself, “the practices adopted within the business reality in which the complainant works [...] have led to the communication [...] of the Agency, directed to the same complainant (and forwarded to the certified email address used by the complainant), being 'passed from hand to hand within XX' without any filter, allowing that 'first the employees of the Secretariat Office, then the members of the Board of Directors' could have full knowledge of it” (cf. note of July 25, 2024), matter, given that the violation of the regulations on personal data protection - which is attributable, in the terms indicated above, to the Agency - is independent of any processing that may have been carried out by the company XX through the persons appointed for that purpose.

5. Conclusions. 
In light of the evaluations mentioned above, it is noted that the statements made by the data controller during the investigation - the truthfulness of which may be subject to accountability under Article 168 of the Code - although deserving of consideration, do not allow for overcoming the objections notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure and are insufficient to allow for the archiving of the present procedure pursuant to Article 14, paragraph 1, of the Garante Regulation no. 1/2019, as none of the cases provided for by Article 11 referenced therein apply. 

Therefore, the preliminary assessments of the Office are confirmed, and the illegality of the processing of personal data carried out by the Agency of Customs and Monopolies is noted on the assumption that the processing of the complainant's personal data - consisting, in particular, of the acquisition and subsequent communication to XX of the complainant's data - was carried out in a manner not compliant with the principles of lawfulness, fairness, and transparency, “data minimization,” data protection by design and by default, as well as in the absence of an appropriate legal basis, in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and c), 6, and 25 of the Regulation and Article 2-ter of the Code. 

Considering that the violation of the aforementioned provisions occurred as a result of a single conduct, Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation applies, according to which the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation. Given that, in this case, the most serious violations are subject to the sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation, as also referenced by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code, the total amount of the sanction is to be quantified up to €20,000,000. 

In this context, considering, in any case, that the conduct has exhausted its effects, also taking into account the adoption by the Agency of Customs and Monopolies of circular no. 14/2023 of June 8, 2023, which introduced corrections to circular no. 28/2021, the prerequisites for the adoption of further corrective measures referred to in Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not exist.

**2. Relevant Aspects Pursuant to Article 83, Paragraph 2, Letter b) of the Regulation**

In this specific case, the notice of rejection of the renewal request (see act no. XX of XX) was adopted by the Agency and notified to the company without considering that Circular no. 28/2021, while also providing for the collection of data concerning legal representatives, established that for these individuals, “tax liabilities not related to the work performed should not be considered” (see Circular no. 28/2021, in the version applicable to the matter raised by the complainant). **3. Relevant Aspects Pursuant to Article 83, Paragraph 2, Letter g) of the Regulation**

The processing in question involved personal data that, although not belonging to the special categories referred to in Article 9 of the Regulation or coinciding with those related to criminal convictions or offenses under Article 10 of the Regulation, nonetheless pertains to the private life of the data subject and presents a particular sensitivity and delicacy, concerning the debtor situation of the data subject.

**4. Assessment of the Violation**

It is believed that, in this case, the level of severity of the violation committed by the data controller is medium (see European Data Protection Board, “Guidelines 4/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines pursuant to the GDPR” of May 24, 2023, point 60). 

**5. Mitigating Circumstances**

In light of the above, it is considered that, for the purpose of quantifying the fine, the following mitigating circumstances should be taken into account: 

- In the context of the matter in question, in order to mitigate the harmful consequences of the violation, the data subject “was promptly heard by the competent Monopolies Office the day after the notice of rejection of the renewal request was submitted, formalized on December 19, 2022,” and “the assessment of the additional information acquired led the Monopolies Office to reassess the data in its possession and to archive the aforementioned notice of rejection with an act dated February 15, 2023” (see note of July 25, 2024; see Article 83, paragraph 1, letters c) and k) of the Regulation).

- With a view to accountability, the Agency implemented, even before the initiation of the investigation by the Authority, technical and organizational measures aimed at mitigating the risk of similar violations occurring, given that “Circular 14/2023 has already standardized and disseminated the new model to be submitted in support of the renewal request for a concession for the public sale of monopoly goods, carried out through special resale, which has been promptly made available online on the official website of the Agency of Customs and Monopolies.” Furthermore, “currently, for the purpose of adopting a level of security appropriate to the risk for the rights and freedoms of individuals, as well as for the implementation of data protection by design and by default, the competent central structure [of the Agency of Customs and Monopolies] is working on drafting specific operational guidelines (and interpretative of the repeatedly mentioned Circular 14/2023) to reiterate that, under current law, declarations regarding tax liabilities concern exclusively the holder of the resale (in the case where it is registered to a natural person) or the company (in cases where it is the holder of a resale)” (see note of July 25, 2024; see Article 83, paragraph 2, letters d) and k) of the Regulation).

- The Agency of Customs and Monopolies has offered full cooperation with the Authority during the investigation (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter f) of the Regulation).

**6. Aggravating Circumstances**

Additionally, as an aggravating circumstance, there is a previous relevant violation committed by the Agency of Customs and Monopolies (see provision of March 7, 2019, no. 56, web doc no. 9123989; Article 83, paragraph 2, letter e) of the Regulation).

**7. Conclusion**

In light of the aforementioned elements, assessed in their entirety, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the administrative fine at €40,000.00 (forty thousand/00) for the violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and c), 6, and 25 of the Regulation and Article 2-ter of the Code, as an administrative pecuniary sanction deemed, pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.

In this context, it is also considered that, pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Regulation of the Guarantor no. 1/2019, the publication of this section containing the injunction order on the Guarantor's website should proceed. 

This is in consideration of the fact that the processing involved, in this case, personal data characterized by particular sensitivity and delicacy, such as those related to the debtor conditions of the complainant, as well as due to the...
general scope assumed by the processing during the period in which the Agency, in carrying out the controls for which it is responsible, applied the aforementioned circular no. 28/2021 prior to the adoption of circular no. 14/2023 of June 8, 2023. It is finally noted that the conditions referred to in Article 17 of Regulation no. 1/2019 are met.
