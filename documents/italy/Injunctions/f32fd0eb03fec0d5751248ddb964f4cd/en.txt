**3. Outcome of the Investigative Activity.**  
**3.1 The Regulatory Framework.**  
The regulation on the protection of personal data provides that public entities, even when acting in the performance of their duties as employers, may process personal data of workers if the processing is necessary, in general, for the management of the employment relationship and to fulfill specific obligations or tasks provided for by national sector regulations (Articles 6, paragraph 1, letter c), 9, paragraphs 2, letter b), and 4, and 88 of the Regulation) or “for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or related to the exercise of public powers vested in the data controller” (Article 6, paragraph 1, letter e), of the Regulation).  
Such processing must be based on Union or Member State law, which must pursue a public interest objective and be proportionate to the pursuit of the same. The purpose of the processing must be necessary for the execution of a task carried out in the public interest or related to the exercise of public powers.  
The employer, as the data controller, is, in any case, required to comply with the general principles regarding the protection of personal data (Article 5 of the Regulation) and must process the data through “authorized” and “trained” personnel regarding access and processing of data (Articles 4, point 10), 29, and 32, paragraph 4, of the Regulation).  

**3.2 The Processing of Personal Data through the IT Protocol System.**  
As is well known, since 2007, the Guarantor has clarified that the administration must adopt technical and organizational measures to prevent the unjustified knowledge of personal data of its employees by other colleagues or third parties, in order to avoid the improper circulation of personal information, not only externally but also within the workplace among unauthorized subjects. This is because making data available to subjects who, although part of the data controller's organization, cannot be considered “authorized” to process (see Articles 4, no. 10, 28, paragraph 3, letter b), 29, and 32, paragraph 4, of the Regulation, as well as Article 2-quaterdecies of the Code), due to their role and functions exercised within that organization, may lead to a communication of personal data without a legal basis (see points 2, 4, 5.1, and 5.3 of the “Guidelines on the Processing of Personal Data of Workers for Employment Relationship Management in the Public Sector,” of June 14, 2007, published in G.U. July 13, 2007, no. 161, and at www.garanteprivacy.it, doc. web no. 1417809).  
In this regard, according to the consolidated orientation of the Guarantor, recently confirmed in numerous provisions on individual cases, the personal data of employees cannot be disclosed to subjects other than those who are part of the specific employment relationship, taking into account the definitions of “personal data” and “data subject,”
the following measures:

1. **Imposition of Administrative Fine**  
   The Garante, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation, as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the authority to impose an administrative fine as per Article 83, in addition to other corrective measures outlined in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case.

2. **Consideration of Circumstances**  
   In determining the amount of the fine, the Garante shall take into account the nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, the intentional or negligent character of the infringement, any actions taken by the controller or processor to mitigate the damage suffered by data subjects, the degree of responsibility of the controller or processor, any previous infringements by the controller or processor, the degree of cooperation with the supervisory authority, and the categories of personal data affected by the infringement.

3. **Final Decision**  
   The Garante shall issue a final decision regarding the imposition of the administrative fine and any additional sanctions, ensuring that the decision is proportionate to the infringement and takes into account the mitigating factors presented by the controller.

4. **Notification of Decision**  
   The decision shall be communicated to the parties involved, providing them with the opportunity to appeal the decision in accordance with applicable laws.

5. **Implementation of Corrective Measures**  
   The Garante may also require the implementation of specific corrective measures to ensure compliance with the Regulation and the protection of personal data.

6. **Conclusion**  
   In light of the above considerations, the Garante confirms the preliminary assessments of the Office and finds the processing of personal data carried out by the Azienda Sanitaria to be unlawful for having conducted personal data processing without a legal basis, in violation of Articles 5, 6 of the Regulation, as well as Article 2-ter of the Code. 

7. **Final Remarks**  
   Given that the violation of the aforementioned provisions occurred as a result of a single conduct (the same processing or interrelated treatments), Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation applies, under which the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation. Considering that, in this case, the most serious violations related to Articles 5, 6 of the Regulation, as well as 2-ter of the Code, are subject to the sanction provided for in Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation, as referenced also by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code, the total amount of the sanction is to be quantified up to €20,000,000.

8. **Final Decision on Corrective Measures**  
   In this context, considering that the conduct has exhausted its effects and taking into account the organizational and technical measures implemented by the Azienda Sanitaria, the conditions for the adoption of further corrective measures pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not exist.

Considering that:
- with specific regard to the nature, severity, and duration of the violation, it should be noted that the processing occurred in relation to the personal data of only one worker (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a), of the Regulation);
- with specific regard to the subjective profile, the violation was committed following a mere material error in the management of activities related to the IT protocol (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter b), of the Regulation);
- the processing involved very sensitive data related to the complainant's employment in reference to a disciplinary procedure (cf. Article 83, paragraph 2, letter g), of the Regulation),

it is believed that, in this case, the level of severity of the violation committed by the data controller is medium (cf. European Data Protection Board, “Guidelines 4/2022 on the calculation of administrative pecuniary sanctions pursuant to the GDPR” of May 24, 2023, point 60). 

That being said, it is believed that, for the purposes of quantifying the sanction, the following circumstances should be taken into consideration:
- the Health Authority has offered, from the outset, good cooperation with the Authority during the investigation (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter f), of the Regulation);
- there are some previous violations, even if in a different context (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter e), of the Regulation);
- the violation occurred during a period characterized by numerous organizational difficulties, particularly for Health Authorities, related to the issues of the emergency period characterized by the spread of the Sars-cov 2 virus (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter k), of the Regulation).

In light of the aforementioned elements, evaluated as a whole, it is believed to determine the amount of the pecuniary sanction at the sum of 4,000 euros (four thousand) for the violation of Articles 5, 6 of the Regulation, as well as Article 2-ter of the Code, as an administrative pecuniary sanction deemed, pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.
