1. The complaint against the company and the investigative activity.  
On May 28, 2020, Mr. XX, XX, XX, and XX filed a complaint against Shardana Working Soc. Coop. a r.l. (hereinafter, the Company), which was finally regularized on September 23, 2020, alleging presumed violations of the Regulation.  
Following a conciliation before the Court of Milan, Mr. XX withdrew the complaint filed with the Guarantor; therefore, the complaint, exclusively concerning the issues related to Mr. XX, was archived.  
In particular, the complaint alleged the impossibility of fully exercising the right of access under Article 15 of the Regulation regarding the "processing of personal data and [the] information used to process mileage reimbursements and hourly monthly wages as well as the logic of processing" by the Company and the "data concerning the [complainants] as well as the authorization documents in […] possession [of the Company] with which information on data processing and the terminal is formulated."  
This was also following a request to exercise rights dated November 22, 2019, concerning Mr. XX and XX, and January 23, 2020, regarding Mr. XX, given the deemed partial response that the Company would have provided on December 3, 2019, concerning Mr. XX and on March 25, 2020, regarding Mr. XX.  
On February 11, 2021, following an invitation to adhere, the Company stated that:  
"with regard to the purposes, duration, methods of storage, and data processing processes [...], it is noted that the Company has already provided extensive feedback, through communications dated 3.12.2019 and 25.03.2020 [...], in which it forwarded to the complainants' lawyer: the Information pursuant to Article 13 GDPR duly signed by the [complainants]; the Regulation concerning the processing and the security standards to be observed in the use of electronic and non-electronic tools; the Consent Declarations for processing duly signed; the Indication of the data used, with the relevant storage period, as well as the criteria for justifying its use; the Indication of the data controller" (see note 11.2.2021, cited, p. 1, 2);  
"it was also specified the methods and tools of satellite geolocation (carried out through devices - smartphones - mobile telephony: models Samsung Galaxy Xcover2, Xcover3, Galaxy 7, with Android operating systems, equipped with GPS satellite localization systems, assigned to employees as work tools). The Company highlighted that geolocation is necessary to allow the operator to identify the route to be taken to reach the meters, since the terminal indicates where to carry out the readings. This modus operandi also allows for verifying the requests of the client, aimed at checking the exact location of the readings (in case of irregularities and/or anomalies in the readings). It was also clarified that satellite localization is activated and limited only at the moment of turning on the devices and is deactivated with the shutdown of the terminal in the possession of the operator to whom every activity and discretion regarding it is exclusively entrusted" (see cited note, p. 2);  
"no control and/or monitoring has been carried out regarding the activities performed and/or the routes ("mileage") taken by the complainants" (see cited note, p. 2);  
"Shardana, pursuant to Article 4 L. 300/70, submitted a specific request on June 16, 2017, to the then DTL of Como, requesting authorization for the installation and use of GPS satellite localization on terminals for the detection of meter readings. On July 27, 2017, the DTL communicated that no authorization was necessary, as the aforementioned satellite system is a necessary tool for the performance of the work" (see cited note, p. 2);
“in relation to the further […] request to acquire ‘the content of the personal file, including the mileage monitoring of the work performance, daily duration of geolocation, as well as all documents concerning the work performance in any way processed and stored,’ it is only appropriate to note that Shardana has already sent all suitable documentation to the claimants” (see cited note, p. 3);  
“the present proceeding before the Authority [was] (entirely instrumentally) initiated solely to ‘support’ and compensate for the evidentiary shortcomings of the claimants in the labor cases already pending before the Labor Section of the Court of Milan between the same parties, concerning the recognition of alleged claims for wage differences” (see cited note, p. 3);  
“it is acknowledged that Shardana: - processes the data contained in the employee's file exclusively for purposes related to the employment relationship; - the processing of the data present in the personal file may, however, be carried out to assert or defend a right, even by a third party, in judicial proceedings, as well as in administrative proceedings; - the methods adopted for the processing of data are essentially automated and telematic in nature and are duly secured and, in any case, strictly comply with the principles of necessity, relevance, non-exceedance, and graduality; - the consultation and/or extraction of the information contained therein is ordered solely in cases where the processing is essential to continue the work activity or where it is necessary to exercise or defend a right in court; - the logic applied to the processing carried out with electronic tools (for geolocation purposes) is strictly related to the lawful purposes mentioned above; - in addition to the data controller, any subjects to whom the data may be communicated are exclusively those indicated in the information (already voluntarily provided to the claimants)” (see cited note, pp. 3, 4);  
“the Company […] makes itself (once again and as needed) available to provide a copy of the ‘personal files’ (within the limits and compatibly with what is actually in its possession), which may be made available to the interested parties for any further consultation” (see cited note, p. 4).  
On October 13, 2021, the Company, following the invitation from the Department dated September 17, 2021, provided further clarifications stating that “the alleged willingness to provide a copy of the personal file has, moreover, been misrepresented by the Authority, as the Company clearly deduced that the consultation of the file could have occurred (and once again) with reference to the documentation already provided ‘(within the limits and compatibly with what is actually in its possession)’” (see note dated October 13, 2022, p. 3).  
On September 22, 2022, following further requests from the Authority dated August 8, 2022, the Company also stated that:  
- “the disputes concerning the positions of the [claimants] have been concluded with provisions issued by the ordinary judiciary, which has, among other things, ascertained the right of the same to the recognition of the requested wage differences, which is why, also with reference to such positions, any interest in continuing the present proceeding before the Authority has ceased, resulting in its archiving” (see note dated September 22, 2022, p. 2);

2.2. Conclusions.
Based on the findings of the investigation and the applicable legal framework, it is concluded that the Company, in its capacity as data controller, has failed to comply with the regulations regarding the exercise of the right of access recognized to the data subject by the Regulation (Article 15), by not providing an adequate response to the requests made by the claimants. 

3. Sanctions and Measures.
3.1. Sanctions.
In light of the violations identified, the Authority may impose administrative fines in accordance with Article 83 of the Regulation, which establishes the conditions under which fines may be imposed, taking into account the nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, as well as any mitigating or aggravating factors.

3.2. Recommendations.
The Authority recommends that the Company take immediate corrective actions to ensure compliance with the data protection regulations, including but not limited to:
- Implementing appropriate procedures to handle requests for access to personal data effectively and within the timeframes established by the Regulation.
- Providing training to employees on data protection rights and obligations to prevent future non-compliance.
- Reviewing and updating privacy policies and practices to ensure transparency and clarity in communications with data subjects.

4. Final Remarks.
The Company is advised to take these findings seriously and to act promptly to rectify the identified issues. Failure to comply with the recommendations may lead to further enforcement actions by the Authority.

15 of the Regulation.
In this regard, it is necessary to examine in detail
the response and the attached documentation. This is
because it cannot be considered sufficient, to comply
with the aforementioned norms, to provide a response
if the latter is found to have insufficient content.
In particular, it has emerged that the Company did not
fully provide what was requested through the
complainants' requests, nor is it believed that the
documents provided by the Company contained the
information in a comprehensive manner, despite the
clarity and detail of the requests.
The complainants, in fact, submitted a request for
access to data related to the processing carried out
by the Company in order to calculate working hours
and mileage reimbursements, particularly regarding the
processing carried out through a “terminal” provided
by the Company (smartphone).
However, in formally responding to the requests, the
Company did not communicate the specific data
related to the complainants processed, among other
things, through geolocation on the terminal provided
to them in the context of their work performance, nor
all the information requested by the complainants

This, despite Article 12 par. 3 of the Regulation

data subject exercising one of the rights recognized
by the Regulation with the information requested by
them.
The Company, therefore, within the limits of the data
retained, should have provided the complainants with
the data related to the specific readings/geographical
coordinates obtained with the GPS of the smartphone
activated by the workers near the meter for reading
the same.
In this regard, it is recalled that the right granted

concrete terms.

**3. Conclusions: Declaration of Illegality of the Processing. Corrective Measures pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, Regulation.**  
For the reasons stated above, the Authority believes that the statements, documentation, and reconstructions provided by the data controller during the investigation do not allow for overcoming the observations notified by the Office with the initiation of the proceedings and are therefore unsuitable to allow for the closure of the present proceedings, as none of the cases provided for by Article 11 of the Garante Regulation No. 1/2019 apply. The conduct carried out by the Company, which consisted of providing an inadequate response to the access requests submitted by the complainants, is indeed illegal, as stated above, in relation to Articles 12 and 15 of the Regulation. The violation, established as outlined in the reasoning, cannot be considered "minor," taking into account the nature, severity, and duration of the violation itself, the degree of responsibility, and the manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the violation (cf. 148 of the Regulation).  
Therefore, in light of the corrective powers attributed by Article 58, paragraph 2 of the Regulation, in light of the specific case:  
- the Company is ordered to satisfy the access requests of the complainants, as formulated on November 22, 2019, and January 23, 2020 (Article 58, paragraph 2, letter c), Regulation), providing the complainants with the data relating to them as well as the information expressly requested in the complainants' requests, excluding what has already been limitedly indicated on December 3, 2019, and March 25, 2020;  
- an administrative pecuniary sanction is imposed pursuant to Article 83 of the Regulation, commensurate with the circumstances of the specific case (Article 58, paragraph 2, letter i) Regulation).

**4. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i), and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code).**  
As a result of the proceedings, it is found that Shardana Working Società Coop. a r.l. has violated Articles 12 and 15 of the Regulation. For the violation of the aforementioned provisions, the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, letter b) of the Regulation is foreseen, through the adoption of an injunction order (Article 18, Law 24.11.1981, No. 689).  
It is deemed necessary to apply paragraph 3 of Article 83 of the Regulation, which states that "If, in relation to the same processing or related processing, a data controller [...] violates, with intent or negligence, various provisions of this regulation, the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction shall not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation," the total amount of the sanction is calculated so as not to exceed the maximum penalty provided for by the same Article 83, paragraph 5.  
With reference to the elements listed in Article 83, paragraph 2 of the Regulation for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and its quantification, considering that the sanction must "in any case [be] effective, proportionate, and dissuasive" (Article 83, paragraph 1 of the Regulation), it is noted that, in this case, the following circumstances have been considered:

a) in relation to the nature, severity, and duration of the violation, the nature of the violation concerning the exercise of the rights of the interested party has been considered, as well as the duration of the violation itself, since the data controller did not provide an adequate response even after the intervention of the Authority; the number of interested parties, which is three, has also been evaluated;  
b) with reference to the intentional or negligent nature of the violation and the degree of responsibility of the controller, the persistent lack of an adequate response to the requests for the exercise of the right of access has been taken into account;  
c) in favor of the controller, the absence of previous relevant violations committed by the Company has been considered.  
It is also believed that the economic conditions of the offender, determined based on the revenues obtained by the Company with reference to the abbreviated financial statements for the year 2021, are relevant in this case, considering the aforementioned principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and dissuasiveness to which the Authority must adhere in determining the amount of the sanction (Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation). Lastly, the amount of sanctions imposed in similar cases is taken into account.  
In light of the above elements and the evaluations made, it is deemed appropriate, in this case, to apply to Shardana Working Soc. Coop. a r.l. the administrative sanction of payment of an amount equal to €20,000.00 (twenty thousand).  
In this context, it is also deemed appropriate, considering the type of violations established that concerned the exercise of the rights of the interested party, that pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Garante Regulation No. 1/2019, the publication of this provision on the Garante's website should be carried out.
It is also considered that the prerequisites of Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 are met.
