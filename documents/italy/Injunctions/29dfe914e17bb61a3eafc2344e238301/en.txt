**3. Outcome of the investigative activity.**  
**3.1 The processing of personal data carried out by the Municipality.**  
Based on what emerged during the investigation, it is established - and not disputed - that the Municipality, as the data controller, utilizing the support of APM, which acted as the data processor, carried out the processing of personal data from March 2021 to June 9, 2022 (see note from the Company of XX, prot. n.XX, on file), through the use of video devices installed near three ecological stations (four devices for each station), in order to prevent and detect acts of vandalism or events relevant to the safety of the facilities or the safety of users.  
The devices in question, which were activated by motion sensors, had a storage capacity limited to a maximum of twenty-four/thirty hours, after which the images were automatically overwritten, being able to record, from time to time, both during the day and at night, images related to an area confined to one and a half meters from the facility, with a maximum duration of fifteen seconds, on a “secure digital” (SD) type memory, located inside each video device.  

**3.2 Transparency of the processing**  
In compliance with the principle of “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency,” the data controller must adopt appropriate measures to provide the data subject, before starting the processing, with all the information required by the Regulation in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible form, using simple and clear language (Articles 5, par. 1, lett. a), 12 and 13 of the Regulation).  
When video devices are used, the data controller, in addition to providing the first-level information through the placement of warning signage near the area under video surveillance, must also provide the data subjects with “second-level information,” which must “contain all the mandatory elements pursuant to Article 13 of the [Regulation]” and “be easily accessible to the data subject, for example through a complete information page made available at a central hub […] or posted in an easily accessible location” (“Guidelines 3/2019 on the processing of personal data through video devices” of the European Data Protection Board, adopted on January 29, 2020, particularly par. 7; but see also the “Provision on video surveillance” of the Guarantor of April 8, 2010, doc. web n. 1712680, particularly par. 3.1; lastly, see the FAQs of the Guarantor regarding video surveillance, doc. web n. 9496574, n. 4; cf. also provisions of January 11, 2024, n. 5, doc. web n. 9977020; October 20, 2022, n. 341, doc. web n. 9831369; April 28, 2022, n. 162, doc. web n. 9777974; April 7, 2022, n. 119, doc. web n. 9773950; September 16, 2021, n. 327, doc. web n. 9705650 and March 11, 2021, n. 90, doc. web n. 9582791).  
The first-level information (warning sign) “should communicate the most important data, such as the purposes of the processing, the identity of the data controller, and the existence of the rights of the data subject, along with information on the most significant impacts of the processing” (Committee Guidelines, cited, par. 114). Furthermore, the signage must also contain those pieces of information that could be unexpected for the data subject. This could include, for example, the transmission of data to third parties, particularly if located outside the European Union, and the retention period. If such information is not indicated, the data subject should be able to rely on the fact that there is only real-time surveillance (without any data recording or transmission to third parties) (Committee Guidelines, cited, par. 115). The first-level warning signage must contain a clear reference to the second level of information, for example by indicating a website where the text can be consulted.
of the extended notice.  
That said, it is noted that during the investigation, the Municipality declared that “in light of the characteristics of the system put in place to protect the Ecological Stations […] it was not deemed necessary to post any informational sign.”  
As evidenced in the documents, the Municipality had therefore not provided a first-level notice (warning sign) to the interested parties regarding the processing of personal data carried out through the video devices in question.  
In this regard, the assessment made by the Municipality regarding the non-existence of the necessity to provide a notice to the interested parties is incorrect, based on the assumption that the “image detection system [subject of the complaint] was not designed as a ‘video surveillance system’ but rather as a ‘security alert system.’” This is because such a system, regardless of the specific purpose pursued, involved the acquisition and recording of footage depicting natural persons, an activity that undoubtedly constitutes the processing of personal data.  
It should be noted that the Regulation defines “personal data” as “any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘data subject’)” (art. 4, par. 1, n. 1). The use of the expression “any information,” also used in the analogous definition in art. 2, lett. a), of Directive 95/46, “reflects the objective of the Union legislator to attribute a broad meaning to this notion, which is not limited to sensitive or private information, but potentially includes any type of information, both objective and subjective […] provided that it is ‘concerning’ the data subject. Regarding this last condition, it is satisfied if, by reason of its content, purpose, or effect, the information is connected to a specific person” (Court of Justice of the European Union, judgment of December 20, 2017, C-434/16, Nowak, para. 34).  
Based on the case law of the Court of Justice of the European Union regarding the processing of personal data through video devices, it is well established that the image of a person's face constitutes personal data and that the recording of such an image involves the processing of personal data (see judgments of October 20, 2022, Koalitsia "Demokratichna Bulgaria - Obedinenie," C 306/21, para. 32, February 14, 2019, C 345/17, Buivids, paras. 31 and 32, and December 11, 2014, C 212/13, Ryneš, paras. 22 and 25), with the circumstance that the data controller does not know the identity of the person in question or does not have information that would allow them to identify the same being entirely irrelevant (cf. recital 26 of the Regulation, which specifies that, to determine the identifiability of a person, it is appropriate to consider all means that not only the data controller but also a third party can reasonably use to identify that natural person directly or indirectly; in case law, see Cass. Civ., judgment no. 17440 of September 2, 2015, where it is stated that “it does not seem possible to doubt that the image constitutes personal data […] being an immediately identifiable data point for a person, regardless of their notoriety”). It is, in fact, theoretically possible to trace the identity of a person from the image of their face, particularly using information that is in the possession of third parties (for example, public or private databases) or publicly available (for example, social networks), considering that “for a data to be qualified as ‘personal data’ […] it is not required that all the information that allows identifying the data subject be in the possession of a single person” (C 434/16, Nowak, cited, para. 31; see also judgment of October 19, 2016, Breyer, C 582/14, para. 43; see also provision of April 13, 2023, no. 22, of April 13, 2023).  
This is also implicitly confirmed in recital 51 of the Regulation, when it states that “the processing of photographs should not systematically constitute the processing of special categories of personal data, as they fall within the definition of biometric data only when processed through a specific technical device that allows the unique identification or authentication of a natural person,” from which it follows that the image of a person, such as that depicted in a photograph or video, constitutes personal data, although it is not systematically a personal data related to special categories as per art. 9 of the Regulation.  
Regarding the further defense put forward by the Municipality, concerning the circumstance that the informational signs related to the distinct municipal video surveillance system could be considered sufficient to inform the interested parties of the processing carried out, it should be noted that they—placed “close to the Historic Center” and not near the aforementioned ecological stations—referred to the different purpose of urban security, namely the prevention of widespread and predatory criminal phenomena (cf. art. 5, par. 2, lett. a), of Legislative Decree February 20, 2017, no. 14), and were therefore inadequate to ensure the transparency of the distinct and specific processing.  
The Municipality has also not proven that it provided the interested parties with a complete notice regarding the processing of their personal data through the video devices in question, having limited itself to stating, in response to specific requests for clarification from the Authority, that “the Municipality has
published the extended information on video surveillance on its website” (note of XX, cited) and that “the update of the information is of XX” (note of XX), a date subsequent to that of the complaint submission, therefore the Entity has neither declared nor demonstrated that the date on which such information would have been made available to the interested parties was attributable to before the start of the processing, that is, before March 2021. It is also noted that the information produced in the records is devoid of any temporal reference, making it impossible to place this document in time in any way.

The processing of the personal data of the interested parties, captured by the video devices in question, was therefore carried out in a manner not compliant with the principle of “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency” and in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 12, paragraph 1, and 13 of the Regulation.

3.3 The relationship with the data processor
Pursuant to Article 28, paragraph 3, of the Regulation, “processing by a processor shall be governed by a contract or other legal act under Union law or the law of the Member States, which binds the processor to the controller, which stipulates the subject matter and duration of the processing, the nature and purpose of the processing, the type of personal data and categories of data subjects, the obligations and rights of the controller,” and which provides for all the commitments required by the same Article 28, paragraph 3, of the Regulation (see recital 81 of the Regulation).

The contract or other legal act must be “in writing, including in electronic form” (Article 28, paragraph 9, of the Regulation). 

In this case, the Municipality, as the data controller, outsourced to APM the activity of installing and managing the devices in question, entrusting it with the processing of the captured images, without, however, previously entering into a data protection contract pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation.

The data protection contract prepared by the Municipality, registered under protocol number XX of XX, and filed in the records during the investigation, is, in fact, signed only by the Municipality. In this regard, the Municipality has stated that APM did not sign it “due to mere oversight.”

Only on XX, that is, after the initiation of the investigation by the Authority and a few days before the date on which the processing ceased (see APM's note of XX, protocol number XX), did APM sign a data protection contract with the Municipality, registered under the Entity's protocol on XX (number XX).

That said, it is noted that, as clarified by the European Data Protection Board, “since the regulation clearly establishes the obligation to enter into a written contract, if no other relevant legal act is in force, there is a violation of the [Regulation], or of ‘Article 28, paragraph 9, of the [Regulation].’” Considering that “both the controller and the processor are responsible for ensuring the existence of a contract or other legal act governing the processing,” the competent supervisory authority “may impose an administrative fine on both the controller and the processor” (“Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of controller and processor under the GDPR,” adopted by the European Data Protection Board on July 7, 2021, paragraph 103).

Therefore, where, as in this case, there exists “a controller-processor relationship […] even in the absence of a [valid] written processing agreement” - since the entity processing the data is actually processing it not for its own purposes but on behalf of the contracting entity, within the scope of an activity outsourced by it and in the execution of a service contract or other similar legal relationship in place between the parties (see the definition of “processor” in Article 4, paragraph 1, number 8, of the Regulation) - “this implies […] a violation of Article 28, paragraph 3, of the [Regulation]” (ibid., paragraph 103).

The Municipality, as the data controller, has, instead, failed to enter into a data protection contract with APM until April 19, 2022, which – as a special company and instrumental entity of the Municipality, established under Law 142/1990, also for the management of urban hygiene services - was acting in fact as the processor regarding the images collected through the video devices subject to the complaint, in violation of Articles 28, paragraphs 3 and 9, of the Regulation.

That being said, it is noted that the data protection contract, which was belatedly entered into between the parties on XX, was, in any case, not suitable to meet the requirements set forth by European data protection legislation. 

It merely replicated the provisions required by Article 28, paragraph 3, of the Regulation, without making any reference to the specific processing of personal data entrusted by the controller to the processor (i.e., the management of the images acquired through the video devices in question) and without the controller, that is, the Municipality, having given the processor, that is, APM, any specific instructions regarding the processing in the data protection contract, including with regard to the specific technical and organizational measures aimed at ensuring a level of security appropriate to the risk, which the processor...
should have been adopted in this context. The data protection contract entered into between the data controller and the processor "should [, instead,] not merely reiterate the provisions of the [Regulation], but rather provide more specific and concrete information on how the requirements will be met and the level of security provided for the processing of personal data subject to the agreement." Therefore, "the contract between the parties should be drafted taking into account the specific data processing activity," regulating, in particular: "the subject matter of the processing […] with sufficient specifics so that the main subject of the processing is clear"; "the duration of the processing"; "the nature of the processing: the type of operations performed in the context of the processing (for example: 'capture', 'recording', 'storage of images', etc.) and the purpose of the processing […]", provided that, "such description should be as complete as possible, depending on the specific processing activity, so as to allow external parties (for example, supervisory authorities) to understand the content and risks of the processing entrusted to the relevant processor"; "the type of personal data [, with the specification that] this element should be specified in as much detail as possible"; "the categories of data subjects [, with the specification that] this aspect should also be indicated in a rather specific manner" (ibidem, sec. 1.3). 

Nor can such elements be otherwise inferred from a joint reading of the data protection contract and other agreements in place between the parties, given that the data protection contract in question does not contain any reference – even by relation – to the act governing the underlying supply relationship that bound the Municipality to the Company. In the preamble of the data protection contract, it is stated, in fact, quite generically, that "the Municipality of Monterotondo, as the Data Controller, avails itself of your services based on a specific contractual relationship in place and therefore, in this capacity, your company acts as the 'Data Processor', so that the data protection contract can abstractly refer to a plurality of service supply contracts or other legal acts that are not objectively identifiable. 

Therefore, the data protection contract entered into on XX between the Municipality and APM cannot be considered compliant with the requirements set forth by the data protection regulations, having, therefore, the Municipality acted, in the period of time between XX and XX, the date on which the video devices in question were deactivated, in violation of Article 28, paragraph 3, of the Regulation. 

3.4 Security of Processing 
Article 5, paragraph 1, letter f), of the Regulation establishes that personal data must be "processed in a manner that ensures an adequate level of security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage" (principle of "integrity and confidentiality"). 

In application of this principle, Article 32 of the Regulation, concerning the security of processing, provides that "taking into account the state of the art and the costs of implementation, as well as the nature, subject matter, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risk of varying probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller and the data processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk […]" (paragraph 1) and that "in assessing the appropriate level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing that arise in particular from the destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or access, in an accidental or unlawful manner, to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed" (paragraph 2) (cf. recital 83 of the Regulation). 

It should be noted, in this regard, that the controller has a "general responsibility […] for any processing of personal data that it has carried out directly or that others have carried out on its behalf," and the controller must "implement adequate and effective measures [and] demonstrate compliance of the processing activities with the […] Regulation, including the effectiveness of the measures" adopted (recital 74 of the Regulation), even when relying on a processor to carry out certain processing activities, to which it must provide specific instructions, including regarding security (recitals 79 and 81 and Articles 28, paragraph 3, letter c) and 32 of the Regulation). The controller remains, therefore, responsible for the implementation of appropriate technical and organizational measures and must ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is carried out in accordance with the Regulation (Articles 5, paragraph 2, and 24 of the Regulation; cf. the "Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of data controller and data processor under the GDPR," cited, paragraph 2.1.4, point 41). 

That said, it is noted that, during the investigation, the Municipality stated that "the device does not allow the use of encryption but, once the SD CARD is extracted from the device, access to the images contained therein can only be obtained through authentication via account and password. No further technical and/or organizational measures have been adopted by the Entity." 

Despite the images captured by the video devices in question being stored locally on a memory support that is abstractly removable from
anyone, the Municipality has therefore not adopted data encryption techniques (see art. 32, par. 1, lett. a) of the Regulation) or other techniques suitable to prevent the risk of unauthorized access to the recorded images prior to the possible removal of the memory support by the authorized personnel and the transfer of data to a computer system with protected access via username and password. 

As stated by APM, the recorded footage in the memory "was made available to the Data Controller through access to the application with a personal username and password"; therefore, the authentication via username and password pertained to the IT application used by the Municipality for viewing the images and not to the memory card itself, which, if removed by unauthorized third parties, would have allowed access to the recorded, unencrypted footage without any prior authentication. 

Regarding the defense argument, based on which, in the event of possible attempts to remove the removable memory by unauthorized third parties, the camera system would have generated an alert, allowing an operator to "activate the SIM lock" and determine the "blocking of processing," it is noted that this would not have affected the images already recorded in the aforementioned memory, resulting only in the interruption of subsequent recording activities. 

Also, the fact that such memory was "installed in an inaccessible compartment of the camera's casing," while suitable to mitigate possible risks of unauthorized access, did not entirely exclude them. Access would, in fact, "involve several and complicated steps" but would still have been abstractly possible. 

For these reasons, it is established that, in relation to the processing of images collected through the video devices subject to complaint, the Municipality has failed to adopt technical and organizational measures suitable to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, in violation of articles 5, par. 1, lett. f), and 32 of the Regulation. 

4. Conclusions. 
In light of the evaluations mentioned above, it is noted that the statements made by the data controller during the investigation - the veracity of which may be subject to accountability under art. 168 of the Code - although deserving of consideration, do not allow for overcoming the observations notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure and are insufficient to allow the archiving of the present procedure, as none of the cases provided for by art. 11 of the Garante Regulation no. 1/2019 apply. 

Therefore, the preliminary assessments of the Office are confirmed, and it is noted that there has been a failure to comply with the data protection regulations, as the Municipality processed personal data through video devices in violation of articles 5, par. 1, lett. a) and f), 12, par. 1, 13, 28, pars. 3 and 9, and 32 of the Regulation. 

Considering that the violation of the aforementioned provisions occurred as a result of a single conduct, art. 83, par. 3, of the Regulation applies, according to which the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation. Given that, in this case, the most serious violations, relating to articles 5, 12, and 13 of the Regulation, are subject to the sanction provided for by art. 83, par. 5, of the Regulation, as also referenced by art. 166, par. 2, of the Code, the total amount of the sanction is to be quantified up to €20,000,000. 

In this context, considering that the conduct has exhausted its effects, since the Municipality has declared to have uninstalled the video devices in question, the conditions for the adoption of further corrective measures under art. 58, par. 2, of the Regulation do not apply. 

5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (arts. 58, par. 2, lett. i and 83 of the Regulation; art. 166, par. 7, of the Code). 
The Garante, pursuant to arts. 58, par. 2, lett. i) and 83 of the Regulation as well as art. 166 of the Code, has the power to "impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to article 83, in addition to [other] corrective measures [provided for] in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case" and, in this context, "the College [of the Garante] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Garante's website pursuant to article 166, par. 7, of the Code" (art. 16, par. 1, of the Garante Regulation no. 1/2019). 

In this regard, considering art. 83, par. 3, of the Regulation, in this case, the violation of the cited provisions is subject to the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by art. 83, par. 5, of the Regulation. 

The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into due account the elements provided for by art. 83, par. 2, of the Regulation.
