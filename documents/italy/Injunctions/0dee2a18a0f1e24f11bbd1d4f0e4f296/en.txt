Alla luce di quanto emerso durante l’attività ispettiva, si evidenzia che l’Istituto Tumori Giovanni Paolo II di Bari ha adottato alcune misure per garantire la protezione dei dati personali, ma sono state riscontrate delle lacune significative in relazione agli obblighi di informativa e alla valutazione di impatto. a) Rivedere e aggiornare le procedure di informativa ai pazienti, assicurando che siano chiare e complete, in particolare per quanto riguarda il trattamento dei dati per scopi di ricerca;

**3. The applicable regulations**  
The processing of personal data for scientific research purposes must be carried out in compliance with the Regulation and the Code, the Provisions relating to the processing of genetic data (if necessary), and the Provisions relating to the processing of personal data carried out for scientific research purposes (doc. web no. 9124510), as well as the Ethical Rules (doc. web no. 9069637) which constitute an essential condition for the lawfulness and fairness of the processing (Article 2-quater of the Code and Article 21, paragraph 5, of Legislative Decree August 10, 2018, no. 101).  
According to the Regulation, personal data must be processed "lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject."
**(principle of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency" (art. 5, par. 1, lett. a) of the Regulation).**  
The principle of lawfulness requires that any processing is based on a specific legal basis (art. 6 of the Regulation). In relation to particular categories of data, including health data, art. 9 of the Regulation establishes a general prohibition on processing unless one of the specific exemptions to this prohibition applies, among which is the consent of the data subject.  
In cases where the condition of lawfulness is represented by consent, it must be given through a positive act by which the data subject expresses a free, specific, informed, and unequivocal will regarding the processing of personal data concerning them (Recitals 32, 42, and 43, arts. 5, 6, par. 1, lett. a) and 7 of the Regulation and Guidelines 5/2020 on consent under Regulation (EU) 2016/679, adopted by the European Data Protection Board on May 4, 2020).  
With specific reference to particular categories of data, consent must also be expressed in writing (art. 9, par. 2 lett. a) of the Regulation and par. 4 of the aforementioned Guidelines 5/2020 on consent and art. 7, par. 2, lett. b) of the Ethical Rules).  
On this point, it is noted that the European Data Protection Board has reiterated that "the notion of research cannot be extended beyond its common meaning and that for 'scientific research' in this context, it is understood as a research project established in accordance with the relevant methodological and ethical standards, in line with good practices," thereby confirming that in this sector the purpose of processing must be identified in the specific research project that is intended to be carried out (Guidelines 5/2020 on consent cited; “A preliminary Opinion on scientific research” of the European Data Protection Supervisor, cited).  

Without undermining the obligations related to consent, recital 33 of the Regulation acknowledges that "In many cases, it is not possible to fully identify the purpose of the processing of personal data for scientific research at the time of data collection. Therefore, data subjects should be allowed to give their consent to certain areas of scientific research where there is compliance with recognized ethical standards for scientific research. Data subjects should have the option to give their consent only to certain areas of research or parts of research projects to the extent permitted by the intended purpose." It therefore admits, in residual circumstances, that data subjects may give consent in phases for the processing of personal data for scientific research purposes when it is not possible to fully identify the specific purposes of the processing at the time of collection. This is considering that, even in relation to the processing in question, it is not possible to derogate from the requirement of specificity and granularity of consent (arts. 6 and 7 of the Regulation and point 7.2 of Guidelines 5/2020 on consent cited).  
In such cases, therefore, the data controller is subsequently required to define one or more specific research projects in accordance with the ethical and methodological rules of the sector and integrate the expressions of will of the data subjects already collected, with specific consents to progressively obtain a legal basis suitable for the processing of data for scientific research purposes (arts. 5, par. 1 lett. a) 6, 7, and 9 of the Regulation; Guidelines 5/2020 on consent, cited; see also “A Preliminary Opinion on data protection and scientific research” of the European Data Protection Supervisor, dated January 6, 2020; “Document on response to the request from the European Commission for clarifications on the consistent application of the GDPR, focusing on health research” adopted by the European Data Protection Board on February 2, 2021), or, where it is in one of the conditions referred to in art. 110 of the Code and point 5.3 of the Provisions, to carry out the obligations referred to in art. 110 of the Code as amended by art. 44, par. 1-bis of the decree-law of March 2, 2024, no. 19, converted with law of April 29, 2024, no. 56 and indicated by the Supervisor in the resolution promoting the new ethical rules for the processing of data for statistical and scientific research purposes (provision of May 9, 2024 doc. web 10016146).  
For what is relevant here, it is also noted that the pursuit of scientific research purposes in the medical, biomedical, and epidemiological fields is permitted upon obtaining the consent of the data subject. "Consent is not necessary when the research is carried out based on provisions of law or regulation or Union law in accordance with article 9, paragraph 2, letter j), of the Regulation, including the case where the research falls within a biomedical or health research program provided for under article 12-bis of legislative decree December 30, 1992, no. 502, and an impact assessment is conducted and made public in accordance with articles 35 and 36 of the Regulation" (art. 110 of the Code, art. 9, par. 2, lett. j) and par. 4 of the Regulation).  
With specific reference to the processing of personal data for scientific research purposes carried out by IRCCS, it is reiterated here, as already highlighted in the note of XX, prot. no. XX, which refers to art. 110-bis, par. 4, of the Code, according to which "The processing of personal data collected for clinical activity, for research purposes, by scientific hospitalization and care institutes, public and private, does not constitute further processing by third parties, due to the instrumental nature of the healthcare assistance activity carried out by the aforementioned institutes in relation to...
research, in compliance with the provisions of Article 89 of the Regulation.” Article 110-bis, paragraph 4 of the Code, must be read in conjunction with the sector-specific regulations for IRCCS, particularly as outlined in Legislative Decree No. 288 of October 16, 2003, and subsequent amendments, which provides for the "Reorganization of the regulations governing hospitals and healthcare facilities of a scientific nature" that identifies appropriate and specific measures to protect the fundamental rights and interests of the data subject (see, in particular, Article 8, paragraph 5-bis of Legislative Decree No. 288 of 2003). 

It follows that Article 110-bis, paragraph 4 of the Code, provides IRCCS with a specific legal basis, under which, pursuant to Article 9, paragraph 2, letter j) of the Regulation, they may process the data collected for healthcare purposes also for further scientific research purposes in the medical, biomedical, and epidemiological fields without the need to obtain prior consent from the patients. 

Therefore, Article 110-bis, paragraph 4 of the Code constitutes one of those "legal provisions" referred to in Article 110 (first part of the first paragraph) of the Code, which prescribes as an additional requirement for the processing of data for research purposes in the medical, biomedical, and epidemiological fields, carried out pursuant to Article 9, paragraph 2, letter j) of the Regulation, the performance and publication of the impact assessment, pursuant to Article 35 of the Regulation (see FAQ on the legal bases and main obligations for the processing by IRCCS of personal data collected for health care purposes for further research purposes dated May 6, 2024, web doc No. 10024215).

In any case, the principle of the alternative legal bases remains firm; therefore, in implementation of the principle of lawfulness, it is up to the data controller to identify, for each individual study, among the different lawful conditions provided for in Articles 6 and 9, paragraph 2 of the Regulation, the one that is concretely most appropriate, corresponding to the objective and essence of the processing, and consequently indicate it in the impact assessment and in the information provided to the data subjects, also taking into account the different implications that each of them may have on the rights of the data subjects (Articles 15-22 of the Regulation).

The principles of transparency and fairness imply that the data subject is to be informed in advance of the existence of the processing and its purposes, providing, in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible form, using simple and clear language, the information referred to in Article 13 of the Regulation, in the case of data collected directly from them, or pursuant to Article 14 of the Regulation, in the case of data collected from third parties (recitals 58 and 60 and Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a) and 12 of the Regulation). 

It is particularly noted that the aforementioned regulations provide that, if the data has been previously collected for other purposes, as in the case at hand, the data controller must provide the data subject with information regarding that different purpose and any further relevant information before such further processing (Article 13, paragraph 4 of the Regulation). 

In any case, regarding data not collected from the data subjects, the controller may refrain from providing the information to the extent that communicating it proves impossible or involves a disproportionate effort. This is particularly relevant in the context of processing carried out for scientific research purposes, without prejudice to the conditions and guarantees provided for in Article 89, paragraph 1 of the Regulation and the adoption of appropriate measures to protect the rights, freedoms, and legitimate interests of the data subject, including making the information public (Article 14, paragraph 5, letter b) of the Regulation and Article 6, paragraph 3 of the Ethical Guidelines).

Furthermore, the principle of data retention limitation is significant, which mandates that data be “kept in a form that permits identification of the data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the data are processed” (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter e) of the Regulation). 

Among the principles applicable to processing established in Article 5 of the Regulation, the principle of accountability merits highlighting in this context, according to which “the data controller must comply with and be able to demonstrate compliance with the principles and obligations set forth in the Regulation” (Articles 5, paragraph 2, 24 of the Regulation). 

This is linked to the duty to ensure that the rights and regulations regarding the protection of personal data of the data subjects are safeguarded and applied from the design stage and by default (privacy by design and by default, Article 25 of the Regulation). 

In compliance with the obligation of data protection from the design stage, controllers must take an active approach in applying the principles, aiming to achieve a real protective effect. The requirement set forth in Article 25 of the Regulation obliges controllers to ensure that data protection is integrated into processing from the design stage and by default throughout the entire lifecycle of the processing. 

The Regulation also provides that “when a type of processing, particularly when it involves the use of new technologies, considering the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, may present a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller shall carry out, prior to proceeding with the processing...”
treatment, an assessment of the impact of the
proposed treatments on the protection of personal data. A single assessment may examine a
set of similar treatments that present analogous high risks” (art. 35; Article 29 Working Party Guidelines No. 248 concerning "The data protection impact assessment and the criteria for determining whether a processing" adopted in amended form on 4.10.2017).
In particular, the impact assessment is
necessary whenever the processing may present a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, particularly those that involve: i) the
evaluation or assignment of a score; ii) automated decision-making that has legal effects or significantly affects the data subject in a similar manner; iii) systematic monitoring; iv) processing of sensitive data or data of a highly personal nature; v) processing of data on a large scale; vi) the creation of
matches or combinations of data sets; vii) processing of data relating to vulnerable data subjects; viii) innovative use or application of new technological or organizational solutions; ix)
processing that prevents data subjects from exercising a right or availing themselves of a service or a contract. In particular, the European Data Protection Board believes that a DPIA must
certainly be conducted whenever at least two of the aforementioned criteria are simultaneously present (Part III, letter B) of the aforementioned Guidelines).
4. The defensive memoranda and assessments of the Authority
By note dated XX (prot. n.XX), the Institute submitted its defensive memoranda, not requesting to be heard in a hearing, as provided for by art. 166, paragraph 5 of the Code.
4.1. On the principle of lawfulness, fairness, and transparency
In relation to the allegations made by the Authority,

the Institute represented, with statements for which it is criminally liable under art. 168 of the Code, preliminarily that “in the context of the two studies, the subject of the allegations, the patient recruitment phase had not been initiated, as stated in the minutes [...], a determining circumstance for declaring the merely potential and theoretical nature of the possible harm from the alleged violations and, therefore, exempting, or at least strongly mitigating, the alleged violations.”
This statement, confirming what was already represented in relation to study 1), also clarifies the meaning of what was recorded following the inspections of XX, during which the Institute, regarding Study 2), stated that “[...]. The study is ongoing and the patient recruitment phase has not yet been completed. Currently, 58 potential patients have been identified, both living and deceased.”
In particular, with reference to the allegation of violation of the principles of transparency and fairness, linked to the circumstance that at the patient acceptance stage a preliminary and generic consent is collected from the data subjects for research purposes, the Institute represented that it intended to collect such consent by implementing
recital 33 of the Regulation, hypothesizing a sort of phased consent.
In this regard, notwithstanding the above highlighted in relation to the case referred to in the aforementioned recital 33 of the Regulation, taking into account the good faith of the data controller, which is widely evident from the submitted memoranda and in particular from the circumstance that, where possible, prior to the commencement of personal data processing for research purposes, the consent of the data subjects is nonetheless obtained, it is believed that the allegation of violation of the principles of transparency and fairness referred to in art. 5, par. 1,

letter a) of the Regulation is overcome.
4.2 On the impact assessment
With reference to the allegation of violation of the obligation to conduct a DPIA in relation to the processing of personal data necessary for the realization of clinical studies and in particular those examined during the inspection (study 1 and 2), the Institute defended itself by stating in particular that it is “preparing the assessment activities [of impact] certainly for those newly initiated and, in any case, also for the ongoing studies.”
While positively acknowledging the commitment made by the controller, it is noted, however, that the processing of personal data related to health and, if applicable, genetic data, concerning vulnerable subjects (such as patients) often carried out through combinations of data or on a large scale or through the use of new technologies (such as in Study 2, which specifically involves the use of artificial intelligence techniques for the analysis of clinical and imaging data), certainly falls among those for which a DPIA must be conducted in advance, pursuant to art. 35 of the Regulation (cf. Guidelines No. 248 cited, Part III, letter B). This is also relevant with specific reference to retrospective clinical studies that the Institute claims to base on art. 110-bis, paragraph 4 of the Code.
Furthermore, considering that the document following which it is decided at the Institute whether the processing is worthy of a DPIA or not does not examine the risks related to the processing, no elements have emerged from the defensive memoranda that would overcome the allegation of violation of art. 35 of the Regulation.
In this last regard, it is noted that the circumstance that the Institute has not initiated the patient recruitment phase in Studies 1) and 2), while it may serve as a mitigating factor, is not in itself sufficient in this case to dismiss the alleged violation, as the DPIA by its nature
same nature must be carried out before the start of the treatments and the documentation of the studies shows how the Institute conducted internal evaluations erroneously concluding that it did not need to carry out such compliance.

4.3 On the principle of transparency and the obligation to inform the interested parties
With reference to the challenge regarding the violation of the principle of transparency as per Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation and the obligation to make public the information on the processing of personal data relating to deceased and uncontactable subjects enrolled in Study 2), as per Article 14, paragraph 5, letter b) of the Regulation and Article 6, paragraph 3 of the Ethical Rules, in its defensive memoranda, the Institute represented that it considered itself "satisfied precisely because of the double information provided to the patient, one at the first access to the Institute, the other at the start of the specific research for the individual project, in addition to the privacy information also provided on the Entity's website, in the page dedicated to this purpose" and that "all retrospective and prospective studies are published on the corporate website, in the form of a resolution of acknowledgment."

In this regard, while positively acknowledging the commitments made by the Institute concerning the informational obligations, no elements useful to overcome the challenge of the violation of the principle of transparency, as per Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation, are found in the submitted memoranda, even in relation to the processing of personal data concerning deceased and uncontactable subjects due to the systematic omission of the obligation to publish the information pursuant to Article 14, paragraph 5, letter b) of the Regulation and Article 6, paragraph 3 of the Ethical Rules.

With reference, instead, to the information on data processing within healthcare services, supplementary documentation has been produced that proves how the previous one was incomplete, as it was missing the last page (3/3). The complete text containing all the information referred to in Articles 13 and 14 of EU Regulation 2016/679 has been transmitted to the records and is available in the dedicated Privacy section of the institutional website (https://www.sanita.puglia.it/web/irccs/privacy1).

It is therefore considered that the challenge regarding the violation of Articles 12 and 13 of the Regulation concerning the information provided at the time of patient acceptance has been overcome. 

Finally, it should be noted that the challenges related to the information on the processing of personal data concerning Studies 1) and 2) prepared for contactable subjects, placed at the end of the documentation and the information preliminary to the collection of informed consent, have not been overcome. They are indeed found to be erroneous and incomplete according to what was contested by the Office of the Guarantor and recalled in the previous point 2.3.

For these reasons, the violation by the Institute of the principle of transparency is therefore established, taking into account the preparation of incomplete and/or erroneous informational forms and the total absence of organizational measures as well as a specific policy to make public the information on the processing for research purposes of the personal data of deceased or uncontactable interested parties (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) and 13 of the Regulation).

4.4 On the limitation of data retention
With reference to the challenge regarding the violation of the principle of limitation of data retention, the Institute represented that "the limitation of the retention period will be indicated in compliance with the law and taking into account the specificity of the case, as already specified above."

While positively acknowledging "the manifest willingness of the Entity to comply with the regulations concerning health and research regarding the correct processing of personal data" and the commitment made in this regard by the Institute, the violation of the principle of limitation of retention, as per Article 5, paragraph 1 letter e) of the Regulation, is nonetheless established for the reasons outlined above (cf. paragraph 2 or 3).

4.5 On the Principle of accountability and privacy by design and by default
Regarding the challenge of the Institute's failure to effectively apply the principle of accountability and the obligations of personal data protection by design, in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 2 and 25 of the Regulation), the latter, in its defense, requested the Authority to evaluate "benevolently the corporate system of the Entity" highlighting some specific actions that are intended to be undertaken.

On this basis, the Guarantor, while acknowledging the good faith of the Institute and the commitment already made to improve the technical and organizational measures aimed at ensuring effective application of the principles regarding personal data protection, nonetheless considers the violation of the obligations of personal data protection by design, in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 2 and 25 of the Regulation, to be established and confirmed for the reasons outlined above (cf. paragraph 2.5).

5. Conclusions
In light of the evaluations mentioned above, taking into account the statements made pursuant to Article 168 of the Code during the investigation and the elements provided by the data controller in the defensive memorandum, although deserving of consideration, do not allow, as illustrated and motivated above, to overcome most of the observations notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure, nor do any of the cases provided for by Article 11 of the Regulation apply.
Garante n. 1/2019.  
For these reasons, the illegality of the processing of personal data carried out by the Giovanni Paolo II Institute for Hospitalization and Scientific Care in Bari is noted as it violates Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter e), paragraph 2, 9, paragraph 2 letter j), Article 14, paragraph 5, letter b), 25, and 35 of the Regulation, as well as Article 110, paragraph 1, first part, and Article 110-bis, paragraph 4 of the Code, and Article 6, paragraph 3 of the Ethical Rules. The violation of the aforementioned provisions also makes the administrative sanction provided for in Article 83, paragraphs 4 and 5 of the Regulation applicable, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i), and 83, paragraph 3, of the same Regulation and Article 166, paragraph 2 of the Code.  

6. Corrective Measures  
Article 58, paragraph 2 of the Regulation provides the Garante with a series of corrective powers, of a prescriptive and sanctioning nature, to be exercised in the event that an unlawful processing of personal data is established, including the power to "order the data controller or the data processor to bring the processing into compliance with the provisions of this regulation, if necessary, in a specific manner and within a specified time frame" (Article 58, paragraph 2 letter d) of the Regulation). In light of the evaluations mentioned above, it is deemed necessary to order the Institute, pursuant to the aforementioned Article 58, paragraph 2, letter d) of the Regulation, to adopt, within ninety days from the notification of this provision, the following corrective measures:  
- to carry out the impact assessment pursuant to Article 35 of the Regulation and Articles 110 and 110-bis, paragraph 4 of the Code (paragraph 4.2);  
- to publish the information regarding the processing of personal data related to deceased and non-contactable subjects, pursuant to Article 14, paragraph 5, letter b) of the Regulation and Article 6, paragraph 3 of the Ethical Rules (paragraph 4.3);  
- to integrate, modify, and rectify the information on the processing of personal data related to Studies 1) and 2) prepared for contactable subjects (paragraph 2.3 and 4.3), in particular:  
  - eliminating the indication that following the entry into force of the Regulation, it would have replaced the resolution 52 of 24/7/2008 containing the "Guidelines for the processing of personal data in the context of clinical trials of medicinal products, in order to more thoroughly describe its rights [...]";  
  - clearly indicating the legal bases for the processing (Article 13, paragraph 1, letter c) of the Regulation);  
  - indicating the retention period of personal data or the criteria used to determine it (Article 13, paragraph 2, letter a) of the Regulation);  
  - clearly representing the rights of the data subjects and the ability to withdraw consent;  
  - providing the possibility to exercise the right of access for the entire duration of the processing.  

7. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letters i and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code).  
The violation of Article 5, paragraph 1, letter e) and paragraph 2, of Article 9, paragraph 2, letter j), of Articles 13, 14, paragraph 5, letter b) and of Articles 25 and 35 of the Regulation, of Articles 110, paragraph 1, first part and 110-bis, paragraph 4 of the Code and of Article 6, paragraph 3 of the Ethical Rules committed by the Institute is subject to the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction, pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 4, letter a) and 5, letter a) and b) of the Regulation. The Garante, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation, as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to "impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to the [other] corrective measures referred to in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case" and, in this context, "the College [of the Garante] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Garante's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code" (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Garante Regulation n. 1/2019).  
The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into account the principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and deterrence, as indicated in Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, in light of the elements provided for in Article 83, paragraph 2 of the Regulation in relation to which it is noted that:  
- the processing carried out particularly concerned information capable of revealing the health status of patients enrolled in the clinical studies conducted by the Institute within its research lines (Article 4, paragraph 1, numbers 13 and 15 of the Regulation and Article 83, paragraph 2, letters a) and g) of the Regulation);  
- from the perspective concerning the subjective element, no intentional attitude on the part of the data controller emerges, as the established violations occurred in good faith (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter b) of the Regulation);  
- there are no previous relevant violations committed by the data controller nor have any previous measures pursuant to Article 58 of the Regulation been imposed (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter e) of the Regulation);  
- the Institute has shown itself to be cooperative during the inspection assessment and this proceeding;
- Despite the ongoing profiles of non-compliance with the current regulatory framework regarding the protection of personal data highlighted above, the Institute has already implemented some corrective measures concerning the processing of personal data carried out (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter c) of the Regulation).  
In light of the aforementioned elements, assessed as a whole, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the administrative fine provided for by Article 83, paragraphs 4 and 5, of the Regulation, in the amount of € 10,000.00 (ten thousand/00) for the violation of Article 5, paragraph 1, letter e), and paragraph 2, of Article 9, paragraph 2, letter j), of Articles 13, 14, paragraph 5, letter b) and of Articles 25 and 35 of the Regulation, of Articles 110, paragraph 1, first part and 110-bis, paragraph 4 of the Code and of Article 6, paragraph 3 of the Ethical Rules as an administrative monetary sanction deemed, pursuant to Article 83, paragraphs 1 and 3, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.  
It is also considered that the accessory sanction of publication on the website of the Authority of this provision should apply, as provided for by Article 166, paragraph 7 of the Code and Article 16 of the Regulation of the Authority No. 1/2019, also considering the type of personal data subject to unlawful processing.
