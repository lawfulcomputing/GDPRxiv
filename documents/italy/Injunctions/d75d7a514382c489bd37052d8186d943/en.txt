2.2.1. Identification of Authorized Personnel  
Realmaps must ensure that only authorized personnel have access to the database. This includes defining the specific roles and responsibilities of each individual who will interact with the data, as well as implementing appropriate security measures to protect personal data.

2.2.2. Training and Awareness  
All personnel authorized to access the database must undergo training regarding data protection regulations and the importance of safeguarding personal data. This training should cover the legal obligations under the Regulation, as well as the internal policies and procedures established by Realmaps.

2.2.3. Monitoring and Compliance  
Realmaps should implement monitoring mechanisms to ensure compliance with data protection policies. Regular audits and assessments should be conducted to verify that authorized personnel adhere to the established protocols and that any unauthorized access is promptly addressed.

2.2.4. Documentation of Access  
A log of all access to the database should be maintained, detailing who accessed the data, when, and for what purpose. This documentation is crucial for accountability and for demonstrating compliance with data protection obligations.

2.2.5. Revocation of Access  
In the event that an individual no longer requires access to the database, Realmaps must have a procedure in place to promptly revoke that access. This ensures that personal data is only accessible to those who need it for legitimate purposes.

In conclusion, the qualification of roles and responsibilities of those authorized to access the Realmaps database is a critical aspect of ensuring compliance with data protection regulations and safeguarding personal data.

2.3. Exercise of Rights and Investigative Outcomes Related to Complaints Received by the Authority

In relation to the handling of requests for the exercise of rights, it has been established that, as a rule, real estate agencies/clients - in response to requests from data subjects for information on the processing of their personal data in the context of the complained commercial activity - indicate an email address associated with Realmaps (XX). This email appears to be monitored by the "privacy consultant" of Realmaps (who, as mentioned, has been found to lack a formal designation or an act of appointment recognizing their responsibilities), who forwards the request first to the list provider (in most cases to the main supplier XX, at the email address XX to obtain information on the existence of consent related to a personal record and a telephone number, and, secondly, to the company XX to register the opposition by inserting the concerned number into the "excluded_numbers" table. Sometimes it is the same "privacy consultant" who contacts the data subjects/requesters by phone to provide them with a verbal response and subsequently proceeds with the insertion of the number into the "excluded_numbers" table (see pages 4 and 5 of the minutes of April 10, 2024).

With reference to the specific complaints received by the Authority, which led to the aforementioned inspection, it emerged that only in one case (file no. 295373) did the number of the data subject appear among the "excluded_numbers." In contrast, the telephone numbers of data subjects who had opposed the processing of their personal data through formal communication to Realmaps (files no. 198910 – 255444 – 319814 – 360344) were found to be included in the "numbers" table, and therefore contactable. In other cases, the telephone numbers targeted by unwanted contacts were not found within the Company's database (see files no. 302375 – 223355 – 245765).

Regarding the aforementioned complaints, however, the opposition/deletion requests submitted by the data subjects were not found in the dedicated email inbox for handling requests from data subjects (XX), nor were the alleged responses from the Company. This is because, for some of these requests (files no. 198910 – 209288 – 302375 - 319814), the data subjects used the Company's certified email address (XX), which was recently replaced by a new digital domicile (XX) registered with the Chamber of Commerce on February 12, 2024 (see page 5 of the minutes of April 10, 2024 and Attachment 9). In this regard, the scant communications (n. 3) related to the exercise of rights received at Realmaps' new certified email address were viewed. In two cases, despite the opposition expressed by the data subjects and assurances from Realmaps to one of them, the telephone numbers were still found to be present in the table of contactable users (table "numbers").

In light of the above, it emerged that, in general, the requests for the exercise of the rights of the data subjects were not found to be addressed, nor, in almost all cases analyzed by the Authority, were the requests for deletion of records accepted, as they were found, upon further checks, in the list of contactable users (table "numbers"). 

It should also be noted that the possible inclusion of the concerned user in the aforementioned "excluded_numbers" table does not appear to be an adequate solution to ensure the management of the right to object, considering that the same table appeared devoid of any reference that would allow for contextualizing and specifying the will of the data subjects in this regard. A conduct inconsistent with the obligation of the data controller to facilitate, with appropriate measures, the exercise of the rights provided for by the legislation in this area and to satisfy, without delay, the related requests, including the right to object which can be raised "at any time," has thus been observed. Therefore, a violation of Article 12 paragraphs 2 and 3, as well as Article 21, paragraph 2, of the Regulation has been deemed to be established.

2.4. Data Retention Periods

The Company currently retains a significant amount of data in its database, the collection of which has not been sufficiently specified, particularly with regard to the date of acquisition of such information. Consequently, the failure to indicate the date of data upload into the database does not allow for an understanding of the retention periods of the same; nor is it possible to reconstruct the history of alleged (but undocumented) consents in the absence of any temporal placement, potentially resulting in an indefinite retention of data. This gap cannot even be overcome by generically referring to the terms contained in contracts entered into with suppliers, as the tacit renewal of such agreements over the years (as represented by the Company during the inspection) could lead to retention periods significantly longer than those indicated in the contracts themselves.

It has therefore been deemed that a violation of the principle of data retention limitation under Article 5, paragraph 1, letter e) of the Regulation has been established.

4. The defense of Realmaps and the corresponding legal evaluations of the Authority

On June 13, 2024, Realmaps responded to the aforementioned notice by sending a defensive memorandum (the content of which, particularly regarding the corrective actions taken, was reiterated during the hearing held on July 8, 2024, and confirmed in the integration of July 15, 2024). That said, the Company confirmed that it had relied “in good faith” on compliance with the regulations regarding the protection of personal data.

4.2. With reference to point 2.2 of this provision (Qualification of the roles of the subjects authorized to access the Realmaps database), the Company clarified that only XX., a provider of ICT services, possesses the access credentials to the Realmaps database; the employees and collaborators of the latter, instead, "access only the Automatch program to formulate the estimates to be sent to the client agencies via a link, which only the agencies access, through a password."

In light of the above, first of all, it should be noted that the use of the software called "Automatch" - with which the data of property owners located in the area of interest of the Agency/Client are selected and extracted - involves, in any case, operations of access to the Realmaps database. In fact, the "Automatch" software is nothing more than an application that facilitates the search in the Company's database for subjects not necessarily equipped with appropriate technical skills; this software allows downloading the results of the search in the form of "csv files," called "download file mapping" and "download file numbers," which contain the lists of data requested by Realmaps' clients (see attachment 11 to the minutes of April 10, 2024).
It is necessary to clarify that the “csv” format of the files allows for easy export of structured data (stored in a database or spreadsheet, for example) and import into other programs, in order to visualize and reuse it. The “csv” files used by the employees of the Company therefore allow for the creation of partial or total copies of the Realmaps database. Moreover, contrary to what the Company claims, there appears to be a real possibility for its collaborators to view the data contained in the files to be sent to the real estate agencies, as evidenced during the inspection, since for such files the password to open the file itself is indicated, which therefore cannot be said to be exclusively the prerogative of the client (the purchasing real estate agency).

This setup raises critical issues regarding the actual possibility of attributing accesses to the formally authorized subject for processing. Access through shared and non-personal accounts also undermines the effectiveness, for example, of controls on log files and the application of measures provided by the Authority's provision of November 27, 2008, regarding system administrators, which is still considered valid as a guideline (at www.gpdp.it, web doc. no. 1577499 – “Measures and precautions prescribed for data controllers processing with electronic tools regarding the attribution of system administrator functions,” modified by the provision of June 25, 2009). In fact, paragraph 4.3 of the aforementioned provision contains the measure to prepare a list of system administrators indicating the “identifying details of the physical persons who are system administrators along with the list of functions assigned to them […]”. In this case, this measure has been completely ignored by the Company since access to the system is also operated by subjects with non-nominative but shared credentials and in the absence of the related assignments.

In the defense memorandum and following the hearing, only the contracts signed with three employees of the Company were produced, one of which is with a minority partner. Moreover, this collaborator, contracted on March 29, 2024, does not appear among the subjects who had access to the Realmaps database as early as 2022 (as per attachment 11 to the minutes of April 10, 2024). Conversely, regarding one of the other two employees of Realmaps, whose contracts were produced, it emerged that this collaborator had access to the Company’s database during a period prior to the formalization of the employment relationship and the related designation as a data processing officer (see cited attachment 11 to the minutes of April 10, 2024). In relation to all other collaborators - authorized to consult the database and, as described in point 2.2 of this provision, to the related operations of filtering and cross-referencing the data contained therein for the management of estimates to real estate agencies/clients - no contracts or formal agreements defining the scope of intervention on the personal data and the methods of processing them have been produced; the Company, in fact, declared that it had not proceeded with a formal designation of the subjects authorized to access the Realmaps database. This is particularly detrimental to the security of the systems given the easy accessibility of the database by a multitude of subjects, not all included in the corporate organization of Realmaps. 

The described setup has proven to be affected by vulnerabilities related to the risk, deemed high, in terms of loss of confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the involved data. In fact, as extensively represented above, it is possible to extract a considerable amount of data contained in the Realmaps database and make it accessible/viewable to anyone (to unauthorized subjects and to real estate agencies that request it, for a fee), resulting, in fact, in massive and uncontrolled processing of the same data.

4.5. Regarding the point 2.5 of this provision (Data Retention Period), the Company has argued that it was established in 2021 "and therefore cannot be held responsible for exceeding the data retention period for marketing purposes [...] which is two years. Moreover, it is possible to derive the initial date from the moment of sharing the data with the List Provider."

It should be noted, first of all, that the two-year retention period for marketing purposes invoked by the Company is a temporal parameter indicated in the provision regarding "Fidelity cards and consumer guarantees" adopted by the Authority on February 24, 2005 (available at www.gpdp.it, document web no. 1103045). This provision, with the entry into force of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, has taken on the value of a non-binding guideline since the paradigm shift introduced by European legislation has recognized the data controller's overall responsibility for the processing of personal data (accountability), including the definition of the retention periods for personal data being processed. Therefore, in exercising its accountability, the data controller "is required to implement appropriate and effective measures and to be able to demonstrate compliance of processing activities with the [...] regulation" (see Recital 74 of the Regulation, in this regard see Articles 5 and 24 of the Regulation), including compliance with the principles of purpose limitation, data minimization, and storage limitation, pursuant to Article 5, paragraph 1, letters b), c), and e) of the Regulation. Ultimately, in light of the European regulatory framework, the aforementioned provision aims to guide the choices of the data controller, including with regard to retention periods (setting the general rule of two years); however, the decision on processing and related timelines rests solely with the data controller itself.

Having clarified this, even considering the temporal parameters indicated in the aforementioned provision, it is observed that the two-year period invoked by the Company, at the time of the inspection, which took place in April 2024, had nonetheless expired. Furthermore, as already stated in point 2.4 of this provision, it is not possible to trace back to the moment of data upload into the Company's systems for the start of the retention period; this is because the database was found to lack temporal references and the tacit renewal of supply contracts with list providers does not allow for a clear definition of the scope of the retention period.

services: request for 'mandatory' consent for
promotional purposes”, point 3.1. - October 27, 2016,
no. 439, web doc no. 5687770; provision June 12, 2019,
no. 130, point 3, web doc no. 9120218; at
www.gpdp.it).
It should also be noted that the “link problem”
that would have justified the temporary
inaccessibility of the privacy policy text has
prevented data subjects from obtaining information regarding
the processing of their personal data
at least for the period of time highlighted
by the Authority. In fact, this informational gap was
ascertained by the Authority on January 26,
2024, prior to the inspection, and formalized
in a specific record of operations carried out (ref.
prot. no. 24/24 of January 29, 2024). At the time
of the inspection, which took place in April 2024, three months
after the remote assessment,
the notice continued to be unavailable on the
Company’s website, which, moreover, was
unaware of this and only addressed it
after the Authority's intervention and following the initiation
of the procedure on May 14, 2024. Additionally,
the new text of the notice contains the indication of the old
PEC address of the Company (XX) which was replaced on February 12,
2024, with a new digital domicile.
The indication of an incorrect contact - as it is
no longer used by the Company - compromises
the exercise of rights by data subjects.
In light of the above, due to the lack of
transparency regarding the processing and the inadequate legal basis

for the consent requested upon completion of the online form, the
violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 6, paragraph
1, letter a), 7 and 12, paragraph 1, of the Regulation is confirmed.
5. Conclusions
Based on the above overall exposition, while
acknowledging the initiatives taken by the
Company following the Authority's contestation,
with reference to the processing already carried out and
subject to the inspection assessment, the responsibility of Realmaps is deemed
established regarding the violation of the following provisions:
Articles 5, paragraph 1, letters a), e) and f) and paragraph 2; 6, paragraph
1, letter a); 7; 12, paragraphs 1, 2 and 3; 13; 14; 21, paragraph
2; 24; 25; 28; 29; 30; 32, paragraph 1, letters b) and d) and
paragraph 2; 35; 37
and Article 2-quaterdecies of the Code.
Therefore, having established the illegality of the above-described
conduct of the Company, it is necessary to:
- pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter f) of the
Regulation, prohibit any further processing for
commercial purposes carried out through lists, even
acquired from suppliers (list providers), for which
the Company does not have free,
specific, and informed consent for the communication of data to
third parties for promotional purposes (Articles 6 and 7 of
the Regulation);
- pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter d), of the
Regulation, order the Company, should it intend
to engage in promotional activities in the future through the
marketing of telephone numbers provided by
third parties, to adopt suitable procedures aimed at
constantly verifying, including through appropriate
random checks, that personal data are
processed in full compliance with the relevant provisions
(acquisition in advance of free, specific, unequivocal, documented, as well
as informed consent from data subjects for the sending of
commercial communications) (Articles 6, 7 and 14 of
the Regulation);
- pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter d) of the Regulation, to order Realmaps to adopt appropriate technical and organizational measures to facilitate the exercise of the rights provided for by the legislation on personal data protection and to satisfy, without unjustified delay, the related requests, including the right to object that may be raised "at any time" by the data subject (Articles 15, 17, and 21, paragraph 2, of the Regulation);
- pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter d) of the Regulation, to order that the operations on personal data carried out both by partner entities and by its employees and collaborators who access the Realmaps database be preceded by their designation, respectively as data processors and authorized persons for the various phases of processing (Articles 28 and 29 of the Regulation and Article 2 quaterdecies of the Code);
- pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter f) of the Regulation, to prohibit the processing of personal data collected through the website https://... without having obtained the necessary prior informed, free, and specific consent of the data subjects in relation to marketing activities, pursuant to Articles 6 and 7 of the Regulation and Article 130 of the Code; it is also deemed necessary to order, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter d) of the Regulation, to indicate in the privacy notice the correct email address to which requests for the exercise of rights regarding the processing of personal data should be directed, pursuant to Articles 12 and 13 of the Regulation. Finally, regarding the processing already carried out and considering the violations identified above, it is believed that the conditions exist for the application of an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation.

6. Order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction
Based on the above, various provisions of the Regulation and the Code related to processing carried out by Realmaps are found to be violated, for which it is necessary to apply Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation, according to which, "if, in relation to the same processing or related processing, a data controller violates, with intent or negligence, various provisions of the Regulation, the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation," resulting in the application of only the sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation. For the determination of the amount of the sanction, which must "in any case [be] effective, proportionate, and dissuasive" (Article 83, paragraph 1), it is necessary to take into account the elements indicated in Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation. As aggravating circumstances, it is deemed appropriate to consider:
1) the high number of subjects involved in the contested processing (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a of the Regulation): in the table titled "numbers," containing the contactable users, there were 32,510 records provided by XX, 2,052,933 from XX, 112,117 from XX, and 10,049 from XX. To the users acquired from the list providers, the large volume of data from property owners "downloaded" from public registers, such as SISTER and the Archives of the Agency of the Territory, is added;
2) the severity of the violations detected (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a of the Regulation) with particular reference to the absence of random checks of the numbers provided by the list providers, the inadequate management of the right to object of the data subjects, as well as the lack of control over processing carried out by entities not expressly authorized, with possible repercussions also on the data security front; such failures refer to "systemic" behaviors, thus rooted in corporate procedures;
3) the duration of the processing carried out for years and interrupted only following the intervention of the Guarantor (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a of the Regulation);
4) the severely negligent nature of the violation, as the Company has demonstrated negligence in the processing of personal data (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter b of the Regulation);
5) the overall assessment of the Company's economic capacity, taking into account the latest available corporate turnover (as resulting from the VAT declaration 2024 for the tax period of the year 2023): the Company, for the tax period of 2023, recorded an increase in turnover of almost double that of the previous year (2022) (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter k of the Regulation). As mitigating factors, it is deemed necessary to consider:
1) the adoption of corrective measures, some of which were initiated immediately after the conclusion of the inspection assessments, and which partially reflect the prescriptions imposed by this provision (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter c of the Regulation);
2) the absence of previous proceedings initiated against the data controller (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter e of the Regulation);
3) the cooperation with this Authority in the context of the inspection assessment and the consequent proceedings (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter f of the Regulation). Based on the overall elements indicated above, in application of the aforementioned principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and dissuasiveness, as per Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, also taking into account the necessary balancing between the rights of the data subjects and the freedom of enterprise, in order to limit the economic impact of the sanction on the
organizational, functional, and employment needs of the Company, it is deemed that the administrative sanction of a payment of one hundred thousand euros (€100,000.00), equivalent to approximately 0.5% of the maximum administrative fine of 20 million euros, should be applied to Realmaps. 

In this case, it is also considered that the accessory sanction of publication on the website of the Authority of this provision, as provided for by Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and Article 16 of the Authority's Regulation No. 1/2019, should be applied. 

In implementation of the principles set forth in Article 83 of the Regulation, the imposition of this accessory sanction appears proportionate in light of the severity and particular disvalue of the conduct subject to censure, specifically with regard to the high number of subjects involved and the massive processing (which lasted for years) carried out concerning their personal data, as noted in points 1 and 3 of the aggravating circumstances presented above. 

Moreover, the failure to adopt security measures that could ensure the integrity and confidentiality of the data, including a formal designation of the subjects authorized to access the company systems, along with the lack of a verification procedure for lists acquired from third parties, demonstrates a culpable insensitivity to the issue of personal data protection. 

It is reminded that pursuant to Article 170 of the Code, anyone who, being obliged, does not comply with this prohibition on processing is punished with imprisonment from three months to two years, and that, in case of non-compliance with the same provision, the sanction referred to in Article 83, paragraph 5, letter e) of the Regulation is also applied administratively. 

Finally, the prerequisites referred to in Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 concerning internal procedures of external relevance, aimed at carrying out the tasks and exercising the powers entrusted to the Authority, for the annotation of the violations identified here in the internal register of the Authority, as provided for by Article 57, paragraph 1, letter u) of the Regulation, are present.
