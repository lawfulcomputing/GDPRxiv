**3. Outcome of the investigative activity**  
**3.1. The regulations regarding personal data protection.**  
The processing of personal data must occur in compliance with the applicable regulations regarding personal data protection and, in particular, the provisions of the Regulation and the Code. 

For “personal data” it is understood as “any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘data subject’)”. Furthermore, “a natural person is considered identifiable who can be identified, directly or indirectly, with particular reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific elements of their physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity” (Article 4, paragraph 1, no. 1, of the Regulation). 

Additionally, it is noted that the processing of personal data carried out by public entities is, as a rule, lawful only if necessary “for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject” or “for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller” (Article 6, paragraph 1, letters c) and e), of the Regulation). It is also provided that “Member States may maintain […] more specific provisions to adapt the application of the rules of this regulation regarding processing, in accordance with paragraph 1, letters c) and e), determining more precisely specific requirements for processing and other measures to ensure lawful and fair processing […]” (Article 6, paragraph 2, of the Regulation). 

Processing must also always occur in compliance with the principles regarding personal data protection set out in Article 5 of the Regulation, including the principle of “accountability” (Article 5, paragraph 2 and Article 24 of the Regulation). 

Lastly, it is highlighted that, pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation, the controller may entrust processing to third parties that provide sufficient guarantees regarding the implementation of appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that the processing is in compliance with the regulations regarding personal data protection (“data processors”). In this case, “processing by a processor shall be governed by a contract or other legal act under Union or Member State law, binding the processor to the controller and stipulating the subject matter of the processing and the duration of the processing, the nature and purpose of the processing, the type of personal data and categories of data subjects, the obligations and rights of the data controller.” The data processor is, therefore, authorized to process the data of the data subjects “only on documented instructions from the controller” (Article 28, paragraphs 1 and 3, of the Regulation). 

**3.2. Lack of regulation of the relationship with the Company and violation of the principle of accountability**  
It is useful to preface that the management activity of
Urban waste falls within the institutional activities entrusted to local authorities, who are the data controllers, and can be carried out by third parties – data processors – on behalf of the data controller. 

From the assessment carried out based on the elements gathered and the facts emerged during the investigative activity, it was found that the processing of the data in question, carried out by the Company on behalf of the Municipality, was initiated without the role being regulated pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation (nor pursuant to Article 29 of the Code, in the text prior to the amendments made by Legislative Decree No. 101 of August 10, 2018). 

In fact, the service contract signed in 2001 and subsequently integrated in 2011, which initially conferred the functions of managing the "public urban hygiene service," in place at the time of the personal data breach and only subsequently superseded by the new contract of XX, did not meet the characteristics of the legal act aimed at regulating the relationship with the data processor, as it did not contain either the elements provided for by Article 29 of the previous Code or the elements provided for by Article 28 of the Regulation. 

In this regard, it should be clarified that, although the processing was initiated by the Municipality in the period prior to the entry into force of the Regulation (the relationship having been adopted since 2001 and subsequently integrated – initially – in 2011), for the purposes of identifying the applicable legislation, from a temporal perspective, it must be noted that, based on the principle of legality set forth in Article 1, paragraph 2, of Law No. 689/1981, "Laws that provide for administrative sanctions apply only in the cases and times considered therein." This implies the necessity to take into account the provisions in force at the time of the committed violation; in the case at hand, given the permanent nature of the contested unlawful act, such moment must be identified at the time of cessation of the unlawful conduct, which occurred in the month of XX with the regulation of the relationship with the Supplier pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation and, therefore, during the full validity of the provisions of the Regulation and the Code (as amended by Legislative Decree No. 101/2018). 

In any case, it is stated that the Municipality has claimed not to have appointed the Company as the data processor, having considered it an autonomous data controller for the personal data underlying the management of urban waste. 

In this regard, as represented by the Authority, the Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of data controller and data processor under the GDPR, version 2.0, adopted on July 7, 2021, by the European Data Protection Board, provide that "the controller determines the purposes and means of the processing, that is, the reason and the methods of the processing. The data controller is called to decide both on the purposes and on the means. However, certain more practical aspects related to the implementation of the processing ('non-essential means') may be delegated to the data processor. To be qualified as a data controller, it is not necessary for that subject to have actual access to the data processed" (see p. 3). 

Furthermore, the aforementioned Guidelines express a general concept according to which "The data controller is the subject who decides on certain key elements of the processing itself. The ownership can be defined by law or can derive from an analysis of the factual elements or the circumstances of the case" (see p. 3). 

Highlighting the circumstances that emerged in this specific case, the following observations are made: 
a) in the document concerning the notification of the personal data breach sent to the Authority on XX, the Municipality stated, in section B, that it is the data controller, and indicated, in section B2 (Additional subjects involved in the processing "Indicate the references of additional subjects involved and the role played (co-controller or data processor, representative of the controller not established in the EU)") the Company as the data processor; 
b) in the note of XX (see Annex 7 of the aforementioned note of XX), the Extraordinary Commissioner addresses the Company, asking "pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation [...] to keep the Municipality of Civita Castellana promptly informed about the outcomes of the ongoing investigation"; 
c) the Company did not define itself as an autonomous controller nor did it contest its role as "data processor," attaching the service contract of December 30, 2010. This contract, not containing the elements provided for by Article 28 of the Regulation (see specifically paragraph 3), does not meet the characteristics of the legal act aimed at regulating the relationship between controller and processor, but contains provisions from which it is clearly evident how this Municipality has, in fact, defined both the purposes and the methods of managing the "public urban hygiene service." 

It is finally emphasized that "The presence (or absence) of a written agreement, however, is not decisive for the existence of a controller-processor relationship. [...] a controller-processor relationship could exist even in the absence of a written processing agreement. However, this would imply a violation of Article 28, paragraph 3, of the GDPR. Moreover, in certain circumstances, the absence of a clear definition of the relationship between the controller and the data processor may lead to the issue of the lack of a legal basis on which any processing should..."
based, for example, on the communication of data between the data controller and the alleged data processor” (see, note 42 to point 103 of the aforementioned Guidelines). 

On the other hand, the obligation to regulate the relationship with subjects acting on behalf of and in the interest of the data controller was already provided for by the previous framework (see, Articles 4, letter g) and 29 of the Code, prior to the amendments made by Legislative Decree No. 101/2018, which provided that "the data controllers enter into written legal acts with the aforementioned processors, specifying the purpose pursued, the type of data, the duration of the processing, the obligations and rights of the data processor, and the methods of processing (paragraph 4-bis).... The processor carries out the processing in accordance with the conditions established under paragraph 4-bis and the instructions given by the controller, who, also through periodic checks, supervises the strict compliance with the provisions of paragraph 2, their own instructions, and what is established in the acts referred to in paragraph 4-bis. (paragraph 5)"; in this regard, see Cass., Section I Civil, judgment No. 21234 of July 23, 2021).

The violation of the aforementioned provisions, even if it did not result in the application of an administrative sanction (unlike what is now provided for by Articles 28 and 83, paragraph 4, of the Regulation), still led to processing that was not compliant with data protection regulations, potentially constituting an unlawful communication of personal data in favor of a subject who, not having been designated as a processor, was a third party with respect to the processing (see Decision No. 312 of July 18, 2023, web doc. 9920578; Decision No. 313 of July 18, 2023, web doc. 9920645; Decision No. 314 of July 18, 2023, web doc. 9920664; Decision No. 163 of April 27, 2023, web doc. 9900808; Decision No. 164 of April 27, 2023, web doc. 9900826; Decision No. 270 of July 21, 2022, web doc. 9811732; Decision No. 162 of April 28, 2022, web doc. 9777974; Decision No. 292 of July 22, 2021, web doc. 9698558; Decision No. 293 of July 22, 2021, web doc. 9698597; Decision No. 294 of July 22, 2021, web doc. 9698724; Decision No. 351 of September 29, 2021, web doc. 9716256; Decision No. 294 of July 22, 2021, web doc. 9698724; Decision No. 49 of February 11, 2021, web doc. 9562852; Decision No. 81 of March 7, 2019, web doc. 9121890).

The failure to define the relationship with the Company under Article 28 of the Regulation (formerly Article 29 of the Code prior to the amendments made by Legislative Decree No. 101/2018) has also resulted in a violation of the accountability principle set forth in Articles 5, paragraph 2 and 24 of the Regulation, as clarified in the aforementioned Guidelines, this principle - from the perspective of trust and legitimate expectation that data subjects place in the data controller - imposes on the latter the obligation to ensure and be able to demonstrate that “the processing is carried out in accordance with the Regulation” also in terms of defining the roles played by the various parties involved in a personal data processing activity and the consequent allocation of responsibilities.

From the perspective of adopting the necessary technical and organizational security measures to ensure compliance with the personal data processing carried out, it is noted that, although the Regulation also imposes specific obligations in this regard on the data processor (Article 32 of the Regulation), it remains clear that the data controller is the subject on whom a “general responsibility” falls (see Article 5, paragraph 2 and Article 24 of the Regulation), even when these are carried out by other subjects “on its behalf” (recital 81, Articles 4, point 8) and 28 of the Regulation).

In fact, pursuant to the aforementioned Article 28 of the Regulation, the data controller may entrust the processing of personal data to third parties that provide sufficient guarantees regarding the implementation of appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that the processing is compliant with the data protection regulations, regulating the processing by the relevant data processor through a contract or other legal act under Union law or the laws of the Member States, which binds the processor to the controller and thus legitimizes the former to process the data of the data subjects “only on documented instructions from the controller” (Article 28, paragraphs 1 and 3, of the Regulation; in this regard, see, among others, Cass., Section I Civil, judgment No. 21234 of July 23, 2021).

The failure to enter into a contract or legal act with the Company has therefore resulted in the absence of defined instructions (see paragraph 3 of the aforementioned Article 28) aimed at ensuring greater compliance with the processing of personal data. This is evidenced precisely by the illicit dissemination in question, which, although attributable - based on the investigation carried out - to the responsibility of the Company, has been endorsed precisely by the absence of precise and adequate technical and organizational security measures capable of ensuring compliance with the data protection regulations and raising greater awareness on this issue, also in terms of attention from the internal personnel of the Company to the risks arising from unauthorized or unlawful processing.

All of the above having been stated, it is established that - without prejudice to the assessments regarding the lawfulness of the processing carried out by the Company, which is the subject of a separate proceeding - the Municipality, until the month of XX, operated in violation of Article 28 of the Regulation by not having regulated the relationship with the Supplier from the perspective of data protection and in a manner not compliant with the principle of “accountability” set forth in Articles 5, paragraph 2 and 24 of the Regulation.
