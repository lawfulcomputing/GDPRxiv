**2. Applicable Regulations**

Preliminarily, it is represented that the processing of personal data must occur in compliance with the applicable regulations regarding the protection of personal data. 

According to the Regulation, personal data must be processed "lawfully, fairly, and transparently concerning the data subject" (principle of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency" (art. 5, par. 1, lett. a) of the Regulation). 

The principle of lawfulness requires that every processing is based on a specific legal basis (art. 6 of the Regulation). In relation to special categories of data, including health data, Article 9 of the Regulation establishes a general prohibition on processing unless one of the specific exemptions to this prohibition applies, among which is the consent of the data subject. 

In cases where the condition of lawfulness is represented by consent, it must be given through a positive act by which the data subject expresses a free, specific, informed, and unequivocal will regarding the processing of personal data concerning them (Recitals 32, 42, and 43, arts. 5, 6, par. 1, lett. a) and 7 of the Regulation and Guidelines 5/2020 on consent under Regulation (EU) 2016/679, adopted by the European Data Protection Board on May 4, 2020). 

With specific reference to special categories of data, such consent, considering the nature of such data, particularly sensitive from the perspective...
of rights and fundamental freedoms, must be not only explicit but also manifested in writing (Article 9, paragraph 2 letter a) of the Regulation and paragraph 4 of the aforementioned Guidelines 5/2020 on consent and Article 7, paragraph 2, letter b) of the Ethical Rules for processing for statistical or scientific research purposes published pursuant to Article 20, paragraph 4, of Legislative Decree No. 101 of August 10, 2018, of December 19, 2018, Annex A5 to the Code (web doc no. 9069637). 

In this context, the European Data Protection Committee has clarified that “the informed consent required by the clinical trial regulation should not be confused with consent as a legal basis for the processing of personal data under the General Data Protection Regulation. The provisions of Chapter V of the clinical trial regulation relating to informed consent, particularly Article 28, primarily respond to the fundamental ethical requirements of research projects involving human beings arising from the Declaration of Helsinki. The obligation to obtain informed consent from participants in a clinical trial is primarily a measure that ensures the protection of the right to human dignity and the right to personal integrity as provided for in Articles 1 and 3 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union; it is not designed to comply with data protection obligations. Under the General Data Protection Regulation, consent to processing must be given freely and must be specific, informed, and unambiguous, and, in relation to special categories of data such as health data, it must be explicit” (see points 15, 16, and 17 of Opinion 3/2019 regarding questions and answers on the interaction between the clinical trial regulation and the General Data Protection Regulation (Article 70, paragraph 1, letter b), adopted on January 23, 2019).

It is also highlighted the principle of data retention limitation according to which data must be processed and retained only for as long as necessary for the purposes of collection (Article 5, paragraph 1 letter e) of the Regulation). 

In this framework, the processing of personal data for scientific research purposes must also be carried out in compliance with the Code, the Prescriptions, and the Ethical Rules, which constitute an essential condition for the lawfulness and correctness of the processing (Article 2-quater of the Code and Article 21, paragraph 5, of Legislative Decree No. 101 of August 10, 2018). Specifically, Article 110 of the Code, which concerns medical, biomedical, and epidemiological research, states that “The consent of the data subject for the processing of health-related data for scientific research purposes in the medical, biomedical, or epidemiological field is not necessary when […] due to particular reasons, informing the data subjects proves impossible or involves a disproportionate effort, or risks making it impossible or severely jeopardizing the achievement of the research objectives. In such cases, the data controller adopts appropriate measures to protect the rights, freedoms, and legitimate interests of the data subject, the research program is subject to a reasoned favorable opinion from the competent ethical committee at the territorial level, and must be submitted for prior consultation with the Authority pursuant to Article 36 of the Regulation.” 

In this regard, “when it is not possible to obtain the consent of the data subjects, the data controllers must document, in the research project, the existence of the reasons considered entirely particular or exceptional, for which informing the data subjects proves impossible or involves a disproportionate effort, or risks making it impossible or severely jeopardizing the achievement of the research objectives” (see point 5.3 of the Prescriptions regarding the processing of personal data carried out for scientific research purposes). 

Personal data must also be processed in compliance with the principle of transparency (Article 5, paragraph 1 letter a) of the Regulation), providing the data subjects in advance with the information referred to in Article 13 of the Regulation, in the case of data collected directly from them, or pursuant to Article 14, in the case of data collected from third parties. 

It is also noted that the aforementioned regulations provide that, if the data is obtained from third parties, as in the present case, the data controller may not provide the information referred to in paragraphs 1 to 4 of Article 14 of the Regulation, to the extent that communicating such information proves impossible or involves a disproportionate effort. This, in particular, within the scope of processing carried out for scientific research purposes, without prejudice to the conditions and guarantees provided for in Article 89, paragraph 1 of the Regulation. In such cases, the data controller is still required to adopt appropriate measures to protect the rights, freedoms, and legitimate interests of the data subject, including making the information public (Article 14, paragraph 5, letter b) of the Regulation). 

On this point, the Ethical Rules for processing for statistical or scientific research purposes, Annex A5 to the Code, provide that, if the data controller collects personal data from third parties and providing information to the data subject involves a disproportionate effort compared to the protected right, it must adopt suitable forms of publicity, indicating by way of example certain specific methods (Article 6, paragraph 3). 

The regulation on the protection of personal data also concerns data subject to prior pseudonymization, which is understood as: “the processing of personal data in such a way that the data...
personal data can no longer be attributed to a specific data subject without the use of additional information, provided that such additional information is kept separately and subject to technical and organizational measures intended to ensure that such personal data cannot be attributed to an identified or identifiable natural person” (recital 26 and art. 4, point 5 of the Regulation). Pseudonymization is a measure of extreme importance in the field of scientific research, particularly in order to ensure effective application of the principle of minimization (art. 5, par. 1, lett. c) and 89 of the Regulation).

The regulation on the protection of personal data does not apply to anonymous data. In this regard, it is also useful to clarify that “(...) information that does not relate to an identified or identifiable natural person or to personal data rendered sufficiently anonymous to prevent or no longer allow the identification of the data subject” is considered anonymous, including for processing carried out for statistical or research purposes (see recital 26 of the Regulation). However, the risk of re-identification of the data subject must be carefully assessed, taking into account “all means, [...], that the data controller or a third party can reasonably use to identify that natural person directly or indirectly. To ascertain the reasonable likelihood of using means to identify the natural person, all objective factors should be considered, including the costs and time required for identification, taking into account both the technologies available at the time of processing and technological developments” (see recital 26 of the Regulation and WP29 Opinion 05/2014 on Anonymisation techniques, adopted on April 10, 2014).

In particular, anonymization cannot be considered achieved merely by removing the identifying details of the data subject or replacing them with a pseudonymous code. An anonymized data set is indeed only such if it does not allow, in any way, the direct or indirect identification of a person, taking into account all means (economic, information, technological resources, skills, time) available to those (data controller or another party) attempting to use such tools to identify a data subject. An anonymization process cannot be deemed effective if it does not prevent anyone using such data, in combination with “reasonably available” means, from being able to:
1. isolate a person in a group (single-out);
2. link an anonymized data set to data referring to a person present in a distinct data set (linkability);
3. deduce new information relating to a person from an anonymized data set (inference).

Lastly, regarding the impact assessment, the Regulation identifies the minimum content of such a document, which particularly includes the assessment of risks to the rights and freedoms of data subjects and the measures to address the identified risks, which must include guarantees, security measures, and mechanisms to ensure the protection of personal data and demonstrate compliance with the Regulation (art. 35, par. 7, lett. c) and d); Guidelines on data protection impact assessment and the criteria for determining whether a processing “may present a high risk” under Regulation 2016/679 - WP248rev.01, adopted on April 4, 2017, as amended and last adopted on October 4, 2017).

3.2. Measures pursuant to art. 89 of the Regulation
The Authority, having taken note of the clarifications recently provided by the Company, which stated that it had improperly used the term “anonymization of data” in the context of the processing carried out for the realization of the Study, as it actually concerns pseudonymized data, notes that the aforementioned Company has correctly applied art. 89.
of the Regulation, providing that the data shall be subject to minimization implemented through specific pseudonymization measures throughout the processing phase, as indicated in the documentation on file in the proceedings and briefly described in the previous paragraph 1. 

Regarding the anonymization of data, which is to take place at the end of the retention period indicated as 7 years, also in order to allow the dissemination of the results of the Study, the Company has declared that, once the correlation list between the direct identifiers of the patients and the pseudonymous codes assigned to each of them has been eliminated, the data will be aggregated “[...] according to a threshold value of 4; if this value cannot be reached, the data will be deleted.” 

With reference to the aforementioned aggregation techniques, it is necessary to consider that the availability of a high number of aggregated statistics concerning a sample composed of a very limited number of patients may increase the identifying power of each of them, up to the possible complete reconstruction of a dataset (so-called “reconstruction attack”). To avoid this, the number of statistics to be disseminated must be significantly lower than the number of variables intended for disclosure. In other words, by ensuring the dissemination of a limited number of statistics, it is avoided that through mathematical calculations, one could arrive at the identification of individual subjects belonging to the sample. 

In light of the above, it is deemed necessary that the Company, at the end of the data retention period for the conduct of the Study, indicated as 7 years, considering the number of variables subject to aggregation, ensures that the number of aggregated statistics to be made known is significantly lower than the number of considered variables; this, in order to avert the risk of reconstruction of data relating to individual individuals. 

Furthermore, within the framework of the periodic checks that the data controller is required to carry out also regarding the persistence of the effectiveness of the data anonymization measures and technological evolution, it is deemed necessary that the Company commits to removing any singularity, should it become aware of it by any means in a phase subsequent to the application of the aforementioned anonymization techniques, and to keep track of such events in order to repeat the risk assessment of re-identification upon reaching 1% of singularities identified out of the total records included in the dataset (see, on this point, the Opinion issued on June 30, 2022, available at www.gpdp.it doc. web 9791886).
