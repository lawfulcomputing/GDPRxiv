3. Outcome of the proceedings.
3.1 Established facts and observations on the regulations regarding personal data protection.
As a result of the examination of the elements acquired during the investigation and the subsequent assessments of the Authority, based on the findings of the specific technical reports prepared during the proceedings, it emerged that the Company has engaged in conduct that is not compliant with the regulations on personal data protection. 
In particular, it has been established that the Company, despite the significance of the data breach suffered, submitted an incomplete notification of the violations to the Authority; it has also been established that the Company did not maintain conduct in compliance with the data protection regulations even regarding the security measures that it should have adopted in the terms that will be indicated. 
In this regard, it is highlighted that, unless the act constitutes a more serious crime, anyone who, in a proceeding before the Guarantor, falsely declares or certifies news or circumstances or produces false acts or documents is liable under Article 168 of the Code “Falsehood in declarations to the Guarantor and interruption of the execution of the tasks or exercise of the powers of the Guarantor.” 
Article 5, paragraph 1, letter f), of the Regulation establishes that personal data must be “processed in a manner that ensures adequate security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, using appropriate technical and organizational measures.” 
In this regard, Article 32 of the Regulation, concerning the security of processing, establishes that “taking into account the state of the art and the costs of implementation, as well as the nature, scope, context and purposes of processing, as well as the risk of varying probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller and the data processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk […]” (paragraph 1) and that “in assessing the appropriate level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing, which arise in particular from the destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure or accidental or unlawful access to personal data transmitted, stored or otherwise processed” (paragraph 2). 
According to Article 25, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, the data controller “taking into account the state of the art and the costs of implementation, as well as the nature, scope, context and purposes of processing, as well as the risks of varying probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons posed by the processing, both at the time of determining the means of processing and at the time of processing itself [must] implement appropriate technical and organizational measures, such as pseudonymization, aimed at effectively applying the principles of data protection, such as minimization, and integrating into the processing the necessary safeguards to meet the requirements of this Regulation and protect the rights of data subjects” (principle of data protection by design). 
Article 25, paragraph 2, of the Regulation further provides that the data controller must “implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that, by default, only personal data necessary for each specific purpose of processing are processed” with reference to “the amount of personal data collected, the scope of processing, the retention period and accessibility,” ensuring, in particular, “that, by default, personal data is not made accessible to an indefinite number of natural persons without the intervention of the data subject” (principle of data protection by default). 
Article 33 of the Regulation provides that “in the event of a personal data breach, the data controller shall notify the competent supervisory authority pursuant to Article 55 without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, unless the personal data breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons […]” (paragraph 1) and “the notification referred to in paragraph 1 shall at least: a) describe the nature of the personal data breach including, where possible, the categories and approximate number of data subjects concerned as well as the categories and approximate number of personal data records concerned; b) communicate the name and contact details of the data protection officer or other contact point where more information can be obtained; c) describe the likely consequences of the personal data breach; d) describe the measures taken or proposed to be taken by
**on the part of the data controller to remedy the violation of personal data and also, if applicable, to mitigate its possible negative effects** (par. 3).

3.2 Confirmed Violations.  
3.2.1 Insufficiency of the information contained in the notification of the violation.  
It has been first established, taking into account the findings of the specific technical reports prepared by the Authority, that the Company, following a data breach that affected certain servers and some workstations of the Company, made a notification of the violations, pursuant to art. 33 of the Regulation, lacking the elements deemed necessary for the exercise, by the Authority, of the tasks and powers provided for by the Regulation.  
Art. 33, par. 3, of the Regulation requires that the notification of a data breach, among the elements it must necessarily contain, includes, among other things ("at least"), a description of the nature of the violation (including, where possible, categories and approximate number of data subjects, categories and approximate number of personal data records subject to breach) as well as a description of the measures taken or proposed to be taken to remedy the violation and, if applicable, to mitigate its possible negative effects.  
Paragraph 5 of the cited article also provides that, in the event of any data breach, the data controller must document it, keeping track of the "circumstances related to it, its consequences, and the measures taken to remedy it. This documentation allows the supervisory authority to verify compliance with this article."  
Recital 87 of the Regulation, referenced by the Guidelines 9/2022 on personal data breach notification under GDPR (adopted on March 28, 2023, see point 26), clarifies in this regard that the notification of a data breach "may lead to an intervention by the supervisory authority within the scope of its tasks and powers provided for by this regulation."  
From the literal wording of the cited norms as well as from their systematic reading, it clearly emerges that the notification provided for by art. 33 of the Regulation must contain suitable and exhaustive information regarding the breach event. It is essential, that is, that the notification includes all the necessary information to identify the characteristics of the cyber incident from which the data breach originated. These elements are necessary to allow the Authority, in the event of a data breach, to exercise its powers - and ascertain that appropriate technological and organizational measures have been implemented for the specific case, also with a view to restoring an adequate level of protection for the breached personal data.  
Regarding, therefore, the notification submitted by the Company, it is believed instead that it lacks such fundamental elements and, therefore, does not comply with the provisions of art. 33 of the Regulation: in particular, the notification does not indicate the impacted servers (specifically the Exchange servers), the type of vulnerability exploited by the attacker, and some elements regarding the kill chain of the attack. Such information, absent in the initial notification, is not found even in the subsequent integrations, although the activity of integrating the original notification covered a long period of time (from the first communication of 08/17/2023 until the sending of the final version on 10/04/2023), so much so that the aforementioned activity inevitably resulted in an extension of the time for the Authority to verify compliance with data protection regulations.  
The mere completion of "all fields of the standard notification form available on the official website of the Authority" (see defense writings of 01/12/2024, p. 4) with generic information (including, for example, "This attack resulted in the blocking of some servers and workstations," see supplementary notification of 10/04/2023, section F.7), cannot be considered sufficient in itself to provide adequate information regarding the breach event, given the vagueness of the content of such information.  
This is also taking into account the fact that, in indicating the measures applied to the systems at the time of the event (see section F.9 of the form), the Company made no reference to the patching activities of the indicated vulnerabilities, or to the mitigation/elimination actions of the vulnerabilities carried out, but limited itself to listing the measures generically adopted ("The servers and endpoints were and are protected through authentication systems, antivirus, antimalware, defender for identity, IPS, firewall, and SIEM. Backup and business continuity and disaster recovery systems are in place"; see supplementary notification of 10/04/2023, section F.9).  
The information regarding the type of vulnerabilities used by the attacker and the details of the security measures that were applied to the systems involved in the attack were indeed provided only in response to a specific request for information from the Authority (in particular, request for information of 10/13/2023).  
The cited Guidelines 9/2022 on personal data breach notification under GDPR clarify in this regard that, in any case, in addition to the information expressly required by the Regulation, the data controller, evaluated the specific case, must proactively provide all those additional necessary information to fully explain the circumstances of each case of data breach (see point 54 "Article 33(3) GDPR states that the controller «shall at least» provide this information).

Furthermore, in the aforementioned ruling, the Court of Justice clarified that “from the provisions of Article 5, paragraph 2, Article 24, paragraph 1, and Article 32, paragraph 1, of the GDPR, it is unambiguously clear that the burden of proving that personal data is processed in such a way as to ensure adequate security in accordance with Article 5, paragraph 1, letter f), and Article 32 of that regulation lies with the data controller in question” (point 52), and further on the matter, “on the one hand, since the level of protection under the GDPR depends on the security measures adopted by data controllers, they must be compelled, bearing the burden of demonstrating the adequacy of such measures, to do everything possible to prevent processing operations that do not comply with that regulation” (point 55). 

From the examination of what has been stated and the documentation produced by the Company, it has become evident that it did not do everything it could to prevent the data breach, considering that, as already extensively clarified, the Company did not adopt those mitigation measures that, publicly disseminated, were strongly recommended first by Microsoft and, at the national level, also by the National Cybersecurity Agency. 

In this regard, it is noted that what was presented by the Company cannot be deemed adequate to justify the lack of complete and timely updates of its systems. The Company’s assertion that “due to human error in the configuration of the scanning activities, the Exchange server subject to the attack was excluded from the scanning itself” (see defense writings of 01/12/2024, p. 7) is of no merit: given the criticality of the vulnerabilities in question (assessed, as already noted, by Microsoft as high risk for the loss of integrity, availability, and confidentiality of the related data, see https://msrc.microsoft.com/update-guide/en-US/advisory/CVE-2022-41040, and https://msrc.microsoft.com/update-guide/en-US/advisory/CVE-2022-41082) and the sensitivity of the impacted systems, the patching and updating activities should have been subjected to repeated and necessarily redundant checks by the Company and not carried out based on a manual activity that was not subject to any subsequent verification. 

Regarding its own systems, the Company, for a very long period of time (almost 12 months), was unable to guarantee the necessary protection against the loss and dissemination of the personal data processed, except partially through workarounds, which by their nature should be considered a temporary and emergency solution, among other things and in any case not applied to all Exchange servers. 

In this regard, it is noted that the failure to update all systems, although not directly the cause of the attack perpetrated by the Medusa cyber gang, certainly rendered the systems and the data processed by them vulnerable, making them inadequately protected against the impending risks. 

For these reasons, the Company was therefore unable to ensure on a permanent basis the confidentiality, integrity, and resilience of the processing systems and services and did not adopt a procedure aimed at regularly verifying the effectiveness of the technical measures applied to them. 

In relation to the overall motivations reported above, it is therefore believed that the Company has violated Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter f), 25, and 32 of the Regulation. 

As for the contestation made at the beginning of the proceedings regarding the violation of Article 28, paragraph 3, letter f), of the Regulation concerning the alleged shortcomings in the assistance provided to the data controllers involved in the data breach event, it is believed that what was represented by the Company in the defense writings of January 12, 2024, has highlighted how it has implemented adequate and timely assistance activities towards the data controllers involved in the event; this taking into account the necessary actions for the analysis of the cyber incident that the Company had to carry out. 

For these reasons, it is not believed that there are grounds in this case to adopt measures concerning the violation of Article 28, paragraph 3, letter f), of the Regulation, contained in the notification of violations dated December 15, 2023, which is therefore deemed to be archived in the part concerning this specific contested profile. 

4. Conclusions: declaration of unlawfulness of the treatment. Corrective measures pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, Regulation. 

For the reasons stated above, the Authority believes that the statements, documentation, and reconstructions provided by the data controller during the investigation do not allow for overcoming the observations notified by the Office with the initiation of the proceedings and are therefore inadequate to allow for the archiving of this proceeding, nor do any of the cases provided for by Article 11 of the Garante Regulation No. 1/2019 apply. 

The conduct carried out by the Company - specifically, the submission to the Authority of a data breach notification lacking the fundamental elements and the failure to adopt updating activities for its systems - is indeed unlawful, in the terms outlined above, in relation to Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter f), 25, 32, and 33 of the Regulation.
The violation, established in the terms outlined in the reasoning, cannot be considered "minor," taking into account the nature and severity of the violation itself and the degree of responsibility (see Recital 148 of the Regulation). The Authority also took into account the average level of severity of the violation in light of all relevant factors in the specific case, and in particular the nature, severity, and duration of the violation, considering the nature, purpose, or objective of the processing in question as well as the number of affected individuals harmed by the damage and the level of damage they suffered. The Authority also considered the criteria related to the intentional or negligent nature of the violation and the categories of personal data affected by the violation (see Art. 83, para. 2 and Recital 148 of the Regulation).

Therefore, in light of the corrective powers conferred by Art. 58, para. 2 of the Regulation, in light of the specific case:
a. the controller is ordered:
- to carry out a timely assessment of vulnerabilities in its systems and to promptly resolve them, taking into account the level of risk arising from the inadequate protection of the personal data processed;
- to establish a formalized procedure for managing vulnerabilities, which includes, in particular, the planning of controls for all IT assets of the organization in order to detect any presence of known or potential vulnerabilities as well as the identification of the related correction and mitigation procedures;

With reference to the elements listed in Art. 83, para. 2 of the Regulation for the purpose of applying the administrative monetary sanction and its quantification, considering that the sanction must “in any case [be] effective, proportionate, and dissuasive” (Art. 83, para. 1 of the Regulation), it is represented that, in this case, the following circumstances have been considered:
in relation to the nature of the violation, it involved, among other things, cases punished more severely under Art. 83, para. 5, of the Regulation (general principles of processing, in particular the principle of integrity and confidentiality);
in relation to the severity of the violation, the circumstance of the significant number of individuals whose personal data were involved in the violation (about 25,000), the loss of availability of a portion of the data subject to the violation, and the high impact that the violation may have caused to the affected individuals (in terms of loss of control over data, identity theft, fraud, reputational risks) was taken into account;
with regard to the duration of the violation, the duration of the violation was considered relevant since, from the time the vulnerabilities exploited for the attack were made known (September 2022), the Company, almost twelve months later (August 2023), had not yet updated its systems;
the degree of responsibility of the controller or processor, taking into account the technical and organizational measures implemented pursuant to Arts. 25 and 32 of the Regulation, which were specifically highlighted in the proceedings;
with reference to the intentional or negligent nature of the violation and the degree of responsibility of the controller, the conduct of the Company was considered, which failed to adopt appropriate technical measures to protect the personal data processed on its systems, despite public warnings about vulnerabilities and countermeasures to be adopted, coming from the software provider and the ACN;
in favor of the Company, account was taken of the cooperation with the supervisory Authority demonstrated during the proceedings and the decision to update the systems and implement a Cybersecurity Improvement Plan in order to update and improve the management process of security critical alerts.

**In the case at hand, taking into account the aforementioned principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and deterrence to which the Authority must adhere in determining the amount of the sanction (Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation), first and foremost the economic conditions of the offender, determined based on the revenues achieved by the Company with reference to the ordinary financial statements for the year 2023. Lastly, consideration is given to the amount of sanctions imposed in similar cases.**

**In quantifying the sanction, it has also been taken into account that, in this case, the monetary sanction is in addition to other corrective measures imposed by the provision.**

**In light of the above-mentioned elements and the evaluations made, it is deemed appropriate, in this specific case, to apply to Postel S.p.A. the administrative sanction of payment of an amount equal to €900,000 (nine hundred thousand euros).**
