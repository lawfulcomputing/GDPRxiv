3. Outcome of the investigative activity  
Taking note of what has been represented and documented during the procedure by the Hospital Company, first with the responses to the aforementioned information requests and, subsequently, in the defensive memorandum, the following observations are made.

3.1 Data subject to processing  
Personal data is understood as “any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘data subject’)” and “data concerning health” refers to “personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care, which reveals information about their health status” (art. 4, paragraph 1, nos. 1 and 15 of the Regulation). The latter data deserves greater protection since the context of their processing could create significant risks to fundamental rights and freedoms (Recital no. 51). The data processed in the case in question, pertaining to health services, are classified as health-related data.

3.2 Failure to respond to the request for the exercise of rights (art. 12 in relation to art. 15 of the Regulation).  
Regarding information, communications, and transparent modalities for the exercise of the rights of the data subject, art. 12, par. 3, of the Regulation establishes that the data controller must respond to the request of the data subject, made pursuant to arts. 15 to 22 of the Regulation, without undue delay and, in any case, no later than one month from receipt of the request. This term may be extended by two months, if necessary, taking into account the complexity and number of requests, provided that the data subject is informed of such extension and the reasons for the delay within one month of receiving the request. If the data controller does not comply with the request of the data subject, they must, in any case, inform the data subject without delay and, at the latest, within one month from receipt of the request, of the reasons for non-compliance and the possibility of lodging a complaint with a supervisory authority and of seeking judicial remedy (cons. 59 and art. 12, par. 4, of the Regulation).

In relation to the request for the exercise of rights under art. 15 of the Regulation made by the data subject on XX, the data controller did not provide an adequate response within the timeframes indicated by art. 12 of the Regulation. The response - deemed adequate by the complainant (cf. complainant's note to the Authority of XX) - was provided by the data controller on XX. This late compliance occurred following an exchange of preliminary emails between the data controller and the data subject and subsequently to an initial partial response, provided, however, beyond the deadlines set by art. 12 of the Regulation, on XX, the date on which “the complainant's husband, appointed by her, was received in the offices of the company’s strategic management to receive the necessary apologies and the digitized copy of the documents” (cf. defensive memorandum of XX). This should be taken into account when assessing the severity of the data controller's conduct, but it is also relevant concerning the duration of the violation, as the data controller, in these exchanges with the complainant (cf. emails attached to the complaint between the Hospital Company and the complainant) never highlighted reasons for complexity in fulfilling the request for access to the data, so that there were no reasons that would have made a possible two-month extension - in the sense indicated by art. 12, par. 3) of the Regulation - for responding to the request justifiable.

3.3 Unlawful communication of health-related data in violation of the principle of integrity and security of data and the security obligations related to data processing as provided by the Regulation (Articles 5, letter f), 32, and 9 of the Regulation)  
The Regulation stipulates that the data controller is required to comply with the principles regarding data protection, among which is the principle of “integrity and confidentiality,” according to which personal data must be “processed in a manner that ensures adequate security (…), including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical and organizational measures” (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter f) of the Regulation); moreover, the data controller must implement “appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk,” taking into account, among other things, “the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risk of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons” (Article 32 of the Regulation).  
In the healthcare sector, the regulations regarding the protection of personal data stipulate that information about health status can only be communicated to the interested party and can be communicated to third parties based on an appropriate legal basis or at the request of the interested party, with prior written delegation from the latter (Article 9 of the Regulation and Article 84 of the Code in conjunction with Article 22, paragraph 11, Legislative Decree No. 101 of August 10, 2018).  
What was delivered on XX by the Hospital Company to the interested party (as a response to the request made in the exercise of the right under Article 15 of the Regulation - namely a copy of the data pertaining to their medical record related to the hospitalization “(...) which began on XX and concluded with the discharge date of XX”) - containing the medical report of another patient, resulted in an unlawful communication of health-related data as there was no legal basis that would render the knowledge of such report by the interested party lawful.  
It should be noted that the incident that occurred during the claimant's hospitalization at the Hospital Company (XX) coincides with the emergency period for the Covid-19 pandemic, regarding which the Company claimed that “it severely strained the Company, as indeed all healthcare facilities, both at the assistance level and in the management and programming of the staff in service. Moreover, the A.O.U. “G. Martino” was a Covid Hospital and this, consequently, led to a general organizational revision even for the management of human resources from which some critical issues for the processes regarding the release of health documentation could have arisen.” Generally, the insufficiency of human resources compared to the tasks related to the activities of the data controller does not constitute a justification aimed at mitigating the responsibility of the data controller (in this sense, see the document “Guidelines on the application and setting of administrative fines for the purposes of Regulation 2016/679,” WP 253 adopted on October 3, 2017, regarding the application of administrative sanctions); however, in cases like those caused by the recent Covid-19 pandemic, in which there has been a shortage of personnel in Companies and healthcare facilities throughout the national territory for unpredictable and unpreventable reasons, such organizational criticality must be taken into account (in this sense, see also the EDPB document “Guidelines 04/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines under the GDPR” Version 2.1 adopted on May 24, 2023, paragraph 5.9, point 111).  
It is also considered that the Hospital Company has adopted corrective measures aimed at “improving the management process of the medical record in the Gynecology and Obstetrics department and (…) at the Medical Records Office with particular reference to the phase of ‘preparation of the copy for the entitled party,’ in addition to planning other measures to be adopted, including training interventions for the staff.

4. Conclusions  
In light of the evaluations mentioned above, taking into account the statements made by the data controller during the investigation and considering that, unless the act constitutes a more serious crime, anyone who, in a proceeding before the Guarantor, falsely declares or certifies news or circumstances or produces false acts or documents is liable under Article 168 of the Code “Falsehood in declarations to the Guarantor and interruption of the execution of the tasks or exercise of the powers of the Guarantor” - the elements provided by the data controller in the defensive memoranda, although deserving of consideration, do not allow for overcoming the objections notified by the Office with
the initiation of the procedure, as none of the cases provided for by Article 11 of the Garante Regulation No. 1/2019 apply. Therefore, in relation to the matter in question raised by the complainant, the preliminary assessments of the Office are confirmed, and the illegality of the processing of personal data carried out by the Hospital Company is noted, as it has been established, for the reasons mentioned above, that the latter, as the data controller:  
a) did not provide, within the terms set out in Article 12 of the Regulation, an adequate response to the request for the exercise of rights made, pursuant to Article 15 of the Regulation, by the data subject on XX, responding to this request on XX, that is, after 232 days from the deadline by which it should have responded to the data subject;  
b) communicated health-related data without a legal basis, providing the complainant - along with the copy of the health data requested by her - also with data related to a report concerning another patient of the Hospital without authorization from the latter.  
Such conduct is therefore found to be in violation of Article 12, in relation to Article 15 of the Regulation, as well as in violation of the principles set out in Articles 5 par. 1, lett. f) and 9 and the security obligations set out in Article 32 of the same Regulation.  
In this context, considering that the conduct of the data controller has exhausted its effects as the Hospital Company has provided - with reference to the request for access to the data in question - a response to the data subject and, with reference to the unlawful communication of health data, has proceeded to destroy the copy of the report of another patient mistakenly delivered to the complainant, as well as to adopt technical and organizational measures deemed necessary to prevent future similar omissions and occurrences like those in question and, in any case, to minimize human error, the conditions for the adoption of measures, of a prescriptive or prohibitory nature, pursuant to Article 58, par. 2, of the Regulation, do not apply.

The Garante, pursuant to Articles 58, par. 2, lett. i) and 83 of the Regulation, as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to “impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to [other] corrective measures referred to in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case” and, in this context, “the Board [of the Garante] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Garante's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code” (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Garante Regulation No. 1/2019).  
In this case, in relation to the processing operations carried out by the data controller, considering that they were related treatments, it is deemed necessary to apply paragraph 3 of Article 83 of the Regulation where it provides that “If, in relation to the same processing or related treatments, a data controller […] violates, with intent or negligence, various provisions of this regulation, the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation, the total amount of the sanction is calculated so as not to exceed the maximum penalty provided for by the same Article 83, par. 5” (see also the EDPB document “Guidelines 04/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines under the GDPR” Version 2.1 adopted on May 24, 2023).  
The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into account the principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and deterrence, as indicated in Article 83, par. 1, of the Regulation.  
Regarding the assessment of the severity of the violation (Article 83, par. 2, lett. a), b) and g) of the Regulation), concerning the failure to respond to the request made in exercising the right referred to in Article 15 of the Regulation as per lett. a) of the previous paragraph 4 “Conclusions”, it is deemed that this was an isolated case and without intent, considering that a first partial response was provided to the data subject on XX and that apologies were expressed to the data subject; the response, provided on XX, after 232 days from the deadline set by Article 12 of the Regulation, was also provided before the Authority acted against the data controller who became aware of the complaint made by the data subject after the response was provided; regarding the unlawful communication of data referred to in lett. b) of the previous paragraph, it is noted that this concerned health-related data and involved 1 data subject; based on what was stated by the data controller, no intentional behavior emerges regarding the incident as it was a human error.
In relation to the assessment of aggravating and mitigating circumstances, it is taken into account that:
- no measures have previously been adopted against the Hospital Company regarding relevant violations (art. 83, par. 2, lett. e) of the Regulation);
- the data controller has specified that the historical context at the time of the occurrence related to the violation referred to in lett. b) of the previous paragraph n. 4 "Conclusions" was characterized by organizational difficulties caused by the emergency situation due to the Covid-19 pandemic, concerning the “(...) processes for the issuance of health documentation” (art. 83, par. 2, lett. k) of the Regulation).

In light of the aforementioned elements, assessed as a whole, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the administrative fine provided for by art. 83, par. 4, lett. a) and par. 5, lett. a) of the Regulation, in the amount of €8,000.00 (eight thousand) for the violation of arts. 5, par. 1, lett. f), 9, 12 in relation to arts. 15 and 32 of the Regulation, as an administrative pecuniary sanction deemed, pursuant to art. 83, par. 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.

It is also considered that the accessory sanction of publication on the website of the Authority of this provision should be applied, as provided for by art. 166, paragraph 7 of the Code and art. 16 of the Regulation of the Authority n. 1/2019, in consideration of the nature of the data processed.
