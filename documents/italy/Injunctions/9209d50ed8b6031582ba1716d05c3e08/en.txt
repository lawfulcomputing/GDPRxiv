PREMISED  

1. Notification of data breach  
With notes dated XX and XX, the Local Health Authority TO4, hereinafter "Authority," notified a personal data breach, pursuant to Article 33 of the Regulation, stating, in particular, that "on XX at 14:04, the interested party (…) submitted via email to the URP (…) a detailed complaint in which she stated that she had received on XX, at 10:32, from the neurology clinic's secretariat an email (from the address ambulatoriosm.cirie@aslto4.piemonte.it) with the subject 'methods for renewing the therapeutic plan,' sent to another 44 email addresses in clear text and, after about half an hour, a second email correcting the first with the same modalities. She complained of a violation of her personal privacy. The URP, with an internal note via email at 14:35, communicated the complaint to the director of the Neurology SC and to the medical director of the Ciriè-Lanzo hospital facility. On 15/7, the Neurology SC, with a note signed by the director, communicated to the facility management the outcome of the first internal investigation, which revealed that the event occurred due to human error by the secretary, who, mistakenly disregarding the instructions on data processing for the purpose of sending communications, transmitted a circular of instructions on the therapeutic plan (lacking specific indications) to 45 addresses of patients affected by multiple sclerosis, inserting the respective addresses in the CC field in clear text. Subsequently, on XX, at 16:24, the medical director of the Ciriè Lanzo facility sent the company privacy office the documentation related to the episode, attaching the complaint and the two communications sent by the clinic. The privacy office, as soon as the investigation was completed, proceeded with the preliminary communication to the Guarantor Authority."  
In the aforementioned notes, the Authority indicated, as technical and organizational measures present at the time of the breach, adopted to ensure the security of the personal data involved, the "operational instructions on the use of electronic mail" and the "specific instructions on the processing of health data, present in the letter of authorization for data processing."  
According to what was communicated by the Authority, the data subject to breach involved "45 email addresses and, partially, about 45 personal references related to the holders of such addresses; the nature of the communication allows inferring the status of a patient affected by multiple sclerosis and/or demyelinating diseases."  
The Authority also stated that, following the breach, a "verbal warning was issued to the operator who incorrectly sent the email" and that the technical and organizational measures adopted (or proposed for adoption) included "1 - Specific training for URP staff as the point of reception for communications/complaints from users; 2 - Specific training for the staff of the Neurology SC as the unit affected by the DB; 3 - Renewal of operational instructions for URP staff for internal communications (assignment by competence); 4 - Renewal of specific instructions on the use of electronic mail for internal staff. 5 -
**Update of the manual on the use of IT tools.**

2. Assessments of the Department on the processing carried out and notification of the violation referred to in Article 166, paragraph 5 of the Code

In relation to the facts described in the notification of violation, the Office, with note of XX (prot. n. XX), notified the Company, pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 5, of the Code, of the initiation of proceedings for the adoption of measures referred to in Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation, inviting it to submit defensive writings or documents to the Authority or to request to be heard by the Authority (Article 166, paragraphs 6 and 7, of the Code; as well as Article 18, paragraph 1, of Law No. 689 of 24/11/1981). This was because it was deemed that the Company had communicated personal data and health data of 44 patients to the same number of patients, in the absence of a suitable legal basis and, therefore, in violation of the basic principles of processing referred to in Articles 5, 6, and 9 of the Regulation and Article 2-ter of the Code.

With note of XX, the Company submitted its defensive memoranda, in which it highlighted, in particular, that:
- “the processing involved in the violation concerns the transmission of correspondence in support of the Company’s diagnostic and care activities; this processing is included in the processing of health data (i.e. data capable of revealing the health status of the data subject) (…)”;
- “this processing occurs on the legal basis of the combined provisions of the following norms: Article 9, paragraph 2 letters “g”, “h” and “i” of EU Regulation No. 2016/679 (“GDPR”); Article 2-sexies, paragraph 2, letters t, u, v, cc, Legislative Decree 196/2003 and subsequent amendments (reasons of significant public interest related to the specific case) (…)”;
- “the purpose of the data processing in question is therefore that of diagnosis, assistance, and healthcare therapy for the interested patient and, related to it, administrative activities related to specialized outpatient care (…)”;
- “for the performance of the administrative activity in question, consisting of handling correspondence via email, aimed at correctly informing the neurological patient about the progress of care activities, the Company has provided training and information to the operators, and this despite the fact that the correct use of email should be considered, in the current historical context, common knowledge among people with average education, similar to all other tools for conducting correspondence”;
- “the events reported in the notification of violation that initiated the proceedings stem from the failure to apply, by the designated personnel, due to simple oversight, the precautions normally used for written communication. Such sending is, as a rule, individual or - in the case of collective sends - characterized by the use of the “blind carbon copy” (BCC) mode in order to avoid communications to incorrect recipients. The reason for the choice made in favor of email as a means of communication is dictated on the one hand by the widespread use of this tool, and on the other hand by the fact that this means, being essentially qualified as “postal”, is supported by the guarantees provided by current legislation”;

**3. Outcome of the Investigative Activity**  
Taking note of what has been represented by the Company in the documentation on file, in the defense memoranda, it is observed that:  
1. email addresses fall under the notion of personal data, even if devoid of references to the name and surname or otherwise to other directly identifying information of the data subjects; the circumstance that, from the context of the communication, it could be inferred that the recipients of the emails, sent by the neurology clinic's secretariat and concerning the "renewal of the therapeutic plan", were patients being treated at the aforementioned clinic, has resulted in the described processing involving health data, as it concerns information related to healthcare services, which reveal information about health status (Article 4, paragraph 1, no. 15 of the Regulation; see, among others, the provision of June 25, 2002, web doc no. 29864, provision of January 9, 2020, web doc 9261234, provision of June 24, 2003, web doc no. 1132562);  
such data merit greater protection since the context of their processing could create significant risks for fundamental rights and freedoms (Cons. no. 51);  
2. the Code, in the version reformulated by Legislative Decree no. 101/2018, defines the communication of personal data, understood as "the disclosure of personal data to one or more specific subjects other than the data subject, the representative of the data controller in the territory of the European Union, the processor or their representative in the territory of the European Union, and the persons authorized, pursuant to Article 2-quaterdecies, to process personal data under the direct authority of the controller or the processor, in any form, including by making them available, consulting, or through interconnection" (Article 2-ter, paragraph 4, letter a);  
3. the regulations regarding the protection of personal data provide - in the health sector - that information about health status can only be communicated to the data subject and can be communicated to third parties only on the basis of an appropriate legal basis (Article 9 of the Regulation and Article 84 of the Code in conjunction with Article 22, paragraph 11, Legislative Decree of August 10, 2018, no. 101);  
4. the data controller is required to comply with the principles regarding data protection, among which is the principle of "integrity and confidentiality", according to which personal data must be "processed in a manner that ensures adequate security (...), including protection, through appropriate technical and organizational measures, against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage" (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter f) of the Regulation).  
In light of the evaluations mentioned above, taking into account the statements made by the data controller during the investigation - and considering that, unless the fact constitutes a more serious offense...
**Criminal Offense**  
Anyone who, in a proceeding before the Authority, falsely declares or certifies news or circumstances or produces false acts or documents is liable under Article 168 of the Code "Falsehood in declarations to the Authority and interruption of the execution of the tasks or exercise of the powers of the Authority." It is represented that the elements provided by the Company in the notification of violation, in the defensive memoranda, and during the hearing do not allow for overcoming the objections notified by the Office with the aforementioned initiation act of the proceeding, nor do any of the cases provided for by Article 11 of the Authority's Regulation No. 1/2019 apply.

As described above, the sending of the two communications, transmitted approximately half an hour apart from each other, originating from the neurology office's secretariat and concerning the "methods for renewing the therapeutic plan," occurred through a single email message addressed to multiple recipients, whose addresses were clearly included in the carbon copy (c.c.) field; this circumstance has, in fact, without justified reason and in the absence of a legal basis, mutually revealed to the recipients of the same communications the health status of other patients, giving rise to a communication of health data concerning the interested parties (to whom the email addresses pertain), in violation of the basic principles set forth in Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter f), and 9 of the Regulation.

On this point, the arguments put forth by the data controller are not sufficient to exclude their liability in relation to the contested matter, since, regarding the error in which the authorized person who carried out the processing operations in question would have incurred twice, there is no condition that allows us to assert that the error was unavoidable and blameless, such that it could have been avoided with ordinary diligence. 

In light of the established case law of the Supreme Court (Cass. No. 7885/2011, Cass. No. 16320/2010, Cass. No. 19759/2015, Cass. No. 33441/2019, and Cass. No. 17822/2021), for the purposes of applying the invoked Article 3 of Law No. 689/1981, it is necessary that good faith or error is based on a positive element, foreign to the agent and capable of determining in them the conviction of the lawfulness of their behavior (excusable error). This positive element must be such that it cannot be avoided by the agent with the use of ordinary diligence. In this case, the operator could have diligently verified, through more accurate control, the correctness of the operations carried out during the sending of the emails, thereby avoiding the communication of health data to unauthorized third parties.

For these reasons, the unlawfulness of the processing of personal data carried out by the Company is noted, as stated in the reasoning. In this context, considering, in any case, that the conduct has exhausted its effects and that the Company has expressed the intention to implement technical and organizational measures deemed necessary to prevent future similar occurrences and, in any case, to minimize human error, the prerequisites for the adoption of measures, of a prescriptive or prohibitory nature, under Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not exist.

**4. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code).**  
The violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter f) and 9 of the Regulation is subject to the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 5 of the Regulation. 

It should be noted that the Authority, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation, as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to "impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to [other] corrective measures [provided for in this paragraph], or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case," and, in this context, "the Board [of the Authority] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Authority's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code" (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Authority's Regulation No. 1/2019).

The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into account the principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and deterrence, as indicated in Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, based on the elements provided for in Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation. 

In light of the above and, in particular, of the category of personal data involved in the violation, the number of affected subjects (44), and the unintentional nature of the violation, it is believed that the level of severity of the violation committed by the Company is medium (cf. European Data Protection Board, “Guidelines 04/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines under the GDPR” of May 23, 2023, point 60). 

It is also noted that the Company has already been the recipient of a measure adopted pursuant to Article 58 of the Regulation, which, however, concerned the activation of video surveillance systems in violation of Article 4 of Law No. 300 of May 20, 1970 (cf. provision of March 5, 2020, No. 53, web document No. 9433080). Therefore, the aforementioned violation cannot be classified as a "previous relevant violation" pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 2, letter e) of the Regulation.
