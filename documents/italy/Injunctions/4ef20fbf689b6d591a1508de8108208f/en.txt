3. Outcome of the investigative activity.

The regulation on the protection of personal data provides that public entities, within the work context, may process the personal data of the interested parties, including those related to special categories, if the processing is necessary, in general, for the management of the employment relationship and to fulfill specific obligations or tasks provided by law or by Union or Member State law (Articles 6, paragraph 1, letter c), 9, paragraph 2, letter b) and 4, and 88 of the Regulation). The processing is also lawful when it is “necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller” (Article 6, paragraphs 1, letter e), 2 and 3, and Article 9, paragraph 2, letter g), of the Regulation; Articles 2-ter and 2-sexies of the Code, in the text prior to the amendments made by Legislative Decree No. 139 of October 8, 2021, in force at the time of the facts subject to the complaint). 

European legislation provides that “Member States may maintain or introduce more specific provisions to adapt the application of the rules of [...] the regulation with regard to processing, in accordance with paragraph 1, letters c) and e), determining more precisely specific requirements for processing and other measures to ensure lawful and fair processing [...]” (Article 6, paragraph 2, of the Regulation). In this regard, it is highlighted that the operation of disseminating personal data (such as publication on the Internet) by public entities is permitted only when provided for by a legal norm or, in cases provided for by law, by regulation (cf. Article 2-ter, paragraphs 1 and 3, of the Code, in the text prior to the amendments made by Legislative Decree No. 139 of October 8, 2021, in force at the time of the facts of the complaint). 

The data controller is required, in any case, to comply with the principles regarding data protection, among which are “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency” as well as “data minimization,” according to which personal data must be “processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject” and “adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed” (Article 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and c), of the Regulation). 

That said, in light of what emerged from the preliminary investigation and the statements made by the Chamber of Commerce, it is established that, between XX and XX, although due to a mere human error, the Chamber of Commerce published on its official website a copy of the Executive Board resolution No. XX of XX, titled “XX,” with which the Chamber Board acknowledged the outcome of a civil judgment brought by the complainant against the Chamber of Commerce in relation to matters related to the employment relationship. 

The publication of this document consequently resulted in the online dissemination of the aforementioned information related to the complainant, who was identified by reference to the name and surname, clearly stated in the document. Furthermore, since judgments are subject to publication for legal informatics purposes (cf. Article 51 of the Code), the clear indication of the details of the judgment potentially allowed third parties to become aware of the entire content of the same, enabling them to link the matters subject to dispute to the identity of the complainant. 

It is also established that the Chamber of Commerce, during the period between XX and XX, published on its official website the same resolution, omitting the complainant's personal details. It should be noted that, although during this period the Chamber of Commerce had arranged for the obscuring of the complainant's personal details, the latter was still identifiable due to the specific context and details provided in the document.
**of clear references to the judicial authority**  
that issued the ruling (“Court of XX – Labor Judge”), the general register number of the proceeding (“XX”) as well as the number and date of publication of the same (“ruling no. XX, published on XX”). It is not, in fact, proven in the records that the judicial authority had ordered the annotation on the aforementioned ruling aimed at preventing, in the event of reproduction of the same in any form, the indication of the personal details and other identifying data of the interested party (see art. 52, paragraph 1, of the Code).  
The publication of the Executive Committee's resolution in a manner unsuitable to ensure that the complainant could not be identified - either with a clear reference to the name and surname and either without such reference but with the indication of the details from the ruling, which nonetheless allowed for indirect identification of the interested party - has, therefore, resulted in the dissemination of personal data relating to the same.  
In relation to this dissemination, the Chamber of Commerce has not indicated any legal provision or, in cases provided for by law, regulation, that could justify the processing in question.  
On the other hand, the same art. 62 of the Royal Decree of September 20, 1934, no. 2011, cited by the Chamber of Commerce as the provision under which the publication of the resolution in question would occur, provides that “the resolutions of the Council [but not also, as in this case, of the Executive Committee], within fifteen days following the meeting in which they were taken, must be published on the council board and kept displayed there for one week” (paragraph 1), specifies that “however, for resolutions of a reserved nature, which do not concern the budget, the Council may decide on a case-by-case basis that publication be omitted or delayed” (paragraph 2).  

It should also be noted that in numerous cases the Guarantor has over time declared the illegality of the dissemination of personal data of workers, with particular regard to the publication of information relating to disputes initiated between the interested parties and the employer administration (see, among many, provisions of April 13, 2023, no. 124, web doc no. 9890273; May 26, 2022, no. 196, web doc no. 9789541; April 28, 2022, no. 149, web doc no. 9777127; January 27, 2021, no. 34, web doc no. 9549165).  
Moreover, even in the presence of a specific publicity regime - a circumstance that, as mentioned, does not occur in this case, given that art. 62 of the Royal Decree of September 20, 1934, no. 2011 refers to the resolutions of the Council but not also those of the Executive Committee - it cannot entail any automaticity regarding the online dissemination of personal data, nor a derogation from the principles regarding data protection. This is further confirmed by the data protection system contained in the Regulation, under which it is provided that the data controller must implement “appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that, by default, only personal data necessary for each specific purpose of the processing are processed” and must be “able to demonstrate” – in light of the principle of “accountability” – that it has done so (arts. 5, para. 2, and 24, 25, para. 2, of the Regulation; see also provision of April 13, 2023, no. 124, cited).  
In light of the above considerations, it must be concluded that the Chamber of Commerce has disseminated the personal data of the complainant in a manner not compliant with the principle of “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency” and in the absence of a legal basis, in violation of arts. 5, para. 1, letter a), and paras. 2 and 3, letter b), and 6 of the Regulation, as well as 2-ter, paragraphs 1 and 3, of the Code (in the text prior to the amendments made by the decree-law of October 8, 2021, no. 139, in force at the time of the complaint facts).  

From the documentation in the records, however, it does not appear that the dissemination concerned special categories of personal data or “judicial data,” as suggested in the complaint, it being necessary to recall that the guarantees provided for in arts. 10 of the Regulation and 2-octies of the Code apply exclusively to personal data relating to criminal convictions and offenses or related security measures, and not to information relating to civil disputes.  
**4. Conclusions.**  
In light of the evaluations mentioned above, it is noted that the statements made by the data controller during the investigation - the truthfulness of which may be subject to accountability under art. 168 of the Code - while deserving of consideration, do not allow for overcoming the objections notified by the Office with the initiation of the proceeding and are insufficient to allow for the archiving of the present proceeding, given that none of the cases provided for in art. 11 of the Guarantor Regulation no. 1/2019 apply.  
Therefore, the preliminary assessments of the Office are confirmed, and the illegality of the processing of personal data carried out by the Chamber of Commerce is noted, for having disseminated the personal data of the complainant in a manner not compliant with the principle of “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency” and in the absence of a legal basis, in violation of arts. 5, para. 1, letter a), and 6 of the Regulation, as well as 2-ter of the Code (in the text prior to the amendments made by the decree-law of October 8, 2021, no. 139).  
Considering that the violation of the aforementioned provisions occurred as a result of a single conduct, art. 83, para. 3, of the Regulation applies, according to which the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation. Given that, in this case, all violations are subject to the sanction provided.
from Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation, as also referenced by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code, the total amount of the sanction is to be quantified up to €20,000,000. 

In this context, considering, in any case, that the conduct has exhausted its effects, given that the publication of the resolution in question ceased on XX, the prerequisites for the adoption of further corrective measures under Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not exist. 

5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code). 

The Authority, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to “impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to [other] corrective measures [provided for] in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case” and, in this context, “the College [of the Authority] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Authority's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code” (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Regulation of the Authority No. 1/2019). 

In this regard, taking into account Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation, in the specific case, the violation of the cited provisions is subject to the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation. 

The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into due account the elements provided for by Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation. 

That said, it is noted that the violation concerned personal data relating to a single data subject and the dissemination of the same occurred over a limited time frame, namely from XX to XX (see Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a), of the Regulation). Furthermore, the violation is of a negligent nature, having occurred, at least with regard to the clear mention of the name and surname of the complainant, due to mere human error (see Article 83, paragraph 2, letter b), of the Regulation), and did not concern particular categories of personal data or personal data relating to criminal convictions and offenses (see Article 83, paragraph 2, letter g), of the Regulation). 

In light of these considerations, it is believed that, in the specific case, the level of severity of the violation committed by the data controller is low (see European Data Protection Board, “Guidelines 04/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines under the GDPR” of May 23, 2023, point 60). 

For the purposes of quantifying the sanction, it is then believed that the following mitigating circumstances should be taken into account: the Chamber of Commerce has offered good cooperation with the Authority during the investigation, having also represented (see note of 16 XX, cited) that, in light of the facts that occurred, it has initiated the review of its “Regulation on the management of the Online Register” (see Article 83, paragraph 2, letter f), of the Regulation); there are no relevant previous violations committed by the Chamber of Commerce (see Article 83, paragraph 2, letter e), of the Regulation). 

By reason of the aforementioned elements, evaluated in their entirety, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the pecuniary sanction at €2,000 (two thousand) for the violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), and 6 of the Regulation, as well as 2-ter of the Code (in the text prior to the amendments made by Decree-Law No. 139 of October 8, 2021), as an administrative pecuniary sanction deemed, pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. 

Considering that the dissemination of the complainant's personal details and the details of the judgment issued by the judicial authority could indirectly allow third parties to become aware of the content of that judgment, further compromising the privacy sphere of the data subject, it is also believed that the accessory sanction of publication on the Authority's website of this provision should be applied, as provided for by Article 166, paragraph 7 of the Code and Article 16 of the Regulation of the Authority No. 1/2019. 

Finally, it is noted that the prerequisites of Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 are met.
