PREMISED  

1. THE INSTRUCTIONAL ACTIVITY CARRIED OUT  
On July 18, 2023, the Authority adopted  
provision n. 323, with which an administrative sanction of  
5,000 euros was imposed on Maximum International Corp. S.r.l. (hereinafter “Maximum” or “Company”). The procedure was initiated following a complaint regarding the persistent receipt of promotional phone calls on behalf of the Company even after the complainant had expressly opposed during the unwanted contacts and through a formal request to exercise rights to which no response was received.  
With the aforementioned provision, the Authority noted  
various critical issues in 2023 regarding the  
fulfillments related to the information and consents  
required through the online form available on  
the relevant website (Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a, 6  
paragraph 1, letter a, and 7 of the Regulation), as well as the inadequate  
management of the opposition expressed  
by the complainant, which was not acknowledged  
during the promotional phone calls, along with the  
request to exercise the rights of access and deletion  
that was not responded to by the Company (in violation of  
Articles 12, paragraphs 2 and 3, 15, 17, and 21, paragraph 2, of  
the Regulation); furthermore, the absence of  
controls within the supply chain of partners promoting  
Maximum products was sanctioned (Articles 5, paragraph 2,  
24, and 25 of the Regulation).  
Following the adoption of the aforementioned provision  
n. 323/2023 and until early 2024, further reports continued to  
arrive, including various communications from the  
complainant who initiated the previous  
procedure. These reports concerned the  
receipt of unwanted phone calls made  
in the interest of Maximum to even  
private numbers or those registered in the Public  
Opposition Register, and in any case in the absence of  
specific consent from the interested parties for the processing of data  
related to their phone numbers for promotional purposes.  
By note dated March 12, 2024 (ref. prot. n. 0030725/24),  
the Company was asked to provide observations,  
pursuant to Article 157 of the Code, regarding the  
aforementioned reports as well as “to communicate what  
initiatives have been undertaken [...] in order to  
implement what is prescribed in the […]  
provision […] n. 323 of July 18, 2023 and whether  
the Authority has been informed of such initiatives within  
the terms indicated in the same provision.” No response  
was provided to this request.  
Therefore, with act n. 57072/24 of May 10, 2024  
(notified the following May 13 via certified  
email), the Office initiated, pursuant to Article 166,  
paragraph 5, of the Code, a procedure for the adoption of  
the measures referred to in Article 58, paragraph 2, of the  
Regulation against Maximum due to the lack of response to  
the aforementioned request for information, thus in violation of  
Article 157 of the Code.  
On June 12, 2024, the Company, pursuant to Article  
166, paragraph 6, of the Code and Article 13 of  
the internal regulation of the Guarantor n. 1/2019, requested  
to be heard in the procedure initiated against it.  
During the hearing, which took place on July 10, 2024,  
Maximum stated that it has not been involved in the sale of products to individuals since  
2003 – 2005 but exclusively engages in  
the production of systems to be marketed to distributors; in particular, the  
Company reiterated what was already expressed in the  
previous procedure, declaring itself unrelated  
to the complained conduct, which is solely attributable  
to the concessionaires who would have acted for  
their own promotional purposes as data controllers.  
The Company clarified that it did not oppose in the past  
to the sanctioning provision of the Authority “despite others  
being responsible for the violation.” Furthermore, it claimed  
to have convened, in September 2023 after the adoption  
of the sanctioning provision, all the companies  
concessionary of Maximum products, reminding them of  
the strict observance of Article 9 of the contract  
signed with them, since 2020/2021; this  
provision states that, during promotional contacts,  
the concessionaires must identify themselves  
by their own name and possibly “as a concessionary company of Maximum.” In this regard, it  
produced, during the hearing, the aforementioned contracts.
jointly with a note, dated September 11, 2023, with which the Company informed the concessionary companies that it had made a payment of an administrative monetary fine to the Guarantor for violations regarding the protection of personal data for which it did not consider itself responsible, stating that “any further recall or sanction will be attributed to the reference agency.” 

In confirmation of the autonomy recognized to the agencies in the direct sales activity of Maximum products to individuals, the Company provided a copy of the contract that these concessionaires entered into with XX, a company responsible for ensuring, for each of them, “privacy compliance,” that is, the conformity of processing to current regulations, including the identification of the Data Protection Officer (so-called DPO).

Regarding the reports subject to the request for information dated March 12, 2024, the Company attributed the responsibility for the complained calls to agency XX “which has provided a printout of its database used for contacts,” which all seem to be supported by prior marketing consent given by the recipients of the promotional communications. Furthermore, it stated that, as far as it is aware, this agency regularly checks, on a biweekly basis, the Public Opposition Register of the numbers to be contacted before each promotional campaign; this, at least, since the start date of the collaboration with the privacy consulting company, XX.

With reference to the failure to respond to the request for information dated March 12, 2024, made pursuant to Article 157 of the Code, the Company stated that this negligence is attributable to an error in the registration procedure by the secretary: in particular, the note from the Office, although regularly received, was not registered by the aforementioned employee, preventing the competent departments from processing it. “Following this incident – Maximum continued – which is nonetheless within the organizational responsibility of the Company, the employee responsible for registration has been replaced and all employees have been reminded of the importance of […] processing communications from Administrative Authorities sent via certified email.”

Regarding the initiatives undertaken following the Guarantor's provision, on July 15, 2024, the Company communicated the recent changes made to its website, accessible at https://www.maximumsrl.com/, in order to align the processing with the regulations on the protection of personal data as well as the prescriptions issued by the Authority with the aforementioned provision. It also emphasized that it promptly activated measures to resolve the issues identified in the provision, holding the concessionary agencies accountable during the described meeting in September 2023. However, the Company clarified that it did not inform the Authority of the initiatives undertaken as the provision adopted against it, sent to the certified email of Maximum, was not notified to the addresses actually indicated in the procedure. 

For the reasons outlined above, the Company requested the archiving of the proceedings initiated against it “and, in any case, the non-adoption of any sanctioning measure.”

2. LEGAL ASSESSMENTS

Based on the statements of Maximum, for which the declarant is responsible pursuant to Article 168 of the Code, and considering the documentation produced during the hearing, the following legal assessments are made.

Unlike what emerged in the investigation related to the previous provision, Maximum provided, during the current proceedings, evidence supported by external confirmations suitable to prove its lack of involvement in the conduct complained of in the reports received by the Authority. This conduct, in light of the aforementioned confirmations, appears to be solely attributable to the concessionary agencies that would have acted as independent data controllers. In particular, the third-party company that would have made the unwanted contacts for marketing purposes was identified as an independent data controller, deviating from the contractual provision to identify itself with its corporate name. Against this company, for a complete assessment of the aspects related to the lawfulness of the processing in question, the Authority has initiated a separate investigation. 

It should also be added that Maximum, during the hearing, represented that over time its activity has been limited to the production of systems and not to the marketing of the same. Based on this entrepreneurial choice, therefore, the relationship that currently exists between Maximum and the concessionary companies of its products is comparable to that between producers and retailers of a particular good. This is also evidenced by the fact that the concessionary companies have established an independent organization not only concerning the contact of potential customers but also regarding the overall structuring of personal data processing. Proof of this is that these concessionaires have independently commissioned a third-party company to verify the full compliance of the processing in question. It is worth adding, on this point, that Maximum, due to its choice also related to the relationships with the aforementioned concessionaries, did not assert the above-described circumstance during the
preceding provision, relying on the
awareness of such companies following the
observations highlighted by the Company and taking
on the burden of defining the procedure
through the payment of the imposed sanction. This
choice, dictated solely by the reasons of
opportunity highlighted by Maximum, does not appear
to be in contrast with the statements made by the
same in the current proceeding which, in light of
the previously mentioned findings, allow for the
outlining of the new framework of responsibilities
in relation to the facts in question.
While acknowledging the new distribution of
responsibilities in processing, it must be noted
that this circumstance does not absolve Maximum of
responsibility for failing to respond to the
Authority's request for information.
In this regard, it should be noted that the request
for information was addressed exclusively to the
Company and it was clearly stated therein that, in
the event of non-compliance, “the administrative
monetary sanction provided for by art. 166, paragraph
2, of the aforementioned Legislative Decree No.
196/2003” would be applicable. Therefore, Maximum
was the sole recipient of the request for
information and should have, in any case, provided
a timely and complete response to the Authority,
in light of the overall duties of collaboration
identified by art. 31 of the Regulation and by art.
157 of the Code.
Only during the hearing did Maximum present the
reasons for the lack of response attributed to
alleged negligence of one of its employees and
stated, on this point, that it had introduced
corrective measures to comply promptly with the
requests of the interested parties and the
Authority. Such statements, although deserving of
consideration in the quantification of the
sanction, are not sufficient to exclude the
Company's responsibility, given that it is
accountable, for the contested administrative
aspects, also in relation to the conduct of
employees included in the corporate organization
and subject to its direct authority and oversight.
It should also be added, with reference to the
failure to communicate to the Authority the
initiatives undertaken following provision No. 323
of July 18, 2023, that while acknowledging that it
was notified to Maximum's certified email and not
to the addresses actually indicated during the
proceeding, it is represented that this procedural
flaw certainly did not prevent the Company from
becoming aware of the decision of the Guarantor.
By express admission of the party, it was well
aware of the provision sent to its certified email
address and, in this regard, it is appropriate to
add that Legislative Decree No. 76/2020 (the so-called
“simplification decree”), converted with amendments
by Law No. 120/2020, has qualified, in art. 37, the
certified email address of companies as a “digital
domicile” valid for electronic communications
having legal value (see provision No. 126 of April 7,
2022, web doc No. 9771529; see provision No. 23 of
January 27, 2022, web doc No. 9746068).
Therefore, considering that Maximum's certified
email address was fully operational and since the
delivery of the provision was followed by positive
actions on the part of the Company in order to
comply with the prescriptions contained therein, it
must be deemed that full knowledge of the act in
question was achieved, from which a judgment of
overall negligence on the part of the holder in
relations with the Authority arises, particularly
with regard to the obligations of collaboration and
information, a judgment that further supports and
proves the assessment of responsibility in relation
to the contested failure to respond mentioned in the
previous paragraphs.
3. CONCLUSIONS

In light of the above, the contested violation
noted in the initiation of the proceeding of May 10,
2024, must be considered confirmed as the Company
did not respond to the Authority's request for
information of March 12, 2024, and this conduct,
far from constituting a sporadic and occasional
incident, has proven to be yet another episode of
superficial management of requests received from
the Guarantor, as demonstrated by the aforementioned
circumstance of the failure to communicate regarding
the prescriptions imposed by the provision of July
2023.
However, considering that Maximum has already
adopted corrective measures for a timely response
to the Authority's requests, and acknowledging the
initiatives undertaken immediately after the
adoption of the sanctioning provision of 2023
regarding the promotional activities of the
concessionary companies, it does not seem
necessary to impose further prescriptions but,
having established the described violation of art.
157 of the Code, it is believed that the conditions
exist for the application of an administrative
monetary sanction pursuant to arts. 58, paragraph 2,
letter i) and 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation.
4. ORDER FOR THE APPLICATION OF THE ADMINISTRATIVE
MONETARY SANCTION
The conduct described above, constituting a
violation of art. 157 of the Code, requires the
adoption of an order for injunction, pursuant to
arts. 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and 18 of Law
No. 689/1981, for the application against Maximum of
the administrative monetary sanction provided for
by art. 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation.
For the determination of the amount of the sanction,
which must “in any case [be] effective, proportionate
and dissuasive” (art. 83, paragraph 1, of the
Regulation), it is necessary to take into account
the elements indicated in art. 83, paragraph 2, of
the Regulation. 
As circumstances to be considered in
In the specific case, the following aggravating factors must be considered:

1) The severity of the violation, taking into account the repeated omissions by Maximum, which provided late responses to the notes sent by the Authority and only after the initiation of the present proceeding; this pertains both to the communication of compliance with the requirements of the 2023 provision and, lastly, regarding the request for information pursuant to Article 157 of the Code dated March 12, 2024 (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a of the Regulation);

2) The negligent nature of the conduct, considering that the Company acted carelessly with respect to the obligations arising from the regulations on personal data protection; in fact, it should be noted that the occurrence of human error as a reason for the omission is indicative, in itself, of inadequate training of the collaborators, as well as insufficient organizational measures aimed at tracking the activities carried out on behalf of Maximum (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter b of the Regulation);

3) The fact that the Company has already been subject to a sanctioning measure concerning the issue of telemarketing from which the present proceeding originated (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter e of the Regulation).

As mitigating factors, the following should be taken into consideration:

1) The measures adopted, albeit late and after the initiation of the present proceeding, to avoid future disruptions in receiving requests from the Authority, along with the initiatives undertaken to align the processing with the requirements imposed by the 2023 provision (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter c of the Regulation).

In an overall perspective of necessary balance between the rights of the data subjects and the freedom of enterprise, it is essential to prudently evaluate the aforementioned criteria, also in order to limit the economic impact of the sanction.

Therefore, it is believed that - based on the overall elements mentioned above - an administrative sanction of a payment of €10,000.00 (ten thousand) should be applied to Maximum, equivalent to 0.05% of the maximum administrative sanction of €20 million.

In this case, it is also believed that the accessory sanction of publication on the Authority's website of this injunction order should be applied, as provided for by Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and Article 16 of the Authority's Regulation No. 1/2019.

In implementation of the principles set forth in Article 83 of the Regulation, the imposition of such an accessory sanction appears proportionate due to the repeated omission and the aggravation of the times and costs of the proceeding, which results in a significant devaluation of the conduct itself.

Finally, it is noted that the conditions set forth in Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 concerning internal procedures with external relevance, aimed at carrying out the tasks and exercising the powers assigned to the Authority, are met for the annotation of the violations identified here in the Authority's internal register, as provided for by Article 57, paragraph 1, letter u) of the Regulation.

ALL OF THE ABOVE CONSIDERED, THE AUTHORITY

pursuant to Article 57, paragraph 1, letter f), of the Regulation, declares the conduct of Maximum International Corp. S.r.l. - headquartered in Palermo, Contrada Zachia 90038, VAT No. 04434710820 - illegal, as described in the reasoning, consisting of the violation of Article 157 of the Code in relation to the failure to respond to the Authority's request for information;

ORDERS

pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter i), of the Regulation, Maximum International Corp. S.r.l., represented by its legal representative, to pay the sum of €10,000.00 (ten thousand) as an administrative pecuniary sanction for the violations indicated in the reasoning; it is noted that the offender, pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 8, of the Code, has the option to settle the dispute by paying, within 30 days, an amount equal to half of the imposed sanction;

ENJOINS

the aforementioned Company, in the event of failure to settle the dispute pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 8, of the Code, to pay the sum of €10,000.00 (ten thousand), according to the methods indicated in the attachment, within 30 days from the notification of this provision, under penalty of the adoption of subsequent enforcement actions in accordance with Article 27 of Law No. 689/1981;

DISPOSES

a) pursuant to Articles 154-bis of the Code and 37 of Regulation No. 1/2019, the publication of this provision as well as, pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code, the publication of this injunction order on the Authority's website;

b) pursuant to Article 17 of the Authority's Regulation No. 1/2019, orders the annotation in the Authority's internal register, as provided for by Article 57, paragraph 1, letter u) of the Regulation, of the violations and the measures adopted.

Pursuant to Article 78 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, as well as Articles 152 of the Code and 10 of Legislative Decree No. 150 of September 1, 2011, an appeal against this provision may be filed with the ordinary judicial authority, with a petition submitted to the ordinary court of the place where the data controller resides, or, alternatively, to the court of the place of residence of the data subject, within thirty days from the date of communication of this provision, or sixty days if the applicant resides abroad.
