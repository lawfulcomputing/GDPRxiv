to withdraw from the contract within the cooling-off period.

1.3. The outcome of the investigation
Following the information provided by the Company, the Authority conducted further assessments and found that the promotional calls made by Olimpia were indeed conducted without the prior consent of the individuals concerned or involved numbers registered in the Public Opposition Register (RPO). 

2. CONCLUSIONS
2.1. Findings
Based on the investigation, it is concluded that Olimpia S.r.l. has violated the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the national data protection legislation by failing to obtain the necessary consent for the promotional calls made to individuals.

2.2. Measures
In light of the violations identified, the Authority orders the following measures to be implemented by Olimpia S.r.l.:
- Immediate cessation of all promotional calls made without prior consent.
- Implementation of a compliance program to ensure adherence to data protection regulations, including training for staff involved in telemarketing activities.
- Regular reporting to the Authority on the measures taken to comply with the GDPR and the national data protection legislation.

3. FINAL PROVISIONS
This decision is effective immediately and must be complied with within 30 days from the date of notification. Failure to comply may result in further sanctions as provided by law.

3. AUTHORITY'S ASSESSMENTS  
It should be noted that, contrary to what was claimed by the Company, the procedure was conducted in full compliance with the procedural rules and the deadlines provided therein.  
It is worth reiterating that the investigation carried out by the Authority involved multiple requests for information to the Company, one of which related to the direct sales made by it in a week of March 2023. Following this request, the Authority sent the list of numbers subject to possible contact by the sales network of Olimpia to the Fondazione Bordoni to verify whether some of them were present in the Public Register of Oppositions, managed by the same foundation. The relevant feedback was provided by the Fondazione Bordoni on July 21, 2023.  
The communication initiating the procedure, containing the contestation of the alleged violations, was notified to Olimpia via certified electronic mail on December 19, 2023, and thus within the deadline set by table no. 2 attached to internal regulation no. 2/2019 (available on the website www.garanteprivacy.it doc-web no. 9107640), which establishes a period of 120 days from the date of verification (excluding the suspension of deadlines during the summer) for the communication of the initiation of the procedure pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 5, of the Code.  
On the merits, it does not seem possible to share the defensive thesis put forward by the Company regarding the reports and complaints that are the subject of this procedure.  
Indeed, the assertion that "within the Company's IT systems, there are no personal data of reporters and complainants, except for those of the reporter in file no. 191157, whose data were processed within the scope of the right of reconsideration, as well as for the 2 additional reports for unwanted calls (files 313167 and 3131545), received by the Authority during the investigation," appears irrelevant, considering that, as is logically to be assumed, complaints regarding unwanted promotional calls rarely come from interested parties who have activated a service and/or supply contract.  
Similarly, the alleged attribution of such telephone contacts to so-called competitors motivated by the intent to induce the unfortunate user to change provider seems little credible and devoid of any evidential basis, in light of the consideration that the initiatives in this regard adopted by the Company appear to be limited to sending warnings and reports, which were never pursued either with the competent authorities or extrajudicially. Furthermore, it is not clear how one could exploit the name of a company to then propose the conclusion of a supply contract with another.  
With specific reference to report no. 184158, the holder highlighted that the contact cannot be attributed to the Company, as the interested party stated that the caller had "posed as their gas provider." In fact, with the complaint in question, using the same form, the interested party intended to report two different telephone contacts. The first contact, made at 12:45 on July 22, 2022, is undoubtedly attributed to Olimpia, as in the column "company/business making the advertising call," the reporter indicated "Sinergy Luce." Conversely, the second report was not taken into consideration – and could not be – by the Authority, as it was generic and thus lacked the elements necessary for its evaluation.  
In the same way, complaint no. 184868, also contested by the Company, contained two reports, one of which was detailed and complete with temporal references, the calling number, and attribution to the Sinergy brand. The other report, recorded on the same form, although it also contained temporal references and the calling number, did not include elements suitable for identifying the data controller and therefore was not taken into consideration by the Authority.  
On this point, the assertion that the company had never received a prior request from the interested parties regarding the complaints related to the exercise of the rights under Articles 15 et seq. of the Regulation seems to be contradicted by the documentation in the case file and provided to Olimpia along with the request for information pursuant to Article 157 of the Code.  
In fact, all reports and complaints containing requests related to the exercise of rights were sent by the same senders to the Company, and for some, Olimpia had already provided feedback (see files nos. 179911, 180374, 181096, 191140, 313167).  
Regarding the telephone contacts made during the so-called "sample week" that generated 4,223 contractual proposals, the exception raised by the party concerning the 604 contracts concluded door-to-door, the 133 duplications, the test number, the interested party already being a customer for a different distribution point, and the 90 numbers contacted, prior to granting consent subsequent to registration with the RPO, is worthy of acceptance.  
It follows that the remaining 34 telephone contacts were made during the so-called "week."
"sample" in the absence of an appropriate legal basis. More specifically, the two phone contacts made with former clients cannot be classified as legitimate, since any consents previously obtained have been invalidated by the subsequent registrations of the numbers in the Register of Oppositions, in the absence of elements relating to the termination of the contract. The remaining 32 numbers - by the company's own admission - are the result of unlawful data processing. 

Contacting 34 phone numbers as part of telemarketing activities carried out during the period of February-March, while the same numbers were registered with the RPO - and therefore under the opt-out mechanism established by current regulations - constitutes a violation of Article 130, paragraphs 3 and 3-bis, of the Code, concerning electronic communications, as well as, more generally, Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a) and 6, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation, regarding the principle of lawfulness and the necessity of a legal basis of consent to legitimize the processing of the data in question for promotional purposes.

In this regard, the alleged attribution of the majority of the unlawful phone contacts made during the so-called sample week to Agency XX is irrelevant, considering that the warning issued by Olimpia concerns a single attempt at telephone fraud and that from the analysis of the list of proposals in the documents, it emerges that only one contact can be traced back to this agency within the "residential teleselling" network.

The observations made, when considered together, on one hand allow for a favorable view of the effort made by the Company to better align its organizational structure with the current regulations on personal data protection and to curb the phenomenon of so-called wild telemarketing. As a result, it must be considered partially archived, pursuant to Article 11, paragraph 1, letter b) of internal regulation no. 1/2019 (available for consultation on the website www.gpdp.it doc-web n. 9107633), the complaint regarding the possible violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter f), 25, and 32 of the Regulation.

On the other hand, they suggest the appropriateness of indicating to the Company the adoption of suitable measures to prevent contracts generated from a contact made upstream in the absence of an appropriate legal basis from entering the company's assets. In this regard, the implementation of a series of measures (such as, for example, OTP signature and blocking check-call) aimed at assessing the actual willingness of the interested party to enter into the contract appears certainly commendable.

Moreover, such types of control are also provided for by Article 16 of the Code of Conduct for telemarketing and teleselling activities (available on the website www.garanteprivacy.it doc-web n. 9868813 - Provv. GPDP no. 70 of March 9, 2023 in GU no. 73 of March 27, 2024), which, regardless of adherence, presents undeniable value in terms of best practices, in that it provides that "1. Data controllers adopt organizational and/or technical procedures aimed at demonstrating that the data of the interested party/contractor/user have been acquired in compliance with the principles set forth in Article 5, paragraph 1, of the Regulation; in particular, taking into account the principle of proportionality, through default measures, they implement in the systems specific procedures that identify promotional campaigns, contact lists, and the operators involved in each contract concluded at a distance and are able to demonstrate the correctness of the aforementioned information. Such procedures prevent the registration of contracts for which the aforementioned information is not retrievable (...). 2. Those adhering to this Code of Conduct ensure that the entire supply chain processes the data exclusively on the basis of appropriate consent to processing for telemarketing and teleselling purposes that is clearly distinct from the manifestation of the contractual will. (...) 6. The client develops its processes so that contracts concluded following teleselling activities occur in the presence of unequivocal consent to the original contact, except in cases falling within the scope of Article 130, paragraph 3-bis of the Code. In the initial application of this Code of Conduct and solely for the protection of the interested party, if following the checks contracts emerge for which the first contact is found to be flawed, such contracts may continue to be executed provided that the client informs the interested party of the flawed origin of the contract and that the interested party confirms the desire to maintain it, without prejudice to residual cases where the client does not follow up on proven attempts to contact the client (...)."

In this sense, the numerous complaints received by the Authority also support this. The high number of reports submitted, equal to that of the numbers disowned by the company, only apparently represents a data point separate from the further one relating to the contracts expressly requested by the Authority in the "sample" week. A treatment chain correctly conceived and structured according to the indications that the GDPR provides regarding lawfulness and accountability would not yield two such concerning data points: a) the presence of phone contacts that should not have occurred from the outset; b) the presence of complaints from citizens "foreign" to the company's information assets (in the sense of non-customers, by the company's own admission) who report contacts made in the name and on behalf of the Sinergy brand. 

Furthermore, from the investigation, it emerged that the Company...
had full awareness of the phenomenon, having been, in more than one case, a direct recipient of the requests from the interested parties and therefore was fully capable of intervening.  
It must therefore be confirmed that Olimpia is responsible for the violations contested through the initiation communication of the procedure pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 5, of the Code of December 19, 2023.  
However, in order to correctly modulate the corrective measures to be adopted, also in relation to the indications contained in Chapter 7 of the Guidelines 04/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines under the GDPR (available for consultation on the website www.edpb.europa.eu), all the technical and organizational security measures adopted by the Company so far must be duly considered, as well as what Olimpia stated in the exercise of the right of defense, specifically regarding the overall process aimed at no longer using teleselling activities, favoring forms of contact with potential customers through door-to-door sales and the enhancement of the promotional channel established by the Company's physical stores, which are already widely present in the territory.  
In this regard, it must be considered that the significant unwanted contacts coming from the so-called "underbrush" of telemarketing, that is, from subjects not included in the Company's sales network, could, due to the aforementioned choice and future reorganizations of the same sales network, be eliminated at the root, resulting in a significant benefit for users.  
4. CONCLUSIONS  
For the reasons stated above, it is preliminarily deemed necessary to archive, pursuant to Article 11, paragraph 1, letter b) of the internal regulation no. 1/2019, the contestation regarding the violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter f), 25, and 32 of the Regulation.  

Furthermore, considering that 22 complaints and reports regarding unwanted contacts have been received against Olimpia and that such contacts have been made entirely by subjects external to the Company's sales network; also considering that even following a sample check, illicit contacts emerged that involved, over the course of a week, 34 subjects who then concluded a contract with Olimpia, and taking note of the defensive considerations, it is deemed that Olimpia's responsibility has been established regarding the following violations:  
a) of Article 130, paragraphs 3 and 3-bis, of the Code for Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a) and 6, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation for having contacted 34 telephone numbers in the context of telemarketing activities, while the same numbers were registered with the RPO - and therefore under the opt-out mechanism - as determined by current legislation;  
b) of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a) and 5, paragraph 2, 6, paragraph 1, letter a), 24 paragraph 1 of the Regulation for the failure to implement adequate technical and organizational security measures and controls regarding the commercial supply chain and partners.  
It has also been established that the conduct of the Company concerning the treatments examined is unlawful, and it is necessary to:  
- impose on Olimpia, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter f) of the Regulation, the prohibition of any further processing of the data of the reporters and complainants;  
- order Olimpia, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letters d) and e) of the Regulation, to communicate to the 34 interested parties, whose personal data entered the Company's systems following illicit contacts, the outcomes of today's procedure based on a text to be agreed upon with the Authority in the application of this provision;  
- order Olimpia, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter d) to implement adequate controls within its sales network and appropriate system enhancements, in order to exclude that contracts generated from illicit contacts may enter the company's assets;  
- adopt an injunction order, pursuant to Articles 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and 18 of Law no. 689/1981, for the application against Olimpia of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraphs 3 and 5, of the Regulation.  
5. INJUNCTION ORDER FOR THE APPLICATION OF THE ADMINISTRATIVE PECUNIARY SANCTION  
The violations indicated above necessitate the adoption of an injunction order, pursuant to Articles 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and 18 of Law no. 689/1981, for the application against Olimpia of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraphs 3 and 5, of the Regulation (payment of an amount up to €20,000,000.00 or, for companies with over €500,000,000 in revenue, up to 4% of the annual worldwide revenue of the previous financial year).  
For the determination of the amount of the sanction, it is necessary to take into account the elements indicated in Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation.  
In this case, the following are relevant:  
1) the severity of the violations (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a) of the Regulation), taking into account the subject matter and purposes of the processed data, related to the overall phenomenon of telemarketing, regarding which the Authority has adopted, particularly in the last three years, numerous measures that have thoroughly examined the multiple elements of criticality, providing the holders with numerous indications to adapt the treatments to the current legislation and to mitigate the impact of nuisance calls on the interested parties;  
2) as a mitigating factor, the degree of responsibility of the data controller, taking into account the technical and organizational security measures already implemented.
