**3. Outcome of the investigative activity**

**3.1. The legislation on personal data protection.**

The processing of personal data must occur in compliance with the applicable legislation on personal data protection and, in particular, with the provisions of the Regulation and the Code. In particular, "personal data" is defined as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('data subject')." Furthermore, "an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, particularly by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person" (Article 4, paragraph 1, no. 1, of the Regulation).

Regarding special categories of data, these are considered to be "personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, or trade union membership, as well as [...] genetic data, biometric data intended to uniquely identify a natural person, data concerning health or the sexual life or sexual orientation of the person" (Article 9, paragraph 1, of the Regulation).

With particular reference to the case brought to the Authority's attention, it is noted that the operation of "dissemination" of personal data – that is, "making personal data known to unspecified subjects, in any form, including by making them available or consulting them" (Article 2-ter, paragraph 4, letter b) of the Code) – processed for the execution of a task of public interest or related to the exercise of public powers, is permitted only when provided for by a legal norm or, in cases provided for by law, by regulation (Article 2-ter, paragraph 3, of the Code, in the text prior to the amendments made by the aforementioned Legislative Decree No. 139 of October 8, 2021, converted, with amendments, by Law No. 205 of December 3, 2021) and always in compliance with the principles regarding personal data protection, including the principles of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency," according to which data must be "processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject" and "integrity and confidentiality," according to which data must be "processed in a manner that ensures adequate security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage" (Article 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and f) of the Regulation).

In particular, regarding the adoption of technical and organizational security measures, Article 32 of the Regulation establishes that "taking into account the state of the art and the costs of implementation, as well as the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, and the risk of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller and the data processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk."

**3.2. The dissemination of special categories of data by the Company.**

From the assessment carried out based on the elements acquired and the facts emerged during the investigative activity, it was found that the processing of the data in question, carried out by the company on behalf of the Municipality, was initiated without the role being regulated pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation (nor pursuant to Article 29 of the Code, in the text prior to the amendments made by Legislative Decree No. 101 of August 10, 2018), as the service contract signed in 2001 and subsequently supplemented in 2011, which initially conferred the functions of managing the "public urban hygiene service," in existence at the time of the personal data breach and only subsequently superseded by the new contract of XX, did not meet the characteristics of the legal act intended to regulate the relationship with the data processor, as it did not contain either the elements required by Article 29 of the previous Code or the elements required by Article 28 of the Regulation.

In this regard, it is emphasized that "The presence (or absence) of a written agreement, however, is not decisive for the existence of a data controller-data processor relationship. [...] a data controller-data processor relationship could exist even in the absence of a written processing agreement. [...] Moreover, under certain circumstances, the absence of a clear definition of the relationship between the controller and the processor may lead to the issue of the lack of a legal basis on which any processing should be based, for example regarding the communication of data between the controller and the alleged processor" (see, in this sense, the Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of data controller).
processing and of the data controller pursuant to the GDPR, version 2.0, adopted on July 7, 2021, by the European Data Protection Board, specifically note 42 to point 103). However – also considering the state of emergency related to the COVID-19 epidemic, declared by the Council of Ministers on January 31, 2020, and the legislative interventions aimed at identifying urgent measures for the containment and management of the epidemiological emergency – the subject of the dispute within the investigation conducted by the Authority was not the legitimacy of the processing concerning the communication, by the Municipality, of the list containing the minimized data of citizens affected by the Sars Covid-19 virus, to the company for the activation of special differentiated waste collection and disposal services, but rather the dissemination, in the sense defined above, of such a list, which constitutes an excessive processing compared to the aforementioned communication. The dissemination of health-related data, as emerged during the investigation, was attributable to the Company, which did not provide indications regarding the legal basis that would legitimize such processing. 

In this regard, it is noted that the processing of personal data carried out by public entities or managers of public services is, as a rule, lawful only if necessary “to comply with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject” or “for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller” (art. 6, par. 1, lett. c) and e), of the Regulation). It is also provided that “Member States may maintain […] more specific provisions to adapt the application of the rules of this regulation with regard to processing, in accordance with paragraph 1, letters c) and e), determining more precisely specific requirements for the processing and other measures to ensure lawful and fair processing […]” (art. 6, par. 2, of the Regulation). As for processing concerning special categories of personal data referred to in Article 9, par. 1, of the Regulation, necessary for reasons of substantial public interest pursuant to par. 2, lett. g), of the same article, “are permitted where they are provided for by Union law or, in domestic law, by statutory provisions or, in cases provided for by law, by regulations that specify the types of data that may be processed, the operations that may be carried out, and the relevant substantial public interest, as well as the appropriate and specific measures to safeguard the fundamental rights and interests of the data subject” (art. 2-sexies, comma 1, of the Code, in the text in force at the time of the facts, prior to the amendments made by the aforementioned d.l. October 8, 2021, n. 139, converted, with modifications, by law December 3, 2021, n. 205). 

In any case, health-related data, that is, those “related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, revealing information about their health status” (art. 4, par. 1, n. 15 of the Regulation), due to the greater guarantees recognized by the Regulation and the Code, given the particular sensitivity of this category of data, it is expressly provided that “they may not be disseminated” (art. 2-septies, comma 8, of the Code and art. 9, par. 4, of the Regulation). 

Furthermore, regarding the emergency situation, it is highlighted that, within the management of the state of emergency related to the COVID-19 epidemic, declared by the Council of Ministers on January 31, 2020, numerous provisions have been adopted that involve the processing of personal data. These provisions provide, on the one hand, that public administrations may carry out the processing of personal data that is necessary for the performance of the functions assigned to them in the context of the emergency caused by the spread of Covid-19, and on the other hand, that the necessary measures are adopted to ensure compliance with the current regulations on the protection of personal data and, in particular, the principles applicable to processing, as set out in art. 5 of the Regulation (cf. art. 5 of the ordinance of the Head of the Department of Civil Protection of February 3, 2020, n. 630; art. 17 bis of the decree-law of March 17, 2020, n. 18, converted with modifications, by law April 24, 2020, n. 27). 

As already highlighted, the current regulations prohibit the dissemination of health-related data, and this prohibition has not been derogated by the emergency legislation on Covid-19 (art. 9 of the Regulation; arts. 2-septies, comma 8, of the Code and 166, comma 2 of the Code; art. 14 of the decree-law of March 9, 2020, n. 14 and art. 17bis of the aforementioned d.l. of March 17, 2020, n. 18, converted with modifications, by law April 24, 2020, n. 27). Such processing, besides being prohibited, in the context of managing the emergency adds to the risks for the data subjects and their families, in addition to the penalty for exposure to the disease, also that arising from an unacceptable dissemination of very sensitive aspects related to their private lives. 

With particular reference to the context in question, it is also emphasized the risk that, even in the case of the publication of documents in which the complete identifying data of the data subjects are not reported (for example, the communication containing only the pointed initials, etc.), especially in territorial realities such as that of the aforementioned Municipality, the data subjects may still be identifiable. As emerged during the investigation, the dissemination of health-related data was also caused by a lack of adequate technical and organizational measures (e.g., circulars, guidelines, etc.) suitable to ensure adequate security of the processing.
personal data also in terms of attention, on the part of the internal staff of the Company, to the risks arising from unauthorized or unlawful processing. In this regard, it is appropriate to highlight that, although the data controller is obliged to process the data in accordance with the instructions given by the data subject, certain obligations provided for by the regulations on personal data protection are also directly imposed on the data processor. 

In this regard, Article 32 of the Regulation establishes that “[…] the data controller and the data processor implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk.” Therefore, it is also the responsibility of the data processor, due to their experience and expertise in the specific sector in which they operate, to adopt appropriate technical and organizational measures. 

In light of the above considerations, the processing of personal data concerning the dissemination of health-related data by the company appears to have been carried out in violation of the regulations on personal data protection, in a manner inconsistent with the principle of “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency” and in the absence of a legal basis, in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), and 9 of the Regulation, as well as Article 2-septies, paragraph 8 of the Code (in the text in force at the time of the facts, prior to the amendments made by Decree Law No. 139 of October 8, 2021, converted, with amendments, by Law No. 205 of December 3, 2021). It is also established that the principle of “integrity and confidentiality” (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter f) and Article 32 of the Regulation has been violated.

4. Conclusions. 
In light of the assessments mentioned above, it is noted that the statements made by the Company, as the data processor, during the investigation – the truthfulness of which may be subject to accountability under Article 168 of the Code – although deserving of consideration, do not allow for overcoming the objections notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure and are insufficient to allow for the archiving of the present procedure, as none of the cases provided for by Article 11 of the Regulation of the Guarantor No. 1/2019 apply. 

Therefore, the preliminary assessments of the Office are confirmed, and the unlawfulness of the processing of personal data carried out by the Company is noted, for having processed personal data in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and f), 9, and 32 of the Regulation, and Article 2-septies, paragraph 8 of the Code (in the text prior to the amendments made by Decree Law No. 139 of October 8, 2021, converted, with amendments, by Law No. 205 of December 3, 2021). Considering that the violation of the aforementioned provisions occurred as a result of a single conduct, Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation applies, according to which the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation. Given that, in this case, the most serious violations, relating to Articles 5 and 9 of the Regulation and Article 2-septies, paragraph 8 of the Code are subject to the sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation, as recalled also by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code, the total amount of the sanction is to be quantified, considering that the turnover of the Company is equal to €3,151,959.00 (three million one hundred fifty-one thousand/00) for the year 2022, up to €20,000,000 (twenty million/00). 

Furthermore, considering that the conduct has exhausted its effects, the prerequisites for the adoption of further corrective measures under Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not exist.

5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code). 
The Guarantor, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to “impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to [other] corrective measures [provided for in this paragraph], or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case” and, in this context, “the Board [of the Guarantor] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Guarantor's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code” (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Regulation of the Guarantor No. 1/2019). 

In this regard, considering Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation, in this case, the violation of the cited provisions is subject to the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation. 

The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into due account the elements provided for by Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation. 

With specific regard to the nature and severity of the violation (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a), of the Regulation), both the specific nature of the processing – concerning the limited dissemination of personal data through a private WhatsApp chat, and the limited duration of the same must be considered. It is also believed that the fact that the publication concerned a small number of individuals (nine cases) (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a) of the Regulation) should be taken into account. 

However, the sensitivity of the data processed must be considered unfavorably to the Company (Article...
83, par. 2, lett. g), of the Regulation), as they may reveal the health conditions of the data subjects. 

In light of these circumstances, it is believed that, in this specific case, the severity level of the violation committed by the data controller can be considered medium (cf. European Data Protection Board, “Guidelines 04/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines under the GDPR” of May 23, 2023, point 60). 

That being said, it must be taken into account, in favor of the Company for the purposes of quantifying the sanction, that there are no previous relevant violations committed by the data controller and that the aforementioned Company has taken action (by initiating an internal procedure and filing a complaint regarding the events that occurred) in order to understand the dynamics and the individuals responsible for the dissemination, also to adopt measures aimed at mitigating the damage suffered by the data subjects (art. 83, par. 2, lett. c) and e) of the Regulation). Additionally, it is noted, also in favor of the Company, that the violation occurred during the initial phase of the state of emergency related to the COVID-19 epidemic, declared by the Council of Ministers on January 31, 2020, in the presence of a peculiar organizational and management situation in which the Company found itself for the management of the waste collection service from positive or quarantined individuals, as well as from a peculiar climate of uncertainty and tension on the part of the population regarding the precautionary measures to be adopted to prevent the spread of the virus (art. 83, par. 2, lett. k) of the Regulation). 

In light of the aforementioned elements, assessed as a whole, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the monetary sanction at the sum of €6,000.00 (six thousand/00) for the violation of arts. 5, par. 1, lett. a) and f), 9 and 32 of the Regulation, and of art. 2-septies, par. 8 of the Code (in the text prior to the amendments made by the decree law of October 8, 2021, no. 139, converted, with amendments, by law of December 3, 2021, no. 205), as an administrative monetary sanction deemed, pursuant to art. 83, par. 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. 

In light of the above and considering the type of violations established that concerned the general principles of processing, and in particular the principles of lawfulness, integrity, and confidentiality, it is also believed that the accessory sanction of publication on the website of the Authority of this provision should be applied, as provided for by art. 166, paragraph 7 of the Code and art. 16 of the Regulation of the Authority no. 1/2019. 

Finally, it is noted that the conditions set forth in art. 17 of Regulation no. 1/2019 are met.
