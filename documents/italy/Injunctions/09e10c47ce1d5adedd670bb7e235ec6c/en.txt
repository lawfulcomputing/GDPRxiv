3. Outcome of the investigative activity
Taking note of what was represented by Dr. Anzalone in the documentation on file, in the defense briefs, and in the hearing, the following observations are made:
1. “The data controller, or anyone acting under its authority or that of the data controller, who has access to personal data may not process such data unless instructed to do so by the data controller, unless required by Union or Member State law” (Article 29 of the Regulation);
2. “The data controller” is “the natural or legal person, public authority, service, or other body that, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data” (Article 4, paragraph 1, point 7, of the Regulation);
3. “Determining the purposes and means is equivalent to deciding, respectively, the ‘why’ and the ‘how’ of the processing: given a specific processing operation, the data controller is the subject that has determined the why of the processing (that is, ‘for what purpose’ or ‘for what reason’ it is carried out) and how that objective is achieved (that is, what means are employed to achieve it)” (point n. 35 of the EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of data controller and data processor under the GDPR, adopted on July 7, 2021; see also point n. 88, which highlights that “to the extent that the employee processes personal data for their own purposes, different from those of the employer, they will be considered a data controller, will be liable for all consequences, and will assume all responsibilities arising from the processing of personal data”);
4. “Taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks having varying probabilities and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller implements appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure, and to be able to demonstrate, that the processing is carried out in accordance with the (…) regulation” (Article 24, paragraph 1, and Article 5, paragraph 2, of the Regulation in relation to the principle of ‘accountability’);
5. The information subject to the described communication to third parties concerns health-related data, where “health-related data” refers to “personal data concerning the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care, which reveals information about their health status” (Article 4, paragraph 1, numbers 1 and 15 of the Regulation). Such data merit greater protection since the context of their processing could create significant risks for fundamental rights and freedoms (Recital n. 51);
6. The regulation on the protection of personal data provides that such information may only be communicated to the data subject and may be communicated to third parties only on the basis of an appropriate legal basis or at the request of the data subject itself, following a written delegation from the latter (Article 9 of the Regulation and Article 84 of the Code - in the previous version prior to the reformulation of the same Code by the legislator with Legislative Decree No. 101 of August 10, 2018 - in conjunction with Article 22, paragraph 11, Legislative Decree No. 101 of August 10, 2018);
7. It is no longer necessary to request the consent of the data subject (Article 9, paragraph 2, letter h) and paragraph 3 of the Regulation) only for processing that is “necessary” for the pursuit of specific care purposes, carried out by (or under the responsibility of) a healthcare professional subject to professional secrecy). Such processing is that
“essential” for the achievement of one or more specific purposes explicitly related to health care (see recital 53 of the Regulation) (...), as clarified by the Guarantor in decision no. 55 of March 7, 2019 (available on the official website www.gpdp.it, web doc no. 9091942). In this decision, the Guarantor also expressly highlighted that “any treatments related, only in a broad sense, to care, but not strictly necessary, therefore require, even if carried out by healthcare professionals, a distinct legal basis to be identified, possibly, in the consent of the data subject or in another condition of lawfulness” (art. 9, par. 2, lett. a) of the Regulation);

8. for “consent of the data subject” it is understood as “(...) any manifestation of free, specific, informed, and unequivocal will of the data subject, by which the same expresses his or her assent, through a declaration or unequivocal positive action, that the personal data concerning him or her are subject to processing” (art. 4, no. 11, of the Regulation). “Where processing is based on consent, the data controller must be able to demonstrate that the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data” (art. 7 and recital 42 of the Regulation). Such consent, in the case of processing of special categories of data, must be “explicit” (art. 9 par. 2, lett. a) and recital 51 of the Regulation);

9. the data controller is required to comply with the principles regarding data protection, including that of “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency,” “data minimization,” as well as “integrity and confidentiality,” according to which personal data must be “processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject”; “adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed”; “processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security (…), including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage” (art. 5, par. 1, lett. a), c) and f) of the Regulation);

10. the conduct of Dr. Anzalone, through the transmission, via email, of reports containing the results of the polysomnographic examination carried out on Mr. XX, to the company Linde, resulted in a communication of data related to the health of the aforementioned complainant.

4. Evaluations of the Guarantor and conclusions
In light of the evaluations mentioned above, taking into account the statements made by Dr. Anzalone and the company Aesthetic Medical Care s.r.l. during the investigation - and considering that, unless the act constitutes a more serious offense, anyone who, in a proceeding before the Guarantor, falsely declares or certifies news or circumstances or produces false acts or documents is liable under art. 168 of the Code “Falsehood in declarations to the Guarantor and interruption of the execution of the tasks or the exercise of the powers of the Guarantor” - it is represented that the elements provided by Dr. Anzalone in the defense briefs and during the hearing do not allow for overcoming the objections notified by the Office with the aforementioned initiation act of the proceeding, nor do any of the cases provided for by art. 11 of the Guarantor Regulation no. 1/2019 apply.

In fact, from the documentation on record, it does not appear, in any way, documented that the initiative regarding the communication of health data to the supplier of the machinery with which to carry out CPAP ventilatory therapy was undertaken following specific instructions from the company Aesthetic Medical Care s.r.l.; on the contrary, it appears that the decision in this regard was made independently by the doctor, who deemed it necessary to anticipate the communication to the company Linde of the patient’s data, in order to allow the latter to carry out the “titration” as soon as possible. It is therefore believed that, for the processing of the data in question, Dr. Anzalone should be considered an independent data controller, having determined the purposes and means of the processing.

Furthermore, the argument regarding the circumstance that the processing in question was aimed at pursuing a “care purpose” cannot be accepted, considering that the operation of communication described above cannot be deemed “essential” for achieving a specific purpose explicitly related to health care; this is because the patient had already received the indication of the company to which he could have turned, had he wished to do so.

From the email exchange obtained in the records, it emerges, in fact, that Dr. Anzalone had informed the patient that the “SSN provides the necessary ventilator for therapy only as a provision for the disabled, and one must have a Civil Disability equal to or greater than 34%; otherwise, he must proceed to purchase the instrument at his own expense. Regarding the acclimatization and titration of the instrument, necessary steps for the prescription, and the acquisition of estimates for the machinery,” Mr. XX was therefore invited to contact “Linde Care Point - Via del Ponte alle Mosse.” The patient, having obtained the aforementioned information, expressed his intention to “purchase the machinery as soon as possible,” thereby demonstrating the intention to proceed independently. Therefore, the advance communication to the company Linde of the information related to Mr. XX, although allegedly aimed at facilitating the acquisition of the machinery and the related “titration” activity, cannot be considered indispensable for care.
considering that the patient was already in a position to act independently by procuring the machinery. Moreover, the assertion that the claimant, in explicit response to Dr. Anzalone's communication, did not raise any objections to his doctor but, on the contrary, clearly confirmed that he agreed with his actions (“I will proceed (as soon) as possible to purchase the machinery”) with the consequence that through Mr. XX's email, the consent of the interested party would thus have been acquired (“Mr. XX's email of XX is a clear expression of consent to the processing of data!”), does not appear to be shareable. In fact, for consent to be valid, in addition to being “free,” “specific,” “informed,” and “unequivocal” (art. 4, n. 11 of the Regulation), in the case of processing special categories of data, as referred to in art. 9 of the same Regulation, it must also be “explicit.” In any case, for processing based on consent, the data controller must be able to demonstrate that the interested party has consented to the processing (art. 7 and recital 42 of the Regulation). In this regard, the acquisition of the alleged (by the doctor) “implicit consent” of Mr. XX has not only not been demonstrated in any way, but has also been contradicted by the fact that the patient is, precisely in this regard, the current claimant, who complains that Dr. Anzalone “did not obtain the specific consent of the patient for sending the report to parties other than the patient, particularly to commercial companies.” This conclusion is also supported by the evidence that the initiative to contact the aforementioned company was left to the patient, who was free to contact it or not, having Dr. Anzalone himself provided the patient with the contact details of the company to reach out to. Furthermore, the signing of the consent form for the processing of data, which Dr. Anzalone transmitted as an attachment, was submitted by Aesthetic Medical Care and concerns the relationship between it and Mr. XX and not with the specialist; in any case, the consent provided does not appear to have been given in relation to the operation of communicating the personal data in question. Moreover, in compliance with the principle of data minimization, for the purpose of “titling” activities, the company would have needed to acquire elements relating only to the pathology of the potential recipient of the machinery, but not the entire report, including identifying data. For the reasons outlined above, the illegality of the processing of personal data carried out by Dr. Anzalone is noted for having communicated health data relating to Mr. XX to a third party, in violation of the basic principles of processing referred to in art. 5, par. 1, lett. a), c), and f) of the Regulation and in the absence of the legal grounds provided for in art. 9 of the Regulation, considering that the acquisition of the consent of the interested party for the processing of his personal data for the aforementioned operation has not been demonstrated. Therefore, the preliminary assessments of the Office regarding the established violation of the basic principles of processing referred to in arts. 5, par. 1, lett. a), c), and f) and 9 of the Regulation are confirmed. In this context, taking into account that the conduct has exhausted its effects, the conditions for the adoption of corrective measures referred to in art. 58, par. 2, of the Regulation do not exist.  
5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (arts. 58, par. 2, lett. i and 83 of the Regulation; art. 166, comma 7, of the Code).  
The violation of arts. 5, par. 1, lett. a), c), and f) and 9 of the Regulation, caused by the conduct carried out by Dr. Anzalone, is subject to the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to art. 83, par. 5, lett. a) of the Regulation.  
It should be noted that the Guarantor, pursuant to arts. 58, par. 2, lett. i) and 83 of the Regulation, as well as art. 166 of the Code, has the power to “impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to article 83, in addition to the [other] [corrective] measures referred to in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case” and, in this context, “the Board [of the Guarantor] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Guarantor's website pursuant to article 166, comma 7, of the Code” (art. 16, comma 1, of the Guarantor Regulation n. 1/2019).  
The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into account the principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and deterrence, indicated in art. 83, par. 1, of the Regulation, in light of the elements provided in art. 85, par. 2, of the Regulation in relation to which it is observed that:  
- the communication made by Dr. Anzalone, as the data controller as a natural person, involved a single recipient and concerned data suitable to reveal information about the health of a single patient (art. 83, par. 2, lett. a) and g) of the Regulation);  
- Dr. Anzalone has shown a collaborative behavior with the Authority during the investigation and the present procedure (art. 83, par. 2, lett. f) of the Regulation);  
- no further reports or complaints have been received regarding the conduct subject to the present procedure (art. 83, par. 2, lett. h) of the Regulation);  
- no measures have been taken against the same doctor.
**PRECEDING MEASURES ADOPTED REGARDING RELEVANT VIOLATIONS (ART. 83, PAR. 2, LETT. E) OF THE REGULATION).**

In light of the aforementioned elements, evaluated as a whole, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the monetary sanction provided for by art. 83, par. 5, lett. a) of the Regulation, in the amount of euro 5,000.00 (five thousand) for the violation of arts. 5, par. 1, lett. a), c) and f) and 9 of the Regulation as an administrative monetary sanction considered, pursuant to art. 83, par. 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and deterrent.

It is also considered that the accessory sanction of publication on the website of the Authority of this provision should be applied, as provided for by art. 166, comma 7 of the Code and art. 16 of the Regulation of the Authority n. 1/2019.

Finally, it is noted that the conditions referred to in art. 17 of Regulation n. 1/2019 concerning internal procedures with external relevance, aimed at carrying out the tasks and exercising the powers entrusted to the Authority, are met.
