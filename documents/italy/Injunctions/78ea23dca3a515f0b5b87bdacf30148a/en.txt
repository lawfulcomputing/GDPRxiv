**PREMISES**  

1. **Introduction.**  
The Authority has learned from public sources that the Municipality of Forlì (hereinafter, the “Municipality”) had made available to citizens a software application, called “Falco – Città protetta,” developed by FMI S.r.l. (hereinafter, “FMI” or the “Company”), which allowed citizens to send reports to the local Police regarding situations of degradation or that raised a certain level of social alarm, however not to the extent that would require an emergency call to 112.  
Once a report was received, the personnel of the local Police responsible for viewing surveillance images from cameras installed in the municipal territory for urban safety purposes could monitor the situation in the specific area concerned by the report and, if necessary, send a patrol on-site to carry out checks, having temporarily acquired the GPS position and the phone number of the reporting device.  
From an informational page published on the FMI website (https://www.fmi.fc.it/falco/), it also appeared that, following the receipt of a report, the local Police could also send drones to the area concerned to carry out the appropriate checks.  
In relation to the processing of personal data of the interested parties, the Municipality qualified as the data controller, indicating FMI as the data processor (see the information on the processing of personal data published at the time on the Municipality's institutional website, at the address https://www.fmi.fc.it/privacy-falco/#1651136340326-95cb6497-1535), while FMI in turn qualified as the data controller (see the different information published at the time on the Company’s website, at the address https://www.fmi.fc.it/privacy-falco).

3. Outcome of the investigative activity.
3.1 The processing of personal data carried out within the framework of the "Falco" project.
At the conclusion of the investigation, as reconstructed above, it emerged that, within the so-called "Falco" project, the Municipality, utilizing FMI, had created a computer system consisting of a "Web on centralized server" component and, on the user side, a specific software application for mobile devices (see note prot. no. 0083196/2022 of July 18, 2022). The software application named "Falco" allowed users to verify their presence in an area under surveillance by the Municipality, as well as to send reports regarding possible situations of degradation or generic potential risk - not such as to necessitate an emergency call - highlighting for local police operators, responsible for viewing the surveillance images, the specific footage transmitted by the camera present at the location subject to reporting.

As for the server-side application of the Falco system, it was made available to the Local Police for tracking all anonymized reports sent via the aforementioned application, in order to identify which areas had more or fewer reports over time, facilitating the analysis of the Local Police Command regarding the areas of the municipal territory that required greater monitoring. It was only possible to trace temporarily (for seven days) the phone number associated with the geographical coordinates of the report and the so-called timestamp, to allow the Operational Centers to possibly contact the user who made the report. After seven days, the phone number associated with the coordinates was deleted from the archives, with only the static GPS coordinates of the report and the timestamp when it was made remaining available for statistical purposes.

In terms of timing:
the "Falco" application was made available in the online stores "Google Play Store" on December 20, 2021, and "Apple Store" on January 26, 2022, in a version that allowed the user to send a report to the local police, without the possibility of classifying it with predefined settings; moreover, the user could activate the "keyboard" functionality of the phone preset with the number 112; reports could be voluntarily anonymized by the user by pressing a specific button, and were in any case automatically anonymized, through the deletion of the phone number associated with the static geographical position of the terminal from which the report originated, after seven days from the date of sending it (see FMI report attached as annex 9 to note prot. no. 0003022/2023 of January 11, 2023);

on April 6, 2022, the application was modified as follows: the possibility of specifying the type of report from a predefined list of items (littering, vandalism and graffiti, traffic accident, domestic violence, bullying, other report) was introduced; the possibility for the user to anonymize their report before the seven-day period was eliminated; the possibility of attaching a photograph was introduced, but only if taken anew, i.e., without the option to choose a previously saved photo from the user's terminal (ibidem);

from the end of April 2022, the application was deactivated, only to be reactivated at the beginning of June 2022 (see report of the DPO of the Municipality attached as annex 5 to note prot. no. 0083196/2022 of July 18, 2022);

on July 11, 2022, following the initiation of the investigation by the Authority, the application was removed from online stores (see FMI report cited) and all reports received were anonymized.

It also emerged that during the normal use of the application, a request was sent to the server every 3.5 seconds containing a so-called digitally signed token, associated with the user's phone number and the static position of the device, which would be "discarded (deleted from the server's memory) after verifying the signature and its expiration date, as its purpose is solely to prevent robots from massively contacting the web service," without it being "ever stored even transiently in a database, not even in cache or temporary storage"; the static geographical coordinate was "never saved in any case; upon receiving the request to
webservice, [was] confronted with the list of monitored areas (geometric polygons saved in the database) and calculated whether it is within one of them or not. The result of this verification [was] sent to the app and the coordinates [were] deleted without ever being stored in any database, neither in support nor in transit” (see report of the FMI under annex 9 to note prot. 0003022/2023 of January 11, 2023). 

Thus summarized the main characteristics of the Falco system, it is noted that the processing of personal data carried out by the Municipality, also with regard, more generally, to the management of the municipal video surveillance system, has resulted in the violation of certain provisions of the data protection legislation, which are detailed below.

3.2. The violation of the principles of accountability and data protection by design

The data controller, as the subject on whom the decisions regarding the purposes and methods of processing the personal data of the data subjects fall, bears a “general responsibility” for the processing carried out (recital 74 of the Regulation). Based on the principle of “accountability,” it is, in fact, responsible for compliance with the principles of data protection (Article 5, paragraph 1, of the Regulation) and must be able to demonstrate this (Article 5, paragraph 2, of the Regulation). This also includes implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure, and be able to demonstrate, that the processing is carried out in accordance with the Regulation (Article 24, paragraph 1, of the Regulation).

In particular, considering the risk that threatens the rights and freedoms of the data subjects, the data controller must - “by design” and “by default” (Article 25 of the Regulation) - adopt appropriate technical and organizational measures to implement the principles of data protection, integrating the necessary safeguards into the processing to meet the requirements of the Regulation and protect the rights and freedoms of the data subjects (see “Guidelines 4/2019 on Article 25 - Data Protection by Design and by Default,” adopted by the European Data Protection Board on October 20, 2020, especially points 42, 44, and 49).

In this specific case, as highlighted above, the “Falco” application was made available in the online stores “Google Play Store” on December 20, 2021, and “Apple Store” on January 26, 2022. 

From the documentation on file, it emerges that:
- the “Falco” application was made available “in beta version” by […] FMI, on behalf of the Municipality, although it “still needed to be tested by the company’s technicians” (note from the Municipality dated July 18, 2022, prot. no. 0083196/2022);
- already in December 2021, the DPO of the Municipality, newly appointed, during the first meetings with the representatives of the Entity, expressed “the urgency for the administration to prioritize the regularization of the video surveillance projects and the related application already active […] called ‘Falco’.” An external professional was also involved who “confirmed the assessments expressed by the [DPO] regarding the critical issues concerning the use of video surveillance systems and the related applications lacking adequate organizational structuring” (see the report of the DPO, under annex 5 to note prot. no. 0083196/2022);
- based on the observations made by the DPO and the external consultant, the Municipality, in April 2022, planned a series of actions to ensure compliance with the data protection legislation, including the “identification of the legal bases for the video surveillance processing and management of the related applications,” the “implementation of a risk assessment and a privacy impact assessment limited to the video surveillance systems,” the “adoption of a risk assessment and a privacy impact assessment for the Falco application in testing, with possible activation of the prior consultation mechanism under Article 36 [of the Regulation] in case of [impact assessment] with a high-risk outcome” and the “drafting of cascading acts, including […] [the] appointments under Article 28 [of the Regulation], etc.” (ibid.);
- only “once these processes are grounded, the legal basis well defined, and the impact assessment carried out” would the Municipality have “proceeded with the formalization of the appointments under Article 28 GDPR (that related to the management of Falco already in the Municipality’s possession in draft form) […] and the cascading acts including, very importantly, the instructions to be provided to the local police officers tasked with using the application” (ibid.);
- on April 15, 2022, a meeting was held between the Municipality and the F.M.I., during which the DPO discussed the “lawfulness profiles of the app and the state of the [video surveillance] systems in general, highlighting the first evident critical issues” (ibid.);
- on April 21, 2022, the DPO, in agreement with the external consultant, sent an email to the Municipality, “confirming the urgency of the situation” (ibid.);
- despite the DPO having invited the Municipality “to exercise caution suggesting to suspend the operation of the application until the conclusion of all processes, especially the drafting of a dedicated regulation and an ad hoc [impact assessment],” the Entity first proceeded to deactivate the application and then, on June 8, reactivated it, contravening the DPO’s indications (ibid.);
- on April 22, 2022, the DPO wrote to the Municipality’s contact person “an urgent communication,”
containing "a first draft of the privacy notice specifically drafted for the Falco app," highlighting "the necessity for it to be published on the institutional website in the relevant section and that it should be clearly specified that [it] referred to an active application in the experimental phase" (ibidem); on April 26, 2022, the DPO urged "once again and more emphatically the administration to exercise caution," suggesting "to suspend the app pending compliance with regulations and to downgrade it to an embryonic state, awaiting the definition of all processes" (ibidem); the DPO emphasized the need to "restrict the scope of application to only 'urban security' (excluding activities related to public safety), advising the removal of the categories 'bullying' and 'violence' from the types of reports that citizens could opt for" (ibidem); in April 2022, a "beta" version was made available, allowing citizens to "make a report specifying its type among the categories proposed by Falco (littering, vandalism and graffiti, road accidents, domestic violence, bullying, other reports)" and "make a call to 112 by pressing the corresponding button (112) with redirection to the user's mobile keyboard," although, at this stage, "the Local Police did not proceed with the processing and management of any reports, except on a sample basis, and only to support FMI in the testing phase of the application, pending explicit directives for the Operations Center regarding 'privacy' regulation and the instruction manual for the application" (ibidem); the DPO, together with the external consultant, suggested "to update the notices related to Falco again to align them with the choices made, also outlining an ideal operational timeline, including the creation of an ad hoc VDS regulation as a legal basis and the relevant regulations, the elimination of all personal data collected, and the realization of a specific DPIA" (ibidem); on April 27, 2022, the DPO, in agreement with the external consultant, sent a "new wording to be uploaded to the website, in the section dedicated to the Falco app, highlighting the downgrading and the experimental phase" and "summarized, once again, the steps to update the notice" for both the website and the application (ibidem); on April 29, 2022, the DPO sent "an updated version of the notice" (ibidem). On April 30, 2022, the external consultant of the Municipality "outlined the path to regularize the processes, with particular attention to the Falco application" (ibidem); on May 4, 2022, the DPO warned that even if "the appointment of an external responsible party or the adoption of regulations and procedures before the approval of the regulation (legal basis) would result in a sanction in any case, as the entire privacy by design aspect was completely lacking," reiterating the need to "follow the […] operational timeline" (ibidem); on July 6, 2022, the DPO sent a communication to the Municipality, reiterating "the suggestion to suspend the Falco App until the entire compliance process with the regulations was achieved – started, but […] deemed nonetheless not sufficiently robust," with particular regard to "the attachment of the legal basis and the risk and impact assessment" and "consider[ed] it more prudent that the entire [video surveillance] system and the architecture of the Falco project be regularized before allowing its use by citizens." At the same time, the DPO asked an engineer to conduct "a preliminary technical assessment of the app and the related privacy risk profiles necessary for the risk assessment and impact assessment." 3.4 Il consenso dell'interessato
In base al principio di “liceità, correttezza e trasparenza”, il trattamento dei dati personali deve essere basato sul consenso dell'interessato, salvo che il trattamento non si basi su un'altra base giuridica (art. 6, par. 1, lett. a), del Regolamento). Il consenso deve essere fornito in modo libero, specifico, informato e inequivocabile.

Nel caso in esame, il Comune non ha ottenuto il consenso degli utenti per il trattamento dei loro dati personali attraverso l’applicazione “Falco”. Nonostante l’applicazione consentisse agli utenti di effettuare segnalazioni, non è stato fornito alcun meccanismo chiaro e trasparente per la raccolta del consenso, né sono state fornite informazioni adeguate riguardo alle modalità di trattamento dei dati.

Pertanto, si conclude che il Comune ha violato il principio di liceità del trattamento, in quanto non ha ottenuto il consenso degli interessati, come richiesto dall’art. 6 del Regolamento.

3.5 La sicurezza del trattamento
Il Regolamento richiede che il titolare del trattamento adotti misure tecniche e organizzative adeguate per garantire un livello di sicurezza adeguato al rischio (art. 32 del Regolamento). Tali misure devono garantire la riservatezza, l'integrità e la disponibilità dei dati personali.

Nel caso in esame, il Comune non ha implementato misure di sicurezza adeguate per proteggere i dati personali trattati attraverso l’applicazione “Falco”. Nonostante le segnalazioni ricevute riguardo alle criticità relative alla protezione dei dati, il Comune ha continuato a gestire i dati senza adottare le necessarie misure di sicurezza, esponendo così i dati a potenziali rischi di violazione.

In conclusione, il Comune ha agito in violazione degli obblighi di sicurezza previsti dal Regolamento, non garantendo un livello di protezione adeguato ai dati personali trattati.

**3.4 The failure to define the relationships with the data processor**  
Pursuant to Article 28, par. 3, of the Regulation, "the processing by a processor shall be governed by a contract or other legal act under Union or Member State law, which binds the processor to the controller, which stipulates the subject matter and duration of the processing, the nature and purpose of the processing, the type of personal data and categories of data subjects, the obligations and rights of the controller," and which provides for all the commitments required by the same Article 28, par. 3, of the Regulation (see recital 81 of the Regulation). The contract or other legal act must be "in writing, including in electronic form" (Article 28, par. 9, of the Regulation). As clarified by the European Data Protection Board, "since the regulation clearly establishes the obligation to enter into a written contract, if no other relevant legal act is in force, there is a violation of the [Regulation], or of 'Article 28, paragraph 9, of the [Regulation].' Considering that 'both the controller and the processor are responsible for ensuring the existence of a contract or another legal act governing the processing,' the competent supervisory authority 'may impose an administrative fine on both the controller and the processor' ('Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of controller and processor under the GDPR,' adopted by the European Data Protection Board on July 7, 2021, par. 103). Therefore, where, as in this case, there is "a controller-processor relationship [...] even in the absence of a [valid] written processing agreement" - since the entity processing the data is actually processing it not for its own purposes but on behalf of the contracting entity, within the scope of an activity outsourced by it and in the execution of a service contract or other similar legal relationship in existence between the parties (see the definition of "processor" in Article 4, par. 1, n. 8, of the Regulation) - "this implies [...] a violation of Article 28, paragraph 3, of the [Regulation]" (ibid). That said, it is highlighted that, as emerged during the investigation, the Municipality has over time entrusted FMI with the execution of a series of services, namely:  
- by Municipal Council Resolution No. 333/2017, the maintenance and support activities for the management of automatic video surveillance systems for violations of the Highway Code for access to the ZTL and those for the detection of goods movement;  
- by Municipal Council Resolution No. 111 of December 19, 2018, and subsequent contract No. 91 of June 6, 2019, for the management of the Municipality's video surveillance system and related urban surveillance. In this contract, FMI was identified as the "data controller for its areas of competence";  
- by Municipal Council Resolution No. 200 of June 23, 2021, the maintenance of the systems and technical equipment of the systems of
**Video Surveillance to be Implemented as Part of the Project “Città Protetta 2020 – 2nd and 3rd Phase,” which also Includes the Application “Falco.”**

Although the FMI, in the context of providing a plurality of services for the Municipality, has processed and continues to process personal data on behalf of and in the interest of the same, thus acting as a “data processor” (art. 4, par. 1, n. of the Regulation), the Municipality has failed to enter into a contract with it regarding the protection of personal data, as required by art. 28 of the Regulation. 

In this regard, it is noted that, on April 26, 2022, FMI had requested the Municipality to “be classified as an external processor pursuant to art. 28 [of the Regulation], with the consequent need to sign the appropriate appointments” (see the report of the DPO, annex 5 to note prot. n. 0083196/2022). On April 28, 2022, the DPO of the Municipality and its external consultant further highlighted the necessity that “FMI [be] classified as an external data processor” (ibidem). Furthermore, on May 4, 2022, the DPO sent “a communication to the FMI contact person, in response to their request for the appointments pursuant to art. 28, highlighting that only following the adoption of the [video surveillance] regulation […] and all consequent acts, particularly disciplinary and DPIA, [the Municipality would] be able to proceed with the appointments.” Nevertheless, as stated by the Municipality, the formalization of the agreement occurred only “in August 2022.” 

The Municipality, as the data controller, therefore failed, until August 2022, to enter into a data protection agreement with FMI, which - as a wholly publicly-owned company, entrusted with multiple services by the Municipality - was effectively acting as a data processor, in violation of art. 28, par. 3, of the Regulation.

**3.5 The Omitted Data Protection Impact Assessment**

In cases of high risks for the data subjects - arising, for example, from the use of new technologies - the data controller must carry out a data protection impact assessment in order to adopt, in particular, appropriate measures to address such risks, consulting the Supervisory Authority in advance, where the conditions apply (see arts. 35 and 36 of the Regulation). 

In this case, the use of the application “Falco” involved the collection of data on the geographical location of users, as well as implied the possibility that, even in the case of unauthorized or unlawful access, third parties could become aware of the reports made, with possible repercussions for the data subjects, including retaliatory consequences. As illustrated above, it could not be excluded that the collection of personal data relating to crimes, which by their nature are particularly sensitive, could occur. 

Therefore, although the processing resulting from the use of this application, made potentially available to all citizens, presented a high risk to the rights and freedoms of individuals, the Municipality did not draft a data protection impact assessment before commencing the processing in question (see the “Guidelines on Data Protection Impact Assessment and Determining Whether Processing is ‘Likely to Result in a High Risk’ for the Purposes of Regulation (EU) 2016/679,” adopted by the Article 29 Working Party on April 4, 2017, WP 248 rev.01, endorsed by the European Data Protection Board with “Endorsement 1/2018” of May 25, 2018, with particular regard to the criteria for data of a highly personal nature, such as those relating to location or whose processing may have serious repercussions on the daily life of the data subject, as well as the processing of data on a large scale). 

The Municipality has, therefore, acted in violation of art. 35, par. 1, of the Regulation.

**3.6 The Security of Processing**

Art. 5, par. 1, lett. f), of the Regulation establishes that personal data must be “processed in a manner that ensures an adequate level of security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage” (principle of “integrity and confidentiality”). 

In application of this principle, art. 32 of the Regulation, concerning the security of processing, provides that “taking into account the state of the art and the costs of implementation, as well as the nature, scope, context and purposes of the processing, and the risk of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller and the data processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk […]” (par. 1) and that “in assessing the appropriate level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing which arise, in particular, from accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to personal data transmitted, stored or otherwise processed” (par. 2) (see rec. 83 of the Regulation). 

As a result of the investigative activity, it emerged that the local police operators of the Municipality, “in the absence of personal credentials for consulting the reports made by users, lacking a specific service provision from the Deputy Commander […]”, accessed the data collected through the Falco application “using the
**Generic password provided by FMI**, not having, therefore, specific and personal authentication credentials to access the computer system (see note from the Local Police of the Municipality dated July 13, 2022, in the records). 

Considering the nature, object, context, and purposes of the processing, which involved the acquisition and management of reports made by users, with the collection of personal data related to the reporter, such as the phone number and the device's location, it is believed that the aforementioned methods of access to the computer system cannot be considered adequate from a security perspective (cf. provision of June 10, 2021, no. 236, web document no. 9685947). 

The use of non-nominal accounts by multiple subjects prevents attributing actions performed in a computer system to a specific subject, also to the detriment of the data controller and the data processor, who are effectively deprived of the ability to control the actions of subjects operating under their authority. Furthermore, when a non-nominal account, such as the one in question, is used by multiple subjects, situations may arise where there is inconsistency between the authorization profiles assigned and the actual operational needs for managing the systems, thus allowing an unauthorized subject to operate, in the absence of a specific intention from the data controller or the data processor, within the systems and processing services (see provision of April 4, 2019, no. 83, web document no. 9101974; January 14, 2021, no. 4, web document no. 9582744). 

Moreover, the use of "authentication credentials exclusively used by subjects operating under its authority or that of the data processor" in the previous regulatory regime was expressly provided as a minimum security measure that all data controllers were required to adopt (pursuant to the technical regulations referred to in Annex B to the Code, in the text prior to the amendments made by Legislative Decree no. 101/2018), the violation of which also entailed the application of a criminal sanction (cf. art. 169 of the Code, in the text prior to the amendments made by Legislative Decree no. 101/2018). 

For these reasons, in light of the methods of access to the computer system for managing reports, with the characteristics described above, the Municipality acted in a manner not compliant with the principle of "integrity and confidentiality" and in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter f), and 32 of the Regulation.

**5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code).** 

The Guarantor, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to "impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to the [other] [corrective] measures referred to in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case," and, in this context, "the Board [of the Guarantor] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Guarantor's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code" (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Regulation of the Guarantor no. 1/2019). 

In this case, the Municipality has engaged in two distinct conducts, which must be considered separately for the purposes of quantifying the administrative sanction to be applied. 

**5.1 Violations related to the processing of personal data through video devices.** 

Considering that the violations of Articles 5, paragraph 2 (in conjunction with Article 24), 25, and 28, paragraph 3, of the Regulation, in relation to the processing carried out through video devices, occurred as a result of a single conduct (same processing or interrelated treatments), Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation applies, according to which the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation. Considering that, in this case, the most serious violation concerns Article 5, paragraph 2, of the Regulation (in conjunction with Article 24), subject to the administrative sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation, the total amount of the sanction is to be quantified up to €20,000,000.
The aforementioned administrative monetary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into due account the elements provided for by Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation. 

Considering that:
the violation concerns the processing carried out, over an extended period, through video devices (surveillance cameras and control of access areas to ZTL), which may involve a very large number of citizens and other interested parties transiting in the municipal territory; 
the investigation did not, however, reveal that they suffered specific damages as a result of such processing (cf. Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a), of the Regulation); 
although the Municipality had not entered into a data protection agreement with the FMI, the latter is a single-member limited liability company with complete public participation, whose ownership is nonetheless traceable to the Municipalities belonging to the Union of Municipalities of Romagna Forlivese, including the Municipality of Forlì; moreover, the parties had nonetheless signed a service contract, having at least generally defined the subject matter governed and the duration of the processing, the nature and purpose of the processing, the type of personal data, and the categories of interested parties (cf. Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a), of the Regulation); 
the violation is of a negligent nature (cf. Article 83, paragraph 2, letter b), of the Regulation); 
the processing did not concern personal data belonging to special categories (see Article 9 of the Regulation), it must, however, be considered that the surveillance systems, installed on public roads for the protection of urban security, may involve the processing of personal data related to crimes referred to in Article 10 of the Regulation (cf. Article 83, paragraph 2, letter g), of the Regulation), 
it is believed that, in this case, the level of severity of the violation committed by the data controller is medium (cf. European Data Protection Board, “Guidelines 04/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines under the GDPR” of May 23, 2023, point 60). 

That said, it is believed that, for the purposes of quantifying the sanction, the following mitigating circumstances should be taken into account:
the Municipality has a high degree of responsibility, having substantially failed to consider the data protection profiles underlying the processing in question, before carrying it out and from the design of the systems used (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter d), of the Regulation); 
the Municipality has offered good cooperation with the Authority during the investigation, having also promptly activated to remedy the violations, even resorting to the support of the DPO and expert consultants in data protection matters (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter f), of the Regulation); 
there are no previous relevant violations committed by the Municipality (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter e), of the Regulation). 

By reason of the aforementioned elements, assessed in their entirety, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the monetary sanction at the sum of €10,000 (ten thousand) for the violation of Articles 5, paragraph 2 (in conjunction with Article 24), 25, and 28, paragraph 3 of the Regulation, as an administrative monetary sanction deemed, pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. 

Considering that the surveillance activity in question has involved public places, constituting a processing of personal data that “allows [to detect] the presence and behavior of people in the considered space” (European Data Protection Board, “Guidelines 3/2019 on the processing of personal data through video devices” of January 29, 2020, paragraph 2.1), without ensuring, from the design of the surveillance system, the overall respect of the principles of data protection, it is also believed that the accessory sanction of publication on the website of the Authority of this provision, provided for by Article 166, paragraph 7 of the Code and Article 16 of the Regulation of the Authority No. 1/2019, should be applied. 

It is finally noted that the conditions referred to in Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 are met. 

5.2 Violations related to the “Falco” system. 
Considering that the violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and f), and paragraph 2 (in conjunction with Article 24), 12, paragraph 1, 13, 25, 28, paragraph 3, 32, and 35, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, in relation to the processing carried out through the so-called “Falco” system, occurred as a result of a single conduct (same processing or related treatments), Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation applies, according to which the total amount of the administrative monetary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation. 

Considering that, in this case, the most serious violations concern Articles 5, 12, and 13, subject to the administrative sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation, the total amount of the sanction is to be quantified up to €20,000,000. 

The aforementioned administrative monetary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into due account the elements provided for by Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation. 

Considering that:
the processing involved a limited number of interested parties compared to the total number of residents in the Municipality (approximately 116,726), given that, as stated by the Municipality during the investigation, the reports received since the first activation on a trial basis (April 2022), including numerous test evidence and...
