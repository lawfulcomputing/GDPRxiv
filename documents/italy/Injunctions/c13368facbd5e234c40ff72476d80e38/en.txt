1. **Premise**  
With a report submitted to the Authority pursuant to Article 144 of the Code, a citizen of the Municipality of Modica (hereinafter, the “Municipality”) complained that he had received several violation notices for the administrative violation of a mayoral ordinance for having incorrectly disposed of unsorted waste, as would be evident from viewing the footage recorded by video devices placed near the bins designated for waste collection. Based on the received notices, the complainant argued that the checks for the violation, through viewing the footage, occurred more than a month after the date on which the footage was recorded.  
The complainant also lamented the absence of suitable information regarding the processing of personal data, as an informational sign was placed directly on the bin, which was not easily visible and lacked the indication of the data controller and the purposes pursued.

3. Outcome of the investigative activity
3.1 Video surveillance in public areas
The processing of personal data through video
surveillance systems by public entities is

generally permitted if it is necessary to fulfill a
legal obligation to which the data controller is subject or for the execution of a task of
public interest or related to the exercise of
public powers conferred upon it (Article 6,
paragraphs 1, letters c) and e), and 3, of the Regulation, as well as
2-ter of the Code; see paragraph 41 of the “Guidelines
3/2019 on the processing of personal data through
video devices,” adopted by the European Data Protection Committee on
January 29, 2020). In this context, it is noted that waste management
falls within the institutional activities entrusted to
local authorities.
The data controller is, in any case, required to
comply with the principles regarding data protection
set forth in Article 5 of the Regulation, among which
the principle of “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency” (Article
5, paragraph 1, letter a), of the Regulation), under which the data controller must adopt
appropriate measures to provide the data subject with all
the information referred to in Articles 13 and 14 of
the Regulation in a concise, transparent,
intelligible, and easily accessible form, using
simple and clear language (see Article 12 of
the Regulation).

**3.2 Information on the Processing of Personal Data**  
When surveillance systems are employed, the data controller, in addition to providing first-level information through the placement of warning signage in proximity to the area under surveillance, must also provide the data subjects with "second-level information," which must "contain all the mandatory elements in accordance with Article 13 of the [Regulation]" and "be easily accessible to the data subject" ("Guidelines 3/2019 on the processing of personal data through video devices," cited, in particular, para. 7; but see also the "Provision regarding video surveillance" of the Garante dated April 8, 2010, web doc. no. 1712680, in particular para. 3.1; cf. FAQ no. 4 of the Garante regarding video surveillance, cited).  
First-level information (warning sign) "should communicate the most important data, such as the purposes of the processing, the identity of the data controller, and the existence of the rights of the data subject, along with information on the most significant impacts of the processing" ("Guidelines 3/2019 on the processing of personal data through video devices," cited, para. 114). Furthermore, the signage must also contain information that may be unexpected for the data subject. This could include, for example, the transmission of data to third parties, particularly if located outside the European Union, and the retention period. If such information is not indicated, the data subject should be able to rely on the fact that there is only real-time surveillance (without any data recording or transmission to third parties) (ibidem, cited, para. 115). The first-level warning signage must also contain a clear reference to the second level of information, for example, by indicating a website where the text of the extended information can be consulted.  
Moreover, first-level information should be positioned in such a way as to allow the data subject to easily recognize the circumstances of the surveillance before entering the monitored area. It is not necessary to reveal the location of the camera, as long as there are no doubts about which areas are subject to surveillance and the context of the surveillance is clearly stated (ibidem, cited, para. 113; cf. also provisions dated October 20, 2022, no. 341, web doc. no. 9831369; April 28, 2022, no. 162, web doc. no. 9777974; April 7, 2022, no. 119, web doc. no. 9773950; September 16, 2021, no. 327, web doc. no. 9705650; and March 11, 2021, no. 90, web doc. no. 9582791).  
In this case, the Municipality, during the period in which the assessment reports subject to notification were issued, used an information sign (cf. annex 6 note of XX) that is not fully compliant with data protection regulations. It mentions generic "security reasons" underlying the video surveillance but does not indicate how the data subjects (i.e., not only those to whom an administrative violation is contested but all individuals who enter the range of the cameras) can receive complete second-level information on the processing, nor does it emerge from the documents that the Municipality, during that period, prepared such complete information and made it known to the data subjects, for example, by publishing it on its institutional website. In this regard, the Municipality has indeed stated that the complete information on the processing of personal data in the context of video surveillance was published on its institutional website only on XX.  
For completeness, it should be noted that from the investigations carried out by the Office (cf. service report of XX), it also emerged that on the Municipality's institutional website, there was an information notice related to the "XII-ecology" sector, which stated, regarding the legal basis for the processing, that "the processing of personal data carried out through the use of video surveillance systems aims to pursue a legitimate interest of the Controller aimed at ensuring public safety and the public interest, as the processing is necessary for the execution of a task of public interest or related to the exercise of public powers vested in the data controller. […] In this case, the processing is carried out for the protection of public hygiene and public safety"; "the […] processing [of images] responds to the need to pursue the interest of protecting persons and property against unlawful acts (assaults, thefts, and robberies) and to collect evidence."  
Thus, this information was also inadequate, as it did not refer to the purpose pursued in the specific case, which is the responsibility of the Municipality, regarding the assessment of conduct sanctioned administratively (cf. Articles 192, "prohibition of abandonment," and 255 "abandonment of waste," of Legislative Decree April 3, 2006, no. 152; Article 13, Law November 24, 1981, no. 689) and which must be distinguished from the different areas of...
"public safety" and "public security."
It should also be noted that the simplified sign used by the Municipality was affixed directly to the waste bin, even in proximity to other signs, thus generating confusion and poor visibility of the same, and therefore not allowing the interested parties to be aware of the presence of the video surveillance system before entering its field of view. In this regard, it is observed that the Municipality's assertion regarding the circumstance that "the informational signs that were displayed at the locations of the established violations were not only those applied to the waste collection bins [...] but also those placed independently on posts adjacent to the areas concerned" (note of XX) is not supported by the documentation on file, as the Municipality has not produced any evidence to support this statement. 

Furthermore, it is recorded that for the period from November to July 2019, the retention time of the images was increased from seven to fifteen days, although the interested parties were not informed of this circumstance. In this regard, it was stated (see note of XX) that "the methods of retention and the purposes [...] indicated did not provide clarity either on the temporary extension granted or on the new purposes underlying the same extension."

Lastly, regarding the sign used by the Municipality starting from the month of XX (see annex 7 note of XX), the existence of an automatic data deletion mechanism is also declared, which, based on the documentation on file, does not appear to have been effectively adopted by the Municipality. Moreover, concerning the indication of the methods by which the interested parties can obtain complete information on the processing of personal data, only the possibility of physically going to an "information desk" at the Municipality's headquarters is mentioned, thereby effectively hindering their ability to easily acquire complete information on the processing of the personal data in question.

For these reasons, the processing of personal data through video devices has been carried out by the Municipality in a manner not compliant with the principle of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency" as per Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) and in violation of the obligations set forth in Articles 12 and 13 of the Regulation (whereas, instead, the contested violation of Article 14 should be dismissed, considering that the personal data processed through such video systems were not acquired from third parties).

3.3 The principles of data minimization, limitation of retention, accountability, and data protection by design and by default
Pursuant to Article 5, paragraph 1, letters c) and e) of the Regulation, personal data must be "adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed" ("data minimization") and "kept in a form which permits identification of the data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed" ("limitation of retention"). 

Based on the principle of accountability (Articles 5, paragraph 2, and 24 of the Regulation), it is the responsibility of the data controller to determine the retention periods for the images, taking into account the context and purposes of the processing, as well as the risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons; unless specific legal provisions expressly provide for certain data retention periods (see FAQ no. 5 of the Authority regarding video surveillance, cited). In most cases, "taking into account the principles of Article 5, paragraph 1, letters c) and e) of the GDPR, namely data minimization and limitation of their retention, personal data should be [...] deleted after a few days, preferably through automatic mechanisms. The longer the retention period is (especially if it exceeds 72 hours), the more substantiated the analysis regarding the legitimacy of the purpose and the necessity of retention must be" (paragraph 8, point 121, of the Guidelines of the European Data Protection Board, cited; see also FAQ no. 5 of the Authority, cited).

In accordance with the principle of data protection by default (pursuant to Article 25, paragraph 2, of the Regulation), the data controller must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that the images captured by video devices are processed, by default, only for the retention period previously defined by them.

That said, it is first observed that, from what has been stated by the Municipality, it has emerged that the video surveillance system in question was not installed solely for the purpose of preventing and detecting so-called administrative offenses by citizens, but also for the purposes of so-called urban security, that is, for reasons of prevention and deterrence of offenses (vandalism, theft, etc.).

Following some acts of vandalism, the Municipality, during the period from March to November 2019, increased the retention period of the images captured by the video surveillance system from seven to fifteen days (see note of XX), in violation of Article 6, paragraph 8, of Decree Law No. 11 of February 23, 2009, according to which "the retention of data, information, and images collected through the use of video surveillance systems is limited to seven days following detection, except for special needs for further retention."

In this regard, it should be noted that the reference to
**Part of the Municipality pursuant to Article 6, paragraph 8, of Decree-Law No. 11 of February 23, 2009, for the purpose of defining the retention periods of footage acquired through video devices, is not entirely coherent, given that, in the context in question, the Municipality was not pursuing objectives related to so-called urban security, which is governed by a specific regulatory framework (see Article 5, paragraph 2, letter a), of Decree-Law No. 14 of February 20, 2017, according to which municipalities may install surveillance systems to pursue objectives of "prevention and contrast of widespread and predatory crime," subject to the stipulation of a pact for the implementation of urban security with the territorially competent Prefecture). Moreover, the occurrence of "criminal acts [including] a serious intimidating act," consisting of the arson of a vehicle belonging to the "Ecology and Environment Sector" of the Municipality, cannot in itself give rise to a special need to extend the seven-day term provided by Decree-Law No. 11 of February 23, 2009, given that in relation to any consequences arising from conduct of the same nature, as they are immediately or promptly ascertainable, the seven-day term provided by law is certainly adequate for the purpose of ascertaining any responsibilities and acquiring the necessary images for the purposes of investigation by the competent investigative bodies.**

**Considering that the aforementioned seven-day term, defined by Decree-Law No. 11 of February 23, 2009, is not applicable in the specific context, the Municipality, in compliance with the principles of accountability, limitation of retention, and data protection by design and by default, should have defined the maximum retention period of the images collected through video devices for the purpose of ascertaining administrative violations in environmental matters, adequately justifying the choices made.**

**Moreover, from the complaint, it emerges that the administrative violations were nonetheless ascertained more than a month after the date on which the images were recorded, thus the Municipality has nonetheless retained said images beyond the aforementioned seven-day term.**

**In light of the above, the violation of the principles of "minimization," "limitation of retention" (Article 5, paragraph 1, letters c) and e)), "accountability" (Article 5, paragraph 2, of the Regulation, in conjunction with Article 24 of the Regulation), and data protection by design and by default (Article 25 of the Regulation) has been established.**

**3.4 Relationships with Data Processors**

**Pursuant to Article 28, paragraph 3, of the Regulation, "processing by a processor shall be governed by a contract or other legal act under Union law or the law of the Member States, which binds the processor to the controller, which stipulates the subject matter and duration of the processing, the nature and purpose of the processing, the type of personal data and categories of data subjects, the obligations and rights of the controller," and which provides for all the commitments required by the same Article 28, paragraph 3, of the Regulation (see Recital No. 81 of the Regulation; Provision No. 81 of March 7, 2019, web doc 9121890; Provision No. 160 of September 17, 2020, web doc 9461168; Provision No. 294 of July 22, 2021, web doc 9698724; Provision No. 351 of September 29, 2021, web doc 9716256; Provision No. 270 of July 21, 2022, web doc 9811732).**

**That said, it is noted that, following the investigation, it emerged that the Municipality entrusted the task to the company CAT S.r.l. of Ragusa (starting from XX) and, subsequently, to the individual enterprise Ermeslink of Giovanni Di Stefano (effective from XX), concerning specific operations of personal data processing, such as the collection and analysis of footage related to environmental violations, both administrative and criminal, appointing the owners of the aforementioned companies as judicial police auxiliaries for this purpose.**

**In this specific case, the company CAT Srl of Ragusa participated in the processing in question from August 8, 2017, until December 2019, without its role being defined for the purposes of data protection legislation. In relation to the company Ermeslink, however, which has been entrusted with the service in question since XX, a late act of appointment as data processor pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation intervened (determination No. XX of XX).**

**Nor does it matter that the aforementioned companies were assigned functions of "judicial police," considering that the acts adopted for this purpose by the Municipality cannot be considered "agreements" on data protection entered into between the controller and the processor, lacking the elements that are unconditionally required by Article 28 of the Regulation.**

**In light of the preceding considerations, it is noted that the aforementioned companies participated in the processing of the data in question without their role being correctly defined by the Municipality prior to the commencement of processing through the stipulation of a data protection agreement, resulting in a violation by the Municipality of Article 28 of the Regulation.**
