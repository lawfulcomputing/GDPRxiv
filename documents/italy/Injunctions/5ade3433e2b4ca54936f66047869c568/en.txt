**VIEWED** the note of December 28, 2021 (prot. 64041) with which the Office requested RCS Mediagroup S.p.a. to provide its observations regarding what was represented in the complaint;

**VIEWED** the note of January 17, 2022 (followed by several supplementary certified emails of the same date) with which the Company provided a response contesting:

- that it had received the complainant's request of April 28, 2021 (sent to the responsible director) and had not responded because, in addition to deeming it unfounded, it arrived the day before the distribution of the magazine; it then responded to the subsequent request of the complainant on May 6 the following day, stating that it could not provide the data of the author of the shots due to the provisions that protect professional secrecy;
- the unfounded nature of the complaints regarding the unlawful acquisition of the images, the realization of which occurred without the use of any invasive instrument, as they were taken by the photographer from inside his car parked on the street "who used a 70-200 zoom, which is a completely normal lens, found in any store"; thus, not an ultra-invasive telephoto lens or even a "drone," as claimed by the complainant, which would have produced clearer images than those used "in which the subjects depicted would not have been independently recognizable, without the commentary that accompanied them"; the subjects portrayed were perfectly visible from the street and therefore reproducible even by a mobile phone, as they were positioned in front of the illuminated window not "screened" by any curtain;
- the unfounded nature of the complaints regarding the manner of proposing the service, with reference to the time gap between the taking of the photographs and the publication of the news (which, according to the complainant, was not correctly highlighted by the Publisher); on the contrary, the timing choice "is not at all random and further confirms the correctness of the editor of the publication who, having received them at the time of their realization, did not publish them, not having had precise data from the photographer on the identity of the man, not recognizable from the images in his possession, who was in the company of the complainant, whose identity, on the other hand, was certain, as the photos were taken through the unshielded windows of her residence"; the publication therefore occurred only when the editor of the publication had confirming elements of the facts, thanks to information provided by other news outlets, "indicating the date of realization of the photos, also to highlight that it was a lasting and ongoing relationship";
- the irrelevance of the lack of consent of the interested party to the publication of the photographs, "given their relevance, which made them essential for complete information; and given the frequency with which news and images concerning the affectionate relationships of the complainant occupy specialized mass media"; she, on the other hand, "has never hidden her romantic life, nor has she ever protested against the dissemination of articles and images that have dealt with it," and this is also documented by a wide and recent press review (attached to the Company's note) that "unequivocally illustrates the presence of numerous boyfriends in the life of the charming presenter, confirming that the circulation of news and images related to her private life has never been a problem for her, except - it is inexplicable why - in the case at hand, which allows for the presumption of implicit and repeated consent";

**VIEWED** the note of February 2, 2022, with which the complainant replied to the observations of RCS Mediagroup S.p.a. highlighting:

- the inadequacy of the arguments regarding the timing and content of the responses to the requests of the complainant of April 28 and May 6;

- the pretextual nature of the statements regarding the timing of publication, given that the complainant did not assert that there was no indication.
the date of the snapshot (December 2020) in the article, but rather that it did not appear at the time of the advertising launch, thus leading the public - prima facie - to believe that there was a betrayal on the part of the complainant regarding the relationship that was actually ongoing at the time of the publication of the service (April 2021); the fact that several months were waited before publishing the photos, in other words, far from being motivated by the cautionary reasons expressed by the Publisher, was functional to stimulate the curiosity of the public sensitive to this type of news and therefore to achieve maximum sales results for the magazine;  
- the irrelevance of the reference to other publications depicting the complainant, even related to her romantic life, given that these are images often taken in public places; the extensive press review provided by the Publisher, on the other hand, confirms how the complainant "has never complained about the presence of photographers and photographs depicting her life and that of her companions" as long as they were lawfully captured; instead, in this specific case "for the first time in many years, she felt violated in her private sphere and in her privacy, an unprecedented fact, despite the dozens of publications she has been subject to"; a circumstance that does not allow the treatment to be traced back to the exercise of the right to report and the related provisions of the Code that allow for disregarding the consent of the interested party for the purposes of the treatment itself;

CONSIDERING that, unless the act constitutes a more serious crime, anyone who, in a proceeding before the Guarantor, falsely declares or certifies news or circumstances or produces false acts or documents is liable under Article 168 of the Code "Falsehood in declarations to the Guarantor and interruption of the execution of the tasks or the exercise of the powers of the Guarantor";  
CLARIFIED that the Guarantor is called to assess whether, in the specific case, there has been a processing of personal data not in compliance with the current regulations in the matter, not being able to pronounce on the possible existence of the violation referred to in Article 615 bis of the Penal Code - a hypothesis also proposed in the report - as this is a matter for the judicial authority;  
CONSIDERING, in particular, that the publication subject to complaint must be evaluated in light of the general principles of processing (Article 5 of the Regulation) and the specific provisions governing journalistic activity, pursuant to Articles 136 - 139 of the Code and the Ethical Rules referred to in Article 139 of the same Code;  
NOTED, from the evidentiary elements that emerged, that the images subject to reporting:  
- were acquired (and stored) with a remote device;  
- based on what was stated by the owner, the nature of the images - due to the viewpoint offered by the position of the shot - alone did not allow for the identification of the subjects depicted, which was instead "integrated" into the processing and revealed by the owner at the time of the publication of the journalistic service, which took place four months after the shot;  
- depict, unbeknownst to the reporter, moments of private life that took place in certain areas of her apartment located at a height (fourth floor) suitable, as such, to suggest a certain reserve with respect to the gaze of those passing by on the street;  
CONSIDERED, due to the characteristics described, that the photographic service reveals an incorrect use of "invasive techniques" (Article 3 of the Ethical Rules) - which can be implemented with more or less common and more or less sophisticated devices - and, therefore, a collection of personal data, even strictly private, in violation of the general principles of fairness and transparency (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation) that also operate with reference to processing carried out for journalistic purposes;  
CONSIDERED on the other hand, that the prerequisites for derogating from such principles for a possible impossibility of pursuing the informative function otherwise (Article 2 of the Ethical Rules) do not exist in the specific case, also considering what has been highlighted by the owner in his submissions, namely that, very often, it is the reporter herself who makes known the facts related to her private and sentimental life through behaviors or public statements;  
CONSIDERED in this regard that the availability shown by the reporter to submit to "media scrutiny" does not, in itself, as instead claimed by RCS Mediagroup S.p.a., legitimize any form of collection and use of data and images concerning her, as the existence of the prerequisites that make the processing lawful must instead be evaluated on a case-by-case basis (a circumstance already represented to the owner in relation to a similar case: provision of December 22, 2009, web doc no. 1686747, see also for the same case, Civil Cassation, Section I, June 16, 2021, no. 17217 and Civil Cassation, Section I, January 23, 2019, no. 1875);  
NOTED that the photographs refer to events occurring several months prior to the time period of the publication of the journalistic service - a circumstance that is not immediately perceivable in light of how the launch and subsequent packaging of the service itself were carried out - during which, moreover, the personal and emotional situation of the complainant - based on what was made known by her through public statements and behaviors - did not correspond with the facts proposed in the service subject to reporting;  
CONSIDERED, from the perspective of their dissemination, that the declared purpose of the owner - to corroborate the hypothesis of a romantic relationship between the reporter and the man depicted in the photographs (still) in effect at the time of the publication of the service - as much as it may fall within a type of journalistic information of public interest (at least with reference to a certain audience), cannot justify the compression of the right to respect for the private life of the interested party, even if a public figure, also in light of the aforementioned temporal gap between the facts and the publication of the service and therefore the interference of such editorial choice in the reporter's relational life;  
CONSIDERED that - without prejudice to the above observations regarding the methods of collection - the subsequent use of the images constitutes a processing of personal data, of a nature also strictly private, exceeding and such as to determine a sacrifice of the private sphere of the interested party that is disproportionate and unjustified with respect to the journalistic purpose pursued (to the benefit of the "lovers of the subject," to quote the words of the owner himself), thus also in contrast with the principles of data minimization (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter c) of the Regulation and of "essentiality of information" referred to in Article 137, paragraph 3 of the Code and Article 6 of the Ethical Rules;
CONSIDERING that the processing subject to the report, in terms of data collection and dissemination, constitutes a violation of the general principles of lawfulness, fairness, and transparency as well as data minimization of personal data as per Article 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and c) of the Regulation, as further specified—with reference to processing for journalistic purposes—by Article 137, paragraph 3 of the Code and by the Ethical Rules (Articles 2, 3, and 6, paragraph 2), compliance with which constitutes "an essential condition for the lawfulness and fairness of the processing" (Article 2 quater of the Code);

CONSIDERING therefore, pursuant to Articles 57, paragraph 1, letter f) of the Regulation, due to the violations found, that it is necessary to order, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter f), against RCS Mediagroup S.p.a. the prohibition of further dissemination—even online, including the historical archive—except for mere preservation for potential use in judicial proceedings;

NOTING that, in the event of non-compliance with the provisions of the Guarantor contained in this measure, the criminal sanction referred to in Article 170 of the Code may apply, in addition to the administrative sanction referred to in Article 83, paragraph 5, letter e), of the Regulation;

CONSIDERING, regarding the established violation, that it is necessary to adopt an injunction order pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7 of the Code and Article 18 of Law No. 689/1981 for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5 of the Regulation;

NOTING that, for the determination of the amount of the pecuniary sanction, it is necessary to take into account the elements indicated in Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation and that, in this specific case, the following aggravating circumstances must be considered:
a) the severity of the violation (Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation), taking into account the manner of acquisition of personal data and the nature of the data collected, relating to moments of strictly private life occurring at the domicile of the interested party;
b) the duration of the violation, given the ongoing availability of the images subject to complaint and the failure to adopt measures aimed at limiting the effects of the processing, along with the partial response to the requests made by the reporting party at the time of the first publication of the images;
c) the existence of previous violations related to the provisions concerning journalistic activity, committed by the data controller since the entry into force of the Regulation (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter c);
d) the conditions of significance on the organizational, economic, and professional level of the offender (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter k), of the Regulation) taking into account what emerged in the financial statements for the year 2021;
