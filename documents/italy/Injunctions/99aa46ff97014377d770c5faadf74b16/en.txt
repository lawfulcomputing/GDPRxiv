**3. Outcome of the investigative activity**  
Taking into account what was represented and documented by the Company during the investigative proceedings, first with the acts of notification of violation and subsequently with the related defensive memos, it is observed that:  
- **For "personal data," it is understood as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('data subject')" and for "data related to health," "personal data concerning the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care, which reveals information related to their health status" (art. 4, par. 1, nos. 1 and 15 of the Regulation). These latter data deserve greater protection since the context of their processing could create significant risks for fundamental rights and freedoms (Recital no. 51 of the Regulation);**

- **Information about health status can only be communicated to the data subject and can be communicated to third parties only on the basis of an appropriate legal basis (art. 9 of the Regulation and art. 84 of the Code in conjunction with art. 22, paragraph 11, Legislative Decree of August 10, 2018, no. 101);**

- **The data controller is, in any case, required to respect the principles regarding data protection, among which is the principle of "integrity and confidentiality," according to which personal data must be "processed in a manner that ensures adequate security (…), including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage" (art. 5, par. 1, lett. f) of the Regulation).**

- **Regarding the security of processing, art. 32 of the Regulation establishes that "taking into account the state of the art and the costs of implementation, as well as the nature, subject matter, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risk of varying probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk [...]” (par. 1) and that “in assessing the appropriate level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks presented by the processing that arise in particular from destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or accidental or unlawful access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed” (par. 2).**

**4. Conclusions.**  
In light of the evaluations mentioned above, considering the statements made by the data controller during the investigation and given that, unless the act constitutes a more serious crime, anyone who falsely declares or certifies news or circumstances or produces false acts or documents in a proceeding before the Authority is liable under art. 168 of the Code "Falsehood in declarations to the Authority and interruption of the execution of the tasks or exercise of the powers of the Authority," the elements provided by the Company, as the data controller in the aforementioned defensive memos, although deserving of consideration, do not allow for overcoming the observations notified by the Office with the aforementioned acts initiating the proceedings, nor do any of the cases provided for by art. 11 of the Regulation of the Authority no. 1/2019 apply.  
In particular, the arguments put forth by the Company are not suitable for accepting the requests for archiving made in the defensive memos. In fact, it is emphasized, as already highlighted on many other occasions by the Authority (see Decisions of the Authority of June 25, 2002, doc. web no. 29864 and June 24, 2003, doc. web no. 1132562, available at www.gpdp.it), that, in light of the definition of personal data mentioned above, email addresses fall under the notion of personal data and that, even if no indication was provided regarding the severity or illness of the patient, the circumstance that could be inferred from the context of the communications that the recipients were patients being treated at the multiple sclerosis center means that the treatments for which the notification of data breach was made to the Authority concerned information related to health, as they pertain to information regarding the provision of health care, which reveals information about the health status of the data subjects involved (art. 4, par. 1, no. 15 of the Regulation) (see, in this sense, decisions of the Authority of May 13, 2021, no. 206, doc. web no. 9688020, of September 16, 2021, no. 328, doc. web no. 9722297, of January 11, 2023, no. 7, doc. web no. 9861356, no. 7, of 28).
April 2022, no. 164, web doc no. 9779057, of July 7, 2022, no. 242, web doc no. 9809998, available at www.gpdp.it). This conclusion also applies in relation to the summons for a visit by the Company Commission for the assessment of civil disability, based on the medical documentation provided by the interested parties. Therefore, the sending of a communication via a single email message addressed to multiple recipients, whose addresses were clearly included in the carbon copy (c.c.) field, has, in fact, without justified reason and in the absence of a legal basis, mutually revealed to the recipients of the communications the health status of the other patients. Similarly, a communication of health data has also materialized in the behaviors described in the notifications of violations referred to in points 1. a) and 1. c). For these reasons, the preliminary assessments of the Office are confirmed, and the unlawfulness of the processing of personal data carried out by the Company is noted, for having communicated health data of the interested parties, in violation of the basic principles set out in Articles 5, letter f), and 9 of the Regulation and the obligations regarding security, as per Article 32 of the Regulation. The violation of the aforementioned provisions makes applicable, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter i), the administrative sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraphs 4 and 5 of the Regulation, as also referenced by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code. In this context, considering, in any case, that the conduct has exhausted its effects and considering that the Company has declared to have adopted further measures deemed necessary to prevent future similar occurrences, the prerequisites for the adoption of measures, of a prescriptive or prohibitory nature, referred to in Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not exist.
