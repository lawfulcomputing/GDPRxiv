4. The outcome of the investigation and the procedure for the adoption of corrective and sanctioning measures.
4.1. The reconstruction of the facts.
Following the examination of the statements made to the Authority during the procedure as well as the documentation acquired, it appears that the Company, as the data controller, has carried out certain processing operations related to the claimant that are not compliant with the regulations on personal data protection. 

In this regard, it is highlighted that, unless the act constitutes a more serious crime, anyone who, in a proceeding before the Guarantor, falsely declares or certifies news or circumstances or produces false acts or documents is liable pursuant to Article 168 of the Code “Falsehood in declarations to the Guarantor and interruption of the execution of the tasks or exercise of the powers of the Guarantor.” 

In substance, it emerged that the Company sent the claimant, on February 22, 2021, a disciplinary notice containing, among other things, specific references to activities other than the performance of work activities carried out by the claimant himself between December 1, 2020, and January 18, 2021, after having certified his presence in service by means of clocking in (Doc. 2, complaint 15/11/2021). 

The specific references to the claimant's activities contained in the disciplinary notice were drawn from the investigative report, drafted on January 25, 2021, by the “Privacy Investigative Agency,” commissioned by the Company on November 23, 2020. 

On February 25, 2021, the claimant sent the Company, via email, the request, related to the aforementioned disciplinary notice, “in order to exercise his right of defense,” for “access to his data concerning the facts and behaviors allegedly irregular indicated in the...
"Letter of contestation of charges" (Doc. 3, cited complaint).  
On the same date, the Company provided the complainant with a response via a letter titled: "Request for access to the Company personal computer," in which it was communicated that "your request for access to the company computer cannot be accepted, also due to the precautionary suspension measure imposed against you with the disciplinary notice of 22/02/2021, our letter prot. no. 197/2021. However, we inform you of our willingness to make available to you, in the forms deemed most appropriate, the documentation that you wish to specify in detail by March 5 of this year." (Doc. 4, cited complaint).  
In a subsequent email dated March 2, 2021, the complainant reiterated the previous request to the Company in the following terms: "With reference to your letter of 26/2 last, I must clarify that in my letter of 25.2 last, I did not request access to the company computer but to access the data concerning the facts and behaviors allegedly irregular that have been charged against me and therefore all the documentation you used to raise the charges against me. I therefore renew the request that you may fulfill, possibly also extracting from the documentation data and names if prejudicial to the privacy rights of third parties." (Doc. 5, cited complaint).  
In response to such requests, the Company, in a communication dated April 3, 2021, stated, among other things, that "In any case, you, contrary to what was requested by us, did not specify the specific documentation you were requesting to be exhibited for defensive purposes; your generic requests for the exhibition of 'all the documentation used to raise the disciplinary charge' cannot be accepted." (Annex 7, note from the Company 19/4/2022).  
After receiving this correspondence, the complainant, in an email dated March 4, 2021, specified, as per the Company's request, the subject of the request in the following terms: "to be able to access [...] the company computer [...] used during the period subject to your investigations (December 1, 2020 – January 18, 2021) and until the sending of the letter of charges; - to receive, in any case, in paper form or on a USB stick, a copy of all electronic correspondence sent, received, and archived by him, along with the relevant attachments, during the same period; - to receive clarification on the duration and spatial/temporal placement of the individual activities allegedly not work-related charged against him by the Company, along with a description of the same; - to know what the average number of Work Orders entered by Colleagues would be and what the number of his orders is, allegedly significantly lower."  
From the documents acquired during the investigation, it emerges that, in response to a request for "access to data" related to the complainant and in particular to the disciplinary charges raised by the Company in the letter of February 25, 2021, the Company initially unilaterally qualified this request as "access to the Company personal computer," deeming it, moreover, not acceptable, while inviting the interested party to specify "in detail" the "documentation" subject to the request.  
Even following the complainant's clarification regarding the subject of the request ("I did not request access to the company computer but to access the data concerning the facts and behaviors allegedly irregular that have been charged against me and therefore all the documentation you used to raise the charges against me"), the Company insisted on its position and defined the requests "for the exhibition of 'all the documentation used to raise the disciplinary charge'" as "generic," concluding that these "cannot be accepted."  
In response to the Company's request to "detail" the access request, the complainant sent the note of March 4, 2021, summarized above. In any case, it is noted that even with this third request, the complainant asked the Company to clarify the specific aspects of his conduct subject to the disciplinary charge ("to receive clarification on the duration and spatial/temporal placement of the individual activities allegedly not work-related charged against him by the Company, along with a description of the same").

4.2. Violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 12, and 15 of the Regulation.  
It is noted, preliminarily, that, contrary to what was asserted by the Company during the proceedings, in the appeal presented before the ordinary judicial authority for the challenge of the dismissal, the complainant did not raise "the same objections and arguments regarding the processing of his personal data carried out by the Company now subject to the Complaint."  
In fact, the appeal, regarding the specific point, raised the violation of Article 7, Law 20/5/1970, no. 300 concerning the alleged "genericity of the charge" and "failure to deliver."
of the information on which the disciplinary contestation was based, resulting in a violation of the right to defense, without any reference to the right of access to one's personal data and the regulation on personal data protection (points 1 and 3, appeal pursuant to art. 1 co. 48 L. 28.6.2012 N. 92, filed on 2/12/2021; Annex 1 regularization note 13/1/2022).

On the merits, it is first believed that the requests for access to one's data made by the complainant can be classified as the exercise of the right of access guaranteed by art. 15 of the Regulation, even though this specific provision was not expressly referenced (on this point, see the Guidelines 01/2022 on data subject rights - Right of access, EDPB, March 28, 2023, 3.1.1, n. 47: “Data subjects are not required to specify the legal basis in their request”; “Gli interessati non sono tenuti a specificare la base giuridica nella loro richiesta”, unofficial translation). This is considered given that the content of the requests clearly indicates that the complainant wanted access to data concerning them.

That said, it is noted, first of all, that it is not in compliance with what is established by the aforementioned art. 15 to subordinate the response to the access request to the detailed indication by the data subject of the “documents” to which access is requested, as instead proposed by the Company in the responses of 25/2 and 3/4/2022. 

Moreover, the complainant's request was anything but generic, referring expressly to all data used to carry out the disciplinary contestation. On this point, the aforementioned Guidelines 01/2022 on data subject rights - Right of access, have stated that the request for access to data generally concerns all data held by the controller and that the latter may have doubts about the content of the request only if it has been formulated in extremely generic terms (see in particular par. 2.3.1, n. 35: “Data subjects have the right to obtain, with the exceptions mentioned below, full disclosure of all data relating to them […]. Unless explicitly requested otherwise by the data subject, a request to exercise the right of access shall be understood in general terms, encompassing all personal data concerning the data subject”; “Gli interessati hanno il diritto di ottenere, con le eccezioni di seguito indicate, la piena divulgazione di tutti i dati che li riguardano […]. Salvo diversa richiesta esplicita dell'interessato, la richiesta di esercitare il diritto di accesso è intesa in termini generali, comprendendo tutti i dati personali che lo riguardano”, unofficial translation; among the exceptions, the Guidelines, n. 35 cited, mention cases where “the data controller processes a large quantity of data concerning the data subject” and the “request for access [has been] expressed in very general terms”, unofficial translation: “In situations where the controller processes a large quantity of data concerning the data subject, the controller may have doubts if a request of access, that is expressed in very general terms, really aims at receiving information on all kind of data being processed or on all branches of activity of the controller in detail”).

The aforementioned art. 15 of the Regulation, regarding the subject matter of the right of access, clarifies that this also includes “categories of personal data” as well as “where the data have not been collected from the data subject, all information available on their origin” (see art. 15, par. 1, lett. b) and g) of the Regulation). 

Therefore, the Company, in responding to the complainant's requests, should have provided all the data collected with the investigative report of 25/1/2021, considering that it also contains categories of data relating to the complainant (photographs, a GPS survey, descriptions of places, people, and situations: see the cited investigative report, pp. 7 to 10) that were not transferred into the disciplinary contestation.

Furthermore, as already noted, art. 15 provides that if the data are not collected directly from the data subject (as in this case), the data controller must indicate their origin. This information was also not provided in the response to the access request.

Regarding the modalities of the response that the data controller must provide following the request for the exercise of rights by the data subject, art. 12 prescribes that the controller “facilitates the exercise of rights” and provides “the information regarding the action taken […] without undue delay and, in any case, no later than one month from the receipt of the request.” Furthermore, “if the controller does not comply with the request of the data subject, the controller informs the data subject without delay […] of the reasons for non-compliance and the possibility of lodging a complaint with a supervisory authority and seeking judicial remedy.”

Therefore, the circumstance, represented in the defense memoranda, that the first request for access by the complainant was “sent to an address different from those indicated in the notice,” cannot be taken into consideration regarding this aspect, especially since the personnel management subjects, upon receiving the request, proceeded to provide a response without – at least – forwarding the request to the addresses indicated in the notice.

In any case, it is noted on this point that the aforementioned Guidelines 01/2022 on data subject rights - Right of access have clarified that “the GDPR does not impose any requirement on data subjects regarding the form of the request for access to personal data. Therefore, there are, in principle, no requirements under the GDPR that data subjects...
**1. Introduction**

The GDPR does not impose any requirements on data subjects regarding the form of the request for access to the personal data. Therefore, there are, in principle, no requirements under the GDPR that the data subjects must observe when choosing a communication channel through which they enter into contact with the controller.

**2. Compliance with Article 2-undecies of the Code**

Furthermore, even if the Company had deemed that, in the specific case, one of the concrete conditions provided for by Article 2-undecies of the Code applied, it would have had to communicate this to the data subject with a reasoned response. The conduct of the Company, in response to the requests of the complainant, also does not appear to be compliant with the principle of fairness of processing, which constitutes one of the general principles in the matter, particularly with regard to the employment relationship (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation), since the controller did not indicate the specific origin of the data used for the disciplinary contestation nor did it represent the existence of a concrete prejudice to the exercise of its own right.

**3. Non-compliance with the Regulation**

The Company, therefore, in the terms described above, did not comply with the obligation to provide feedback to the data subject following the exercise of one of the rights provided for by the Regulation - in this case, the right of access pursuant to Article 15 - within the terms and in the manner prescribed by Article 12 of the Regulation. The conduct also occurred in violation of the general principle of fairness established by Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation.

**4. Conclusions: Declaration of Illegality of Processing. Corrective Measures pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, Regulation.**

For the reasons stated above, the Authority believes that the statements, documentation, and reconstructions provided by the data controller during the investigation do not allow for overcoming the observations notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure and are therefore unsuitable to allow for the closure of the present procedure, as none of the cases provided for by Article 11 of the Garante Regulation No. 1/2019 apply. The processing of personal data carried out by the Company, and specifically the inadequate response to the access requests made by the complainant, is indeed unlawful, in the terms outlined above, in relation to Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 12, and 15 of the Regulation.

The violation established in the terms of the reasoning cannot be considered "minor," taking into account the nature of the violation concerning the exercise of the rights of the data subject, the seriousness and duration of the violation itself (considering that the complainant became aware of the existence of the investigative report only during the judicial appeal procedure against the dismissal), the degree of responsibility, and the manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the violation (see Recital 148 of the Regulation).

Furthermore, contrary to what was established by the Garante with the provision of November 25, 2021, doc. web No. 9731887, referenced in the defense briefs, in this case, there was no adherence to the requests of the data subject during the procedure before the Authority since the procedure itself was initiated after the complainant had learned that their data had been collected by an investigative agency.

It is recalled that pursuant to Article 160-bis of the Code, "The validity, effectiveness, and usability in judicial proceedings of acts, documents, and provisions based on the processing of personal data not in compliance with legal or regulatory provisions remain governed by the relevant procedural provisions."

Therefore, in light of the corrective powers attributed by Article 58, paragraph 2 of the Regulation, an administrative pecuniary sanction is imposed pursuant to Article 83 of the Regulation, commensurate with the circumstances of the specific case (Article 58, paragraph 2, letter i) of the Regulation).

**5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i), and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code).**

As a result of the procedure, it appears that AcegasApsAmga S.p.A. has violated Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 12, and 15 of the Regulation. For the violation of the aforementioned provisions, the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, letters a) and b) of the Regulation is foreseen, through the adoption of an injunction order (Article 18, Law No. 689 of November 24, 1981).

It is deemed necessary to apply paragraph 3 of Article 83 of the Regulation, which states that "If, in relation to the same processing or related processing, a data controller [...] violates, with intent or negligence, various provisions of this regulation, the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation," the total amount of the sanction is calculated so as not to exceed the maximum penalty provided for by the same Article 83, paragraph 5.

With reference to the elements listed in Article 83, paragraph 2 of the Regulation for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and its quantification, considering that the sanction must "in any case [be] effective, proportionate, and dissuasive" (Article 83, paragraph 1 of the Regulation), it is represented that, in this case, the following circumstances have been considered:
a) In relation to the nature, severity, and duration of the violation, the nature of the violation concerning the exercise of the rights of the data subject has been deemed relevant, as well as the duration of the violation itself, which lasted for approximately one year;  
b) With reference to the intentional or negligent nature of the violation and the degree of responsibility of the data controller, the conduct of the company and its degree of responsibility have been taken into account, as it did not comply with the regulations regarding data protection in relation to multiple provisions;  
c) In favor of the Company, consideration has been given to its cooperation with the Supervisory Authority, the fact that the established violation concerned only the complainant, and the adoption of measures aimed at facilitating the response to requests for the exercise of the rights of data subjects.  
It is also considered that the following factors are relevant in this case, taking into account the aforementioned principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and deterrence to which the Authority must adhere in determining the amount of the sanction (Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation): primarily, the economic conditions of the offender, determined based on the revenues obtained by the Company in relation to the ordinary financial statements for the year 2022. Finally, the amount of sanctions imposed in similar cases is taken into account.  
In light of the above elements and the assessments made, it is deemed appropriate, in this specific case, to apply an administrative sanction against AcegasApsAmga S.p.A. in the amount of €10,000 (ten thousand).  
In this context, it is also considered, in view of the type of violations established that concerned the exercise of the rights of the data subject, that pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Regulation of the Guarantor No. 1/2019, the publication of this provision on the Guarantor's website should proceed.  
It is also considered that the conditions referred to in Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 are met.
