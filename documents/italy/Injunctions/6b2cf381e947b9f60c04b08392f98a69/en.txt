2. Outcome of the investigative activity. 
Preliminarily, it is stated that the processing of personal data must occur in compliance with the applicable regulations regarding personal data protection and, in particular, the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016 (hereinafter, the "Regulation") and Legislative Decree No. 196 of June 30, 2003 (Code regarding the protection of personal data – hereinafter, the "Code"). 

With particular reference to the issue at hand, it is highlighted that personal data must be "processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently" (principle of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency" and "in a manner that ensures adequate security (...), including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing (principle of "integrity and confidentiality")" (art. 5, par. 1, lett. a) and f) of the Regulation). 

Data must also be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed (principle of data minimization) (art. 5, par. 1, lett. c) of the Regulation). 

The Regulation further provides that the data controller must implement "appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk," taking into account, among other things, "the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risk of varying probability and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons" (art. 32 of the Regulation). 

With reference to the processing activities subject to this provision, the Authority has adopted the "Guidelines on Health Dossier - June 4, 2015" (Provision of 4.6.2015, published in G.U. 164 of July 17, 2015, available at www.gpdp.it doc web n. 4084632), which, like other provisions of the Authority, continue to apply even after the full implementation of the Regulation, as long as they are compatible with it (art. 22, paragraph 4, Legislative Decree No. 101/2018). 

In the aforementioned Guidelines, the Authority, in order to prevent the risk of access to the information processed through the health dossier by unauthorized persons or the communication of health data to third parties by authorized persons, specifically requested the data controller to pay particular attention in identifying the authorization profiles and in training the authorized personnel, with access to the dossier being limited to only healthcare personnel involved in the patient's care process and adopting technical authentication methods to the dossier that reflect the access cases specific to each healthcare facility. To this end, in the aforementioned Guidelines, the Authority has indicated to the data controllers to monitor the instances in which the relevant healthcare personnel may need to consult the health dossier for care purposes.

2. Duration of access to the dossier. The access to the health dossier must be limited to the duration of the care process. However, the possibility of re-accessing the dossier should remain if necessary regarding the type of medical treatment to be provided to the interested party. The measures implemented by the Company did not sufficiently ensure that access was granted only during the time the healthcare personnel were involved in the patient's care.

3. Technical-organizational solutions. The holder must identify, in relation to the various functions assigned to the personnel, technical-organizational solutions that allow the administrative bodies of the health management to access a more complete information base than that present in the company’s health dossier, within the limits of the attributions provided by law. The Company has not demonstrated that it has put in place such solutions to ensure comprehensive oversight of access to the health dossier.

4. Control systems for access. The Authority has emphasized the need for the holder of the treatment to implement systems for controlling access to the database and for detecting any anomalies that may constitute unlawful processing. The Company has not sufficiently addressed this requirement, as it has not activated specific alerts to identify anomalous or risky behaviors related to the operations performed by the data processors.

5. Compliance with previous directives. The Company was previously instructed to complete the implementation of specific measures to ensure that only healthcare professionals who were actively caring for the patient could access the health dossier. The Company has not fully complied with this directive, as evidenced by the ongoing access issues highlighted in the current proceedings.

6. Right to erasure. The Authority had also prescribed that the Company implement specific measures allowing the interested party to express the desire to erase individual clinical events from their health dossier, including those related to past events. The Company has not adequately addressed this requirement, which remains a critical area of concern.

In conclusion, the Company must take immediate action to rectify the identified deficiencies in access control, duration of access, technical-organizational solutions, and compliance with previous directives to ensure that the processing of personal data through the health dossier is lawful and respects the rights of the interested parties.

For these reasons, the illegality of the processing of personal data carried out by the Company in relation to the aforementioned proceedings initiated following communications of violations and complaints is noted, in the terms set out in the reasoning, in particular, for having processed personal data in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and f), 9, 25, and 32 of the Regulation.

In this context, considering that a disciplinary procedure has been initiated and in one case also a criminal one against the author of the access and that the necessary adjustments have been made to overcome the aforementioned criticalities, the prerequisites for the adoption of the corrective measures referred to in Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not exist.

**4. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code).**

The violation of Articles 5, paragraph 2, letters a) and f), 9, 25, and 32 of the Regulation, caused by the conduct of the Company, is subject to the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, paragraphs 4 and 5, of the Regulation.

Considering that the aforementioned proceedings concern the same data controller, similar personal data processing, occurring within the same time frame, and that the Company in the defensive memoranda related to the aforementioned proceedings has provided the same defensive elements, it is deemed appropriate to adopt the respective administrative sanctions in a single provision (Articles 10, paragraph 4, and 19 of the Guarantor Regulation No. 1/2019).

It should be noted that the Guarantor, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation, as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to “impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to the [other] [corrective] measures referred to in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case” and, in this context, “the Board [of the Guarantor] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Guarantor's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code” (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Guarantor Regulation No. 1/2019).

The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into account the principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and deterrence, as indicated in Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, in light of the elements provided in Article 85, paragraph 2, of the Regulation in relation to which for both proceedings it is observed that:
- the Authority became aware of the event following two notifications of violation and two complaints (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter h) of the Regulation);
- with reference to all events subject to notification and complaint, the unlawful accesses concerned the health dossier of three patients by healthcare professionals who were not involved in their care process and against whom a disciplinary procedure has been initiated and in one case also a criminal one (Article 83, paragraph 2, letters a), b) and g) of the Regulation);
- in the case of the first and second complaint, which was also subject to notification of violation, the accesses were not justified by clinical reasons (two in the first case and five in the second) and occurred during the period XX, while in the case subject to the last notification of violation between XX and XX (access by an employee of the Company to the health dossier of their spouse without their knowledge) (Article 83, paragraph 2, letters a) and b) of the Regulation);
- the aforementioned accesses were possible because the measures in place regarding the processing of data suitable for revealing health information carried out through the company health dossier were not fully proportionate to ensure adequate security and integrity of personal data and to prevent unauthorized accesses, although the Authority had already intervened in this regard with the aforementioned provision of July 3, 2014, and subsequently with the Guidelines of 2015 (Article 83, paragraph 2, letters d) and e) of the Regulation);
- the Company has modified the methods and types of access to the company health dossier following the initiation of the investigation by the Authority, as well as introduced alert systems, cooperating with the Authority for this purpose (Article 83, paragraph 2, letters c) and f) of the Regulation);

In light of the aforementioned elements, evaluated in their entirety, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, letter a) of the Regulation, for the violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and f), 9, 25, and 32 of the Regulation in the amount of:
