3. Applicable Legislation.  
3.1 The Regulatory Framework.  
Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016 (hereinafter, the “Regulation”), defines “personal data” as “any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘data subject’); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, particularly by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person” (Article 4, point 1 of the Regulation).  
According to the Regulation, the processing of personal data is lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the conditions listed in Article 6 of the Regulation applies.  
In particular, the processing of personal data carried out in the public sector is lawful when it is necessary “for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject” or “for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller” (Article 6, paragraph 1, letters c) and e) and paragraphs 2 and 3 of the Regulation; Article 2-ter, paragraph 1 of the Code).  
The legal basis for the aforementioned processing must be established by Union or Member State law, which must pursue “a public interest objective [and must be] proportionate to that objective” (Article 6, paragraph 3, of the Regulation).  
In this context, it is established that “Member States may maintain or introduce more specific provisions to adapt the application of the rules of […] the Regulation with regard to processing, in accordance with paragraph 1, letters c) and e), specifying more precisely the specific requirements for processing and other measures to ensure lawful and fair processing…” (Article 6, paragraph 2, of the Regulation).  
The Code has established that “the legal basis provided for in Article 6, paragraph 3, letter b), of the Regulation consists of a law or regulation or general administrative acts” (Article 2-ter, paragraph 1).  
In particular, processing operations that consist of the “dissemination” and “communication” of personal data are permitted only when provided for by a law, regulation, or general administrative acts (Article 2-ter, paragraph 3 of the Code).  
The data controller is in any case required to comply with the principles regarding data protection, including that of “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency” under which data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation).  
3.2 The processing of personal data carried out by the Institute.  
From the assessment carried out, based on the elements acquired and the facts emerged following the activity
**Investigation** 

From the investigation, as well as the subsequent evaluations of the Department, it appears that the Institute published a Christmas greeting video on the school's YouTube channel, featuring some students from XX of the Institute, including the claimant's son (art. 4, par. 1, n. 1, of the Regulation). 

As previously mentioned, in order for a specific processing of personal data to be lawfully carried out by a public entity, such processing must be necessary for the fulfillment of a legal obligation by the data controller or for the execution of a task of public interest or related to the exercise of public powers and must be based on a provision that has the characteristics referred to in art. 2-ter, c. 1, of the Code. 

In this context, the public administration is required to verify, even for common data, the existence of a legal norm, regulation, or general administrative acts that legitimize the dissemination of the personal data of the interested parties. 

In this regard, it is noted that the school did not indicate any specific legal basis suitable for legitimizing the dissemination, on the school's YouTube channel, of images of minors featured in the video (see art. 2-ter, paragraph 3 of the Code). 

Regarding the alleged consent (art. 7 of the Regulation) expressed by the claimant's son and by the claimant herself, which this Institute considered, in good faith, as a valid basis for the processing in question - while not deeming this legitimacy requirement applicable under art. 7 of the Regulation as a legal basis - the following observations are made, also in order to assess the conduct of this Institute as a whole. 

Firstly, it is noted that consent, in general, must be expressed by a major student or by a person exercising parental responsibility over a minor student; for this reason, the Institute's belief that the student had turned fourteen is irrelevant. 

It is also observed that the reference to art. 2-quinquies of the Code, which concerns the possibility for individuals who have reached the age of fourteen to express consent to the processing of their personal data in relation to the direct offering of information society services, is also irrelevant. 

Furthermore, regarding the expression of will by the mother of the student featured in the video, it is noted that in the aforementioned information provided to the claimant in the enrollment form of her son at the Institute, in the section "Authorization for image processing," it is expressly stated that "the parent/guardian agrees not to raise objections against the publication of any photographs or audio-video recordings, made exclusively in the exercise of institutional functions. Institutional link: [...]". The described processing thus seems to relate to the potential publication of photos or videos on the Institute's institutional website, while the processing concerning the publication on YouTube that occurred in this case is not mentioned. 

Moreover, it is observed that this form does not seem to contain a field/flag through which explicit consent for the processing of images and/or videos can be expressed, contrary to what is provided for in art. 7, par. 2 of the Regulation. In this regard, the "Guidelines 5/2020 on consent under Regulation (EU) 2016/679," adopted on May 4, 2020, also state that "the data controller must avoid ambiguity and ensure that the action through which consent is expressed can be distinguished from other actions" (par. 84) and that "if consent must be provided by the parent, it may be necessary to provide a set of information that allows adults to make an informed decision" (par. 126). 

In light of the above considerations, it must be concluded that the publication, on the school's YouTube channel, of a Christmas greeting video featuring some students from XX of the Institute, including the claimant's son, has resulted in the dissemination of personal data in the absence of an appropriate legal basis, as such processing does not fall within the public interest purposes attributed to the Institute and no other condition of legality exists, in violation of arts. 5, par. 1, lett. a) and 6, par. 1, lett. c) and e), par. 2 and par. 3, lett. b) of the Regulation and 2-ter, paragraphs 1 and 3, of the Code. 

Nonetheless, it is positively noted that the Institute has adopted appropriate measures such as the deletion of the video featuring the students from the YouTube channel once the investigation was initiated. 

**4. Conclusions.** 

In light of the evaluations mentioned above, taking into account the statements made by the data controller during the investigation - the truthfulness of which may be subject to accountability under art. 168 of the Code - it is represented that the elements provided by the data controller in the defensive memoranda do not allow for overcoming the objections notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure and are insufficient to allow for the closure of the present procedure, as none of the cases provided for in art. 11 of the Regulation of the Guarantor n. 1/2019 apply. 

Therefore, the preliminary evaluations of the Office are confirmed, and the unlawfulness of the processing of personal data carried out by the Institute is noted, having occurred in the absence of conditions of legality, in violation of arts. 5, par. 1, lett. a) and 6, par. 1, lett. c)
and e), paragraphs 2 and 3, letter b), of the Regulation and 2-ter, paragraphs 1 and 3, of the Code.  
The violation of the aforementioned provisions makes the administrative sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation applicable, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i), and 83, paragraph 3, of the same Regulation, as also referenced by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code.  
Considering, in any case, that the conduct has exhausted its effects, as the Institute has promptly removed the video in which the claimant's child was recorded, the conditions for the adoption of further corrective measures referred to in Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not apply.  

5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the monetary administrative sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i, and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code).  
The Guarantor, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to "impose a monetary administrative sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to [other] corrective measures [referred to in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case," and, in this context, "the Board [of the Guarantor] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Guarantor's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code" (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Guarantor's Regulation No. 1/2019).  
Considering that the violation of the aforementioned provisions by the Institute occurred as a result of a single conduct, Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation applies, according to which the total amount of the monetary administrative sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation. Given that, in the specific case, all the established violations – Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a) and 6, paragraph 1, letters c) and e), paragraphs 2 and 3, letter b), of the Regulation and 2-ter, paragraphs 1 and 3, of the Code – are subject to the sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation, as also referenced by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code, the total amount of the sanction is to be quantified up to €20,000,000 (twenty million/00).  
The aforementioned monetary administrative sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into due account the elements provided for by Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation.  
It is believed that, in this specific case, the level of severity of the violation committed by the data controller is medium (cf. European Data Protection Board, "Guidelines 4/2022 on the calculation of administrative monetary penalties pursuant to the GDPR" of May 24, 2023, point 60), considering that:  
- with specific regard to the nature, severity, and duration of the violation, it should be noted that the online dissemination of personal data involved minors (cf. Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a), of the Regulation);  
- with particular regard to the subjective profile of the violation, it occurred under the erroneous belief by the Institute that it had obtained the consent of the data subjects (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter b), of the Regulation);  
- regarding the categories of personal data communicated, no special categories of data are included (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter g) of the Regulation).  
In preface, considering that the data controller is a school Institute and, therefore, a subject of small size, the following circumstances must also be taken into account:  
- it promptly deleted the video concerning the students once it became aware of the complaint filed (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter c), of the Regulation);  
- there are no previous relevant violations committed by the data controller (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter e), of the Regulation);  
- the degree of cooperation shown by the data controller with the supervisory authority (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter f) of the Regulation);  
- the high accessibility and visibility of the tool used for the described dissemination, given that the YouTube channel is accessible even to those who do not know and do not attend the Institute and, therefore, is more prone to the risk of loss of control over the personal data of the data subjects (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter k), of the Regulation).  
In light of the aforementioned elements, assessed in their entirety, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the monetary sanction at €2,000.00 (two thousand/00) for the violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a) and 6, paragraph 1, letters c) and e), paragraphs 2 and 3, letter b), of the Regulation and 2-ter, paragraphs 1 and 3, of the Code, as the monetary administrative sanction deemed, pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.  
In this context, it is also believed that, pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Guarantor's Regulation No. 1/2019, the publication of this section containing the injunction order on the Guarantor's website should proceed. This is in consideration of the specific circumstances of the concrete case, concerning the publication of a video involving minors on the YouTube channel in the absence of a condition of lawfulness.  
Finally, it is noted that the conditions referred to in Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 are met.
