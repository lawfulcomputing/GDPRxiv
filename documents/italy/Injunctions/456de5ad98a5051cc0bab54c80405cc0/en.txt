2. Considerations on the complaint
The complaint raised by Ms. XX highlights significant concerns regarding the unauthorized dissemination of her images on social media, specifically Instagram. The images in question, which depict her before and after a medical aesthetic procedure, were shared without her consent, raising issues related to the protection of personal data as stipulated in the applicable regulations.

3. Legal framework
The processing of personal data, including images, is governed by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the national legislation that implements it. According to Article 6 of the GDPR, processing is lawful only if at least one of the specified conditions is met, including the necessity of processing for the performance of a contract or the consent of the data subject.

4. Findings
Based on the information provided by both the complainant and Dr. Rubino, it is evident that there was a lack of consent for the publication of Ms. XX's images. The absence of explicit consent for the dissemination of her personal data, particularly in a public forum such as Instagram, constitutes a violation of her rights under the GDPR.

5. Conclusion
In light of the findings, it is necessary to take appropriate measures to ensure compliance with data protection regulations and to protect the rights of the complainant. The Garante for the Protection of Personal Data will issue a formal decision regarding the actions to be taken in this case, including potential sanctions against the parties involved for the unauthorized processing of personal data.

6. Orders
The Garante orders:
- The immediate removal of the images of Ms. XX from all social media platforms where they have been published without her consent.
- A review of the internal protocols regarding patient consent for the publication of images to prevent similar occurrences in the future.
- A formal apology to Ms. XX for the unauthorized use of her images and any distress caused by this incident.

This decision is subject to appeal in accordance with the provisions of the law.

The Guarantor, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter i) of the Regulation and Article 166 of the Code, has the power to “impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to (other) corrective measures referred to in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case,” by adopting an injunction order (Article 18, Law No. 689 of November 24, 1981), in relation to the processing of personal data carried out by Dr. Rubino, which has been found to be unlawful, as stated above.

It is deemed necessary to apply paragraph 3 of Article 83 of the Regulation, in relation to the violation of Articles 5 and 9 of the Regulation and Article 2-septies of the Code, where it provides that “if, in relation to the same processing or related treatments, a data controller [...] violates, with intent or negligence, various provisions of this Regulation, the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction shall not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation,” the total amount of the sanction is calculated so as not to exceed the maximum penalty provided for by the same Article 83, paragraph 5.

In light of the above and, in particular, of the category of personal data involved in the violation, which, by their nature, are particularly sensitive in terms of fundamental rights and freedoms, as well as the type of processing operation (dissemination), it is believed that the level of severity of the violation committed by Dr. Rubino is high (cf. European Data Protection Board, “Guidelines 04/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines under the GDPR” of May 23, 2023, point 60). It has also been assessed that the Guarantor became aware of the event following the receipt of a complaint from a patient of Dr. Rubino (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter h) of the Regulation).
