**4. LEGAL ASSESSMENTS OF THE AUTHORITY.**  
**4.1. Exercise of rights under Articles 15-22 of the Regulation.**  
While acknowledging the non-systematic nature of the violation that concerns, as per the documents, only one interested party, it must still be highlighted, considering the relevance of the right to object to processing for marketing purposes, which—if not channeled into the correct paths of proper processing—results in a violation of the right to personal data protection and the rights of the person connected to it. Therefore, the hypothesized violation of Articles 6; 12, par. 3; 15; 21, par. 2, of the Regulation, as well as Article 130 of the Code, must be confirmed. The Authority, in consideration of the above and the asserted revision of the procedures related to the exercise of rights by the interested parties, does not believe it needs to adopt corrective measures in this regard.  

**4.2. The processing of personal data related to the use of e-sims.**  
In light of the varied multitude of data collected within the Coopvoce service, and in particular "the data of telephone and/or telematic traffic and internet navigation and/or position and/or geolocation (obtained via GPS and/or services or functionalities on location and/or with identification of mobile phones and antennas including Wi-Fi and/or postal code and/or city name)," Coop has, however, represented in the memorandum that "The collection and use of such data... occurs... primarily, to fulfill the contractual obligations undertaken by Coop Italia towards its customers for the provision of telephony and data communication services... Coop Italia, in using the data for direct marketing, specifically relies only on the personal information (first name and surname) and contact data (email and SMS) provided by the interested party, in compliance with the consents granted."  
It is therefore deemed necessary to archive the observations made in the complaint regarding the violation of Article 6, par. 1, lett. a), of the Regulation and not to impose any corrective measures in this regard.  
From a different perspective, despite what Coop has argued, the concept of marketing cannot be expanded to include also the specific statistical and economic analysis activities, which are structurally different from promotional activities. In this regard, it is not valid to refer to the 2013 Guidelines for two significant reasons: the Authority, in the 2013 Guidelines referenced by the Company, referred only to the purposes typified by Article 130, paragraph 1, of the Code, namely "those of sending advertising material, direct sales, conducting market research, and commercial communication [...]" believing that only "the aforementioned activities—and not additional purposes, such as those of economic and statistical analysis—are functionally, in most cases, aimed at pursuing a single (broadly speaking) marketing purpose, with the consequence that the related processing appears to justify—generally—the acquisition of a single consent." A non-specific consent is not free as it results in a coercion of the will of the interested party, consequently violating the principles of fairness of processing and the freedom of expression of consent (a principle already established by Article 23 of the previous Code). Each purpose of processing, different from contractual, administrative, and accounting (e.g., profiling, marketing, etc.), requires free, specific, informed, and distinct consent for each of them (Article 6, par. 1, lett. a, of the Regulation). This capacity for self-determination is not ensured when consent is collected indiscriminately to pursue distinct purposes, each of which could be pursued individually in the presence of an autonomous evaluation and determination by the interested party. This orientation finds full and constant correspondence also in the measures of this Authority and has been confirmed even after the full operation of the Regulation (see provision of June 12, 2019, web document no. 9115; provision of January 15, 2020, web document no. 9256486); the Regulation has further strengthened the principle of consent, along with its requirements of freedom, specificity, and, additionally, unequivocability (see: Articles 5, par. 1, lett. a, and 7, par. 4; and in particular Recital 32, but also Recitals 39, 40, 42, and 43).  
Based on the overall outcomes of the investigation conducted, it is possible to include such statistical and economic activities within profiling, with respect to which, according to the statements made, they are positioned in terms of strict and direct...
the absence of previous violations and therefore of a history of non-compliance (lett. d).

In light of the above considerations, it is deemed appropriate to impose a monetary administrative sanction on Coop Italia, taking into account both the aggravating and mitigating circumstances identified.
