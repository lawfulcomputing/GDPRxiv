3. Outcome of the investigative activity.
3.1. Applicable regulations regarding personal data protection.
According to the regulations on personal data protection, public entities may process the personal data of the data subjects if the processing is necessary, in general, to fulfill specific obligations or tasks provided by laws (art. 6, paragraph 1, letter c) of the Regulation). The processing is also lawful when it is “necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller” (art. 6, paragraph 1, letter e) and paragraphs 2 and 3 of the Regulation; 2-ter of the Code). The data controller is also required, in any case, to comply with the principles regarding personal data protection, including those of “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency,” according to which personal data must be “processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject” (art. 5, par. 1, letter a) of the Regulation).

3.2. Information to data subjects.
In light of the aforementioned principle of transparency, the data controller must adopt appropriate measures to provide the data subject with all information in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language (art. 12, par. 1, of the Regulation). In particular, when video devices are used, the data controller, in addition to providing first-level information through the placement of warning signs near the area under surveillance, must also provide data subjects with “second-level information,” which must “contain all the mandatory elements pursuant to Article 13 of the [Regulation]” and “be easily accessible to the data subject, for example through a complete information page made available at a central hub […] or posted in an easily accessible location” (“Guidelines 3/2019 on the processing of personal data through video devices adopted by the European Data Protection Board on XX), particularly par. 7; but see also the “Provision on surveillance” from the Authority dated April 8, 2010, web doc. no. 1712680, particularly par. 3.1; lastly, see the FAQs from the Authority regarding surveillance, web doc. no. 9496574, no. 4; see also provisions dated January 11, 2024, no. 5, web doc. no. 9977020; no. 312 of July 18, 2023, web doc. no. 9920578; October 20, 2022, no. 341, web doc. no. 9831369; April 28, 2022, no. 162, web doc. no. 9777974; April 7, 2022, no. 119, web doc. no. 9773950; September 16, 2021, no. 327, web doc. no. 9705650 and March 11, 2021, no. 90, web doc. no. 9582791). 

The first-level information (warning sign) “should communicate the most important data, such as the purposes of the processing, the identity of the data controller, and the existence of the rights of the data subject, along with information on the most significant impacts of the processing” (Committee Guidelines, cited, par. 114). Furthermore, the signage must also contain those pieces of information that could be unexpected for the data subject. This could include, for example, the transmission of data to third parties, particularly if located outside the EU, and the retention period. If such information is not indicated, the data subject should be able to trust that there is only real-time surveillance (without any data recording or transmission to third parties) (Committee Guidelines, cited, par. 115). 

The first-level warning signage must contain a clear reference to the second level of information, for example, indicating a website where the text of the extended information can be consulted. During the investigation, it emerged that from the date the video system in question became operational (XX) until XX, the Municipality did not provide the data subjects...
**1. First and second level information,**  
thereby acting in violation of the principle of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency" as set forth in Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation, as well as Articles 12 and 13 of the Regulation.  

**3.3. Impact assessment.**  
In cases of high risks for the data subjects - arising, for example, from the use of new technologies and always present where systematic large-scale monitoring of a publicly accessible area is carried out (see Article 35, paragraph 3, letter c) of the Regulation) - the data controller must carry out a data protection impact assessment in order to adopt, in particular, appropriate measures to address such risks, consulting the Authority in advance, where the conditions apply (see Articles 35 and 36, paragraph 1, of the Regulation).  

In this regard, the Municipality has stated that, at the time of the installation of the video devices in question, it did not conduct an impact assessment as it erroneously believed it was unnecessary, proceeding to draft it only after the initiation of the investigation.  

However, it is noted that the Municipality was certainly subject to the obligation to draft a data protection impact assessment, considering that, pursuant to the aforementioned Article 35, paragraph 3, letter c) of the Regulation, it is always required in cases of "systematic large-scale monitoring of a publicly accessible area," which allows for the collection of numerous data, including information related to the location and movement of the data subjects, a circumstance that occurs in this case.  

Furthermore, it is highlighted that Article 35 of the Regulation obliges controllers to conduct the impact assessment before commencing the processing, also considering that this tool is suitable for demonstrating the accountability of the controller regarding the processing carried out (see Provision No. 162 of April 28, 2022, web document No. 9777974).  

In light of the above considerations, having not drafted a data protection impact assessment with a certain date prior to the commencement of processing, it must be concluded that the Municipality acted in violation of Article 35 of the Regulation.  

**4. Conclusions.**  
In light of the evaluations mentioned above, it is noted that the statements made by the Municipality, as the data controller, during the investigation - the truthfulness of which may be subject to accountability under Article 168 of the Code - while deserving of consideration, do not allow for overcoming the objections notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure and are insufficient to allow for the archiving of the present procedure pursuant to Article 14, paragraph 1, of the Authority's Regulation No. 1/2019, as none of the cases provided for by Article 11 referenced therein apply.  

Therefore, the preliminary evaluations of the Office are confirmed, and the unlawfulness of the processing of personal data carried out by the Municipality is noted, for having processed personal data in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 12, 13, and 35 of the Regulation.  

The violation of the aforementioned provisions makes the administrative sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation applicable, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i), and 83, paragraph 3, of the same Regulation, as also referenced by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code.  

**5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i, and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code).**  
The Authority, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to "impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to [other] corrective measures referred to in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case," and, in this context, "the College [of the Authority] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Authority's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code" (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Authority's Regulation No. 1/2019).  

Considering that the violation of the aforementioned provisions by the Municipality occurred as a result of conduct that can be considered unitary (same processing or related processes, considering that they are configured as compliance related to the same video surveillance system), Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation applies, according to which the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation. Given that, in this case, the most serious violations, which pertain to Articles 5, 12, and 13 of the Regulation, are subject to the sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation, as also referenced by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code, the total amount of the sanction is to be quantified up to €20,000,000 (twenty million/00).  

The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into due account the elements provided for by Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation.  

With specific regard to the nature and severity of the violation (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a), of the Regulation), it must be considered that the Municipality does not...
has provided the first and second level information to the interested parties regarding the processing of personal data carried out through a video system, nor has it drafted the impact assessment. 

In favor of the Municipality, however, the following should be considered:
- the negligent nature of the violation, given the Municipality's initial erroneous belief regarding the non-existence of the obligation for such compliance, which, in fact, was fulfilled as soon as the investigation was initiated (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter b) of the Regulation);
- that the processing in question does not relate to special categories of data (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter g).

In light of these circumstances, it is believed that, in this specific case, the level of severity of the violation committed by the data controller can be considered medium (cf. European Data Protection Board, “Guidelines 04/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines under the GDPR” of May 23, 2023, point 60).

That said, it is considered that, for the purposes of quantifying the sanction, it should be taken into account, in favor of the Municipality, that there are no previous relevant violations committed by the data controller (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter e) of the Regulation) as well as the degree of cooperation shown by the data controller with the supervisory authority in order to remedy the violation and mitigate its possible negative effects, considering that it promptly provided the first-level information by posting signs near the video systems installed in five locations in the city and published a second-level information notice on the institutional website, as well as drafted the impact assessment (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter f) of the Regulation).

In light of the aforementioned elements, evaluated in their entirety, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the monetary sanction at €6,000.00 (six thousand/00) for the violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 12, 13, and 35 of the Regulation, as an administrative monetary sanction deemed, pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.

In this context, it is also considered that, pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Garante Regulation No. 1/2019, the publication of this section containing the injunction order on the Garante's website should be carried out. 

This is in consideration of the specific circumstances of the concrete case, concerning the Municipality's failure to comply with the principle of transparency referred to in Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation, having failed to provide the information (not even of "first level") to the interested parties regarding the processing in question, and the failure to draft the impact assessment prior to the commencement of the aforementioned processing.

Finally, it is noted that the conditions referred to in Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 are met.
