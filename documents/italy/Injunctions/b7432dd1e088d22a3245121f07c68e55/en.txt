1. The violation of personal data  
By note dated XX, subsequently supplemented by  
communication dated XX, the Health Authority of Alto  
Adige, hereinafter “Authority,” transmitted  
to the Authority, pursuant to Article 33 of the Regulation,  
a notification of a personal data breach, in  
which it was stated that “a contracted physician  
operating at the Authority's clinics, regarding the issue of  
‘reduction of service hours,’ wrote to several  
internal and external subjects (info@ordinemedici.bz.it)  
attaching to his communication the list of  
patients seen on a specific day. The  
communication was sent in plain text and included the following data: name –  
surname – date of birth – residence of the patient  
as well as the location of service provision, service  
provided, indication on paying patient, exempt (with  
indication of the exemption code), tax code and mobile number.”  
In the same communication, it was highlighted that  
“the author of the breach sent in a single  
email communication the attachment with the breached data to 15  
Company Structures and various Subjects including  
the order of physicians of the Province” and that, as  
technical and organizational measures adopted (or proposed for  
adoption) to remedy the breach and reduce its negative effects for the  
interested parties and to prevent similar future breaches, the following were indicated:  
“Involvement of the DPO; Request to all  
structures and subjects that received the attachment  
to proceed with the relative deletion; Initiated disciplinary  
procedure; Initiated process for  
reporting the incident to the order of physicians”;  
“the Authority provided guidance on the  
correct method of sending so-called sensitive data – such  
guidance is available on the intranet page in the privacy section of the Authority itself.”  
The same Authority attached the  
documentation containing the aforementioned email, from which  
it is inferred that the transmission of the list of  
patients (13) was carried out in order to provide  
clarifications regarding the hours worked  
(“hereby to emphasize that the hours  
written in the resolution regarding the Tuesday  
clinic in Laives are not the actual ones compared to the list of  
patients set by the CUP. See attached example”).

3. The outcome of the investigative activity  
Taking note of what was represented by the Company during the proceedings, it is observed that:  
for “personal data” it is understood as “any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘data subject’)” and for “data concerning health” as “data concerning the health status of the data subject that reveals information related to their past, present, or future physical or mental health. This includes information about the natural person collected during their registration to receive healthcare services or the related provision referred to in Directive 2011/24/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council; a number, symbol, or specific element assigned to a natural person to uniquely identify them for health purposes” (art. 4, par. 1, nos. 1 and 15 of the Regulation; Cons. no. 35);  
the Court of Cassation has deemed that “the very act of communicating the need for healthcare treatment and, therefore, the existence of a ‘disease’ in a broad sense – understood as a situation that necessitates healthcare treatment – pertains to health data: it is not necessary, for this purpose, to specify what treatment or what disease is involved” (Sent. no. 28417/2023; see also, press release from the Court of Justice of the European Union no. 159/24, regarding the judgment of October 4, 2024, in case C-21/23);  
for “communication” it is understood as “making personal data known to one or more specific subjects other than the data subject, the representative of the data controller in the territory of the European Union, the processor or their representative in the territory of the European Union, and the persons authorized, pursuant to art. 2-quaterdecies, to process personal data under the direct authority of the controller or the processor, in any form, including by making them available, consulting, or through interconnection” (art. 2-ter, par. 4, lett. a) of the Code);  
information about health status may only be communicated to the data subject and may only be communicated to third parties based on an appropriate legal basis (art. 9 of the Regulation);  
the data controller is required to comply with the principles regarding data protection, including those of “minimization” and “integrity and confidentiality,” according to which personal data must be “adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed” and “processed in a manner that ensures adequate security (…), including protection, through appropriate technical and organizational measures, against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage” (art. 5, par. 1, lett. c) and f) of the Regulation).  
The adequacy of such measures must be assessed by the data controller in relation to the nature of the data, the object, the purposes of the processing, and the risk to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects, taking into account the risks arising from the destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or access to personal data transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed (art. 32, par. 1 and 2 of the Regulation).

4. Conclusions: declaration of unlawfulness of the processing. Corrective measures pursuant to art. 58, par. 2, Regulation  
In light of the above, it emerges that the conduct of the doctor was aimed, as stated by him, at providing clarifications regarding the working hours he performed. This purpose could have been achieved, in compliance with the aforementioned principle of minimization, without transmitting the documentation related to the patients, including information on the type and location of the service provided and on the exemption code.  
Furthermore, in compliance with the principle of integrity and confidentiality of data and the obligation to adopt appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, considering the particular category of data processed, the Company should have provided specific instructions to healthcare operators who process health data of the Company’s patients and draw attention to the regulations regarding personal data protection and the greater protection that health data deserve, which, by their nature, are particularly sensitive in terms of rights and fundamental freedoms, given that the context of their processing could create significant risks to the rights and fundamental freedoms.  
In light of the evaluations mentioned above, taking into account the statements made by the data controller during the investigation and considering that, unless the act constitutes a more serious crime, anyone who falsely declares or certifies news or circumstances or produces false acts or documents in a proceeding before the Guarantor is liable under art. 168 of the Code “Falsehood in declarations to the Guarantor and interruption of the execution of the tasks or the exercise of the powers of the Guarantor,” the elements provided by the data controller in the aforementioned defensive memorandum and during the hearing do not allow for overcoming the observations notified by the Office with the aforementioned initiation act of the proceedings, nor do any of the cases provided for in art. 11 of the Guarantor Regulation no. 1/2019 apply.
