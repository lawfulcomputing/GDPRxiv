PREMISE  
1. The finding of the Municipal Police and the initiation  

of the procedure.  
With a note received on 06/30/2023 and registered on  
07/03/2023, the Municipal Police Command and  
Civil Protection of the Municipality of Reggio Emilia – transmitted to this Authority the report of the inspection  
carried out on 03/24/2023, by its  
personnel, at the “neighborhood establishment”  
named “Green Land African Minimarket,” located at  
Viale IV Novembre No. 19/f, Reggio Emilia, managed  
by the sole proprietorship “Green Land African Market  
Di Owe Amenze” (hereinafter “Business”) of Owe Amenze, VAT No. 02981850353, with legal headquarters at the same  
address.  
The report confirmed the presence, at the  
aforementioned premises, of a surveillance system  
composed of 2 regularly functioning cameras.  
The external camera was found to extend the  
framing to a wide part of the public road on  
which the store is located, including the sidewalk  
opposite to that on which the establishment is situated. The  
internal camera was found to be suitable for recording  
also the employee personnel, in the absence of the  
obligations provided for pursuant to Article 4 of Law  
No. 300/1970 (Workers' Statute). In this regard,  
it is noted that the owner of the establishment has  
declared to have an employee.  
Based on the findings in the aforementioned  
report, a notification was therefore made to the  
Business, with a note dated 11/07/2023 (protocol No.  
0150259.07/11/2023), regarding the initiation of the procedure for  
the adoption of the measures referred to in Articles 58,  
paragraph 2, and 83 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter “Regulation”), in accordance with the provisions of  
Article 166, paragraph 5, of Legislative Decree No. 196  
of June 30, 2003 (hereinafter “Code”).  
Regarding the functionality of the external camera, the violation  
of the general principles concerning the protection of  
personal data referred to in Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a)  
and 6 of the Regulation was contested, while in relation to the  

internal camera, the violation of the principle of lawfulness of processing (Article 5,  
paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation in relation  
to Article 114 of the Code) and of Article 88 of  
the Regulation regarding the applicable discipline was contested.  
Subsequent to the violations, the Business  
adopted a collaborative attitude and sent  
documentation from which it was possible to ascertain  
that it has removed the external camera that  
recorded areas of non-strict pertinence, updated  
the sign indicating the surveillance system, and complied  
with the requirements of Article 4 of Law No. 300/1970, thus regularizing the  
processing.  
2. The legal framework of the processing carried out.  
The use of surveillance systems can  
result, in relation to the positioning of the  
cameras and the quality of the images captured, in  
the processing of personal data.  
Such processing, which occurred in this case, must  
be carried out in compliance with the general principles  
contained in Article 5 of the Regulation.  
The data controller must also follow the  
guidelines contained in the provision regarding  
surveillance - April 8, 2010 [1712680] (in this sense, also the FAQs regarding surveillance,  
published on the Authority's website), and in Guidelines No. 3/2019 of the European Data Protection Board on the processing of  
personal data through video devices.  
The field of view of the cameras must be  
limited to the only areas of its own pertinence, also  
potentially through the activation of a  
function to obscure the exceeding parts, with the  
perspective, however, that a minimal involvement  
near access points may be considered  
acceptable.  
In exceptional cases where there are actual risk situations, the data controller may,
based on a legitimate interest, to extend the surveillance of video cameras to public areas or areas open to the public, immediately adjacent to those of relevance, provided that the public space captured is only that immediately facing the entrances and windows of one's residence and that such extension is necessary and proportionate, in relation to the context, to ensure effective protection (see in this regard Guidelines No. 3/2019 of the European Data Protection Board on the processing of personal data through video devices, point 27). Furthermore, if the video surveillance system is capable of capturing employees during work activities, the related processing of personal data is lawful only if it complies with the provisions of Article 4, Law No. 300 of May 20, 1970. In fact, the processing of personal data carried out within the employment relationship, if necessary for the purpose of managing the relationship itself (see Articles 6, paragraph 1, letter b) and c); 9, paragraph 2, letter b) of the Regulation), must be carried out in compliance with the general principles indicated by Article 5 of the Regulation, and in particular the principle of lawfulness, according to which the processing is lawful if it is in accordance with the applicable sector regulations (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation). Consistent with this approach, Article 88 of the Regulation has preserved national provisions of greater protection ("more specific rules") aimed at ensuring the protection of rights and freedoms regarding the processing of personal data of workers. The national legislator has approved, as a more specific provision, Article 114 of Legislative Decree No. 196 of June 30, 2003 (hereinafter "Code") which among the conditions for the lawfulness of processing has established compliance with the provisions of Article 4, Law No. 300 of May 20, 1970. The violation of the aforementioned Article 88 of the Regulation is subject, if the requirements are met, to the application of an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 5, letter d) of the Regulation. Based on the aforementioned Article 4, Law No. 300 of 1970, video surveillance systems, if they also allow for "remote monitoring" of employees' activities, "may be used exclusively for organizational and productive needs, for workplace safety and for the protection of company assets," and the related installation must, in any case, be carried out after the conclusion of a collective agreement with the unitary trade union representation or with the company trade union representatives or, where it has not been possible to reach such an agreement or in the absence of representatives, only if preceded by the issuance of a specific authorization by the Labor Inspectorate. The activation and conclusion of this guarantee procedure is therefore an indispensable condition for the installation of video surveillance systems. The violation of this provision is subject to criminal penalties (see Article 171 of the Code).

3. The outcome of the investigation and the sanctioning procedure. Based on the findings made by the Municipal Police, it has emerged that, with reference to the video surveillance system installed at the commercial establishment and consisting of two cameras, the external camera was found to capture a wide area of the public road on which the store is located, including the sidewalk opposite that where the establishment is located, in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a) and 6 of the Regulation. The internal camera was also found to be capable of capturing both customers and employees, and the related processing of personal data was carried out in the absence of the guarantee measures provided for under Article 4 of Law No. 300/1970, referenced by Article 114 of the Code, in relation to the presence of employees in the video-monitored premises, in violation of the principle of lawfulness of processing (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation in relation to Article 114 of the Code) and Article 88 of the Regulation.

4. Conclusions: declaration of unlawfulness of processing. Corrective measures pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, Regulation. The processing of personal data carried out by the Company has therefore been found to be unlawful, in the terms outlined above, in relation to Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a) and 6 of the Regulation regarding the processing of data carried out through the external camera, and in relation to Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a) (principle of lawfulness), 88 of the Regulation and Article 114 of the Code in relation to the internal camera. The violation established in the terms outlined in the reasoning cannot be considered "minor," taking into account the nature, severity, and duration of the violation, the degree of responsibility, and the manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the violation (see recital 148 of the Regulation). The party, during the investigation, has regularized all the detected violations, providing proof with adequate documentation. Following the investigation and the established unlawfulness of the conduct as described above, an injunction order must be adopted pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter i) of the Regulation for the application of an administrative pecuniary sanction.

The Guarantor, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter i) of the Regulation and Article 166 of the Code, has the power to impose an administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, letters a) and d), of the Regulation, by adopting an injunction order (Article 18, Law No. 689 of November 24, 1981), in relation to the processing of data.
