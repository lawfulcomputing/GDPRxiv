1. Introduction.  
Following a specific report from the Trade Unions OO.SS. FILT-CGIL Milan and FILT-CGIL Lombardy (see notes of XX and XX on file), a presumed violation of the regulations concerning the protection of personal data was reported, regarding the dissemination of personal data of numerous employees of certain companies managing local public transport services in the territory of the Lombardy Region (hereinafter “Region”) (companies Milano Serravalle – Milano, Tangenziali p.A., Autostrada Pedemontana Lombarda p.A. and Serravalle Engineering r.l.).

The file containing the aforementioned contract and its attachments “was freely downloadable at the address: http://areadocumentale.servizirl.it/atti/download/AAAAYBnbrd+mLfAGu+72pN0QYxN3M4aK32vCcmNvmk8hVXf3fjep0bQsNQC7UmdgDnC9xEGz8zuE2iFMHjU8OyA+9y/77kJ+AfRKA8burSpa3whChTFoy/A==”, the domain “servizirl.it” being registered in the name of the Lombardy Region. “Only after the intervention of the union representatives was the file finally removed, after being freely accessible to anyone for over a year.”  
According to the report, “in the context of the negotiations for the conclusion of the sale contract and the due diligence operations, the companies Milano Serravalle S.p.A., Autostrada Pedemontana S.p.A. and Serravalle Engineering communicated, pursuant to and for the purposes of Article 14 of Reg. 2016/679, to the Lombardy Region and FNM S.p.A. the names of the employees, divided by category under Article 2095 of the Civil Code, the tasks performed by each, the position held, the monthly salary, the gross annual salary, the allowances received, and the amount of the performance bonus received.” In particular, the published resolution was accompanied by “the list of all pending disputes with the workers, indicating for each the name of the employee, the competent authority, the reasons for the dispute, the hearing dates, and the value of the dispute.”

**3. Outcome of the investigative activity.**

**3.1 The regulatory framework**

The regulation on the protection of personal data provides that the processing of personal data by public entities can only be carried out if necessary “to comply with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject” or “for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller” (Article 6, paragraph 1, letters c) and e) of the Regulation). 

Such processing must, in any case, find its basis in Union or Member State law, which must pursue a public interest objective and be proportionate to the pursuit of the same. The purpose of the processing must be necessary for the execution of a task carried out in the public interest or connected to the exercise of public powers vested in the data controller (cf. Article 6, paragraph 3, of the Regulation and Article 2-ter of the Code).

With regard to special categories of personal data, including those related to health (for which a general prohibition on processing is provided, except in the cases indicated in Article 9, paragraph 2 of the Regulation and, in any case, a regime of greater guarantee compared to other types of data, particularly due to Article 9, paragraph 4, as well as Article 2-septies of the Code), processing is permitted where “necessary for reasons of substantial public interest based on Union or Member State law, which must be proportionate to the pursued purpose, respect the essence of the right to data protection, and provide for appropriate and specific measures to safeguard the fundamental rights and interests of the data subject” (Article 9, paragraph 2, letter g), of the Regulation). 

The national legislator has defined “relevant” the public interest for processing “carried out by entities performing tasks of public interest or connected to the exercise of public powers” in the areas indicated, albeit not exhaustively, by Article 2-sexies of the Code, establishing that the related processing “is permitted when provided for by European Union law or, in the domestic legal system, by provisions of law or, in cases provided for by law, by regulations that specify the types of data that may be processed, the operations that can be carried out, and the reason for substantial public interest, as well as the appropriate and specific measures to safeguard the fundamental rights and interests of the data subject.”

In any case, health-related data, i.e., those “related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, which reveal information concerning their health status” (Article 4, paragraph 1, no. 15 of the Regulation), due to the greater guarantees recognized by the Regulation and the Code, given the particular sensitivity of this category of data, “cannot be disseminated” (Article 2-septies, paragraph 8, of the Code). 

The data controller is, in any case, required to comply with the general principles regarding the protection of personal data (Article 5 of the Regulation).

**3.2 The online dissemination of personal data of employees of companies managing local public transport services in the territory of the Lombardy Region**

As evidenced by the documents, as well as the findings made based on the elements acquired, following the investigative activity and the subsequent assessments of the Office, it has been established that the Region published, from XX to XX on its institutional website, the “Regional Council Resolution no. XX of XX concerning the sale of the shareholdings held in Milano Serravalle – Milano Tangenziali S.p.A. and the related contract for the sale of the shares, including the attachments” and that the file containing the aforementioned contract and the related attachments “was freely downloadable at the address: http://areadocumentale.servizirl.it/atti/download/AAAAYBnbrd+mLfAGu+72pN0QYxN3M4aK32vCcmNvmk8hVXf3fjep0bQsNQC7UmdgDnC9xEGz8zuE2iFMHjU8OyA+9y/77kJ+AfRKA8burSpa3whChTFoy/A==.”

Although, as stated by the Region, such data “were published not in the context of the employment relationship and the processing of employee data by the employer,” the reference to the transparency obligations under Article 22 of Legislative Decree March 14, 2013, no. 33, which, in the opinion of the Region, would have justified the dissemination of personal data of employees, although not employees of the Region, contained, in particular, in the attachments to the draft contract (attached to resolution XX of XX), does not appear relevant. It is represented, in fact, that such provisions do not provide for the publication of data related to
**Employment Report of Employees of the Companies Involved**

including, among others, the income received by each worker, broken down by income category, nor the list of judicial proceedings against individual workers. 

In particular, paragraph 1 letter d-bis) of Article 22 of the aforementioned decree provides, among the obligations for the publication of data relating to public entities under supervision and private law entities under public control, exclusively for the publication of “provisions concerning the establishment of public participation companies, acquisition of shares in already established companies, management of public shareholdings, disposal of shareholdings,” without any specific and direct obligation to publish “identifying data, classification, qualification, date of hiring, and length of service, as well as the total company cost in terms of gross annual salary and other remuneration elements” related to individual workers (see point 9.18.1 of the draft contract attached to the response note of XX) nor of “pending judicial proceedings with workers” (see attachment 9.18.12 to the draft contract).

The legislation, in fact, does not authorize the publication of personal data, but exclusively provides for a specific regime of transparency regarding “provisions concerning the establishment of public participation companies, acquisition of shares in already established companies, management of public shareholdings, disposal of shareholdings, listing of public control companies in regulated markets, and periodic rationalization of public shareholdings” by public administrations, to be updated annually.

In this regard, in the specific case, the Resolution of the Regional Council No. XX of XX contained, in the annex, the list of approximately 732 employees of the aforementioned companies and in particular information primarily related to “litigations, remuneration, length of service, qualification, and generally career aspects” of the workers. 

From the documentation on file, it also emerges that, in the case of one employee, information related to health was also reported concerning the request for compensation for damages caused to the worker's health due to harassing behavior by the employer (see paragraphs 9.18.12 and 9.19 of the aforementioned resolution).

Reiterating that the personal data protection system requires a suitable legal basis for any processing operation (Article 4, point 2 of the Regulation), including dissemination (Article 2-ter, paragraph 4, letter b) of the Code), it is noted that the Guarantor has long provided guidance regarding the prerequisites (and the occurrence of these, the specific modalities) for the lawful publication, including online, of acts and documents containing personal data (see provision of May 15, 2014 No. 243 “Guidelines on the processing of personal data, including in administrative acts and documents, carried out for the purposes of publicity and transparency on the web by public entities and other obligated entities” web doc No. 3134436).

In this regard, it should be noted - given, in any case, the prohibition on the dissemination of health-related data - that the Guarantor has clarified on several occasions that even the possible presence of a specific publicity regime (a circumstance that does not occur in this case) cannot entail any automaticity regarding the online dissemination of personal data and information, nor a derogation from the principles regarding personal data protection (see cited Guidelines). This is also confirmed by the personal data protection system contained in the Regulation, according to which the data controller must implement “appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that, by default, only personal data necessary for each specific purpose of the processing are processed” and must be “able to demonstrate” - in light of the principle of “accountability” - that they have done so (Articles 5, paragraph 2; 24 and 25, paragraph 2, Regulation).

In this context, before proceeding with the publication of the resolution and the related annexes, the Region should have been aware of the presence of personal data relating to numerous workers and, consequently, should have verified the existence of a legal or regulatory provision that legitimized such publication, in compliance with the principle of lawfulness (see Article 5 of the Regulation).

Also considering that the document subject to publication also contained a reference to a litigation concerning compensation for damages caused to the worker's health due to harassing behavior by the employer, it must be deemed that the dissemination also concerned data related to health, namely the data “related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, which reveal information concerning their health status” (Article 4, paragraph 1, No. 15, of the Regulation; see also recital 35 of the same).

This category of data also includes the mere reference to “any information from which one could infer, even indirectly, the state of illness or the existence of pathologies of the subjects concerned” (see cited Guidelines) as in this case. 

Therefore, such data, although relating to a single worker, must be processed in compliance with the stricter regime applicable to health-related data which, as mentioned, cannot be disseminated (Article 2-septies, paragraph 8 of the Code). 

This principle has been reiterated in numerous decisions of the Guarantor concerning individual cases (see among the
tanti, provision no. 68 of February 25, 2021, web doc no. 9567429; no. 255 of June 24, 2021, web doc no. 9688099; no. 404 of December 1, 2022, web doc no. 9842783; no. 405 of December 1, 2022, web doc no. 9844727; no. 420 of December 15, 2022, web doc no. 9853429; no. 3 of January 11, 2023, web doc no. 9857610).

Although the incident that led to this investigation occurred "during a period in which the Lombardy Region, more than what was happening in other parts of Italy, was in full pandemic emergency. A period in which the Region had to promptly address, in a situation of difficulty, the numerous requests arising from health, social, and economic needs," and although it immediately took action to ensure the definitive removal of the aforementioned personal data from the website upon learning of the news, it must nevertheless be concluded, for the reasons stated above, that the personal data of hundreds of workers (approximately 730), in one case even related to health, were published for an extended period - from XX to XX - on the institutional website of the Region, and freely accessible online, in the absence of a legal basis and in violation of the general prohibition on disseminating health-related data in violation of Articles 5, 6 par. 1 lett. c) and e) and 9 of the Regulation and Articles 2-ter and 2-septies, paragraph 8, of the Code.

However, the initiatives taken by the Region, also involving the Data Protection Officer, through the creation of "a working group to define the correct rules for data retention and removal of data, documents, and information subject to mandatory publication," are acknowledged favorably.

4. Conclusions.

In light of the evaluations mentioned above, it is noted that the statements made by the data controller during the investigation - the truthfulness of which may be subject to accountability under Article 168 of the Code - while deserving of consideration, do not allow for overcoming the objections notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure and are insufficient to allow for the archiving of the present procedure, as none of the cases provided for by Article 11 of the Garante Regulation no. 1/2019 apply.

Therefore, the preliminary assessments of the Office are confirmed, and the illegality of the processing of personal data carried out by the Region is noted, given that the personal data of numerous workers (approximately 730), including data related to the health of one worker, were published from XX to XX on the institutional website of the Region, and freely accessible online, in the absence of a legal basis and in violation of the general prohibition on disseminating health-related data, in violation of Articles 5, 6 par. 1 lett. c) and e) and 9 of the Regulation and Articles 2-ter and 2-septies, paragraph 8, of the Code.

The violation of the aforementioned provisions makes the administrative sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation applicable, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, lett. i), and 83, paragraph 3, of the same Regulation, as also referred to by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code.

In this context, considering that the conduct has exhausted its effects, given that the aforementioned data has been removed from the institutional website of the Region and from the network, the conditions for the adoption of further corrective measures under Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not exist.

5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, lett. i) and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code).

The Garante, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, lett. i) and 83 of the Regulation as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to "impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to [other] corrective measures [provided for in this paragraph], or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case," and, in this context, "the College [of the Garante] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Garante's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code" (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Garante Regulation no. 1/2019).

In this regard, considering Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation, in this case, the violation of the cited provisions is subject to the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation.

The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into due account the elements provided for by Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation.

In relation to the aforementioned elements, the particular sensitivity of the personal data subject to online dissemination concerning the management of the employment relationship and ongoing disputes of numerous interested parties has been considered, as well as, with regard to one worker, also data related to health. The dissemination also occurred over an extended period (from XX to XX). It was also considered that the failure to comply with the indications that the Garante has long provided to all public entities with the aforementioned Guidelines and in numerous provisions on individual concrete cases adopted over the years by the Garante.

On the other hand, it was taken into account that the Region, upon becoming aware of the publication even before the initiation of the investigation by the Garante, took all necessary actions.
necessary to remove the personal data in question from its institutional website and from the network. It was also taken into account that the violation began during a particularly delicate phase (XX) in which the Region was engaged in addressing the specific needs arising from the state of emergency. Furthermore, the initiatives undertaken by the Region were evaluated, including the establishment of "a working group for the definition of the correct rules for data retention and removal of data, documents, and information subject to mandatory publication." There are also previous measures pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, against the Region, regarding relevant violations. 

In light of the aforementioned elements, assessed as a whole, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the administrative fine at €20,000 (twenty thousand) for the violation of Articles 5, 6, paragraph 1, letters c) and e), and 9 of the Regulation and Articles 2-ter and 2-septies, paragraph 8, of the Code, as an administrative pecuniary sanction considered, pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. 

Considering that the personal data subject to online dissemination concerned the personal data of numerous workers, including data related to health, it is also deemed necessary to apply the accessory sanction of publication of this measure on the website of the Authority, as provided for by Article 166, paragraph 7 of the Code and Article 16 of the Regulation of the Authority No. 1/2019. 

Finally, it is noted that the conditions referred to in Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 are met.
