1.1.   Premise  
With act of May 6, 2024 n. 54793 (notified on the same date via certified email), which is to be understood as fully referenced here, the Office initiated, pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 5, of the Code, a procedure for the adoption of the measures referred to in Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation against Illumia S.p.A., (hereinafter “Illumia” or the “Company” or the “Data Controller”), represented by the legal representative pro tempore, with registered office in Bologna (BO), Via de Carracci n. 69/2, VAT number 02356770988.  
The procedure originates from an investigation initiated by the Authority following the receipt of two separate complaints filed against the Company regarding the processing of personal data for telemarketing purposes.  
More specifically, with complaint n. 345203, the complainant alleged that they had been contacted, on behalf of Illumia, for promotional purposes on their mobile number, in the absence of an appropriate legal basis and while registered with the Public Opposition Register (hereinafter also “RPO”).  
With complaint n. 352673, on the other hand, the complainant stated that they had been contacted on their son's mobile number, which is habitually provided as a reference contact for managing their household supplies, by an operator who identified themselves as the “commercial office contact” of Illumia. During the phone call, it emerged that the interlocutor was already aware of the request for a so-called switch to XX (hereinafter also “XX”) actually signed by the complainant concerning their electricity and gas supplies. On the same occasion, the telephone operator also informed the complainant that the activations stipulated with XX could not be successful and that therefore it was necessary to conclude a new contract with Illumia.  
In both cases subject to complaint, the calling number was not registered in the Register of Communication Operators (hereinafter “ROC”).

**3. EVALUATIONS OF THE AUTHORITY**

It should be noted at the outset that, contrary to what the Company claims, the requests for information made by the Authority left no room for interpretation regarding the content of the requested responses. The aforementioned requests were made pursuant to Article 157 of the Code, and this regulatory reference was contained both in the subject and in the body of the communication.

Furthermore, considering that the same heading and the text of Article 157 of the Code explicitly refer to the presentation of documents and that based on the combined provisions of Articles 5, paragraph 2, and 24 of the Regulation, the principle of accountability places the burden on the data controller to demonstrate that the processing operations of personal data are conducted in compliance with the applicable regulations, it does not seem plausible that the Company could have encountered any misunderstanding or innocent interpretative error. 

Secondly, the exceptions raised specifically concerning the complaints subject to the proceeding cannot be accepted either. In relation to complaint no. 345203, the Company noted that the interested party would have registered with the RPO only on February 7, 2024, that is, the day before receiving the unwanted communication. However, considering that from the documentation produced by Illumia it is not possible to ascertain whether this is a first registration or a renewal, and that the same interested party stated in the complaint that, under their own penalty of responsibility, they had joined the RPO "on 27/7, following in early January, and subsequently yesterday 7/02/2024," this exception cannot be accepted.

Illumia then contested the regularity of complaint no. 352673 for violation of Article 77 of the Regulation, arguing that the complaint should have been submitted directly by the son of the complainant in his capacity as the account holder of the number receiving the unwanted call and that it cannot be excluded a priori that the contact was intended to reach the latter. On this point, it is appropriate to highlight that the complainant...
has lamented that the telephone operator was aware of the switch request actually made by her for the electricity and gas services of her home and even of the chosen operator. Therefore, considering the content of the phone call and that the interested party stated that she usually uses her son's number as a reference contact, there is no doubt that the call was directed precisely to the complainant and that for the caller that phone number constituted useful information to uniquely identify the interested party, regardless of the actual ownership of the telephone service. 

The concept of personal data, in fact, as established by the Regulation, does not solely hinge on the nature of the data itself, but rather on its identificatory power, understood as the actual possibility of uniquely revealing the identity of the data subject. 

This is confirmed by Article 4, point 1) of the Regulation, which defines personal data as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, particularly by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person," and Recital No. 26 clarifies that "It is desirable to apply the principles of data protection to all information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. Personal data that are subject to pseudonymization, which could be attributed to a natural person by the use of additional information, should be considered information on an identifiable natural person. To establish the identifiability of a person, it is appropriate to consider all means, such as identification, that the data controller or a third party can reasonably use to identify that natural person directly or indirectly. To ascertain the reasonable likelihood of using the means to identify the natural person, all objective factors should be taken into account, including the costs and time required for identification, taking into account both the technologies available at the time of processing and technological developments (...)."

Applying these principles to the case at hand, it is exceedingly clear that based on the statements made in the complaint, the operator was in possession of a series of information (address, phone number, switch request, chosen operator) that, when used together, were exclusively referable to the complainant. It follows that the complaint contains all the formal and substantive requirements provided for by Article 77 of the Regulation. 

Moreover, it should be added that the recipient of protection in data processing for telemarketing purposes is not only the "contractor" of a telephone service supply relationship but also the "user," understood as "any natural person using a publicly accessible electronic communication service for private or commercial purposes, without necessarily being subscribed to it," as specified by Article 121, paragraph 1-bis, letter g) of the Code. 

In relation to complaint No. 352673, the observations made by the Companies regarding the registration with the RPO are entirely irrelevant, since the complainant did not complain about receiving a promotional call to a registered number, but rather about having received a promotional call in the absence of a suitable legal basis made by an operator unlawfully in possession of a series of information referable to her person. 

The belief of the data controller regarding the circumstance that the lack of observations from the complainants serves to demonstrate "the correct management of the episode" cannot be shared, as it is merely a faculty granted to the interested parties, the non-exercise of which certainly does not equate to a waiver of the request, nor to acquiescence regarding the responses provided by the Company. 

With reference to the obligations imposed on the data controller pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation and the security measures implemented by the Company, from the examination of the documentation and the allegations transmitted as a whole, a picture of privacy governance emerges that is not fully compliant and updated with respect to current legislation. 

Moreover, this observation assumes particular gravity when considered in light of the circumstance that the teleselling activity, by the Company's own admission, is entirely outsourced to a significant number of agencies and sub-agencies. This is not to imply that outsourcing or the number of partners constitutes a violation in itself, but rather that the choice to entrust a branch of one's business to a series of external subjects should also be accompanied by the implementation of adequate measures to avert the risk of unlawful activities.
**Regarding teleselling activities, the company's compliance with personal data protection regulations is based on the Technical Regulation for Agency Acquisition, the latest update of which dates back to April 3, 2020.**  
The examination of this regulation and the associated procedures fully confirms the culpa in eligendo and the disputes raised concerning the supplier selection process.  
Except for the acquisition of documentation proving registration with the ROC and for a solitary reference to the purchase of "fubbate" lists, until December 2023, the only requirements evaluated by the Company during the agency recruiting phase, in accordance with the regulation and using the Agency Acquisition Checklist, concerned merely dimensional characteristics and the economic reliability of the Company. In fact, by Illumia's own admission, only from January 1, 2024, has a specific privacy checklist also been implemented. Furthermore, only some of the current agencies – six out of seventeen – were contracted in 2024, so the remaining agencies were selected and continue to process personal data in the absence of a prior and careful evaluation pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation. This omission cannot even be remedied by the provision of a probationary period and express resolutory clauses in case of violations of personal data protection regulations.  
The letter and the wording of the clause included in the contractual forms regarding the probationary period (see Article 10.2 of the agency contract: "In order to verify the mutual convenience of making this contractual bond stable or resolving it, the Parties agree to establish a probationary period of six months. This period will commence from the first month following the Agent's transmission of 50 ILLUMIA contracts, and during this period and from the date of signing this contract, each party may, at any time, withdraw at will from the relationship without any obligation of notice or substitute indemnity. If the probationary period expires in the last quarter of the year, the expiration of the probationary period is automatically extended to January 15 of the following year"), clearly and exclusively pertains to a concept of economic convenience. However, even if one were to accept a broader interpretation, such a clause could in no way fulfill the obligation imposed on the data controller to only engage qualified subjects in the field of privacy, as it is evidently inadequate to avert the risk that personal data processing carried out in the name and on behalf of Illumia is performed in the meantime by subjects who do not possess adequate capabilities and who have not implemented suitable security measures.  
Similarly, the fact that only in 2023 the application of the aforementioned clause led to the termination of five contracts is a symptomatic indicator of the inadequacy of the supplier selection and evaluation process.  
With specific reference to the disputes regarding the obligation of supervision and monitoring of the actions of data processors (so-called culpa in vigilando), the Company has illustrated the contractual obligations imposed on the agencies and has stated that it conducts random on-site audits and document checks. However, for the reasons that will be extensively discussed below, the measures, although commendably adopted, do not fully comply with the current regulatory framework, nor are they sufficient in relation to the risks to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.  
First of all, it must be observed that the mere formal provision of operational instructions, obligations, and indemnity clauses cannot be considered as fulfillment of the obligations imposed on the data controller when not accompanied by periodic and effective monitoring and verification initiatives. Furthermore, the choice to carry out monitoring activities using criteria that result in identifying an excessively limited number of subjects or merely related to the mere occurrence of anomalies and reports does not appear to be justifiable.  
With the entry into force of the European Regulation, among other things, the concept of accountability has been introduced into the Italian legal system, a principle with multiple implications and, in many respects, disruptive. This principle, which undoubtedly reflects the influence of common law systems, proves to be entirely innovative. With the Italian legislator, in fact, the citizen is accustomed to knowing exactly the scope of the obligations and prohibitions imposed by law. Conversely, the principle of accountability grants the data controller a wide latitude of choice regarding the implementation of their governance in data protection matters, to which a complex concept of responsibility is intimately linked. Evidence of this innovative view of the matter is the very formulation of the security norms, which no longer list the minimum measures to be adopted but impose on the data controller the obligation to assess and identify the appropriate measures in relation to the context, the state of the art, and the risks.  
Within this new conception of the role itself...
the holder, the principle of accountability must necessarily be considered as a common thread that informs the interpretation and application of all the rules and principles contained within the Regulation and therefore an indispensable hermeneutic criterion. 

As a result, the obligations imposed on the data controller pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation, when interpreted in light of the principle of accountability, cannot be deemed effectively fulfilled by mere provisions of standard clauses or by actions taken only ex post following an anomaly, but impose a quid pluris, that is to say, the actual governability of the processing chain and the periodic updating of the same technical and organizational measures implemented to achieve it. 

Moreover, while maintaining the validity of a risk-based approach, the criteria used to identify the sample of subjects to be scrutinized in this case appear to be questionable. Considering that only a minimal part of the interested parties is usually inclined to send reports and that the interested party may not realize that they have entered into a contract based on an unlawful contact, the criteria used by Illumia prove to be ineffective and misleading, as they lead to identifying an excessively narrow number of subjects to be scrutinized. 

Furthermore, Illumia's choice to act only upon reaching a predetermined threshold of anomalies does not seem entirely justifiable. The obligations and responsibilities outlined for the data controller indeed impose the necessity to act even in the presence of a single anomalous episode or an isolated report. 

On the other hand, the obligations and instructions given to the data processors also confirm a not fully complete assimilation of the obligations imposed by the current legislation and that certain teleselling activities are carried out in the absence of an appropriate legal basis and the conditions provided by law. 

The instructions attached to the agency contract, in the part where it states that “(...) The Agent is obliged to consult monthly, and in any case prior to the start of each promotional campaign, the Register of Oppositions, and to update their lists, verifying that the personal data to be contacted have not included their telephone contact(s) in the same, established by D.P.R. no. 178 of 2010, and also extended to mobile numbers with Law no. 5/2018, in order to avoid making offers to those who are listed in the Register; The Agent acknowledges and accepts that the consultation of the Register of Oppositions mentioned above is valid for fifteen days (...),” proves to be contradictory and in any case does not take into account the different temporal value of the consultations at the RPO provided by law depending on the means used for the realization of marketing activities (i.e. telephone, email, postal mail, etc.). Similarly, the control procedures for the sources of the personal data used for marketing campaigns explicitly aim to “ascertain that each telephone number present in the contactable list purchased by the Agent refers to a person who has validly consented to the communication of their contact data and its subsequent use for commercial and marketing purposes” and, on one hand, do not distinguish between prospects, clients, and former clients, and on the other hand, do not consider the case of data coming from public registers (cf. Articles 129 and 130 of the Code and Article 1 of Law no. 5/2018). 

From the documentation and the information overall acquired, it finally appears established that there has also been a violation of the principles of integrity, security, and confidentiality established in Articles 5, 25, and 32 of the Regulation. 

While the proceduralization of a contract loading system based on the creation of customized profiles managed by individual operators and protected by passwords is commendable, the Company has not provided any suitable element to prove the adoption of measures aimed at preventing the risk of activating contracts originating from an unlawful contact. More specifically, while it is true that Illumia seems to be able to trace back from the contract to the operator who entered it into the company systems, at the same time it has failed to implement measures and precautions that prevent its entry upstream (e.g., instructions on password management, measures to prevent simultaneous use of the account by multiple subjects, measures suitable for revealing anomalous access based on time, frequency, or geographical area, etc.). 

In this regard, the Company itself has stated that some feasibility assessments are underway regarding the implementation of a tracking system for access to the portal for entering contracts. However, considering the peculiarities of Illumia's sales chain and in light of the current historical-social context, the state of the art, and the principles consistently established through the measures deliberated by the Authority, the failure to implement such a basic and essential safeguard confirms that the processing operations are not being carried out in
in such a way as to ensure adequate security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing, loss, destruction, or accidental damage. For the reasons extensively illustrated, ultimately, the responsibility of Illumia regarding the alleged violations must be confirmed through the communication of the initiation of proceedings pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 5 of the Code.

4.   CONCLUSIONS  
Based on the above, it is deemed established that Illumia is responsible for the following violations:  
a) Articles 5, paragraph 2 and 24 of the Regulation for failing to fully comply within the preliminary investigation of this proceeding with the burden of proving that the processing operations are conducted in accordance with the regulations on the protection of personal data;  
b) Articles 5, 6, and 7 of the Regulation, as well as Article 130 of the Code, for having conducted the promotional contacts subject to complaint in the absence of an appropriate legal basis;  
d) Articles 5, paragraph 2, 24, and 28 of the Regulation for the failure to implement measures and procedures suitable to ensure that in the case of outsourcing processing, only subjects that provide sufficient guarantees to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures are selected so that the processing meets the requirements of this regulation and ensures the protection of the rights of the data subject (so-called culpa in eligendo);  
e) Articles 5, paragraph 2, 24, and 28 of the Regulation for the failure to implement instructions, measures, and procedures fully compliant with the applicable regulations and suitable to ensure effective oversight of the actions of the Data Processors (so-called culpa in vigilando);  
f) Articles 5, 25, and 32 of the Regulation for the failure to implement appropriate technical and organizational security measures to prevent the risk of contracts arising from unlawful contact, as well as access to company systems by unauthorized persons.  

It has also been established that the conduct of the Company regarding the processing under examination is unlawful, thus it is necessary to:  
- issue a warning to Illumia pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter b) for failing to fully comply within the preliminary investigation of this proceeding with the burden of proving that the processing operations are conducted in accordance with the regulations on the protection of personal data;  
- impose on Illumia, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter f) of the Regulation, the prohibition of any further processing of data belonging to the complainants;  
- order Illumia, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter d) of the Regulation, to adopt technical and organizational measures suitable to ensure full compliance with the obligations imposed on the data controller pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation;  
- order Illumia, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter d) of the Regulation to adopt technical and organizational measures suitable to prevent the risk of contracts arising from unlawful contact and access to company systems by unauthorized persons;  
- adopt an injunction order, pursuant to Articles 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and 18 of Law No. 689/1981, for the application against Illumia of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraphs 3 and 5, of the Regulation.  

5.   INJUNCTION ORDER FOR THE APPLICATION OF THE ADMINISTRATIVE PECUNIARY SANCTION  
The violations indicated above necessitate the adoption of an injunction order, pursuant to Articles 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and 18 of Law No. 689/1981, for the application against Illumia of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraphs 3 and 5, of the Regulation (payment of an amount up to € 20,000,000.00 or, for companies, up to 4% of the annual worldwide turnover of the previous financial year, if higher).  

For the determination of the maximum penalty of the pecuniary sanction, it is therefore necessary to refer to the turnover of Illumia, as derived from the latest available financial statement (March 2023) in accordance with previous measures adopted by the Authority, and thus this maximum penalty is determined, in this case, to be € 27,155,872.00.  

For the determination of the amount of the sanction, it is necessary to take into account the elements indicated in Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation;  
In this case, the following are relevant:  
1)   the severity of the violations (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a) of the Regulation), taking into account the subject...
and the purposes of the processed data, related to the complex phenomenon of telemarketing, regarding which the Authority has adopted, particularly in the last three years, numerous measures that have thoroughly examined the multiple critical elements, providing the data controllers with numerous indications to align the processing with the current regulations and to mitigate the impact of nuisance calls on the affected parties;  
2) as a mitigating factor, pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 2, letter d), the technical and organizational measures already implemented by the data controller pursuant to Articles 25 and 32 of the Regulation, such as, by way of example, the use from January 1, 2024, of a specific privacy checklist for the selection of agencies, the implementation of a system of random checks on the lists used and on contracts, the creation of personal accounts protected by passwords for access to systems by third parties.  
Based on the complex of the elements mentioned above, and the principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and deterrence provided for by Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, and taking into account the necessary balancing between the rights of the data subjects and the freedom of enterprise, also in order to limit the economic impact of the sanction on the organizational and functional needs of the Company, it is deemed that an administrative sanction of payment of an amount of €678,897.00, equal to 2.5% of the maximum applicable fine, should be applied to Illumia.  
In this case, it is considered that the accessory sanction of publication on the Authority's website of this provision, as provided for by Article 166, paragraph 7 of the Code and Article 16 of the Authority's Regulation No. 1/2019, should be applied, taking into account the nature of the processing and the conduct of the Company, as well as the risk elements for the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.  
In implementation of the principles of Article 83 of the Regulation, the imposition of such an accessory sanction appears reasonable and proportionate in relation to the severity and particular disvalue of the conduct subject to censure, with specific reference to the duration of the established violations and the number of subjects involved.  
The implementation of a supplier selection procedure that also addresses the issue of personal data protection with considerable delay compared to the issuance of the Regulation, which moreover granted two additional years to data controllers for compliance, reveals a culpable and ongoing insensitivity to the matter.  
Furthermore, considering the duration of the violations, the significant number of agencies that the Company employs for telemarketing activities, as well as their widespread presence throughout the national territory, the audience of subjects involved in the violation appears particularly broad.  
Finally, the prerequisites of Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 concerning internal procedures with external relevance, aimed at carrying out the tasks and exercising the powers delegated to the Authority, are met.
