Legion where he serves (hereinafter, the "Command  
Legion"), a certified email message was received,  
containing an attachment with the subject "Report of conduct [name and  
surname of the complainant]," signed by the President

(also in the capacity of President of the Coordination of  
OPIs of Friuli Venezia Giulia – hereinafter, the  
"Coordination") and by the Presidents of the OPIs of Udine  
and Trieste. With this note, the Command Legion was

information regarding the complainant's wife  
(profession; registration with the Order) and the  
assessments expressed by these OPIs regarding the

that even from the perspective of the ordinary citizen,  
have no purpose or utility," having as

great commitment from the administrative offices,

diverting precious time and also  
considerable economic resources from the ordinary  
activities of the Orders." The OPIs have, therefore, "deemed it  
appropriate to submit to the [Commander of the Command Legion] the

3. Outcome of the investigative activity.
3.1 The processing of personal data relating to the profession and employer of the complainant.
Public entities, in accordance with the principle of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency" (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a), of the Regulation), may, as a rule, process personal data if the processing is necessary to comply with a legal obligation or for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or related to the exercise of public powers (see Articles 6, paragraph 1, letters c) and e), and paragraphs 2 and 3 of the Regulation, as well as Article 2-ter of the Code).

In this case, the Order obtained information regarding the profession of the complainant by querying a search engine and consulting publicly available documents on the Internet. Such online search operations, consultation, and registration of information related to the
**Profession and Employer of the Claimant**

Constitute personal data processing (see art. 4, nos. 1 and 2 of the Regulation), which, however, cannot be considered necessary for the purposes of the administrative proceedings related to the requests for civic access and the reports submitted by the claimant to the Order, regardless of the fact that such information was available on the Internet due to its publication by the interested party or third parties for other purposes not related to the institutional functions of the Order (see provisions of November 24, 2022, no. 385, web doc no. 9839018; March 10, 2022, no. 82, web doc no. 9761383; January 13, 2022, no. 7, web doc no. 9745807; July 2, 2020, no. 118, web doc no. 9440025; February 13, 2020, no. 35, web doc no. 9285411).

The Order has, therefore, processed the personal data related to the profession and employer of the claimant in a manner that is not compliant with the principle of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency" and in the absence of a legal basis, in violation of articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), and 6 of the Regulation, as well as 2-ter of the Code.

**3.2 Communication of Personal Data to Other OPI of Friuli-Venezia Giulia**

From the documentation in the records and the statements made during the investigation, it is also established that the Order shared with other OPIs of Friuli-Venezia Giulia the information related to the requests for civic access and the reports submitted by the claimant and his spouse, carrying out a processing of personal data that cannot be considered necessary for the purposes of the proceedings initiated in relation to the aforementioned requests for access and reports.

In this regard, it should be noted, with considerations that apply to the overall processing of personal data subject to complaint, that the defense argument of the Order, according to which "the access proceedings under Legislative Decree 33/2013 [...] are subject to publication by the recipient entities and are available online as public data, with the identification of the requester," cannot be accepted. As clarified by the National Anti-Corruption Authority (ANAC) in its Guidelines adopted in agreement with the Guarantor, the "register [of access requests, where established,] contains the list of requests with the subject and date and the related outcome with the date of the decision and is published, obscuring any personal data that may be present [...]" (paragraph 9 of Determination no. 1309 of 28/12/2016 containing the "Guidelines providing operational indications for the definition of exclusions and limits to civic access under art. 5, paragraph 2 of Legislative Decree 33/2013," in G.U. no. 7 of 10/1/2017 and at https://www.anticorruzione.it/-/determinazione-n.-1309-del-28/12/2016-rif.-1; see also paragraph 8.2.b. of the Circular of the Minister for Public Administration no. 1 of 2019, containing "Implementation of the rules on generalized civic access (so-called FOIA)," at http://www.funzionepubblica.gov.it/sites/funzionepubblica.gov.it/files/Circolare_FOIA_n_1_2019.pdf).

The Order has, therefore, communicated the personal data of the claimant and his spouse to the aforementioned OPIs in a manner that is not compliant with the principle of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency" and in the absence of a legal basis, in violation of articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), and 6 of the Regulation, as well as 2-ter of the Code.

**3.3 Communication to the Command Legion of the Personal Data of the Claimant and His Spouse Contained in the Joint Note of the Presidents of the OPIs of Friuli Venezia Giulia**

With note prot. no. XX of XX, signed by the President of the Order, the President of the OPI of Pordenone (also in the capacity of President of the Coordination) and the Presidents of the OPIs of Udine and Trieste, and addressed to the Command Legion, where the claimant performs his work activity, the Order communicated to the Command Legion some personal data of the claimant, namely information related to the requests for documentary access and the reports submitted by the claimant to the aforementioned OPIs and other public entities, the evaluations of the OPIs regarding the appropriateness of such requests and reports, and the overall conduct of the claimant, as well as information related to the marital relationship existing between the claimant and a professional registered with the Order.

With the note in question, personal data related to the claimant's spouse were also communicated to the Command Legion, particularly regarding the fact that she had also submitted requests for access and reports, as well as the marital relationship existing with the claimant.

That said, it should be noted that the communication to the Command Legion of the personal data in question cannot be considered necessary to fulfill legal obligations or to perform a task in the public interest or related to the exercise of public powers (see articles 6, paragraph 1, letters c) and e), and paragraphs 2 and 3 of the Regulation, as well as 2-ter of the Code). 

Indeed, the argument of the Order, according to which such processing of personal data would have been necessary for the pursuit of the legitimate interest of the Order to ensure the proper functioning of its administrative action (see art. 97 of the Constitution), cannot be accepted. This is because the legal basis of legitimate interest, as per article 6, paragraph 1, letter f), of the Regulation, "does not apply to the processing of data carried out by public authorities in the execution of their tasks" (article 6, paragraph 1, last sentence, of the Regulation). In this regard, the Court of Justice of the European Union has stated that "from the wording of article 6, paragraph 1, second subparagraph, of the [Regulation], it is clear that a processing of personal data carried out by a public authority in the context of the execution of their tasks..."
processing of personal data, information regarding the processing in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language. This information must include, at a minimum, the identity and contact details of the data controller, the purposes of the processing, the legal basis for the processing, and the recipients of the personal data (art. 13, par. 1, of the Regulation).

In the case at hand, the Order failed to provide the complainant and their spouse with the necessary information regarding the processing of their personal data, thereby violating the principles of “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency” as established in the Regulation. The lack of such information constitutes a breach of the obligations set forth in articles 5, par. 1, lett. a), and 13 of the Regulation, as well as article 2-ter of the Code.

3.6 The absence of a data protection impact assessment.

According to article 35, par. 1, of the Regulation, a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) is required when a type of processing is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. The DPIA must assess the necessity and proportionality of the processing and help manage the risks to the rights of data subjects.

In this instance, the Order and the other OPI did not conduct a DPIA prior to the processing of the complainant's and their spouse's personal data, despite the high risks associated with the processing of sensitive personal data. This omission constitutes a violation of article 35 of the Regulation, which requires that such assessments be carried out to ensure compliance with data protection principles and to mitigate risks to individuals.

4. Conclusion.

In light of the aforementioned violations of the Regulation and the Code, it is evident that the processing of personal data carried out by the Order and the other OPI was not in compliance with the applicable legal framework. The lack of a valid legal basis, the failure to establish a co-controller agreement, the omission of necessary information, and the absence of a DPIA all contribute to a significant breach of data protection obligations.

Therefore, it is imperative that corrective measures be taken to address these violations and to ensure that future processing activities are conducted in accordance with the principles of data protection as outlined in the Regulation and the Code.

Article 29 Working Party, “Guidelines on Transparency under Regulation 2016/679”, WP260 rev.01, adopted on April 11, 2018, and endorsed by the European Data Protection Board with “Endorsement 1/2018” of May 25, 2018).

In this regard, it should be noted that the privacy notice regarding the processing of personal data published on the official website of the Order (see Annex 8 to the note of XX) refers to the processing of personal data of users of that website, and is therefore irrelevant to the personal data processing that is the subject of the complaint. Nor does it appear that the Order had published a general notice on its website regarding the processing of personal data carried out in the context of the performance of its institutional duties and administrative activities, to which the complainant could have referred. 

The Order has consequently acted in a manner inconsistent with the principle of “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency” and in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 13 (in relation to data obtained directly from the data subjects) and 14 (in relation to data not obtained directly from the data subjects) of the Regulation. 

3.6 Response to the data subject's request to exercise the right of access to personal data. 

Article 12 of the Regulation provides that the data controller must provide the data subject with information regarding the action taken in response to a request under Articles 15 to 22 of the Regulation without undue delay and, in any case, no later than one month from the receipt of the request (paragraph 3). 

If the data controller does not comply with the request of the data subject, they must inform the data subject without delay, and no later than one month from the receipt of the request, of the reasons for non-compliance and the possibility of lodging a complaint with a supervisory authority and seeking judicial remedy (paragraph 4). 

From the complaint and the documentation attached to it, it emerges that, on XX, the data subject submitted a request to the Order, pursuant to Articles 12 and 15 of the Regulation, for access to their personal data and the information referred to in Article 15, paragraph 1, of the Regulation. 

The Order has stated that it responded to the aforementioned request “by means of communication dated XX,” attaching (see Annex 10 to the note of XX) a copy of a note dated XX, with protocol no. XX (reasonably to be understood as “XX”) regarding “access to administrative documents,” with which the Order, following “note no. XX of XX from the Commission for access to administrative documents,” provided the data subject with “all documentation concerning them.” 

That said, it should be noted that the note in question does not pertain to the request for access to personal data submitted by the complainant on XX, but rather to a request for access to administrative documents submitted by the same complainant pursuant to Law 241/1990. 

The Order has also failed to provide the data subject with the information required under letters a) to h) of Article 15, paragraph 1, of the Regulation. Moreover, in the access request in question, the data subject had expressed their particular interest in obtaining “the information, if held, regarding their employment status and the profession performed, naturally communicating, also for this type of data, the source of the same (i.e., the subject or specific source from which it was obtained)” (see access request attached to the complaint; see note from the complainant dated XX, cited, where it is stated that in the aforementioned note dated XX “nothing is indicated regarding the communications between the OPI of Gorizia and the other regional OPIs”). 

By failing to respond to the data subject's request to exercise the right of access, the Order has therefore acted in violation of Articles 12, paragraph 3, and 15, paragraph 1, of the Regulation. 

Furthermore, since the Order did not inform the data subject without delay, and no later than one month from the receipt of the request, of the reasons for non-compliance and the possibility of lodging a complaint with a supervisory authority and seeking judicial remedy, the Order has also violated Article 12, paragraph 4, of the Regulation. 

4. Conclusions. 

In light of the above assessments, it is noted that the statements made by the data controller during the investigation – for which they may be held accountable under Article 168 of the Code – while deserving of consideration, do not allow for overcoming the objections notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure and are insufficient to allow for the closure of the present procedure, as none of the cases provided for by Article 11 of the Garante Regulation no. 1/2019 apply. 

Therefore, the preliminary assessments of the Office are confirmed, and the unlawfulness of the personal data processing carried out by the Order is noted, having acted in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 6, 12, paragraphs 3 and 4, 13, 14, 15, paragraph 1, and 26, paragraphs 1 and 2, of the Regulation, as well as Article 2-ter of the Code. 

Considering that the violation of the aforementioned...
provisions occurred as a result of a single conduct (same treatment or treatments connected to each other), Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation applies, according to which the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation. Considering that, in this case, the most serious violations, which are multiple, related to Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 6, 12, paragraphs 3 and 4, 13, 14, and 15, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, as well as Article 2-ter of the Code, are subject to the sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation, as also referred to by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code, the total amount of the sanction is to be quantified up to €20,000,000.

In this context, considering, in any case, that the conduct has exhausted its effects, given that the Order has declared that it has provided the interested party with a response to the request to exercise the right of access to personal data pursuant to Article 15 of the Regulation, albeit after the notification by the Authority of the administrative violation contestation, the conditions for the adoption of further corrective measures under Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not exist.

5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i, and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code).

The violation of the cited provisions is subject to the application of an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to the combined provisions of Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i), and 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation. The administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into due account the elements provided for by Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation.

Considering that:
the violation concerning the unlawful communication of the complainant's personal data to the Legion Command, where the complainant performs his work activity, is characterized by particular seriousness, considering the possible repercussions for the interested party in the workplace (cf. Article 83, paragraph 2, letter a), of the Regulation);
although in order to preserve the effectiveness and efficiency of the administrative action, deemed compromised by the repeated requests for civic access and the reports submitted by the complainant and his wife, moreover during a period when the Order was particularly burdened by the obligations related to the verification of the vaccination requirement for healthcare professionals (no longer required following the issuance of Decree Law No. 162 of October 31, 2022, and starting from November 1, 2022), the Order has exceeded its institutional competencies (cf. Article 83, paragraph 2, letter b), of the Regulation);
the processing did not involve data belonging to special categories as referred to in Article 9 of the Regulation or data related to offenses as referred to in Article 10 of the Regulation (cf. Article 83, paragraph 2, letter g), of the Regulation),
it is believed that, in this case, the level of seriousness of the violation committed by the data controller is medium (cf. European Data Protection Board, “Guidelines 4/2022 on the calculation of administrative pecuniary sanctions pursuant to the GDPR” of May 24, 2023, point 60).

That said, it is considered that, for the purposes of quantifying the sanction, the following circumstances should be taken into account:
the Order has offered good cooperation with the Authority during the investigation (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter f), of the Regulation);
there are no previous relevant violations committed by the Order (Article 83, paragraph 2, letter e), of the Regulation).

By reason of the aforementioned elements, evaluated in their entirety, and considering that the Order is a small public entity, with limited financial and organizational resources, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the pecuniary sanction at €5,000 (five thousand) for the violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 6, 12, paragraphs 3 and 4, 13, 14, 15, paragraph 1, and 26, paragraphs 1 and 2, of the Regulation, as well as Article 2-ter of the Code, as an administrative pecuniary sanction deemed, pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.

Considering that, as highlighted above, the Order communicated the personal data in question to the complainant's employer, with potential repercussions for him in the workplace, and that, therefore, the processing involved in this context a vulnerable subject (cf. Recital 75 and Article 88 of the Regulation and “Guidelines on Data Protection Impact Assessment and the criteria for determining whether processing 'presents a high risk' under Regulation 2016/679”, WP 248 of April 4, 2017), it is also believed that the accessory sanction of publication on the Authority's website of this provision, provided for by Article 166, paragraph 7 of the Code and Article 16 of the Authority's Regulation No. 1/2019, should be applied.

Finally, it is noted that the conditions referred to in Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 exist.
