3. Outcome of the investigative activity.

3.1 The regulatory framework

The regulation on the protection of personal data provides that public entities, within the workplace context, may process the personal data of the data subjects, including data related to special categories, if the processing is necessary, in general, for the management of the employment relationship and to fulfill specific obligations or tasks provided by law or by Union or Member State law (Articles 6, paragraph 1, letter c), 9, paragraph 2, letter b) and 4, and 88 of the Regulation). Processing is also lawful when it is “necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller” (Article 6, paragraphs 1, letter e), 2 and 3, and Article 9, paragraph 2, letter g), of the Regulation; Article 2-ter of the Code (in the text prior to the amendments made by Legislative Decree No. 139 of October 8, 2021).

European legislation provides that “Member States may maintain or introduce more specific provisions to adapt the application of the rules of […] the regulation concerning processing, in accordance with paragraph 1, letters c) and e), determining more precisely specific requirements for processing and other measures to ensure lawful and fair processing […]” (Article 6, paragraph 2, of the Regulation). In this regard, it is noted that the operation of disseminating personal data (such as publication on the Internet) by public entities is permitted only when provided for by a legal norm or, in cases provided for by law, by regulation (see Article 2-ter, paragraphs 1 and 3, of the Code, in the text prior to the amendments made by Legislative Decree No. 139 of October 8, 2021).

The data controller is required, in any case, to comply with the principles regarding data protection, among which are “lawfulness, fairness, and transparency” as well as “data minimization,” according to which personal data must be “processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject” and must be “adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed” (Article 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and c), of the Regulation).

3.2 The dissemination of personal data.

As evidenced by the documents and statements made by the data controller, as well as the findings made based on the elements acquired following the investigative activity, the Region has published on its institutional website the decrees D.D.G. No. XX of XX and No. XX of XX, containing the personal data of 115 data subjects, employees of regional entities.

During the investigation, the Region stated that the publication of the lists (containing the names of the data subjects and the amounts paid to them) attached to the aforementioned decrees is based on the provisions that require the publication of acts of grant, subsidies, contributions, and the allocation of economic advantages (Articles 26 and 27 of Legislative Decree No. 33 of 2013).

In this regard, it is noted that Article 3, paragraphs 19 and 20 of Regional Law No. 27 of 2016 regulates the procedures for the stabilization of precarious personnel. This provision, on which the published lists are based, particularly regulates the modalities for exercising an option right by employees of regional public entities, within fixed-term employment contracts, allowing them to choose between the stabilization of the contract, and thus the permanent hiring of the aforementioned workers (Article 30 of Regional Law No. 5 of 2014), or alternatively the payment “of a comprehensive allowance amounting to 5 years of the salary already being received, to be paid in annual installments” (see Article 3, paragraph 19, Law No. 27 of 2016). In this context, within the framework of regulatory measures aimed at overcoming precariousness, taking into account the needs of the administration and the enhancement of acquired skills, the provision of the aforementioned “comprehensive allowance” in favor of the employee constitutes the monetary quantification as “comprehensive” compensation in lieu of permanent hiring due to the exercise of an option right by the interested parties as provided by regional law. To confirm this, it is highlighted that the amount of this “comprehensive allowance,” as reported in decree No. XX of XX, corresponds “to five years of the salary being received to be paid in annual installments.”

The National Anti-Corruption Authority – ANAC, regarding the acts referred to in Articles 26 and 27 mentioned, clarifies that “these are all those measures that, based on current legislation, are aimed at supporting a subject both public and private by granting them a direct or indirect economic advantage exceeding 1,000 euros through the provision of incentives or benefits that result in reductions, savings, or acquisition of resources.”

That said, in acknowledging the evaluations made by the Region since the preparation of the acts in question, it must be noted that the subsequent removal from the website of the aforementioned decrees and attachments, following the receipt of the warning issued by certain interested parties through their legal representative, does not appear to be consistent with the asserted obligation of such publication. In any case, regarding the aspects of competence, it should be emphasized that even the administrations and other subjects that believe they are subject to the specific obligations of publication must ensure strict compliance with the principles regarding the protection of personal data as well as the applicable sector regulations that constitute the legal basis for the specific processing (Articles 5 letter a) and 6 paragraphs 2 and 3 of the Regulation and Article 2-ter of the Code).

In this context, in light of the combined provisions of Articles 26 and 27 of Legislative Decree No. 33 of 2013, for the purposes of fulfilling the aforementioned obligation, publication is required “in addition to the act of granting, […] the name of the company or entity and their respective tax data or the name of another beneficiary; […] the amount of the economic advantage granted; […] the norm or title on which the attribution is based; […] the office and […] official or manager responsible for the relevant administrative procedure; […] the method followed for identifying the beneficiary; […] the link to the selected project and the curriculum of the appointed subject.”

In this specific case, however, although the Region proceeded with publication on the assumption of being subject to the obligation under the aforementioned Articles 26 and 27, it appears to have published types of personal data not attributable to those expressly identified by the referenced sector regulations, such as, in particular, the place and date of birth and the tax code of the interested parties, and it must therefore be considered, in this regard, that such personal data – referring, moreover, to a significant number of interested parties, namely 115 individuals, including the complainants – have been disseminated in the absence of an appropriate legal basis (see also “Guidelines on the processing of personal data, including in acts and administrative documents, carried out for the purposes of publicity and transparency on the web by public subjects and other obligated entities" of the Guarantor, provision of May 15, 2014, No. 243, web doc No. 3134436, paragraph 9 letter e)).

Finally, it is noted that the reference to Article 7-bis of Legislative Decree No. 33 of 2013 is also not relevant since “the reuse of personal data published pursuant to the aforementioned Articles 26 and 27 is not free, but subject – as established by the same Article 7 of Legislative Decree No. 33/2013 – to specific provisions […], which do not in any way prejudice the level of protection of individuals regarding the processing of personal data” (see also the aforementioned Guidelines paragraph 6).

For all the above reasons, it must be concluded that the Region has engaged in the dissemination of personal data in the absence of an appropriate legal basis, in violation of Articles 5 and 6 of the Regulation and Article 2-ter of the Code (in the text prior to the amendments made by Law Decree No. 139 of October 8, 2021).

4. Conclusions. 

In light of the evaluations mentioned above, it is noted that the statements made by the data controller during the investigation – the veracity of which may be subject to accountability under Article 168 of the Code – although deserving of consideration, do not allow for overcoming the observations notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure and are insufficient to allow for the archiving of this procedure, as none of the cases provided for by Article 11 of the Guarantor Regulation No. 1/2019 apply. 

Therefore, the preliminary evaluations of the Office are confirmed, and the unlawfulness of the processing of personal data carried out by the Region is noted for having disseminated, through online publication on its institutional website, personal data relating to numerous interested parties (115) not provided for by the sector regulations on transparency, in violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letters a) and c) and 6, paragraphs 1, letters c) and e) and 2 and 3, letter b) of the Regulation and Article 2-ter paragraphs 1 and 3 of the Code (in the text prior to the amendments made by Law Decree No. 139 of October 8, 2021). 

The violation of the aforementioned provisions makes the administrative sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation applicable, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i), and 83, paragraph 3, of the same Regulation, as also referenced by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code.

5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code). The Guarantor, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to “impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to the [other] [corrective] measures referred to in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case” and, in this context, “the Board [of the Guarantor] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Guarantor's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code” (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Guarantor's Regulation no. 1/2019). 

In this regard, taking into account Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation, in this case, the violation of the aforementioned provisions is subject to the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation. The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into due account the elements provided for by Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation. 

In relation to the aforementioned elements, the sensitivity of the personal data, subject to online dissemination of numerous interested parties, concerning matters related to the employment relationship of employees who had exercised the right of option instead of permanent employment, as well as the date and place of birth and the tax code of each interested party, has been evaluated. It was also considered the failure to comply with the indications that, for some time, the Guarantor has provided to all public entities since 2014 with the aforementioned Guidelines and in numerous provisions on individual concrete cases adopted over the years by the Guarantor. 

On the other hand, considering that the violation did not concern particular categories of personal data and that the Region acted on the assumption that it was required to publish the personal data of the interested parties - also referring to the place and date of birth and the tax code - based on the obligations established by Articles 26 and 27 of Legislative Decree 33 of 2013 and subsequently removed the aforementioned decrees from the website, albeit following a “warning and formal notice” from the Lawyer of some interested parties. It was also considered that a previous provision against the Region was adopted pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter a) of the Regulation, in relation to the processing of personal data that, if carried out, could have “likely violated the provisions of the […] Regulation.” 

By reason of the aforementioned elements, evaluated in their entirety, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the pecuniary sanction at the sum of 7,000 euros (seven thousand) for the violation of Articles 5, 6 of the Regulation and 2-ter of the Code (in the text prior to the amendments made by Legislative Decree of October 8, 2021, no. 139), as an administrative pecuniary sanction deemed, pursuant to Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation, effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. 

Considering that the decrees and the related attachments, subject to publication, contained personal data of numerous interested parties concerning a delicate matter related to their employment relationship, it is also deemed necessary to apply the accessory sanction of publication on the Guarantor's website of this provision, as provided for by Article 166, paragraph 7 of the Code and Article 16 of the Guarantor's Regulation no. 1/2019. 

Finally, it is noted that the conditions of Article 17 of Regulation no. 1/2019 are met. 

ALL OF THE ABOVE, THE GUARANTOR declares, pursuant to Article 57, paragraph 1, letter f), of the Regulation, the unlawfulness of the processing carried out by the Sicilian Region, for violation of Articles 5 and 6 of the Regulation and 2-ter of the Code, in the terms set out in the motivation;
