**3. Applicable Regulations.**

**3.1 The Regulatory Framework.**

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016 (hereinafter, the "Regulation"), defines "personal data" as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person" and "data concerning health" as "personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, which reveal information about his or her health status" (Article 4, points 1 and 15, of the Regulation). 

According to the Regulation, the processing of personal data carried out in the public sector is lawful when it is necessary "to comply with a legal obligation."
to the data controller" or "for the execution of a task in the public interest or related to the exercise of public powers vested in the data controller" (Article 6, paragraph 1, letters c) and e) and paragraphs 2 and 3 of the Regulation; Article 2-ter of the Code). 

More generally, European legislation provides that "Member States may maintain or introduce more specific provisions to adapt the application of the rules of this Regulation regarding processing, in accordance with paragraph 1, letters c) and e), determining more precisely specific requirements for processing and other measures to ensure lawful and fair processing (...)" (Article 6, paragraph 2 of the Regulation). 

National legislation has introduced more specific provisions to adapt the application of the rules of the Regulation, determining, more precisely, specific requirements for processing and other measures to ensure lawful and fair processing (Article 6, paragraph 2 of the Regulation) and, in this context, has provided that processing operations consisting of the "dissemination" and "communication" of personal data are permitted only when provided for by a legal norm, regulation, or general administrative acts (Article 2-ter of the Code). 

Regarding special categories of personal data, processing is, as a rule, permitted where "necessary for reasons of substantial public interest based on Union or Member State law, which must be proportionate to the pursued purpose, respect the essence of the right to data protection, and provide for appropriate and specific measures to safeguard the fundamental rights and interests of the data subject" (Article 9, paragraph 2, letter g), of the Regulation), provided that the processing is "provided for by Union law or, in domestic law, by provisions of law or regulation or by general administrative acts that specify the types of data that may be processed, the operations that can be carried out, and the relevant substantial public interest, as well as the appropriate and specific measures to safeguard the fundamental rights and interests of the data subject" (Article 2-sexies, paragraph 1, of the Code). 

The data controller is then required to comply with the principles regarding data protection, including the principle of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency" under which data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation). 

3.2 The processing of personal data carried out by the Institute. 

From the assessment carried out, based on the elements acquired and the facts that emerged following the investigative activity, as well as the subsequent evaluations of this Department, it has been established that the Institute has sent, on several occasions, the calendar of GLO meetings, indicating the initials of the first name and surname of all the students concerned, distinguished by class, to all teachers, students, and parents, as well as to the healthcare personnel invited to participate in the GLO meetings scheduled for the days indicated in the calendar. 

Preliminarily, it is noted that, pursuant to Article 4, paragraph 1, no. 15 of the Regulation, health-related data are considered "personal data concerning the physical and mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, which reveal information about his or her health status." 

Given the definition of personal data and health-related data (Article 4, points 1 and 15, of the Regulation), it is believed that the convening of a GLO meeting for school inclusion, provided for by sector legislation on disabilities, represents in itself information related to the health status of the student for whom it is convened. 

It is also stated that the Authority has traditionally clarified that "the practice followed by some administrations of replacing the name and surname of the data subject with only the initials is in itself insufficient to anonymize the personal data contained in the acts and documents published online. Moreover, the risk of identifying the data subject is all the more likely when, among other things, alongside the initials of the name and surname, further contextual information remains that still makes the data subject identifiable (...)" (see Provision of May 15, 2014, no. 243 containing "Guidelines on the processing of personal data, including in acts and administrative documents, carried out for publicity and transparency purposes on the web by public entities and other obligated entities," in particular paragraph 3). 

The information related to the convening of the aforementioned working group, indicating the name or initials of the first name and surname of the student for whom they were organized, may only be communicated to the parents of the student concerned, to the teachers of the class to which the student belongs, and to the subjects identified by sector legislation, involved in the therapeutic and educational intervention followed by the student himself (see Article 9, paragraph 10, Legislative Decree of April 13, 2017, no. 66; see Provision of April 28, 2022, no. 148, web doc 9777156). 

That said, while taking into account the intention of the Institute, as the data controller, not to make the students identifiable by replacing the name with the initials of the first name and surname, it is noted that the information related to the convening of the aforementioned working group is suitable for disclosing information about the health status of the subjects concerned (see Provision of May 12, 2022, no.
175 doc. web n. 9781912). Furthermore, “identification does not only refer to the possibility of retrieving the name and/or address of a person, but also to the potential identifiability through identification, correlation, and deduction” (Article 29 Working Party, Opinion 05/2014 on anonymization techniques, WP216; regarding the identifiability of initials see also Decision of March 2, 2023, no. 65, doc. web n. 9874480; Decision of February 25, 2021, no. 68, doc. web n. 9567429; Decision of July 2, 2020, no. 118, doc. web n. 9440025; Decision of July 2, 2020, no. 119, doc. web n. 9440042). 

Nor can it be considered relevant, for the purposes of evaluating the overall conduct of the data controller, what was stated regarding the fact that parents, teachers, and healthcare personnel could have been “already made known and/or learned even informally or generically” within the school community. 

In light of the above considerations, the Institute has informed the families of the interested parties, the teaching staff, and some healthcare professionals regarding the information related to the health status of the students (see the definition of “communication” of personal data contained in Article 2-ter, paragraph 4, letter a) of the Code). 

For these reasons, the Institute has carried out a processing of personal data, in violation of Articles 5, 6, 9 of the Regulation and Articles 2-ter and 2-sexies of the Code. 

4. Conclusions. 
In light of the evaluations mentioned above, it is noted that the statements made by the Institute, as the data controller, during the investigation – the veracity of which may be subject to accountability under Article 168 of the Code – although deserving of consideration, do not allow for overcoming the observations notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure and are insufficient to allow for the archiving of the present procedure pursuant to Article 14, paragraph 1, of the Garante Regulation no. 1/2019, as none of the cases provided for by Article 11 therein apply. 

Therefore, the preliminary evaluations of the Office are confirmed, and the unlawfulness of the processing of personal data carried out by the Institute is noted, in violation of Articles 5, 6, and 9 of the Regulation as well as Articles 2-ter and 2-sexies of the Code. The violation of the aforementioned provisions makes the administrative sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation applicable, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i), and 83, paragraph 3, of the same Regulation, as also referenced by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code. 

Considering, in any case, that the conduct has exhausted its effects, the prerequisites for the adoption of further corrective measures under Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation do not exist. 

5. Adoption of the injunction order for the application of the administrative pecuniary sanction and accessory sanctions (Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i and 83 of the Regulation; Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code). 
The Garante, pursuant to Articles 58, paragraph 2, letter i) and 83 of the Regulation as well as Article 166 of the Code, has the power to “impose an administrative pecuniary sanction pursuant to Article 83, in addition to the [other] [corrective] measures referred to in this paragraph, or in lieu of such measures, depending on the circumstances of each individual case” and, in this context, “the College [of the Garante] adopts the injunction order, which also provides for the application of the accessory administrative sanction of its publication, in whole or in part, on the Garante's website pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code” (Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Garante Regulation no. 1/2019). 

Considering that the violation of the aforementioned provisions by the Institute occurred as a result of a single conduct, Article 83, paragraph 3, of the Regulation applies, according to which the total amount of the administrative pecuniary sanction does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation. Given that, in the specific case, all the established violations – Articles 5, 6, and 9 of the Regulation as well as Articles 2-ter and 2-sexies of the Code - are subject to the sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, of the Regulation, as also referenced by Article 166, paragraph 2, of the Code, the total amount of the sanction is to be quantified up to €20,000,000 (twenty million/00). 

The aforementioned administrative pecuniary sanction imposed, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, must be determined in amount taking into due account the elements provided for by Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation.
