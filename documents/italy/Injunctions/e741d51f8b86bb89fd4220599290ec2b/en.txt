PREMISED  

1. Request of the National Council of Notaries.  
With a note dated 3/7/2018, the National Council of Notaries requested this Authority for some clarifications regarding the correct framing of the relationships between credit institutions and notaries when, in the stipulation of loan contracts, some lending banks use the services of a third-party company, “Euro Servizi per i Notai S.r.l.” (hereinafter “ESpN” or “the Company”) for the management of document exchange with notaries, through the IT Platform for Notarial Acts Management, called PIGNA (hereinafter “Pigna” or “portal”).  
In particular, some concerns were raised regarding the role played by this Company in the relationship with the notaries tasked with the stipulation, considering that the operational methods of the aforementioned platform imply the processing of personal data, not only of the borrowers but also of third parties closely related (e.g., seller of the property) as well as data concerning the notaries themselves.  
The requests for clarification particularly concerned two aspects:  
- the role to be assigned - in relation to the management of personal data - to the various subjects who, in different capacities, operate within the Pigna platform (notary, banks, and ESpN);  
- the relationship between the portal and the individual notary. In particular, the Council represented that the notaries tasked (chosen from time to time by the borrowers or, in some cases, by the banks using the platform) access the platform through specific authentication credentials provided directly by the manager, who, in this regard, seems to provide the notaries with “a very concise information”; moreover, it was highlighted that the notaries receive from the portal and via email various communications, “in some cases without even the intervention of the portal being preceded by communications directly from the chosen lending bank” (such as, for example, the communication with which they are assigned the task and the consequent invitation to use the portal for the exchange of pre- and post-stipulation acts with the bank). In particular, some communications coming “from platform accounts” are aimed at providing notaries “a summary view of all individual open positions” and are accompanied “by a table or an Excel sheet in which the various acts in progress or executed are reported, all referring to the same notary, in relation to different banks, with an internal reference code to the portal, the indication of the name of the interested party (the borrower), the interested bank, the type of fulfillment deemed missing, and the time considered appropriate from the moment the portal's regulations requested the availability of the relevant document or element.” In the opinion of the Council, the aforementioned would configure “a case of authentic profiling under Article 22 of the Regulation” by ESpN - the portal manager - which, in this way, would carry out a processing of personal data relating to the notaries, particularly regarding not only the identifying data but also “those that seem to refer to the professional performance and in general to the activity” of the notaries themselves.  

2. The investigative activity: requests for information.  
In order to acquire evaluation elements, the Office initiated an investigation, making requests for information to some of the most important banking institutions that adhere to the platform and to ESpN itself, within which it was requested to clarify the functioning of the aforementioned platform, the type of relationship between the banks and ESpN, as well as the measures taken to comply with the principles and provisions in force regarding the protection of personal data.  
From the elements acquired from the credit institutions and the documentation produced by them, it emerged
**Subject:**

The PIGNA portal is a service activated and made available to credit institutions by ESpN, through which banks and notaries (appointed for the various procedures related to the stipulation of mortgage contracts and their related guarantees) share the necessary documents, both in the phase preceding and in the phase following the stipulation of the contract itself (with the sole exception of the copy of the contract in enforceable form); for this purpose, the credit institutions that use the service sign a service supply contract directly with ESpN or with Centro Istruttorie S.p.a. (hereinafter, CISPA); within the framework of these outsourcing contracts, which are also accompanied by specific data protection agreements, the banks operate as data controllers; for the designation of ESpN as the data processor pursuant to Article 28, not all consulted credit institutions adopt the same solution. In particular, some banks directly designate ESpN as the data processor, while others designate CISPA as the processor, which, in turn, as provided in the service supply contract with the controller, may subcontract certain activities to another entity, including ESpN, which thus expressly assumes the role of sub-processor; the use of the portal by the notary, which is not mandatory, requires the bank to communicate the details of the notary responsible for the stipulation to ESpN, which will provide them with authentication credentials, subject to identity verification and communication of the terms of use of the portal. Through the portal, the bank makes its acts available to the notary, and the notary transmits the preliminary notarial report and other stipulation and post-stipulation documents to the bank. If the notary fails to transmit the complete documentation within the deadlines, the bank - via the portal - will send them a reminder communication. Upon completion of the procedure, ESpN and the bank proceed to the so-called "closure of the position." Based on the elements received from ESpN, it has emerged that: “the services of the portal are offered exclusively to banks and/or on behalf of banks,” which propose to the notary to use the portal, determining the purposes of the processing; “all processing carried out by ESpN in relation to the portal [is] already predetermined by the banks and made available to notaries for the execution of mortgage practices;” “the processing carried out by notaries in relation to individual practices for each bank is performed in the interest and under the ownership of the same bank, as it is a service performed and provided by the bank (through ESpN)”: when the notary accesses their reserved area within the portal, they can use the services of the portal “only within the limits of the documentation made available by and for the individual banks” and it is not possible for the notary “to request ESpN for customizations or different functionalities of the Portal”; ESpN, after receiving from the bank (or through CISPA) the assignment to enable the notary to use the portal, proceeds to create an account for the notary. In this phase, if the notary does not already have access credentials, “ESPN, on behalf of the bank,” after searching for the email address of the notary in question in the professional register of Notaries, sends to them, to the official email address, the credentials for the first access; in this phase, characterized by the management of authentication credentials for the creation of the account assigned to the notary “(username and password, where the username is the official public email address assigned by the notariat),” ESpN acts as the data controller “only for the creation and maintenance of the account and the connection of the same to the exclusive use spaces of the individual banks. […] This pertains only to the ownership of the credentials and access logs of the notary, without any extension to the documents and therefore to the data exchanged between the notary and the individual banks.” In this capacity, ESpN provides notaries with the information pursuant to Article 13 of the Regulation, through publication on the portal. The data collected by ESpN, as the data controller (related to the credentials and access logs), are retained “until the last day of the year following the last date among: the date of account creation; the date of the last access to the portal made by the notary; the date of the last update of the notary's credentials”; the “reports” that the portal periodically sends to notaries to provide them with a summary of the open positions with respect to all banks serve as a reminder, for the exclusive use of notaries and are not viewable by banks in any way (which instead have access to the practices under their responsibility assigned to each notary) and the data contained therein are definitively deleted as soon as the notary completes the upload of the missing documents. This processing would be carried out by the portal, as the processor/sub-processor, on behalf of each bank and would concern personal data related to the financing practices whose ownership belongs, for each practice, to the individual bank that assigned the task to the notary; it follows that the relevant information must be provided to the interested parties (the borrowers) by the bank.

**3. The investigative activity: the initiation of the procedure.**  
The Office, based on the statements made by the parties and the elements acquired during the investigation, with a note dated June 16, 2022, notified ESpN of the initiation of the procedure.
for the adoption of the measures referred to in Articles 58, paragraph 2, and 83 of the Regulation, in accordance with the provisions of Article 166, paragraph 5, of the Code, in relation to the violation of the provisions of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), and 6, paragraph 1, letters a) and b), 13 and 14, paragraph 3, letter b), of the Regulation. In particular, it has been noted that:

a) in the information that ESPN provides to notaries at the time they proceed with registration on the portal (information that is published on the portal itself), the legal basis for the processing has been identified in Article 6, paragraph 1, letter f), of the Regulation (legitimate interest). In this regard, it has been noted that it is necessary to distinguish the processing that ESPN carries out for the registration of each notary (and the creation of the related account) and the related procedure for electronic authentication, from the subsequent processing concerning the generation and storage of access logs to the portal. In fact, while for the latter processing the legitimate interest of the data controller (Article 6, paragraph 1, letter f), of the Regulation) may constitute a suitable legal basis as it is functional to ensuring the security of the networks and information systems of the Company (see recital 49 of the Regulation, and Opinion of April 9, 2014, WP 217 of the Article 29 Working Party), this cannot be said for the processing of personal data related to the registration phase on the portal and the generation of the notary's authentication credentials; such processing is indeed necessary for the provision of a service by ESPN to the notary, with the consequence that the processing of personal data that follows is lawful in relation to Article 6, paragraph 1, letter b), of the Regulation;

b) with reference to the report that ESPN makes available to notaries in order to provide each of them with a summary of the open positions with respect to all banks, which has a "mere reminder function for the exclusive use of the notary," it has emerged that this is an activity of processing carried out by ESPN entirely autonomously - both in decision-making and management - with respect to the banks (which have not expressly requested this processing); therefore, in relation to the processing in question, the Company - instead of acting as the data processor/sub-processor for each bank - operates as an independent data controller. It follows that the processing carried out in the terms indicated above is done without a suitable legal basis and without it being adequately highlighted to the data subjects within the information;

c) the information that ESPN, as the data controller, provides to notaries for the creation and management of authentication credentials and access logs to the portal is provided after the start of the processing and is therefore late; in fact, it is made available to the data subjects on the portal, in that part reserved for which each notary can access, only after the sending, by ESPN, of the email containing the instructions and credentials for the first access; this is in violation of Article 14, paragraph 3, letter b) and of recital 61 of the Regulation.

In a communication dated 15/7/2022, ESPN submitted its defense writings in which, while requesting a hearing, it asked for the closure of the proceedings or, alternatively, the issuance of a warning (with the related confidentiality of the confidential information contained in the memorandum, as they have "the nature of business and/or commercial secrets disclosed exclusively within ESPN and the companies belonging to the MutuiOnline Group") based on the considerations set forth below. In particular:

in relation to the first observation made by the Authority, in contesting the identification of the Company as a digital identity manager, it specified that "in the operation of the portal, the element of the digital identification of the notary is completely absent"; the Company, in fact, "like any other digital platform, is limited to the creation and management of authentication credentials (using the notary's email and generating a temporary password) to allow access and registration on the portal, with the consequent authentication of individual credentials." On the other hand, ESPN believes it does not share the Authority's position that identifies the contractual relationship between the parties as the legal basis for the processing; this is because "within the operation of the portal, not only is there no digital identification service, but there are also no prerequisites for the possible provision of the service, as ESPN does not collect personal data and documents of the notary for their identification. The objective element for the contractual qualification of the relationship between ESPN and the notary is completely lacking." At the same time, ESPN reiterates that the legal basis for the processing in question must be identified in the legitimate interest as it is "functional to ensuring the security of networks and information systems" - ensuring "the protection of individual accesses to the portal and making navigation secure through a security measure" - and "such legal basis [...] is also a condition of lawfulness more compatible with the relationships between ESPN and the notaries (who are directly engaged at the request of the Bank and, in some cases, without prior consultation with the notaries themselves), thus balancing the legal interests of the notaries within the broader framework of the balancing act. This is also due to the fact that [...] the data subject can propose a possible objection pursuant to Article.
21 of the GDPR at any time and if it believes there is a specific reason related to its situation”; regarding the absence of legal basis and transparency for the processing related to reporting, the Company stated that the “service provided by ESPN to the banks creates a contractual obligation with a result obligation on the part of ESPN [...]. Consequently, all activities carried out, primarily and/or secondarily, by ESPN are performed to ensure the result obligation and thus the provision of the main service […]. The report, in fact, represents a means or a method to ensure the achievement of the main obligation that binds ESPN to Centro Istruttorie and the Banks. For this reason, such activity should not be separated from the uniqueness of the contractual relationship but, on the contrary, should be considered as one of the tools with which ESPN provides its service. The resulting processing falls within the scope of the processing covered by the appointment letter as data controller or sub-controller that ESPN signs with respect to the banks or Centro Istruttorie; regarding the last aspect of illegality, the Company communicated that it has acted in the manner indicated by the Authority, providing that the information be given to the notaries at the time of the first communication “or attached to the email of instructions for access to the portal, which represents the first moment of contact with the notaries.” 

During the hearing held on 27/10/2022, the Company, reiterating what was already stated in the aforementioned memorandum, specified that “the context in which the Company operates is characterized by a wide complexity, also of a regulatory nature, which requires continuous monitoring and improvement of the security of the processing carried out within the Pigna platform,” also specifying that: “the identification of legitimate interest as the legal basis for processing has been made by the Company also due to the purpose of IT security (as the authentication credentials are a security measure) and because this choice is aimed at greater protection of the data subject who can, if necessary, exercise the right to object […]”; “in relation to the sending to notaries of reports on the status of processing of the practices entrusted to them, even if the banks have never explicitly requested the Company to send such reports, the activity constitutes a tool through which the Company believes it can fulfill its contractual obligation with the banks themselves.”

4. The Authority's evaluations and the outcome of the investigation. 
At the conclusion of the examination of the statements made by the Company during the proceedings (for the truthfulness of which the author is responsible pursuant to and for the effects of Article 168 of the Code “Falsehood in declarations to the Guarantor and interruption of the execution of the tasks or the exercise of the powers of the Guarantor”) as well as the documentation acquired, the following is represented. 

4.1. With reference to the legal basis for the processing of personal data related to the registration on the PIGNA portal by the notary and the generation and management of the IT authentication credentials used to verify their identity at the time of subsequent accesses to the portal, the Authority observes, first of all, that the Company, while not directly carrying out the identification of the notary (by requesting the presentation and verification of their identity document), relies on the identification carried out by a third party, namely the National Council of Notaries; this is because registration on the portal occurs through the use of the email address assigned to the notary by the Council and published in the relevant register, which is collected by ESpN from the bank or directly from the Council. Conversely, if ESpN did not have sufficient elements to guarantee the identity of the person registering, it would have to carry out its own checks on the identity of the latter to avoid attributing authentication credentials to unauthorized persons to access the PIGNA portal, with the consequent violation of the principle of integrity and confidentiality and the obligations of security of processing (Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter f), and 32 of the Regulation). 

Secondly, it is represented that the processing of personal data in question is necessary to allow the notary to benefit from an IT authentication service functional to access - through appropriate authentication credentials - the Pigna portal for document exchange with the banks; it follows that a contractual relationship is established between ESPN and the notary, and the related processing of personal data therefore finds its condition of legality in Article 6, paragraph 1, letter b) of the Regulation. 

It is noted, moreover, that the right to object - invoked by the Company in its defense writings (and again during the hearing) to support the choice to identify legitimate interest as the legal basis for the processing of the authentication credentials of the notaries - does not appear to be concretely applicable to the processing in question, as the exercise of such right would result in the impossibility of verifying the identity of the notary at the time of access to the portal and, therefore, of allowing the same notary in charge to use it for the management of the documentation related to the mortgage loan practices entrusted to them by the banks.
In light of the above, the processing of personal data related to the registration on the portal by the notary and the generation and management of electronic authentication credentials is carried out by the Company in violation of the general principle set forth in Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) (“lawfulness and fairness”) and the lawfulness requirement provided for in Article 6, paragraph 1, letter b) of the Regulation.

4.2. With reference, instead, to the processing carried out by the Company through the sending of reports to the notaries, it has emerged that these are carried out by the Company itself in full autonomy and outside the instructions received pursuant to Article 28 of the Regulation from the banks which, as data controllers, “have never explicitly requested the Company to send such reports.” 

In this regard, it is highlighted that the processing of personal data must be carried out in compliance with the general principles set out in Article 5 of the Regulation; in particular, paragraph 1 of the aforementioned article of the Regulation establishes that personal data must be “processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently in relation to the data subject (‘lawfulness, fairness, and transparency’)” (Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) of the Regulation). 

Article 6, paragraph 1, of the Regulation also provides that the processing of personal data is lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the conditions for lawfulness indicated therein applies, including, in particular, the explicit consent of the data subject to the processing of their data for one or more specific purposes (Article 6, paragraph 1, letter a), and recitals 40, 42, 43, and 44). 

With particular reference to the principle of transparency referred to in the above-mentioned Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a), this translates into the obligation, on the part of the data controller, to provide the data subject with all information concerning the processing of personal data relating to them, in a manner that is easily understandable, informing them, at the time when the personal data is obtained, also of the purposes and methods of processing and the legal basis for it, as well as all further information necessary to ensure that the processing is fair and transparent in accordance with the provisions of Articles 13 and 14 of the Regulation. 

Article 28, paragraph 3, of the Regulation, in providing that processing by the processor must be governed by a contract that specifically identifies “the duration, nature, and purposes of the processing, the type of data and categories of data subjects, the obligations and rights of the controller,” expressly provides that personal data must be processed “on the basis of documented instructions from the controller” (Article 28, paragraph 3, letter a) of the Regulation). The “Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of controller and processor” adopted by the EDPB on July 7, 2021, provide that “controllers shall give processors instructions regarding each processing activity. […] The processor is limited to what is provided by the controller” (paragraph 1.3.1). It must therefore be excluded that the processor can be granted such a wide margin of autonomy as to allow them to process the collected data for a purpose different and additional to that contractually provided (i.e., the collection activity related to a single case). However, it remains established that the processor, in carrying out their tasks, may act with “autonomy of means and organization,” provided that they respect the instructions received (see in this regard the aforementioned Guidelines where it is established that “the instructions of the data controller may leave a certain margin of discretion [...]; this allows the processor to choose the most suitable technical and organizational means,” paragraph 4).

It is noted, moreover, that based on the provisions of paragraph 10 of the same Article 28 of the Regulation, where a processor determines the purposes and means of processing, they must be “considered a controller of the processing in question.” 

In light of the above, the processing concerning the production and sending of reports is carried out by the Company in the absence of the consent of the data subjects and of an appropriate information notice in this regard, resulting in a violation of the principles set forth in Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 6, paragraph 1, letter a), 13, and 14 of the Regulation.

4.3. With reference to the noted untimeliness of the information provided to the data subjects at the time of access to the portal, the Authority takes note of what the Company stated regarding the regularization of the timing of delivery of the information itself at the time of the first communication “or attached to the email of instructions for access to the portal, which represents the first point of contact with the notaries.”

5. Conclusions: unlawfulness of the processing carried out. Corrective measures pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, of the Regulation. 

In light of the evaluations that precede, it is noted that the statements made by the data controller in the defense writings – the truthfulness of which may be subject to accountability pursuant to Article 168 of the Code – do not allow for overcoming all the observations notified by the Office with the initiation of the procedure and are insufficient to allow for its archiving, as none of the cases provided for in Article 11 of the Garante Regulation No. 1/2019, concerning internal procedures of the Authority with external relevance, apply. 

The processing carried out by ESpN is unlawful in the terms outlined above, in relation to Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 6, paragraph 1, letters a) and b), 13, and 14 of the Regulation.
The violation of the aforementioned provisions entails the application of the administrative sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, letters a) and b) of the Regulation. Furthermore, considering the above-mentioned considerations, it is deemed necessary, should ESPN intend to continue processing data related to notaries for the purpose of sending them the aforementioned reports, to order it, pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter d) of the Regulation, to carry out the processing in question as the data controller, with consequent compliance with all obligations imposed on data controllers by the data protection legislation.

6. Order of injunction.  
The violation of the aforementioned provisions entails the application of the administrative sanction provided for by Article 83, paragraph 5, letters a) and b) of the Regulation. With reference to the elements listed in Article 83, paragraph 2, of the Regulation for the application of the monetary administrative sanction and its quantification, considering that the sanction must be “effective, proportionate and dissuasive in each individual case” (Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation), it is represented that, in this case, the following circumstances have been taken into account:  
a) the nature of the violations has been considered relevant, as they concerned the general principles of lawfulness in the processing of personal data;  
b) the Company, during the proceedings, collaborated with the Authority, providing adequate clarifications regarding the choices made;  
c) the Company has regularized, during the investigation, the profile related to the information to be provided to the data subjects pursuant to Article 14, paragraph 3, letter b), of the Regulation;  
d) there are no previous violations or measures by the Authority pursuant to Article 58 of the Regulation against the Company.  

In consideration of the aforementioned principles of effectiveness, proportionality, and dissuasiveness (Article 83, paragraph 1, of the Regulation) to which the Authority must adhere in determining the amount of the sanction, the economic conditions of the offender have been taken into account, determined based on the revenues reported in the financial statements for the year 2022.  

In light of the aforementioned elements, evaluated as a whole, it is deemed appropriate to determine the amount of the monetary sanction at €5,000 (five thousand) for the violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 6, paragraph 1, letter a), 13, and 14 of the Regulation.  

Finally, it is informed that, as per the regulatory and regulatory provisions of the Office (Article 154-bis, paragraph 3, of the Code; Article 37 of the Regulation of the Guarantor No. 1/2019), a copy of this provision will be published on the Guarantor's website.  

In this context, also considering the type of violation ascertained, it is deemed that, pursuant to Article 166, paragraph 7, of the Code and Article 16, paragraph 1, of the Guarantor's Regulation No. 1/2019, the accessory sanction of publishing the injunction order should be imposed (see Article 38 of the internal regulation No. 1 of 2019).  

It is noted, finally, that the conditions referred to in Article 17 of Regulation No. 1/2019 concerning internal procedures with external relevance, aimed at carrying out the tasks and exercising the powers assigned to the Guarantor, are present.  

ALL OF THE ABOVE HAVING BEEN STATED, THE GUARANTOR  
a) declares, pursuant to Articles 57, paragraph 1, and 83 of the Regulation, the unlawfulness of the processing carried out, in the terms stated in the motivation, for the violation of Articles 5, paragraph 1, letter a), 6, paragraph 1, letters a) and b), 13, and 14 of the Regulation;  
b) pursuant to Article 58, paragraph 2, letter d), of the Regulation, orders the aforementioned Company to conform, within six months from the date of notification of this provision, the processing of personal data related to the sending of reports to the principles indicated in paragraph 4.2.
