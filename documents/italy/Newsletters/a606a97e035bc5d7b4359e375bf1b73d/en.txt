FSE 2.0, Privacy Authority: proceedings initiated against 18 Regions and 2 Autonomous Provinces  
The serious situation reported to the Prime Minister and the Minister of Health in recent days  
It is urgent to intervene to protect the rights of all Italian patients involved in the processing of health data carried out through the Electronic Health Record 2.0.  
For this reason, the Privacy Authority has notified 18 Regions and the Autonomous Provinces of Bolzano and Trento of the initiation of corrective and sanctioning proceedings for the numerous violations found in the implementation of the new regulations on FSE 2.0, introduced by the decree of the Ministry of Health of September 7, 2023.  
In the days prior, the serious situation and the urgency of corrective interventions had been reported to the Prime Minister and the Minister of Health.  
The outcomes of the investigative activity on the FSE, initiated at the end of January, showed that 18 Regions and the two Autonomous Provinces of Trentino Alto Adige - not being in line with what is contained in the decree of September 7, 2023 - have modified, even significantly, the information model prepared by the Ministry, following the opinion of the Authority, which should have been adopted throughout the national territory.  
The discrepancies found have made it clear that some rights (e.g., obscuring, delegation, specific consent) and measures (e.g., security measures, differentiated access levels, data quality) introduced by the decree, precisely to protect patients, are not guaranteed uniformly across the country. Or they are exercisable and enforceable only by patients from certain Regions and Autonomous Provinces, with a potential and significant discriminatory effect on patients.  
Such heterogeneity also contradicts the spirit of the FSE 2.0 reform aimed at introducing homogeneous measures, guarantees, and responsibilities throughout the national territory, thus risking to compromise the functionality, interoperability, and efficiency of the FSE 2.0 system.  
The violations committed by Regions and Autonomous Provinces, with varying levels of severity and responsibility, may result in the application of the sanctions provided for by the European Regulation.  

Telemarketing, Privacy Authority fines Eni Plenitude over 6 million euros  
Of the 747 contracts signed in a "sample week," 657 came from an unlawful contact  
Promotional calls made without the consent of the interested party or directed to numbers registered in the Public Opposition Register and the absence of controls on contracts acquired through unlawful contacts: the Privacy Authority has fined Eni Plenitude 6,419,631 euros.  
The measure comes following 108 reports and 7 complaints against the company, which complained about receiving unwanted phone calls.  
During the investigation, the Authority also requested from Eni Plenitude the data of the purchase proposals made by the sales network and concluded with the activation of energy services, related to a "sample week": of the 747 contracts signed in the identified period, 657 came from an unlawful contact. Numbers that, if hypothetically projected over a year, would lead to 32,850 supplies activated unlawfully.  
In particular, the gaps regarding the control and monitoring of agencies and sub-agencies and the mixing of databases are serious. According to the Authority, to comply with the regulation, it is not enough to remove the individual agent or conduct audit activities in case of anomalies, but measures are needed to prevent contracts signed based on unlawful phone contacts from entering the company systems or to derive economic benefit from unlawful conduct.  
In addition to the payment of the fine, the Authority has imposed on Eni Plenitude a ban on any further processing of the data of the complainants and reporters. The company must also inform the 657 unlawfully contacted individuals of the outcomes of the proceedings based on a text to be agreed upon with the Authority, prepare controls to ensure that contracts generated from unlawful contacts do not enter the company assets, and ensure compliance with the principles of processing, with particular reference to the obligations of updating, deletion, and rectification of personal data relating to customers.
Facial Recognition: Authority Sanctions a
Dealership
No to Illegal Attendance Monitoring
A fine of 120,000 euros has been imposed by the
Privacy Authority on a dealership for violating
the personal data of employees through the use
of facial recognition systems for monitoring
attendance in the workplace.
The Authority intervened following a complaint from
an employee who reported the unlawful processing
of personal data through a biometric system
installed at the two production units of the
company. The complaint also noted the use of
management software that required each employee
to record the repair interventions carried out on
the assigned vehicles, the times and methods of
execution of the work, as well as the downtime
with specific reasons.
From the inspection activity conducted by the
Authority, in collaboration with the Special
Privacy and Technological Fraud Unit of the
Guardia di Finanza, numerous violations of the
European Regulation by the company emerged.
Regarding the processing of biometric data,
the Authority reiterated that the use of such
data is not permitted because there is no legal
provision currently in place that allows for the
use of biometric data for attendance monitoring.
Therefore, the Authority reminded that even the
consent expressed by employees cannot be considered
a valid basis for legality, due to the asymmetry
between the respective parties in the employment
relationship.
The Authority also found that the dealership had
been collecting personal data related to the
activities of employees for more than six years,
using management software to prepare monthly
reports to be sent to the parent company,
containing aggregated data on the time taken by
the workshops for the work performed. All of this
was done in the absence of an appropriate legal
basis and adequate information, which, in the
context of the employment relationship, is an
expression of the principle of fairness and
transparency. In addition to sanctioning the
company, the Authority ordered it to align the
data processing carried out through the management
software with the provisions of privacy legislation.

SMEs, with Olivia 15 Free Courses on GDPR and
Control Tests
The tool will allow data controllers and
processors to verify compliance with privacy
regulations
Data protection within everyone's reach, through
text lessons, video seminars, and questionnaires
to verify acquired skills. This is Olivia, the
free virtual tool developed as part of the
European ARC II project, of which the Privacy
Authority is a partner, presented during the
recent Privacy Symposium in Venice.
Olivia ("general data protection regulation on
Virtual Assistant") has been designed to provide
training opportunities for small and medium-sized
enterprises and to assist them in their
compliance with the European Data Protection
Regulation (GDPR). However, it can also serve as
a useful knowledge tool for all data controllers
and processors in the public sector.
The platform indeed features a series of learning
modules, ranging from basic concepts about the
GDPR to principles and legal bases for data
processing, as well as conditions for the use of
cookies or surveillance systems in the workplace.
Most importantly, the tool, by processing answers
to the questionnaires provided, allows companies
to verify compliance with privacy regulations.
Particularly useful in this regard are the
documentation templates proposed by Olivia
concerning data protection impact assessments
(DPIA) and assessments of legitimate interest,
which represent the most complex legal basis—
especially for an SME—on which to base a
processing, as it requires demonstrating the
prevalence of the organization's interests over
the rights of the data subjects.
Completely free and available in Italian,
English, and Croatian, Olivia will be officially
released in September. Registered users will find
on the platform video recordings of the 10 remote
seminars conducted as part of ARC II and all the
presentations made by the speakers.
The ARC II project, funded by the European
Commission, involves the Croatian Data Protection
Authority, the Privacy Authority, and the
Universities of Florence, Zagreb, and Brussels
(Vrije Universiteit), and aims to simplify
compliance with the Regulation for SMEs, reduce
compliance burdens, and demonstrate how
compliance with data protection regulations can
enhance business activities and build trust
relationships with users and customers.
