Approval from the Privacy Authority for the new Culture Card  
and the Merit Card  
The verification questionnaires must, however, deal with anonymous and aggregated data  
Favorable opinion from the Privacy Authority on the criteria for  
attribution and use of the “Youth Culture Card” and the “Merit Card.”  
The draft regulation, submitted to the Authority by the  
Ministry of Culture, modifies certain aspects of the  
already established Student Card, providing that from 2023 the Card will be replaced by  
two cultural promotion tools for young people, which can be combined: the “Youth Culture Card”  
and the “Merit Card.”  
To access the Culture Card, it will be necessary to be eighteen years old starting from the year 2023 and  
have an ISEE not exceeding 35,000 euros. To obtain the “Merit Card,” one must have  
achieved a high school diploma with a score of 100 out of 100.  
The draft regulation introduces some variations in the rules, including, in cases where the  
beneficiaries do not have full legal capacity, the  
possibility that authentication through SPID or  
CIE can be carried out by the legal representative, using the same methods provided for the  
beneficiary.  
The draft then lists the products that cannot be purchased with  
the bonus, such as subscriptions to channels or  
platforms that offer audiovisual content. However, dance courses are included.  
Regarding the registration of merchants via SPID or CIE on the dedicated  
computer platform, the draft provides that the  
owners or legal representatives of the merchants  
must also indicate the certified email address at the time of registration.  
The Ministry of Culture, in continuity with the procedure already followed during previous  
editions of the initiative, has submitted for the Authority's opinion the draft decree of the  
Secretary General containing the rules regarding the methods and times for managing and  
storing personal data.  
The Authority has deemed that the changes introduced by the new draft do not present particular  
issues from the perspective of personal data protection, and the security measures provided by the  
decree of the Secretary General of the Ministry and the related  
attachments are adequate to the level of risk  
associated with the considered treatments.  
In issuing its favorable opinion, the Authority requested, however, that the  
draft specifies that the treatment carried out through the questionnaires  
administered by the Ministry of Culture to  
beneficiaries and merchants, for the verification  
of the regulatory impact (“VIR”), must  
concern exclusively anonymous and aggregated data.  

Journalism: no to witness data on the online will  
The Authority fines the newspaper 10,000 euros  

A healthcare worker finds their personal data published in an  
online newspaper in a photo depicting the holographic will of a famous actress, who recently  
passed away, to whom they had served as a witness. Unable to obtain its removal, they turn  
to the Authority, which fines the publication.  
The photo accompanied an article that reported the actress's last wishes and the disputes  
over the inheritance. Following the online publication, the  
healthcare worker was recognized and, in the following days, contacted by acquaintances and  
journalists seeking information about the matter.  
The will published online clearly displayed the personal data (name,  
surname, date of birth, residence address, witness status) of the individual and also made  
recognizable the personal data of a second witness present at the drafting of the document.  
Following the complaint from the healthcare worker, the  
publication responded that the identities of the  
witnesses were an integral part of the will and that, in any case, the public interest  
in the news deprived the document of any confidentiality aspect.  
In its decision, the Authority reminded that the  
publication of data of individuals connected to  
the protagonists of a matter of public interest must  
always occur within the limits of the essentiality  
of the information, which journalists are obliged to respect. The Authority emphasized that the  
will, while being a public document, cannot  
be shared with anyone, as access to the register of wills at the Ministry of  
Justice is regulated by sector legislation.  
Moreover, its publication in the online newspaper  
resulted in the dissemination of personal data of  
individuals—such as in the case of the healthcare worker—to an indeterminate multitude of  
people, in violation of the principles of
**Essentiality, including correctness, relevance, and non-excessiveness of information concerning the matter.**  
In light of these reasons and considering that the publication obscured the data of the two witnesses only after the initiation of the proceedings, the Authority imposed a fine of 10,000 euros on the publisher.

**Smart Cities and Data Protection: The Working Document of the Berlin Group**  
The International Working Group on Data Protection in Technology (IWGDPT), known as the "Berlin Group," which brings together representatives from European and non-European authorities, international organizations, and experts from around the world, has adopted a working document on "Smart Cities," to which the Privacy Authority also contributed.  
From traffic control to mobility, from resource management to social services, cities are adopting new and innovative processes to make life and stay more comfortable. However, the path towards "smart" or "connected" cities, with the introduction of new technologies or the adoption of new treatments of pre-existing data, requires significant governance to avoid risks to the rights and freedoms of individuals.  
The working document, through risk analyses, case studies, and recommendations, aims to provide a practical support tool for local administrations, service providers, and regulatory authorities to define solutions that respect the protection of personal data.  
One of the proposed cases concerns the analysis of passenger movements connected to the Wi-Fi of the London transport company. The project, carried out through the immediate pseudonymization of users' personal data and according to the principle of minimization, aimed to monitor crowding at subway stations and improve commuter movements. In this way, the company was able to immediately identify the aggregated data to be used for the purpose, without needing to cross-reference it with other data in its possession, such as that present in subscriptions.  
Another challenging aspect in terms of data protection is the transparency regarding the processing of individuals' data and the exercise of rights by the data subjects. In this regard, the experiences of the Amsterdam Algorithm Register, a site that illustrates all the algorithms used by the administration in the provision of municipal services, and the city of Helsinki, which announced the creation of a dashboard where citizens can manage the consents granted, are particularly interesting.  
Finally, the Berlin Group's recommendations emphasize that data should be processed according to the principle of purpose limitation, in the case of "smart home" devices monitoring public housing, and integrity and confidentiality, concerning the security standards of IoT (Internet of Things) tools.
