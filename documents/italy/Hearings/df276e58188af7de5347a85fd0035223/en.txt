**Memorandum of the President of the Authority for the Protection of Personal Data on the Draft Budget Law 2020**  
**5th Commission (Budget) of the Senate of the Republic**  
**(November 12, 2019)**  

1. **Premise.**  
The draft law contains several provisions of interest from the perspective of personal data protection, deserving of certain considerations for the full compliance of the examined norms with the relevant European and national regulations.  
In this context, we will limit our analysis to the norms worthy of integration or modification, not considering the numerous provisions aimed at legitimizing further processing of personal data, the application of which must, as is known, comply with the relevant European and national regulations.  

2. **Risk Analysis for the Purpose of Preventing and Combating Tax Evasion.**  
Among the norms deserving of particular attention, the first is Article 86, which in the first paragraph, with reference to the data contained in the Financial Relations Archive and for the purposes of Article 11, paragraph 4, of Decree-Law No. 201 of 2011, legitimizes the Revenue Agency—following the pseudonymization of personal data—to utilize technologies, processing, and interconnections with other databases at its disposal, to develop risk criteria useful for identifying positions to be subjected to control and to encourage voluntary compliance. Similar powers are granted, in paragraph 3 of the article, concerning the same data, to the Financial Police, despite the absence of an adequate specification of the related role, also in relation to the activities of the Revenue Agency, as well as the coordination between the Financial Relations Archive and the other databases with which it interconnects, with evident risks of misalignment or duplication of information, as well as a reduction in data quality.  
Regarding paragraph 1, the planned interconnection of databases, particularly functional to the processing of tax risk parameters, does not seem particularly innovative, since this possibility was already implied in the reform of Decree-Law No. 201 of 2011, converted, with modifications, by Law No. 214 of 2011, without the Authority having raised objections on this point or requested the pseudonymization of the data.  
Furthermore, paragraph 1 of the article contains a reference to Article 23 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter: Regulation), concerning the limitations of the rights of data subjects—not to the entire data protection framework that, in any case, the Revenue Agency and the Financial Police are required to respect—which appears likely misleading in the absence of the clarifications, which will be discussed, regarding the proposed amendments to the subsequent paragraph.  
In this provision, it would instead be more useful to include a reference to Article 22, paragraph 2, letter b) of the Regulation, in the part where, for decisions based on automated processing provided for by law, it imposes the introduction of adequate measures to protect the rights and freedoms of data subjects. In this sense, a reference to implementing provisions of the Revenue Agency itself, which, following the Authority's opinion, provide— as also represented in the explanatory report—specific security measures, controls on data quality and logical processing, as well as precautions related to automated processing, could also be appropriate, in order to reduce risks for data subjects, particularly regarding erroneous representations of the contributory capacity.  
These are precautions that, far from undermining the effectiveness of the action against evasion, could instead promote it—correcting potential errors or distortions in the automated decision-making process—and confer upon it, also in the perception of citizens, that stronger legitimacy that a fair combination of technology and "human factor" can ensure for administrative action.  
Regarding the pseudonymization provided for in the same paragraph 1 of Article 86, it should first be noted that personal data subjected to pseudonymization do not lose their characteristic of "personal" data, as they still refer to identifiable natural persons, albeit indirectly, with consequent application of data protection regulations (cf. Article 4, items 1) and 5) and Recital 26 of the Regulation).  
In particular, the use of pseudonymization within the information assets of the Revenue Agency, which, constantly increasing, already contains billions of detailed information regarding every aspect of the private life of the entire population, including minors, does not constitute an effective guarantee. This is for two main reasons.  
First, because, due to the detail of the information that would be associated with the pseudonym instead of the tax code at the data controller, the data subject would still be identifiable.  
Second, because the purposes for which the processing of pseudonymized data would be carried out, namely the identification of positions to be subjected to control and to encourage voluntary compliance, are in themselves aimed at identifying the taxpayer, so that essentially the measure provided would contradict the pursued purpose and result in an unnecessary burden for the Agency.  

3. **Limitations of the Rights of the Data Subject.**  
Paragraph 2 of Article 86 includes the interests protected under the regulations concerning the prevention and combating of tax evasion among the prerequisites that, pursuant to Article 2-undecies of Legislative Decree No. 196 of 2003 and subsequent amendments, containing the Code regarding the protection of personal data (hereinafter: Code), allow for the limitation or exclusion of the exercise of the rights of the data subject, recognized by the regulations on personal data protection and, in p
articulate, from the Regulation. Such a modification risks, paradoxically, being dysfunctional with respect to the very interests pursued, as well as being of dubious legitimacy if not adequately circumscribed. The same reasons underlying the modification, which are not explicitly stated in the Report, do not appear to be immediately evident, since, as formulated, the provision does not seem to introduce elements of real utility concerning the action of prevention and contrast against tax evasion. Furthermore, the Authority has not encountered any cases of the exercise of rights by taxpayers that have ever harmed the effectiveness of the actions of the tax administration.

It should first be clarified that, far from outlining specific measures aimed at enhancing the effectiveness of the fight against tax evasion, Article 86, paragraph 2, of the draft law introduces a general limitation on the rights exercisable by citizens in any procedure, even merely administrative, relating generally to the matter of prevention and contrast against tax evasion, also excluding the possibility of filing a complaint with the Guarantor.

The prohibition on the direct exercise, by the interested parties, of rights concerning their own data appears, first and foremost, to be in contrast, to say the least, with the Statute of the Rights of the Taxpayer (Law No. 212 of 2000), which intended to regulate the relationships between the tax administration and taxpayers according to principles of maximum transparency. The right of access to information held by the tax administration, in addition to being recognized by administrative jurisprudence, also in favor of third parties, according to the standards of Law No. 241 of 1990—even in relation to data present in the financial relations archive—is, in fact, functional to the correctness of relations with the taxpayer, who is directly called upon to declare to the tax authorities the information relevant for fulfilling tax obligations. This is especially true with the introduction of the pre-filled declaration, which is based precisely on making available to the taxpayer the information in the possession of the tax administration.

Moreover, prohibiting (or even just limiting) the exercise, directly by the interested parties, of the right to rectify inaccurate data risks hindering the detection of errors in the assessments leading up to tax audits, which could result in a false representation of the taxpayer's ability to contribute, thus diverting and weakening the effectiveness of the fight against tax evasion. 

The limitation of the right to directly request the deletion of data, for example, unlawfully acquired, far from facilitating the fight against evasion, risks prolonging unlawful conduct, thereby exposing the administration to significant compensation claims as well as substantial administrative penalties. 

It is therefore unjustifiably burdensome to allow the exercise of such rights solely through the Guarantor, pursuant to Article 2-undecies, paragraph 3, as this entails timelines and procedures that are quite different from the direct request of the individual to the data controller. 

While these doubts about the functionality and effectiveness of the proposal remain, it should be considered that limitations on the rights of the interested party can only be admitted, by express provision of data protection legislation, if the exercise of such rights could cause actual and concrete harm to the public interests pursued and within the limits of what is "necessary and proportionate in a democratic society" (Article 23 of the Regulation and Article 2-undecies of the Code). 

To prevent excessively heterogeneous evaluations, if not outright incorrect decisions, it will be necessary to delineate, with the same law, cases and conditions that allow for the identification of the aforementioned "actual and concrete harm." This is even more crucial if it is decided to maintain the reference to the prevention (as well as the contrast) of tax evasion among the public interest objectives that legitimize such limitations, considering that while the contrast includes at least, in part, criminal offenses, prevention by definition alludes to a much broader range of behaviors, not necessarily illegal in themselves or otherwise preliminary to more serious offenses.

The scope of the limitations imposed must therefore be adequately circumscribed to make the norm compliant (at least in part) with Article 23 of the Regulation, which constitutes, moreover, a parameter of legitimacy of the internal legislation also pursuant to Article 117, paragraph one, of the Constitution, regulating, in particular, the categories of data involved, the guarantees to prevent various types of offenses, and the risks to rights and freedoms. Any limitation, even if legislatively imposed, cannot indeed undermine – by express provision of the aforementioned Article 23 – the essence of the rights and freedoms considered and must respect the principles of necessity and proportionality.

On this point, especially, the legislator is invited to rigorously evaluate the proposed modification, also to avoid profiles of incompatibility with the relevant European legislation, which would render the norm itself illegitimate. 

In its generality, in fact, the proposed modification risks introducing a limitation exceeding the real needs pursued, hindering the exercise of citizens' rights even in cases where it does not genuinely harm activities functional to containing the risk of tax evasion. 

The comparison with the regulation of limitations on the exercise of the rights of interested parties, provided in the context of money laundering (to which the proposed modification also textually associates the matter of tax offenses, within Article 2-undecies), is significant in this regard. 

Article 39 of Legislative Decree No. 231 of 2007, as modified...
to d.lgs. 125 of 2019, indeed restricts the limitations on the exercise of rights (related to processing) to the sole activities of reporting suspicious transactions and the consequent communications (further information requested by the UIF, data relating to the existence or likelihood of investigations or inquiries regarding money laundering or terrorist financing). The same rights subject to limitation are, in the aforementioned Article 39, selected, as, for example, the right referred to in Article 19 of the Regulation is excluded (with the consequent exclusion of the possibility of limitation), which imposes on the data controller the obligation to communicate to those to whom the data has been transmitted any corrections, deletions, or limitations made to the data itself. This is a useful tool to prevent inaccurate data (and therefore corrected, deleted, etc. from a database) or, worse, unlawfully processed data from remaining in other information systems, posing risks not only for the data subject but also for the effectiveness of the counteraction measures. Naturally, this is a right that finds its rationale in cases where the limitation of the rights of deletion, correction, etc., although abstractly provided for by the aforementioned Article 39, has not been opposed due to the absence of concrete harm to the public interests pursued. 

Therefore, the proposed amendment to Article 86, paragraph 2, should adequately delineate the objective scope of application of the derogatory norm (which, as such, would also be incapable of analogical extension) by indicating at least, pursuant to Article 23 of the Regulation and the general provisions of Article 2-undecies of the Code (paragraph 3, in particular):
- the processing activities (and hopefully also the data controllers) with respect to which the possibility of limiting the exercise of rights is foreseen. It could be particularly useful to refer to some of the processing activities mentioned in paragraph 1 of Article 86 itself—thus enhancing the content of Article 23 of the Regulation, whose reference to the aforementioned paragraph 1 would otherwise be of little functional value—as well as to the processing activities related to evaluations inferred from the analysis of the original data acquired or otherwise non-objective data, concerning judgments or decisions being made;
- the specific rights subject to limitation, further selecting only those whose exercise could jeopardize the activities of (prevention and) counteraction against tax evasion, such as in the case of correction or integration of the evaluative data mentioned above. In this sense, it might be appropriate to exclude from the list of rights subject to limitation the right referred to in Article 19 of the aforementioned Regulation, in analogy with what is provided for in the area of money laundering and due to the need not to retain, in various archives, data that has been, for example, corrected or deleted precisely because, hypothetically, it was inaccurate or unlawfully collected or stored. Moreover, it remains firm what has been stated regarding the inappropriateness of limiting the data subject's right of access and the right to rectify objective data, which is also useful for the correctness of the administrative action based on the analysis of such data;
- the parameters based on which to consider the existence, in the event of the exercise of rights by the data subject, of actual and concrete harm to the indicated needs, in order to better guide, in practice, the application of the norm, avoiding unjustified disparities in treatment.

4. Publicity of public personnel recruitment procedures.
Article 18 of the draft law, in amending Article 19 of Legislative Decree No. 33 of 2013, imposes on subjects required to comply with the transparency regulations (Article 2-bis of the same decree) the publication of the outlines of the tests (no longer only written) and the final rankings of the personnel recruitment procedures, updated with any progression of suitable candidates who did not win. 

In its current formulation, Article 19 limits publication to only the competition announcements for personnel recruitment, the evaluation criteria followed by the examining commissions, and the outlines of the written tests, remaining subject to the specific obligations of legal publicity provided for by other sector regulations, generally relating to final provisions and rankings of proceedings related to competitions, selective tests, and career progressions (cf. Article 7, Presidential Decree of January 10, 1957, No. 3; as well as Article 15, Presidential Decree of May 9, 1994, No. 487, in particular, paragraphs 5, 6, and 6-bis and, more generally, on the publicity of personnel recruitment procedures of public administrations, Article 35, paragraph 3, Legislative Decree of March 30, 2001, No. 165). 

These provisions regulate the timing and methods of publicity (e.g.: posting at the headquarters of the public entity, publication in the administration's bulletin or, for local entities, in the public register) in accordance with the sector regulations, to allow participants in the competitive or selective procedures to exercise the rights recognized to them by the legal system, in relation to the specific administrative procedure considered. 

The proposed amendment would not only duplicate these publicity obligations (thus preserving them), but would also result in a significant change, correlating further and relevant implications due to the inclusion of the required publication within the more articulated context of the regulation of administrative transparency, as outlined by Legislative Decree No. 33 of 2013. The implications related—in terms of indexability, duration of publication, reuse—to the publicity obligations provided for by the transparency decree have been, not coincidentally, one of the elements considered by the Constitutional Court during the scrutiny.
**On the Proportionality and Reasonableness of the Regulations on the Advertising Obligations for Public Officials (Judgment No. 20/2019)**

The Court, in that instance, recalled how "the indexing and free traceability on the web, with the aid of common search engines, of published personal data, is not consistent with the aim of promoting the correct understanding of the conduct of public management and the methods of using public resources. Such forms of publicity rather risk allowing the 'random' retrieval of personal data, also stimulating forms of search inspired solely by the need to satisfy mere curiosity."

The inclusion, within the framework of transparency regulations, of the advertising obligations for the final rankings of personnel recruitment procedures – with the implications that arise regarding the applicability of consequent institutions such as: civic access (art. 5, c.1), indexing (art. 7-bis, paragraph 1, and 9), reuse (art. 7), the duration of the publication obligation (art. 8) – thus raises certain concerns from the perspective of compliance with the principle of proportionality, which has become an essential hermeneutic parameter in this matter according to the jurisprudence of the Court of Justice (see judgments of May 20, 2003, in joined cases C-465/00, C-138/01, and C-139/01, Österreichischer Rundfunk and others, and November 9, 2010, in joined cases C-92/09 and 93/09, Volker und Markus Schecke and Eifert, April 8, 2004, C-203/12 and C-594/12, Digital Rights Ireland; December 21, 2016, Tele2 Sverige, C-203/15 and 698/15, as well as, concerning the European Court of Human Rights, regarding the principle of proportionality in general, see, in particular, section II, judgment of November 28, 2017, application no. 70838-13; Grand Chamber, September 5, 2017, Bărbulescu v. Romania, no. 61496/08).

The necessity of restrictive measures on the right to data protection is, moreover, a principle that the Court of Justice (but also the European Court of Human Rights) has repeatedly emphasized, admitting more invasive measures only in the face of the inadequacy for the purpose of less restrictive systems of the right, since "derogations and restrictions" on fundamental rights must occur "within the limits of what is strictly necessary" (see, among others, CJEU, C-362/14, Maximillian Schrems v. Data Protection Commissioner [GC], October 6, 2015).

The same Constitutional Court, with the aforementioned judgment No. 20 of 2019, has attributed to these principles a significant hetero-integrative function of the reasonableness standard under art. 3 of the Constitution, basing the partial declaration of unconstitutionality, for violation of the thus integrated internal parameter, of art. 14, paragraph 1-bis, of Legislative Decree No. 33 of 2013.

Participation in a competitive selection (and the related outcome), even in the presence of another employment relationship, constitutes a datum that deserves adequate protection (also for the purposes of art. 8 of the Labor Code and art. 10 of Legislative Decree No. 276/2003), according to methods that can balance, in an equitable manner, the principle of administrative transparency and the right to personal data protection.

The provision in question must, therefore, be reconsidered in light of this need for balance and the assessment regarding the actual necessity of such a regulatory change.

Antonello Soro
