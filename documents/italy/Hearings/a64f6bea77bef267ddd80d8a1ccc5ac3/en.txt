Memorandum of the President of the Authority for the Protection of Personal Data on the bill for the conversion into law of the decree-law of January 28, 2019, no. 4 containing urgent provisions regarding citizenship income and pensions (AS 1018) Permanent Commission 11a (Public and Private Employment, Social Security) of the Senate of the Republic (February 8, 2019)

The decree-law under examination introduces important, innovative measures aimed at economic support and the labor and social inclusion of individuals at risk of marginalization in society and the labor market. From the perspective of personal data protection, it is particularly important to focus on the specific provisions governing citizenship income, with respect to which some observations will be formulated.

1 - Firstly, it should be noted that the proposed mechanism for the recognition, disbursement, and management of citizenship income (hereinafter: Rdc) involves large-scale processing of personal data relating to applicants and members of their household (including minors) who are granted the highest level of protection due to their relevance to the most intimate sphere of the person or because they may expose the individual to discrimination. This particularly concerns data related to health status and any imposition of restrictive measures on personal freedom, as well as conditions of hardship, particularly in economic, familial, or social terms. Such a mechanism, as outlined, presupposes a complex and articulated information asset, based on the interconnection of multiple databases, the circulation of highly sensitive information among a plurality of public entities, as well as the monitoring and evaluation of the consumption and behaviors of individual family members of the beneficiary. In this regard, since the opinion required under Article 36, paragraph 4 of the General Data Protection Regulation, which governs the prior consultation of the Authority, was not previously requested, it was not possible to highlight in detail the risks arising from the various processing activities (which, as will be said, affect a large number of citizens, including those who are not interested in applying for the Rdc) - and to identify in advance suitable measures to mitigate them, so as to avoid disproportionate and unjustified limitations on the rights of the individuals concerned in relation to the legitimate public interest objective pursued. The provisions of the decree-law present, in this regard, significant criticalities, some of which may be overcome within the framework of specific implementing measures (currently not foreseen), while others are already in this phase, during the conversion. The processing of personal data, even if carried out by public administrations and aimed - as in this case - at pursuing reasons of significant general interest, must be designed and structured according to the principles of the European Regulation on data protection. The implementation of the Rdc cannot, in fact, circumvent the guarantees of the rights and freedoms enshrined in data protection legislation, to the detriment of the very individuals that this benefit aims to protect. The latter entrusts the national legislator with the provision of the necessary conditions and limits, according to the principle of proportionality, to reconcile the dignity and fundamental rights of the person with general interest needs such as combating fraud and abuse, as well as achieving paths of social inclusion and other active labor policy objectives. In other words, national legislative provisions must be proportionate to the pursued objective, respect the essence of the right to personal data protection, and provide adequate and specific measures to safeguard the rights, freedoms, and legitimate interests of the individuals involved. The regulatory provisions must, therefore, identify with sufficient precision, in accordance with the principles of transparency towards the individuals concerned, data minimization, privacy by design, and default settings: the data controllers, the types of data processed, the subjects to whom they may be communicated, and their respective purposes, as well as data retention periods that are proportionate (and not excessive) in relation to the pursued objectives. In these respects, the regulation of the Rdc, as formulated, does not appear, in several points, suitable to meet the requirements set by European law.

2 - The decree-law contains, in fact, general provisions that are inadequate to define with sufficient clarity the procedures for consultation and verification of the various databases. The public entities involved are not identified with sufficient clarity, nor are the criteria established under which the use of certain categories of information can be deemed justified from time to time, in relation to the specific objectives pursued and in compliance with the principle of proportionality. More specifically, it provides for the establishment of "two digital platforms," respectively at ANPAL and at the Ministry of Labor, in order to enable the activation and management of the Employment Pacts and Social Inclusion Pacts connected to the Rdc, as well as for purposes of analysis, monitoring, evaluation, and control of the benefit granted. These platforms are defined as tools for sharing, respectively, with employment centers and municipalities, the information r
**Relative to beneficiaries residing in the areas of competence (art. 6, paragraph 1). To this end, every piece of information present in the INPS archives and functional to the implementation of the measure is recorded, referring to the members of the beneficiary family units (art. 6, paragraph 3). Any anomalies in the consumption and behaviors of the beneficiaries, reported by employment centers and municipalities, are also communicated to the same platforms (art. 6, paragraph 6).**

**On the other hand, once the request for the benefit is submitted at post offices and Caf, a general verification task is assigned to INPS regarding the possession of the requirements for access to the benefit, based on the information available in its own archives and those of connected administrations, such as the Tax Registry, the PRA, and other public administrations holding the data (art. 5, paragraph 3). At the same time, municipalities verify the requirements of residence and domicile and communicate the results to the aforementioned platforms (art. 5, paragraph 4).**

**Although some provisions of the decree-law delegate detailed regulation to implementing decrees (on which the opinion of the Guarantor must necessarily be requested), they, as formulated, presuppose a massive flow of information among those assisted by greater protection, including those present in the financial relations archive, among different public entities. And this, in the absence of an adequate framework that identifies relevant rules for selective access to databases, introduces measures suitable to ensure the quality and accuracy of the data, as well as technical and organizational measures aimed at preventing the risks of unauthorized access, fraudulent use of data, or violation of information systems, in addition to procedures suitable for ensuring that interested parties can easily exercise their rights.**

**3 - There are also significant critical issues in the regulation of "monitoring" the use of the Rdc card by beneficiaries, who are prohibited from using it to participate in games that involve cash winnings or other benefits. To this end, it is stipulated that all transactions on the card be made available to the digital platforms at Anpal and the Ministry of Labor, through the Mef (art. 5, paragraph 6).**

**It is then delegated to a decree from the Minister of Labor (in agreement with the Mef) the definition of the methods by which the actual enjoyment of the benefit can be verified through the monitoring of expenses made on the card (art. 3, paragraph 15).**

**Equally concerning is the provision that assigns to the operators of employment centers and municipal services the function of monitoring the consumption and behaviors of beneficiaries, as well as evaluating any anomalies from which the non-existence of declared requirements can be deduced (art. 6, paragraph 6).**

**To the activities of centralized and systematic monitoring of purchases made through the card—capable of involving the acquisition of particularly sensitive data—are added the specific checks on individual consumption choices, conducted by the operators of employment centers and municipal services, in the absence of well-defined procedures and legally established criteria. In this context, the legitimate needs to verify any abuses and fraudulent behaviors translate into large-scale, continuous, and pervasive surveillance of card users, thus resulting in a disproportionate and unjustified intrusion into every aspect of the private lives of the interested parties.**

**In this regard, it cannot be overlooked that these provisions are in clear contrast with the guarantees established by data protection regulations—and with a limiting effect on both legislative and administrative discretion—protecting the rights and fundamental freedoms of citizens against various forms of violation. For these reasons, the provisions in question should be implemented following a careful risk assessment, in accordance with what is required by the European Regulation (arts. 25 and 35).**

**In particular, even in the implementation phase, the prerequisites for initiating such monitoring activities must be precisely defined, the types of control identified, the criteria for classifying anomalous behaviors established, as well as the subjects authorized to carry out such activities, the guarantees for the interested parties, and the data retention periods.**

**4 - Finally, some provisions regarding the regulation of the issuance of ISEE certificates (art. 11, paragraph 2, letter d), number 2), raise strong concerns, as they could jeopardize the security of the data contained in the Tax Registry and, above all, in the financial relations archive of the Revenue Agency, which has so far been inaccessible even within the framework of ordinary tax control activities, due to the high risks associated with the processing of such information.**

**A prerequisite for the recognition of the Rdc is the submission of the Single Substitute Declaration (Dsu) (preliminary to the issuance of the ISEE certificate), within which it is necessary to declare to INPS, on the institutional website or through Caf, the personal, income, financial, and asset information relating to all members of the family unit.**

**The regulations in this area establish that, starting this year, the declaration will be pre-filled by INPS, with the collaboration of the Revenue Agency, which, for this purpose, provides the Institute with the information present in the Land Registry and the Tax Registry, including those on financial relations with balances and average holdings of the movable assets of all members of the family unit (art. 10, Legislative Decree No. 147 of 2017). With the pre-filling, the declarant will have co...**
**Yes to own disposal** - on the Inps website or at Caf - not only the information provided by him, but also that contained in the databases of Inps and the Revenue Agency, including data on the balances of financial relationships and the related average holdings, referring to third parties, that is, all members of his household.  
On this issue, the Guarantor had already engaged with the Ministry of Labor, Inps, and the Revenue Agency, representing the indispensable need to ensure appropriate technical and organizational security measures for the protection of such valuable information, both for other members of the household and for third parties.  
It is, in fact, necessary to adequately protect such data, also avoiding even the risk of fraudulent identity substitutions at Caf, or cyberattacks, facilitated by the involvement of the same Caf and their information systems (not always adequately protected) in the processing chain.  
In this context, the provisions of the decree-law are inserted, which, while aiming for simplification, condition the pre-filling of the Dsu on the release of consent, or the absence of a prohibition on the processing of one's data, which each member of the household, if of legal age, can express at Inps offices, on the Institute's or Agency's website, and at Caf (see the new paragraph 2-bis of Article 10 of Legislative Decree 147 of 2017).  
However, the introduction of this complex mechanism of consent/inhibition to processing by the interested parties - which is also not compliant with the requirements of European law, as in this case consent cannot constitute a valid basis for the lawfulness of the processing itself - does not represent an adequate safeguard regarding the security of such information.  
This is especially true if one considers the further paradox introduced by the decree-law in question (see the new paragraph 2-ter of Article 10 of Legislative Decree 147 of 2017) according to which, even in contrast to the aforementioned expression of will of the interested party, in the case of the submission of the DSU in paper form by a member of the household, detailed information regarding any omissions or discrepancies found in the archives of Inps and the Revenue Agency, of all members of the household, including those relating to balances and average holdings of movable assets, must still be returned to the declarant (and therefore also to the Caf) at the time of issuing the Isee certification.  
The aforementioned provisions - independent of the functioning of the citizenship income and therefore likely to affect those who are not interested in such a benefit - should therefore be reformulated.  
The commendable needs for simplification, in fact, cannot be realized in such a way as to jeopardize the security, integrity, and confidentiality of the data contained in the archives of Inps and the Revenue Agency, managed to date in compliance with stringent security measures, also in implementation of specific indications provided by the Guarantor.  
In particular, technical and organizational measures should be introduced to prevent the risks of fraudulent use of data, unauthorized access, or violation of information systems.  

**5 - A final observation relates to the architecture of the Government's website dedicated to citizenship income.**  
It is noted, in this regard, that the site reveals, already in its current state of development, some shortcomings, particularly in the information on data processing and the technical methods of its implementation (which, to date, involve an undue and non-transparent transmission to third parties of navigation data, such as IP addresses and connection times, by visitors to the same site).  
It is necessary that the realization of this tool occurs prior to the adoption of technical measures suitable for effectively implementing data protection principles (particularly the principle of data minimization), integrating the necessary guarantees in processing to reduce risks to protect the rights of citizens.  
Such measures will be even more essential if recourse is made - for the proposed purpose of increasing the efficiency of the program and the allocation of work - to IT tools and platforms provided by entities controlled or supervised by State administrations, or by in-house companies (Article 6, paragraph 8).
