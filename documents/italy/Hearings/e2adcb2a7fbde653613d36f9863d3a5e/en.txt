**Memorandum of the President of the Authority for the Protection of Personal Data on Bill C. 2220, Conversion into Law of Decree-Law No. 124 of 2019, Containing Urgent Provisions on Tax Matters and for Indispensable Needs**  
**Commission VI (Finance) of the Chamber of Deputies**  
**(November 5, 2019)**

1. **Premise.**  
The decree-law under conversion contains several provisions of interest from the perspective of personal data protection, deserving of certain considerations for the full compliance of the examined norms with the relevant regulations in the matter. In this context, we will limit ourselves to analyzing only those norms deserving of integration or modification in conversion or, possibly, in the implementation phase, without considering the numerous provisions aimed at legitimizing further processing of personal data, the application of which must, as is known, comply with the relevant European and national regulations.

2. **Storage and Use of Data Derived from Electronic Invoices**  
Among the norms deserving of some observations, the first is that of Article 14 of the decree-law, which, in amending Article 1 of Legislative Decree No. 127 of August 5, 2015, provides for the storage of electronic invoice files until December 31 of the eighth year following the year of submission of the reference declaration, or until the conclusion of any legal proceedings.  
The data to be stored includes—according to the explanatory report—those concerning the nature, quality, and quantity of the goods and services subject to the transaction, as referred to in Article 21, paragraph 2, letter g) of Presidential Decree No. 633 of 1972.  
They are made accessible—according to what is provided for in paragraph 1 of the article under analysis—to the Financial Police in the performance of economic and financial police functions, as well as to the Revenue Agency and the Financial Police itself for verification and tax risk analysis activities.  
Thus, the scope of use of billing data is extended to all economic-financial police functions assigned to the Financial Police by Legislative Decree No. 68 of 2001 and not only— as under current legislation—limited to tax audits. This modification aims to enhance "the activity of combating any form of illegality, even in sectors other than strictly tax-related, such as public spending, the capital market, and the protection of intellectual property" (Explanatory Report AC 2220, p.19).  
The norm thus extends both the object of storage and the scope of use of billing data, without excluding (as confirmed by the Explanatory Report) certain categories, such as data that are not fiscally relevant or those concerning the description of the services provided, regarding the proportionality of which the Authority had expressed concerns in the measures of November 18 and December 20, 2018, in relation to the draft measure of the Director of the Revenue Agency regarding electronic invoicing.  
While the discretion of the legislator remains in evaluating the functionality of the use of the aforementioned information concerning activities outside the strictly tax-related scope, it is appropriate to make some considerations regarding the number of stored data.  
As mentioned, in the measures of November and December 2018, the Authority deemed the storage of non-fiscally relevant data and those concerning the description of the services provided to be disproportionate, potentially including data capable of revealing health status or the possible subjection of the data subject to criminal proceedings, such as for invoices related to services in the healthcare or legal fields.  
Annually, approximately 2.1 billion invoices are issued, which typically contain data, often very detailed, aimed at identifying—often for warranty, insurance, or commercial practice purposes—the goods and services sold, with descriptions of the services, the relationships between the seller and buyer and other parties, also referring to discounts applied, customer loyalty, consumption habits, in addition to mandatory data imposed by specific sector regulations, particularly concerning transport, energy service provision, or telecommunications (types of consumption, detailed billing, payment regularity, belonging to particular user categories).  
The presence, within electronic invoice files (for example, attachments), of information not relevant for tax purposes—which may also concern particular categories of personal data and personal data related to criminal convictions and offenses referred to in Articles 9 and 10 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, potentially relating to every aspect of daily life—is, moreover, expressly contemplated in the measure of the Agency of April 30, 2018, which indeed provides for a selection of data present in the invoice, subject to storage by the Agency, as they are not relevant for tax purposes.  
With specific reference to the use of electronic invoices for control purposes by the Revenue Agency and the Financial Police, the tax control activities carried out can be categorized into two types: the first based on automated processing and risk analysis (for example, those aimed at detecting inconsistencies between the declared data and those available to the Agency, as well as those related to tax evasion risk analysis) and the other, more analytical, based on a detailed examination of the taxpayer's tax position and tax documentation.  
Automated controls and risk analysis require, by their nature, the storage and massive processing of data extracted from the XML files of invoices (so-called invoice data), among which should not be included...
**Regarding the XML file field containing the description of the operation subject to invoicing, which, in addition to potentially containing significant detailed personal data, as exemplified above, related to the nature, quality, and quantity of the goods and services invoiced, and therefore presenting high risks for the individuals concerned, is not suitable for mass processing, being a free-text and unstructured field that requires, instead, a case-by-case examination of the content.**

**With reference, instead, to the specific checks that may require the analytical examination of invoices, from the documentation provided by the Revenue Agency during the aforementioned opinions of 2018, it appears, in particular, that in the years 2016 and 2017, there were 121,849 and 163,339 checks conducted against VAT taxpayers, respectively, against approximately 4.7 million subjects who submitted the VAT declaration.**

**In light of these elements, the complete archiving of all issued and received invoices, including data that is not fiscally relevant (also, of course, the recipients of the invoiced services) and those related to the description of the services provided, for the purpose of conducting specific checks within the framework of tax assessments and audits, including by the Financial Police, therefore appears disproportionate.**

**As already noted, the obligations to store XML files of electronic invoices by economic operators, even at the Revenue Agency, can instead facilitate remote checks by the Agency and the Financial Police as provided for by paragraph 5 of Article 1 of Legislative Decree 127 of 2015, through the new methods of invoice acquisition that will need to be identified by the ministerial decree to be issued pursuant to the same provision. The authenticity and integrity of each file, essential for the checks, is moreover guaranteed by the Agency's storage of a so-called unique fingerprint of the invoice (hash value).**

**The provision of an obligation to store (and potentially also to use) personal data that is disproportionate – in terms of quantity and quality of information – compared to the actual needs pursued would render the norm illegitimate for violation of the principle of proportionality of data processing, which has become an essential hermeneutic parameter in the jurisprudence of the Court of Justice (see judgments of May 20, 2003, in joined cases C-465/00, C-138/01 and C-139/01, Österreichischer Rundfunk and others, and November 9, 2010, in joined cases C-92/09 and 93/09, Volker and Markus Schecke and Eifert, April 8, 2004, C-203/12 and C-594/12, Digital Rights Ireland; December 21, 2016, Tele2 Sverige, C-203/15 and 698/15, as well as, regarding the European Court of Human Rights, concerning the principle of proportionality in general, see, in particular, Section II, judgment of November 28, 2017, application no. 70838-13; Grand Chamber, September 5, 2017, Bărbulescu v. Romania (no. 61496/08)).**

**The necessity of measures limiting the right to data protection is, moreover, a principle that the Court of Justice (as well as the European Court of Human Rights) has repeatedly emphasized, admitting more invasive measures only in the face of the inadequacy of less restrictive systems for the purpose, since "derogations and restrictions" to fundamental rights must occur "within the limits of what is strictly necessary" (see, among others, CJEU, C-362/14, Maximillian Schrems v. Data Protection Commissioner [GC], October 6, 2015).**

**The same Constitutional Court, with judgment 20 of 2019, has attributed to such principles a significant hetero-integrative function of the reasonableness criterion under Article 3 of the Constitution, basing the declaration of partial illegitimacy, for violation of the internally integrated parameter, of the publicity obligations provided for public officials by Article 14, paragraph 1-bis, of Legislative Decree March 14, 2013, no. 33.**

**In the conversion phase, it will therefore be appropriate, first of all, to assess the actual necessity of the complete archiving of invoicing data, for the duration provided, for the realization of various investigative activities, in the tax and non-tax sectors, for which the use of such information is permitted.**

**It will, in particular, be desirable to obtain from the Government elements suitable to overcome the critical issues already represented in the aforementioned provisions of the Guarantor, evaluating whether the storage of such an extensive number of data is truly functional to the pursuit of the considered purposes and not replaceable with equally effective but less invasive measures or even just with the masking of irrelevant data that may be present in the invoices.**

**The necessary measures aimed at preventing such sensitive informational assets from being exposed to risks of exfiltration or cyberattacks should, moreover, be identified by a regulatory act pursuant to Article 6, paragraph 3, of the Regulation, and not already by the provisions of the Financial Police and the Revenue Agency (see paragraph 5-ter).**

**3. Tax Certifications and Electronic Payments**

**Article 21 of the decree-law adds, within the body of Article 5 of the CAD, paragraphs 2-sexies and 2-septies.**

**The first provision, in particular, extends the functionalities of the platform (so-called "pagoPA"), governed by paragraph 2 of the same article, to include tax certification processes between private parties and, in particular, electronic invoicing and the telematic transmission to the Revenue Agency of data concerning daily revenues from the sale of goods and the provision of services, in replacement of registration obligations.**

**The platform would therefore automatically provide, during payment, the necessary data for the generation of the electronic invoice to the participating merchants and their eventual...**
billing provider, for transactions carried out by end customers using payment cards on appropriately integrated POS systems. The technical rules for the implementation of the regulatory provision will be issued - as stipulated in paragraph 2-septies - by decree of the President of the Council of Ministers or the Minister delegated for technological innovation and digitalization, in agreement with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Revenue Agency. 

Due to the particular significance of the information flow in question and the need to ensure full compliance of such processing with data protection regulations, it is appropriate that this provision be reformulated, specifying at least the role assumed by the Platform manager and the characteristics of the processing intended to be regulated in relation to tax certification processes, for the purposes of electronic invoicing and the storage and transmission of daily receipts. 

In any case, the Guarantor will have the opportunity to express its assessments on the draft DPCM during the opinion to be rendered pursuant to the relevant regulations (see Articles 36 paragraph 4 and 57 paragraph 1 letter c) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679).
