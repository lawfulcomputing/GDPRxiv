Hearing of the President of the Authority for the Protection of Personal Data, Prof. Pasquale Stanzione, on issues related to online consumer profiling  
Parliamentary Inquiry Commission on the Protection of Consumers and Users  
(May 17, 2022)  
- THE VIDEO OF THE HEARING  

I thank the Commission for the attention given to the issue of data protection, which is so crucial for the protection of consumers in a socio-economic context that is inevitably, increasingly projected into the virtual dimension, which, as is now known, has dramatically concrete implications for people's lives.  
And I particularly thank for the discussion, made possible today, on a topic such as online privacy protection, which constitutes one of the key points of European regulation. It indeed underlies the need to prevent data on online behavior from being exploited, through microtargeting, for commercial purposes and (more recently recognized) even on a political-electoral level, as demonstrated by the Cambridge Analytica case referenced in the draft EU regulation on political targeting. Through the improper collection of browsing data, in fact, that digital tracking can be realized which fuels the so-called “extractive” capitalism of platforms and, above all, the power to influence the choices (not only commercial, as we have seen) of internet users, exercised by suggesting content, products, services, with the risk of anticipating and steering their choices.  

One of the tools with which this form of control and, at the same time, nudging is realized is precisely cookies, particularly profiling cookies, which are not strictly technical in nature (given their various possible configurations). If, in fact, they can serve essentially technical functions, allowing, among other things, web pages to load faster, they can also convey behavioral advertising by providing information on the effectiveness of the promotional message, or shaping the type and manner of services provided based on the behaviors exhibited by the user.  

In this context, since 2022, EU Directive No. 58 has established the obligation for Member States to ensure that users have the option to refuse the installation of cookies, subject to technical requirements and those strictly arising from contractual obligations. Directive 2009/136 further strengthened the guarantees in this area, transposed into the text (which has remained unchanged since then) of Article 122 of the Code, which, in accordance with the directive, chooses the more protective paradigm of opt-in for the expression of the user’s will. This, in fact, requires the prior expression (albeit in simplified ways) of informed consent for the activation of non-technical cookies.  

With Provision No. 229, dated May 8, 2014 (followed by clarifications in 2015), the Authority deemed it appropriate to provide useful indications to “identify simplified methods for informing users online about the storage of so-called cookies on their terminals by the visited websites,” as well as “on the methods for acquiring their consent, where required by law.” In particular, the Authority clarified the necessity of obtaining the prior and informed consent of users for the installation of cookies used for purposes other than merely technical ones, without the lack of acceptance precluding access to the site’s content, introducing important simplification measures. It was also clarified that the “place” for expressing the user’s will was the banner, whose appearance on the homepage of the site also allowed for initial information on the purposes of the processing, thus enabling a conscious determination regarding the type of subsequent navigation.  

In this way, a “crossroad” was outlined for providers with two alternatives: a “profiled” navigation freely accepted by the user in which, through the use of cookies, targeted advertising messages could be conveyed based on individual usage habits of the information society services, and a “non-profiled” navigation, with the sending of non-targeted and more contextual advertising messages—based on the general content of the site and not on the observation of the user’s historical behaviors—without penalties in accessing resources for the mere choice made.  

That solution has recently been revised, integrated, and updated, considering both the time elapsed and, above all, the advent of Regulation 2016/679/EU, which, in addition to embedding the main guarantees in the same configuration and setting of systems and devices (privacy by design and by default), strengthens the dispositive power and, broadly speaking, the guarantees of self-determination in which the right to data protection is expressed. With the Regulation, in particular, among the requirements prescribed for a valid expression of will, the unequivocability is added to the already known freedoms, specificity, and prior information. This requirement had, moreover, been emphasized by the jurisprudence, particularly with the CJEU ruling of October 1, 2019 – preliminary ruling in case C-673/17 – rendered in the “Planet 49” case. In that instance, the Court had highlighted the implications of the necessarily explicit nature of consent, as such resulting from an active and specific manifestation of the user’s will, to be excluded in the case of a pre-checked box.

1. **Introduction**
   The purpose of these guidelines is to ensure individual self-determination, access to services, and functionalities without the requirement of user consent for the storage of information or access to information already stored on their device.

2. **Cookie Wall**
   The use of cookie walls, which are digital barriers that allow users to access the site only upon acceptance of cookies, particularly profiling cookies, has been deemed non-compliant with the Regulation.

3. **French Authority Guidelines**
   On October 1, 2020, the French Data Protection Authority (Commission Nationale de l’Informatique et des Libertés, “CNIL”) published an updated version of its guidelines on cookies and similar technologies, along with final recommendations on practical methods for obtaining user consent for the storage or use of non-essential cookies and similar technologies on their devices.

4. **Essential Contents of the Guidelines**
   Key contents of the guidelines include:
   - The ability to refuse cookies with the same ease as granting consent.
   - Greater flexibility for conditions exempting consent for analytical cookies.
   - Case-by-case evaluation of the admissibility of cookie walls.

5. **Regulatory Updates**
   The strengthening of guarantees introduced by the Regulation, along with the evolution of the technological landscape, has suggested an update to the previous 2014 guidelines, as per deliberation no. 231 of June 10, 2021, while maintaining their substantial framework.

6. **Public Consultation**
   The draft deliberation was subjected to public consultation, receiving approximately 50 contributions from stakeholders in business, electronic telecommunications, academia, consumer protection movements, and private citizens.

7. **Information Requirements**
   The document specifies that the information provided must also indicate any other recipients of personal data and the retention periods for the information. It clarifies that the information can be provided through multiple channels and in various formats (e.g., pop-ups, videos, voice interactions).

8. **User Self-Determination Guarantees**
   It emphasizes that the online consent acquisition mechanism must ensure that, by default, no cookies or similar tracking tools are placed on the user's device upon their first access to a website, nor should any other active (e.g., third-party cookies) or passive (e.g., fingerprinting) tracking techniques be used.

9. **Scrolling and Consent Requests**
   Guidelines provide indications regarding scrolling (the action of scrolling down the page to reveal content below the banner containing the so-called short notice) and the reiteration of consent requests to users. Specifically, scrolling is deemed inadequate for collecting valid consent unless it is part of a structured process where the user generates an event that can be recorded and documented on the site’s server, qualifying as a positive action unequivocally indicating consent to processing.

10. **Consent Necessity**
    It is clarified that the installation of cookies or other tracking tools necessarily requires consent, as the legitimate interest basis is inadequate for this purpose, in accordance with the provisions of the draft e-privacy regulation.

11. **Cookie Wall Legality**
    The Authority reiterated the illegality of cookie walls, except in cases where the site offers users the option to access equivalent content or services without consenting to the installation and use of cookies, in substantial alignment with the draft e-privacy regulation.

12. **Simplification and User Choice**
    It is clarified that the repeated presentation of the consent banner upon each new access for users who previously denied consent has no legal basis and leads to "consent fatigue," which risks creating a misleading perception of privacy guarantees. The user's choice must be duly recorded and not solicited again unless the conditions of processing change significantly, it cannot be determined whether certain cookies have already been stored on the device, or at least six months have passed. Users retain the right to revoke consent at any time.

13. **Cookie Coding**
    The guidelines also advocate for a universally accepted coding of cookies, which is currently absent, to objectively distinguish technical cookies from analytical or profiling cookies. In the absence of such prior coding, the Authority has invited publishers to indicate in the information the coding parameters of the respective trackers adopted to enhance transparency guarantees.

14. **Final Guidelines Publication**
    Following the expiration of the deadline for comments on the draft deliberation on January 9, the Authority published the definitive guidelines, which will come into effect on April 1, 2022. These guidelines represent a significant step towards greater protection of users' personal data and a strengthening of their self-determination rights, in line with the provisions of the Regulation.
**Compliance with the Guidelines**

The completeness of compliance will be progressively verified, with the awareness of how relevant it is, for the sustainability of the data economy, to observe the obligations imposed on providers regarding the protection of users' self-determination. The protection of behavioral privacy indeed revolves around the stronger safeguarding of self-determination: informed consent, which to be such must, however, first and foremost be conscious.

This is why, in addition to enforcement activities, the Authority has long been promoting a broad digital education initiative, as a necessary prerequisite for free and, indeed, conscious choices, which are as difficult as they are indispensable in the era of the "zero-price economy," where seemingly free services are instead paid for at the high cost of our data and, therefore, our freedom.
