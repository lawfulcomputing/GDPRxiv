**Hearing of Antonello Soro, President of the Authority for the Protection of Personal Data, on the subject of PDL 1066 and associated "Measures to prevent and combat mistreatment or abuse, including psychological abuse, against minors in nurseries and kindergartens and against individuals hosted in socio-health and social-assistance facilities for the elderly and persons with disabilities, and delegation to the Government regarding staff training" before the Joint Committees I Constitutional Affairs and XI Public and Private Labor of the Chamber of Deputies (October 2, 2018) (text of the intervention)**

1. **Premise**  
I thank the Committee for the request for a hearing and, as a methodological premise, I specify that I will primarily refer to PDL 1066, as it is the subject of the urgency declaration adopted by the Chamber under Article 107, paragraph 1, of the Chamber's regulations, due to its continuity with the unified text approved by the Assembly in the previous legislature.  
Nevertheless, some elements present in PDL 480, particularly regarding the technical characteristics of the video surveillance system, may also be considered.  
On the subject of the proposed laws, we have already had the opportunity to express ourselves in the previous legislature before both branches of Parliament.  
Returning once again to this text (which, in fact, re-proposes what has already been approved by the Chamber) is, however, not only necessary for the purpose of complying with the obligation to acquire the opinion of the Authority and updating the legal references to the new legal framework, but also appropriate due to the particular complexity and relevance of the legal goods involved.  
The regulation of this matter must, in fact, necessarily combine the protection of individuals in conditions of particular vulnerability (such as minors and incapacitated persons) against the (unfortunately not remote) risk of abuse and violence, the need for evidentiary reconstruction of crimes—such as those under examination—for which, in most cases, there are no witnesses able to facilitate investigations, the freedom of the worker in the performance of the service (cf. Article 1 of the PDL), and finally, the right to the protection of personal data of the various subjects captured by the video surveillance system. Not only workers, therefore, but also the very guests of the educational or care facilities considered in the proposal.  
The guarantee of the worker's freedom of self-determination is, moreover, functional to the spontaneity that must necessarily characterize work performances such as those of a training or assistance nature, the quality of which could be prejudiced by the awareness of the interested party of being subjected to constant surveillance, with inevitably negative implications on the educational or care relationship itself.  
The proposed video surveillance system, in fact, while realizing a control, even if not intentional, of the work activity (as the dynamics of the educational or care relationship is the subject of the recording), does not pursue objectives internal to the employment relationship, as in the classic paradigm of remote controls on work activity.  
The external nature of the objective pursued by such processing places, therefore, this case outside the traditional framework already regulated by Article 4 of the Workers' Statute.  
The guarantees established there for the protection of workers (concertative procedure or, alternatively, authorization) remain, in fact, certainly applicable (as provided for by the same Article 4, paragraph 4, of the PDL) from a procedural standpoint, to the extent that the proposed system realizes remote control over work.  
However, the regulatory framework of the Workers' Statute does not exhaust the particular complexity and articulation of this processing, which, while affecting work activity, aims to protect third parties with respect to the employment relationship.  
The excess, in this sense, of the proposed processing compared to traditional schemes must, therefore, lead the legislator to an effort of reflection on the peculiarities of the balancing to be achieved, around legal interests of primary constitutional relevance such as the protection of vulnerable subjects from the risk of possible abuses and the need for evidentiary ascertainment of crimes that may have been committed, as well as the self-determination of the worker.  
The complexity of regulating this matter is, moreover, heightened by the intersection of two distinct data protection disciplines: the General Regulation (with the integration, at the national level, of the Code on data protection, as amended by Legislative Decree 101/18) applicable to the installation and maintenance of video surveillance systems and Directive 680 (transposed by Legislative Decree 51/18), applicable to the phase of access to data acquired by cameras and the related processing by investigative bodies.  
The common denominator of these two disciplines (aimed at regulating, therefore, distinct phases of the overall processing of personal data that is intended to be legitimized) particularly pertains to the foundational principles of the matter and, notably, to the criteria of lawfulness, purpose, and proportionality of the processing in relation to the pursued needs.

2. **Beyond Work Controls: The Peculiarities of These Treatments**  
In summary and reserving the right to elaborate further on what we will observe in detail later, it is a matter of understanding what degree of limitation of the worker's freedom—inevitably resulting from the continuous operation of cameras—can be admitted for the protection of incapacitated individuals entrusted to others' care, as well as for facilitating the possible evidentiary reconstruction in criminal proceedings, which is naturally hindered by the incapacity of almost all potential witnesses of the possible crimes committed.  
In other words, it is a matter of tracing the threshold beyond which...
the fundamental right to the protection of personal data is compressed within that essential core, of which Article 52 of the Charter of Nice prescribes the inviolability, even in the face of the need to protect legal goods relevant to the legal system. 

In this assessment, the issue of the proportionality of the processing in relation to the specific needs pursued comes into play, which, due to the specific context of reference, manifest a different intensity and, therefore, a different capacity to legitimize significant limitations on the right to data protection of workers. 

The proposed text indeed presents undeniable improvements compared to those originally presented in the last legislature:
- the installation of cameras has been made optional rather than mandatory, subject to the consent of guests in the case of social-health or social-assistance facilities,
- provisions have been made for the encryption of collected data and the prohibition of access to the same, which can only be overridden by investigative authorities during investigations,
- a reference has been added to the sanctions applicable in case of violation of the provisions for the protection of data security.

However, the scope of the processing remains quite broad and heterogeneous. 

Video surveillance would, in fact, be permitted in nurseries, kindergartens, as well as in social-health and social-assistance facilities for the elderly and disabled, whether residential, semi-residential, or daytime. 

From this perspective, therefore, further reflection could be conducted on the operational perimeter of the norm, assessing whether all the indicated places actually present a level of risk adequate to justify a significant limitation of the worker's freedom in fulfilling educational or care duties. 

The capacity for expression and awareness of a child in the last year of kindergarten is, in fact, not comparable to that of a child in the first year of nursery, just as the degree of vulnerability (and therefore the risk factor) of the guests in various social-health or social-assistance facilities is extremely heterogeneous. 

This consideration can also be useful for circumscribing the discretion of the choice regarding installation, guiding the authority granted by law to the individual facility based on parameters such as the risk factors inherent to the context of reference, the actual necessity of video surveillance due to the characteristics of the hosted subjects, the duration of their stay, or the specificities of the facility itself. 

In this way, greater respect for the principles of proportionality and necessity of processing would be ensured, ensuring that the use of such an invasive monitoring tool occurs only where other less privacy-limiting measures prove ineffective, thus also guiding the discretion of the holders that would otherwise risk degenerating into arbitrariness.

3. The adaptation to the new regulatory framework

For the purpose of adapting the proposed law to the changed regulatory framework regarding data protection, in addition to the considerations mentioned above, related to compliance with the foundational principles of the matter, it is also necessary to make some amendments to the text, drafted before the initial applicability moment of the new regulation. 

In this sense, in particular, it seems necessary to replace the reference contained in Article 4, paragraph 1, second sentence of the proposed law, to the preliminary verification to be submitted to the Guarantor pursuant to Article 17 of the Code on personal data protection (reserved for processing that presents specific risks to the rights, fundamental freedoms, and dignity of the data subjects), due to the repeal of that provision and the related institution. 

In the new legal framework, processing such as that under examination falls within the category referred to in Article 35 of the Regulation (which prescribes an impact assessment in such cases), as by nature, object, context, and purpose, it can be considered to present a high risk. 

And this – as recognized by the same national legislator with reference to Article 17 of the Code – is due to the potential extent of such processing, the subjects involved (not only workers but also minors and individuals assisted in care facilities, from whom the condition of illness can easily be inferred), as well as the continuity of video recordings. 

In the process of adapting the internal legal system to the Regulation, the national legislator has provided that processing carried out for the execution of a public interest task (such as those under examination), which may present high risks pursuant to Article 35 of the Regulation, must comply with the prescriptions made, “for the protection of the data subject,” by the Guarantor, with a general provision (to be adopted pursuant to Article 36, paragraph 5 (Article 2-quinquiesdecies of the Code, as amended by Legislative Decree 101). 

It should therefore invoke this norm, possibly hypothesizing (in the proposed law itself) that the Guarantor may, with respect to the processing under examination and due to their complexity, adopt a specific general provision pursuant to Article 2-quinquiesdecies, also setting the related deadline. 

In this way, this provision (of the Guarantor) would also absorb that provided for by paragraph 8 of Article 4 of the proposed law, essentially fulfilling its function. 

Among the regulatory parameters to which access, by investigative authorities, to the recordings must conform, pursuant to Article 4, paragraph 3, of the proposal, the reference to Legislative Decree 51 of 2018 should instead be added, which, in transposing Directive 680 on the processing of personal data for police and criminal justice purposes, provides...
principles and guarantees to be observed also, in general, in the investigative field.  
Finally, the reference contained in paragraph 10 of Article 4 to the sanctions applicable in case of violations of the regulations in question remains valid despite the substantive changes to the provisions.  
In particular, the provisions of Articles 166 and 167 of the Code are certainly applicable, which expressly refer, among other things, to Article 2-quinquiesdecies, among the norms whose violation constitutes – provided that the additional prerequisites are met – the offenses regulated therein.  
However, the aforementioned paragraph 10 should be supplemented with reference to the general category of administrative offenses referred to in Article 83 of the Regulation, as well as – with reference to any violations committed by the investigating authorities during access to and processing of recorded data – to the provisions of Chapters V and VI, Section II of Legislative Decree 51/2018.  
These precautions – along with the reflection on the scope of application of the proposed regulation – would certainly contribute to improving the legislative proposal.  
It is, however, desirable that measures aimed at investing, in a preventive manner, in the training of operators are also valued, introducing more articulated control systems that actively involve all personnel and, if necessary, the families themselves without compromising the trust relationship.  
In relational contexts such as those examined – where what matters is the quality of the relationship established between the parties – no camera can ever compensate for inherent shortcomings in the selection and training of personnel responsible for the education and assistance of individuals deserving of greater attention.
