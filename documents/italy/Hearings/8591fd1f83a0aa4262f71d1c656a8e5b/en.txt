**Memorandum of the President of the Guarantor for the Protection of Personal Data on the bill for the conversion into law of the decree-law of October 7, 2020, no. 125, containing urgent measures related to the extension of the declaration of the state of epidemiological emergency due to COVID-19 and for the operational continuity of the COVID alert system, as well as for the implementation of Directive (EU) 2020/739 of June 3, 2020**  
**1st Commission (Constitutional Affairs) of the Senate of the Republic**  
**(October 19, 2020)**  

1. **The Context and Previous Interventions of the Guarantor**  
I would like to first thank the Commission for the requested contribution, which will highlight some particularly interesting aspects of "contact tracing" carried out through the national alert system referred to in Article 6 of decree-law no. 28 of 2020, converted, with amendments, by law no. 70 of 2020.  
The issue of digital contact tracing, developed in Italy through the "Immuni" app, has represented and still represents a decisive matter in managing the emergency, expressing, like few others, the balance between individual freedoms and collective interests, made all the more complex by the pandemic context.  
At stake are two fundamental rights: the right to health, including its meta-individual component of the "interest of the community" in public health, and the right to the protection of personal data, qualified as a "freedom" right, autonomous from the traditional right to respect for private life, as stated in Article 8 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union (CFR).  
Since there can be no 'tyrannical' hierarchies among fundamental rights (Constitutional Court, ruling 85/2013), both the rights to health and to data protection require a balance that preserves their essential content, which Article 52 CFR qualifies as inviolable.  
The search for such balance has indeed represented one of the defining features of the positions taken by the Authority regarding the various provisions adopted in recent months to combat the pandemic: from those concerning the circulation of personal data, including health data, among the subjects involved in prevention actions (Articles 14 of decree-law 14/2020 and, subsequently, 17-bis of decree-law 18/2020) to, specifically, the digital contact tracing system regulated by Article 6 of decree-law 28, which has been shaped according to the indications provided by the Guarantor, starting from the hearing in the Transport Commission of the Chamber on April 8.  
In that context, the essential parameters of legitimacy for contact tracing tools were indicated, primarily identifiable in their functionalization for social utility purposes (containing infections), rather than for repressive-sanctioning purposes, in the necessary partiality—these solutions must integrate into a broader and more complex health prevention strategy—and in compliance with data protection regulations, which is also an indispensable condition for the necessary social trust in such measures.  
The Guarantor then emphasized the need for the contact tracing system to be regulated by law, due to the impact of the measure on individual privacy and the need for a unified regulatory model capable of absorbing (also due to the attribution of the matter to state legislative power) initiatives from local authorities that were then announced as potentially dysfunctional. The system, under public ownership, should be based—emphasized the Guarantor—on a truly voluntary adherence (thus excluding any type of prejudice against those who do not wish to participate), on data not of geolocation but of mere proximity of devices (far less significant and "invasive" than the former), at least pseudonymized, on a decentralized mechanism for storing information, as well as on solid guarantees of cybersecurity. It was also emphasized that the system should be temporary, to be defined in relation to the persistence of the emergency condition and the necessary limitation of data collection to only the data and the period of retention essential for epidemiological purposes.  
With the opinion (of April 29) on the draft provision that would be included in decree-law no. 28/20 for the regulation of the digital contact tracing system, the choice of the Government in favor of a notification system for exposure to significant contacts with positive subjects was positively acknowledged, based on the voluntary adherence of those who download a specific app on their device and on a national alert platform established at the Ministry of Health, with a strong degree of pseudonymization of data, as well as a prohibition on communication of data to third parties.  
Furthermore, the measures provided for by the regulation to ensure the determination and exclusivity of purpose were positively evaluated: tracing must be aimed exclusively at containing infections, excluding further purposes, while maintaining the possibilities of use for scientific and statistical research, provided that within the general terms established by Regulation (EU) 2016/679, thus also enhancing the solidarity-based destination of the data.  
Finally, the provision for the temporariness of the system was shared (as it conforms to the indications already provided), with the interruption of platform activities upon the cessation of the state of emergency and a final safeguard term, as well as with the deletion or definitive anonymization of the collected data.  
With the authorization measure on the impact assessment of June 1, moreover, the Authority, having favorably noted the choice in favor of a local management system for exposure notifications, indicated further measures aimed at strengthening the guarantees of processing also from the perspective of cybersecurity, transparency, and information of the...
**Users.**  
They must be informed, in particular, of non-secondary aspects such as the risk of false positives (or even negatives), as the system cannot distinguish between dangerous contacts that occurred, for example, in the absence of protective devices and contacts, although qualified by duration and proximity, but safe because they took place outdoors or under protective conditions. It should be noted that, strictly speaking, Immuni represents an app functional to a notification system regarding risky exposures, which therefore does not track movements but recognizes events (the qualified and significant contact, at least in abstract terms, from an epidemiological perspective).  
It has also been noted that, in the event of a positive swab, the initiation of the contact tracing system is nevertheless subject to a voluntary choice by the individual, who must provide the healthcare operator with their OTP to allow the system to send alerts to potential infected individuals. This aspect has not been modified by the dPCM of October 18, which merely intervenes on the obligations of healthcare operators.  
This additional piece of regulation (effectively implemented by ministerial decree, on which the opinion of the Garante has also been obtained) represents the completion and final guarantee of the actual voluntariness of the digital contact tracing system, the adherence to which expresses, in every phase and in every consequence, a free (as much as responsible) choice of the individual, with an express legal exclusion of any possible prejudice in case of non-adherence to the system itself.  
More specifically, for the purpose of assessing compliance with the prescribed requirements, with the provision of June 1, the Garante requested the Ministry of Health to provide feedback, within the following thirty days, on the following aspects:  
- precise indication of the algorithm and configuration parameters used by the system;  
- adequate information to users regarding the possibility (as mentioned above) that, due to the particular circumstances of the contact, the notification of exposure does not reflect an actual risk condition;  
- accessibility of the temporary deactivation function of the app;  
- adequate protection of analytics in the backend of Immuni, avoiding any form of re-association with identifiable subjects;  
- clarification, in the information notice, of the operations carried out concerning the Epidemiological Info analytics and the personal data collected in relation to the different categories of interested parties;  
- adequacy of the information and alert message also with respect to the discernment capacity of minor users, even those over fourteen years old;  
- adequacy of the information provided to users regarding the characteristics of the testing phase;  
- integration of the impact assessment and information regarding the exercise of the rights of deletion and opposition;  
- integration, based on the principle of accountability, of the impact assessment with the description of the role and operations attributable to other subjects potentially involved in the Immuni System;  
- measurement of the retention times of IP addresses to the extent strictly necessary for detecting anomalies and attacks;  
- guarantee of tracking the operations performed by system administrators on operating systems, networks, and databases;  
- adoption of technical and organizational measures to mitigate risks arising from the upload of TEK not related to positive subjects due to possible material or diagnostic errors.  
Following the feedback provided (in terms) on June 23, meetings were initiated between the Offices aimed at identifying the best solutions – still under examination by the Authority – to some difficulties that emerged in compliance, particularly with respect to the requirements of points four, nine, ten, eleven, and twelve of the previous list. In the meantime, the need to ensure interoperability has necessitated the adoption of a new impact assessment, which was received by the Authority on October 16.  
Additionally, the Office conducted an analysis regarding the risks – raised by press reports – of vulnerabilities in the system concerning so-called replay attacks, which could generate false exposure notifications. At present, such specific vulnerability (and the consequent need for further measures) does not seem to be identifiable, as this type of attack, presupposing the illicit possession of the mobile device and the consequent modification of the configuration, would be attributable to criminally illicit conduct carried out in the context (which does not seem to be hypothesized as a typical or inherent risk to the system) of a broader series of crimes, including those against information and communication privacy, public faith, etc.  
However, the investigation regarding some cases reported by press reports concerning the failure to activate the procedure for uploading the TEK of individuals who tested positive for Covid-19 as provided by the alert system is still ongoing. The dPCM of October 18, regarding the obligations of healthcare operators concerning patients who have the Immuni app, evidently aims to overcome these omissions.  
Finally, it should be emphasized that the 2020 Data Protection Report, adopted this month by the Council of Europe, regarding digital solutions for combating the pandemic, recognizes the positive contribution made by the Garante in the legislative process and, more generally, in the context of the Government's development of a contact tracing system based on synergy and a fair balance between public health and privacy.  
**2. The amendment introduced by the decree-law**  
In this context, the amendment referred to in Article
Article 2 of the decree-law in conversion, which contains two essential innovations: the provision for interoperability—subject to a privacy impact assessment—of the national alert system with the platforms that operate, for the same purposes, within the territory of the European Union (paragraph 1, letter a), as well as the postponement of the final deadline for the use of the application and the platform—previously set in accordance with the duration of the national state of emergency declaration, in any case no later than December 31, 2020—until the cessation of the needs for protection and prevention of public health, identified by means of a Prime Ministerial Decree (dPCM) and, in any case, by December 31, 2021.

As noted in the explanatory report of the conversion bill, the drafting of the provision was preceded by the opinion of the Data Protection Authority, provided informally, initially with a note dated September 21, which offered some indications on the initially proposed text, to which the Government subsequently conformed in the final drafting of the provision.

In that context, it was particularly emphasized the need to verify the uniformity, in relation to those granted by our alert system, of the guarantees provided by the platforms used in other States. It was also highlighted the necessity to limit, to only what is necessary, the data exchanged, in any case in pseudonymized form, concerning users who tested positive for the virus, with the platforms of other States.

Finally, it was suggested to integrate these additional guarantees into the specific impact assessment to be submitted for the evaluation of the Authority, prior to the commencement of processing.

With regard to the modifications originally proposed to the temporal effectiveness regime of the platform's activity, there was a need to not detach the final deadline from the emergency condition, due to the derogatory nature (and thus exceptional, precisely not ordinary) of the regulation on contact tracing.

As a result of further discussions, the Government then proposed the version (which would later prove to be definitive) of the provision, deemed by the Authority to be shareable for the reasons outlined below.

The provision for interoperability represents the coherent consequence of the conception of the national alert system as part of a European strategy to combat the pandemic. This characteristic of the systems has, in fact, been anticipated since last April with the recommendation (C(2020)2296), relating to a package of common tools of the European Union for the use of technology and data, with which the Commission, in order to achieve a common European approach to the pandemic, urged "interoperability across the European Union" of tracking systems.

On May 13, EU member states, with the support of the European Commission, agreed, within the eHealth Network, on guidelines for the cross-border interoperability of tracking applications within the Union.

The interoperability between tracking platforms within the territory of the European Union was then the subject of the implementing decision (EU) 2020/1023 of the Commission, dated July 15. This decision constitutes the legal basis that legitimizes, precisely in the European context, the interoperability of national mobile contact tracing and alert applications (the so-called federative gateway), concerning the member states that have adhered to this specific form of collaboration.

The interoperability of alert systems thus represents, in this sense, a functional measure both for strengthening the containment activities of infections—as it allows for the reconstruction of the contact chain even in the case of intra-European mobility of the individual—and for the freedom of movement of citizens within the territory of the Union. This right (and principle, at the same time, foundational of the European legal order) risks, moreover, being excessively prejudiced by the pandemic context and, in this way, can be somewhat safeguarded.

The impact that the interoperability of tracking systems inevitably has on the protection of personal data is, moreover, adequately balanced not only by the tendency towards analogy of the guarantees adopted by member countries on the matter—particularly following the aforementioned implementing decision—but also by the prescriptions that the Authority, during the examination of the impact assessment, may issue to strengthen protections at least from an internal perspective.

Regarding, instead, the modifications referred to in letter b) of paragraph 1, the provision of the deadline for the operation of the platform until the cessation of the needs for protection and prevention of public health conforms to the necessity, expressed by the Authority, to anchor the effectiveness deadline, albeit by reference, to the ongoing pandemic condition.

In this sense, the modification made by the decree-law conforms to this model, as it takes into account, among the parameters to anchor the operation of the system, the substantial one related to the persistence of health prevention needs and the formal-normative one of identifying the existence of those needs with a dPCM. The ultimate guarantee is then entrusted to the provision, with a safeguard clause, of the final operational deadline of the system by December 31, 2021.

The postponement of the effectiveness deadline of the system is therefore based on a dual order of criteria. Firstly, public health needs related to the necessity of reconstructing, even digitally, the contact chain are relevant, regarding which the Government assumes the responsibility of declaring its existence with a dPCM. Secondly, the guarantee against the risk of the "normalization of the emergency" is also a critical consideration.
The term "enza" is entrusted to the provision of the deadline of December 31, 2021, by which, indeed, regardless of substantial reasons, the activity of the tracking system must cease. 

Thus described, in extreme summary, the reasons underlying the favorable opinion expressed by the Authority on the draft regulation then proposed and subsequently, as anticipated, incorporated into the text of the decree-law, can only reaffirm the position expressed at that time. Also as a result of the incorporation of the indications provided by the Authority, in fact, both modifications made to the regulation of contact tracing underpin a reasonable balance between public health needs and the protection of personal data, in accordance with that convergence between personalist demands and a solidarity vocation on which our legal system is based.
