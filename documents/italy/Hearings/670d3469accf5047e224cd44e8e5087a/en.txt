2. The Role of the Garante in the Legislative Process
The Garante has actively participated in the legislative process concerning green certifications, providing opinions and recommendations aimed at ensuring compliance with data protection principles. This involvement has been crucial in shaping a framework that respects individual rights while addressing public health needs. The Garante's contributions have included assessments of the primary legislation and the subsequent implementing measures, ensuring that data protection considerations are integrated at every stage.

3. The Importance of Data Protection in Public Health Measures
The relationship between public health measures and data protection is complex and requires careful consideration. The Garante emphasizes that any measures taken must be proportionate and necessary, ensuring that the rights of individuals are not unduly compromised. The implementation of green certifications must be accompanied by robust safeguards to protect personal data and prevent misuse.

4. Future Perspectives
Looking ahead, it is essential to continue monitoring the implementation of green certifications and their impact on data protection. The Garante will remain vigilant in ensuring that any new developments in this area adhere to the principles of transparency, accountability, and respect for individual rights. Ongoing dialogue between the Garante, legislative bodies, and other stakeholders will be vital in navigating the challenges posed by public health emergencies while safeguarding personal data.

In conclusion, the evolution of green certification regulations reflects a careful balancing act between public health imperatives and the protection of individual rights. The Garante's role in this process is fundamental, ensuring that data protection remains a priority in the face of evolving public health challenges.

2. Legislative Decree 127 of 2021

The implementing measures—essentially contained in the Prime Ministerial Decree of June 17, 2021—represent an important framework of reference, in terms of guarantees, for the system of green certifications, within which the new provisions introduced by the successive legislative decrees regarding the use of the pass have been progressively included. The diversity of contexts to which the certifications have been progressively extended has sometimes created— as in the case of schools— the need for specific adjustments of the general regulations to the specific area considered, with the introduction of peculiar organizational measures, while maintaining the guarantees provided (also) from a privacy perspective. It is essential that, although tailored according to the most suitable organizational methods for each context, the guarantees established in general for the verification of green certifications are not diminished in any way. They are, in fact, functional, among other things, to avoid intolerable discrimination and potentially, also, undue conditioning of individual self-determination regarding vaccination choices.

An additional modulation will likely also be warranted for the implementation of Legislative Decree 127, which extends the use of green certifications to significantly different areas, such as public employment (whether contractual or not, including constitutional bodies), private employment, and judicial offices (limited to magistrates of every jurisdiction, as administrative personnel are subject to the more general obligations provided for the public sector). The obligation to possess certifications also extends to holders of elective or institutional top positions, as well as—based on autonomously issued provisions—to constitutional bodies.

The extension of the application scope of green certifications (as a requirement for access to the workplace) is carried out, for work in both public and private sectors, by referring to the regulatory system provided for the school sector regarding verification obligations for employers (paragraph 4 of Articles 1 and 3) and administrative sanctions in case of violations by the employer or the worker of the respective imposed prescriptions. The equivalence between unjustified absence and lack of the green pass is similarly borrowed from previous interventions, although it is not relevant in disciplinary terms (whereas access to the workplace without certification is, on the contrary, relevant, even for magistrates according to their respective regulations).

Employers are tasked with providing specific organizational methods for conducting checks, including sampling and primarily at the time of access to the workplace, following the formal identification of the individuals assigned (who, for data protection purposes, must be designated as authorized subjects for processing, pursuant to and for the effects of Articles 2-quaterdecies of Legislative Decree 196 of 2003 and subsequent amendments and Article 29 of EU Regulation 2016/679). For the public sector (non-magistrate), the homogeneous definition of organizational methods will also be provided by specific Guidelines from the Presidency of the Council of Ministers. Cross-sectoral is the reference to the Prime Ministerial Decree adopted pursuant to Article 9, paragraph 10, Legislative Decree 52 (the Prime Ministerial Decree of June 17 and subsequent amendments), for the definition of verification methods according to the aforementioned criteria.

However, for the judicial sector, the Ministry of Justice is also granted the authority to identify, through its own circular, additional verification methods (Article 3, paragraph 5, final part). In this regard, it is important to clarify that even these additional measures must not derogate, at least for data protection purposes, from the guarantees generally established by the Prime Ministerial Decree of June 17.

In general, it is essential that checks are carried out in full compliance (in terms of both the "how" and "what") with the principle of minimization. In this sense, therefore, since the requirement to possess a valid certification is functional to access the workplace, the verification should only be conducted on personnel whose actual presence in service is required on the day of the verification.
