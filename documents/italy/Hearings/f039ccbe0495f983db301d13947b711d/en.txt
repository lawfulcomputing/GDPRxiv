VIDEO OF THE HEARING  
Memorandum from the President of the Data Protection Authority, Prof. Pasquale Stanzione - "Amendments to Articles 1058 and 1462 of the Military Code, as per Legislative Decree No. 66 of March 15, 2010, regarding the documentation of suitability assessments for promotion and the attribution of merit scores as well as the awarding of commendations and praises”  
Commission IV (Defense) of the Chamber of Deputies  
(May 28, 2021)  

1. Introduction  
I thank the Commission for seeking to acquire, within the legislative process, the viewpoint of the Authority regarding a rather particular interpretation of the relationship between privacy and administrative transparency.  
This relationship has built, especially since Legislative Decree 33/2013, an important component of participatory democracy, increasingly perceived as the main tool for the re-legitimation of the res-publica.  
In the face of the crisis of representation and the emergence of populist pressures, indeed, administrative transparency has been entrusted with the function of ensuring the visibility of power which, according to Bobbio, constitutes the essence of democracy.  
Transparency thus becomes, more and more clearly, a form and organizational rule of public action, recalled among the cornerstone principles of administrative action in the constitutional reform project of the last legislature, evoking that principle of democratic control over the exercise of administrative power that some Founding Fathers would have wanted expressly enshrined in the Constitution.  
As the Constitutional Court observes in ruling 20/2019, the principles of publicity and transparency must be "referred not only, as a corollary of the democratic principle (Article 1 of the Constitution), to all relevant aspects of public and institutional life, but also, pursuant to Article 97 of the Constitution, to the proper functioning of the administration (rulings No. 177 and No. 69 of 2018, No. 212 of 2017) and (…) to the data it possesses and controls.”  
However, to maintain this democratic function without paradoxically degenerating, due to a singular heterogenesis of ends, into its opposite, transparency has had to seek a sustainable balance with privacy, whenever the publicity of administrative action involves those fragments of individual life that are personal data.  
The fulcrum of this balance has been identified by the Constitutional Court (ruling 20/19, based on the rest of the jurisprudence of the CJEU) in the principle of proportionality, around which the relationship between transparency and confidentiality must be articulated.  
It particularly requires, as the Court reminds, the necessity and suitability of the norm "for the achievement of legitimately pursued objectives, in that, among several appropriate measures, it prescribes the least restrictive one of the rights in question and establishes burdens that are not disproportionate to the pursuit of said objectives.”  
On the other hand, there cannot be – the Court observes with general reference to balancing operations, § 5.3.1. Cons.dir. – a decrease in the protection of a fundamental right, if it does not correspond to a corresponding increase in the protection of another interest of equal rank.  
And with particular reference to the relationship between privacy and administrative transparency, the Court recalls how account must be taken of the exponential amplification that the web determines for every form of publicity and the necessity to distinguish the knowledge functional to widespread scrutiny over the exercise of power from the satisfaction of "mere curiosity.” This is particularly favored – the ruling further observes – by the "casual retrieval" of personal data, made possible by indexing and the indiscriminate accessibility via the web of information.  
The proposed law under discussion thus transposes the relationship between privacy and transparency onto the terrain of military law, quite particular due to the strong hierarchical-vertical characterization of its structure and the margins of discretion that characterize, to some extent, certain decisions, such as those related to career advancement, especially for exceptional merits.  
The need from which the proposed law arises, to ensure greater scrutiny of some of these decisions, strengthening their motivation and publishing the final act, is therefore certainly shareable in this perspective.  
But like any form of publicity that also involves personal data, it must indeed respond to that principle of proportionality and reasonableness to which the Constitutional Court has called the legislator in a notable ruling, invoking the parameters of privacy regulation in an integrative function of the principle of reasonableness.  
This is, therefore, the limit that encounters legislative discretion in regulating the matter and on which we must reflect.  

2. The Content of the Legislative Proposal  
The aim of ensuring greater transparency in the evaluative process for career advancement and in the awarding of commendations and praises is pursued by the proposed law, primarily, through a stricter obligation of motivation.  
Regarding the second category of proceedings, the aim of transparency is also pursued through a broader regime of publicity for solemn commendations and the establishment of a specific archive, functional to monitoring the awarding activities.  
If we focus on this latter aspect – more relevant for the Authority's competencies – it should first be noted that the most significant innovation brought by the proposed law concerns the publicity regime of the solemn commendation, currently limited to publication in the agenda of the body, units, and superior commands, with essentially symbolic-formative purposes ("so that everyone may take example” states the norm) and which the proposal intends to complement with dissemination "in a specific section of the website."
**Internet of the Ministry of Defense**(1).

Regarding the method of web publication of solemn commendations, the proposed amendment with the introduction of paragraph 2-bis of Article 1462 of the Military Organization Code prescribes its anonymous nature, although with a formulation deserving of refinement, as it is inherently contradictory when it indicates for the "personal data" published the "anonymous form." The latter, in fact, pursuant to Recital 27 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, inherently excludes the personal nature of the data (as defined under Article 4, no. 1) of the Regulation itself. 

If, therefore, the aim pursued by the amendment is to prescribe the publication of commendations in an individual (not aggregated, that is, referring only to the numerical data) but truly anonymous form, it is necessary to provide for the removal not only of the name but also of any other data capable of re-identifying, even indirectly, the interested party. 

In general, appropriate anonymization techniques should be provided to be adopted concerning the contents to be published, such as to ensure the effective realization of the aim pursued by the regulation, namely to exclude the identification of the commended subject, also for national security and defense purposes. 

However, it must be considered whether, beyond the techniques used, the particular object of this publication (the solemn commendation itself) is truly capable of excluding any possibility of re-identification of the interested party, through further contextual data, particularly concerning specific professional skills or exceptional merits that uniquely distinguish the subject, as well as due to the publicity regime, albeit internal to the body, granted to such acts by publication in the order of the day. 

In such a case, it could not be considered that the publication, although devoid of the identifying data of the subject, is truly anonymous, and in the face of a not unreasonable risk of re-identification, the provisions of Regulation 2016/679 and the Code on the protection of personal data(2) should still apply. 

In any case, detailed elements that contribute to better defining the essential characteristics of the processing may then be provided, possibly also with a specific implementing regulation to be issued upon the opinion of the Guarantor, as provided for by Article 36.4 of Regulation 2016/679. 

In general, it can be concluded that the balance proposed by the regulation of the aforementioned paragraph 2-bis of Article 1462, between the transparency of the motivations for solemn commendations for military personnel, useful for career advancements, and the protection of the identity of the interested parties (together with the aforementioned reasons of national security and defense) is correct. 

The role played by these individuals could indeed, in some cases, require greater confidentiality, considering the risks to which the indiscriminate revelation (that determined by disclosure on the web) of their identity, their assignments or rank, or their membership in a certain body could expose them. Naturally, the degree of protection granted to the confidentiality of the subject depends, as already noted, on the actual suitability of the forms used for publication to avoid the possibility of re-identification of the commended individual. 

In order to allow for the monitoring of the procedure for granting commendations, praises, and other rewards, paragraph 9-bis, added to Article 1462 of the Military Organization Code, provides for the establishment of a digitized archive containing "the data related" to such acts. 

The provision of such a digital archive is certainly commendable in order to ensure a more precise monitoring of the exercise of the power to grant these merit titles. However, the measure requires further definition, starting from the type of data and documents intended to flow into the archive, clarifying, for example, whether it concerns only the concluding act of the procedure or also those preliminary acts, if truly functional to the purpose of monitoring the rewarding activities. 

It should then be provided, in particular: adequate measures to ensure the constant updating and, therefore, the accuracy of the data; the scope of accessibility of the archive itself and, therefore, the criteria for defining the subjects entitled to consultation, as well as, finally, adequate security guarantees, particularly in terms of information technology, of the archive. 

Such elements could, possibly, also be provided with an implementing regulation (hypothetically also that already mentioned concerning paragraph 2), to be adopted after the opinion of the Guarantor, also in order to allow, possibly, for a more straightforward update in forms less complex than those typical of the legislative procedure. 

These, therefore, are the refinements that the Guarantor suggests to be made, in the amendment phase, to the text, in order to ensure its fullest compliance with the data protection regulations. 

The Authority remains, in any case, at your disposal for the continuation of the work and, possibly, for the implementation phase, where, as suggested, it is intended to delegate to implementing regulations the definition of the detailed regulation. 

Thank you.
