2. The implications of the new provision
The introduction of this derogatory norm raises significant concerns regarding the potential for misuse of personal data and the erosion of privacy rights. It is crucial to emphasize that the original framework was designed to prevent any form of mass surveillance and to uphold the principles of data minimization and purpose limitation. The new provision, however, risks undermining these principles by allowing for the retention of data related to verification activities, which could lead to a chilling effect on individuals' willingness to participate in public health measures.

3. Recommendations
In light of these concerns, I urge the Senate to reconsider the implications of this new provision. It is essential to maintain the integrity of the original framework that prioritizes the protection of personal data while still addressing the public health needs arising from the pandemic. The balance between individual rights and public health must be preserved, ensuring that any measures taken are proportionate, necessary, and respectful of privacy rights.

4. Conclusion
In conclusion, while I appreciate the efforts made to adapt our legal framework to the challenges posed by the COVID-19 pandemic, I strongly advocate for a cautious approach that safeguards personal data and individual freedoms. The dialogue between the legislator and the Authority must continue to ensure that our response to the pandemic does not compromise the fundamental rights enshrined in our legal system. 1. Guaranteeing Maximum Confidentiality
The guarantee of maximum confidentiality regarding the reasons for the issuance of the green pass is essential. The provision allowing workers in both the public and private sectors to submit a copy of their green certification to their employer enables the latter to infer the basis for its issuance. This basis can be easily deduced from the expiration date of the certification, as each reason for issuance (test, recovery, vaccination) determines a different validity period for the green pass. Thus, a choice regarding vaccination—so closely tied to an individual's personal beliefs—risks being stripped of the necessary confidentiality guarantees, potentially detrimental to individual self-determination.

This potential detriment is further exacerbated by the work context in which it occurs. The anticipated presentation (and submission) of the green certificate to a subject, such as the employer, who should be barred from knowing the specific subjective conditions of workers, such as their clinical situation and personal beliefs, seems incompatible with the guarantees established by both data protection regulations and labor law (Articles 88 of EU Regulation 2016/679; 113 of Legislative Decree 196 of 2003; 5 and 8 of Law No. 300 of 1970; 10 of Legislative Decree No. 276 of 2003).

I cannot, therefore, miss this opportunity to suggest, first and foremost to the Commission, further reflection on this regulation, considering the appropriateness of its repeal. I do not believe that any simplification, whether real or presumed, is worth the price of sacrificing confidentiality on choices that are so strongly indicative of a person, such as those in the vaccination context or, more generally, regarding health conditions.

2. Legislative Decree 172
The need to prevent checks on green certifications from leading to access to the data contained therein, and in particular to the reasons for their issuance, is confirmed with respect to the new measures introduced by the legislative decree in question (4). I refer not only to the plan for the constant execution of checks, including random sampling, on compliance with the green pass regulations, which is assigned by Article 7 of the decree to the territorially competent prefect, but also and especially to the provisions of Articles 5 and 6.

With the first, in particular, a 'differentiation' in the regulation of the effects (in yellow or orange zones, starting from November 29) of green certifications has been introduced, depending on whether they result from vaccination or recovery on one hand or from testing on the other. 

With Article 6, this differentiation has been extended to white zones for the period December 6, 2021 - January 15, 2022, concerning the conduct of activities and the provision of services for which limitations are foreseen in yellow zones. The transitional regulation (applicable until the modification of the Prime Ministerial Decree of June 17, 2021) expressly authorizes, in Article 6, paragraph 2, the "necessary adjustment interventions to allow the verification of possession of only the green certifications" that legitimize (and thus those from vaccination or recovery).

This is a significant modification, as the regulation of green certifications has so far been generally oriented to ensure equivalence between the reasons for the issuance of the certification and the need to avoid its undue revelation, in order to also guarantee the exercise of the freedom of self-determination regarding vaccination choices. 

This need cannot, certainly, be disregarded even in the context of such a changed regulatory framework, and thus the adoption of certain precautions in this regard is imperative. If the exclusion of the collection, by the verifying subjects, of the data of the certificate holder is already generally provided for by Article 13, paragraph 5, of the Prime Ministerial Decree of June 17, 2021, what must be guaranteed here is that the "system in operation" allows, through an appropriate IT solution, to correspond "green" only to certifications from recovery or vaccination and "red" only to those from testing. In this way, indeed (and only in this way), it can be ensured that the application of the regulation on the differentiation of certifications occurs without legitimizing access by verifying subjects to the data contained in the pass and, in particular, to the reasons for its issuance.

Naturally, this result can be achieved through specific IT solutions, complementary to the current system, which will be governed by the Prime Ministerial Decree. In this way, moreover, with respect to holders subject to dual regimes (the ordinary green pass for employees and the super green pass for customers, as in the case of restaurateurs), the C19 verification app will need to contain two distinct "paths": the first, traditional, that does not distinguish between types of certifications and the second that, instead, operates this differentiation while only indicating the outcome (green or red, even in this case, albeit based on distinct reasons). This is what the new version of the C19 app, available since yesterday, seems to allow, even in the absence of modifications to the Prime Ministerial Decree of June 17.

The issue of the transitional regulation, applicable until the entry into force of the modifications to the Prime Ministerial Decree of June 17, is more complex. At this stage, Article 6, paragraph 2, of the legislative decree authorizes "the necessary adjustment interventions to allow the verification of possession of only the green certifications" from recovery or vaccination. It is difficult to imagine any other verification solution than that carried out on paper certifications and, therefore, with access by the verifying subject to the data relating to the reasons for the issuance of the pass. Therefore, I can only hope for the adoption of measures that ensure the verification process does not compromise the confidentiality of the reasons for the issuance of the green pass.
