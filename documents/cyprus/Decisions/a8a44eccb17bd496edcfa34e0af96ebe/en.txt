OFFICE OF THE COMMISSIONER FOR PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION

DECISION
Complaint regarding the violation of personal data
Case Incidents

Based on the duties and powers conferred upon me by Article 57(1)(f) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (hereinafter referred to as the "Regulation"), I have examined a complaint submitted to my Office, pursuant to Article 77(1) of the Regulation, against the Publications "ARKTINOS" Ltd (newspaper POLITIS) (hereinafter referred to as the "Respondent" or the "Entity"), the company Facebook Ireland Ltd (hereinafter "Facebook Ireland"), and the company Facebook Inc. The complaint was submitted to the Data Protection Supervisory Authority of Austria on August 17, 2020, by a resident of Austria (hereinafter referred to as the "complainant"), who is represented, pursuant to Article 80(1) of the Regulation, by the non-profit organization noyb – European Centre for Digital Rights.

Based on the investigation, I found a violation of the Regulation by the Respondent and, therefore, I issue this Decision.

According to Article 4 of the Regulation, personal data is interpreted as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, psychological, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person."  

10. As the data controller, Article 4 of the Regulation defines it as "the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data; where the purposes and means of such processing are determined by Union law or the law of a Member State, the controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be provided for by Union law or the law of a Member State."  

11. As the processor, Article 4 of the Regulation defines it as "the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which processes personal data on behalf of the controller."  

12. Article 5 of the Regulation provides the following:  

Regarding the principles governing the processing of personal data,  
"1. Personal data:  
a) shall be processed lawfully and fairly in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject ('lawfulness, fairness, and transparency'), collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and not  
b) processed further in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes; further processing for archiving purposes in the public interest or for scientific or historical research purposes or statistical purposes shall not be considered incompatible with the initial purposes according to Article 89 paragraph 1 ('purpose limitation'),  
c) shall be accurate and, when necessary, kept up to date; every reasonable step must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate, with respect to the purposes for which they are processed, are erased or rectified without delay ('accuracy'),  
d) shall be kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed ('storage limitation'); personal data may be stored for longer periods, provided that the personal data will be processed solely for archiving purposes in the public interest, for scientific or historical research purposes or for statistical purposes, in accordance with Article 89 paragraph 1 and provided that appropriate technical and organizational measures are applied to ensure the rights and freedoms of the data subject ('limitation of storage period'),  
e) shall be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures ('integrity and confidentiality').

18. Based on the information provided by the complainant, it appears that the subject of the complaint is the potential transfer of the complainant's data and whether there was an adequate level of protection for their data, as provided in Article 44 of the Regulation, due to the integration of a Facebook tool on the website. In this context, it should also be investigated whether Facebook Ireland and/or Facebook Inc. have an obligation to comply with Article 44 of the Regulation.

19. At this point, I note that any potential further processing is not examined in this Decision. I also note that I do not consider whether the potential transfer was made directly from the Subject to Facebook Inc. or through Facebook Ireland. The question is whether, in the case of a transfer, there was the required level of protection for the data transferred.

20. The Subject is a private news company. The website of the Subject includes articles on various topics, in the Greek language. Considering the subject matter of the website's content, it appears that the website targets individuals located in Cyprus. Additionally, the Subject is based and operates only in Cyprus and not in any other member state.

21. The Facebook Pixel tool (hereinafter referred to as the "tool") is a piece of code that is placed on a website and allows for measuring the effectiveness of the company's advertisements, understanding the actions users take on the website. According to information on a Facebook webpage, Meta's Pixel tool can help the website/company understand the effectiveness of its advertisements and the actions users take on the website, such as visiting a page or adding a product to the cart. It also allows the company to see when customers took an action after viewing the advertisement on Facebook and Instagram, which can assist with retargeting. The Facebook Pixel tool is used to ensure that the website/company's advertisements are shown to the right people, to increase the company's sales, and to measure the results of its advertisements.

22. The following: "The Meta Pixel can collect the following data:
- Http Headers – Anything that is generally present in HTTP headers, a standard web protocol sent between any browser request and any server on the internet. This information may include data like IP addresses, information about the web browser, page location, document, referrer, and person using the website.
- Regarding the tool, a related Facebook webpage mentions Pixel-specific Data – Includes Pixel ID and the Facebook Cookie."
**Button Click Data** – Includes any buttons clicked by site visitors, the labels of those buttons and any pages visited as a result of the button clicks.  
**Optional Values** – Developers and marketers can optionally choose to send additional information about the visit through Custom Data events. Example custom data events are conversion value, page type and more.  
**Form Field Names** – Includes website field names like email, address, quantity, etc., for when you purchase a product or service. We don't capture field values unless you include them as part of Advanced Matching or optional values.  

23. It is not known when the tool was installed on the website. However, by studying the har file submitted by the complainant, it is confirmed that at the relevant time the tool was installed.  

24. The Respondent decided to integrate the tool into the website for purposes that she has defined. The Respondent did not inform my Office of the purpose for which she integrated the tool. Therefore, due to her own decision - choice, the tool code was installed, which was provided to her by Facebook.  

25. Based on the above, I find that the Respondent is the data controller for this specific processing, as she has defined the purposes and means of the processing.  

26. Due to her own decision to integrate the tool, there was processing of the personal data of the complainant. Even if no processing is carried out directly by the Respondent or, as she stated, she does not maintain a record of personal data of any user who visits the website from a link on Facebook, any processing that occurs is a result of the Respondent's decision to integrate the tool.  

27. Therefore, as the data controller, she was obliged to take all measures so that the level of protection of personal data, which she processes or assigns the processing to a processor, is not undermined.

The complainant also requested clarification on whether the provisions of the Facebook Business Tools Terms and Facebook Data Processing Terms, at the time of the complaint submission and as they would be amended from August 31, 2020, met the requirements of Article 28 of the Regulation regarding the transfer of personal data to third countries.

55. According to Article 5 of the Regulation, it is established that the data controller is responsible for adhering to the principles governing the processing of personal data. However, the possibility of Facebook Inc. receiving requests from U.S. security authorities does not automatically lead to the conclusion that it has determined the purposes and means of processing, i.e., that it is considered a data controller under Article 28(10) of the Regulation. Furthermore, liability cannot be attributed to Facebook Ireland or Facebook Inc. for a violation of Article 28, since under that article, the relevant responsibility lies with the data controller.

56. Beyond the above, it should be examined whether Facebook Inc. is subject, in the present case, to the obligations set out in Chapter V of the Regulation. Based on the Guidelines 5/2021 of the European Data Protection Board, it is established that a transfer occurs when "the exporter discloses or makes available personal data, which are subject to the processing in question, to another data controller, a joint data controller, or a processor ('importer')." Therefore, the requirements of Chapter V of the Regulation must be adhered to by the data exporter, namely the Controller, but not by the data importer, in this case, Facebook Inc.

57. Therefore, assessing the said transfer, no violation of Article 44 of the Regulation by Facebook Inc. can be established.
