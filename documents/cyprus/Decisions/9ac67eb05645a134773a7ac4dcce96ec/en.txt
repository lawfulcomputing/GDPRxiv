Reasoning  

G.  

16. I consider it my duty to emphasize that I am examining only the processing act concerning the issuance and notification of the medical certificate by the Respondent to the former spouse of the complainant. Any use and/or processing of the data of the certificate carried out by the former spouse of the complainant is not the subject of the investigation since, as stated to my Office by the complainant's lawyers, the complaint does not concern the "leak" by the former spouse of the complainant to the Court.  

17. It is further understood that any use and/or processing of the certificate by the former spouse of the complainant, for the purposes she has defined, makes her the data controller, with all the obligations arising from the Regulation.  

18. I also note that the subject of the investigation is the communication of the medical certificate only, and not the accompanying medical results and/or diagnoses (5 pages) as acknowledged by the Respondent, which were accepted by the complainant's lawyers.  

19.  
he himself held the said data and issued the certificate.  

20.  
In the certificate, in addition to the details of the Respondent, the date of issuance of the certificate, and the details of the complainant (name and date of birth), it is stated that "It is certified that the above has been under my supervision from 2003 to 2012. In all visits to the office, he was accompanied by his wife XXX."  

21. As it appears from the certificate, the complainant visited the Respondent until 2012, while the certificate was issued in November 2022, that is, ten years later. The fact that ten years had passed since the complainant's visits should have raised concerns for the Respondent regarding the reason for which the said certificate was requested, and subsequently, the Respondent should have evaluated and/or checked whether he could grant such a certificate, since it records personal data of a third party, namely the complainant.  

21.1. However, the Respondent stated that the reason for which the certificate was requested was unknown. Nevertheless, the Respondent proceeded with the issuance of the certificate without obtaining consent and/or informing the complainant. Also, the fact that, as stated by the Respondent, he was not aware that the complainant and his wife were before a Family Court and/or that a divorce had been issued between them does not exempt him from any of his responsibilities.  

22. The certificate included information concerning both the complainant and his former spouse, as it refers to her and her actions (i.e., that she accompanied the complainant to the Respondent from 2003 to 2012).  

23. However, the data of the Respondent (visits to a cardiologist, specifically to the Respondent, from 2003 to 2012) constitute health data, that is, special categories of personal data, and therefore the provisions of Article 9 of the Regulation must be observed. Specifically, as stated in paragraph 1 thereof, the processing of such data is prohibited unless one of the cases in paragraph 2 thereof applies.  

24. The processing of the complainant's data cannot be based on Article 9(2)(a) of the Regulation, as the complainant did not provide explicit consent. The fact that his former spouse requested to receive a certificate for her actions, thus for her data, does not automatically mean that she is entitled to receive, unconditionally, personal data of other individuals, specifically of the complainant, even if such data were known to her.  

25. The processing of special categories of personal data may be carried out if it concerns data that have been manifestly made public by the data subject.
data, in application of Article 9(2)(e) of the Regulation. I acknowledge the fact that since the complainant allowed his ex-wife to accompany him during his visits to the Respondent, the complainant allowed his ex-wife to be aware of these visits. However, this does not mean that it grants the Respondent the right to prepare a written certificate regarding the complainant's visits and to communicate it to his ex-wife, especially since ten years have passed since the last visit. It is possible that the complainant allowed his ex-wife to know other information related to these visits, such as diagnoses and/or results. Even if this possibility holds true, it does not mean that the communication of such data is legitimized.

26. Furthermore, it cannot be considered that the communication of the certificate is legitimized on the basis of Article 9(2)(f) of the Regulation, that is, for the establishment, exercise, or defense of legal claims or when courts act in their judicial capacity. Moreover, the Respondent stated that the reason for which the certificate was requested was unknown. Even if the aforementioned reason had been mentioned by the complainant's ex-wife, the Respondent should not only have informed the complainant before communicating the certificate but also obtained his consent.

27. Beyond the above, it appears that the processing cannot be based on any other case of Article 9(2) of the Regulation. Specifically and briefly, the processing of the complainant's data cannot be based on:
27.1. Article 9(2)(b) of the Regulation, since the processing was not necessary for the performance of obligations and the exercise of specific rights of the Respondent or the complainant, or his ex-wife in the field of labor law and social security and social protection,

27.2. Article 9(2)(c) of the Regulation, since the complainant was not physically or legally incapable of consenting, and the processing of his data was not necessary for the protection of his vital interests,

27.3. Article 9(2)(d) of the Regulation, since the processing was not carried out within the framework of the legitimate activities of a foundation, organization, or other non-profit entity with a political, philosophical, religious, or trade union aim,

27.4. Article 9(2)(g) of the Regulation, since the processing was not necessary for reasons of substantial public interest,

27.5. Article 9(2)(h) of the Regulation, since the processing was not necessary for purposes of preventive or occupational medicine, assessment of the employee's working capacity, medical diagnosis, provision of health or social care or treatment, or management of health and social systems and services,

27.6. Article 9(2)(i) of the Regulation, since the processing was not necessary for reasons of public interest in the area of public health,

27.7. Article 9(2)(j) of the Regulation, since the processing was not necessary for archiving purposes in the public interest, for scientific or historical research purposes, or for statistical purposes.

28. Taking into account the above, it follows that the issuance and communication of the certificate to the complainant's ex-wife cannot be based on any legal ground, in violation of Article 9(2) of the Regulation. Furthermore, considering that the complainant was not informed about this, it appears that the Respondent did not adhere to the Principle of legality, objectivity, and transparency, namely Article 5(1)(a) of the Regulation, since his data was not subjected to lawful and fair processing in a transparent manner.

29. I acknowledge, however, as a mitigating factor, the fact that the aforementioned data came to the knowledge of the complainant's ex-wife because the complainant himself allowed this. What is recorded in the certificate was already known to her, as it was also known to the complainant that his ex-wife was aware of this information. I also acknowledge as a mitigating factor that the certificate does not delve into further references regarding the medical condition, history, diagnoses, or results concerning the complainant.

D.

30. Taking into account all the above elements, as they have been presented, and based on the powers conferred upon me by Article 57(1)(f) of the Regulation, I find that there is a violation by Dr. XXX of:
30.1. Article 5(1)(a) of the Regulation (EU) 2016/679, because the complainant's data was not subjected to lawful and fair processing in a transparent manner,

30.2. Article 9(2) of the Regulation (EU) 2016/679, because the issuance and communication of the certificate, which included data concerning health, lacked legality.
