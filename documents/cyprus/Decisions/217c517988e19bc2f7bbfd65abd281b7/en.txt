Decision
Excessive data collection by the Cyprus Sports Organization during the issuance of a Health Card for the sport of chess

I refer to the complaint submitted to my Office on November 13, 2022, by XXX (hereinafter referred to as the "Complainant") against the Cyprus Sports Organization (hereinafter referred to as the "CSO") and to all relevant correspondence regarding the above matter, which forms part of the case files. Based on the duties and powers conferred upon me by Articles 57 and 58 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (hereinafter referred to as the "Regulation"), I note the following:

A. Facts of the case

2. The complaint stated, among other things, the following:
i. The CSO requires the provision of detailed sensitive personal health data for the purpose of issuing a Health Card for the intellectual sport of chess.
ii. Specifically, for the issuance of the Health Card, a medical examination by a cardiologist, medical history, hereditary/family medical history, chest X-ray, electrocardiogram, and general blood tests are required. The Complainant considers this collection excessive, inappropriate, and a clear violation of the provisions of the Regulation, particularly the principle of data minimization.
iii. The issuance of the Health Card is a necessary prerequisite for a chess player to participate in events organized by the Cyprus Chess Federation and local chess clubs. As claimed by the Complainant, the CSO does not rely on any scientific basis for this requirement, nor on any European or international practice.

iv. The Complainant perceives this specific requirement as coercion to provide personal data.
v. The Complainant approached the Data Protection Officer of the CSO requesting that it be examined whether the provisions/decisions regarding the issuance of Health Cards comply with the principles governing the processing of personal data as defined in the Regulation, but the response received was unsatisfactory.

3. Along with the complaint form, the Complainant sent to my Office the correspondence exchanged with the CSO's Data Protection Officer, the forms "Medical Examination for Health Card" and "Return Form 'Medical Examination for Health Card'".

Sports and Sudden Cardiac Death
7. Sports and exercise, beyond their undeniable benefits, can also be a source of health problems primarily from cardiovascular issues.
11

system with a leading tragic event being sudden cardiac death (SCD). Specifically, the death of an athlete represents a serious blow to the overall social consciousness, as athletes personify the epitome of vigor and health.  
8. Sudden cardiac death is the most common cause of non-traumatic death among athletes. In a large study by Corrado and his colleagues1, it has been estimated that participation in competitive sports increases the risk of sudden cardiac death by 2.5 times compared to individuals who do not engage in sports.  
9. Epidemiological data have shown that the causes of SCD vary by age. For athletes under 35 years of age, the most common causes of SCD are Hereditary Cardiovascular Diseases (HCD), with the main representatives being Cardiomyopathies, Electrical Arrhythmia Syndromes (Channelopathies), and anomalous coronary artery origin. With the exception of anomalous coronary origin, these diseases are usually due to some mutation in the genome and are inherited from parents to their offspring. The peculiarity of these diseases is that they often do not present symptoms, with the first and last manifestation being SCD. The Health Questionnaire, with targeted questions from the hereditary and personal history of the athlete and with the help of the Electrocardiogram (ECG), aims to detect the possible existence of an HCD in an athlete and thus protect them from the risk of a cardiovascular event during exercise.  
10. For athletes over 35 years of age, studies have shown that the main cause of sudden cardiac death is coronary artery disease, although some of the aforementioned clinical entities3 cannot be excluded. This is also the reason why a Stress Test is required at these ages to detect asymptomatic coronary artery disease that may potentially cause an acute coronary event during exercise at these ages.  
Purpose - Issuance of Health Questionnaires by the Cyprus Sports Organization (CSO)  
11. One of the most important parameters supporting sports in our country has been the adoption of the Health Questionnaire by the sports community of Cyprus (Federations and Clubs).  
12. The issuance of the Health Questionnaire by a State body and/or its adoption by Federations and Clubs constitutes a groundbreaking action in sports not only in Europe but also in the global sports arena.  
13. Over the past 24 years, more than 150,000 Health Questionnaires have been issued, while today more than 15,000 Health Questionnaires are issued annually due to the increase in the population of athletes.  
14. At this moment, the Health Questionnaire represents the safest safeguard regarding the health of those engaged in sports (both physically and mentally). It is issued under the guidance and control of a permanent Committee, which is appointed by the Board of Directors of the Organization and which delineates and modernizes the future policies of the Health Questionnaire based on international literature and recommendations from Scientific Societies.  
15. Based on the above, the purpose of the specific questions for the issuance of the Health Questionnaire is to extract the minimum basic information that would raise suspicion of the existence of an HCD and to inform the athlete about the recommended precautions or avoidance of specific athletic activities. To make the above clearer, some examples are mentioned:  
(a) In the event that the family history indicates SCD in a young family member, there is a high likelihood that an HCD may be present that has been transmitted to other family members, and given that these diseases do not present symptoms, this individual may be at risk.  
(b) The same applies in cases of a coronary event at an age < 50 years in a family member.  
(c) The finding of a patent foramen ovale in a young athlete prohibits athletic activities that involve diving. The targeted information from the medical history in the CSO Health Questionnaire aims precisely to assist the physician in identifying underlying serious asymptomatic cardiovascular disease and consequently preventing pathological conditions that do not necessarily exclude full or limited athletic activity but require special precautions and monitoring.  
15.1. As mentioned in the introduction, the issuance of a Health Questionnaire is mandatory for athletes of recognized sports federations and clubs who train and compete systematically, whether amateur or professional.  
15.2. Furthermore, the recommendations of the CSO / ASYA to the general public who engage in sports and exercise systematically outside of federated and club sports are to undergo the necessary pre-sport medical and cardiological examination and submit an application for the issuance of a Health Questionnaire.  
15.3. The pre-sport medical and cardiological examination and the issuance of the Health Questionnaire, as we mentioned in previous letters, have been established based on International and European standards as well as the guidelines of the European and American Cardiology Societies4.  
15.4. Individuals participating in competitive-level activities, whether these involve sports that require physical activity or sports that do not require physical activity, expose their bodies to conditions of maximum or nearly maximum exertion.  
15.5. The designation of an exercise as "intense" does not equate to maximum effort. The level of intense exertion, whether physical, "mental," or psychosomatic, is defined by the attending physician according to the characteristics of the individual being examined (see individual, hereditary history, physical examination, etc., which are required during the primary medical examination).
13

15.6. The above is particularly emphasized as "intense" exercise has been associated with an increased rate of sudden death, especially in individuals over the age of 35, primarily due to coronary artery disease.  
Issuance of Health Cards and KYSO  
16. As we have mentioned in our previous letters, for the medical and cardiological examinations required by KYSO athletes, the K.O.A. and the A.S.Y.A. have had repeated meetings with KYSO, in the presence of its Cardiologist. A consultation was thus held with the Board of Directors of KYSO in the presence of their scientific collaborator, who fully agreed and consented to the procedure followed in the case of KYSO athletes.  
17. Therefore, following discussion, study, and mutual acceptance, it was decided that for athletes over the age of 34 participating in KYSO training and competitions, the requirements for the issuance of Health Cards would be minimized and "restricted" concerning sports that require physical exercise.  
18. Additionally, it was jointly decided that the duration of the issuance of the Health Card for KYSO athletes would be increased from the two (2) years applicable to sports involving physical exercise to three (3) years.  
19. As a result of the above, the requirements for the issuance of Health Cards for KYSO athletes over the age of 35 have been limited to what is absolutely necessary for the issuance of a Health Card and are proportionate to achieving the purpose for which they are required, namely for the prevention of Sudden Cardiac Death (see paragraph 11).  
19.1. For athletes up to 34 years of age, the same medical examinations are required as for all sports, as we will explain further, chess has been categorized in studies and research as having the same biochemical, physical, and other factors as all sports.  
20. Furthermore, due to the three-year validity of the Health Card for KYSO athletes, these athletes undergo the required medical examinations every three (3) years.  
21. For the evaluation of an application for the issuance of a Health Card for KYSO athletes over the age of 34, the following are required:  
• K.O.A. Form "Application for Medical Examination for Health Card."  
• Electrocardiogram (ECG) with a report from a Cardiologist.  
• General Blood Tests  
• Biochemical Analyses (only the 1st time for athletes over 34 years old)  
• Chest X-ray (only the 1st time for athletes over 34 years old)  
• Issuance for three (3) years.  
21.1. In contrast, for sports involving physical exercise, the issuance of a Health Card for federal and club athletes over 34 years old requires the following, proportionate to achieving the purpose for which they are required, namely for the prevention of Sudden Cardiac Death:  
• K.O.A. Form  
• ECG with a report  
• Exercise Stress Test  
• General Blood Tests  
• Biochemical Blood Analyses (the 1st time over 34 years old)  
• Chest X-ray (the 1st time over 34 years old) Issuance for up to two (2) years.

22. The effect of Mind Sports on the cardiovascular system during a match, particularly in Chess, has not been studied in depth. Based on data from MEDLINE PUB MED, where all valid medical publications are recorded, among 36 million publications, there are only two references regarding the effect of Chess on the cardiovascular system. These studies (refs. 1 & 2) emphasize the impact of the sport in a demanding chess match on cardiovascular variability - Heart Rate Variability (HRV) - Changes in HRV can cause arrhythmias, particularly atrial fibrillation.

The study makes specific references to metabolic load, changes in heart rate (HRV), and many other physiological parameters.  
23.1. Publications and other bibliographic sources for studies and research on Chess athletes (refs. 4 & 5), which we have also highlighted in yellow.

As mentioned above, the level of "intensity" and the load of individuals during exercise is determined by the attending physician, depending on the characteristics of the individual being examined. Even among athletes of the same sport or sports team, the same intensity does not have the same load on the cardiovascular system of individuals but depends on the physiology of each person individually.  
25. Based on the above, the sport of chess has equivalent biochemical, physiological, and other requirements as all "classical" sports, including football, and appropriate precautions must be taken for the health of the chess player in a mentally demanding competition.

Conclusions 
27. The questions and examinations required for the issuance of a Health Card are proportional to the purposes they serve, namely the detection of cardiovascular problems that may endanger an athlete's life and the prevention of SCD (Sudden Cardiac Death). These risks are independent of the type of sport - mental or physical. 
28. The obligation to issue a Health Card applies only to athletes participating in competitions and training of recognized sports federations and clubs. 
29. From 01/01/2019 to 31/12/2023, KOA/ASYA evaluated 62,511 applications for the issuance of Health Cards, and currently, 48,112 Health Cards are in effect. 
30. Both the European Society of Cardiology and the American College of Cardiology agree that it is imperative to screen athletes for both medical and ethical/legal reasons. 
31. This position is supported by the actions and deeds of both the EU ASYA and its members, as well as all members of the Board of Directors of the Organization over time.

C. Reasoning 

13. According to Article 5(1) of the Cyprus Sports Organization Law of 1969 (41/1969), the purpose of the Organization is "the promotion of extracurricular physical education and sports in general on the island, the cultivation and development of the Olympic ideal and the spirit of sportsmanship among the people, the athletic promotion of the island abroad within the framework of existing relations with foreign authorities and sports organizations, and in cooperation with these authorities and organizations, the improvement of this position in the international sports movement, as well as the promotion of an action plan for healthy sports." 

14. For the achievement of the above purpose, pursuant to Article 5(2) of Law 41/1969, the Board of Directors of the Cyprus Sports Organization has the authority, among others, to determine what is required for the issuance of Health Cards for athletes and the procedure for the medical examinations that must be followed. 

15. Based on the same article, the Board of Directors of the Organization, exercising its powers, may establish a committee, body, or instrument to which it may delegate the exercise of any of its responsibilities. Based on these provisions, the Board of Directors of the Cyprus Sports Organization decided to establish the Scientific Committee "Supreme Council for Athlete Health (SCASH)." 

16. According to the publicly available information on the website of the Cyprus Sports Organization, "The terms of reference and the responsibilities of the SCASH Committee have been defined by a decision of the Board of Directors of the Organization dated November 3, 2011. The following are briefly mentioned: 
1. The SCASH is the competent body that addresses issues of preventive examination of athletes with the ultimate goal of preventing sudden death and is responsible for the issuance of health cards under the name Health Cards SCASH. 
2. The SCASH is the competent committee of the Organization for determining the required procedure for the medical examination for the issuance of the health card. 
3. It is responsible for monitoring developments and practices followed in the science of Athlete Health globally and particularly in the area of sudden cardiac death and prevention of athlete health and recommends modifications to the health card issuance procedures where deemed necessary." 

17. Taking into account the above information, pursuant to Article 4 of the Regulation, the collection, storage, and any other use of medical data or other personal data of athletes during the issuance of Health Cards constitutes a processing act. The Complainant, like all athletes, as the individuals whose data is being processed, constitutes the data subjects, and correspondingly the Cyprus Sports Organization, as the organization that determines the purpose and means of processing, is the data controller. 

18. Despite the legality of the issuance of Health Cards, as it is based on Article 5 of Law 41/1969, any processing act carried out within the framework of this issuance must be governed by the Principles of data processing as defined in Article 5 of the Regulation. Among these, the principle of purpose limitation and data minimization (Article 5(1)(b) and (c) of the Regulation). Pursuant to these, the medical data collected for the issuance of Health Cards must be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes, must not be further processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes, must be limited to what is necessary, and must be adequate and relevant to the purpose. I emphasize that the determination of general purposes does not automatically legitimize excessive data collection. 

19. During the investigation of the complaint, to evaluate the above principle, I requested from the Cyprus Sports Organization, as the data controller, to inform me about the purpose of issuing the Health Cards. According to the final positions of the SCASH Committee submitted before me, "The purpose of the Health Card and the examinations conducted for its issuance is the prevention of Sudden Cardiac Death in athletes. At the same time, it also achieves the timely diagnosis and prevention of pathological conditions that do not exclude full or limited athletic activity but require special precautions and monitoring." 

20. The necessity of preventing Sudden Cardiac Death in athletes was justified based on a specific study (Corrado and his collaborators), according to which it has been estimated that participation in competitive sports increases the risk of sudden cardiac death by 2.5 times compared to individuals who do not engage in sports.
25

21. In its initial statements, the Cyprus Sports Organization (ΚΟΑ) indicated that the collection of medical data for the issuance of the Health Card aims to detect health problems that may endanger the lives of athletes, not necessarily during exercise, but generally during any activity, a purpose which I had deemed quite broad and general in my prima facie Decision. Considering my positions, as outlined above, the purpose of issuing the Health Cards was limited to the prevention of Sudden Cardiac Death in athletes.

22. As I had pointed out in my prima facie Decision, since the issuance of Health Cards is a necessary prerequisite for athletes' participation in events organized by their recognized Federations and aims to protect the health of athletes, it should be proportionate to the sport. Taking into account the positions of the 5 other supervisory authorities I consulted, I concluded in my Decision that the necessity of issuing the Health Card for chess players should be reassessed and the proportionality of the collected data should be duly documented.

23.1. Considering my above indications, the ASYA Committee, with specific references to studies, noted that -
- the impact of the sport in a demanding chess competition on cardiovascular variability has been emphasized, changes which may cause arrhythmias, particularly atrial fibrillation,
- the biochemical, physiological, psychological aspects, and changes in the central nervous system in individuals competing in chess are equivalent to all other sports,
- individuals participating in competitive-level activities, whether they involve sports requiring physical activity or those that do not, expose their bodies to conditions of maximum or nearly maximum load,
- the level of "intensity" and load of individuals during exercise is determined by the attending physician, depending on the characteristics of the individual being examined. Even among athletes of the same sport or sports team, the same intensity does not impose the same load on the cardiovascular system of individuals, but depends on the physiology of each person individually,
- "intense" exercise has been associated with an increased rate of sudden death, especially in individuals over 35 years old, with coronary artery disease being the main culprit, justifying the necessity of detecting health problems in chess players for the protection of their health through the issuance of Health Cards.

23.2. To satisfy the principle of proportionality, the Board of Directors of the ΚΟΑ proceeded to minimize the required examinations for the issuance of Health Cards by the athletes of the Cyprus Chess Federation (ΚΥΣΟ), as shown in the table presented in the incidents of this Decision, also modifying the duration of validity. To justify the completion of the Medical Examination Form for the issuance of the Health Card, the ASYA Committee explained that the questions included, with the assistance of the electrocardiogram, aim to detect the existence of hereditary cardiovascular diseases in the athlete to protect them from the risk of a cardiovascular event at the time and place of exercise, thus achieving the purpose of issuing the Health Card.

24. Although the effect of mental sports on the cardiovascular system, particularly for chess, has not been studied in depth and the number of studies examining the effect of chess on the cardiovascular system is limited, as noted by the ASYA Committee, in my opinion, this does not justify the complete abolition of the issuance of the Health Card for this specific sport.

25. Respecting the medical aspect of the issue and the fact that the ASYA Committee justified its positions by providing specific explanations and scientific references, presenting the reasons why it deems the issuance of Health Cards for chess players necessary, and also modified the prerequisites for the issuance of Health Cards for the athletes of the ΚΥΣΟ, I do not find any other changes to the medical data collected to be proportionate. I emphasize that since the issuance of Health Cards also involves issues of personal data, as the health data collected and evaluated constitute special categories of personal data whose processing is generally prohibited and is only legitimized in specific cases, the ΚΟΑ, as the data controller, must monitor medical developments and update/modify accordingly its procedures related to the issuance of Health Cards, so that medical examinations and/or any medical checks for the issuance of Health Cards are determined based on the sport to which they pertain.

26. In reaching the above conclusion, I also take into account the presence of the Federations and their Cardiologists at the meetings concerning the determination of the required examinations when submitting the application for the issuance of Health Cards and I find it important that their views and recommendations on the matter are evaluated and taken into account by the ASYA Committee and consequently by the ΚΟΑ. Regarding the claims of the Complainant, concerning the consideration of...
27

Other factors in the Federation's decision to comply beyond the usefulness of the specific checks for chess (a claim that was not further substantiated), I am of the opinion that if and when the Federation's positions are adequately substantiated, they should be heard.

27. In summary of the above, in examining the matter, on the one hand, specialists have been involved, different each time (both from the involved party (KOA) and from the federations) who assessed the necessity of examining the athletes; on the other hand, during the investigation, I requested and received the positions and opinions of the KOA as the data controller. Therefore, I did not find it necessary to obtain another opinion from an external expert.

28. As I have noted in my preliminary Decision, regarding the position of the ASYA Committee that -
- the process falls within the practices of medical practice,
- the recording of pathological findings forms a database for research purposes for the benefit of reliable statistics and the formation of an incident registry, adhering to the rules of the bioethics committee,
- the extraction of pathological data will contribute to the improvement of practices followed in the prevention of AKTH and the maximization of conditions ensuring the lives of citizens/athletes,
I emphasize that although the above constitute a legitimate purpose of processing, compatible with the initial purpose that has been established (Article 5(1)(b) of the Regulation), the processing of data for scientific research purposes constitutes further processing of personal data. In any case, the personal data collected should be proportional to the purpose that has initially been established.

29. Also relevant is the following excerpt from the "EDPB Document on response to the request from the European Commission for clarifications on the consistent application of the GDPR, focusing on health research," which, although it concerns health care providers, I believe can be analogously applied to the above position of the ASYA Committee:
“Q7. If a health care provider collected health data from patients and wishes to use those data for a scientific research project, to what extent would this be considered compatible further processing?
22. When further processing of health data for scientific research purposes relies on the presumption of compatible use (Article 5(1)(b) GDPR), the controller will have to take Article 9 GDPR into account as well. It could very well be that the exemption to the prohibition on the processing of health data the health care provider relied on for the original purpose does not extend to or does not cover the processing of health data for scientific research purposes. For instance, if the exemption in MS law only allows for the processing of health data by the health care provider in order to provide health care or medical treatments (Article 9(2)(h) GDPR), the health care provider would still need to rely on an exemption based on Union or MS law as required in Article 9(2) GDPR for the processing of health data for scientific research purposes.”

30. Regarding the safeguards that should be taken during the processing of data for scientific research purposes, the EDPS/2019/02-08 report titled: “Study on the appropriate safeguards under Article 89(1) GDPR for the processing of personal data for scientific research” is useful.

31. Regarding the processing of data related to the military service of athletes, which the Complainant raises in his latest letter, this constitutes a separate issue that is entirely based on the legal provision contained in the Cyprus Sports Organization Law of 1969 (41/1969). “It is understood that the health card is in no case issued if the athlete is conscripted who has been called up and enlisted in the National Guard, according to the provisions of the National Guard Law, from the year 2019 onwards and holds, under this Law, a temporary discharge, either due to unfitness for military service (I/5) or due to a granted postponement of fulfilling the obligation of military service, due to a disease/condition related to his mental health (…)”.

32. The above provision assigns specific responsibilities to the KOA. For this matter, I had previously provided relevant guidance to the Ministry of Defense and the KOA regarding the technical solution that would be implemented so that the National Guard would provide the KOA only with the necessary information required by Article 5(2) of Law 41/1969, the implementation of an impact assessment, ensuring that the National Guard would provide the KOA only with the data that is absolutely necessary for fulfilling its legal obligations and the inclusion of relevant text informing the data subjects. Therefore, the matter will not be reconsidered in the present complaint.

33. Finally, I consider that the Complainant's claim of coercion in data collection is directly examined by the re-evaluation of the proportionality of issuing Health Cards.
