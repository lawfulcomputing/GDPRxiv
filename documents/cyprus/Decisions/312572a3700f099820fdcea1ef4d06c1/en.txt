OFFICE OF THE COMMISSIONER FOR PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION

DECISION

Complaint regarding the Statistical Processing Methods of the scores of candidates who participated in the Written Examinations 2019 for Registration and Ranking on the Lists of Appointees

Based on the duties and powers conferred upon me by Article 57(1)(f) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (hereinafter referred to as the "Regulation"), I examined a complaint submitted to my Office regarding the processing of personal data during the application of statistical processing methods of the scores of candidates who participated in the written examinations 2019 for registration and ranking on the lists of appointees.

Based on the investigation, I found a violation of the Regulation and the Law on the Protection of Natural Persons Against the Processing of Personal Data and the Free Movement of Such Data of 2018, Law No. 125(I)/2018, by the Examinations Service (hereinafter referred to as the "Service") of the Directorate of Higher Education of the Ministry of Education, Sports and Youth (hereinafter referred to as the "Ministry").

On the special website of the Ministry, which pertains to the Examinations and which refers to the impact assessment conducted by the Service, there is the document "Methods of Statistical Processing of the Scores of Candidates who Participated in the Written Examinations 2019 for Registration and Ranking on the Lists of Appointable Candidates." This document records the statistical processing methods that are applied. As mentioned, it was practically impossible to utilize all weighting methods for all specialties due to practical difficulties. However, every effort was made to 

combine as many methods as possible for each specialty, so that there are multiple sources of information and to achieve minimization of error through triangulation of results.

The methods recorded in this document are: 
7.1. the "common candidates" method, in which for each candidate who participated in the Examinations in 2017 and in the Examinations in 2019, the difference in their score was calculated separately,

7.2. propensity score matching method, in which the performance of candidates with the same profile in the Examinations in 2017 and in the Examinations in 2019 is compared. Additional information such as gender, degree classification (pass, merit, and distinction), additional academic qualifications (e.g., MA, PhD), teaching experience, etc., was used to create the profiles,

7.3. pseudo anchor method, in which any difference in the difficulty level of the exam papers from the Examinations in 2017 and the Examinations in 2019 can be attributed, primarily, to the difference in the overall difficulty level of the questions, which have not been characterized as common (i.e., questions from the Examinations in the years 2017 and 2019 that have similar characteristics, such as examining the same content, carrying the same number of points, having approximately the same level of difficulty, having the same presentation method, etc.),

7.4. Angoff standard setting method, in which a group of experts estimates the expected score of "novice" candidates on each of the questions of an examination paper and it is considered that the differences in the expected performance of a "novice" candidate in the two examination periods can be attributed to the different difficulty levels of each examination,

7.5. 3DC standard setting method, in which experts estimate the expected performance of "novice" candidates on groups of questions.

Reasoning

G.

21. I must emphasize that the investigation I am conducting concerns exclusively and solely issues of personal data protection. Therefore, based on the duties with which I am entrusted, I cannot investigate issues that concern, among other things, the reliability or otherwise of the results, the suitability of the statistical processing methods that were applied, the correctness of the scoring system, as well as whether the necessity of statistical processing should be examined.

22. Considering that, pursuant to Law 10/1969, the Service is responsible for "the preparation of examination papers, the conduct of examinations, the extraction of results, and their transmission to the Committee," I conclude that the Service is the data controller for the processing of personal data that takes place within the framework of the statistical processing of the examination results.

22.1. The above conclusion is not affected by the fact that the agreement with the contracting company was made between the latter and the Ministry.

23. On the website of the Educational Service Committee (ESC), which pertains to the application for registration on a list, namely on the list of appointees and/or on the list of eligible candidates, it is stated that:  
"Together with the application, you MUST necessarily submit all the necessary certificates/evidential documents as listed in the instructions of the form. You must submit both the originals and copies so that the relevant certification of the copies can be made by an ESC officer upon receipt of the ESC01X application at the ESC Office."

23.1. On this website, the necessary certificates/evidential documents that must accompany the application are recorded. Specifically, the following are mentioned: birth certificate, identity card or passport, high school diploma, degree(s), proof of studies, photocopy of the Study Guide of the educational institution where the applicant studied, full military status certificate type "A", certificate of a clean criminal record, certificate for educational experience, and certificate of technical experience (for trainers and technology teachers only). In addition to these, which are explained in detail on the relevant website, a recent original medical certificate must be submitted for applications for registration on special lists for persons with disabilities.

24. Based on the above, it follows that applicants must record their qualifications and relevant information, such as the degree grade, whether or not they hold additional academic qualifications (master's degrees, including doctoral degrees), and their teaching experience. They must also submit the relevant certificates/evidential documents to the Educational Service Committee themselves. By submitting these specific certificates/evidential documents, applicants receive, as provided, relevant scoring. It is, therefore, evident that fulfilling the above obligations is a necessary condition for registration on the list of eligible candidates and obtaining the relevant scoring.

25. The sole purpose of conducting the Examinations is to prepare the list of eligible candidates. For a candidate to be included in the list of eligible candidates, success in the Examinations is required. In each specialty, a list of eligible candidates is created, which includes successful candidates from various years. At specified intervals, the list of eligible candidates for each specialty is updated, with the addition of successful candidates from the new Examinations and/or with the differentiation of the scoring of qualifications and/or the scores of each successful candidate. Therefore, comparability of scores from Examinations of different years is required. As a result, as stated in Regulation 8 of the Written Examination Regulations for Registration and Ranking on the Lists of Eligible Candidates of 2017, as amended, statistical processing is applied for the purposes of unified ranking of candidates.

26. The methods applied include those that require the processing of personal data. Beyond the existence of a relevant legal basis since statistical processing is provided for in the relevant Regulations, I consider that the application of such methods does not go beyond, but rather falls within actions aimed at achieving the intended purpose, namely the ranking of candidates on the lists of eligible candidates. Moreover, the submission of the certificates/evidential documents was carried out by the candidates themselves, for their registration on the list of eligible candidates of the relevant specialty or for
**27.** Their scoring. Therefore, I conclude that the application of the relevant statistical methods, which involve the processing of personal data, does not fall outside the framework of legality. Specifically, it includes the processing of data provided by the candidates themselves to the Educational Service Commission and/or data that arose from the Examination process.

**27.1.** However, I must emphasize that the processing of personal data is not solely contingent upon the method of score trend matching, as mentioned in the impact assessment conducted by the Service. The processing of personal data also occurs through other statistical processing methods that were applied. A study of the document "Statistical Processing Methods of the Scores of Candidates who Participated in the Written Examinations in 2019 for Registration and Ranking on the Lists of Appointees" reveals that in the "common candidates" method, the total score obtained by each candidate in the Examinations in 2017 and 2019 is processed, while in the pseudo anchor method, the scores obtained by each candidate on specific questions in the Examinations in 2017 and 2019 are processed.

**28.** As provided in Article 28 of the Regulation, the data controller "uses only processors that provide sufficient guarantees for the implementation of appropriate technical and organizational measures, in such a way that the processing meets the requirements of this Regulation and ensures the protection of the rights of the data subject." The assigned processing is governed by a contract or other legal act and specifies "the subject matter and duration of the processing, the nature and purpose of the processing, the type of personal data and the categories of data subjects, and the obligations and rights of the data controller." Article 28(3) of the Regulation lists all the elements that must be included in the relevant contract. It is further understood that this contract must precede the communication of data from the data controller to the processor and/or any processing of such data by the processor.

**29.** In this case, the Service assigned the company CITO the implementation of the statistical processing of the scores of candidates who participated in the Examinations in the years 2019 and 2021. Therefore, the contractor company CITO acts as the processor for the data processing in question.

**30.** Pseudonymization, or as the Service referred to it, the use of pseudocodes, constitutes a measure for the security of personal data and does not change the nature of such data into non-personal data. That is, the personal data that have been pseudonymized continue to be classified as personal data.

**31.** It follows, therefore, that the communication of data to the contractor company constitutes an act of processing and, as such, the Service's position that personal data are not communicated does not hold, since the data provided by it are characterized by pseudocodes, as stated in its letter dated December 27, 2021. I remind you that the Service, during the exercise of the right to be heard, on September 28, 2023, retracted the above position and acknowledged that there had been processing of the personal data of the candidates.

**32.** Upon reviewing the copy of the agreement made between the Ministry and the contractor company, which was submitted to my Office with the letter from the Service dated December 27, 2021, it appears that this agreement does not constitute a processing assignment contract, as provided in Article 28(3).
of the Regulation. Therefore, there is no apparent reason why the Service, in its letter dated August 29, 2022, stated that it is not feasible to prepare, retroactively, a "new processing assignment contract" with the contracting company.

33. Despite the fact that a processing assignment contract had not been signed with the contracting company, as ultimately acknowledged by the Service, I recognize that the initial agreement - contract between the Service and the contracting company included provisions for data protection, as presented in paragraph 9.3.1. of this document, which were submitted to my Office in the context of exercising the right to be heard.

34. The absence of a signed assignment contract between the Service and the contracting company, as provided for in Article 28(3) of the Regulation, indicates that the Service, as the data controller, cannot prove that the processor, namely the contracting company, provided sufficient assurances for the implementation of appropriate technical and organizational measures, in such a way that the processing meets the requirements of the Regulation and ensures the protection of the rights of data subjects, as stipulated in Article 28(1) of the Regulation.

35. Furthermore, the absence of an assignment contract, as provided for in Article 28(3) of the Regulation, indicates that the Service cannot prove that the processing was carried out in accordance with the Regulation, as stipulated in Article 24(1) of the Regulation.

36. As indicated in Article 36(4) of the Regulation, there is an obligation for prior consultation with the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection when drafting proposals for legislative measures or regulatory measures based on such legislative measures, which concern processing. The same obligation is imposed by Article 13(1) of Law 125(I)/2018. The consultation must take place in such a way as to ensure that the planned processing complies with the Regulation and, in particular, to mitigate risks to data subjects.

37. Therefore, considering that the Regulations on Written Examinations for Registration and Ranking on the Lists of Appointable Persons of 2017, as amended, provide for statistical processing for the purpose of a unified ranking of candidates, which requires the processing of personal data, the Service was obliged to carry out prior consultation with my Office before including the relevant provision.

38. It cannot be considered that the prior consultation is in any way related to the impact assessments ("impact analysis questionnaire") conducted by the Ministry both during the submission of the Draft Regulations to the House of Representatives for the 2017 Regulations and during the submission of the Draft Regulations for the Amending Regulations of 2019.

39. As analyzed in paragraph 30 of this document, the use of pseudonymization does not change the nature of the data. Therefore, I do not accept the position of the Service that there was no consultation with my Office because the application of statistical processing does not involve the handling of personal data. I remind that this position was mentioned in a letter from the Service, dated December 27, 2021, while, in the context of exercising the right to be heard, on September 28, 2023, the Service acknowledged the processing of personal data that was carried out.

40. Additionally, I do not accept the position of the Service that it would not have been feasible to conduct prior consultation with my Office due to the tight deadlines that had to be met. I believe that there was sufficient time for consultation, as the relevant amendments to Regulations 4 and 8 of the Regulations on Written Examinations for Registration and Ranking on the Lists of Appointable Persons of 2017 were published in the Government Gazette on July 30, 2019, that is, at least three months before the conduct of the Examinations, the grading of the answers, and the communication of the data to the contracting company.

41. Regarding the impact assessment conducted by the Service, following my indication, and sent to my Office on August 30, 2022, I find it appropriate to mention the following:

41.1. The assessment does not evaluate whether there is an impact based on the use of statistical methods, but is mainly dedicated to the collection, application of technical measures, and retention of data by the Service,

41.2. The role of data controller is held by the Service and not, as mentioned, by the "Ministry of Education, Sports and Youth (ΥΠΑΝ) through the Directorate of Higher Education, through the Examination Service (ΥΕ)".
(Head of Department: XXX)

41.3. The statement that the role of the data processor is performed by officials of the Service and/or other officials of the Ministry is not valid. The officials of the Service are part of the data controller and, in any case, perform processing under its supervision.

41.4. As analyzed in paragraph 27 of this document, the processing of personal data occurs not only through the application of the method of data matching but also through other methods that have been used.

41.5. Considering that statistical processing is provided for in the Regulations on Written Examinations for Registration and Ranking on the Lists of Eligible Candidates of 2017, as amended, the legal basis should be considered the legal obligation of the Service, namely Article 6(1)(c) of the Regulation, and not, as mentioned, the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of public authority vested in the data controller, namely Article 6(1)(e) of the Regulation. For the same reason, the consent of the candidates should not be considered as a legal basis, namely Article 6(1)(a) of the Regulation. Nevertheless, I emphasize that candidates should have full and transparent information regarding the processing of their data.
