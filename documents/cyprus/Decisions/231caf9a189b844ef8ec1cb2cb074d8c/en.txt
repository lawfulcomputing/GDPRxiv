DECISION  
Subject: Handling of files for the purpose of examining applications for the issuance of final approval certificates – Consultation of the Municipality of Ypsonas with the Department of Labor Inspection  

I refer to the final Notification of a personal data breach incident submitted to my Office on May 15, 2023, by the Municipality of Ypsonas (hereinafter referred to as the "Municipality"), and to all related correspondence regarding the above subject, which forms part of the case file. After examining the incident, based on the powers conferred upon me by Articles 33(5) and 55(1) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (hereinafter referred to as the "Regulation"), I note the following.  

Case Facts  

2. According to the above Notification and the additional information sent by the Municipality:  
2.1. On September 2, 2022, the Municipality, in the context of examining an application for the issuance of a Final Approval Certificate, delivered a file to the Limassol District Labor Inspection Office for the purpose of obtaining the views of the relevant Department, in accordance with Article 55 of the Health and Safety at Work Laws of 1996 to 2023 (Law 89(I)/1996).  

2.2. On October 21, 2022, the Municipality was informed that the Limassol District Labor Inspection Office had delivered the aforementioned file to the former owner of the premises (for which a Final Approval Certificate was to be issued). The Municipality was informed by the former owner about this delivery.  

2.3. On the same day, namely October 21, 2022, the former owner returned the file to the Municipality, stating that he had made a copy of the file for his architect. As noted by the Municipality, the former owner was authorized by the current owners to manage and act on their behalf in all matters related to the licensing of the building/premises for which a Final Approval Certificate was to be issued. Additionally, the architect to whom the copy of the file was delivered, as clarified, is the project architect.  
2.4. After the return of the file, the Municipality initially claimed that documents were missing from the file - specifically, 3 pages. In the initial Notification of the incident, it was mentioned that a responsible officer, during the examination of the file, noticed that documents and/or forms and/or certificates that constituted an integral part of the Technical Services file were missing. Subsequently, in the final Notification of the incident, it was stated that he does not know which specific documents were lost and whether they were ultimately lost since the main documents of the file concerning building permits, etc., are in the file, as became evident after a re-examination of the file.  

2.5. According to the Municipality, the file contains the following:  

- studies and plans (with signatures of owners, designers, and the mayor),  
- applications (which include copies of identity cards and signatures of owners),  
- title deeds and spatial data for the plot,  
- documents of assumption and assignment of the designers and annual professional licenses,  
- consultations between departments,  
- building and town planning permits with conditions,  
- payment receipts (for building permits).  
The file also includes registration certificates and final approval certificates. The documents in the file contain data of the owners of the premises of the said plot. According to the Notification, there were no special category data in the file. The incident may affect data of 5 residents, owners of the said plot, and employee data, as the file contains service data from the Municipality's technical service.  

2.6. The Municipality, to protect the personal data contained in the file from any leakage, delivered the file in person and through its authorized representative to the Limassol District Labor Inspection Office. It therefore expected instructions for the return and delivery of the file to the Technical Services of the Municipality of Ypsonas and to no other person. Specifically, it notes that "the file was delivered by a clerk of the Municipality of Ypsonas to the District Office of the Department of Labor Inspection in Limassol and the file should have been delivered and/or returned to the Municipality and not to any other person or authorized representative."  

2.7. The Municipality stated in the Final Notification of the incident that there cannot be any consequences from this action of the Limassol District Office. It assessed the seriousness of the potential consequences as negligible, stating that "any documents that have been lost, if they have been lost, do not constitute documents of critical or primary importance."  
2.8. For the investigation of the incident, the Municipality sent a relevant letter to the Limassol District Labor Inspection Office and after a relevant request, the necessary authorizations from the property owners were submitted. As stated by the Municipality, it appears that there is no breach.  
2.9. In the Notification, the Municipality states that the necessary measures were not taken by the Limassol District Labor Inspection Office. The file was delivered by a clerk of the Municipality of Ypsonas and the
**File should have been delivered and/or returned directly to the Municipality and not to any other person or authorized representative.**

3. In addition to the above Notification, management authorizations for the former owner and a letter dated December 8, 2022, from the Limassol District Labor Inspection Office were sent to my Office, which states, among other things, the following:  
3.1. For the examination of the application for the issuance of the Final Approval Certificate, it was necessary to conduct inspections at the premises/workspaces. Representatives of the applicants and the premises were also present during the inspection. After the inspection, the necessary recommendation for the issuance of the requested Certificate was made.  
3.2. The former owner, who is the authorized manager of the current owners and representative of the company, volunteered to transfer the file to the Municipality for the purpose of facilitating and expediting the process, at which point the Department, acting in "good faith," provided the file. Due to the large volume of the file and the absence of a clerk, the file was delivered closed in another envelope, which indicated the Municipality as the recipient.  
3.3. The Limassol District Labor Inspection Office does not accept the Municipality's claim of document loss, which it deems unfounded. It also states that during the examination process of the application for the issuance of the Final Approval Certificate, it is necessary to examine only specific elements included in the file (applicant details, architectural plans, and a copy of the terms) and therefore no document is removed from the file, nor is there a reason for the District Office to retain a copy. To avoid similar incidents in the future, the Limassol District Labor Inspection Office recommended that during the examination process of applications for the issuance of Final Approval Certificates, the competent authorities send only the necessary documents for its examination and not the entire file.  
4. For the investigation of the incident, the positions and opinions of the Labor Inspection Department (hereinafter referred to as the "Department") were requested regarding what the Municipality presented before us. According to the Department's letter dated September 11, 2023:  
4.1. The processing conducted during the examination of applications for the issuance of Final Approval Certificates falls under the cases of consultation of the competent authorities (e.g., Department of Town Planning and Housing, District Administrations, Municipal Authorities) with the Department, prior to the issuance of a planning permit or building permit. During the consultation, the Department's views are taken into account regarding whether the buildings and facilities of the requested development are suitable for use for the purposes proposed in the application, in accordance with the provisions of the Occupational Safety and Health Legislation. Therefore, the legal basis for processing personal data for the purposes of examining applications for the issuance of Final Approval Certificates is provided by the provisions of Article 55 of the Occupational Safety and Health Laws of 1996 to 2023 (N. 89(I)/1996) and thus is based on Article 6(1)(c) of the Regulation. The data that is necessary and collected by the Department includes the full name and contact details of the applicants and/or consultants.  
4.2. Instructions for this process and the required documents have been sent by the Department to the other Public Authorities and are also posted on its website (Department of Labor Inspection - Republic of Cyprus (mlsi.gov.cy)).  
- Department of Labor Inspection - Licenses / Certificates  
4.3. The actions and processing that the Department conducts during the examination of such applications are specific and relate to –  
- the recording of the applicant/consultant's details,  
- the study of the architectural plans,  
- the verification of compliance with the terms set by the Department previously.  
Consequently, the Department does not accept any of the Municipality's claims regarding document loss, as it does not remove any document, nor does it retain any copy of it.  

4.4. In the context of the consultation, the competent authorities may either send a copy of the application (with the applicant's details) and the relevant documents or send the entire file of the application under examination. In cases where the entire file is sent, no list of documents is sent by the competent authorities, nor is the totality of the documents contained in the files received by the provincial offices checked or recorded.  

4.5. The plans along with the relevant documents that are sent are returned to the Competent Authorities along with the response of the...
Provincial Offices. The Department considers the Municipality's complaint regarding the loss of documents from the Provincial Office of Labor Inspection of Limassol to be unfounded and unsubstantiated.

4.6. The Department also notes that as soon as the Provincial Office of Labor Inspection of Limassol received the relevant information from the Data Protection Officer of the Municipality regarding the incident in question, it proceeded to an immediate investigation and sent a relevant response providing its positions and opinions along with pertinent information.

4.7. In general, the directive for the circulation of service files and correspondence to the competent authorities stipulates that this is done either by mail, couriers, or representatives of the competent authorities. Since the Provincial Office of Labor Inspection of Limassol does not have a courier and the volume of the file did not allow for its dispatch by mail, the file should have been delivered to the Municipality of Ypsonas through its representative. In good faith and for the purposes of facilitating and expediting the process, the file was delivered closed in another envelope to the authorized manager of the current owners, referencing the Municipal Secretary of the Municipality of Ypsonas as the recipient.

4.8. The Department, for the purposes of avoiding similar incidents and ensuring an appropriate level of security, sent a letter on December 15, 2022, to all Provincial Offices of Labor Inspection, in which it emphasized that the files received for the purpose of examining applications for the issuance of approval certificates should only be delivered to couriers or representatives of the competent authorities.

4.9. Finally, the Department considers that the competent authorities are aware of the documents required to be submitted in the context of examining the aforementioned applications. It is up to them to select and submit only the necessary documents. In cases of the circulation of the entire file, the competent authorities are called upon to submit, along with the file, a detailed list of the documents it contains.

4.10. Attached to the Department's response were the correspondence to and from the Municipality dated 9/11/22 and 8/12/22, and a letter from the Department to the Provincial Offices of Labor Inspection dated 15/12/22.

5. On December 13, 2023, my Office requested some final clarifications from the Municipality.

5.1. On January 15, 2024, the Municipality stated that the file under examination was created by the Municipality, specifically by its technical services.

5.2. Regarding procedures related to the circulation of files and the archiving of documents in the file, the Municipality stated that it follows the procedures outlined in the "Manual of Organization and Operation of Archives in the Public Service" (points 4.4.19.2, 3.2.9, 4.4.12, and 4.4.15).

5.3. The Municipality emphasized that it is in continuous compliance with the provisions of the Regulation. Specifically, the Municipality, among other things, has adopted a policy and conducts staff training as well as training for the Municipal Council on an annual basis on matters related to the Regulation, maintains records of activities, and implements appropriate organizational and technical measures.

5.4. The "Manual of Organization and Operation of Archives in the Public Service" was attached to the Municipality's email.

Legal Framework

6.1. Article 4 of the Regulation defines the following:  
“‘processing’: any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure or destruction;  
‘data controller’: the natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data; where the purposes and means of such processing are determined by Union law or the law of a Member State, the data controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be provided for by Union law or the law of a Member State;  
‘personal data breach’: a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to, personal data which has been transmitted, stored or otherwise processed.”

Considerations

7.1. According to Article 4 of the Regulation, a public authority that determines the purposes and means of processing personal data constitutes a data controller. In the incident under investigation, the handling of the file was carried out by the Municipality and the Limassol District Labor Inspection Office, for the purpose of examining an application for the issuance of a Final Approval Certificate in accordance with the provisions of the Health and Safety at Work Laws of 1996 to 2023 (Law 89(I)/1996). The Municipality and the District Office, determining the purpose of collecting and using the data, carried out a series of processing operations during the handling and examination of the content of the file, during which they had the responsibility to ensure compliance with the Principles of personal data processing and the appropriate level of security against risks.

7.2. According to the positions presented in the Incidents of the Case of this Decision, the Limassol District Labor Inspection Office acted outside the instructions of the Department, delivering the file to the authorized manager of the current owners of the premises and not to the competent staff (clerk/representative of the Municipality). According to the authorizations submitted before me, the said person was authorized to manage and act on behalf of the current owners in all matters related to the licensing of the premises for which the application had been submitted. Therefore, although the delivery of the file to the authorized person does not automatically constitute a data breach, the act in question jeopardized the data of the individuals contained therein.

7.3. The positions of the Department and the Municipality do not agree on all points. The Municipality initially stated that 3 pages might be missing from the file, then noted that this is not certain. The Department, on the other hand, does not accept any such claim. The disagreement between the Department and the Municipality and the inability to ascertain any loss demonstrates a violation of Article 5 of the Regulation, since according to paragraphs (1)(f) and (2) of that Article, each data controller bears responsibility and must be able to demonstrate that personal data is processed securely and protected from
any loss (Principle of Integrity and Confidentiality and Principle of Accountability).

7.4. Appropriate measures (organizational and technical) must be taken to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing activities carried out are in compliance with the provisions of the Regulation. In this case, the Department, as mentioned, had given clear instructions to the Provincial Labor Inspection Offices to circulate the files via mail, couriers, or representatives of the Competent Authorities. The Provincial Office of Limassol acted outside of these instructions. The establishment of these instructions, as it appears, was not sufficient to safeguard the confidentiality, availability, and integrity of the data contained in the file, and therefore is deemed inadequate. Similarly, the delivery of the file by the Municipality through its courier, although to some extent protects the data from unauthorized processing, does not ensure their availability, since in the event of their loss, as indicated by the statements, there was no possibility of verifying the content of the file and replacing the documents.

7.5. Therefore, the Department's recommendation for a detailed inventory of the documents contained in the file is of essential importance. It is also deemed important for the municipal authorities to examine and adopt an appropriate procedure to prevent similar incidents in the future, based on which the retention of copies of the data could be provided for the protection of their availability and appropriate destruction when they are no longer necessary, retention of sheets/memos contained within the files, and numbering of documents or even sending copies of the documents if the submission of originals is not required. In any case, it is noted that the creation of an appropriate procedure and practices is the responsibility of the competent authorities acting as data controllers.

7.6. Regarding the volume of documents circulated between the Provincial Offices and the Municipalities, these should be the minimum necessary required to achieve the purpose, namely the examination of the application. According to Article 5(1)(c) of the Regulation, the data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purposes (Principle of Minimization). I am of the opinion that the file should be circulated in its entirety only in cases where the examination of the entire file is deemed necessary for the fulfillment of the intended purpose.

7.7. Taking into account the provisions of Article 32 of the Regulation, the data controller has the responsibility to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an adequate level of security against the risks that may arise during processing. These measures should be reassessed and updated at regular intervals. The data controller also takes measures to ensure that any natural person acting under their authority, who has access to personal data, processes it only on their instructions. In the incident under investigation, considering what has been presented to me, the measures taken are deemed insufficient, as they were not capable of ensuring the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the data, nor guaranteeing their replacement in the event of loss.

7.8. I emphasize that the responsibility for the protection and ensuring the security of the data was borne by both the Municipality of Ypsonas and the Department of Labor Inspection. Therefore, appropriate measures should have been taken by both parties to prevent and adequately address any incident of breach.

Conclusion

8.1. Based on the above findings and the powers conferred upon me by Articles 58 and 83 of the Regulation (EU) 2016/679, taking into account the analysis I have provided above, I find a violation of Articles 5(1)(c) and (f), 5(2), and 32 of the Regulation by the Municipality of Ypsonas.
