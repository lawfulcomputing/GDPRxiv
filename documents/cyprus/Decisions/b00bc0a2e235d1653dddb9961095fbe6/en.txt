DECISION

Case Incidents

Based on the duties and powers conferred upon me by Article 57(1)(f) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (hereinafter referred to as the "Regulation"), I examined a complaint submitted to my Office on April 28, 2021, regarding access to the account of the General Health System (GeSY) of Ms. XXX (hereinafter referred to as the "Complainant") by Dr. XXX (hereinafter referred to as the "Respondent").

Based on the investigation, I found a violation of the Regulation by the Respondent and, therefore, I issue this Decision.

18. As health data, as provided in the same article of the Regulation, are described as "personal data which relate to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, and which reveal information about the health status of that person."

**Reasoning**

**G.**

24. XXX.

25. XXX.

26. In the case under investigation, there is confirmation from the Organization that the Respondent had access to the beneficiary file of the Complainant, on the date and for the reason mentioned in paragraph 3 of this document.

An important element in this case is the fact that both the Complainant and the Respondent admit that each did not know the other and that the Respondent did not examine the Complainant.

27. The Respondent's claim that she could not know the Complainant's data if it had not been provided by the Complainant to her over the phone, and that it is likely that the Complainant had requested over the phone from the Respondent to see tests or analyses that had been conducted in order to receive medical advice, contradicts the Complainant's position that she never called the Respondent for consultation.

28. However, even if there was communication between the Complainant and the Respondent, the Respondent could not prove this or that she had authorization for the act she performed, since, as she stated, the Complainant's data is not in the records held by the Respondent's secretary, nor can she recall anything specific. Furthermore, the Respondent failed to demonstrate how the personal data of the Complainant came into her possession.

29. I must emphasize that the possession of the Complainant's data, as well as access to the beneficiary file of the Complainant in the General Health System (GeSY), constitutes acts of processing. Therefore, the Respondent's position that no action and/or processing of the Complainant's data took place is not valid.

30. Additionally, the elements mentioned by the Respondent do not affect the violation of the Complainant's data, namely that there was no malicious intent on the part of the Respondent, that medical confidentiality was maintained, that no benefit was derived from access to the Complainant's account, that there was no charge in the Complainant's file, nor the fact that, as confirmed by the Organization, no related claim was made by the Respondent for compensation.

31. As the Respondent stated, in the event that the Complainant claimed that she never spoke with the Respondent, then perhaps the specific incident could be attributed to a random error during the entry of data into the GeSY information system, while the Respondent was trying to locate another patient. However, considering that access to a beneficiary file requires the simultaneous entry of both the date of birth and the identity card number of the beneficiary, the possibility referred to by the Respondent cannot be accepted, as it is...
**Conclusion**  
The probability of this eventuality, namely the combination - verification of these two personal data of a beneficiary in the attempt to locate another beneficiary, is infinitesimal, if not negligible.

33. Taking into account all of the above, primarily that the Respondent was unable to prove that it lawfully obtained the personal data of the Complainant and that it had authorization to access the Complainant's beneficiary portal in the General Health System (ΓεΣΥ), it emerges that the personal data of the Complainant were not subjected to lawful and legitimate processing in a transparent manner. That is, the principle of "lawfulness, fairness, and transparency," as provided in Article 5(1)(a) of the Regulation, was not adhered to.

34. I note that no reasons or circumstances have been presented before me that should be taken into account in the context and for the purposes of imposing an administrative sanction, pursuant to Article 58(2) of the Regulation, since the Respondent did not respond to my prima facie Decision and to the relevant right to a hearing that was granted to it under Article 58(4) of the Regulation and Article 43 of the General Principles of Administrative Law Law of 1999, Law 158(I)/1999.
