III. Decision  
III./1. Applicability of the General Data Protection Regulation

(25) According to Article 99 (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the regulation must be applied from May 25, 2018. The documents affected by the data protection incident were made public on the website www.fotav.hu in the year 2017. The Obligor has maintained statistics on access to the documents since January 2017.

(26) The Obligor was informed that the documents on the website contain personal data from the Authority's decision NAIH/2020/5917/2 dated October 21, 2020. The data processing related to the incident, namely the accessibility of the documents, continued to exist even after the applicability of the General Data Protection Regulation. Therefore, the provisions of the regulation must be applied based on both the stipulations in Article 99 (2) and the provisions regarding the material scope in Article 2 (1).

(27) Furthermore, it should be noted that for the application of the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation regarding the handling of data protection incidents, the date of knowledge of the incident is the relevant point in time, as the prescribed legal consequences are tied to this date by the regulation. From this perspective, the fact of when the accessibility of the documents affected by the incident existed on the website and when the data collected from the affected individuals appeared is not a relevant factor. Both the occurrence of the incident and the inadequate level of data security posed risks to the rights and freedoms of the affected individuals even after the regulation became applicable.

III./2. Findings regarding the handling of the data protection incident

(28) According to point 12 of Article 4 of the General Data Protection Regulation, a data protection incident is defined as a breach of security that results in the unauthorized disclosure of personal data or unauthorized access to such data. In terms of the concept, the connection to the security event is considered a key element. An event involving personal data is only classified as a data protection incident if it can be linked to some form of security breach, which is the triggering cause, and there is a causal relationship between the two. The breach of security may arise from the incomplete, inadequate, or possibly outdated security measures applied for the protection of personal data or from their complete absence.

(29) In the given case, it is important to distinguish between two separate periods: the detection of the data protection incident and the measures taken to address it. The first period of the existence of the data protection incident extends from the public disclosure of the documents containing personal data on the website www.fotav.hu (January 2017) to the first detection by the whistleblower, then by the Authority, and finally by the Obligor, and the subsequent conduct of the related authority inspection. During this period, according to the records and statements taken by the Legal Predecessor, the deletion of the affected documents (which is the...
10

In practice, this only meant the deletion of links pointing to those on the website) took place on October 30, 2020. During this period of more than three and a half years, there were a total of 201 accesses to the documents.

(30) The second period of detecting the data protection incident dates from August 11, 2022, from the second public interest notification, until the time when the Obligor permanently deleted all documents containing personal data from the background server as well. This occurred on August 18, 2022, concerning the document containing the data of the public interest notifier [...], and based on the Obligor's statements, at the latest by December 7, 2022, concerning the documents containing the personal data of other affected individuals.

(31) In the present cases, the security breach arose because the Predecessor did not implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that documents and reports qualifying as public interest data under Section 3(5) of the Infotv. related to the performance of its public duties were properly anonymized, with any potentially non-public personal data redacted. The Obligor identified the reason for this as an administrative omission. The security breach persisted despite the deletion of the links from the website, as the documents remained accessible via the internet with the appropriate reference or, for example, through a name search on Google. This was due to the fact that the documents were still stored on the background server serving the website, in the document repository. These security breaches resulted in unauthorized access to the personal data contained in the documents, thus leading to a data protection incident.

(32) Article 33 of the General Data Protection Regulation contains provisions regarding the measures to be taken in the event of a data protection incident occurring and becoming known to the data controller. According to paragraph (1), the data controller is obliged to notify the supervisory authority of the data protection incident without undue delay and, if possible, no later than 72 hours after becoming aware of the data protection incident. Notification of the incident may only be omitted if the incident is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. Assessing the risks associated with the incident is the responsibility of the data controller.

(33) The Obligor first became aware of the data protection incident when it was notified by the Authority with its decision No. NAIH/2020/5917/2 dated October 21, 2020. As a result, the Obligor reported it electronically on October 30, 2020, which was supplemented electronically on November 6.

(34) Article 33(3) of the General Data Protection Regulation states what information the data controller must provide to the Authority when reporting the data protection incident. According to point d) of this paragraph, it must be stated what measures the data controller has taken or plans to take to remedy the incident, including, where appropriate, measures aimed at mitigating any adverse consequences resulting from the data protection incident.

(35) In its incident report, as well as in its responses sent to the Authority, the Obligor described the measures taken to remedy the incident and mitigate adverse consequences. Among these, it was described that after it was established that personal data appeared in the reports without a legal basis and specific purpose, their deletion was ordered.
11

which was carried out by the PR and communications staff according to the report prepared on this matter. In its response dated November 6, 2020, the Obligor also emphasized that "the part of the website where the contested personal data appeared was immediately deleted." The protocol recorded on October 30, 2020, states that "the immediate cessation of the infringement, that is, the deletion of all unnecessary and the affected reports from the website is necessary from this link: [link3]. […] The task was completed by the PR and communications staff today at 10:30." The response and the report do not indicate that the Obligor's staff only deleted the links on the website pointing to the documents, but not the documents themselves. The Authority did not subsequently verify the unavailability of these documents and accepted the Obligor's statements regarding this matter.

(36) In addition, the Obligor also took measures regarding the deletion of documents available on www.docplayer.hu, resulting in the deletion of the documents from there. The Authority also verified the unavailability of these documents, a fact that is included in the note concluding the authority's inspection.

(37) The deletion of the documents, as a risk-reducing measure taken in response to the incident, was accepted by the Authority based on the statements made by the Obligor during the authority's inspection. The Authority deemed that, based on the information available at the time, the Obligor took appropriate measures to reduce the reported risks to the privacy of the affected individuals and handled the incident in accordance with Articles 33-34 of the General Data Protection Regulation. In light of this, the Authority concluded the inspection.

(38) Later, in connection with the second public interest notification, on August 11, 2022, the Authority learned that the documents were still accessible through the previous access routes pointing to the www.fotav.hu website. During the authority procedure initiated in this regard, the Obligor informed the Authority that although the documents involved in the incident were not directly accessible on the website, they were still available through the precise knowledge of the previous access routes and as a result of searches on Google. The reason for this is that although the links to the erroneously published reports were deleted from the website earlier, the files themselves were still retrievable on the backend server, and they were not deleted from there. Following the new notification and the results of a repeated internal investigation, the Obligor ultimately deleted all documents from the document repository on the backend server serving the website.

(39) According to Article 33(3)(d) of the General Data Protection Regulation, when reporting a data protection incident, the data controller must outline the measures taken to reduce the risks of the incident. During the Authority's inspection, the Obligor informed the Authority both in the incident report and during the clarification of the facts that it had deleted personal data processed without legal basis and purpose. The risks, therefore, in this case, that unnecessary personal data could be found publicly through the Obligor's website on the internet, can only be adequately reduced by a deletion that results in the actual data being deleted/anonymized from the affected documents and not just the references pointing to them. This is also important because the notifier, who is also an affected individual, found their own data as a result of a Google search.

adatait. The removal of the links is therefore neither proportional to the risks nor an appropriate incident management measure.

(40) Since the inadequate incident management measure only came to the Authority's attention afterwards, it could not be assessed during the previous official inspection. The obligated party's incident management does not comply with Article 33(3)(d) of the General Data Protection Regulation, as it failed to take measures that are proportional to the risks and adequately mitigate them during the reporting and handling of the incident, and therefore provided the Authority with inadequate and misleading information about them. This is independent of whether the inadequate incident management and reporting resulted from intentional concealment or from a negligent lack of due diligence in internal procedures.

(41) Therefore, in this context, the Authority establishes that the obligated party violated Article 33(3)(d) of the General Data Protection Regulation (regarding the assessment and management of the risks of the incident) during the reporting and handling of the data protection incident that came to its attention.

III./3. Findings regarding the security of data processing

(42) Article 32 of the General Data Protection Regulation prescribes the requirements related to the security of data processing. According to paragraph (1) of this article, it is the responsibility of the data controller to implement technical and organizational measures that ensure a level of data security appropriate to the risks, taking into account the state of the science and technology and the costs of implementation. According to paragraph (2) of the above article, these security measures must guarantee, among other things, that personal data transmitted cannot be accessed unlawfully. Appropriate security measures may include, based on points (a) and (b) of paragraph (1) of the above article, the encryption of personal data and ensuring the continuous confidentiality of the systems and services used for processing personal data. The purpose of these measures is also to minimize the risks to the privacy of the affected individuals in the event of a potential data protection incident arising from a security breach.

(43) In relation to the examined data processing, it can be established that the protocols involved in the data protection incident were found in the possession of the obligated party because they were generated in connection with the performance of public duties, and therefore qualify as documents of public interest under Section 3(5) of the Infotv. However, these public interest documents included personal data (data of natural persons affected by the investigation), which do not fall under the concepts of either public interest data or publicly accessible data. Based on this, the inspection protocols therefore contain personal data that do not qualify as public interest data.
13

The Obliged Party could have only made the minutes public on its website based on its own statements if it anonymized certain personal data contained therein, for example, by deleting/redacting them.

(44) In the present case, the Obliged Party assessed and managed the risks of the examined data processing operation by regulating in its procedural order concerning the personal data contained in the minutes that they must be anonymized before being made public.

(45) However, the above measure taken by the Obliged Party to reduce risks did not materialize, as the personal data contained in the minutes were not anonymized. According to the Obliged Party, this was due to carelessness during the administrative processes related to the redaction of the data. As a result, the internal measure taken to reduce risks (the redaction of personal data from the report) could not be effective due to this carelessness.

(46) Due to the carelessness exhibited during the administrative processes, the personal data were thus accessible on the website. As soon as the Obliged Party became aware of this from the Authority's order during the official inspection, it took steps to ensure that they would no longer be accessible, thus leading to their deletion. The Obliged Party attempted to resolve this by deleting the links to the documents on its website; however, it did not delete the documents themselves. This measure, however, was inadequate to properly guarantee the security of the personal data and to definitively eliminate the existence of the data protection incident, as the documents were still retrievable later on the internet.

(47) According to the Authority, the security of personal data would have been guaranteed if the Obliged Party had anonymized them in accordance with its own internal procedures, i.e., by redacting them from the minutes classified as public interest data. However, the Obliged Party did not act accordingly, but rather only deleted the links to the minutes from the website, which did not comply with its internal rules nor was suitable for eliminating the incident.

(48) The measures taken by the Obliged Party therefore did not ensure a level of data security proportional to the risks reported regarding the privacy of the affected individuals, which was also assessed by the Obliged Party. The mere removal of the links is not a sufficient measure to guarantee the security of the data; they should have either been properly anonymized or deleted from the server serving the website. The latter measure was only taken by the Obliged Party upon the Authority's repeated request and the initiation of the official procedure.

(49) A data security measure proportional to the risks would have been the proper anonymization of personal data or the deletion of such data from the background server. If the documents do not contain personal data, or if such data do not exist on the background server, they could not have been retrieved through the Google search engine, for example, by searching for names.

(50) In light of the above, the Authority finds that the Obliged Party violated Articles 32 (1)-(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation. This is because it did not apply technical and organizational measures appropriate to the state of science and technology and proportional to the risks concerning the anonymization of the minutes, thus the personal data contained therein remained accessible on the internet.

(51) Beyond establishing the violation, the Authority does not apply any further admonition towards the Applicant, as the unlawful state has already been eliminated by the deletion of the documents in the meantime.

III./5. Legal Consequences

(52) The Authority, during the clarification of the facts, established that the Obliged Party violated the General Data Protection Regulation
- Articles 32 (1)-(2), as well as
- Article 33 (3) (d).

(53) The Authority examined ex officio whether it was justified to impose a data protection fine on the Applicant concerning the established violation. In this regard, the Authority considered all circumstances of the case based on Article 83 (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation and Section 75/A of the Infotv.

(54) In view of this, the Authority decided, based on Section 61 (1) (a) of the Infotv., as stated in the operative part, and in this decision, it obliged the Applicant to pay a data protection fine.

(55) In determining the necessity of imposing the fine, the Authority considered the aggravating, mitigating, and other circumstances of the violations as follows:

(56) Aggravating circumstances:

- The Authority considers the data security deficiencies as a problem that persisted for a long time (between January 2017 and August 2022), and the Obliged Party was already aware of the data protection incident that occurred in this regard during the first official inspection in 2020, yet it did not carry out the appropriate risk-reducing measures. [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83 (2) (a) and (d)]

- The Authority learned of the incident and the data security deficiencies for the first time through the complaints of the affected parties and not through the data controller's incident report. [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83 (2) (h)]

- The same circumstances at the Obliged Party, namely the publication of inadequately anonymized documents, caused the same data protection incident multiple times, which is primarily the result of previous negligent incident management. The Authority assesses this as severely negligent data controller behavior. [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83 (2) (b) and (d)]
15

(57) Mitigating circumstances:

- The infringing practice did not affect a large number of data subjects; a total of 31 natural persons' names and addresses were disclosed in the public release of the records without proper anonymization. [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(a)]

- The Authority has not previously imposed a data protection fine on the Obligor for violating the General Data Protection Regulation. [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(e)]

- The personal data involved in the infringement did not pertain to aspects of the data subjects' private lives that could provide insight into more sensitive areas (e.g., special categories of data as defined in General Data Protection Regulation Article 9(1)). [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(g)]

(58) In making its decision regarding the legal consequences, the Authority did not consider the provisions of General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(c), (i), (j), and (k) to be relevant.

(59) In light of the above, the Authority deems it necessary to impose a fine, considering that merely applying a warning under Section 75/A of the Infotv. was not deemed appropriate or proportional to the severity and circumstances of the infringement.

(60) The amount of the data protection fine was determined by the Authority in the exercise of its statutory discretion.

(61) In determining the amount of the fine, the Authority took into account that the infringements committed by the Obligor qualify as violations falling under the lower maximum fine category according to General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(4).

(62) The Authority considered the provisions contained in the European Data Protection Board's Guidelines 04/2022, which outline the calculation criteria for administrative fines under the General Data Protection Regulation. In this regard, the determination of the fine amount was based on the following criteria:

- The Obligor's net sales revenue for the year 2020 was 44,863,391,000 HUF, that is, forty-four billion eight hundred sixty-three million three hundred ninety-one thousand forints. The imposed fine is 0.035% of this amount.

- The Obligor's net sales revenue for the year 2021 was 62,644,955,000 HUF, that is, sixty-two billion six hundred forty-four million nine hundred fifty-five thousand forints. The imposed fine is 0.025% of this amount.

- The Obligor's net sales revenue for the year 2022 was 117,703,589,000 HUF, that is, one hundred seventeen billion seven hundred three million five hundred eighty-nine thousand forints. The imposed fine is 0.013% of this amount.

- The Authority took into account the period during which the infringement occurred, as well as publicly available management data, considering the years 2020, 2021, and 2022 at the time of the decision.

- According to General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(4), the Obligor may be subject to an administrative fine of "up to 10,000,000 EUR [static maximum fine], or in the case of enterprises, an amount not exceeding 2% of the total annual worldwide turnover of the preceding financial year [dynamic maximum fine]; the higher amount shall be imposed."

- 2% of the Obligor's revenue for 2022 is 2,354,071,780 HUF (that is, two billion three hundred fifty-four million seventy-one thousand seven hundred eighty forints), which, at the current HUF/EUR exchange rate, does not exceed the so-called static maximum fine set by the regulation, that is, an amount equivalent to 10,000,000 EUR. Since the fine must be calculated and proportioned based on the higher category applicable in this case, the Obligor may be subject to an administrative fine of up to an amount equivalent to 10,000,000 EUR.

- According to the Guidelines, for infringements that fall under the fine category of General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(4), where the static fine maximum of 10,000,000 EUR is applicable, further examination is required regarding the previous year's net revenue of the enterprise, as well as the weight of the infringement (low, medium, or significant weight).

- The Authority assessed the infringements committed, the established facts, and the aggravating and mitigating circumstances relevant to the case, deeming the infringements in this case to be of low weight.

- In this case, the Obligor's net revenue for the previous year was 117,703,589,000 HUF, that is, one hundred seventeen billion seven hundred three million five hundred eighty-nine thousand forints, which, calculated at the current HUF/EUR exchange rate, falls within the category between 250,000,000 EUR and 500,000,000 EUR as indicated by the Guidelines regarding the consideration of enterprises' management data.

- According to the Guidelines, if the enterprise's previous year's net revenue falls between 250,000,000 EUR and 500,000,000 EUR, the maximum amount of the fine that can be imposed for low-weight infringements is reduced to 1,000,000 EUR.
17

- In this case, the Obligor may be subject to a fine of up to 1,000,000 EUR, considering the above-mentioned economic data and the minor violations committed.

- The imposed fine is therefore proportional to the severity of the violation and cannot be considered excessive; it is an extremely negligible amount compared to the economic weight of the Obligor and the financial resources available to it.
