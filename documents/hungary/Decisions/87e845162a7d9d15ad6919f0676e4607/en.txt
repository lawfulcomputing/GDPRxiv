DECISION  

The National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Authority) […] (hereinafter: Applicant) submitted a request to the Authority on July 4, 2022, regarding the examination of the source of personal data processed by […] (headquarters: […]; company registration number: […]; tax number: […]; hereinafter: Respondent). Furthermore, the Authority initiated an ex officio data protection authority procedure to investigate the compliance of the Respondent's general data processing practices concerning the collection of users' personal data with the Regulation (EU) 2016/679 on the protection of individuals with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (hereinafter: General Data Protection Regulation), and makes the following decision:  

I. The Authority establishes, at the request of the Applicant, that the Respondent has violated Article 15(1)(g) of the General Data Protection Regulation.  

II. The Authority establishes ex officio that the Respondent has violated Article 5(2) and Article 24(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation.  

III. The Authority instructs the Respondent ex officio to align its data processing operations with the provisions of the General Regulation in accordance with the reasoning of this decision and to provide evidence of compliance to the Authority.  

IV. Due to the violations established in points I and II, the Authority imposes an administrative fine of  

1,000,000 HUF, that is, one million forints  

on the Respondent.  

* * *  

The Respondent must take the measures prescribed in point III within 30 days from the finalization of this decision and must provide written evidence – along with supporting documents – to the Authority.  

The administrative fine must be paid within 30 days from the finalization of this decision to the Authority's centralized revenue collection account (10032000-01040425-00000000 Centralized Collection Account IBAN: HU83 1003 2000 0104 0425 0000 0000). When transferring the amount, reference must be made to the NAIH-2880/2023. FINE number.  

If the Respondent fails to meet the payment obligation within the deadline, it is obliged to pay a late fee to the above account number. The amount of the late fee is the statutory interest, which corresponds to the central bank base rate valid on the first day of the calendar half-year affected by the delay.  

In case of non-compliance with the obligation in point III, as well as non-payment of the administrative fine and the late fee, the Authority orders the enforcement of the decision.  

No procedural costs were incurred during the data protection authority procedure, therefore the Authority did not rule on the bearing of such costs.  

There is no administrative remedy against this decision, but it can be challenged in an administrative lawsuit before the Metropolitan Court within 30 days from the communication of the decision by submitting a statement of claim addressed to the Metropolitan Court. The statement of claim must be submitted electronically to the Authority, which will forward it to the court along with the case documents. A request for a hearing must be indicated in the statement of claim. For those not fully exempt from personal fees, the fee for the administrative lawsuit is 30,000 HUF, and the subject matter of the lawsuit is subject to fee exemption. Legal representation is mandatory in proceedings before the Metropolitan Court.

III. Applicable Legal Provisions

(71) According to Article 2(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the General Data Protection Regulation must be applied to the data processing in this case.

(72) Pursuant to Section 2(2) of Act CXII of 2011 on the Right to Informational Self-Determination and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Infotv.), the General Data Protection Regulation must be applied with the supplements specified therein.

(73) According to Section 60(1) of the Infotv., in order to ensure the enforcement of the right to the protection of personal data, the Authority shall initiate a data protection authority procedure upon request. The rules of Act CL of 2016 on General Administrative Procedure (hereinafter: Ákr.) shall apply to the data protection authority procedure, with the supplements defined in the Infotv. and the deviations according to the General Data Protection Regulation.

**IV. Decisions**

(89) According to the definitions in the General Data Protection Regulation, the data recorded by the Respondent concerning the Applicant and other natural person consumers for the purpose of providing district heating services qualifies as personal data, and any operation performed on personal data, including the storage and use of personal data, is considered data processing.

**IV.1. The Applicant's Access Request**

(90) In this case, the Applicant approached the Respondent in accordance with Article 15(3) of the General Data Protection Regulation to obtain a copy of the personal data processed by the Respondent, as well as the consent statement, specifically a copy of the data request form used by the Respondent, considering that, in the Applicant's view, the Respondent processes the Applicant's personal data without consent. The Applicant received information from the Respondent that led them to conclude that – since they themselves did not submit such a form – the Applicant's personal data was submitted by someone else on the data request form; however, the Respondent could not prove the source of the Applicant's personal data. Subsequently, the Applicant sought to learn the source of their personal data from the Respondent multiple times, but aside from receiving detailed information about the processing of their personal data, they did not receive a clear answer.

(91) In response to the Applicant's letter dated November 29, 2021, the Respondent provided information in a letter dated December 21, 2021, regarding the source of the Applicant's personal data, stating that "the consumer data related to district heating services, some of which are also personal data, is primarily processed by […] as the data controller based on the voluntary consent of the consumers. These consumer (personal) data could be submitted to the Data Controller by consumers by filling out the data request form and sending it by post or submitting it in person at our customer service offices."

(92) In response to the Applicant's letter dated December 27, 2021, the Respondent provided the same information regarding the source of the Applicant's personal data in a letter dated January 10, 2022, with the addition that "considering that […] does not process your personal data based on Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR, we are unable to fulfill your request to provide your consent statement, as our data processing is not based on the consent of the data subject."

IV.2. General Data Processing Practice for Collecting Users' Personal Data  

(102) Article 5 of the General Data Protection Regulation contains the main principles that must be considered during the processing of personal data, and which must continuously apply throughout the data processing.  

(103) An additional requirement for the lawfulness of data processing is that the processing must be based on one of the legal grounds specified in Article 6(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, and the data controller must provide appropriate information regarding the data processing.  

(104) The Respondent referred to the legal grounds for processing the personal data of the Applicant and consumers under Article 6(1)(c), (e), and (a) of the General Data Protection Regulation.  

(105) In this regard, it can be established that the legal basis for data processing related to the provision of district heating services as a public task is Article 6(1)(e) of the General Data Protection Regulation, considering the District Heating Act and its implementing regulation, which define the details of data processing related to the provision of district heating services. Therefore, this mandatory data processing is carried out by the Respondent on an appropriate legal basis.  

(106) This is different from the contested and exceptional data processing operation in the present case, where the Respondent attempted to collect consumers' personal data for the provision of the service. During the issues related to the change of service provider, the previous utility provider, [Company], did not transfer the personal data of the affected individuals to the new provider, the Respondent. Therefore, the Respondent sent a data request form to the […] consumer locations to obtain the personal data of consumers necessary for fulfilling its utility service obligations. This form was distributed by the Respondent through various channels, published in press products, made available for download on its website, and placed in mailboxes concerning district heating consumption locations. The data provided on the data request form was recorded manually into a database created by the Respondent, which was subsequently migrated to the billing software database. The Respondent then stores the consumer personal data exclusively in the software database, as it has destroyed paper-based data carriers to prevent data protection incidents. The Respondent based this data collection through data request forms on the consent of the affected individuals. However, since the legal basis for the Respondent's data processing is Article 6(1)(e) of the General Data Protection Regulation, this specific data collection is also considered to fall under this legal basis.  

(107) The Authority finds it acceptable that the Respondent collected consumer personal data through data request forms. However, this data collection did not exempt the Respondent from the obligation to comply with the requirements of the General Data Protection Regulation during the data collection process.  

(108) Such a requirement is appropriate information. Appropriate information is that through which the affected individuals become aware of the data processing related to their personal data, and the information provided...
**18**  
The right to informational self-determination can be exercised through this. The requirement for prior information of the data subject is detailed in Article 13 of the General Data Protection Regulation.

(109) The Authority reviewed the data processing information prepared in connection with the data collection related to the data request form, from which it can be established that the Applicant provided detailed information regarding the data processing related to district heating services. However, the process of data collection through the data request forms differs from this, and therefore a suitable data processing information document would have been necessary for that as well. However, the Applicant did not have such a document.

(110) It would have been appropriate for the data subject to fill out the data request form with knowledge of the information and to submit it to the Applicant. Furthermore, the Applicant should have ensured the enforcement of principles and guarantees, such as ensuring compliance with the principle of accuracy.

(111) However, this does not mean that the Applicant's mandatory data processing related to district heating services is entirely unlawful. Data collection is a separate specific data processing operation in terms of time and activity, but once the personal data was recorded in the Applicant's registry, the data processing continued as necessary mandatory data processing for the execution of the Applicant's public interest task, in accordance with general rules.

(112) Furthermore, reviewing the statements made by the Applicant regarding the source of the personal data of the Requester and other consumers, and the content of point IV.1 of the reasoning of this decision, it can be established that the Applicant was unable to prove that the Requester and other affected consumers provided their personal data through the data request form.

(113) This is supported by the fact that the Applicant, immediately after recording the data provided on the data request forms in its electronic system, destroyed the forms on the grounds of avoiding data protection incidents. However, in the absence of the data request forms, as this case also demonstrates, the Applicant is unable to provide a clear answer regarding whether the personal data of the Requester and other consumers was indeed collected based on their consent from the data request forms; it can only be assumed.

(114) According to the accountability requirement under Article 5(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller is responsible for compliance with data protection principles and must be able to demonstrate such compliance. Accordingly, the data controller is obliged to document and record the data processing in such a way that its legality can be proven later.

(115) Pursuant to Article 24(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and demonstrate that the processing of personal data complies with the requirements of the General Data Protection Regulation. These measures must be reviewed by the data controller and updated as necessary. Accordingly, the data controller must incorporate measures and guarantees into the data processing process that facilitate compliance with data protection principles and the exercise of the rights of data subjects.

(116) In connection with data collection, the fact that the Applicant collected data from the Requester and consumers on data request forms can be clearly evidenced by the storage of the data request forms, and thus it can also be clearly evidenced that the source of the personal data is indeed the data request form. Additionally, the data request forms need to be retained for a certain period due to the necessity of ensuring further rights of data subjects – in the Authority's view, at least until the resolution of issues related to the service provider change – since, for example, in the case of a typographical error, the data request form...
19  
can certify that the erroneous recording in the electronic register occurred based on it. The Authority's position is that the mere storage of data request forms for these purposes—while maintaining data security requirements, in a locked manner, with appropriate protection and regulation—cannot cause a data protection incident.

(117) The Applicant also stated that it was unable to individually verify the authenticity of the data requests that arrived in extraordinary quantities, and could only perform random checks; however, upon notification from the data subjects, it promptly deleted the erroneous, inaccurate personal data. Regarding this practice, the Authority's position is that the lack of authenticity could lead to a data protection incident, as the Applicant may store data of unidentified individuals—potentially submitted by others—and cannot prove that the data originates from the data controller, nor does it adequately ensure the rights of the data subjects. If no request from the data subjects is received for data correction, the Applicant may store incorrectly recorded personal data without correction for an indefinite period. Therefore, random checks do not ensure the accuracy of the data.

(118) Based on all of this, the Applicant—due to the procedure it established—was unable and cannot prove that the Applicant and other natural person consumers actually provided their personal data to the Applicant through the data request form, or that they consented to the data collection. Thus, beyond not being able to prove to the data subjects what or who the source of their personal data is, it does not store information regarding whether it collected the data from the data subjects or from others, and it cannot present or prove this to the Authority, nor can it demonstrate how many completed data request forms it received in total. Despite the Authority specifically requesting that the Applicant send the consent statement regarding the processing of the Applicant's personal data—along with the data request form—the Applicant was unable to do so. With this practice, the Applicant also hinders the exercise of the rights of the data subjects.

(119) In light of all of this, the Authority officially establishes that the Applicant, through its data collection practice via data request forms, has violated the principle of accountability under Article 5(2) and Article 24(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, as it did not take appropriate technical and organizational measures and is unable to prove the source of the personal data of the data subjects, as well as the authenticity and accuracy of the personal data.

(120) According to the Authority, the Applicant's data collection practice would have complied with the requirements set out in Article 5(2) and Article 24(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation if it could have proven that the data subject consented to the data collection, which could have been evidenced by the storage of the data request forms, or for example, by recording an entry in the Applicant's electronic register indicating that the data subject consented to the data collection, thus the source of the data is the data request form. Accordingly, in any future potential data collections, the Applicant needs to change its data processing practice, ensuring the accuracy of the data and generally the exercise of the rights of the data subjects.

- In this case, the Applicant's net revenue from the previous year was 8,003,866,000 HUF, which, calculated at the current HUF/EUR exchange rate, falls into the category between 10,000,000 EUR and 50,000,000 EUR as indicated by the Guidelines, considering the management data of the enterprises.
