DECISION  

Before the National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Authority)  
………………..……… applicant (address: ……………………….…………………………; hereinafter: Applicant) submitted a request via ePaper to the Vác Mayor's Office Public Area Supervision Department (headquarters: 2600 Vác, Március 15. tér 11.; hereinafter: Respondent) on March 3, 2023, regarding the legality of data processing related to the purchase of an online parking permit available under the E-Permit section of the website https://www.vac.hu/ (https://eberlet.mininform.hu/vac/site/login - hereinafter: Website). Based on this request (hereinafter: application), a data protection authority procedure was initiated. The Authority included the Municipality of Vác (headquarters: 2600 Vác, Március 15. tér 11.; hereinafter: Municipality) as a party in the above data protection procedure on May 12, 2023. The Authority makes the following decisions in the above data protection authority procedure:  

I. The Authority rejects the Applicant's request against the Respondent, as the Respondent is not considered the data controller.  

II. The Authority establishes ex officio that the Municipality violated the provisions of Article 5(1)(c), Article 12(1), and Article 13(1)(a) and (c) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter: General Data Protection Regulation) regarding the protection of natural persons in relation to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, as well as the repeal of Directive 95/46/EC.  

III. The Authority instructs the Municipality ex officio, based on Article 58(2)(d) of the General Data Protection Regulation, to provide information on the Website regarding the online parking permit service in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation, and to modify its data processing practices related to the Website in accordance with the principle of data minimization, and to confirm this to the Authority within 30 days of receiving this decision.  

IV. Due to the data protection violations stated in point II above, the Authority imposes an ex officio data protection fine on the Municipality of  

500,000 HUF, that is five hundred thousand forints.

III.  
Decision  

1. Established Facts  

1.1. Description of Data Processing and Scope of Personal Data Processed  

(23)  
On ……………. 2023, the Applicant registered on the Website for the purpose of purchasing a parking permit for an underground garage. During the mandatory preliminary registration, the Applicant was required to provide several personal data, including their name, address, contact details, tax number qualification, and to upload a complete copy of both sides of the vehicle registration certificate, as well as copies of their address card, identity card, and tax card. Registration is not possible without accepting the terms of use, at the end of which the following user declaration is found: “I have read and accept the terms of use, and I consent to the processing of my personal data provided on the web interface by the Service Provider for the purpose of utilizing the service.”

(24)  
The terms of use available on the Website do not clearly designate a specific data controller; Vác city, the Applicant, the undefined “Service Provider,” and the Representative Body of Vác City Municipality are all mentioned, but their relationship to data processing and to each other is not clarified. At the bottom of the Website, the sentence “The Váci Mayor's Office Public Area Supervision is operated by Mininform Kft.” is found,
6  
while the Website's "Contact" menu item directs to the vac.hu homepage, where the "Public Space Supervision" appears as one of the departments of the Váci Mayor's Office, which employs public officials. According to the laws referenced on the Website, the operation of the parking garage affected by the lease is managed by Váci Városfejlesztő Kft. based on Section 6 (3) of the municipal decree 32/2017 (XII.15.) of the Vác City Council.

(25)  
Following the confirmation of the preliminary registration, on ……………. 2023, the Applicant purchased the annual parking permit at full price on the Website, without being entitled to any discounts. The Applicant received the confirmation and the invoice for the purchase of the permit via email, with the issuer of the invoice being the Municipality of Vác. Based on the Applicant's experience, entry to the Vác parking garage is possible through an access card, via an electronic access control system, and license plate recognition cameras are also operational during vehicle entry.

(26)  
Based on the statements received by the Municipality on May 31, 2023, registered under NAIH-3883-5/2023, the role of the Respondent was to signal the problem and provide the technical and administrative background, while the Municipality's role involved organizational tasks and the creation of the parking regulations. During the development of online administration, the Municipality did not find any option that would eliminate the temporary handling of document copies for the verification of identification data. A copy of the tax card, specifically the tax identification number on it, was necessary for verifying the payment of the vehicle tax. The parking discount is linked not to vehicles but to the operator, and since one person can own multiple vehicles, it cannot be verified based solely on a vehicle's license plate whether that person has fulfilled their vehicle tax obligations for any other vehicles. The Municipality has since reviewed this necessity and has eliminated the requirement for the tax identification number and tax card copy. Following the Authority's inquiry, the Municipality placed new data management information on the website https://vac.hu/vac/dok_nyomtatvanyok.html.

1.2. The identity of the data controller

(27)  
Based on the details in paragraphs (25) and (26) above, the Authority established that the purpose and means of data processing through the examined Website were determined by the Municipality. All other contributors merely technically executed the Municipality's decision according to the conditions set by the Municipality, and only the Municipality is authorized to decide on the provision or cessation of the service in the context of fulfilling public tasks and exercising public authority.

(28)  
The Municipality can establish numerous bodies and legal entities based on the relevant laws; however, this does not affect the fact that decisions are generally made by the Municipality's highest body or by a person authorized by it, unless a law specifically assigns a task to another entity (for example, to the mayor). In the present case, there is no law that assigns parking-related tasks to another entity; it specifically depends on the Municipality's decision. This is further supported by the fact that the fundamental rules of parking are established by the regulation referenced in paragraph (24) above, and all other contributors can only make decisions within that framework for execution.

(29)  
Based on the above, the Authority established that the data controller for the examined data processing is the Municipality.
7

2. Rejection of the Request Against the Respondent

(30) Since the Respondent is not the data controller of the data processing complained of by the Applicant, the Respondent could not have violated the points of the General Data Protection Regulation indicated by the Applicant.

(31) In light of the above, the Authority rejected the request and conducted an ex officio investigation into the general data processing practices related to the Website concerning the Municipality as the data controller.

3. Ex Officio Investigated Violations Concerning the Municipality

3.1. Violation of Article 12(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation

(32) According to Article 12(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller is required to take appropriate measures to provide the data subject with all information regarding the processing of personal data mentioned in Article 13, as well as every piece of information under Articles 15–22 and 34, in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible form, presented clearly and in plain language.

(33) The system of appropriate information in the General Data Protection Regulation serves to ensure that the data subject is aware of which personal data is being processed, by which data controller, for what purpose, on what legal basis, and how it will be processed. This is essential for the data subject to be in a position to effectively exercise their rights.

(34) The Authority found that, as detailed in the above paragraph (24), there is no clear and precise indication on the Website and the related https://www.vac.hu/ website regarding who qualifies as the data controller concerning the online parking permit service (and parking). Average data subjects cannot clearly and unambiguously draw the conclusion about who the data controller is in relation to the examined data processing, and the Authority was only able to determine this through a comprehensive clarification of the facts.

(35) The information available on the Website and the Municipality's related website (https://www.vac.hu/) as well as in the parking-related documents and the invoice sent to the Applicant identified several, a total of four participants (the Respondent, the Municipality, Mininform Ltd., Váci City Development Ltd.); however, their exact roles in data processing and operation could not be identified based on this. This does not constitute clear, transparent, and concise information about the fundamental circumstances of the data processing, particularly regarding the identity of the data controller, to whom, for example, data subject requests could be directed. The fact that local municipalities generally operate public parking is not specific enough and does not always apply to exempt the Municipality as the data controller from the obligation to provide this fundamental information in the specific case, especially when the simultaneous identification of several participants causes even greater confusion than if none were identified.

(36) In light of the above, the Authority, according to the operative part, established ex officio the violation of Article 12(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, as the information provided to the data subjects on the Website did not contain transparent, clearly, and intelligibly formulated information about the
8  
regarding the identity of the data controller and generally the responsibilities of the various actors involved in data processing.  

3.2. Violation of Article 13(1)(a) and (c) of the General Data Protection Regulation  

(37)  
According to Article 13(1) and (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller is obliged to provide certain fundamental data processing information (e.g., the identity of the data controller, legal basis) to the data subjects in an easily accessible manner when collecting personal data directly from the data subjects.  

(38)  
Based on the explanations provided in paragraphs (24) and (35) above, the information available on the Website does not clearly specify the identity of the data controller and incorrectly identifies the legal basis for data processing as the consent of the data subject. Considering that the data controller acts in the exercise of public authority – also taking into account the explanations in the decision NAIH/2019/2076/11 available on the Authority's website – the legal basis for data processing in this case can only be Article 6(1)(e) of the General Data Protection Regulation, as the Municipality operates public parking as a state monopoly, exercising public authority powers. The incorrect designation of the legal basis is evaluated by the Authority in the context of Article 13 of the General Data Protection Regulation.  

(39)  
In the case of data processing based on Article 6(1)(e) of the General Data Protection Regulation, a heightened expectation of information applies to a data controller performing public authority tasks, who does not process data based on the consent of the data subject, compared to a data controller that processes the personal data of the data subject alongside the data subject's right to control. In the absence of adequate information, the data subject is understandably not in a position to exercise their rights appropriately.  

(40)  
In light of the above, the Authority established, upon request, the violation of Article 13(1)(a) and (c) of the General Data Protection Regulation as stated in the operative part.  

3.3. Violation of Article 5(1)(c) of the General Data Protection Regulation  

(41)  
The purpose of the principle of data minimization under Article 5(1)(c) of the General Data Protection Regulation is to ensure that the data subjects' right to the protection of personal data (constitutional right to informational self-determination) is not restricted beyond what is necessary by the data processing. This is especially true for data processing situations where the data subject has even fewer choices than average, such as in the case of data processing conducted by the Municipality in the performance of its public duties, as the Authority has also explained in its decision NAIH-642-4/2022 available on its website. The data processing examined in the present case also falls within this category.  

(42)  
The Authority found that, as detailed in paragraphs (8), (23), (25), and (26) above, during the online purchase of a subscription (which required registration) on the Website, the data subjects were unnecessarily required to upload copies of their identification documents and tax cards, and the processing of these was unnecessary. The purpose and task of the KAÜ is precisely to enable authentic online identification during state services with much less data provision and without copies of non-authentic documents, such as the tax card.

(43)  
Based on the statements received by the Municipality on May 31, 2023, registered under NAIH-3883-5/2023, the Municipality did not examine how the use of the Website could be achieved with the least amount of personal data processing, and it processed a wide range of personal data and document copies that do not have legal evidential force for the purpose of identifying the data subjects. The direct transfer of samples from the offline world to the online world without any modification is a common error. In the online environment, the conditions and rules are not always the same; thus, as the Authority stated in its opinion NAIH/2018/3654/2/V available on its website, a copy of an official identification document suitable for verifying identity does not have evidential force that it is an authentic copy of a valid official document and is not suitable for establishing identity either. Its processing is only possible based on explicit statutory authorization, such as regulations against money laundering (it should be noted that the data controller must make a copy of the original; it is not about a photocopy sent over the internet, but merely proof of viewing the original, which does not replace the original document).  

(44)  
The tax identification number is also not necessary for the Municipality to associate a specific tax number with a specific person, as the tax number inherently identifies a person, who has already been identified by another method (e.g., KAÜ). The Municipality did not specify in its submission any proportional and lawful interest, real risk, for which a photocopy of the tax identification number would be suitable, necessary, and proportional, which would have constituted unnecessary and disproportionate data processing in the period prior to the initiation of the current procedure concerning the Website.  

(45)  
In light of the above, the Authority established, upon request, the violation of Article 5(1)(c) of the General Data Protection Regulation according to the operative part.  

4. The issue of the principle of accuracy  

(46)  
The Authority did not ex officio investigate the violation of the principle of accuracy indicated in the request against the Municipality, as it only concerned the online viewing possibility of data held by the Municipality due to accounting obligations, and did not actually affect the accuracy of the personal data (its content).  

(47)  
The General Data Protection Regulation does not define either the right of the data subject or the obligation of the data controller to make personal data available online to the data subject, and the mere access of the data subject to their own previous personal data does not in itself imply a data protection risk, while the Authority does not have the competence to examine consumer protection aspects.

The Authority's clear legal interpretation, which is also found in the decision from 2019 mentioned in paragraph (38) above, is applicable to the legal basis for data processing carried out by the Municipality based on statutory duties. Any deviation from this by the data subject...

IV. Legal Consequences  

(52)  
Based on its tasks and powers defined in Section 38 (2) of the Infotv., it is undisputed that if the Authority establishes a violation that is still ongoing, it is obliged to require the data controller to remedy the violation in accordance with the measures specified in Article 58 (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation. In this regard, the Authority, in the operative part, obligated the Municipality to provide appropriate information on the Website regarding the online parking permit service as specified in this decision, and to revise its data processing practices by conducting a substantial review of the necessity of the personal data (and document copies) processed through the Website, along with proper documentation and verification.  

(53)  
According to Article 58 (2) (i) of the General Data Protection Regulation, depending on the circumstances of the case, the Authority may impose an administrative fine ex officio, in addition to or instead of the measures mentioned in Article 58 (2). In deciding whether it is justified to impose a data protection fine in addition to other legal consequences, the Authority acted within its statutory discretion, considering Section 61 (1) (a) of the Infotv., Section 75/A of the Infotv., as well as Article 83 (2) and Article 58 (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation.  

(54)  
Based on the Authority's assessment, it was necessary to impose a data protection fine in the case of the violations established ex officio in point III.3 of the operative part, as the sanction of reprimand alone would not be proportional and deterrent.  

(55)  
In terms of the necessity and amount of the fine, the Authority took into account that the Municipality qualifies as a budgetary institution, thus the possible maximum fine was 20,000,000 HUF according to Section 61 (4) (b) of the Infotv.  

11  
(56)  
In determining the amount of the fine, the Authority considered the following aspects:  

(i) The actual total revenue of the Municipality in 2022 was 27,893,301,000 HUF according to the data available on its website.  

(ii) The fine amount of 500,000 HUF established concerning the Municipality is 0.002% of its 2022 annual revenue.  

(57)  
When determining the amount of the data protection fine, the Authority took the following mitigating circumstances into account:  

(i) The duration of the violation—the possibility of online permit purchase—was short (Article 83 (2) (a) of the General Data Protection Regulation);  

(ii) The violation was negligent in nature as explained in point III.5 of the decision (Article 83 (2) (b) of the General Data Protection Regulation);  

(iii) The Authority had not previously established any relevant data protection violation against the Municipality (Article 83 (2) (e) of the General Data Protection Regulation);  

(iv) The Municipality attempted to improve the information available on the Website and to cease unnecessary data processing during the proceedings, as explained in the above paragraph (26), to remedy the violation (Article 83 (2) (f) of the General Data Protection Regulation);  

(v) The current proceedings have extended beyond the statutory procedural deadline (Article 83 (2) (k) of the General Data Protection Regulation).  

(58)  
In determining the amount of the data protection fine, the Authority considered the following aggravating circumstances:  

(i) The Municipality provided inadequate information regarding the fundamental aspects of the data processing in question, namely the identity of the data controller and the legal basis (Article 83 (2) (k) of the General Data Protection Regulation);  

(ii) The Municipality processes an extremely large number of personal data related to its core activities as a public authority, thus the expected level of care and the degree of risk associated with its data processing is greater than that of an average data controller (Article 83 (2) (k) of the General Data Protection Regulation).  

(59)  
In making its decision regarding the legal consequences, the Authority did not consider the points of Article 83 (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation that were not detailed above to be relevant in this case.  

(60)  
The Authority determined the amount of the data protection fine within its statutory discretion, taking into account the statutory maximum according to Section 61 (4) (b) of the Infotv.

(63)
