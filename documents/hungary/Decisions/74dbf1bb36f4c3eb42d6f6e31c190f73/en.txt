I. Preliminary case: NAIH-3266/2022. (NAIH-215/2021. and NAIH/2020/5714) investigation procedure

(1) A report was received by the Authority regarding the activities of the Mayor of Kerepes City (hereinafter: City) (hereinafter: Mayor) that on June 16, 2020, in a public post on the Facebook social media platform – with a comment attached – a picture was published of a resident of Budapest's XIII district placing waste in a public area of the City, along with the personal vehicle used by them, stating that a police report had also been filed in the matter. The report notes that the Mayor's Facebook post was shared by several users – including a profile named "Szervusz" – on the social media platform. According to the report, not all of the "16-20 cameras" placed in the City area have warnings posted about the recording of images, and in some cases, the warnings or cameras are obscured by vegetation, so the affected individuals may not necessarily be aware of the recording. Furthermore, the reporter states that the City’s municipal website does not indicate where the surveillance cameras have been installed.

(2) The Authority initiated an investigation under NAIH/2020/5714 based on Section 38 (3) a) of the Infotv. regarding the legality of the data processing of the surveillance system installed by the municipality in the public areas of Kerepes City, which was closed under NAIH-3266/2022. To clarify the facts, the Authority made several inquiries to the Client as the data controller based on Section 54 (1) a) and c) of the Infotv., to which the newly appointed notary as of December 1, 2020, responded.

(3) According to the response, the cameras of the public area surveillance system installed in the administrative area of the City were placed based on the decision of the City Council – Resolution 118/2015. (V. 26.). The operation of the surveillance system is regulated by the municipal decree 19/2016. (IX. 30.) and the Data Protection, Operational, and Data Security Regulations of the Kerepes Municipal Surveillance System (hereinafter: Regulations). The Client denied that there are no warning signs regarding the camera recordings in all areas around the public cameras, or that they are obscured by vegetation; this was also supported by photographic evidence. In its response, it was noted that the City’s website indicated where the cameras are installed; this was verifiable during the review of the City’s website. According to the Client's response, the recordings of the surveillance system are stored on a closed network, while recordings requiring action are stored on a separate drive; access to the closed network is restricted to public area inspectors, while access to the separately stored "action-required" recordings is granted to public area inspectors, the technical department head, the notary, and the Mayor. According to the statement, the Mayor has access to the recording for the purpose of filing a report due to established local practices, for the duration of the recording used for the report.

III.1. Legal Conditions for the Application of Public Area Surveillance Systems  

(72) According to the interpretative provision of the Kftv., public area refers to any state or municipal property intended for public use, which can be used by anyone in accordance with its purpose, including the part of the public area serving as a road and the part of private property opened and designated for public traffic by the owner (user), as well as that private property which can be used by anyone under the same conditions.  

(73) Based on the legal provisions contained in Sections 7-8 of the Kftv., it can be established that there are clear and strict rules regarding who is entitled to perform data processing operations concerning recordings made by public area cameras. The municipal mayor is not considered a person who can lawfully dispose of the recordings. The transmission of the recordings is also legally possible only in a narrow circle and for specific purposes. Audio recording can only be conducted by the acting supervisor – regarding a specific action involving a person, the environment, circumstances, or subject related to the action – thus, a camera placed in a public area or on private property opened to public traffic cannot record sound.

III.2.1. Quality of Data Controller, Identity of the Data Controller 
 
 
(74) According to the legal definition of the data controller, the characteristic of the data controller is that the data controller is the entity that independently or jointly with others determines the purpose of data processing, makes and executes decisions regarding data processing (including the tools used), or has these executed by a data processor. A substantive decision is considered to be a decision regarding the legal basis for data processing and the duration of data processing, the determination of the scope of the processed data, the design and execution of the data processing method, decisions regarding access to personal data, decisions regarding data transfer, the use of personal data for other data processing purposes, and decisions regarding the adoption of fundamental data security measures.  
 
(75) Therefore, decisions regarding data processing include, for example, the determination that an organization will carry out data processing for a specific purpose. Similarly, decisions regarding which areas will be monitored by surveillance cameras, what camera system will be used, for how long the data will be processed, who is authorized to access the data, what data security measures will be applied, whether and in what specific activities a data processor will be engaged. A decision regarding data processing also includes the execution of data processing operations themselves (e.g., review, storage, transfer, use, deletion). 
 
(76) Joint data processing does not occur if the data controllers participating in the data processing independently, without regard to the other data controllers, determine the circumstances of data processing and make and execute substantive decisions. Thus, joint data controller status does not arise if the same personal data is used independently by different data controllers for different purposes. Therefore, if multiple data controllers use the same camera system and the same recordings but make substantive decisions separately, they are considered independent data controllers.

(88) The Infotv. mandates that, for the purpose of verifying the legality of data processing operations carried out electronically with personal data, the data controller must record in an automated data processing system (electronic log) the definition of the scope of personal data affected by the data processing operation, the purpose and justification of the data processing operation, the exact time of the execution of the data processing operation, the identification of the person executing the data processing operation, and, in the case of data transfer, the recipient of the data transfer. The data recorded in the data controller's register and in the electronic log must be retained for ten years following the deletion of the processed data. 
 
(89) The reasons described in paragraph (20) may be as follows according to the IT expert opinion: 
1.) In user rights management, different logging rules are set for individual users in the logging settings assigned to users, and previous recordings were exported without an admin user. The system allows for this, as the settings permitted access to the Hik-Connect platform necessary for remote access to the security camera system. During the inspection, the account status was "Disconnected," but the system can still be accessed remotely at any time if the user knows the necessary identifiers for connection. 
During the inspection, the Authority also performed the video file extraction with an admin user, and it is recorded in the log (20220914095051logBack log file entry 438292 with a timestamp of 2022-9-14 9:46:54). Thus, the admin's management activities are logged, but user activities are not. The backups created during the inspection are visible in the log file; however, the fact of other backups is only visible on the user interface, not in the log file. 
 
2.) An automatic backup is created of the recordings captured by the camera system, which is not logged by the system. 
 
3.) The log file has been manipulated. 
 
(90) Based on the data obtained during the on-site inspection and the IT expert opinion, the Authority established that, regardless of which of the above cases applies, the Client did not comply with the legal obligation regarding the electronic log, violating the provisions of Infotv. § 25/F (1) and (4). The Client, as the data controller, is obliged to record the data in an automated data processing system (electronic log) for the purpose of verifying the legality of data processing operations carried out electronically, in such a way that it can be determined the scope of personal data affected by the data processing operation (in this case, a specific recording or series of recordings), the purpose and justification of the data processing operation, the exact time of execution, the identification of the person executing the data processing operation, and in the case of data transfer, the recipient of the data transfer. In all cases of recordings examined by the Authority – (3), (21), and (26) – there was no data in the Client's electronic log system that provided information in accordance with the provisions of Infotv. § 25/F (1). 
 
(91) The Authority found that the Client did not have a data controller's register at the time of the inspection, as it could not be presented, and no witness was aware of it. Although the Client had a data protection policy that included several of the data required by Infotv. § 25/E (1), not all data required by this legal provision were included, and a policy cannot be suitable for the registration of those data. The Client thus violated the provisions of Infotv. § 25/E (1) j) and k). The data controller's register must be maintained in written or electronically recorded form and must be made available to the Authority [Infotv. § 25/E (3)]. The Client is responsible for data protection incidents related to the data it processes, as well as for the right of access of the data subjects.

III.2.5. Illegal Handling of Data Protection Incident (1.4.)  

(100) It can be established from the facts that a data protection incident occurred concerning the recording made on August 15, 2023. It is a fact that the recording was published on the Mayor's facebook page on August 18, 2023. The recording contained personal data – specifically criminal personal data – as its publication aimed at the identification of the person featured in it. Neither the public area inspector nor any of the other witnesses heard during the proceedings were aware of how the recording was released from the Client's management.  

(101) The Authority has established that the Client, as the data controller, did not comply with the procedural rules to be followed in the event of a data protection incident, as based on its statements, it was not the public area inspector, as the authorized person for handling the recording, who forwarded the recording to the Mayor, nor did he make it public. Consequently, the recording could have reached the Mayor as a result of some data security breach, through unauthorized access; to the person managing the Facebook page and publishing the recording containing personal data. Despite being aware of this factually ascertainable data protection incident, the Client did not report it to the Authority, nor did it take necessary measures for its investigation, to eliminate its cause, and thus to prevent any potential future incidents, as the Authority revealed on September 14, 2022, the unchanged data processing practice since the occurrence of the incident, which enabled the data protection incident that certainly occurred with the Facebook publication on August 18, 2022. According to the public area inspector's statement, he heard that the Mayor published the recording on Facebook, which he also reported to the Clerk, thus at least two persons within the Client's organization were aware of the incident and could have taken action regarding it.  

In this way, the Client violated the data controller obligations set forth in Section 25/J. (1) and Section 25/K. (1) of the Infotv.

The Authority has examined the necessity of imposing a fine. In determining the amount of the data protection fine, the Authority considered as mitigating circumstances that the purpose of the unlawful data processing was not solely self-serving, but aimed at promoting the public interest and the enforcement of law enforcement interests; furthermore, that the proceedings were prolonged, which can be partly attributed to the Client.

(113)  
Based on Section 112 and Section 116 (1), as well as Section 114 (1) of the Ákr., there is a right to legal remedy against the decision through administrative litigation.  

(114)  
The rules of administrative litigation are determined by Act I of 2017 on Administrative Litigation (hereinafter: Kp.). According to Section 12 (1) of the Kp., administrative litigation against the Authority's decision falls under the jurisdiction of the court, and according to Section 13 (3) a) aa) of the Kp., the Metropolitan Court is exclusively competent for the case.
