The Authority establishes at the request of the Applicant that the Respondent has violated Article 6(1)(f) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council (hereinafter: GDPR) concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, as well as the repeal of Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation).

III. Applicable Legislation

(30)  
According to Article 2(1) of the GDPR, the GDPR must be applied to the data processing in this case.

(31)  
GDPR (47) recitals: The data controller – including the data controller with whom personal data may be shared – or the legitimate interest of a third party may provide a legal basis for processing, provided that the interests, fundamental rights, and freedoms of the data subject do not take precedence, taking into account the reasonable expectations of the data subject based on their relationship with the data controller. Such legitimate interest may arise, for example, when there is a relevant and appropriate relationship between the data subject and the data controller, such as in cases where the data subject is a customer of the data controller or is employed by them. To establish the existence of a legitimate interest, it is essential to carefully consider, among other things, whether the data subject can reasonably expect that their personal data may be processed for that purpose at the time of collection and in that context. The interests and fundamental rights of the data subject may take precedence over the interests of the data controller if the personal data is processed under circumstances in which the data subjects do not expect further processing. Since it is the legislator's task to determine in legislation on what legal basis public authorities may process personal data, a legal basis supporting the legitimate interest of the data controller cannot be applied to data processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks. The processing of personal data necessary for the prevention of fraud is also considered to be in the legitimate interest of the data controller. The processing of personal data for direct marketing purposes is also considered to be based on legitimate interest.

IV. Decision

IV.1. Legality of Public Area Surveillance

(40) The Authority must assess in this procedure whether the data processing conducted by the Applicant through the camera system is necessary for the personal and property protection purposes indicated by it, as well as for crime prevention and detection purposes, and whether the restriction of rights associated with the data processing – the limitation of the right to the protection of personal data – is proportional to the intended objective.

(41) The Authority considers it essential to establish that the Court of Justice of the European Union (hereinafter: CJEU) stated in case C-212/13, František Ryneš v. Office for Personal Data Protection, that if a video surveillance system continuously records and stores personal data, and it extends, even partially, to public areas, meaning that the data processing is directed outside the data controller's private sphere, it cannot be considered household data processing. However, this does not mean that in justified cases, under the conditions set out in Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR, and with appropriate safeguards, partial camera surveillance of public areas cannot take place in proportion to the intended objective.

(42) According to Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR, the processing of personal data is lawful if and to the extent that it is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, particularly if the data subject is a child.

(43) The legitimate interest of the Applicant, and at the same time the purpose of the data processing, is to protect the store from vandalism, to prevent thefts there, to ensure the safety of its employees, and to assist in the detection of crimes or offenses, providing evidence during proceedings.

(44) However, it is not sufficient for the legality of the data processing that a legitimate interest related to the data processing can be identified on the part of the data controller; according to point 44 of the CJEU judgment C-708/18, it must exist and be actual at the time of the data processing, and it cannot be theoretical or hypothetical at that time.

(45) In the Authority's view, the legitimate interest of the Applicant related to the data processing can be considered existing and actual, as confirmed by the […] Police Department, there have already been minor property crimes in the Applicant's store, which have led to administrative or criminal proceedings.

(46) The Applicant examined the alternatives to data processing through the cameras placed on the street front in the attached balancing test, that is, whether there is a method that is equally suitable for achieving the data processing purpose but involves less restriction of rights or does not involve any restriction of rights, that is, the processing of personal data. The Authority accepts the Applicant's argument that the operation of the alarm system – which the store has – does not represent a real alternative in this specific case, as it only operates outside of business hours and does not provide protection against minor thefts committed during business hours, lacking a deterrent effect in that regard.
9

(47) The European Data Protection Board's Guidelines 3/2019 on the processing of personal data through video devices (hereinafter: Guidelines) state in point 27 that "generally, video surveillance should be applied only up to the boundary of the property for the protection of the data controllers' premises and facilities. However, there are cases where monitoring the property is not sufficient for effective protection. In certain specific cases, it may be necessary to extend video surveillance to surrounding areas. In this regard, the data controller should consider physical and technical solutions, such as masking or pixelating irrelevant areas."

(48) According to the Authority, when assessing the legality of camera data processing directed at public areas, it should be taken into account that there is no front yard between the building housing the store and the sidewalk, and the store's entrance opens directly onto the street, thus the camera surveillance of the store entrance can only be implemented in such a way that a small portion of the public area is also included, with the possibility of reducing the monitored area through camera masking. Until March 7, 2023, the Applicant did not apply masking on the cameras; however, from March 8, 2023, masking has been applied, meaning that the sections of the sidewalk in front of neighboring properties, the opposite worn path, and part of the roadway have been masked. The cameras monitor the sidewalk in front of the store, the area suitable for parking between the roadway and the sidewalk, and the roadway to a lesser extent compared to before.

(49) The Authority also considers it important to take into account the fact that the store is open every day until 10:00 PM, in a street with variable but low traffic during the evening, and generally only one employee is present in the store at a time. The Authority holds the view that the cameras placed on the street front have a deterrent effect on the commission of crimes by themselves, and if someone nevertheless commits a crime against the Applicant, the images recorded by the street front cameras may contribute to the identification of the perpetrator, for example, if the perpetrator puts on a hood or cap in front of the store entrance, but may also be significant in terms of crime detection, for instance, regarding which direction the perpetrator leaves the store, thereby facilitating the work of the investigative authority in determining which areas may be worth reviewing the footage from surveillance cameras operated by the police or the municipality.

(50) The Authority finds it necessary to note here that the Applicant referred in their statement to the Authority that there are cases where perpetrators cover their faces on the street in front of the store; however, this aspect was not presented by the Applicant in the balancing of interests test. Nevertheless, since it is relevant in terms of the legitimate interest related to data processing, the Authority takes it into account.

(51) In the balancing of interests test, as well as in identifying the legitimate interest related to data processing, the Applicant did not distinguish between different parts of the public area, meaning that they considered their legitimate interest to be uniformly present for the monitoring of the sidewalk, the roadway, and the area suitable for parking between them. However, according to the Authority, it should be examined in terms of the necessity and proportionality of data processing how much monitoring of certain parts of the public area is necessary for the enforcement of the Applicant's legitimate interest.

(52) As the Authority has already referred to above, according to point 27 of the Guidelines, video surveillance may, in certain justified cases, extend beyond the boundary of the property into a strip – thus into public areas – provided that this is limited to the most necessary area.

(53) During the proceedings, the Respondent did not present any circumstances that would support the necessity of such extensive surveillance of public space, nor did any such circumstances arise in the documents obtained from the Police Department.

(54) Nevertheless, the Authority's position is that the camera surveillance directed at the narrow strip beyond the property boundary, in this case the sidewalk, only minimally restricts the rights and freedoms of the affected parties, as the cameras, while recording their images and movements, are limited to a narrow section of the sidewalk.

(55) In light of the above, the Authority's position is that the Respondent has a legitimate interest in the data processing aimed at the direct surveillance of the narrow area in front of the store through the cameras operated on the store's street front, both at the time of the application and continuously since then, as the Respondent has a legitimate interest in accordance with the above, which is actual and existing, and the data processing is necessary to achieve this interest and the resulting purpose of data processing, namely the appropriate assurance of property protection, and the restriction of rights is proportional to the intended goal, thus in this regard, the data processing is considered lawful.

(56) The Authority examined, based on the Applicant's request, whether the extent of the data processing realized through the cameras was lawful until March 8, 2023 – that is, before the Respondent carried out the masking – and thereafter. According to the Authority's position, the legitimate interest related to the data processing existed even before March 8, 2023, as established in point (50), however, the extent of the surveillance – monitoring the section of the sidewalk in front of neighboring properties, the area suitable for parking between the road and the sidewalk, the road itself, and part of the beaten path without a sidewalk on the other side of the road – exceeded the necessary extent for achieving the data processing purpose and enforcing the legitimate interest. Consequently, both until the masking was performed and thereafter, the Respondent disproportionately monitored public space, as the extent of surveillance and data processing established in points (47)-(50) would have been sufficient to achieve the data processing purpose. Based on all of this, the Authority finds that until March 8, 2023, the Respondent unlawfully operated the cameras located on the street front of the building at number […] in a manner that was capable of monitoring the section of the sidewalk in front of neighboring properties, the areas suitable for parking between the road and the sidewalk in front of the store, the road itself, and part of the beaten path without a sidewalk on the other side of the road, and that with the current settings of the cameras used since March 8, 2023, the Respondent violated Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR, as in reference to the legitimate interest outlined above, the Respondent did not ensure that the data processing was proportional, thereby monitoring not only the section of the sidewalk in front of the store but also the road, as well as the area suitable for parking between the road and the sidewalk in front of the store.

IV.2. Decision on the Applicant's Requests

(57) The Applicant requested that the Authority order the Respondent to cease the unlawful behavior and sanction the Respondent in the strictest possible manner.

(58) The Authority interpreted the latter request as a request for the imposition of a data protection fine based on its wording.

(59) The Authority rejects the Applicant's request for the imposition of a data protection fine, as the application of this legal consequence does not directly affect the Applicant's rights or legitimate interests, and such a decision by the Authority does not create rights or obligations for the Applicant; therefore, regarding the application of this legal consequence falling within the scope of public interest enforcement – the
11

Regarding the imposition of fines – the Applicant is not considered a client under Section 10(1) of the Administrative Procedure Act (Ákr.), thus there is no basis for submitting a request in this regard, and this part of the submission cannot be interpreted as a request.

IV.3. Legal Consequences

(60) The Authority establishes that the Respondent has violated Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR.

(61) The Authority grants the Applicant's request and instructs the Respondent, based on Article 58(2)(d) of the GDPR, to bring its data processing into compliance with the GDPR, namely to cease the monitoring of public areas – excluding the section of the sidewalk in front of the store – that is, the roadway, as well as the area suitable for parking between the roadway and the sidewalk in front of the store, either by relocating or dismantling the cameras or through masking.

(62) According to Section 75/A of the Information Act (Infotv.), the Authority exercises its powers as set forth in Articles 83(2)-(6) of the General Data Protection Regulation, taking into account the principle of proportionality, particularly in that in the case of a first-time violation of the provisions regarding the processing of personal data – as defined in legislation or in a binding legal act of the European Union – the remedy for the violation is primarily addressed by warning the data controller or data processor, in accordance with Article 58 of the General Data Protection Regulation.

(63) The Authority does not find it necessary to impose a data protection fine on the Respondent, considering that the Respondent has violated the provisions of the GDPR for the first time and has taken measures during the procedure to legalize the extent of data processing.

(64) Based on all of the above, the Authority's position is that the established violation cannot be considered serious enough to justify the application of a data protection fine, thus it considers the warning to be a sufficient sanction, also taking into account the (148) recital of the GDPR, which states that in the case of minor violations of the GDPR, a reprimand may be applied instead of a fine.

(65) Based on all of the above, the Authority has decided as stated in the operative part.
