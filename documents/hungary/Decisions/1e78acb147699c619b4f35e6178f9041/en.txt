(33) According to Article 4(1) of the GDPR, "personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, particularly by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.

(34) According to Article 4(2) of the GDPR, "processing" means any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.

IV. Decision:

IV.1. The personal data of the Applicant stored by the Respondent, the quality of data controller, and the subject of the investigation

(56) The identifying data of the Applicant, the data and information related to the Applicant's bank account, and the content of the Application qualify as the personal data of the Applicant according to Article 4 (1) of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and the storage, use, and transfer of such data constitute data processing under Article 4 (2) of the GDPR.

(57) Based on the above, the Authority's position is that the Respondent can be considered an independent data controller regarding the processing of the Applicant's personal data according to Article 4 (7) of the GDPR, as it has an independent purpose for data processing and determines the substantive decisions related to the circumstances of data processing itself.

IV.2. The Application (Application No. 2)
11

(58) The subject of the procedure in this regard was to examine whether the Request was fulfilled/responded to by the Respondent in accordance with the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation, and whether the Respondent adequately justified the failure to fulfill.

(59) The Request contains the Applicant's request for deletion under Article 17(1)(b) of the GDPR, as well as the exercise of the right to object under Article 21 of the GDPR, and refers to Article 16 of the GDPR, in connection with the fact that the residence address is not a mailing address, and that no correspondence should be sent there as it is not secure.

(60) The data subject is entitled, under Article 17(1)(b) of the GDPR, to request the deletion of their personal data from the data controller if they withdraw their consent, which forms the basis for data processing under Article 6(1)(a) or Article 9(2)(a) of the GDPR, and there is no other legal basis for the data processing. Furthermore, the data subject has the right to object to the processing of their personal data under Article 21 of the GDPR, as well as the right to request the rectification of their personal data under Article 16 of the GDPR.

(61) The data controller is obliged to fulfill the requests of data subjects, including requests aimed at exercising the rights mentioned in the preceding paragraph, in accordance with the provisions of Article 12 of the GDPR, or to justify the refusal of fulfillment.

(62) Article 12(2) of the GDPR provides that the data controller must facilitate the exercise of the rights of data subjects, and may not refuse to fulfill a request aimed at exercising these rights unless it demonstrates that it is not in a position to identify the data subject. In the present case, the Respondent claimed that it could not identify the Applicant because the latter's name was not legible, and therefore did not respond to the Applicant's request. The Authority considers this acceptable because the Applicant's request submitted to the Respondent did not contain any personal data other than the Applicant's not clearly readable name. The Request did not include the Applicant's identifying data, bank account number, or other banking identifiers, nor their email address or postal address, based on which the Respondent could have clearly identified the Applicant. The Authority does not share the Applicant's view that identifying the Applicant could not have posed significant difficulties, given that the Respondent was able to identify the Applicant in response to the Authority's inquiry, despite the fact that the Authority did not specify in its call NAIH-3942-3/2023 when the Request was submitted, to which it did not receive a response. The Authority disagrees with this because, in the call written to the Respondent, it described the method, time, and circumstances of submitting the data subject request, with which the Respondent could clearly identify to whom the data subject request was related, and whose identity the Respondent had previously been unable to ascertain. The Authority accepted the Respondent's argument that there were doubts regarding the Applicant's identity, and since the Applicant's name was not clearly indicated on the Request, and there was no contact information, the Respondent could not identify the person submitting the request, i.e., the Applicant.

(63) The Authority also did not accept the Applicant's reference that the Respondent could have identified based on the general key indicated in the Request. The referenced encryption method - PGP, or "Pretty Good Privacy" - works in such a way that if, for example, someone wants to receive an encrypted message, they provide their own public encryption key (which is visible in the Request), with which the other party encrypts the message to be sent, and then sends it to the data subject's email address. The encrypted message received in this way can then be decrypted by the email recipient, and ideally only by the email recipient, using their own private key. However, the entire process also requires that, in addition to this public key, the email sender receives the other party's email address; without it, they cannot send anything. Therefore, the Request does not contain an encrypted email address, but rather a public encryption key, which cannot be used to send an email on its own without...
12

The Applicant knew what the affected email address was, yet this was not included in the Request.

(64) The Authority believes that the fulfillment of the request by the data subject must not only be facilitated by the data controller, but the data subject must also be cooperative and must allow for their identification. Without the cooperation of the data subject, the data controller may refer to the exception mentioned in Article 12(2) of the GDPR, thus proving that it was not possible to identify the data subject. In the present case, the identification of the Applicant was particularly complicated by the fact that, apart from their poorly readable name, they did not provide any other data that could have facilitated their identification, therefore the Respondent could not apply the provisions of Article 12(6) of the GDPR, as it was not in a position to request additional information to confirm the identity of the Applicant.

(65) For the above reasons, the Authority determined that the Respondent did not violate the provisions of Articles 12(1) – (4) of the GDPR regarding the requests of the Applicant as a data subject, and therefore could not be instructed to fulfill the requests of the Applicant, thus the Authority rejected the Applicant's request in this regard.

IV.3. The Respondent's general practice regarding the use of signature pads, eID chips, and the photocopying of identity cards and address cards - investigation request concerning the Applicant's personal data (Requests No. 3, 5, 8, and 9)

(66) The examination of the Respondent's general practice does not directly affect the rights or legitimate interests of the Applicant; such a decision by the Authority does not create rights or obligations for them, therefore, in terms of the application of this legal consequence falling within the scope of public interest enforcement, the Applicant does not qualify as a client according to Section 10(1) of the Administrative Procedure Act (Ákr.). Since this request does not meet the requirements of Section 35(1) of the Ákr., there is no basis for submitting a request in this regard, and this part of the submission cannot be interpreted as a request.

(67) The Authority terminates the procedure aimed at examining the use of signature pads concerning the Applicant's personal data for the following reasons. The procedure was initiated by the Authority at the request of the Applicant based on Article 77(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation and Section 60(1) of the Information Act (Infotv.). The data protection authority procedure initiated upon request is a procedure in which the Authority examines violations concerning the Applicant's own personal data and decides on their validity primarily based on the facts and evidence presented by the Applicant. The peculiarity of this procedure is that it is not initiated at the will of the Authority, but rather the client approaches the Authority of their own accord. In the procedure initiated upon request, it is primarily the Applicant's responsibility to present the facts and relevant evidence regarding the alleged violations. This is confirmed by Section 60(5) of the Infotv., as well as Article 77(1) of the GDPR, which states that in such cases, the violation occurred "according to the assessment of the data subject." According to point d) of Section 60(5) of the Infotv., the request must also contain facts and evidence supporting the claims related to the alleged violation. Therefore, in the procedure initiated upon request, the documents and other evidence supporting the request must primarily be presented by the Applicant, and the Authority cannot be compelled to substantiate the request.

(68) Signature pads may indeed be suitable in certain cases to record biometric data as defined in Article 4(14) of the General Data Protection Regulation, such as writing speed or the pressure of the signing pen, etc. Therefore, according to Article 9(1) and point a) of paragraph (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, they can only be used if the data subject gives their consent.

(69) The Applicant's statement that they did not read the documents before using the signature pad and only asked the clerk questions is not acceptable. The consent that the data subject gives with their signature pertains to what is signed.
13

The documents refer to the written content, not to what is discussed verbally with the official. The Applicant merely listed their assumptions and concerns regarding the use of signature pads and referred to what they discussed with the official, while providing their consent by accepting information they did not read; however, they did not present their concerns regarding that.

(70) Since the Applicant's request does not contain information supporting the alleged data protection violation against the Applicant, even after the call for supplementation, regarding the use of signature pads, the Authority terminated the procedure concerning the recording of the Applicant's personal data using the signature pad and the storage of the personal data recorded in this manner, in accordance with Section 60 (5) d) of the Infotv. and Section 47 (1) a) of the Ákr.

(71) The Respondent is bound by the provisions of the Pmt. regarding the data processing related to document copying, thus, in the case specified in Section 7 (8) a) of the Pmt., they are obliged to photocopy the client's identity card and the specified page of their address card. Considering that the Applicant did not articulate a specific violation regarding this data processing by the Respondent, but only objected to this type of data processing, the Authority also terminated the procedure regarding this request in accordance with Section 60 (5) d) of the Infotv. and Section 47 (1) a) of the Ákr.

IV.4. Mailing of the Applicant's bank card to the permanent address, mailing to the wrong address, and the indication of the bank card number on the bank card (Request No. 10)

IV.4.1. Mailing of the bank card to the permanent address

(72) The Respondent has a legal basis for storing the Applicant's address data under Article 6 (1) b) of the GDPR, as the Applicant is a client of the Respondent and has a contract with the Respondent, thus the processing of this data is necessary for the fulfillment of this contract, and it cannot be deleted even in the case of a deletion/objection request.

(73) In accordance with the above, it can be established that the Respondent has an appropriate legal basis for processing the Applicant's address data as a mailing address, namely Article 6 (1) b) of the GDPR, thus in connection with the fulfillment of the contract (in this case, in connection with the bank account contract and the bank card contract). The mailing of the bank card is also part of the contract fulfillment.

(74) For the above reasons, the Authority's position is that the mailing of the bank card to the Applicant's address, thus the use of the address data for this purpose, is not objectionable from a data protection perspective; therefore, the Authority rejects the Applicant's request for the Authority to determine the illegality of this data processing.

IV.4.2. Indication of the bank card number on the bank card

(75) The bank card number, as its name suggests, is related to the bank card. The bank card number, as the number of the Applicant's bank card, is personal data under Article 4 (1) of the GDPR. However, the question of whether the bank cards used by financial institutions comply with the sectoral regulations regarding payment instruments is not within the Authority's jurisdiction to examine. The Applicant's assertion that "is it not considered a serious data protection omission" regarding the Respondent's practice of indicating the bank card number on the bank card refers to the Respondent's practice, which can be initiated for examination not based on a request but ex officio, based on the Authority's decision. Based on the findings in paragraph (66) of this decision, the examination of the Respondent's general practice does not directly affect the rights or legitimate interests of the Applicant; such a decision by the Authority does not create rights or obligations for them, and therefore this - falling within the scope of public interest enforcement -
