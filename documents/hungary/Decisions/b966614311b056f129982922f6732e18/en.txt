 D E C I S I O N 
 
 
The National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Authority) has initiated a data protection authority procedure against […] (headquarters: […]; hereinafter: Respondent) based on the application submitted by […] (address: […]; hereinafter: Applicant), represented by […] ([…] headquarters: […]; hereinafter: Representative), received by the Authority on July 23, 2022. The procedure concerns the improper provision of access to the data of a capital-saving life insurance contract concluded by the Applicant's deceased mother. The Authority makes the following decisions in this data protection authority procedure: 
 
I. 
The Authority establishes at the request of the Applicant that the Respondent has violated Article 15(1) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council (hereinafter: GDPR) regarding the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, as well as the repeal of Directive 95/46/EC. 
 
II. The Authority establishes ex officio that the Respondent has violated the principle of data minimization as set out in Article 5(1)(c) of the GDPR. 
 
III. The Authority orders the Respondent ex officio to delete the complete inheritance transfer decree numbered […] provided by the Applicant from all its systems, including its email system, within 15 days from the date this decision becomes final! 
 
IV. The Authority issues a warning to the Respondent for the violations established in points I and II. 
 
 O R D E R 
 
The Authority terminates the procedure regarding the Applicant's request for the Authority to compel the Respondent to fulfill the access request. 
 
The Authority warns the Respondent that the data related to the disputed data processing cannot be deleted or destroyed until the expiration of the deadline for initiating a lawsuit against the decision or, in the case of an administrative lawsuit, until the court's final decision. 
 
The measure prescribed in point II must be carried out by the Respondent within 15 days following the expiration of the deadline for legal remedies against this decision, and the Respondent must promptly provide evidence of its execution to the Authority, along with supporting documents. 
 
There is no administrative remedy against this decision and order, but it can be contested in an administrative lawsuit filed with the Metropolitan Court within 30 days from the date of communication. The lawsuit must be submitted electronically to the Authority, which will forward it along with the case documents to the court. A request for a hearing must be indicated in the lawsuit. In the lawsuit against the order, the court will proceed in a simplified manner, outside of a hearing. For those not fully exempt from personal fees, the administrative lawsuit fee is HUF 30,000, and the case is subject to the payment of court fees. Legal representation is mandatory in the proceedings before the Metropolitan Court.

IV. Decision

IV.1. Identification of the Applicant and the Principle of Data Minimization

(46) Article 12(6) of the GDPR requires data controllers to request additional information necessary to confirm the identity of the data subject if they have reasonable doubts regarding the identity of the natural person making a request under Articles 15-21.

(47) In the specific case, the Applicant sought to exercise the right of access provided to them under Section 143(4) of the Act on Information (Bit.) not in relation to their own personal data, but in relation to the personal data of their deceased mother. This, however, resulted in the need to not only verify their own identity but also to prove, in accordance with Section 143(4) of the Bit., that they are the heir of the deceased or a designated beneficiary in the insurance contract.

(48) To support this, the Applicant attached to their letter dated March 3, 2021, sent to the Respondent, a notarized extract of the inheritance distribution decree, which clearly stated that the Applicant is the heir of the deceased. Furthermore, in the letter dated March 5, 2021, the Respondent did not inform the Applicant that they could not identify them.

(49) In contrast, in response to the Applicant's letter dated April 6, 2021, the Respondent disregarded this and requested the Applicant to attach the inheritance distribution decree, which the Applicant did not comply with, and on June 10, 2021, only provided a photocopied part of the complete inheritance distribution decree to the Respondent, which was not accepted. The Applicant ultimately provided the complete inheritance distribution decree to the Respondent on July 4, 2021.

(50) The complete inheritance distribution decree contains significantly more data than the extract regarding the subjects of the inheritance. The extract contains information solely about the claims inherited by the Applicant against [...], while the complete decree names claims against other financial institutions and also identifies a property that is part of the inheritance. The Authority holds the view that the Applicant's personal data includes what they inherited after their mother's death, specifically what constituted the subject of the inheritance transferred to them. However, this is not relevant information for the Respondent, who only required proof of the heir's status.

(51) According to the privacy policy announced by the Respondent with Executive Order No. [...], point 15.5.9.5 states, "To request data as an heir, it is necessary to prove heir status with a final inheritance distribution decree or inheritance certificate. (...) Regarding the data contained in the attached inheritance distribution decree or inheritance certificate, the insurer only needs the data from which the heir status can be established; thus, it is necessary to redact data concerning third parties, which the client must be informed about." The same rule was also included in the Respondent's privacy policy that was in effect between November 18, 2019, and May 13, 2021, in point 15.5.9.5.

(52) In its statement dated June 12, 2023 (NAIH-1245-5/2023), the Respondent stated that it only requires the data from which the heir status can be established, thus such data...
11

in such cases, it is not necessary to submit the complete inheritance transfer decision, but it is sufficient to provide an excerpt prepared by the notary to prove the status of the heir. Consequently, due to an administrative error by the Respondent, the Applicant was requested to submit the complete inheritance transfer decision even after the Applicant had already provided the excerpt issued by the notary in March 2021.

(53)  
Based on all of the above, the Authority established ex officio that the Respondent violated the principle of data minimization under Article 5(1)(c) of the GDPR when it requested the provision of personal data—specifically, all information related to the inheritance found in the complete inheritance transfer decision—that were not necessary to achieve the purpose of identification and the exercise of the right of access.

IV.2. Fulfillment of the access request

(54)  
Section 143(3) of the Act provides that, for the purposes of the Act, the legal provisions regarding the processing of personal data apply to the processing of data related to deceased individuals. According to Section 143(4) of the Act, the rights of the data subject concerning data related to the deceased individual may also be exercised by the deceased's heir or the designated beneficiary in the insurance contract.

(55)  
Thus, Section 143(3) of the Act does not differentiate regarding the rights of data subjects that can be exercised in relation to the personal data of deceased individuals, and it does not set forth stricter rules regarding access, unlike Section 25(1) of the Information Act, which can be considered a special rule in this regard.

(56)  
As a result, the Applicant was entitled to exercise the right of access concerning the personal data of the deceased mother, which the Respondent did not dispute following the proof of heirship.

(57)  
The issue regarding the fulfillment of the access request submitted multiple times by the Applicant arose from the fact that the Respondent partially anonymized the personal identification data of the Applicant's mother—excluding the place of birth—or replaced it with false data, meaning that based on this information, the Applicant's mother could not be identified using the information available to the Respondent, as confirmed by screenshots from the Respondent's record-keeping systems.

(58)  
However, the Applicant attached an excerpt from the account statement of her mother’s account held at […] Bank to her request submitted in person on March 5, 2021, from which the insurance amount was transferred to the Respondent. The transfer notification included the offer number, which re-established the connection between the Applicant's mother and the data of the contract she entered into, meaning that the Applicant provided additional information in accordance with Article 11(2) of the GDPR, which made her mother identifiable concerning the insurance contract.

(59)  
Considering that the connection between the Applicant's mother and the contractual data could be restored, the operation performed by the Respondent to overwrite the personal data...
12

cannot be considered anonymization, but solely pseudonymization, since according to the preamble (26) of the GDPR, personal data can only be considered anonymized if the data subject can no longer be identified based on it.

(60) Considering that the Applicant's mother became identifiable based on the information provided by the Applicant, and the contractual data became attributable to her, the Respondent should have made available to the Applicant, after verifying the Applicant's status as an heir, the information regarding the reimbursement of the insurance premium found in the […] system. Consequently, the Authority established ex officio that the Respondent violated Article 15(1) of the GDPR.

IV.3. Termination of the Procedure

(61) The Authority terminates the procedure regarding the Applicant's request, which aimed to compel the Respondent to provide the requested data – when, in what manner, and to which account the insurance premium was refunded – and to fulfill the access request, based on Section 47(1)(c) of the Administrative Procedure Act (Ákr.).

(62) According to Section 47(1)(c) of the Ákr., the Authority shall terminate the procedure if it has become unnecessary. Considering that the Respondent complied with the Applicant's request during the procedure and informed about the information regarding the disbursement of the contractual amount in its letter dated January 16, 2023, the circumstances justifying the continuation of the procedure no longer exist concerning this request.

IV.4. Consequences of Exceeding the Deadline

(63) According to Section 60/A(1) of the Information Act (Infotv.), the processing time in data protection authority procedures is 150 days. In the present data protection authority procedure, the Applicant's request was completed on September 6, 2022, meaning it was suitable for the procedure to be conducted, thus the starting date of the current procedure was September 7, 2022. The processing deadline – taking into account that, according to Section 60/A(1) of the Infotv., the time from the call for the provision of data necessary for clarifying the facts until its fulfillment is not included in the processing deadline – expired on March 16, 2023.

(64) According to Section 51(b) of the Ákr., if the authority exceeds the processing deadline, it must pay the applicant a sum equivalent to the fee or charge payable for the conduct of the procedure, or in the absence of that, ten thousand forints, and the applicant is also exempt from paying procedural costs. Consequently, since the Authority's procedure is free of charge, the Applicant is entitled to ten thousand forints – to be transferred to a bank account or via postal order, as specified in writing.

IV.5. Legal Consequences

(65) The Authority establishes at the request of the Applicant that the Respondent violated Article 15(1) of the GDPR. The Authority also establishes ex officio that the Respondent violated Article 5(1)(c) of the GDPR. Due to the identified violations, the Authority issues a warning to the Respondent in accordance with Section 75/A of the Infotv.

(66) The Authority orders ex officio the deletion of the entire inheritance transfer decision based on Section 61(1)(a) of the Infotv. and Article 58(2)(g) of the GDPR, as its handling violates the principle of data minimization.

(67) Based on Section 75/A of the Infotv., the Authority exercises its powers as set out in Articles 83(2)-(6) of the General Data Protection Regulation, taking into account the principle of proportionality, particularly that in the case of a first-time violation of the provisions regarding the processing of personal data as defined in legislation or in a binding legal act of the European Union, the remedy for the violation is primarily addressed by warning the data controller or processor in accordance with Article 58 of the General Data Protection Regulation.

(68) The Authority does not find it necessary to impose a data protection fine in this case for the following reasons: the Respondent has violated the GDPR provisions for the first time, and during the procedure, it took action regarding the fulfillment of the Applicant's access request, providing the requested data in its letter dated January 16, 2023, thus taking the necessary steps to remedy the violation. Furthermore, the identified violations are of a negligent nature and can be attributed to administrative errors, and were established solely concerning the Applicant.

(69) Based on all of the above, the Authority decided as stated in the operative part.
