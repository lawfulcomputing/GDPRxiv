I N D O K O L Á S

I. 
Background, main case

(1) 
The Authority received several reports regarding the new data processing practice established by the Obligor in connection with the purchase of alcoholic beverages. The submissions complained of significantly differing data processing practices in various stores. According to the complainants, data processing information was not provided to the affected individuals even upon request, nor did the cashiers have such information, thus the legal basis and duration of data processing regarding the recording of the date of birth were unknown. Based on the information from the complainants, the Obligor recorded the date of birth of the purchaser in stores located in several municipalities when purchasing alcoholic beverages—either based on verbal declaration at the checkout or, according to another complaint, by requiring the presentation of an identity card. According to another complaint, the cashier could not provide information regarding the purpose and legal basis of the data recording, and the regional manager indicated to the store manager that recording the data from the identity card was not necessary if the purchaser's age could be established in another way, and the purchasers were not informed of this fact. One complainant stated that he was visibly over 70 years old, yet he still had to prove his age.

(2) 
Based on the above, on August 19, 2022, the Authority initiated an official data protection authority procedure to verify whether the data processing practice established by the Obligor in connection with the purchase of alcoholic beverages complies with the requirements set forth in the General Data Protection Regulation.

(3) 
During the procedure, the Authority repeatedly called upon the Obligor to provide statements to clarify the facts; it also conducted on-site inspections without prior notice in two randomly selected stores of the Obligor, in accordance with Act CL of 2016 on General Administrative Procedure (hereinafter: Ákr.).

(4) 
In the data protection authority procedure initiated ex officio, the Decision dated March 30, 2023, in point 1, the Authority established that the Obligor violated:

a) Article 5(1)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation;  
b) Article 5(1)(c) of the General Data Protection Regulation;  
c) Article 6(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation;  
d) Article 12(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation;  
e) Articles 13(1) and (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation;  
f) Articles 32(1) and (4) of the General Data Protection Regulation.

(5) 
The violation of the principle of transparency as set forth in Article 5(1)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation occurred because, in the Obligor's stores (i.e., at the location of data processing), it was not transparent for the affected individuals intending to purchase alcoholic beverages regarding the data processing established by the Obligor; the essential circumstances of it were not accessible to the affected individuals, as there was no information displayed.

The data processing information notice, which contains relevant essential information regarding actual data processing; furthermore, the employees of the Obligor were unable to provide information, even verbally, regarding the internet accessibility of the related data processing notice or the essential circumstances of the data processing at the request of the data subjects and the representatives of the Authority [see: paragraph (129) of the reasoning of the Decision].

(6)  
It also constituted a violation of the principle of transparency that the actual data processing practices regarding the age verification of individuals intending to purchase alcoholic beverages were significantly different in the Obligor's stores, as well as at the cash registers within individual stores, thus the data processing affecting the data subjects was not transparent to them [see: paragraph (134) of the reasoning of the Decision].

(7)  
The Obligor violated the principle of transparency as defined in Article 5(1)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation in relation to data processing carried out through logging until the date of the order regarding notification under Section 76 of the Ákr. [Order No. NAIH-6989-16/2022] by failing to ensure the transparency of the data processing for the data subjects, as during the period between August 4 and August 11, 2022, it did not provide information to the data subjects regarding the data processing; during the period between August 12 and August 31, 2022, it provided information to the data subjects on its Website (https://www.aldi.hu/hu/homepage.html) and during inspections in its stores under Sections 68-69 of the Ákr. that was contrary to reality, regarding the fact that every key press at the cash register (and thus the date of birth of customers in the case of purchasing alcoholic beverages) was logged, and that these log files were retained for 180 days following the recording, and that the data processor engaged by the Obligor also had access to them; finally, that from September 1, 2022, until the date of the order regarding notification under Section 76 of the Ákr., the Obligor did not modify the relevant data processing notice, thereby continuing to fail to inform the data subjects about the aforementioned [see: paragraph (141) of the reasoning of the Decision].

(8)  
The failure of the Obligor to take appropriate measures to ensure that its information regarding data processing was made easily accessible to the data subjects in its individual stores violated the "easily accessible" criterion prescribed in Article 12(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation [see: paragraph (148) of the reasoning of the Decision].

(9)  
The Obligor violated Articles 13(1) and (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, considering that the Obligor did not provide the data subjects with the information specified in Articles 13(1) and (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation at the time of acquiring personal data regarding the data processing involved in the procedure [see: paragraph (157) of the reasoning of the Decision].

(10)  
Regarding the period from August 4, 2022, to August 31, 2022, the Obligor did not demonstrate a legal basis for data processing under Article 6(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation for the data of those data subjects for whom there could be no "doubt" as defined in Section 16/A(4) of Act CLV of 1997 on Consumer Protection (hereinafter: Fgytv.), and who could be clearly identified as having reached the age of eighteen due to their visibly advanced age. Since the Authority could not identify any legal provision or individual decision applicable to the Obligor regarding this group of individuals that would create a legal obligation for data processing, the Obligor violated Article 6(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation [see: paragraph (165) of the reasoning of the Decision].
4

(11) It was also a violation of Article 6(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation that the Obligor incorrectly identified this legal provision and the applicable legal norms as a legal obligation that generally burdens him, extending to all customers, and improperly applied the General Data Protection Regulation and the Act on the Protection of Young People (Fgytv.) by overstepping the applicability scope of the legal obligations applicable to him when entering personal data into the cash register system during the recording in log files, as Section 16/A(4) of the Fgytv. only requires credible proof of age, thus he did not have a proper legal basis for the recording. [see: paragraph (166) of the reasoning of the Decision]

(12) According to Section 16/A(4) of the Fgytv., in case of doubt regarding the purchase of alcoholic beverages, the buyer must be asked to provide credible proof of age (i.e., in cases where it cannot be clearly determined whether the person intending to purchase the alcoholic beverage has reached the age of eighteen or not). However, during the period from August 4, 2022, to August 31, 2022, the Obligor, exceeding the phrase "in case of doubt" as defined in Section 16/A(4) of the Fgytv., generally required, in his own words, the "establishment of a system-wide use," that employees working in the position of store seller check the age of every person intending to purchase an alcoholic beverage. This violated the principle of data minimization set out in Article 5(1)(c) of the General Data Protection Regulation – which must be considered by the data controller in every data processing, regardless of the legal basis – as it processed and recorded in its system irrelevant data for the purposes of data processing concerning customers who have evidently reached the age of eighteen. [see: paragraph (169) of the reasoning of the Decision]

(13) During the sales process of alcoholic beverages, both the viewing of an ID suitable for proving age and the entry, recording, and storage for 180 days in log files of the birth date read from the ID suitable for proving age or the birth date provided by the customer in the cash register system are considered data processing operations according to the definition in Article 4(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation. However, the general mandatory requirement for operations beyond viewing was not necessarily needed for the purpose of the Obligor determining whether the affected individuals have reached the age of eighteen. Thus, the Obligor violated the principle of data minimization set out in Article 5(1)(c) of the General Data Protection Regulation, as there exists a method that is appropriate for achieving the goal but involves fewer data processing operations and is less harmful to the affected individuals, meaning that the processing of personal data was not limited to what was necessary. [see: paragraph (174) of the reasoning of the Decision]

(14) The data security requirements set out in Articles 32(1) and (4) of the General Data Protection Regulation were violated because the Obligor did not establish adequate measures to protect the personal data of the affected individuals in his stores and within individual stores; did not apply the procedures prescribed by the Obligor; and some affected individuals had to communicate their birth dates at the explicit request of cashiers, in a manner audible to others, when purchasing alcoholic beverages. [see: paragraph (181) of the reasoning of the Decision]

(15) Due to the violations established in point 1 of the Decision, the Authority obligated the Obligor in point 2 of the Decision to

a) align his data processing practice for age verification in the purchase of alcoholic beverages with the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation, as well as with Sections 16/A(1) and (4) of the Fgytv., in such a way that
5

a) Only in case of doubt, verify the age of the customers and do not perform data recording at the log file level, and modify the relevant data processing information;

b) Place the valid data processing information prepared in accordance with Article 13 of the General Data Protection Regulation regarding the data processing activities taking place in each of its stores in a clearly visible and easily accessible location for the data subjects.

(16) Based on the violations established in point 1 of the Decision, due to the illegality of the data processing, the Authority has officially ordered the Obligor to pay a data protection fine of HUF 95,000,000, that is, ninety-five million forints, within 30 days from the finality of the Decision, as stated in point 3 of the Decision.

(17) In point 4 of the Decision, the Authority has officially ordered the publication of the final Decision with the identification data of the Obligor on the Authority's website, as well as on the homepage of the Obligor's website, in a clearly visible and easily accessible location, within 30 days from the finality of the Decision, and it should be available there for at least 30 days.

(18) On April 26, 2023, the Obligor paid the data protection fine of HUF 95,000,000 imposed in point 3 of the Decision to the Authority [see: document NAIH-3227-4/2023].

(19) In a statement received by the Authority on April 28, 2023 (statement NAIH-3227-5/2023), the Obligor stated that it had modified its practice, and the "software element requesting the submission of age has been deleted from the cash register software as of April 14, 2023." The Obligor also informed the Authority in the statement that the data processing information regarding the data processing activities carried out in the stores has been amended and published.

(20) In its lawsuit addressed to the Metropolitan Court, the Obligor (as the plaintiff) requested the annulment of points 1, 3, and 4 of the Decision.

(21) In its judgment (document NAIH-3932-1/2024), the Metropolitan Court annulled point 3 of the Decision and ordered the Authority (as the defendant) to conduct a new procedure in this regard. Beyond this, the Metropolitan Court rejected the lawsuit.

(22) According to paragraph [41] of the Reasoning of the Judgment, “[...] the court found that the defendant's decision regarding the retention of the date of birth in the log file is based on the violations established in point 1 of the operative part, as well as the provision imposing a fine of HUF 95,000,000 on the plaintiff (point 3) is unlawful. Regarding point 3, the decision was annulled by applying Section 89 (1) b) of the Administrative Procedure Code, and the defendant was ordered to conduct a new procedure in this regard. Concerning the data processing carried out with access and input, the plaintiff did not contest the subpoints a) and c)-f) of point 1 of the operative part of the decision, thus the court did not affect the decision in this regard. Furthermore, regarding the legal basis for the violation established in subpoint b) of point 1, as well as the provision ordering the publication of the decision in point 4, the lawsuit was rejected based on Section 88 (1) a) of the Administrative Procedure Code. In light of all this, the annulment of point 1 of the decision was not justified, as the defendant established a violation by evaluating the plaintiff's data processing as a whole, and concerning the data processing carried out with access and input, the court did not address the content of point 1.”
6

The violation of provisions exists regardless of whether the same findings were not lawful concerning the retention.

(23) According to paragraph [42] of the Judgment Reasoning, "In the repeated procedure, the defendant must make its decision with regard to the fact that the plaintiff did not commit a legal violation concerning the retention of the date of birth in the log file. It must again decide on the legal consequences applicable due to the established violations related to data processing carried out through access and input, noting that in the case of imposing a fine, its amount must be determined considering the narrower scope of the violation; the circumstances related to retention in the log file should not be taken into account during the consideration, and the duration of the violation established in connection with mandatory age verification (access, input) cannot be evaluated as an aggravating circumstance. Therefore, the amount of the fine must be proportionate, disregarding all these circumstances."

(24) In light of the above [see: paragraphs (1) – (23) of the reasoning of this decision] – except for point 3 of the Decision – the rest of the Decision has become final.

III. Applicable Legal Sources

(32) According to Article 2(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the General Data Protection Regulation applies to the data processing in this case.

(33) According to Article 4(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, personal data means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.

(34) According to Article 4(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, processing of personal data means any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.

(35) According to Article 4(7) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller is the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or any other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data; where the purposes and means of such processing are determined by Union or Member State law, the data controller or the specific aspects of the designation of the data controller may also be provided for by Union or Member State law.

IV. Judgment

(63) In its judgment, the Metropolitan Court annulled point 3 of the Decision based on Section 89, paragraph (1), point b) of Act I of 2017 on Administrative Procedure (hereinafter: Kp.) and ordered the Authority to conduct a new procedure in this regard. Beyond this, the Metropolitan Court rejected the claim.

(64) In paragraph [27] of the Justification of the Judgment, the Metropolitan Court stated that "the court found that after the purchase, the date of birth recorded and retained in the format of the keystroke 'NNHHÉÉÉÉ' due to input at the given cash register was not considered personal data, as it no longer allowed for the identification of the data subject. The date of birth in this form, along with all keystrokes, was retained solely for troubleshooting purposes and not for the identification of the data subject, without any other data that could be linked to the data subject. The numeric sequence of the date of birth recorded by keystroke during retention did not allow for the identification of the buyer as a natural person, but rather enabled the identification of the purchase […] Consequently, the retention of the date of birth in the log file for 180 days did not constitute data processing."

(65) According to paragraph [42] of the Justification of the Judgment, in the present repeated data protection authority procedure, the Authority must take into account that the Obligor "did not commit a violation regarding the retention of the date of birth in the log file."

(66) In its judgment, the Metropolitan Court further emphasized (see: paragraph [41] of the Justification of the Judgment) that the annulment of point 1 of the Decision was not justified because, in that point, the Authority established a violation by evaluating the Obligor's data processing as a whole; and the violation of the provisions contained in point 1 of the Decision regarding data processing realized through inspection and input exists regardless of the fact that the same findings regarding retention were considered "unlawful" by the Metropolitan Court.

(67) In light of the above [see: paragraphs (63) – (66) of the justification of this decision], the Metropolitan Court decided in its Judgment that – contrary to what was stated in the Decision – "the Obligor did not carry out data processing with the retention of the date of birth in the log file for 180 days."

V.1. Data Protection Fine

(69) The Authority may impose a data protection fine instead of or in addition to other measures, in accordance with Article 58(2)(i) and Article 83(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation.

(70) In the matter of whether it is justified to impose a data protection fine in the current repeated data protection authority procedure, the Authority acted within its statutory discretion, taking into account point a) of Section 61(1) of the Infotv., Section 75/A of the Infotv., as well as Article 83(2) and Article 58(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation. The Authority considered all circumstances of the case, paying particular attention to the contents of the Judgment, and determined that in the current repeated data protection authority procedure, a warning and a reprimand alone would neither be proportionate nor have a deterrent effect; therefore, the imposition of a fine is necessary, considering the market position of the Obligor, the general and nationwide nature of the practice it introduced, and the significant number of affected individuals. In this case, the protection of personal data—which is the Authority's task—cannot be achieved without the imposition of a data protection fine based on the totality of the circumstances for imposing the fine detailed below. The imposition of the fine serves both specific and general prevention, whereby the decision will be published not only on the Authority's website but also in a prominent and easily accessible location on the homepage of the Obligor's website.

(71) In determining the amount of the fine, the Authority primarily took into account that the Metropolitan Court decided in its Judgment that "the retention of the date of birth in the log file for 180 days did not constitute data processing" (see: paragraph [27] of the Reasoning of the Judgment); the Obligor "did not commit an infringement regarding the retention of the date of birth in the log file" (see: paragraph [42] of the Reasoning of the Judgment). However, the Authority cannot disregard that the additional, fundamental legal infringements established in the Decision still qualify as infringements falling under the higher fine category according to point a) of Article 83(5) of the General Data Protection Regulation, based on which the maximum fine that can be imposed is 20,000,000 EUR, or, in the case of enterprises, an amount not exceeding 4% of the total annual global turnover of the previous financial year, with the higher of the two amounts to be imposed.

(72) In determining the amount of the fine imposed in the Decision (95,000,000 HUF), the Authority considered the data from 2021, based on which the net revenue from sales of the Obligor in 2021 was 315,282,601,000 HUF. At the same time, the Authority also took into account that in comparison, the Obligor's after-tax profit was 8,504,878,000 HUF in that year [see: paragraph (184) of the reasoning of the Decision].

(73) In the current repeated data protection authority procedure, in determining the fine, the Authority took into account the business year of the Obligor for 2021, considering the period during which the infringements occurred.

(74) Based on the data from 2021, taking into account the amount of 315,282,601,000 HUF, which must be considered by the Authority, the statutory maximum fine in this case is 12,611,304,040 HUF. In comparison, the data protection fine of 80,000,000 HUF mentioned in this decision is 0.025% of the Obligor's net revenue from sales, which is significantly below the maximum fine amount.
15

(75) The Authority took the following mitigating circumstances into account when determining the amount of the data protection fine:

- Employees working in the position of store seller employed by the Obligor have, since September 1, 2022, only called upon customers to provide credible proof of their age for the purchase of alcoholic beverages in case of doubt; this is also included in paragraph [39] of the Judgment's Reasoning [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(a)];

- The infringement affected a narrow range of categories of personal data of the data subjects, as the recording was limited to the birth dates of the customers [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(g)];

- The Authority assessed that the primary aim of the measures introduced by the Obligor was to protect individuals under the age of eighteen (which was also confirmed in paragraph [35] of the Judgment's Reasoning), to comply as fully as possible with consumer protection legal requirements, and there was no information suggesting that it aimed at obtaining any other unlawful advantage [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(k)].

(76) The Authority took the following aggravating circumstances into account when determining the amount of the data protection fine – also considering paragraph [42] of the Judgment's Reasoning:

- The Obligor – as acknowledged in paragraph [39] of the Judgment's Reasoning – committed multiple infringements, violating other fundamental provisions as well, therefore the Authority deemed the nature of the infringements to be moderately serious, and such an assessment is not unreasonable based on paragraph [39] of the Judgment's Reasoning, even though the Obligor's practice of retaining data in the log file was "not unlawful" [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(a)];

- The number of affected individuals was significant, as confirmed in paragraph [33] of the Judgment's Reasoning [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(a)];

- The infringements existed at a national level [see: paragraphs (38) and (120) of the Decision's reasoning], and were not isolated incidents as the Obligor generally mandated the mandatory verification of the age of every person intending to purchase any alcoholic beverage during the period from August 4, 2022, to August 31, 2022; this was also confirmed in paragraphs [33] and [38] of the Judgment's Reasoning [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(a)];

- The data processing operations were opaque to the data subjects for an extended period as the data processing notifications were not easily accessible and provided incomplete information to the data subjects in several respects; this was also confirmed in paragraph [32] of the Judgment's Reasoning [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(a)];

- As a result of the data processing, certain data subjects who had reached the age of eighteen were prevented by the Obligor from exercising their right to contract (see: minutes of case number NAIH-6989-5/2022, page 6; minutes of case number NAIH-6989-6/2022, page 6); this was also confirmed in paragraph [37] of the Judgment's Reasoning [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83(2)(a)];

- The Authority, based on multiple reports received against the Obligor, perceived the likelihood of the unlawful nature of the Obligor's data processing practice, which resulted in the initiation of proceedings ex officio under case number NAIH-6989/2022; this was also confirmed in the Judgment.

The reasoning in paragraph [34] also confirmed [General Data Protection Regulation Article 83, paragraph (2), point h].

(77) In determining the amount of the data protection fine, the following circumstances – consistent with the reasoning in paragraph (187) of the Decision – were not considered to aggravate or mitigate the amount of the fine and were neutral in effect:

(79) Taking into account paragraphs [30], [31], and [42] of the Judgment's Reasoning, the Authority determined the amount of the fine in the current repeated data protection authority procedure by considering a narrower scope of the extent of the infringement; the circumstances related to the retention in the log file were not taken into account during the assessment; and the duration of the infringement established in connection with mandatory age verification (viewing, input) was not evaluated as an aggravating circumstance. However, the Authority cannot overlook that the Obligor – based on paragraph [39] of the Judgment's Reasoning, which was not disputed by them – committed multiple infringements; violated several fundamental provisions; furthermore, the number of affected individuals was significant; and the infringements existed on a national level, not being of an occasional nature.

(80) Based on the above [see: paragraphs (68) – (79) of the reasoning of this decision] and all the circumstances of the case, the Authority no longer evaluated 1 of the 6 aggravating circumstances originally considered in the Decision (the duration of the infringement) as an aggravating circumstance during the current repeated data protection authority procedure; thus – although not every infringement can be considered equally serious – the Authority decided to reduce the amount of the fine originally imposed in the Decision (95,000,000 HUF) by approximately 1/6 (15,000,000 HUF), resulting in a total data protection fine of 80,000,000 HUF. The amount of the data protection fine imposed in this decision – also considering the provisions of paragraph [42] of the Judgment's Reasoning – has become proportionally less than the fine originally imposed in the Decision.

(81) Based on the above and all the circumstances of the case, the Authority deemed the amount of the data protection fine specified in the operative part to be proportionate and deterrent in terms of both specific and general prevention, which amount remains significantly below the maximum fine; at the same time, it is proportionate to the severity of the infringements and, when compared to the revenue data for the year 2021, should not impose an unreasonable financial burden on the Obligor. This amount may be significantly different in other cases based on individual circumstances and does not bind the Authority in other matters.

(82) The Obligor paid the data protection fine of 95,000,000 HUF imposed in point 3 of the Decision to the Authority on April 26, 2023 [see: document with case number NAIH-3227-4/2023]. In light of this, the Authority will take action ex officio regarding the refund of the difference in the data protection fine to the Obligor as stated in the Decision and this current decision.

(83) Considering the period during which the infringements occurred, and that the Authority did not have to apply the European Data Protection Board's guidelines on the calculation of administrative fines under the General Data Protection Regulation (Guideline No. 04/2022) at the time of the Decision, the Authority also omits the application of the provisions contained therein during the current repeated data protection authority procedure. At the same time, the Authority notes that if the Guideline were to be applied in this case, the amount of the data protection fine would significantly exceed both the fine amount stated in the Decision and that in this current decision.

(87) Based on the provisions in paragraph [40] of the Reasoning of the Judgment, regarding the ordering of the publication of the final Decision – on both the Obligor's and the Authority's websites – the Metropolitan Court established that the Authority explicitly based this provision on Section 61 (2) a) of the Infotv., which was clearly stated in point 4 of the operative part of the Decision and in paragraph (194) of the reasoning of the Decision. The Authority justified the publication not by the severity of the legal violation but by the fact that the Decision affected a wide range of individuals. Considering that the Obligor ordered its criticized data processing practice in a general manner, nationwide, covering all its stores, meaning that the criticized practice was applied to every individual purchasing alcoholic beverages during the investigation period, it can undoubtedly be established that the Decision affected a wide range of individuals. For this reason alone, the Authority could order the publication of the Decision based on Section 61 (2) a) of the Infotv.

(88) Based on the above, the Metropolitan Court rejected the Obligor's claim regarding point 4 ordering the publication of the Decision based on Section 88 (1) a) of the Kp. (see: paragraph [41] of the Reasoning of the Judgment).

(89) Considering that the Obligor – as acknowledged in paragraph [39] of the Reasoning of the Judgment – committed multiple legal violations and violated several fundamental provisions, the Authority deemed the nature of the violations to be moderately severe, and this assessment was not unreasonable even though the Obligor's practice of retaining logs "was not unlawful." The number of affected individuals was significant, the legal violations were present nationwide across all of the Obligor's stores, were not of an occasional nature, and affected a wide range of natural persons.

(90) Since the decision made in the present repeated data protection authority procedure is closely related to the Decision and should be evaluated together with it, in light of the above [see: paragraphs (84) – (89) of the reasoning of this decision] – similarly to the Decision – the Authority ordered ex officio the publication of the final decision made in the present repeated data protection authority procedure with the identification data of the Obligor not only on the Authority's own website but also on the homepage of the Obligor's website, in a clearly visible and easily accessible location, for a duration of at least 30 days from the finalization of this decision.
