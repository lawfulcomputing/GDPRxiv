DECISION  

The National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Authority) hereby rejects the application of […] (address: […]; hereinafter: Applicant) in the data protection authority procedure initiated against the Municipality of Budapest, District XIII (headquarters: 1139 Budapest, Béke tér 1.) (hereinafter: Respondent), in which the Authority involved the Mayor's Office of the Municipality of Budapest, District XIII (headquarters: 1139 Budapest, Béke tér 1.; hereinafter: Mayor's Office) as a client.  

No procedural costs were incurred during the authority's procedure, therefore the Authority did not make a decision regarding their bearing.  

There is no administrative remedy against this decision, but it can be challenged in an administrative lawsuit by submitting a statement of claim addressed to the Metropolitan Court within 30 days from the notification. The statement of claim must be submitted electronically to the Authority, which will forward it along with the case documents to the court. A request for a hearing must be indicated in the statement of claim. For those not fully exempt from personal fees, the court fee for the administrative lawsuit is HUF 30,000, and the subject matter is subject to fee recording rights. Legal representation is mandatory in proceedings before the Metropolitan Court.  

REASONING  

I. The course of the procedure and the factual background  

(1) The Applicant submitted a request to the Authority on January 13, 2023, stating that on December 6, 2022, they submitted fifteen requests for public interest data to the Respondent, in which they requested that all contracts related to the reconstruction of Budapest District XIII, […] be sent to them in electronic form, along with the date of the public procurement procedures, the materials submitted for the procurements, the dates of decisions, and the documents preparing the decisions, the impact assessment related to the reconstruction, and the dates and results of preliminary consultations with the affected parties.  
(2) The Mayor's Office responded to the Applicant's data request on December 21, 2022, informing them that their data requests had been forwarded to XIII District Public Service Zrt., as the data controller.  
(3) For all these reasons, the Applicant turned to the Authority, as they believe that their personal data was forwarded by the Respondent to another independent data controller without legal basis.  
(4) The Applicant stated that by forwarding their data request, the Respondent severely violated their right to the protection of personal data, as their personal data was handed over to XIII District Public Service Zrt. without their consent and prior information.  
(5) According to the Applicant, the Respondent violated the principle of confidentiality set out in the General Data Protection Regulation by forwarding their personal data to a data controller with whom the Respondent did not wish to share the questions posed by the Applicant.  
(6) By its decision NAIH-1096-2/2023, dated March 6, 2023, the Authority contacted the Respondent and requested clarification on the purpose of the Applicant's requests for public interest data submitted on […] and the legal basis under Article 6 of the GDPR.

III. Decision  

III.1. General Findings  

(31) The email containing the request for access to public interest data contested by the Applicant included the Applicant's name, email address, and the request for access to public interest data, which, according to the definitions in the general data protection regulation, qualify as the Applicant's personal data.  
(32) The transfer of personal data, as an operation performed on personal data, is considered data processing under Article 4 (2) of the general data protection regulation.
5

III.2. Determination of the Data Controller

(33) According to the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller is the entity that determines the purposes and means of processing personal data in a substantive manner.  
(34) The Applicant submitted their request for a data protection authority procedure against the Municipality. According to Section 41 (2) of the Act on Local Governments, the Mayor's Office is the body of the Municipality's representative body, thus decisions related to it fundamentally fall within the competence of the Municipality's representative body, and within the framework of the Act on Local Governments, the representative body also decides on its establishment.  
(35) According to the municipal resolution on the organizational and operational regulations of the Mayor's Office, the Mayor's Office was established by the Municipality. Since the Mayor's Office possesses the rights and obligations associated with independent legal personality as defined in the Civil Code and other regulations, it is considered an independent legal entity. Under the Act on Local Governments, the tasks related to the management of the Mayor's Office are performed by the Notary.  
(36) According to Chapter 6 of the Notary's Instruction No. XXIII/4-4/2018 (II.5.) regarding the fulfillment of requests for access to public interest data available on the Mayor's Office website, the Mayor's Office is responsible for handling requests for access to public interest data managed by it. According to point 6.15 of the same chapter, if the Mayor's Office refuses to fulfill a request for access to public interest data, it must notify the applicant within 15 days. The Notary is authorized to issue the response.  
(37) The Applicant sent their request for data access to the Mayor's Office at the email address jegyzo@bp13.hu.  
(38) Since the Mayor's Office determined in relation to the Applicant's request that the requested data is not in its possession, it decided to forward the request.  
(39) Based on the above, the Mayor's Office decided on the forwarding of the Applicant's request - including the Applicant's personal data - and is therefore considered the data controller.  
(40) In relation to the contested data processing, the Respondent is not considered a data controller for the reasons stated above.  

III.3. Legal Basis for the Transfer of the Applicant's Personal Data

(41) Article 6 (1) of the General Data Protection Regulation provides several alternatives for data controllers regarding the selection of legal bases.  
(42) The data controller must review which legal basis it places the execution of the specific activity on before starting the data processing.  
(43) The Mayor's Office stated that the transfer of data in response to the data request was carried out due to the legal interest of the data requester, the Applicant, as stipulated in Article 6 (1) (f) of the General Data Protection Regulation.  
(44) According to Article 6 (1) (f) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the processing of personal data may be lawful if it is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or a third party.  
(45) The Applicant, as the data subject, cannot be considered a third party according to Article 4 (10) of the General Data Protection Regulation; therefore, the reference to legitimate interest cannot apply in this case. For all these reasons, the Authority does not consider the legal basis indicated by the Mayor's Office to be acceptable.  
(46) Data processing may be lawful under Article 6 (1) (e) of the GDPR if it is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller. The Authority's position is that, as established in its consistent and established practice, public authorities or bodies performing public tasks should generally apply this legal basis, as all public law and private law relationships of these bodies, and the data processing relationships related to them, can only be subject to the performance of their public tasks, and any other qualification is excluded. Therefore, since the Mayor's Office carried out the forwarding of the Applicant's request for access to public interest data in the exercise of its public authority and public task, the appropriate legal basis for the data transfer may be Article 6 (1) (e) of the GDPR.  
(47) In this context, the GDPR provision must be applied, according to which data processing...

(48) The basis must be determined by Union law or national law.  
(49) Although the Mayor's Office referred to its obligation to transfer, since the transfer is a legal institution applicable in administrative authority procedures and the assessment of requests for access to public interest data does not fall under the scope of the Administrative Procedure Act (Ákr.), the Mayor's Office's reference to the provisions of the Ákr. was not appropriate in this case.  
(50) The general rules for accessing public interest data are prescribed in Chapter III of the Information Act (Infotv.), which also does not include a transfer obligation as a requirement.  
(51) At the same time, the Council of Europe’s Convention on Access to Official Documents, which was incorporated into Hungarian law by Act CXXXI of 2009, is a binding regulation for all public bodies in Hungary, stipulating in Article 5(2) that the data controller must, where possible, forward the data request to the competent public authority or inform the requester about which competent public authority they can turn to.  
(52) Neither of the two obligations under Article 5(2) of the Convention takes precedence, and the assessment of which solution the data controller chooses depends on the circumstances of the specific case.  
(53) The Applicant did not claim in their data request that sending the data request would have adverse consequences for them regarding the personal data in question. This circumstance was only elaborated by the Applicant later in a request addressed to the Authority. Therefore, the Mayor's Office could not recognize based on the Applicant's submission that the Applicant would suffer direct disadvantages if the content of their request were known to XIII District Public Utility Ltd. The Authority also agreed with the Mayor's Office's argument that it forwarded the data request precisely to facilitate another prominent fundamental right, the Applicant's access to public interest information, which the Authority considers good practice for the fullest realization of freedom of information.  
(54) Furthermore, according to the statement of the Notary, XIII District Public Utility Ltd. had already previously obtained the Applicant's name and contact details, and the Applicant did not substantiate the mere assertion of direct disadvantage with any evidence, nor did any facts or circumstances arise in this regard.  
(55) For all these reasons, the Authority rejected the Applicant's request.
