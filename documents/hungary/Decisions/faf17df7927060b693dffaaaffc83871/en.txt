2. The process of data processing, clarification of roles in data processing in relation to SMS sending

2.1 According to the definition in Article 4, point 7 of the GDPR, the data controller is a natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or any other body that determines the purposes and means of processing personal data, either alone or jointly with others, while according to point 8, the data processor is a natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or any other body that processes personal data on behalf of the data controller.

According to the EDPB guidelines No. 7/2020 (hereinafter: Guideline No. 7/2020) regarding the definitions of data controller and data processor under the GDPR, the term "determines" among the elements described above refers to the data controller's influence on data processing through the exercise of decision-making authority. The data controller is the one who determines the key elements of data processing. To establish this, it is necessary to examine the specific data processing operations and to understand who determines them, i.e., to investigate why the specific data processing took place and who decided that the data processing should be carried out for a specific purpose.

A crucial component of the definition of the data controller is the controller's influence on the purposes and means of data processing. Defining the purposes and means means deciding why and how data processing occurs. In the case of a specific data processing operation, the data controller is the entity that determines why the data processing should take place—i.e., for what purpose, or why—and how this purpose can be achieved, i.e., what means should be used to achieve the purpose. According to Article 4, point 7 of the GDPR, a legal entity that influences the processing of personal data participates in determining the purposes and means of processing. The data controller must decide both on the purpose of the processing and on its means. The data controller cannot agree only on the purpose; it must also make a decision regarding the means of processing. In contrast, the party acting as a data processor can never determine the purpose of the data processing. 

In addition to the above, the guidelines emphasize that anyone who decides to process data must consider whether this involves personal data, and if so, what obligations exist under the GDPR. An entity is also considered a "data controller" even if it does not intentionally target personal data or mistakenly determines that it does not process personal data.

Datadat Ltd. commissioned Datadat GmbH to send SMS messages to the phone numbers recorded through the subscription channels on behalf of SzondaPhone Ltd. "v.a."  
According to its statement, Datadat Ltd. ordered SMS sending, CRM system operation, Facebook Messenger chatbot channel operation, software operation supporting the creation of online subscription websites, and mass email sending services from Datadat GmbH. The designated employees of Datadat Ltd. also have access to the database storing personal data collected through the subscription channels.  

The Association – according to its statement – commissioned Datadat GmbH to send SMS messages. The phone number data used for sending SMS messages was collected through the subscription channels available on the Association's website and the WinWithMe Facebook Messenger application, the installation of which was ordered by Datadat Ltd. from Datadat GmbH. (According to the listing in point 2 of the attached contract dated May 1, 2021, between Datadat GmbH and Datadat Ltd., the services provided by the GmbH include: i. Digital Advertisement Production; ii. Digital Campaign Management; iii. Robocall campaigns; iv. SMS campaigns; v. Call center services; vi. SAAS services: Digitribe, WinWithMe, Action Network, Custom Integration.)  

2.5 As outlined in point 2.1, a significant element in defining the data controller is the data controller's influence over the purposes and means of data processing. Based on the above, the Authority establishes that both the Association, Datadat Ltd., and Datadat GmbH are data controllers concerning the examined data processing, as they played an active role in determining the means of data processing and shaping the manner of data processing as described above.  
The Authority also examined, based on Article 26 of the GDPR, whether they qualify as joint data controllers or parallel data controllers. According to Article 26 of the GDPR, if the purposes and means of data processing are jointly determined by two or more data controllers, they are considered joint data controllers.  
In this regard, guidance number 7/2020 highlights that "joint participation in the determination of purposes and means means that more than one legal entity has a decisive influence on whether the data processing occurs, and if so, how." "Joint participation...
12  
may manifest, for example, in the form of a joint decision made by two or more organizations, or may arise from coordinated decisions of two or more organizations regarding the objectives and fundamental means.” “Decisions can be considered coordinated regarding objectives and means if they complement each other and are necessary for the data processing to occur in a way that has a tangible impact on determining the purposes and methods of data processing.” “An important criterion for identifying coordinated decisions is whether the data processing would not be possible without the participation of both parties in terms of objectives and means, in the sense that the data processing carried out by each party is inseparable, that is, inextricably linked.”  
The guidance further emphasizes that “joint data processing also requires that two or more organizations exert influence over the manner of data processing. This does not mean that the existence of joint data processing requires all involved organizations to determine all means in every case.” “Different joint data controllers may determine the manner of data processing to varying degrees depending on who is actually in a position to do so.” “It may also occur that one involved organization provides the means of data processing and makes it available for personal data processing activities carried out by other organizations. The organization that decides to use these means for the processing of personal data for a specific purpose also participates in the determination of the means of data processing.”  

In relation to the data processing examined, the Authority found that the purpose of the data processing – namely, the sending of an additional registration link for the day of the pre-election vote – was determined by the Association. However, according to the Authority's findings, the means of data processing were determined jointly by the Association, Datadat Ltd., and Datadat GmbH: they decided through a chain of mutual mandates that they wished to deliver the message to the data subjects in the form of an SMS, and they made coordinated decisions that telephone number data were necessary for sending SMS messages, which could be collected through the subscription channels created on the Association's websites. In May 2021, Datadat Ltd. ordered SMS sending, CRM system operation, Facebook Messenger chatbot channel operation, online subscription website development support software operation, and mass email sending services from Datadat GmbH, and also commissioned the GmbH to give a specific, technical SMS sending mandate to SzondaPhone Ltd. “v.a.”. Ultimately, all these means contributed to the realization of SMS messaging, in which they jointly participated in determining the necessary services and made coordinated decisions regarding the manner of data processing, its means, and its implementation, which data processing operations were inseparable and closely related; these agreements represented the establishment of the substantive process of data processing and went beyond mere technical execution and technical operations. All of this presupposes the existence of specific, and in some cases innovative, expertise related to infocommunication solutions, the data economy, the business use of social media, and political marketing, which these companies provide as a service. Therefore, as a result of the investigation, the Authority concluded that the Association, Datadat Ltd., and Datadat GmbH qualify as joint data controllers concerning the examined data processing.  

The Authority emphasizes that data controller liability cannot be evaded by organizations attempting to circumvent data controller responsibility through mutual mandates, multiple contractual constructions, essentially through a contractual chain, or by hiding behind a financially less accountable association to disguise their role in data processing. Due to the principle of accountability, the data controller is responsible for the compliance of the data processing and must be able to demonstrate this, even if it attempts to disguise its data controller status in a given case.  

Guidance No. 7/2020 also emphasizes that “it is not possible to become a data controller or to evade data controller obligations simply by structuring the contract in such a way that the factual circumstances suggest otherwise,” as this would result in the nullification of Article 26(3) of the GDPR. This was confirmed by the Capital Court.
13  
In its judgment No. 105.K.701.083/2023/9, the Court stated that "Article 26(1) of the GDPR allows for the allocation of tasks and responsibilities related to the obligations imposed by the GDPR arising from data processing, and does not authorize any of the data controllers to be exempt from all data controller responsibilities, as this would annul the data controller status itself and lead to the emptying of Article 26(3) of the GDPR."

In relation to the above, the Authority emphasizes that although the GmbH can be identified as a joint data controller, it has determined that it does not have jurisdiction over the GmbH, and therefore it is not in a position to make a determination regarding this organization. However, the Authority stresses that in the future, matters concerning the company's data processing can be requested as local cases within the framework of the One Stop Shop.

3. Legality of Data Processing

According to the findings established in the previous point, in relation to SMS messaging, the Association, Datadat Ltd., and Datadat GmbH qualify as joint data controllers. The Association carried out the data processing related to SMS sending by utilizing Datadat GmbH, which in turn engaged another subcontractor for the data processing related to SMS sending, specifically for the technical execution of SMS sending and the collection of phone numbers used for SMS sending.

Based on the established facts, the collection of personal data occurred directly from the data subjects through the subscription pages of kilencvenkilenc.hu and karacsonygergely.hu, and sympathizer data was also collected via the WinWithMe Facebook application.

During the data collection through the kilencvenkilenc.hu website, there was an option for online data submission under the "Join" menu item, specifically under "manual joining," and in addition to this manual joining, the site also offered the possibility to apply via Facebook. The site promoted the joining with the following text: "Let Gergely Karácsony be the Prime Minister of Hungary! We create a homeland that offers a chance to everyone, a future for everyone, and a happy life. It is time to build a new policy that represents the interests of 99% of the country instead of the wealthiest 1%. Join us!"  
Currently, this menu item is not available on the site.

To join, it was possible to provide the following data: last name, first name, email address, which data fields were marked with an asterisk (*).  
There was also the option to provide phone number, postal code, and address details, which were not marked with an asterisk.  
Subsequently, the data subjects had to indicate their acceptance of the privacy notice by checking the checkbox "I have read and accept the privacy statement" before clicking the "I join" button, which statement was accessible via a hyperlink to the now also unavailable https://kilencvenkilenc.hu/adatkezelesi-tajekoztato/ page.

In addition to the above data collection, it occurred on the https://karacsonygergely.hu website, where by clicking on the "Join" tab, the site also navigated to the previously described interface of the kilencvenkilenc.hu website.

According to the data processing notice and the statement of the Association, the legal basis for the processing of personal data provided during the joining process through the designated pages is the consent of the data subjects as per Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR. The purpose of data processing, according to the notice, is to enable the Association to maintain contact with its sympathizers and to send them targeted information (newsletter, advertisement). The duration of data processing, as stated in the data processing notice, lasts until the withdrawal of consent.

3.1. Legal Basis for Data Processing  
a) According to the definition in the GDPR, the name, email address, phone number, and residential address data are considered personal data of the data subject, while any operation performed on the data, such as collection, recording, storage, and use of the data, is classified as data processing.  
Under the provisions of the GDPR, several requirements must be met for the legality of data processing. Among these, the principles of legality, fairness, and transparency, as well as purpose limitation, as stated in Article 5(1)(a) and (b) of the GDPR, play a prominent role. Furthermore, the data controller must have a legal basis for data processing in accordance with Article 6(1) of the GDPR.  

The consent referred to as the legal basis for data processing under Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR means that the processing of personal data is lawful if the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes.  

In order for the data controller to lawfully rely on consent as the legal basis, all conceptual elements of consent must meet the relevant requirements.  

b) Considering that the data subjects have expressed their political opinions by joining based on the information available on the designated websites, the data provided in this manner is also considered information specifically indicating the political opinions of the data subjects, and as such, it qualifies as special categories of personal data under Article 9(1) of the GDPR.  

The processing of special categories of personal data is generally prohibited by the regulation and is subject to strict conditions. Such special categories of personal data may be processed, among other things, if the data subject has given explicit consent to the processing for one or more specific purposes, as stated in Article 9(2) of the GDPR.  

3.2 Purpose of Data Processing  

According to Article 5(1)(a) of the GDPR, the processing of personal data must be carried out lawfully and fairly, and in a transparent manner for the data subjects. Furthermore, paragraph (b) of the same article provides for the principle of purpose limitation, which states that the collection of personal data may only occur for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes, and that such data may not be processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes.  

Articles 5(1)(a) and (b) of the GDPR, along with the (39) recital, further state that it must be transparent to natural persons how their personal data is collected, used, accessed, or otherwise processed, as well as the extent to which their personal data is processed and will be processed. The principle of transparency also applies to informing data subjects about the purposes of data processing. The specific purposes of personal data processing must be explicitly formulated and lawful, and must already be defined at the time of collecting the personal data.  

Article 13 of the GDPR specifies the information that must be provided to data subjects at the time of data acquisition.  

The content of the message inviting to join on the website https://csatlakozz.kilencvenkilenc.hu/form/segitek does not indicate that the data subjects would be joining an unnamed community for further contact purposes, nor does the data collection interface itself contain any reference to the specific purpose for which the data subjects are consenting to data processing, or that they are "joining" something according to the data collection interface.  

In contrast, the data processing information notice informs the data subjects about the purpose of data processing, stating that by "joining" on the site, the data subjects...
15  
express their intention to join as sympathizers, thereby contributing inseparably to the Association's ability to maintain contact with them and send targeted information – newsletters, advertisements – to them.

It is, however, not clear for the affected parties based on the information and the text placed on the website https://csatlakozz.kilencvenkilenc.hu/form/segitek, to whom/which organization their declared intention to join actually signifies support. The website https://csatlakozz.kilencvenkilenc.hu/form/segitek refers to support for Gergely Karácsony and joining his sympathizers, while the data processing information already indicates in the section describing the purpose of data processing that the Association intends to maintain contact with its sympathizers.  
Thus, it was not clear and transparent for the affected parties whose management their personal data provided during the "joining" process would actually fall under, and for what specific purpose they were giving their consent to data processing. This is further supported by the fact that by clicking on the "Join!" menu item on the karacsonygergely.hu website, the affected party is directed to the Association's website.

In general, it is important to clearly indicate who the data controller is, who manages personal data for the given purposes; however, this is an even more prominent requirement in the case of political data processing.

The preamble (32) of the GDPR states that if data processing serves multiple purposes simultaneously, consent must be given separately for each data processing purpose. If the data controller does not attempt to request separate consent for each purpose, the freedom of decision is lacking.

The lawfulness of the processing of the affected parties' personal data can therefore be established if the affected party has received adequate information regarding the specific purpose(s) for which their personal data is being collected, and if the affected party has given separate, i.e., clear and specific consent to the processing of their personal data for all purposes.

The Authority finds that the Association, by failing to provide the affected parties with adequate information regarding the purpose of data processing, violates the transparency requirement set forth in Article 5(1)(a) of the GDPR.

4. Information on data processing

4.1 According to Article 12(1) of the GDPR, the data controller is obliged to take appropriate measures to provide the affected parties with all information regarding the processing of personal data mentioned in Articles 13 and 14, as well as every piece of information under Articles 15-22 and 34, in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible form, clearly and comprehensibly formulated.  
The system of appropriate information in the GDPR serves to ensure that the affected party is aware of which personal data will be processed by which data controller and for what purpose, and how it will be processed. This is essential for the affected party to be in a position to effectively exercise their rights.

In the case of data processing based on Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR, the data controller is obliged, according to Article 4(11) of the GDPR, to provide information that allows for informed consent to be given not only before the commencement of data processing but also before obtaining consent.

Consent to data processing can only be valid if it is requested for specific purpose(s) – which can be specified separately for each purpose – and if adequate information is provided beforehand, placing the affected party in a position to make an informed decision regarding the granting of consent, and meeting all validity requirements set forth in the GDPR. Both the provisions in the preamble and Article 12(1) of the GDPR prescribe the achievement of the results.
16  
When determining the obligations of the data controller, it must provide assistance to the data subject so that they can exercise all their rights in an informed manner.

The principle of fair and transparent data processing, as stated in Article 5(1)(a) of the GDPR, requires that the data subject be informed about the fact and purpose of the data processing. Therefore, one essential condition for the lawfulness of data processing is that the data controller adequately informs the data subject about all important circumstances of the data processing.

Articles 13-14 of the GDPR specify what information must be provided regarding the processing of personal data, while Article 12 of the GDPR provides guidance on the formal requirements for such information.

4.2 The Authority establishes that the content of the data processing information does not provide adequate information about all the actual purposes of the data processing, as it did not give the data subjects clear and detailed information about what exactly they are consenting to (the processing of their personal data in a supporter registry and/or further data processing for contact purposes).

Although the name and email address fields were marked with an asterisk on the online data collection page, there was no information regarding the significance of this, either on the data collection interface or in the data processing information, about whether all data must be provided for the "joining" to be successful, or if it is sufficient to provide only the personal data marked with an asterisk. Thus, it may have seemed to the data subjects that providing all data was mandatory.

Furthermore, the data processing information did not include any information on how the data controller plans to maintain further contact, so the data subject could not clearly decide which method they prefer if the data controller wishes to maintain contact with the data subjects through multiple channels in the future, and which necessary personal data they would provide for that.

4.3 The Authority establishes that the data processing information provided by the data controller included information about which data processors are used for the data processing; however, during the clarification of the facts, the Datadat Ltd. referenced by the Association, as well as any further subcontractors it employs, are not named, nor is it specified what activities the organizations and legal entities participating in the data processing perform. In the case of the particularly complex, multi-tier subcontracting system applied in the examined data processing, it would be especially important to provide a proper and easily understandable description of which legal entity performs which tasks in relation to the personal data.

4.4 The Authority further establishes that, according to the Association's statement, it continues to process the personal data of more than 63,000 data subjects collected through the designated data collection, but the data processing information is no longer available on the Association's website, nor is there any information regarding data processing on the designated website of the Association.

4.5 Based on the above, the Authority establishes that

(i) The Association and Datadat Ltd. have not provided clear, adequate, and accurate information to the data subjects about all essential circumstances of the data processing in connection with the data collection on the designated websites, and they are currently not providing any information to the data subjects regarding the data processing, as the online data processing information has been removed from the Association's website despite the fact that it continues to carry out data processing, thereby violating Articles 13(1)-(2) of the GDPR.

(ii) The Association and Datadat Ltd. also violate the provisions of Article 12(1) of the GDPR by currently not providing any information to the data subjects regarding their data processing.

5. Validity of the Legal Basis for Data Processing

According to the definition in Article 4(11) of the GDPR, the consent of the data subject is a voluntary, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes, by which the data subject signifies agreement to the processing of personal data concerning them, either by a statement or by a clear affirmative action. Therefore, consent can only be considered a valid legal basis for data processing if all of its conditions are met.

All conceptual elements of consent can be fully realized if the request for consent is preceded by adequate information. Adequate information is that through which data subjects become aware of the data processing concerning their personal data, and through this information, the right to informational self-determination can be exercised: the data processing can be lawful if the circumstances are fully known to the data subjects.

The Authority finds that, as a result of the explanations provided in points 3 and 4 above, the consent of the data subjects for data processing lacks the most important conceptual elements, the presence of which is necessary for the legal basis of data processing, namely the validity of consent. Consequently, the Association and Datadat Ltd. are processing the personal data provided by the data subjects on the designated websites without a valid legal basis, thereby violating Article 6 of the GDPR. Since this data falls into the special categories of personal data, the data processing also violates Article 9(1) of the GDPR.

6. According to Article 31 of the GDPR, the data controller must cooperate with the supervisory authority in the performance of its duties – upon the authority's request. Considering that the Association has not responded at all to the Authority's requests despite several calls, it has violated the obligation to cooperate under Article 31 of the GDPR.
