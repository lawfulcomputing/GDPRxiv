DECISION  

The National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Authority) […] (hereinafter: Applicant) (represented by: […] individual attorney; registered office: […] ; hereinafter: legal representative) regarding the data processing conducted by the camera system installed on the facade of the residential building with number […] (hereinafter: Residential Building) against […] (hereinafter: Respondent) initiated in the data protection authority procedure – in which the Authority also granted the Residential Building party status – rejects the Applicant's application.  

No administrative remedy is available against this decision, but it can be contested in an administrative lawsuit by submitting a statement of claim addressed to the Metropolitan Court within 30 days from the notification. The statement of claim must be submitted to the Authority electronically1, which will forward it to the court along with the case documents. A request for a hearing must be indicated in the statement of claim. For those not fully exempt from personal fees, the administrative lawsuit fee is HUF 30,000, and the subject matter is subject to fee recording rights. Legal representation is mandatory in proceedings before the Metropolitan Court.

II. Clarification of the Facts

(18) The Applicant, acting with legal representation, stated in their request for the data protection authority procedure that they are the owner of the properties located at numbers […] and […] in the Condominium.

(19) The Applicant further stated that the three buildings that make up the Condominium have been operating separately for decades, and the ownership communities of the individual buildings have elected their own common representatives over time, “who in many cases have disregarded what is stated in the founding document and treated the individual buildings as quasi-independent condominiums, which has led to an uncertain situation where it has not been clarified, for example, who the common representative of the condominium is, and to what extent the common representatives elected by the individual buildings can represent the condominium.”

(20) According to the Applicant, the common representative of the building at number […] was elected without legal basis, and […] installed a camera system consisting of four cameras on the building, which has been continuously operating since then, recording images from the entrance door, the garden gate, the vehicle parking area, and the garden.

(21) The Applicant stated that the Condominium has never made a decision regarding the installation of the camera system, nor is there any knowledge of such a provision concerning the partial assembly of the building at number […]; however, in their opinion, even if there were such a decision, it would not meet the legal requirements due to its unlawful operation.

(22) The Applicant explained that the lawful operation of the Condominium was restored thereafter, and on December 16, 2021, the general assembly elected the Respondent as the common representative, who, according to the Applicant's position, qualifies as a data controller and data processor regarding the data processing performed by the camera system.

(23) The Applicant objected that there is no information board, sticker, or other notice regarding the operation of the camera system, and the residents are not aware of how the recorded footage is stored, for how long, and for what purpose it is used; consequently, the data controller has the opportunity to misuse personal data.

(24) According to the Applicant, the Respondent unlawfully records, processes, and stores personal data through the camera system installed on the building and neglects to communicate the necessary notices and information in this regard. The Applicant asserts that the Respondent has not fulfilled the information obligation set out in Article 13(1)-(2) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter: General Data Protection Regulation) concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, as well as the requirement under Article 12(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation that the information be concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible, in a written form that is clear and plain.

(33) According to Section 2(2) of Act CXII of 2011 on the Right to Informational Self-Determination and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Infotv.), the General Data Protection Regulation must be applied with the supplements specified in the provisions indicated therein.

(37) According to point 1 of Article 4 of the General Data Protection Regulation: “personal data”: any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘data subject’); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, particularly by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural or social identity of that natural person.”

IV. Decision  

(45) Based on the definitions in the General Data Protection Regulation, a person's face, image, and actions visible in the recording are considered personal data, and the act of making the recording, as well as any operation performed on the personal data, is classified as data processing.  

(46) The main rules regarding the establishment and operation of cameras in condominium buildings are contained in the General Data Protection Regulation and Section 25 of the Thtv.  

(47) A camera system in a condominium can be applied based on the principle of purpose limitation of data processing as defined in Article 5(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation for a lawful purpose, provided that the given purpose cannot be achieved by other means, that the use of technical equipment is absolutely necessary for this purpose, and that it does not result in an unreasonable restriction of the right to informational self-determination.  

(48) An additional requirement for data processing related to camera surveillance conducted by the condominium as a data controller is that the establishment and operation of an electronic surveillance system, built with a closed system technical solution for monitoring common property areas, rooms, and spaces, must be decided by the general assembly with the affirmative vote of co-owners holding at least two-thirds of the ownership shares. Therefore, the affirmative vote of co-owners holding two-thirds of the ownership shares is required for a valid decision regarding the establishment and commissioning of the camera system, rather than a majority or two-thirds of the co-owners present at the general assembly.  

(49) The camera system must not target the entrance or other openings of a privately owned apartment or non-residential premises, even if it is located on common property, building parts, or areas. The camera system cannot be placed in common property and in areas jointly used by co-owners where monitoring could violate human dignity due to the nature of the room (e.g., changing room, restroom).  

(50) Another requirement is that the organizational and operational regulations of the condominium must include the data processing rules necessary for the operation of the camera system, established in accordance with the provisions on the protection of personal data. In addition, individuals intending to enter or stay in a building, building part, or area monitored by the camera system must be informed of the necessary information regarding the protection of personal data, particularly about the existence of the camera system, the rights of the data subjects, the identity of the operator, and their contact details.  

(51) Furthermore, based on a contract concluded by the common representative or the management board, the operator of the camera system may be a person defined in the Szvtv.
8  
(52) Cameras can be operated in condominium buildings based on these rules. In the present case, according to the Applicant, the Condominium and the partial assembly of the building at […] did not make a decision regarding the installation and operation of the system. The Respondent also does not have any documents regarding this matter and, according to their statement, the Condominium does not have an operational contract, consequently, they do not operate the camera system either.  

(53) The examination of the circumstances surrounding the installation of the four cameras is not the subject of this procedure; the Applicant objected that the camera system unlawfully records, processes, and stores personal data, and there are no notices or information regarding data processing.  

(54) However, based on the statements presented to the Authority and the on-site inspections conducted by the Notary, it can be established that none of the four cameras installed by the previous common representative, […] on the building at […] are operational, and both the cameras and the recording unit have become irreparably inoperable. No contrary information is available to the Authority, and the Applicant has not presented any evidence to substantiate their claims. Therefore, the cameras neither transmit live images nor record footage, thus they are not suitable for processing personal data and do not perform any data processing.  

(55) Consequently, the Authority rejects the Applicant's request due to the absence of data processing and, consequently, a violation of data protection laws.
