H A T A R O Z A T  

The National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Authority) has established in the repeated new data protection authority proceeding concerning the data breach reported electronically by Digi Telecommunications and Service Ltd. (registered office: 1134 Budapest, Váci út 35., company registration number: 01-09-667975) (hereinafter: Client or Data Controller) (represented by: […]) on September 25, 2019, under identification number […], regarding the fulfillment of obligations set out in Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter: General Data Protection Regulation) on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, as well as the repeal of Directive 95/46/EC, initiated on October 8, 2019, based on findings established during the authority inspection, which was officially initiated on December 16, 2019, and closed with decision number NAIH/2020/1160/10, and subsequently in accordance with the judgment number 105.K.704.076/2022/4 of the Metropolitan Court, the following is determined:  

1.  
It is established that  

a. the Client violated Article 5(1)(e) ("storage limitation") of the General Data Protection Regulation when it did not delete the test database involved in the data breach after running the necessary tests and correcting the error. Thus, the large number of customer data stored in the test database was kept in its systems in such a way that the personal data of natural persons could be identified, and the affected individuals remained identifiable for the following one and a half years, even though it no longer needed this data in connection with the provision of its services. The failure to delete the database directly enabled the occurrence of the data breach and the accessibility of personal data.  

b. The Client violated Articles 32(1)-(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, thus it did not implement appropriate technical and organizational measures proportional to the risks regarding the security of data processing, in that  
- the content management system used by it ([…]) had a vulnerability that had been known for more than 9 years and could otherwise be detected and fixed with appropriate tools, which allowed access to the databases affected by the incident through the publicly accessible digi.hu website;  
- regarding the personal data affected by the data breach, ([…]) did not apply encryption, which significantly increased the risks arising from the incident.  

2.  
The lack of these measures directly allowed the customer data stored in the databases to become accessible through the vulnerability exploited by the attacking hacker.  

2.  
The Client is obliged to review all databases containing personal data processed by it to determine whether encryption is warranted and to inform the Authority of the results!  

3.  
Due to the above violations, the Client is obliged to pay a data protection fine of  

80,000,000 HUF, that is, eighty million forints  

within 30 days from the date this decision becomes final;  

4.  
It orders the publication of the final decision with the identification data of the data controller, but with the business secrets redacted.  

In case of non-compliance with the request in point 2, the Authority orders the enforcement of the decision, the fine, and the late payment penalty.

c.  
First decision made during the data protection authority procedure and its judicial review  

(30) Based on the facts described above, the Authority found a violation by the Client, therefore on May 18, 2020, under case number NAIH/2020/1160/10, it issued a decision in which it held the Client liable for violating Article 5(1)(b) and (e) (principles of “purpose limitation” and “storage limitation”) as well as Article 32(1)-(2) (security of processing) of the General Data Protection Regulation. As a result, the Authority obliged the Client to review all of its databases containing personal data to determine whether encryption is warranted, and to notify the Authority of the results. Furthermore, due to the established violations, the Authority imposed a data protection fine of 100,000,000 HUF on the Client and ordered the publication of the decision with the Client's identification data.  

(31) The Client, through its legal representative, challenged the Authority's decision NAIH/2020/1160/10 before the Metropolitan Court (hereinafter: Court). In the proceedings, the Court initiated a preliminary ruling procedure before the Court of Justice of the European Union (hereinafter: CJEU) for the interpretation of Article 5(1)(b) and (e) of the General Data Protection Regulation.  

(32) In its judgment C-77/21, the CJEU stated that it is not contrary to the principle of “purpose limitation” if the data controller records and stores personal data previously collected and stored in another database in a database created for the purpose of conducting tests and correcting errors, provided that such further processing complies with the specific purposes for which the personal data was originally collected, which must be determined in light of the criteria set out in Article 6(4) of the General Data Protection Regulation. At the same time, it considered it contrary to the principle of “storage limitation” if the data controller uses personal data collected for other purposes for the purpose of conducting tests and correcting errors.
8

the created database for a longer period than necessary for the performance of these tests and the correction of these errors.

(33) The Court, taking into account the findings established in the EUB decision, in its judgment numbered 105.K.704.076/2022/4, determined that the Client's action challenging the Authority's decision NAIH/2020/1160/10 was partially well-founded.

The Court's findings regarding the principle of purpose limitation (34-38):

(34) In its judgment, the Court pointed out that it was not disputed between the parties (the Client as the plaintiff and the Authority as the defendant) that the plaintiff Client collected and stored the personal data of its residential customers for the purpose of entering into and fulfilling subscriber contracts, and this original data processing purpose was lawful under Article 6(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation. Additionally, in April 2018, a technical error affecting the operation of the server led to the creation of the Test Database for troubleshooting purposes, resulting in the termination of subscriber data. Although the original data processing purpose was the conclusion and fulfillment of subscriber contracts, this alone did not mean that additional so-called "ancillary purposes" could not be associated with the realization of this purpose, which also results in the principle of "purpose limitation" being applicable in relation to this "ancillary purpose."

(35) The EUB, in its judgment (in paragraph 35), established that further data processing for another purpose is permissible if the further processing of personal data is compatible with the original purpose of data collection, and to determine this – considering Article 6(4) of the General Data Protection Regulation – several factors must be taken into account, including: the purpose of collecting personal data and the purpose of the planned further processing, the circumstances of the collection of personal data, the nature of the personal data, the possible consequences of the further processing for the data subjects, and the existence of appropriate safeguards. The criteria set out in Article 6(4) of the General Data Protection Regulation are illustrative, and the existence of even a single circumstance among them may be sufficient for compliance with the provisions of Article 5(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation. The EUB also stated in its Judgment (in paragraph 38) that it is the task of the national court to determine the original purpose of data collection and the purpose of further processing, taking into account the aforementioned criteria and the totality of circumstances characteristic of the given case, and if the purpose of further processing differs from the original purpose of data processing, to examine whether the further processing is compatible with the original data collection purpose.

(36) The Court, in light of the EUB judgment and the undisputed facts presented by the parties, established that the plaintiff originally collected and stored the personal data for the purpose of entering into and fulfilling subscriber contracts, and that the direct purpose of creating the Test Database was related to a specific error that had arisen, namely to perform the necessary tests and correct the error. The court further established, in accordance with what is stated in paragraph 44 of the Judgment, that although the Test Database was created for troubleshooting purposes by the plaintiff, it was nonetheless specifically related to the original data processing purpose of the personal data. Testing and correcting errors is clearly necessary for the plaintiff to access the subscriber data and utilize it for the
9

for the conclusion and performance of subscriber contracts, thereby facilitating the realization of the original data processing purpose.

(37) The Authority – in its statement made during the trial, in light of the EUB Judgment – did not dispute the compatibility of the original data processing purpose (conclusion and performance of contracts) and the further data processing purpose (error correction) during the proceedings, and it was not significant that, based on other circumstances to be examined, compatibility could not be established, since even the existence of a single circumstance could justify compatibility.

(38) Based on the above, the Client – with regard to Article 6(4)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation – processed the personal data of customers in the test database lawfully, in accordance with the provisions of Article 5(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation, in a manner compatible with the original data processing purpose, according to the Court.

The Court's findings regarding the principle of limited storage (39-41):

(39) The Court further stated in its judgment – referring to points 54 and 57 of the EUB decision – that over time, even originally lawful data processing may become incompatible with the General Data Protection Regulation if the data is no longer necessary for achieving the purposes of data processing, and the data must be deleted once these purposes have been fulfilled. Although the purpose related to the creation of the test database was indeed closely related to the original data processing purpose, it was not entirely identical, as it served directly to perform the necessary tests and to correct the errors that arose.

(40) Consequently, compliance with the principle of limited storage regarding the data processing in the test database also needed to be examined in relation to this purpose, and it had to be established that the storage of data in the Test Database was lawful for the duration necessary to perform the tests and to correct the errors. Since the error was rectified in April 2018, and the Client only terminated the test database in September 2019, the data processing duration of more than one and a half years exceeded the necessary data processing period by an unreasonable length of time. The Client could not demonstrate compliance with this circumstance with the principles set out in Article 5(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation; it acknowledged that it did not delete the test database out of negligence, even though its maintenance was no longer justified by the error correction, and then forgot about the test database until becoming aware of the attack. All of this clearly indicates that the contested data processing no longer had a direct purpose for the Client (but, as stated above, this does not exclude the purpose-bound nature of data processing).

(41) Based on the above, the plaintiff Client's data processing, according to the judgment of the Metropolitan Court, is contrary to the principle of "limited storage" as set out in Article 5(1)(e) of the General Data Protection Regulation.
10

Further Findings of the Court (data security, legal consequences) (42-45):

(42) In addition to the above, the Court found in its judgment that the Authority's original decision NAIH/2020/1160/10 was in violation of the provisions set forth in Article 32(1)-(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation.

(43) The Court further established that, based on the facts, the findings in the Authority's decision do not substantiate a violation of Article 5(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation; therefore, pursuant to Section 89(1)(b) and Section 92(1)(b) of Act I of 2017 on Administrative Court Procedure (hereinafter: Kp.), the decision was annulled concerning the determination of a violation of Article 5(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation. The Court also annulled the parts of the decision that ordered the amount of the imposed fine and the publication of the decision among the legal consequences, and it obliged the Authority to conduct a new procedure regarding the legal consequences. Beyond this, based on Section 88(1)(a) of the Kp., the Court rejected the claim. Due to the differing determination of the violations from that of the Authority, the Court did not examine the claim regarding the assessment of legal consequences on its merits.

(44) In its judgment, the Court stated that in the repeated procedure, the Authority must only reconsider the legal consequences and assess how the absence of a violation of the principle of "purpose limitation" affects the imposition of the fine and the applicability of the decision's publication. If the Authority takes a position on the imposition of a fine in the repeated procedure, it must also consider that this violation does not exist in relation to the Client's examined data processing activities when determining the amount of the fine. In this regard, the amount of the fine imposed cannot reach the 100,000,000 forints imposed in the original authority procedure.

(45) In light of the above-mentioned judgment, the Authority has made the present decision in the repeated authority procedure.

Article 4(12) of the General Data Protection Regulation defines what constitutes a data protection incident; according to this, a "data protection incident" is a security breach that leads to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or unauthorized access to personal data that has been transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.

According to Article 5(1)(e) of the General Data Protection Regulation, personal data shall be stored in a form which permits identification of data subjects only for as long as is necessary for the purposes of processing the personal data; […] (“storage limitation”).

According to Article 5(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller is responsible for compliance with the principles laid down in Article 5(1) and must be able to demonstrate such compliance (“accountability”).

According to Article 6(4) of the General Data Protection Regulation:
If processing for purposes other than that for which the personal data were collected is not based on the data subject's consent or on Union or Member State law which is necessary and proportionate to achieve the objectives laid down in Article 23(1), in order to determine whether the processing for another purpose is compatible with the purpose for which the personal data were originally collected, the data controller shall take into account, among other things:
a) the purposes of collecting the personal data and any links between those purposes and the intended further processing purposes;
b) the circumstances of the collection of personal data, in particular the relationship between the data subjects and the data controller;
c) the nature of the personal data, in particular whether special categories of personal data referred to in Article 9 are processed, or whether data concerning criminal convictions and offenses are processed under Article 10;

e) the existence of appropriate safeguards, which may include encryption or pseudonymization.

**III. Decision**

a. Applicability of the General Data Protection Regulation

(46) According to Article 99 (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the regulation must be applied from May 25, 2018. The test database named "body" affected by the data protection incident was created in April 2018 based on the Client's statement for testing and troubleshooting purposes.

(47) The database named "digihu," which contains data related to the digi.hu website, included data of newsletter subscribers for direct marketing purposes and system administrator data that provides access to the website interface, which were not test data but live, up-to-date data.

(48) The Client learned that unauthorized external access to the two aforementioned databases could be exploited through a vulnerability of the digi.hu website at the earliest on September 23, 2019, from an external attacker who uncovered the security flaw. The data processing affected by the incident, namely the storage of customer data in the databases affected by the incident, continued to exist even after the applicability of the General Data Protection Regulation came into effect. d. Compliance of the management of the test database affected by the incident with certain principles

Purpose limitation (88-96):

(88) The principle of "purpose limitation" specified in Article 5(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation requires that the collection of personal data be for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes, and that they are not processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes.
20

(89) According to the opinion of the 29th Data Protection Working Group (WP203), the fundamental principle consists of two additional sub-principles, namely, first, the obligation to define the purpose and, second, the obligation related to the use thereof.

(90) In its previous decision NAIH/2020/1160/10, the Authority established that the purpose of creating the database named "test" (error correction) is distinct from the purpose of the original processing of personal data (performance of the contract). According to the previous decision, error correction, as a separate data processing purpose, can be legitimate; however, this separate data processing must also comply with the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation, including the principle of purpose limitation. The purpose of error correction in relation to the creation of the test database remains until the error itself has been rectified by the Data Controller. According to the previous decision, once the error has been corrected, the separate data processing purpose also ceases, thus, in consideration of Article 17(1)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the test database containing personal data should have been deleted. By unnecessarily retaining the database, the Client's data processing activity, according to the Authority's position expressed in the previous decision, therefore violated the principle of "purpose limitation" specified in Article 5(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation.

(91) In point I/c of the present decision, the Court also referred to judgment no. 105.K.704.076/2022/4, in which it established that the principle of purpose limitation—considering the findings established in the preliminary ruling procedure of the CJEU—was, in its opinion, not violated in relation to the examined data processing, contrary to what was stated in the Authority's previous decision.

(92) The Court indicated that the reason for this was that the Client collected and stored the personal data of its residential customers for the purpose of entering into and performing subscriber contracts, and this original data processing purpose was lawful under Article 6(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation. Associated with this legitimate data processing purpose, there may be so-called "ancillary purposes," which also result in the principle of "purpose limitation" being applicable in relation to this "ancillary purpose."

(93) The data processing for these additional "ancillary purposes" can be lawful if the further processing of personal data is compatible with the original purpose of data collection, considering Article 6(4) of the General Data Protection Regulation. The determination of these criteria in Article 6(4) of the General Data Protection Regulation is illustrative, and the existence of even a single circumstance among them may be sufficient for compliance with the principle of purpose limitation. The CJEU also stated in its Judgment (in paragraph 38) that in the present case, the national court must examine whether the storage of data in the test database deviates from the original purpose of data processing (performance of the contract), and if so, whether the further data processing is compatible with the original data collection purpose.

(94) The Court, considering the CJEU judgment and the undisputed facts presented by the parties, established that the claimant originally collected and stored the personal data for the purpose of entering into and performing subscriber contracts, and that the direct purpose of creating the test database was to perform the necessary tests related to a specific error that had arisen and to correct the error. The court further established, in accordance with what is stated in paragraph 44 of the Judgment, that although the Client created the test database for error correction purposes, it was specifically related to the original data processing purpose of the personal data. Testing and correcting errors is clearly necessary.
21

to enable the claimant to access the subscriber data and use it for the conclusion and fulfillment of subscriber contracts, thereby facilitating the realization of the original data processing purpose.

(95) Based on the Court's judgment, the Client – in accordance with Article 6(4)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation – lawfully processed the personal data of customers in the test database, in accordance with the provisions of Article 5(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation, in a manner compatible with the original data processing purpose.

(96) In accordance with the provisions of the judgment, the Authority establishes in this repeated procedure that there is no violation of the principle of purpose limitation concerning the processing of the test database involved in the incident.

Limited Storage (97-103):

(97) The principle of "limited storage" specified in Article 5(1)(e) of the General Data Protection Regulation requires that personal data be stored in such a form that identification of the data subjects is possible only for as long as is necessary for the purposes of processing the personal data.

(98) This principle, which is closely related to the principle of purpose limitation, prohibits the storage of personal data that is outdated and no longer usable for any purpose. However, the principle limits the storage period in terms of data storage that allows for the identification of data subjects. The data controller may still store anonymized data, but it must be done in such a way that it is not possible to draw conclusions about the data subject from it, nor to identify them further.

(99) In its previous decision NAIH/2020/1160/10, the Authority established that a violation of the principle of limited storage could also be identified concerning the processing of the test database involved in the incident.

(100) In the judgment referenced in point I/c of the present decision, the Court stated in judgment No. 105.K.704.076/2022/4 – referring to points 54 and 57 of the CJEU decision – that over time, even originally lawful data processing may become incompatible with the General Data Protection Regulation if the data is no longer necessary for achieving the purposes of processing, and the data must be deleted once these purposes have been fulfilled. Although the purpose related to the creation of the test database was indeed closely related to the original data processing purpose, it was not entirely identical, as it served directly to carry out the necessary tests and to correct the errors that arose.

(101) Consequently, the Client had a lawful opportunity to store data in the test database only for the time necessary to perform the tests and to correct the errors. Since the error was rectified in April 2018, and the Client deleted the test database only in September 2019, the data processing period of more than one and a half years exceeded the necessary data processing duration by an unjustifiably long time. The Client could not demonstrate compliance with this circumstance with the provisions of Article 5(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation; it also acknowledged that the test database
22

did not delete it due to lack of necessity for its retention after the error was rectified, and then forgot about the test database until becoming aware of the attack.

(102) The above – according to the Court's judgment – does not, in itself, exclude the purpose limitation of data processing; however, the contested data processing (error rectification) no longer had a legitimate purpose that could be directly reconciled with the original data collection (contract performance) at the Client. Due to the cessation of the close relationship with the originally reconcilable and therefore legitimate "additional" purpose, the Client no longer had a lawful basis for further storage of the test database in a form that allowed for the identification of the data subjects.

(103) Based on the above, the Authority establishes in this repeated procedure that the Client's further storage of the test database, which is no longer reconcilable with the original legitimate data processing purpose, is unreasonably long and contrary to the principle of "storage limitation" as laid down in Article 5(1)(e) of the General Data Protection Regulation.

- The storage of the database affected by the incident did not violate the principle of purpose limitation (General Data Protection Regulation Article 5(1)(b)). See: the reasoning in paragraphs (88)-(96) of this decision.

IV. 
Other Issues

The Authority's jurisdiction is defined by Section 38 (2) and (2a) of the Infotv., and its competence extends to the entire territory of the country.

Pursuant to Section 112 and Section 116 (1), as well as Section 114 (1) of the Ákr., there is a right to legal remedy against the decision through administrative litigation.

The rules of administrative litigation are determined by the Kp. According to Section 12 (2) a) of the Kp., administrative litigation against the Authority's decision falls under the jurisdiction of the court, and based on Section 13 (11) of the Kp., the Capital Court is exclusively competent for the litigation. According to Section 72 of Act CXXX of 2016 on Civil Procedure (hereinafter: Pp.) – applicable based on Section 26 (1) of the Kp. – legal representation is mandatory in cases falling under the jurisdiction of the court. According to Section 39 (6) of the Kp., unless otherwise provided by law, the submission of the statement of claim does not have a suspensive effect on the validity of the administrative act.

According to Section 29 (1) of the Kp. and in consideration of this, Section 604 of the Pp. applies, and according to Section 9 (1) b) of Act CCXXII of 2015 on the General Rules of Electronic Administration and Trust Services (hereinafter: E-Administration Act), the client's legal representative is obliged to maintain electronic communication.

The time and place for submitting the statement of claim are determined by Section 39 (1) of the Kp. Information regarding the possibility of requesting a hearing is based on Sections 77 (1)-(2) of the Kp. The amount of the administrative litigation fee is determined by Section 44/A (1) of Act XCIII of 1990 on Fees (hereinafter: Itv.). The party initiating the proceedings is exempt from the prior payment of the fee under Section 59 (1) and Section 62 (1) h) of the Itv.

According to Section 132 of the Ákr., if the obligated party does not comply with the obligation set forth in the Authority's final decision, it is enforceable. The Authority's decision becomes final upon notification, according to Section 82 (1) of the Ákr. According to Section 133 of the Ákr., enforcement is ordered by the authority that made the decision unless otherwise provided by law or government decree. According to Section 134 of the Ákr., enforcement is carried out by the state tax authority unless otherwise provided by law, government decree, or a local government regulation in a municipal authority matter. According to Section 60 (7) of the Infotv., the Authority carries out the enforcement of the decision regarding the obligation to perform a specific act, to behave in a certain way, to tolerate, or to cease a specific action.
