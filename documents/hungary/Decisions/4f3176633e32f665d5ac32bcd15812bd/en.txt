(42) According to Article 4(1) of the GDPR, "personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier,

for example, name, number, location data, online identifier, or one or more factors related to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of the natural person;

(43) According to Article 4(2) of the GDPR, "processing": any operation or set of operations performed on personal data or sets of personal data, whether automated or not, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction;

(43) According to Article 4(7) of the GDPR, "controller": the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or any other body which determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data, alone or jointly with others; where the purposes and means of such processing are determined by Union or Member State law, the controller or the specific criteria for the designation of the controller may also be provided for by Union or Member State law;

(44) According to Article 4(8) of the GDPR, "processor": the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or any other body which processes personal data on behalf of the controller;

(44) According to Articles 12(1) to (6) of the GDPR:

(1) The controller shall take appropriate measures to provide the data subject with all information regarding the processing of personal data referred to in Articles 13 and 14, and all information referred to in Articles 15 to 22 and 34, in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language, particularly when addressing children. IV. Decision:

IV.1. The personal data of the Applicant stored by the Respondent, the data processing carried out on them, the data controller and data processor status concerning the data processing recorded in the viewing certificate

IV.1.1. Personal data and its source

(55) According to Article 4(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the Applicant's name, mother's name, nationality, address, identification document number, phone number, email address, place and date of birth qualify as personal data of the Applicant, the storage and use of which constitutes data processing under Article 4(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation.

(56) The source of the aforementioned personal data is the Applicant, as this data was provided to the Respondent through the so-called viewing declaration. The use of the viewing declaration as a form was made mandatory for the Respondent by the Client, as the franchise owner.

IV.1.2. Data processing operations carried out on the Applicant's personal data – examined in the current procedure

(57) The recording, storage, and transmission of the data indicated in the viewing certificate are also considered data processing under Article 4(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation. In the current data protection authority procedure, the Authority specifically examined the data processing related to the recording of these personal data, and separately the data processing concerning the transmission of these personal data by the Respondent for claims management purposes. The Authority made findings regarding the latter data processing operation in point IV.2.

IV.1.3. Data controller and data processor established concerning the data processing related to the recording of the Applicant's personal data as per point IV.1.1.

(58) The concept of data controller is defined in Article 4(7) of the GDPR, and the concept of data processor is defined in Article 4(8) of the GDPR. According to Article 4(7) of the GDPR, a data controller is a natural or legal person […], which determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data, either alone or jointly with others […]. According to the same Article 8, a data processor is a natural or legal person […], which processes personal data on behalf of the data controller.

(59) The Data Processing Information compiled by the Client regarding the Respondent's data processing also records – in accordance with the statements made by the Respondent and the Client – that the Client determines the forms, contracts, sales processes, and procedures to be followed in case of breach of contract applied during the data processing. The Data Processing Information explicitly states, as elaborated in paragraph (30) of this decision in point 1.3, that the purpose, means, and rules of the data processing are determined by the Client, while it also records that the purpose and means of the data processing are not jointly determined by the data controllers (i.e., the Respondent and the Client) and do not qualify as joint data controllers. The Data Processing Information refers to the fact that the individual data controllers (in this case, the Respondent and the Client) thus carry out their data processing independently, while most data processing activities are carried out based on the joint activities of the data controllers.

(60) In the Authority's opinion, the above information is quite contradictory, and it is also contradictory that the Client determines all forms, meaning that the Applicant has no say in this matter, yet is still an independent data controller in every respect.

(61) The European Data Protection Board's Guidance No. 07/2020 (hereinafter: Guidance), which discusses the concepts of data controller and data processor under the GDPR, further details the circumstances under which we can speak of joint data processing. On page 3 of the Guidance, regarding the essence of joint data processing, it states: “[...] An important aspect is that data processing is not possible without the participation of two parties in the sense that the data processing carried out by each party is inseparable, meaning that they are inextricably linked. Joint participation must include, on the one hand, the determination of the purposes and, on the other hand, the means. [...]”

(62) The Applicant cannot be considered an independent data controller; however, neither can the Applicant and the Client be considered joint data controllers in relation to the data processing associated with the viewing certificate, since the Client determined the content of the data, and the data processing occurs in accordance with what is stated in the Data Processing Information prepared by the Client. This follows from the operational principles of the franchise system, as within this framework, every aspect of the Applicant's activity – and the related data processing – is regulated in detail. The documents used within the franchise system, including the viewing certificate, contain references to the franchise system, indicating that the partners carry out their activities according to the specifications determined by the Client. The viewing certificate form includes the name and logo of the Client, while the name of the franchise partners appears only to the extent that the client states that they became aware of the property through XY Real Estate Agency. The viewing certificate is not a document required by law; its design and content are solely at the discretion of the data controller, in this case, the Client.

(63) According to point 11.6 of the franchise agreement submitted as an annex to document (NAIH-3772-7/2022), the Client can delete data from its system, to which the Applicant is granted access, and into which the Applicant can enter data.

(64) On page 4 of the Guidance, it is also stated: “[...] The qualification as a data processor has two fundamental conditions: a separate organization from the data controller, and processing personal data on behalf of the data controller. The data processor cannot process the data in a manner contrary to the instructions of the data controller. The instructions of the data controller still provide a certain degree of discretion regarding how to best represent the interests of the data controller, allowing the data processor to select the most appropriate technical and organizational measures. [...]” The Applicant actually processes the data on behalf of the Client, as it cannot make any data controller decisions regarding them, except for the decision to collect the service fee. The Applicant made this decision and forwarded the personal data of the Applicant to its lawyer for the purpose of enforcing the claim. Therefore, the Applicant enforces the fee in its own name; this is the Applicant's claim. In this case, the criterion of a separate organization is also met, as the Client and the Applicant are two separate legal entities. In this regard, the Applicant is an independent data controller in this case. However, this is not always the case according to point 4.4 of the Data Processing Information, based on what is stated in paragraph (33) of this decision.

IV.2.1. Legitimate interest as a legal basis

(66) The Respondent is a legal entity separate from the Client. Its independent legal personality implies that the Respondent may have claims against other individuals, thus the Respondent can be considered a data controller in this capacity, as it can decide whether to take steps to enforce the given debt. This is also indicated by the viewing certificate, which states that the real estate agency is authorized to instruct the Client to pursue the claim. The economic decisions of independent legal entities, including claim enforcement, are independent. Therefore, while the franchise system may stipulate how the Respondent should conduct its real estate brokerage activities using the name of the franchise system, its financial management is independent of this. Thus, as long as the personal data recorded on the viewing certificate is not used for claim enforcement purposes, the Respondent qualifies as a data processor, but once the data processing for claim enforcement begins, the Respondent becomes a data controller from that point onward.

(67) Based on the above, as long as the Respondent maintained a claim against the Applicant for which the Applicant did not submit a court decision establishing that it does not exist, the Respondent had a legitimate interest in processing data, particularly considering that legal proceedings were ongoing. The legitimate interest in relation to the Applicant's personal data – personal identification data, as well as data related to the claim – and the processing of the necessary data for enforcing the claim, as per Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR, existed against the Applicant until a final judgment established that the claim does not exist. Therefore, the Respondent lawfully processed (stored and transmitted to the court) the Applicant's personal data for the purpose of enforcing its claim.

(68) According to the Respondent's statement, it will delete the Applicant's personal data after the judgment rejecting its claim becomes final, provided that it is not necessary for the current administrative procedure. After the judgment becomes final, the Applicant's personal data can only be processed by the Respondent and the Client based on their legitimate interest in relation to the current administrative procedure, and not for claim enforcement purposes.

(69) At the Authority's request, the Respondent submitted the court's final judgment and the ruling on the finality concerning the lawsuit initiated with reference to the Respondent's existing claim. Accordingly, the court rejected the Respondent's claim, and therefore, after the judgment became final (since February 16, 2023), the Respondent can no longer process the Applicant's personal data based on its legitimate interest for debt collection purposes.

(70) In light of the above, the Authority granted the Applicant's request and ordered the Respondent to delete the Applicant's personal data from its records for claim purposes.

(71) According to the preamble (47) of the GDPR, in order to establish the existence of a legitimate interest, it is essential to carefully examine, among other things, whether the interests and fundamental rights of the data subject may take precedence over the interests of the data controller when personal data is processed under circumstances where the data subjects do not...

(72) According to the Authority's findings, the statement regarding the priority of the Applicant's legitimate interest does not specifically relate to the personal data of the Applicant; rather, it makes very brief general statements regarding the legitimate interest. Furthermore, it does not address the circumstances mentioned in the GDPR (47) preamble. The Applicant did not identify the interests of the data subjects and did not compare them with the interests of the data controller, nor was there an examination or substantiation of the necessity and proportionality of the data processing. As part of the submitted Data Processing Information, the "Brief Presentation of the Balancing Test" emphasizes that if the data subject does not voluntarily meet the requirements of the data controllers, there is no other way to achieve the objective, and that personal data necessary for the enforcement of the requirements is processed.

(73) According to the Authority's findings, the statement regarding the priority of the Applicant's legitimate interest does not specifically relate to the personal data of the Applicant; rather, it makes very brief general statements regarding the legitimate interest. Furthermore, it does not address the circumstances mentioned in the GDPR (47) preamble. The Applicant did not identify the interests of the data subjects and did not compare them with the interests of the data controller, nor was there an examination or substantiation of the necessity and proportionality of the data processing. Given that the Applicant only made general statements regarding their legitimate interest, they completely failed to conduct the balancing of interests concerning the Applicant, and their general statements cannot be considered sufficient, as they do not meet the requirements of the GDPR (47) preamble.

IV.3. Preliminary Information

(78) The Respondent stated that the client reads the viewing certificate in every case before signing, interprets it, requests an explanation if necessary regarding its contents, and then signs it, which the Applicant also did. The certificate includes the data processing information, which names the data controllers, the purpose of data processing, and contains other elements defined in the GDPR. The information also states which website the detailed data processing information is available on. The Applicant was able to familiarize themselves with the viewing certificate, read it, signed it, and received a copy of it.

(79) There was a legal proceeding ongoing between the Applicant and the Respondent. Regarding the data processing related to the legal proceeding, the Authority established in point IV.2.1. that the data processing based on legitimate interest was lawful concerning the Applicant's specified personal data until the final closure of the legal proceeding.

(80) If personal data concerning the data subject is collected from the data subject, the data controller must provide detailed information to the data subjects at the time of obtaining the personal data for the purpose of fair and transparent data processing, as stated in Article 13(1) of the GDPR.

(81) The GDPR preamble (39) stipulates that the information regarding data processing must be transparent and made available to the data subjects in an appropriate manner (e.g., on the data controller's website), taking into account any additional conditions of the chosen legal bases.

(82) Article 12 of the GDPR defines the formal requirements that data controllers must consider when enabling the exercise of data subject rights, including the prior information of data subjects. Accordingly, data controllers must provide all information regarding the processing of personal data in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible form, clearly and in plain language.

(83) According to the Applicant's position, it does not comply with the GDPR "Article 7(2)", which states that if the data subject gives consent in the context of a written declaration that relates to other matters, the request for consent must be presented in a manner clearly distinguishable from those other matters, in an intelligible and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language. The data processing information was presented in particularly small font size, with narrow line spacing.
16

(84) The consent given on the viewing certificate pertains to two separate purposes, as the information indicates that consent is granted regarding the personal data listed therein, which may be processed for the purposes of viewing and asserting claims, should that occur. This was also the case for the Applicant.

(85) The Authority agrees that the font size on the viewing certificate is quite small; however, the link to the website is also included, and the font size of the data processing information available at the provided link is standard. Furthermore, the Authority believes that the text on the viewing certificate is unstructured and not segmented, thus failing to meet the requirement for transparent information. According to point 71 of the European Data Protection Board's Guidelines 5/2020 on consent under Regulation (EU) 2016/679, “[...] In order to adapt to small screens or situations where there is little space for information, it may be considered – in order to avoid excessive disruption of the user experience or product design – to present information in a multi-layered manner.” Therefore, in the Authority's view, it could be resolved either through multi-layered information or simply by expanding the one-page viewing certificate to a two-sided format to ensure it is adequately readable.

(86) For the above reasons, the Authority finds that the Respondent did not provide adequate information regarding its independent data processing for the purpose of asserting claims, and therefore violated Article 12(1) of the GDPR.
