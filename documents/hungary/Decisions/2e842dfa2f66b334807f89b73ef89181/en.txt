JUSTIFICATION

I. Facts

(1) On January 10, 2019, a notification was received by the Authority, in which the notifier objected to the data processing conducted by the Company using cameras installed at the above location. Considering that the facts could not be revealed in the investigation initiated based on this, the Authority initiated an official procedure ex officio on October 11, 2021 (NAIH/7643-1/2022). The decision with case number NAIH/7643-1/2022 was received by the Company on October 18, 2021. The 15-day deadline set for responding expired on November 2, 2021, but the Company only mailed its response on November 4, 2021, and the correspondence was received by the Authority on November 8, 2021.

(2) According to the Company's statement with case number NAIH/7643-2/2021, the Company conducted camera surveillance at the location based on the legitimate interest legal basis under Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR for the protection of life, physical integrity, personal freedom, and for property protection purposes. The Company attached snapshots showing the field of view of the 4 cameras to its response. Based on these, one of the cameras was directed at one of the Company's workstations, one monitored the external entrance and, in connection with that, partially the public area, and two others were directed at the internal areas opened for customer traffic in the solarium. Based on the camera images submitted by the Company, there was no masking set up to cover irrelevant areas for the purpose of surveillance or to filter the observed part with IT tools (hereinafter: masking). The fields of view of the camera system were also presented by the Company on a site plan. 

From the camera images and site plan submitted by the Company, it could be established that some tanning machines and the doors leading to the standing tanning booths were partially included in the fields of view. According to the Company, paper-based data processing information could be requested by solarium customers from the person working at the reception desk; the employees were informed verbally and in job descriptions, according to the Company, but it did not substantiate these claims with evidence. However, based on the photographs submitted by the Company, warning stickers regarding the fact of camera data processing were placed outside the entrance door and also within the premises of the location.
3

(3) In light of the Company's response, further clarification of the facts became necessary; therefore, the Authority, by its order with case number NAIH/2866-1/2022, once again contacted the Company by mail with its questions. The Authority set a deadline of 15 days from the receipt of the order for the Company to respond. According to the delivery confirmation from Magyar Posta Zrt., the Company received the Authority's order with case number NAIH/2866-1/2022 on February 21, 2022, thus the deadline for response fell on March 8, 2022. The Company's response to the Authority was received by mail on March 28, 2022.

(4) In its response with case number NAIH/2866-2/2022, the Company informed the Authority that it no longer operates a tanning salon at the specified address, nor does it conduct any other activities there. Additionally, it stated in its response that the Company does not have a website. The Company's response did not contain any further information. The Company did not attach any annex to its response. The Company did not provide answers to the questions posed in the referenced order, and despite the Authority's request, the Company did not present the balancing test, nor did it declare that it possessed such a document. The Company's response did not contain any statement or evidence that it had ceased the camera data processing or that the camera system had been dismantled.

(5) On the day of receiving the response, the Authority requested the Company's company extract, which still listed (…) as the Company's premises, thus at the time of the response, it was the location associated with the Company's business activities as stated in the founding document.

(6) In light of the above, the Authority, in its order with case number NAIH/2866-3/2022, imposed a procedural fine and again called upon the Company to clarify the facts. The Company received the order on April 26, 2022, according to the relevant delivery confirmation, but only responded on June 2, 2022.

(7) In its response with case number NAIH/2866-4/2022, the Company stated that it does not operate any business at the referenced premises, nor does it use it for any other activities. According to its statement, the same situation applies to the multiple premises listed in its company extract, as it only actually uses one of the premises indicated. It claimed that the cameras previously operated at the premises have already been dismantled and removed from the property. It stated that due to personal tragedy, health issues, and financial difficulties, it is unable to fulfill its administrative burdens, including the necessary company procedures, on a permanent basis. In light of this, it requested a reduction or waiver of the procedural fine. The relevant statement did not contain any attached evidence or annex, and despite the Authority's request, the Company did not present the balancing test, nor did it declare that it possessed such a document.

(10) In the absence of any differing provisions in the General Data Protection Regulation, the data protection authority procedure initiated upon request shall be governed by the provisions of Act CL of 2016 on General Administrative Procedure.

The provisions of Act CL. (hereinafter: Ákr.) shall be applied with the deviations defined in the Infotv.

(11) In official procedures, the provisions of the Ákr. concerning procedures initiated upon request shall be applied with the deviations set forth in Sections 103-104 of this law.

(12) According to Article 4(1) of the GDPR, "personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.

(13) According to Article 4(2) of the GDPR, "processing" means any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.

(14) According to Article 6(1) of the GDPR:
The processing of personal data shall be lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the following applies:
a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes;
b) the processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;
c) the processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject;
d) the processing is necessary in order to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person;
e) the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller;
f) the processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular if the data subject is a child.
Point (f) of the first subparagraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.

III. Decision  

(17) III.1. In the case of camera surveillance data processing, the data controller's legitimate interest is typically the legal basis applied. The conceptual element of legitimate interest is the data controller's obligation to conduct a balancing test. The data controller is required to carry out a prior, written interest balancing test in order to rely on this legal basis. The legitimate interest legal basis can be applied by the data controller only if the outcome of the balancing test supports its application (thus, the mere existence of the balancing test is not sufficient). The balancing test is based on the identification and weighing of different interests. This includes examining the necessity-proportionality question and the reasonable expectations of the data subjects. In weighing these, it must be considered that the interests of the data subjects may take precedence over the interests of the data controller, and it must also be assessed whether the data subjects could reasonably expect that the data controller would process their data for the specified purpose at the beginning of the processing.

f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular if the data subject is a child. The first subparagraph (f) does not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.

(18) Despite multiple requests from the Authority, the Company did not present such a legitimate interest balancing test, thus it cannot lawfully rely on the legal basis of legitimate interest.

(19) Moreover, even if the data processing had been conducted in the established manner alongside the submission of a legitimate interest balancing test, it would not have been supported by a legal basis of legitimate interest. Based on the photographs and floor plan submitted by the Company, the camera observing the entrance door also partially monitored public space. The employee and their workstation were under continuous surveillance, with the camera's field of view partially extending to a lying tanning bed. Several of the internal cameras captured, either partially or entirely, one of the tanning machines or the door leading to the standing tanning beds. The affected individuals using the service could not effectively avoid these cameras, and masking was not set up for them.

(20) Regarding the questioned cameras, neither the suitability for achieving the data processing purpose—presumably asset protection—nor the proportionality was justified. Particularly from an asset protection perspective, other available solutions (such as locks, padlocks, window bars, safes, alarms, motion detectors, security guards, etc.) could provide greater security in certain cases than camera-based data processing. In terms of the purpose of data processing, in the absence of a legitimate interest balancing test, it is neither substantiated nor likely that the camera system was the most effective solution among the available options and also the one that least burdens the privacy of the affected individuals.

(21) According to the Company's statement, the data processing information could only be made available to the affected individuals using the service on-site, in paper form, upon explicit request, thus adequate information regarding the data processing was not accessible to them. The Company also did not substantiate the provision of such information to employees; according to its statement, this was done through verbal information or information included in job descriptions, but it did not support its claims with evidence.

(22) Based on the above, the Authority established a violation of Article 6(1) and Article 13(1)-(2) of the GDPR and decided according to the provisions set out in the operative part (decision) based on Article 58(2)(b) of the GDPR.

(23) According to the Company's statement, it has ceased the examined data processing and dismantled the devices at its premises.

(24) III.2. Clarifying the circumstances of data processing was not possible in the investigation procedure; therefore, the Authority decided to initiate an official authority procedure ex officio. Nevertheless, in the ex officio initiated authority procedure, the Company's statements were incomplete and delayed. Considering these circumstances, a procedural fine was imposed on the Company, and thus the Authority decided, as stated in the operative part (order), to reject the Company's request for the waiver or reduction of the procedural fine.
