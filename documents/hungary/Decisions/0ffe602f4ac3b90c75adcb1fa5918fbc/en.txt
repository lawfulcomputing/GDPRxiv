In the above data protection authority procedure, the Authority, by its decision NAIH-4319-13/2022 dated September 12, 2022, included the Everyone's Hungary Movement (headquarters: 1137 Budapest, Szent István körút 16. 4th floor/21.; legal representative: ………………….. attorney; hereinafter: Obligor) as a party. The Authority makes the following decisions in the above data protection authority procedure:  

I. The Authority establishes at the request of the Applicant that the Respondent and the Obligor violated Article 12(1) and Article 14(1) and (2) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter: General Data Protection Regulation) concerning the processing of personal data related to Facebook Messenger messages sent to the Applicant on March 10, 2022, as well as Article 6(1) and Article 21(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation in connection with the sending of a second Facebook Messenger message requesting subscription, disregarding the right to object, without a valid legal basis following the initial subscription request message.  

II. The Authority ex officio establishes that the Respondent and the Obligor violated during the period between February 2022 and April 2022 in connection with the data processing practice related to Facebook Messenger  

(i) Article 12(1), Article 14(1) and (2), and Article 26(1) and (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation by failing to provide adequate information on data processing regarding the first subscription request messages sent to commenting, not yet subscribed data subjects on the Facebook page, particularly regarding the written identification of the roles of multiple data controllers involved in the data processing and the communication of this to the data subjects,  

(ii) Article 6(1) and Article 21(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation by setting up data processing settings to send repeated subscription request messages to commenting, not yet subscribed, and previously rejected subscription data subjects, as well as to unsubscribed data subjects,  

(iii) Article 12(1) and Article 13(1) and (2), and Article 26(1) and (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation by failing to provide adequate and prior information about the data processing to the data subjects who consented to the subscription.

V. Due to the above-mentioned data protection violations, the Authority warns the Respondent ex officio under Section 75/A of Act CXII of 2011 on the Right to Informational Self-Determination and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Infotv.).

REASONING

1. The procedure and clarification of the facts

1. The procedure

(1) The Applicant submitted a request on March 31, 2022, via ePaper, stating that on March 10, 2022, they commented on the Respondent's Facebook page, as a result of which they received an automated message via Facebook Messenger in the name of the Respondent without any data processing information. According to the attached screenshot, the content of the message was as follows: "Péter Márki-Zay: Dear Fellow Citizen! Thank you for commenting on my page. Would you like to stay in touch and learn about the events of my campaign firsthand?"

(2) The Applicant replied "No" to the above message. In response, they received the following automated reply message in the name of the Respondent: "Alright. Then I will not contact you via Messenger."

(3) The Applicant subsequently commented again on the Facebook page, to which they again received an automated question via Messenger regarding the subscription request after the first comment.

(4) The Applicant requested the Authority to establish the unlawful processing of their Facebook identifier in the context of a data protection authority procedure and requested a temporary measure to prohibit the data controller of the Facebook page from committing further violations through the Facebook page during the procedure.

(5) In response to the Authority's request for supplementation, the Applicant, in a letter sent on April 6, 2022, indicated their own Facebook profile identifier, stated that they use it solely for private purposes, and attached the screenshot of the contested message exchange, which also shows the message exchange in the browser.

(6) On April 5, 2022, the Authority did not find any other data controller identified on the Facebook page besides the Respondent, and the only official contact listed was the postal address "6800 Hódmezővásárhely, Kossuth tér 1."

(7) In response to the Authority's inquiry sent on May 18, 2022, and a repeated inquiry sent on July 11, 2022, the Respondent's legal representative, following a request for an extension of the deadline approved by the Authority, stated on August 26, 2022, that regarding the Facebook page, Meta Platforms Ireland Limited (hereinafter: Meta Ireland), which operates the Facebook platform, and the Obligor are joint data controllers, and the Respondent, as a private individual, does not process data and has no information regarding the data processing of the Facebook page. 2. Facts established by the Authority  

(16) On March 10, 2022, the Applicant commented on a Facebook page operating under the name of the Respondent. As a result, they received a message via Facebook Messenger in which the Respondent requested them in the first person singular to subscribe for further news on Facebook Messenger. The Applicant responded negatively, to which they received an automated confirmation message stating that they would not be contacted again. (17) The name on the Facebook page is the natural person name of the Respondent, and they write posts on the Facebook page to promote their election as a candidate for a political position. The contact and identification data subpage of the Facebook page lists the address "Kossuth tér 1, Hódmezővásárhely, Hungary, 6800," and there are random character links pointing to the website https://markizaypeter.hu, as well as to the "markizaypetermzp" Twitch video-sharing website, and a Spotify page. The details subsection specifically provides options for contacting the Respondent: a Reddit forum link to the username "markizaypetermzp," an Apple Podcast link to the Respondent's name encoded in HTML (with HTML codes in place of accented characters), and a link to the https://mindenkimagyarorszaga.hu/kapcsolat website. Regarding the specific relationship between the Facebook page and the Respondent, there is a reference link without the above description.

(21) According to the settings, the "silence period" also applies to those who have unsubscribed once, meaning that they will receive the subscription request message again upon the first comment after indicating their intention to unsubscribe.  

II.  
Applicable Legal Provisions  

(22) According to the preamble (26) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the principles of data protection must be applied to any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. Pseudonymized personal data, which can be linked to a natural person using additional information, must be considered data relating to an identifiable natural person. In determining the identifiability of a natural person, all methods must be taken into account—including, for example, designation—that can reasonably be assumed to be used by the data controller or another person for the direct or indirect identification of the natural person. In determining which means can reasonably be assumed to be used for the identification of a specific natural person, all objective factors must be considered, including the costs and time required for identification, taking into account the technologies available at the time of data processing and the development of technology. Accordingly, the principles of data protection do not need to be applied to anonymous information, namely information that does not relate to an identified or identifiable natural person, as well as to personal data that has been anonymized in such a way that the data subject is no longer identifiable or cannot be identified. Therefore, the General Data Protection Regulation does not apply to the processing of such anonymous information, including data processing for statistical or research purposes.  
(23) According to the preamble (74) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the powers and responsibilities of the data controller must be regulated concerning any type of processing of personal data carried out by the data controller or on behalf of the data controller. The data controller must be obliged, in particular, to take appropriate and effective measures.
9  
to carry out, as well as to be able to demonstrate that the data processing activities comply with the General Data Protection Regulation and that the effectiveness of the measures taken is at the level required by the General Data Protection Regulation. These measures must be taken considering the nature, scope, circumstances, and purposes of the data processing, as well as the risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.  
(24) According to Article 2(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the General Data Protection Regulation applies to the processing of personal data that is carried out by automated means in whole or in part, as well as to the non-automated processing of data that forms part of a filing system or is intended to form part of a filing system.  
(25) According to Article 4(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, "personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, particularly by reference to an identifier such as a name, number, location data, online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.  
(26) According to Article 4(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, "processing" means any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.  
(27) According to Article 4(5) of the General Data Protection Regulation, "pseudonymization" means the processing of personal data in such a manner that the personal data can no longer be attributed to a specific data subject without the use of additional information, provided that such additional information is kept separately and is subject to technical and organizational measures to ensure that the personal data is not attributed to an identified or identifiable natural person.  
(28) According to Article 4(7) of the General Data Protection Regulation, "data controller" means the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or any other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data. Where the purposes and means of such processing are determined by Union or Member State law, the data controller or the specific criteria for the designation of the data controller may also be provided for by Union or Member State law.  
(29) According to Article 4(11) of the General Data Protection Regulation, "the data subject's consent" means any freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes, by which the data subject, by a statement or by a clear affirmative action, signifies agreement to the processing of personal data relating to them.

III.  
Decision  

1. Subject of the Procedure  

(54) Based on the specific request, the Authority examined whether the handling of the second Facebook Messenger subscription request message sent to the Applicant after the first negative response was carried out with an appropriate legal basis and information. The Applicant stated, based on Section 76 of the Ákr., that in their opinion, the first message was already inadequate at the conclusion of the fact-finding process; however, they did not submit a new specific request regarding this, so the Authority examined it ex officio rather than as part of the request.  
(55) In uncovering the circumstances of the data processing, the Authority also clarified what the general data processing practice related to the message sent to the Applicant was and whether this (uniformly set, thus in some cases exceptionally not modifiable) practice was applied in the case of the Applicant as well. There is no possibility of submitting a request by the data subject within the general practice; the Authority made findings in this regard based on the ex officio observation that occurred during the applicant procedure.
16  
(56) The data processing examined took place at the Applicant with similar settings between February 2022 and April 2022, within the framework of a political campaign.  
(57) The contact and Facebook technical identifier (ID) personal data are not considered sensitive, and the subject of the procedure is not their general processing, but rather a narrower data processing related to automated Facebook Messenger messages sent in response to comments, which did not directly target special categories of personal data. However, the Facebook page operates specifically for purposes closely related to the handling of political opinions, and the Facebook Messenger subscription is closely associated with this, thus the data processing related to it also indirectly involves the handling of political opinions. The data protection risk associated with data processing related to political opinions is generally greater than that of data processing without this element.  

2. The identity of the data controllers  

(58) The Authority established during the clarification of the facts that the Facebook page and the associated Facebook Messenger account operate in the name of the Respondent. Although the Respondent stated on August 26, 2022, following multiple requests from the Authority, that as a private individual, they are not the data controller of the Facebook page, it is clear from the explicit political nature of the Facebook page and the Respondent's actions as a candidate for political office that the Respondent acted not as a private individual but as a political public figure, and that the Facebook page operated with their knowledge and cooperation, in their interest; no contrary circumstances arose. On the Facebook page and Facebook Messenger, the Respondent clearly acted in their own name—not as the Obligor or another representative—under their own photograph and name, and the formulation of the messages was also in the first person. Therefore, their involvement in the data processing—despite its lesser degree—is indisputable; without them, the data processing in question would not exist in this form.  
(59) According to Article 4(7) of the General Data Protection Regulation, it is not necessarily the case that the data controller is the one who possesses the personal data, but rather the one who determines the essential conditions, purposes, and means of the data processing. The Facebook Messenger data processing complained of by the Applicant and examined by the Authority, as well as the data processing of the Facebook page inextricably linked to it and the information found there, are clearly determined by the Obligor for the purpose and interest of the Respondent, based on the established facts, in agreement with the Respondent. For the same reason, the circumstance interpreted oppositely by the Obligor and the Applicant, regarding whether the Obligor processed the Applicant's personal data in connection with sending Facebook Messenger messages, is not relevant, since possession of the specific data is not equivalent to substantive decision-making authority regarding data processing; the Obligor can still have the status of a data controller without possessing the specific data. In many cases, data processing occurs solely through data processors, such that the data controller does not physically handle the personal data; this alone does not exclude the status and responsibility of the data controller as a general rule.  
(60) The Respondent and the Obligor referred to the fact that according to the data processing information of the Facebook service (as well as existing judicial practice), Meta Ireland may, in certain cases, be a joint data controller with the person operating the Facebook page and the associated Facebook Messenger account. The Authority emphasizes in this regard that, according to the information cited in point (8)(iii) above, in "most cases," thus not always, Meta Ireland is not a data controller in connection with these data processing activities. Furthermore, the European Union
17  
The Court's decision C-210/16 highlights in paragraph 43 that "Moreover, it should be clarified that – as noted in paragraphs 75 and 76 of the Advocate General's opinion – the existence of joint responsibility does not necessarily imply the same level of responsibility for the various actors involved in personal data processing. On the contrary, since these actors may participate in different stages of data processing and to varying degrees, the extent of their responsibility must be assessed taking into account all relevant circumstances specific to the present case."  
(61) The Authority did not examine the data processing activities of the Facebook Messenger service in general or the Facebook page, but specifically the particular data processing activity that is the automatic sending of Facebook Messenger messages in the case of commenting on the Facebook page. This is not enabled by default and can only be turned on or off at the discretion of the Controller; Meta Ireland only provides the technical framework for this, and processes the personal data of the commenter based on the instructions indicated by the Controller in the settings, under the conditions specified by the Controller (e.g., the existence and duration of a "silence period"). The activities of Meta Ireland related to the examined automatic consent-requesting message are similar to those of traditional telecommunications service providers, which typically do not qualify as data controllers in this context. Data processing activities carried out solely on the instructions of a third party qualify as data processing under Article 4(8) and Article 28(3)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation, not as data controller activities. In light of the above points discussed in III.2, the Authority, considering all the circumstances of the case and the violations revealed below, concluded that with respect to the narrower data processing activity affected by the specific data protection violations, Meta Ireland did not make a substantive decision, and thus there are no specific circumstances that would justify initiating cross-border proceedings before the competent Irish data protection authority; the Authority has jurisdiction over the Applicant and the Controller, and thus acted accordingly. The assessment of the data processing behavior and decisions of the Applicant and the Controller is possible independently of Meta Ireland.  
(62) In light of the above, the Authority determined in the operative part that regarding the data processing activities examined in this case, the data controller decisions leading to the violation should be regarded as jointly made by the Applicant and the Controller under Article 4(7) of the General Data Protection Regulation. The decisions related to the examined data processing were made in the interest and intent of the Applicant to increase the voter base of the Applicant as a political public figure, in which the Controller, as a supporting political organization, had an interest and practically participated to a greater extent in shaping the decision. The Authority took this into account when assessing the legal consequences. The fact that Meta Ireland, as a data processor, created and operates the automatic messaging function activated for commenting on Facebook Messenger does not exclude the possibility that the Applicant sees the subscription-requesting message in their own account, but the Applicant and the Controller do not, thus the Authority did not conduct counter-evidence regarding the Controller's statement in response to the call under Section 76 of the Act, that the Applicant's Facebook identifiers were not processed in relation to the automatic messaging and that they do not see the specific automatic messages with a negative response, as this did not affect the data controller status as explained in this III.2 point. Since, as explained in the above points (11), (i), (iv), (vi), (14)(i), (20), and (21), the sending of the second message was caused not by a technical error, contrary to previous statements made by the Controller, but by the administrator settings of the Facebook page, there is no need for a technical error.
18  
Regarding the substantive evidence proceedings, as well as the extent of the data controller's liability of the Respondent and the Obligor concerning the second message, it is otherwise identical to the first message. Based on the disclosed documents and statements (see point (8)(xi)), Datadat GmbH participated only as a data processor in the data processing, and did not make any data controller decisions.  
(63) Joint data processing does not necessarily require the Respondent and the Obligor to make a specific decision at the same time and place; it is sufficient if their collaboration, conducted with regard to each other and in an inseparable manner, can be established in terms of the objectives and means of the decisions resulting in data processing, as is the case here. The specific data processing could not have occurred without either the Respondent or the Obligor; it could not have been realized with only one of them. This does not affect the responsibility of the joint data controllers to establish a written agreement in accordance with Article 26(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, however, the lack of a formal agreement – as well as the lack of communication of the essential conditions of their cooperation to the data subjects under Article 26(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation – does not in itself result in exemption from data controller liability. Since the Respondent and the Obligor did not fulfill the above obligations regarding the written recording of the conditions of joint data processing, the Authority established the infringement of the above articles according to the operative part.  

3. The Highlighted Importance of Information Related to Data Processing  

(64) According to Article 12(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller is obliged to take appropriate measures to ensure that all information regarding the processing of personal data mentioned in Articles 13 and 14, as well as every piece of information under Articles 15–22 and 34, is provided to the data subjects in a concise, transparent, intelligible, and easily accessible form, clearly and in plain language.  
(65) The system of appropriate information in the General Data Protection Regulation serves to ensure that the data subject is aware of which personal data will be processed, by which data controller, for what purpose, and how. This is essential for the data subject to be in a position to effectively exercise their rights and to be aware if a data controller intends to process their personal data for purposes that do not align with the data subject's wishes.  
(66) In connection with the processing of potentially significant numbers of personal data of data subjects for political purposes, conducted by multiple contributors (Respondent, Obligor, Datadat GmbH), a risk exceeding the usual level arises. Therefore, heightened informational expectations apply. In the absence of appropriate information, it is evident that the data subject is not in a position to adequately exercise their rights.  
(67) In the case of data processing based on Article 6(1)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation, according to Article 4(11) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller is obliged to provide information that allows for informed consent not only before the commencement of data processing but also before obtaining consent.  
(68) Regarding the legal basis of data subject consent under the General Data Protection Regulation, it is important to emphasize that consent to data processing can only be valid if it is requested for specific purpose(s) – which can be specified separately for each purpose – and if appropriate information is provided beforehand, placing the data subject in a position where...
19  
to make an appropriate decision regarding the granting of consent, and complies with all other validity requirements set forth in the General Data Protection Regulation. Article 12(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation explicitly imposes an obligation on the data controller, meaning that it must provide assistance to the data subject so that they can exercise all their rights in an informed manner.  
(69) The obligation to provide information, as elaborated above, does not merely represent a "paperwork" obligation under the General Data Protection Regulation. Both the recitals and the articles of the General Data Protection Regulation prescribe the achievement of results when determining the obligations of the data controller, not just the demonstration of a certain minimal effort on the part of the data controller by sending an informative follow-up. The purpose of the information is to place the data subject in a position where they can make informed decisions regarding the exercise of their rights. If this is not objectively achieved for the average data subjects, then it will not comply with the General Data Protection Regulation.  

4. Data processing related to the first Facebook Messenger message requesting subscription sent to the Applicant  

(70) Regarding the first Facebook Messenger message requesting subscription sent to the Applicant, it is not a general function affecting all accounts of the Facebook service, as explained in points (11), (i), (iv), (vi), (14)(i), (20), and (21) above. The activation of Facebook Messenger messaging triggered by comments, as well as the setting of its content and conditions, including repetition, falls within the interest and responsibility of the Respondent and the Obligor, as outlined in section III.2 above, rather than Meta Ireland. This determined the purpose and existence of the related data processing and the substantive characteristics of the tool (under what conditions it will be repeated, with what information).  
(71) The Authority did not find the Applicant's statement in response to the call under Section 76 of the Act logical – that automatic messaging would not be a built-in function of the Facebook service because they had not encountered it yet. The fact that not all of Facebook's billions of web pages use this function, and that the Applicant has not yet encountered it, does not refute the availability of the function on the platform, nor does it support the claim that this would be a unique development on the part of the Obligor. A general analysis of the Facebook service is not the subject of this procedure and cannot be, however, regarding the specific data processing activity under examination, based on publicly available documentation, the Authority established the following facts:  
(i) Sending recurring messages (the subscription) is only possible with explicit consent, and the relevant document (so-called "Developer Policy", source: https://developers.facebook.com/devpolicy) point 2.b explicitly states that spamming is prohibited, meaning, for example, in this case, attempts to contact at very frequent intervals. The above documentation does not define specific numbers, and in this case, it concerns two messages, so this alone likely does not violate Facebook's own policies; however, in matters of non-compliance with its own policies, Meta Ireland is entitled to act, which is not the subject of this procedure, as the Authority examines compliance with data protection laws.

(ii) When a data controller seeks to obtain consent for further communication, it is a prerequisite that the user performs an interaction on the site that triggers the sending of a consent request according to the settings provided by the data controller, for example, in this case, writing a comment on some content on the Facebook page. On behalf of the data controller (in this case, the Applicant and the Obligor), the system sends an API Request, i.e., a request for data transmission, to the given user. As a result, a pop-up message appears on the user's device, which does not appear to the data controller. If the user approves the request, the system sends the user's so-called PSID as part of the technical response message to the data controller, which is one of the user identifiers used by the Facebook service among several identifiers. This corresponds to the declaration of the Obligor under point (9)(iii), thus the Authority accepted this as a fact.  
(72) The Authority does not share the Applicant's view that an attempt to contact within the framework of the Facebook service would only be possible with prior consent. The fundamental function of social media platforms – such as Facebook – is to enable contact and communication. The service can also be used if the data subject objects to it among the general settings. This is not consent but rather the general exercise or non-exercise of objection, analogous to the fact that under electronic communications legislation, one can prohibit appearing in public online directories or indicate that they do not wish to receive calls for certain purposes (e.g., marketing, research). For this to qualify as consent as stated by the Obligor in point (8)(v), prior information is lacking, and there is no individual specification and revocability concerning the specific (purpose-specific, conducted by a specific data controller) data processing. However, this does not exclude the possibility of contacting data subjects who do not exercise the above general objection for contact purposes on one occasion. In such cases, it is necessary to provide counter-evidence for the legitimate interest specifically within the framework of social media platforms. A similar attempt to contact is made through the displayed advertisements, which are also not directly displayed to the data subject by the advertiser but by the platform provider; however, the contact is initiated by the advertiser, without which it would not occur. Therefore, the general unlawfulness of the first (not repeated many times within a short period) message cannot be established solely due to the lack of consent concerning either the Applicant or other data subjects.  
(73) However, regarding the above data processing, the party that can primarily determine the content of the contact must ensure the appropriate data processing information, in accordance with Article 14 of the General Data Protection Regulation (since the data subject does not directly provide the data to the Obligor and the Applicant for the contact, the data is processed by the Meta Ireland data processor based on instructions given by the Obligor with administrative settings without the active involvement of the data subject to send the message requesting consent from the Obligor and the Applicant). As stated in points (8)(viii), (x), (9)(iv), (11)(i), and (18) above, the reference to the information was set up so that it only appears to subscribers after subscribing, not at the end of the text requesting subscription. Thus, those who do not subscribe do not receive information about the role of the Obligor and other information under Article 14(1) and (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation. This provision violates Article 12(1) and Article 14(1) and (2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, as the Applicant and the Obligor – evaluated in the section on legal consequences with varying degrees of involvement – did not ensure the appropriate information for the data subjects and the proper enforceability of their rights.  
5. Data processing related to the second Facebook Messenger message requesting subscription sent to the Applicant.
21

(74) The content of the second message and its triggering conditions are identical to those of the first message, thus the provisions described in section III.4 regarding the notification are equally applicable to the second message concerning related legal violations.  
(75) The Obligor – for example, in points (8)(vi) and (9)(vii) – emphasized multiple times during the proceedings that the subsequent request for re-subscription with identical content following the Applicant's first negative response was the result of a technical error arising on the Facebook service page. The Authority reached a contrary conclusion based on the statements indicated in points (11)(iv), (v), and (vi). The second message was sent because the administrator interface of the Facebook page was set to have no "silence period," meaning that any comments made on Facebook pages during the period from February 2022 to April 2022 would generate further messaging regardless of the previous objection statement. According to the Obligor's statement, this was justified by a legitimate interest – which actually pertains to the interest of the Applicant as a public figure appearing on the Facebook page – however, this interest was merely asserted and not adequately substantiated.  
(76) The documents sent by the Obligor do not indicate that the rights of the affected individuals were considered in any capacity, nor that their actual interests were genuinely weighed against this; on the contrary, based on the Obligor's statements presented in points 11(iv) and (vi), they explicitly disregarded them. The exercise of one fundamental right – for example, in this case, commenting on a political website as an exercise of the right to freedom of expression, often as an expression of disagreement with the website operator – cannot serve as a justification for automatically depriving the individual of another fundamental right – the right to object to data processing through further subscription requests under Article 21(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation – without any possibility of review. Therefore, the logic presented by the Obligor is erroneous, as it could result in a chilling effect on the expression of opinions by linking the affected individual's comment to the loss of rights under the General Data Protection Regulation due to the Obligor's unilateral decision. Consequently, the outcome of weighing the legitimate interest can only be that the political interest sought is not a proportionate and necessary restriction; it cannot override the rights of every individual expressing opinions online. Since Article 21(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation explicitly allows for the rejection of objections only through case-by-case assessment based on legitimate interest, and due to the "silence period" setting and the Obligor's own statement under point 11(vi), the data controller did not intend to conduct this assessment, thus the automatic rejection setting would render the reference to legitimate interest unlawful in cases of messages requesting re-subscription following the first negative response, regardless of the aforementioned.  
(77) In light of the above, the Authority determined, according to the operative part, that there was a violation of Article 6(1) and Article 21(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation concerning the data processing related to the second Facebook Messenger message requesting re-subscription, which was sent without considering the right to object and without a valid legal basis following the first message requesting re-subscription.

The details of the data processing could not have been known to the data subjects at the time of giving their subscription consent, as they only received data processing information after subscribing, and according to the facts established in point I.2., the Applicant appeared on the Facebook page not on behalf of the Obligor, but in their own individual capacity as a political public figure, which did not clarify to the data subjects the role of the Obligor in the data processing (and its exact conditions) concerning the Facebook page – and the related Facebook Messenger service sending messages in the first person singular as stated by the Applicant.
