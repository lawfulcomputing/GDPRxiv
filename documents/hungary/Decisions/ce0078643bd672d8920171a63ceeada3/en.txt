**DECISION**  

The National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Authority) has made the following decisions regarding the request submitted by […] (hereinafter: Applicant) to the Authority on December 30, 2021, concerning the sending of letters related to […] by […] (hereinafter: Respondent) to the Applicant and the failure to respond to the data protection complaint, as well as the general data processing practices of the Respondent regarding letters to be sent to the data subjects:  

1. The Authority finds that the Respondent has violated  
- Article 5(1)(a) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter: General Data Protection Regulation) concerning the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, as it relates to the Applicant and other data subjects;  
- Articles 13(1)-(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation concerning the Applicant and other data subjects.  

2. The Authority prohibits the Respondent from processing the names and email addresses of the Applicant and other data subjects based on Article 6(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation in connection with the information regarding […].

(83) Article 5 of the General Data Protection Regulation contains the main principles that must be considered during the processing of personal data and which must continuously apply throughout the data processing. GTC sections 6.3.1 and 6.3.2, moreover, refer to system messages received by the Applicant, thus they are not relevant to the present case, while GTC section 9.2 states that "the Sender or the Recipient will be notified of the arrival of the shipment and the possibility of receipt via an automatic system message sent to the mobile phone number, which notification will include the code necessary to open the compartment." (94) According to the principle of legality, fair processing, and transparency as set out in Article 5(1)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation, legality primarily presupposes the existence of an appropriate legal basis, meaning that the data processing must be based on the consent of the data subject or must have another legal basis as specified in the General Data Protection Regulation, including the necessity of compliance with legal obligations applicable to the data controller, the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party, and steps prior to entering into a contract. The requirement of transparency is closely related to the expectation of legality, in that the process of data processing and its documentation must be accessible to the outside world. Communication between the data controller and the data subject must be ensured, so that the data subject is informed of all essential aspects of the data processing and receives this information in a clear, simple manner, but with substantive content. Effective and comprehensive information is an essential element of transparency and a prerequisite for the enforcement of the data subject's rights. The General Data Protection Regulation prefers that information be provided in writing. The principle of transparency requires that information related to the processing of personal data be easily accessible and understandable, and that it be formulated in clear and simple language. This principle particularly applies to informing data subjects about the identity of the data controller and the purpose of the data processing. The principle of transparency is also part of and a prerequisite for the enforceability of accountability. Data controllers must inform data subjects and the public that the data will be processed lawfully and transparently, and they must be able to demonstrate that the data processing operations comply with the General Data Protection Regulation. In relation to online services, the characteristics of the data controller's systems must enable data subjects to actually find out what happens to their personal data.  

(95) Accordingly, the Authority cannot examine the compliance of the legal basis of consent under the Infotv. and the 2016 data processing information in this procedure; however, it can be established that, contrary to the statements, point 4.1 of the 2016 data processing information indeed includes sending system messages related to the services as a purpose of data processing, but according to the information, the consent of the data subject was the legal basis for data processing during this period. This point of the information also includes sending a questionnaire regarding measuring user satisfaction as a purpose of data processing, but not as a purpose compatible with the status message. However, there is no information about […] in the information. None of the data processing mentioned and described in the information has the legal basis of Article 6(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation. Furthermore, at the bottom of the two emails sent to the Applicant, there is a statement that the letter was sent by the Respondent based on Article 54(2)(a) of the Postal Act. Therefore, the legality of the legal basis for data processing cannot be established, nor can it be determined that the data processing complies with the General Data Protection Regulation.

(98) According to the right to prior information under Article 13 of the General Data Protection Regulation, data controllers are obliged to provide appropriate information to data subjects regarding the manner and circumstances of the processing of their personal data. This right is intended to make data subjects aware that the data controller intends to process personal data concerning them. On the other hand, through this information, data subjects can assess how the planned data processing may affect their privacy, as well as what other risks and dangers it may entail. Finally, individuals become capable of exercising their right to informational self-determination based on the information received. The right to prior information primarily aims to uphold the principle of transparency. Through this entitlement, data subjects can become acquainted with the planned data processing. However, it is also closely related to the requirement of fair procedure and the principle of accountability. Article 13 (1)-(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation contains the information that data controllers must provide to data subjects. Considering that during the examined period, the data processing information was based on the information requirements under the Infotv. in effect in 2016 – including information about the legal basis under the then Infotv. – it can be established that the Respondent did not provide information in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation, thereby violating Article 13 (1)-(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation.  

(99) According to the Authority's position, all the data processing purposes specified in the Respondent's statement – and partly in the 2016 data processing information – are deemed lawful, meaning it is lawful for the Respondent to use the data subject's name and email address to inform them about the status of the delivery of the ordered product, to request feedback in the form of a questionnaire regarding the quality of the service, and to inform the data subjects about the so-called [...] as a discount.  

(100) The Authority also considers it acceptable for the Respondent to base the data processing related to status messages on the legal basis under Article 6 (1) b) of the General Data Protection Regulation among the contested data processing activities.  

(101) The contractual legal basis is applicable because the terms and conditions applied by the Respondent also extend to the recipients, for example, regarding the receipt of shipments and their return. According to the Respondent's statement, the recipients are at the center of its service based on point 4 of the terms and conditions, which states that the Respondent undertakes the delivery of shipments ordered by the recipient, the organization of delivery, and the delivery of the package to the recipient. Sending emails for the traceability of postal shipments is necessary for both the proper provision of the service offered by the Respondent and for the fulfillment of the contract between the online store and the recipient, as well as for the Respondent to meet its obligations towards the recipient as stipulated in the terms and conditions. According to the Authority's position, the data processing provides information related to the fulfillment of the main subject of the contract, concerning the receipt of the specific product or goods, which directly affects the performance of the specific contract, and the data subject can reasonably expect the data processing, and the terms and conditions explicitly include this, thus the contractual legal basis is applicable in this case of data processing.  

(102) Regarding the request to fill out a questionnaire aimed at assessing customer satisfaction and the related data processing, it should be considered that the use of personal data – name, email address – for this different purpose – so-called further data processing – is in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation.
24  
According to Article 5(1)(b) and Article 6(4) of the General Data Protection Regulation, data processing can only be lawful—except for processing for the purposes of public interest archiving, scientific and historical research, or statistical purposes—if it is not based on the consent of the data subject, but rather on legitimate public interest objectives as defined by Union or Member State law in accordance with Article 23(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, and if it is otherwise compatible with the original purpose for which the personal data was collected.

(103) In this case, there is no need for a separate legal basis that differs from the one that allowed for the original collection of personal data. However, in this case, the data controller must analyze the relationship between the original purpose of data processing and the new purpose of data processing, considering the circumstances under which the personal data was collected (particularly the relationship between the data subject and the data controller), as well as which personal data is subject to processing (whether it involves special categories of data). Additionally, it must examine what potential consequences further data processing may have for the data subjects and must incorporate appropriate safeguards in relation to the new data processing.

(104) The Authority reviewed the aspects presented by the Applicant during the procedure in accordance with Article 6(4) of the General Data Protection Regulation, based on which it established that there is a close relationship between the original and the further purposes of data processing. The sending of status messages and the measurement of customer satisfaction as objectives are all related to the service, its fulfillment, and evaluation as a unified purchasing process. The Applicant collects only the name and email address of the data subjects who enter into a customer relationship with it for the purpose of delivery to the […] parcel locker, providing them with information about the delivery process, and then invites the same group of individuals to evaluate its service—without processing any further personal data. In the Authority's view, this invitation for further data processing is closely related to the original purpose and does not adversely affect the privacy of the data subjects, considering that the request to complete a questionnaire aimed at understanding customer satisfaction can easily be disregarded in a letter placed alongside the notification of the successful delivery, and those who do not fill it out do not suffer any disadvantage, as the service has already been fulfilled. A mere invitation to complete a questionnaire evaluating a service and the processing of the data subject's name and email address for this purpose generally does not involve a high risk.

(105) Based on all of the above, the Authority accepts the Applicant's reasoning, considers the modifications made during the procedure regarding the contested data processing, and finds the processing of the names and email addresses of the data subjects for the purpose of inviting them to complete a questionnaire aimed at understanding customer satisfaction to be compatible with the original purpose of the Applicant's data processing, as well as the processing of the names and email addresses of the data subjects for the purpose of providing information related to the fulfillment of the service, thus no separate legal basis is required.

(106) The Authority also reviewed the currently applicable data processing information available on the Applicant's website in this context and found that its current point 6 addresses data processing related to the sending of status messages, while point 7 pertains to data processing related to the invitation to complete a questionnaire aimed at understanding customer satisfaction, in accordance with the statements made by the Applicant. Therefore, no action is required in this regard.

(107) The Authority, however, has a February 19, 2024 email sent to an employee of the Authority regarding […] which invites the data subject to evaluate the service and complete a questionnaire. At the bottom of the email, there is a notification stating that “[...], provides you with electronic tracking of your shipment based on a legal obligation.” However, this notification differs from the statement made by the Applicant and what is written in its data processing information, referring to the legal basis of a statutory obligation.

The Respondent is not legally obligated to send an email inviting the completion of a questionnaire for the evaluation of the service; furthermore, this specific letter does not serve the purpose of electronic tracking of the shipment, which is why the information contained in the letter is incorrect. (108) Regarding the information about […] – and the handling of the names and email addresses of the data subjects for this purpose – the Authority also reviewed the aspects examined by the Respondent in accordance with Article 6(4) of the General Data Protection Regulation. Based on this, the Authority's position is that considering that the data subject can send a package at a discounted rate through the Respondent's network, the information about […] – contrary to the Respondent's position, which aims to promote the Respondent's service – is therefore classified as economic advertising according to Section 3(d) of the Grt. The Authority believes that this type of message is not directly related to the performance of the contract and the sending of related status messages, nor to the original purpose of data processing; rather, it is an independent, not closely related additional information with direct marketing content aimed at promoting the use of the Respondent's service, which the data subject can obtain not only in connection with the fulfillment of the given service but also through three other means: by registration, by inviting friends as a registered member, and also as a registered member during special promotions organized by the Respondent. Consequently, the information regarding delivery, which also serves the fulfillment of the contract, is separate from the purpose of processing the personal data related to […], and as an independent direct marketing data processing, it cannot be reconciled with the data processing purpose related to the information on delivery. Furthermore, regarding the contractual legal basis, the provision of the service is the main subject of the contract, and the data subject reasonably expects that their data will be processed in connection with this, thus the contractual legal basis can only be applied to the use of the service, not for sending advertising messages.

(109) Considering that the information about […] as direct marketing content cannot be reconciled with the original purpose of data processing, the Respondent must have an appropriate legal basis to send such messages.

(110) Although the Respondent claims that the data processing related to the information about […] has a negligible impact on the data subject and is also beneficial for the data subject, the Authority's position is that the Respondent should not apply provisions for data processing that deviate from the purpose, but rather this data processing can be carried out based on a separate, independent data processing purpose with an appropriate legal basis.

(111) Furthermore, the data processing related to the information about […] does not appear in the data processing information as a direct marketing letter, neither during the examined period nor currently; no information about it is visible or known to the data subjects. In this regard, it is necessary to amend the Respondent's data processing information.

(112) In light of all this, the Respondent cannot process the name and email address of the Applicant in connection with the information about […] based on Article 6(1)(b) of the General Data Protection Regulation; therefore, the Authority prohibits the Respondent from processing the Applicant's personal data on this legal basis.
