D E C I S I O N 
 
The National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Authority) has initiated an official procedure regarding the unauthorized transfer of data at the request of […] applicant (hereinafter: Applicant) against […] Law Firm (hereinafter: Lawyer) representing […] Ltd. (hereinafter: Respondent 1) and […] Ltd. (hereinafter: Respondent 2) (hereinafter collectively: Respondents), in which the Authority makes the following decisions: 
 
I. 1. The Authority grants the Applicant's request 
 
and 
 
I.2. establishes that the transfer of the Applicant's personal data between the Respondents, as well as to […] (hereinafter: Bank), constitutes a violation by the Respondents of the protection of natural persons in relation to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, as well as the repeal of Directive 95/46/EC, as set out in Article 6(1) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter: GDPR or General Data Protection Regulation). 
 
I.3. The Authority ex officio establishes that Respondent 2 has violated Article 28(3) of the GDPR, and instructs Respondent 2 to align its data processing agreement with its credit intermediaries with the provisions of the GDPR within 30 days following the expiration of the deadline for initiating administrative litigation or, in the case of initiating administrative litigation, following the termination of the administrative litigation. 
 
Respondent 2 must provide written evidence to the Authority of the measures prescribed in point I.3 within 8 days from the date of taking those measures, along with supporting evidence. 
 
There is no administrative remedy against this decision, but it can be challenged in administrative litigation by a statement of claim addressed to the Metropolitan Court within 30 days from the notification. The statement of claim must be submitted electronically to the Authority, which will forward it to the court along with the case documents. A request for a hearing must be indicated in the statement of claim. For those not fully exempt from personal fees, the administrative litigation fee is HUF 30,000, and the case is subject to the fee recording right. Legal representation is mandatory in proceedings before the Metropolitan Court.

III. Applicable Legal Provisions

(46) According to Article 2(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the regulation shall apply to the processing of personal data that is carried out in whole or in part by automated means, as well as to the non-automated processing of data that forms part of a filing system or is intended to form part of a filing system.

(47) For data processing falling under the General Data Protection Regulation, according to Section 2(2) of the Infotv., the General Data Protection Regulation shall be applied with the supplements specified therein.

(48) According to Article 4(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, "personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, particularly by reference to an identifier such as a name, number, location data, online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.

(49) According to Article 4(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation, "processing" means any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction;

(50) According to Article 4(7) of the General Data Protection Regulation, "data controller" means the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or any other body which alone or jointly with others determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data; where the purposes and means of such processing are determined by Union or Member State law, the data controller or the specific criteria for the designation of the data controller may also be provided for by Union or Member State law.

(51) GDPR Article 4(11) "the data subject's consent": the voluntary, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes by which the data subject signifies agreement to the processing of personal data relating to them, by a statement or by a clear affirmative action.

(52) GDPR Article 6(1)(a), (b), (c), (f): The processing of personal data shall be lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the following applies:
       a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of their personal data for one or more specific purposes;
       b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract;
       c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject;
       f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular if the data subject is a child.

IV.2. Personal Data, Data Processing, Data Controllers, Data Processor, and the Subject of the Investigation

(68) According to Article 4(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the content of the Letter sent by the Applicant (intention to withdraw) qualifies as the Applicant's personal data, meaning that the Applicant's legal declaration is to be considered personal data. The transfer of personal data is classified as data processing under Article 4(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation.

(69) In the application submitted to the Authority, the Applicant essentially disputed the legality of the data transfer that occurred when Respondent 1 forwarded the Letter to Respondent 2, and then Respondent 2 forwarded it to the Bank. Therefore, based on the Applicant's request, the Authority must examine the legality of this data transfer by the Respondents in the current procedure.

(70) According to Article 4(7) of the GDPR, a data controller is a natural or legal person […] that determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data, either alone or jointly with others […].

(71) The Respondents clearly identified themselves as data controllers regarding the processing (transfer) of the Applicant's personal data in their statement sent to the Authority, and this is also evident from the attached data processing notices and the analysis of their activities. Since no facts were raised during the proceedings that would challenge this, the Authority considered the Respondents as independent data controllers, as they determine the purposes and means of the data processing.

(72) […] According to the information provided by Respondent 1, it qualifies as a data processor concerning the data processing under examination, and based on the attached data processing agreement, it cannot make substantive decisions. Respondent 2 did not dispute that it was among the tasks of […] as a data processor to forward the Letter to the Bank. Respondent 2 identified itself as a data controller in relation to the contested data processing and argued in its statement that […] lawfully forwarded the Letter to the Bank as a data processor.

(73) The Authority emphasizes that the mandate agreement between Respondent 2 and […] does not establish that Respondent 2 provided detailed instructions to its contractor, as point f) explicitly states that "The Contractor shall act independently in performing the task. The Contractor independently organizes and conducts negotiations with third parties." Therefore, the attached document does not clearly indicate […]'s status as a data processor. However, this part of the mandate agreement contradicts the provision in the attached data processing agreement that it cannot make substantive decisions regarding data processing and can only process personal data in accordance with the data controller's instructions.

(74) The data processing agreement submitted by Respondent 2 does not clearly indicate that it must have forwarded letters containing withdrawal intentions, including the Letter, to the credit intermediary; this is not explicitly stated in the agreement.

(75) Article 28(3) of the GDPR stipulates that the processing of personal data by the data processor must be governed by a contract that specifies the subject matter, duration, nature, and purpose of the processing, the types of personal data, the categories of data subjects, as well as the rights and obligations of the data controller. The European Data Protection Board's Guidelines 07/2020 (hereinafter: Guidelines), which discuss the concepts of data controller and data processor under the GDPR, also detail the fundamental criteria for data processing agreements. According to point 112 of the Guidelines, "The data processing agreement must therefore not merely repeat the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation; instead, it should contain more specific, concrete information […]."
11

According to point 118 of the Guidance, "The instructions given by the data controller must be documented. For this purpose, it is advisable that the contract or the annex of another legal act contains a procedure and template for further instructions. Alternatively, instructions may also be provided in writing (e.g., by email) and in any other documented form, provided that a record can be kept of such instructions. In any case, in order to avoid difficulties in proving that the instructions of the data controller were properly documented in writing, the European Data Protection Board recommends that these instructions be included in the same text as the contract or other legal act."

(76) The Authority found that, regarding the data processing under review, the Respondent 2 can be considered a data controller due to the fact that it interpreted its data processing agreement with […] in this way, thus it did not claim that the data transfer contested by the Applicant contradicted the decision of the data controller, i.e., that the credit intermediary acting as […] "had overstepped" its data processing tasks. At the same time, the Authority established ex officio that by not properly documenting the instruction related to the forwarding of the Letter in writing, Respondent 2 violated Article 28(3) of the GDPR. Therefore, it instructed Respondent 2 ex officio to align its data processing agreement with its intermediaries with the provisions of the GDPR, so that the data controller's operations are detailed therein.

IV.3. Data Transfer Between Respondent 1 and Respondent 2

(77) […] forwarded the Letter to […] who submitted the Applicant's loan application to the Bank on behalf of Respondent 2 based on the Applicant's authorization.

(78) Respondent 1 indicated consent as the legal basis for the data transfer.

(79) In reference to Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR, a fundamental condition for the processing of personal data is, according to Article 4(11) of the GDPR, that the data subjects have appropriate information regarding the circumstances of data processing before making their decision to give consent, and that they clearly express their consent.

(80) In the viewing statement, the Applicant (by checking the box) chose the option to "take advantage of the free consultation opportunity at […] ([…]). I consent to the transfer of the necessary data (name, phone number, email address) and request that your colleagues contact me regarding the appointment."

(81) Considering that the Applicant – in the viewing statement – only consented to the transfer of their personal data necessary for the consultation with Respondent 2, namely their name, phone number, and email address, Respondent 1 did not have a legal basis to also send the Letter to Respondent 2, citing the Applicant's consent. The Letter contained the Applicant's intention to withdraw, which is additional information regarding the Applicant compared to the personal data mentioned in the data processing information. Therefore, the data processing information referred to by Respondent 1 explicitly does not mention the transfer of the document containing the intention to withdraw, thus the criterion of having appropriate information as per Article 4(11) of the GDPR does not apply to the Letter, and therefore the reference to Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR by Respondent 1 is not applicable regarding the transfer to Respondent 2.

(82) Considering that Respondent 1 did not demonstrate any other legal basis besides Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR in relation to the contested data processing, and since for the reasons stated above, Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR is not applicable in this case,
12

Therefore, the Authority granted the Applicant's request to determine that Respondent 1 unlawfully forwarded the Letter to Respondent 2, as the Authority found that Respondent 1 violated Article 6(1) of the GDPR.

IV.4. Data Transfer Between Respondent 2 and the Bank

(83) Respondent 2 stated that it forwarded the Letter "at the instruction" of the Bank, even after the credit assessment. For the purposes of this investigation, it is not possible to determine whether the transfer occurred after the credit assessment, as the Authority does not have the jurisdiction to examine whether the Letter may have influenced the credit assessment process. Furthermore, it is also irrelevant that the data transfer occurred "at the instruction" of the Bank, since, as Respondent 2 is an independent data controller, it must make decisions during its data processing for which it must also take responsibility.

(84) Respondent 2, similar to Respondent 1 based on the previous point, identified consent as the legal basis for the data transfer. However, the conditions for valid consent as per Article 4(11) of the GDPR are not met in this case, as Respondent 2 did not inform that personal data would be forwarded to the Bank. According to the document attached in the copy, dated April 13, 2021, and containing a signature next to the Applicant's name, “[...] The Client consents to the Intermediary processing their personal data recorded during the application in accordance with the laws, the Intermediary's business rules, data processing policy (available at [...]), and in the manner and within the framework authorized in this data sheet. The Client has familiarized themselves with the contents of all these documents prior to signing this data sheet. [...]”

(85) Respondent 2 also claimed that the bank officer "instructed" Respondent 2 to forward the Letter unchanged to the Bank, considering that the Bank has a legitimate interest in being aware if the Applicant seeks compensation based on false statements. Thus, Respondent 2 only mentioned the legitimate interest of the Bank, but did not refer to any legitimate interest of Respondent 2. The fact that the data transfer might be a duty of Respondent 2 arising from the contract between the Bank and Respondent 2 does not change the fact that Respondent 2 should have had the Applicant's valid consent regarding this matter if it identified Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR as the legal basis for the data transfer.

(86) In connection with the data transfer to the Bank (and generally to financial institutions involved in Respondent 2's intermediary activities), Respondent 2's data processing information does not contain any information. In the section describing the data processing activities performed by Respondent 2, the information does not mention the fact of forwarding to financial institutions involved in the intermediation at all, thus neither the scope of the data concerned nor the legal basis for the transfer.

(87) For the above reasons, the criteria under Article 4(11) of the GDPR are not met in this case either, thus the Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR cited by Respondent 2 is not applicable regarding the transfer to the Bank. Given that Respondent 2 did not identify any other legal basis in connection with the contested data processing, the Authority granted the Applicant's request to determine that Respondent 2 unlawfully forwarded the Letter to the Bank, as Respondent 2 violated Article 6(1) of the GDPR.
