**DECISION**  

The National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (hereinafter: Authority) (…) (hereinafter: Applicant) has submitted an application against Igazi Társ Kft. (registered office: 1055 Budapest, Falk Miksa utca 4., tax number: 23147867-2-41, company registration number: 01-09-955189, represented by: Zsolt Morvai, managing director, hereinafter: Respondent) regarding the data processing conducted by the Almássy Éva Társközvetítő Iroda1 operated by the Respondent in the data protection authority procedure initiated by the Applicant.  

The Authority  

grants the Applicant's request  
and makes the following decisions:  
1. The Authority finds the Respondent in violation based on the Applicant's request, as it has infringed upon the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and the free movement of such data, as well as the repeal of Directive 95/46/EC, under Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter: "GDPR" or "General Data Protection Regulation")  
1.1. Article 13 by failing to provide the Applicant with appropriate information regarding data processing;  
1.2. Article 12(1) by providing information that was not transparent and easily accessible, thereby failing to comply with the principle of accountability as stipulated in Article 5(2) of the GDPR;  
1.3. Article 9(2) by lacking a legal basis for the processing and transfer of special data (religion).  

2. The Authority orders the Respondent ex officio to  
2.1. prepare and publish on its websites the data processing information regarding its matchmaking services in accordance with Article 12(1) and Article 13 of the GDPR;  
2.2. ensure the consistency of the prepared data processing information and other customer information documents (especially the contract, forms, and content available on its websites);  
2.3. ensure the legal conditions for the processing of special data or cease such processing.  

The Respondent must provide written evidence of compliance with the measures outlined in points 2.1-2.3 above within 30 days from the date this decision becomes final, along with copies of supporting evidence and a presentation of its actions, referencing the above registration number.  

In the event of non-compliance with the obligations set forth in points 2.1-2.3 above, the Authority will order the enforcement of the decision.

I. The application and the course of the procedure

I.1. Application

(1) The Applicant stated in the application received by the Authority on February 1, 2022, that they entered into a service contract with the Respondent on June 8, 2019, under which the Applicant commissioned the Respondent to search for and mediate a partner with the characteristics described, taking into account the needs of the potential partners (contract Section A/1).

(2) According to the Applicant, as part of the contract conclusion process, a form was filled out according to Annex 1 of the contract, which contained criteria regarding the Applicant's identity and the characteristics of the partner sought by the Applicant. I.5. The Authority's Procedural Competence and the Relation of the Current Authority Procedure to the Judicial Procedure Referenced by the Clients

(143) According to Article 4(11) of the GDPR, "the consent of the data subject" is the voluntary, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes, by which the data subject signifies agreement to the processing of personal data relating to them, through a statement or a clear affirmative action. As part of this, the guidelines on consent highlight, among other things, that the fourth paragraph of Article 7 of the GDPR states that it is considered particularly undesirable for consent to be "tied" to the acceptance of contractual terms; the data controller must also ensure that consent cannot be obtained through the same action as consent to a contract; and if a data controller seeks to process personal data that is actually necessary for the performance of a contract, consent is not the appropriate legal basis.

(145) Consent must be explicit in the case of special data, where an obvious – though not exclusive – option is to obtain a written consent declaration (see the details in point 4 of the guidelines on consent); however, it must be emphasized that the data controller bears an increased burden of proof regarding the provision of the relevant consent declaration, as in the absence of such, they have no legal basis for processing the special data, while the processing of such data is generally prohibited.

(146) The Applicant did not document the consents either in the contract, in the data sheet, in emails, or in any other verifiable manner: 
The declaration must be provided separately; the signing of the contract cannot be considered a consent declaration, as it is not separate from the contractual declaration. 
It is also stated in the data processing policy that by "accepting" it, the customer gives consent. If the Applicant intended to apply this document in a way that affects the Applicant, it would have been necessary to ensure the conditions for this (accessibility of the document, obtaining the consent declaration with prior information, possibility of withdrawing consent). 
Based on the statement made in the administrative procedure, the Applicant also considered the email approval of the data sheet as a kind of consent declaration, while the Applicant was not clearly informed about this; it could not be clear to them that the response email was a consent declaration, nor that the approval related to the modification of the record or the forwarding of the modified data sheet. 
The mere completion of the data sheet does not imply, based solely on the form, that its completion can also be interpreted as consent given for any data processing.

(147) Based on all of the above, if consent had been the applicable legal basis, then the Applicant would not have complied with Article 6(1) of the GDPR, as it would not be able to prove the existence of consent and thus would not have a proper legal basis for data processing. III.4. Data transfer without legal basis (Article 9(2) of the GDPR)

(150) The Applicant stated in its application that, in its opinion, data transfer occurred even after the termination of the contract on June 28, 2019, specifically on July 1, 2019. The third transfer occurred on July 1, 2019, based on the submitted judgment [8] and [17].

(151) The Authority established that June 28, 2019, fell on a Friday, and July 1, 2019, was the following Monday, a working day after the termination of the contract. Given the termination of the contract on Friday, there was no lawful basis for the performance of the contract on the following working day for the third transfer; however, the Authority took into account that the data transfer occurred during the administrative processing of the termination, thus it was essentially the same as previous data transfers, and therefore the Authority does not find it justified to establish an independent violation in this regard.

(152) Regarding religious data as special data, there was no appropriate, lawful basis for data processing, as the Applicant did not provide explicit consent for the processing and transfer of this data, and the Applicant did not substantiate any other lawful basis for this.

(153) Based on the above, the Authority established a violation of Article 9(2) of the GDPR concerning the processing of special data (religion) and decided according to the provisions set out in Article 58(2)(b) and (d) of the GDPR.
