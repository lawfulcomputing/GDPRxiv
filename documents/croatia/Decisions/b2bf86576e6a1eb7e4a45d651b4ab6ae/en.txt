As part of the European project ARC II (Awareness Raising Campaign for SMEs), on Wednesday, July 5th, the Agency organized an online workshop aimed at entrepreneurs in the tourism sector titled "Protection of Personal Data and Compliance with GDPR in Tourism." 

Participants in the workshop had the opportunity to learn about the ten key steps necessary to align their business with the General Data Protection Regulation. Concrete examples were presented on how to conduct an analysis of personal data processed by the data controller (e.g., hotel), how to properly write a privacy policy and guest consent, as well as how to conduct a proportionality test or demonstrate legitimate interest as an appropriate legal basis for processing personal data, specifically sending newsletters to the email addresses of respondents.

Examples of appropriate technical and organizational measures that need to be taken to protect personal data were also presented. For instance, in practice, guests encounter landlords who ask them to send a copy of their credit card for reservation guarantee, which is not in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation. The landlord can use credit card data for the purpose of guaranteeing the reservation by utilizing the services of reliable companies that provide online booking and payment services or pre-authorization of credit cards. Similarly, if the landlord has a website, they can perform pre-authorization and reservation through a web form on their own website. It is crucial that the website has an SSL certificate, is regularly updated, undergoes regular security scanning, and is protected against hacking attacks and viruses.

Additionally, it was an opportunity to provide participants with answers to all questions and thus resolve uncertainties regarding the processing of personal data of their employees and/or guests. Namely, landlords often misinterpret the obligation to register a guest in e-Visitor and believe that they must photograph or copy the guest's ID card as this is their legal obligation. However, copying or photographing an identity document is not a legal obligation for landlords, and it is sufficient to ask guests to present their ID card for inspection and to transcribe the personal data necessary for entry into e-Visitor.
