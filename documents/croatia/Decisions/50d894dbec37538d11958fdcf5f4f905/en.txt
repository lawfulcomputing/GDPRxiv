The Agency for the Protection of Personal Data has imposed an administrative monetary fine in the amount of €15,000.00 (HRK 113,017.50) on the data controller, the hotel (i.e., the legal entity under which the hotel operates), due to the following established violations of the General Data Protection Regulation:

1. The data controller processed personal data of the respondents (hotel guests) to an excessive extent, specifically data regarding the security number of the bank card (CVC number), as well as copies of personal documents during the reservation of accommodation at the hotel via the hotel's online form and via email. There was no legal basis demonstrated for the processing of the CVC number of the bank card and the copies of personal documents, thereby violating Article 6, paragraph 1 of the General Data Protection Regulation. The hotel was not obligated to collect the CVC number from the bank cards of individuals who made a reservation for accommodation, given that the reservation was possible without providing this data.

2. The data controller did not clearly/transparently inform the respondents about the processing of their personal data through the document General Terms and Conditions, which is available on the hotel’s website, regarding the collection of personal data during the reservation of hotel accommodation via the online form and via email, which is contrary to the provisions of Article 13, paragraphs 1 and 2 of the General Data Protection Regulation. In this specific case, the hotel did not adequately provide notice about the processing of personal data to guests who reserved accommodation at the hotel, including information about the collection of data regarding the CVC number and the copy of the identification document. Considering the provisions of the regulations governing the protection of personal data, the hotel was obliged to inform the guest about what types of personal data it collects for what purpose, the legal basis for the processing of personal data, how personal data is used, who uses personal data, and what measures for the protection of personal data have been taken. All information regarding the processing of personal data was to be provided by the hotel in a concise, understandable, and easily accessible form, using clear and simple language, and it was obliged to inform the respondent of all rights that belong to them in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation.

3. Furthermore, the form "Consent for the Use of Personal Data," which the data controller provides to inform respondents about the processing of their personal data when reserving accommodation via email, does not contain accurate or complete information, thereby acting contrary to the provisions of Article 13, paragraphs 1 and 2 of the General Data Protection Regulation.

4. By failing to take appropriate organizational and technical measures for the protection of personal data of respondents by the data controller, there was a violation of Article 32, paragraphs 1 a) and d) and paragraph 4 of the General Data Protection Regulation. The data controller did not take appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, including, among other things, the encryption of personal data and the implementation of processes for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.

5. By appointing the hotel manager as the data protection officer, the data controller acted contrary to the provisions of Article 38, paragraph 6 of the General Data Protection Regulation. Namely, the data protection officer may perform other tasks and duties; however, the data controller ensures that such tasks and duties do not lead to a conflict of interest. When appointing the data protection officer, the data controller must have been aware that there was a conflict of interest regarding the tasks and duties performed by the same. From the job description of the hotel manager, it is evident that they are largely responsible for making managerial decisions at the level of processing personal data, while on the other hand, as a data protection officer, they are obliged to monitor compliance of operations in the processing of personal data with the regulations governing the protection of personal data.
The Agency for the Protection of Personal Data received a complaint from a citizen who stated that when booking accommodation at the hotel in question, confirmation of the reservation was requested by sending the CVC of the credit card (via a form) through completely unprotected channels (via email). Furthermore, the received complaint indicated that the potential guest was not informed about who has access to their personal data, i.e., the personal document that they were required to send at the hotel’s request in order to charge the credit card.

Namely, the hotel in question had three options for booking accommodation – through a service provider, online booking via a web form on the hotel’s website, and via email, with the note that only reservations were made through the web form and email, not payments.

When booking via the web form, it was necessary to enter the guest's personal data: first name, last name, email address, address, and financial information (card number, expiration date, CVC number, and cardholder's name), while for booking via email, it was necessary to provide the aforementioned data along with a copy of a valid identification document with a photograph, all to prevent the misuse of the bank card by third parties, as claimed by the hotel.

In this case, taking into account the established violations, the Agency decided to impose an administrative monetary fine due to the existence of a high risk to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects, which the data controller was obliged to consider before the processing of personal data in question. Therefore, it concerns a data controller whose business involves the processing of personal data, and the aforementioned actions resulted in the collection of personal data without an appropriate legal basis, as the personal data collected were not necessary for the purpose for which they were collected from the data subjects when booking accommodation at the hotel. Additionally, the Agency believes that imposing a fine will lead the data controller to timely and appropriately fulfill their obligations in the area of personal data protection.
