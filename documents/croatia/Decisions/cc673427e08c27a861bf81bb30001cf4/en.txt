The webinar is primarily intended for all interested data controllers and processors, who often do not pay enough attention to information security due to a lack of human and financial resources (as well as knowledge) and are frequently victims of various online frauds. The goal of the webinar is to familiarize participants with the key aspects of Article 32 of the GDPR and to provide practical guidance for implementing appropriate measures for the protection of personal data in your organization, as well as to empower entrepreneurs to learn how to successfully defend against cyber attacks and reduce the risks of personal data breaches.

Topics to be covered:

1. Overview of the requirements of Article 32 of the GDPR
2. Risk assessment and data processing security
3. Technical protection measures (encryption, pseudonymization, backups, etc.)
4. Organizational measures (policies, procedures, employee training)
5. Continuous testing and evaluation of the effectiveness of measures
6. Practical examples and case studies

Article 32 of the GDPR is crucial for cybersecurity for several reasons:

- Article 32 of the General Data Protection Regulation requires the implementation of appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security that is appropriate to the risk. This means that organizations must actively work to protect data from cyber threats.
- By implementing the measures from Article 32 of the General Data Protection Regulation, organizations build a strong defense against various types of cyber attacks, including phishing, malware, ransomware, and other sophisticated threats.
- Measures such as encryption and pseudonymization help preserve data integrity, even in the event of a security breach.
- Demonstrating strong cybersecurity practices through compliance with Article 32 builds trust with clients and partners.
- Article 32 of the General Data Protection Regulation encourages organizations to regularly test, assess, and evaluate the effectiveness of measures, which is crucial in a world where cyber threats are constantly evolving. It encompasses both technical and organizational measures, emphasizing that cybersecurity is not just a technological issue but also involves the human factor, processes, and policies.
- The requirement for the implementation of measures “taking into account the latest advancements” means that organizations must stay updated with the latest cybersecurity trends and threats.

In the context of increasingly frequent and sophisticated cyber attacks, compliance with Article 32 of the GDPR becomes not only a legal obligation but also a key component of a comprehensive cybersecurity strategy for every organization.
