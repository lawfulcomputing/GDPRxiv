Brussels, May 5 – At the last plenary session, the European Data Protection Board (EDPB), which was attended by the EDPB Vice-Chair and Director of the Personal Data Protection Agency Zdravko Vukić, adopted an opinion on the draft decision of the European Commission regarding adequacy based on the General Data Protection Regulation in relation to the European Patent Organization (EPO). Additionally, the Board adopted an opinion on the proposal of the European Commission to extend the validity of adequacy decisions for the United Kingdom based on the General Data Protection Regulation and the Law Enforcement Directive (LED). Finally, the European Data Protection Board granted observer status to the Personal Data Protection Agency of Bosnia and Herzegovina.

“The Personal Data Protection Agency, after successfully implementing a Twinning project in North Macedonia, provided strong support last year to the supervisory authority for data protection of the Republic of Kosovo for observer status in the European Data Protection Board, which was accepted. At this session, we extended our support to the supervisory authority of Bosnia and Herzegovina and congratulate them on their observer status in the EDPB. In this way, the Agency continuously supports the data protection authorities of neighboring countries that have aligned their data protection rules with those of the European Union,” emphasized the Director of the Agency and EDPB Vice-Chair Zdravko Vukić.

**Appropriate Protection of Personal Data by the EPO**  
At the request of the European Commission, the EDPB adopted an opinion on the draft decision of the Commission regarding adequacy in relation to the European Patent Organization (EPO). Once officially adopted by the Commission, this will be the first adequacy decision concerning an international organization, rather than a country or region.  
The adequacy decision is a key mechanism in EU data protection legislation that allows the European Commission to determine whether a third country or international organization provides an adequate level of data protection. The effect of such a decision is the free flow of personal data from Europe to a third country or international organization.  
In its opinion, the Board emphasizes that the EPO's data protection framework is largely aligned with the European Union's data protection framework, including in terms of rights and principles of data protection. This demonstrates that the General Data Protection Regulation, particularly its provisions on data transfers, can facilitate the secure flow of data from Europe to international organizations, taking into account their status.

**Extension of Adequacy Decisions for the United Kingdom for Six Months**  
In the opinion of the European Data Protection Board, requested by the European Commission, the proposed extension of two adequacy decisions for the United Kingdom based on the General Data Protection Regulation and the Law Enforcement Directive, which are set to expire on June 27, 2025, is considered.  
The opinion pertains only to the proposed six-month extension of the aforementioned adequacy decisions and does not address the level of protection of personal data in the United Kingdom, which the European Data Protection Board will examine following the Commission's assessment and if a renewal of the adequacy decisions for the United Kingdom is proposed.  
Given that data protection reform is still in the UK Parliament, the European Data Protection Board recognizes the need for a technical and time-limited extension of the adequacy decisions until December 27, 2025. This will provide the European Commission with sufficient time to evaluate the updated legal framework of the United Kingdom after its adoption. The EDPB emphasizes that this extension is exceptional and is a result of current legislative changes in the United Kingdom.  
The Board reminds of the validity of its opinions 14/2021 and 15/2021 regarding the two adequacy decisions for the United Kingdom made in April 2021 and calls on the European Commission to take them into account in its future assessments.

**New Observer in the Activities of the European Data Protection Board**  
The members of the EDPB agreed to grant observer status in the activities of the EDPB to the data protection authority of Bosnia and Herzegovina, in accordance with Article 8 of the EDPB Rules of Procedure.
