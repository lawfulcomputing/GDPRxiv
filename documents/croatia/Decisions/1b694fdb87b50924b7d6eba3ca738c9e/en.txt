In response to numerous inquiries regarding whether employers are required, under the new Regulation on the Content and Method of Keeping Records of Employees Employed by the Employer (NN 55/24) (hereinafter: Regulation), to collect and retain copies of employees' identity cards, the Agency for the Protection of Personal Data (hereinafter: Agency) emphasizes the following within its jurisdiction:

Based on Article 3, Paragraph 4 of the Regulation, the employer is obliged, in order to ensure evidence of the accuracy of the data from Paragraphs 1, 2, and 3 of this Article, to protect, store, and keep, in original or copy, the documents, records, and acts from Article 8, Paragraph 1 of this Regulation, on the basis of which it enters, modifies, or deletes data about employees in the records.

Thus, according to the aforementioned provision of the Regulation, the employer is not obliged to prove the accuracy of the data from Article 3, Paragraphs 1, 2, and 3 of the Regulation (such as name and surname, personal identification number, gender, day, month, and year of birth, citizenship, residence, and domicile), but is obliged, in order to ensure evidence of the accuracy of the data from Article 3, Paragraphs 1, 2, and 3 of the Regulation, to protect, store, and keep, in original or copy, the documents, records, and acts from Article 8, Paragraph 1 of this Regulation, on the basis of which it enters, modifies, or deletes data about employees in the records.

Article 8, Paragraph 1 of the Regulation lists the documents, records, and acts from Article 3, Paragraph 4 of the Regulation that the employer is obliged to protect, store, and keep.

The mentioned provision of the Regulation does not specify a copy of the employee's identity card as one of the documents, records, or acts that the employer is obliged to protect, store, and keep.

The Agency emphasizes that the retention of a copy of the employee's identity card by the employer cannot be considered a legal obligation of the employer based on this Regulation, in the sense of the provision of Article 6, Paragraph 1, Point c) of the General Data Protection Regulation.

Furthermore, the Agency points out that the employer, in accordance with the principle of integrity and confidentiality from Article 5, Paragraph 1, Point f) of the General Data Protection Regulation, is obliged to ensure appropriate security of employees' personal data, and the storage of copies of employees' identity cards represents an increased risk of unauthorized or unlawful processing. For example, a copy of an identity card is often misused for entering into various remote contracts.

If the employer decides to collect and store copies of employees' identity cards, it must have a legal basis for such processing under Article 6, Paragraph 1 of the General Data Protection Regulation. Likewise, in accordance with Article 13, Paragraph 1, Point c) of the General Data Protection Regulation, at the time of collecting the copy of the identity card, the employer is obliged to inform the employee, among other things, about the purpose of processing and the legal basis for processing.

The Agency emphasizes that, in accordance with the provision of Article 5, Paragraph 1, Point c) of the General Data Protection Regulation (data minimization principle), personal data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed. All other personal data found on the copy of the identity card that are not adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purpose of collecting and storing the copy of the identity card must be appropriately removed or obscured by the employer.
