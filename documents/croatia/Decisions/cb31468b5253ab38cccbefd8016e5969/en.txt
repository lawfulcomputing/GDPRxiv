Government of the Republic of Croatia has announced elections for the President of the Republic of Croatia for December 29, 2024. In order for the processing of personal data of all election participants, especially voters, to be in accordance with regulations on the protection of personal data, the Agency for the Protection of Personal Data provides the following recommendations and guidelines for the upcoming elections.

In principle, any processing of personal data within the framework of the application of the General Data Protection Regulation must respect the principles of processing prescribed by Article 5, must be based on one of the legal bases for lawful processing from Article 6, and, in the case that special categories of personal data (such as data on political opinion) are processed, it must be conducted only with adequate respect for Article 9 of the General Data Protection Regulation.

**Election Campaign – Sending Marketing Messages**

On December 13, the official election campaign begins, lasting until 24 hours before the elections, when the election silence begins. 

During the election campaign, candidates contact citizens and advertise themselves through various channels to promote themselves and their programs. For the purposes of advertising and sending personalized messages to voters, they can generally rely on the consent of the respondents or on legitimate interest. For example, if a person is a member of a political party that has proposed a certain candidate and/or is its donor, they can expect personalized messages, meaning that the political party or candidate has a legitimate interest in sending such messages.

However, if a political party/candidate addresses a voter who has not given consent for the processing of personal data for these purposes or if the voter is not a member or supporter of that party/candidate or has not provided their personal data, such processing of personal data would not be in accordance with personal data protection regulations.

Furthermore, we emphasize that every marketing message (SMS, email) should also contain a notice that the recipient can object to further receiving such messages at any time (for example, the sent message must include a link to unsubscribe from such messages). If the respondent or voter objects to receiving messages, the political party/candidate must no longer send them.

In light of the above, the Agency recommends that all political parties and candidates address citizens/voters as much as possible in ways that do not necessarily involve the processing of their personal data (for example, through public media, public forums, distributing non-personalized leaflets, brochures, and other materials in which they present and explain their program). For sending personalized brochures, letters, leaflets, and other materials (including sending SMS or emails) that are addressed to a specific citizen/voter with the inclusion of their personal data, it is necessary to ensure the existence of a lawful legal basis.

Ultimately, we emphasize that candidates and political parties must inform citizens about the processing of their personal data in a transparent, clear, and concise manner, as prescribed by Articles 13 and 14 of the General Data Protection Regulation (for what purpose personal data will be used; what is the legal basis for data processing; how long the data will be stored; with whom the data will be shared, etc.).

For example, if a presidential candidate has their own website, they must provide all relevant information about the processing of citizens' or voters' personal data through the privacy policy.

After the expiration of the prescribed storage periods, all those who process personal data for election purposes are obliged to adequately destroy/delete them.

We appeal to political parties and candidates to process the personal data of citizens or party members, supporters, in accordance with the legislative framework governing the protection of personal data, as in the case of violations of the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation, the Agency has the authority to impose corrective measures (e.g., issue an official warning, caution, restrict or prohibit processing, etc.), the strictest of which is an administrative monetary fine.

**Rights of Citizens in the Context of the Election Campaign**

During the election campaign, citizens most often complain about receiving messages (either via SMS or email) inviting them to vote for a certain candidate. 

We emphasize that at any time, citizens have the right to object to the processing of their personal data, or they can object to receiving such messages, regardless of whether they have given consent or whether the political party/candidate has a legitimate interest. This right should be explicitly communicated to the voter and presented clearly and separately from all other information. If a political party/candidate continues to send marketing messages even after an objection has been made, citizens can contact the Agency for the Protection of Personal Data.

We also emphasize that if citizens receive a message from a political party/candidate, and they are not a member or supporter nor have they shared their email or mobile number with them, they have the right to request access to their personal data (including information about the source of their personal data) from the data controller (e.g., the political party from which they received the message). The data controller must respond to the request without unnecessary delay, and no later than within one month.

We encourage citizens to inform themselves about their rights guaranteed by the General Data Protection Regulation and to inquire with the data controller about the processing of their personal data. More about rights and how to exercise them: https://azop.hr/prava-ispitanika/

If respondents believe that their personal data is being processed contrary to the conditions mentioned above, in accordance with Article 34 of the Act on the Implementation of the General Data Protection Regulation, they can submit a request for the determination of a violation of the right to personal data protection to the Agency for the Protection of Personal Data at https://azop.hr/zahtjev-za-utvrdivanje-povrede-prava/.

**Support for a Single Candidate by Signature**

Based on the Law on the Election of the President of the Republic of Croatia, voter signatures in the candidacy process for the election of the President of the Republic are collected on a prescribed form, and the same data is entered for each signed proposer. Therefore, citizens who wish to support a candidate for the President of the Republic of Croatia with their signature must enter personal data into the prescribed form as specified in the Mandatory Instructions (Mandatory Instructions P IV and V) of the State Election Commission, namely their name and surname, address, and personal identification number (OIB).

**Public Disclosure of Candidates' Personal Data**

In accordance with the provisions of the Law on the Election of the President of the Republic of Croatia, the State Election Commission of the Republic of Croatia is obliged to publish the list of candidates for the President of the Republic of Croatia in all daily newspapers in the Republic of Croatia and on Croatian Radio-Television. Additionally, the State Election Commission provides the list of candidates for the President of the Republic to diplomatic and consular representations for public disclosure.

The list of candidates for the President of the Republic of Croatia includes their name and surname and OIB, and it is mandatory to state the name of the party or parties or party coalition that proposed the candidate. If the candidate is proposed by voters, it is mandatory to indicate "independent candidate" next to their name and surname.

Thus, the provisions of the Law on the Election of the President of the Republic of Croatia serve as the legal basis for the publication of personal data, and candidates cannot object to the public disclosure of personal data, while their data may only be used for the purpose for which it was published.

**Public Disclosure of Donors' Personal Data on the State Election Commission's Website**

Regarding the publication of reports on donations, in accordance with the Law on Financing Political Activities, Election Campaigns, and Referendums, political parties and persons authorized to represent independent lists, as well as candidates of a group of voters, are obliged to submit reports on donations to the State Election Commission, which is then required to publish the said reports on its website.

The report contains the personal name or title and address, as well as the personal identification number of the donor, the date of the donation payment, the amount of the donation, and the type of each individual donation. The address data of individual donors is not published.

Thus, the provisions of the Law on Financing Political Activities, Election Campaigns, and Referendums serve as the legal basis for the publication of donors' personal data, and their data may only be used for the purpose for which it was published.
