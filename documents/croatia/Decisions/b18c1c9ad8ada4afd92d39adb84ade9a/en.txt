This consultation is an activity within the EU project ARC2, which the Agency is implementing with the aim of providing support to micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises in aligning their business with legal regulations on data protection. At the same time, this consultation marks the 6th year of full application of the GDPR. The Agency emphasizes that even after 6 years, entrepreneurs still have many uncertainties regarding compliance with the GDPR and wishes to provide them with concrete support to raise the level of compliance in their organizations and avoid high monetary fines prescribed for violations of the GDPR.  
As part of the ARC2 project, we have created the online tool Olivia https://olivia-gdpr-arc.eu/hr which can help you master the basics of the GDPR and create documentation with which you will prove compliance during the Agency's supervision. Certificates of completed courses can serve as proof of knowledge acquisition in the field of personal data protection for entrepreneurs and data protection officers.  
The aim of the General Data Protection Regulation is to protect the personal data of individuals, provide citizens with control over their personal data, and create a high and uniform level of personal data protection in the European Union.  
The General Data Protection Regulation specifies the rights of individuals and, accordingly, the obligations of entities that process personal data. All business entities that collect, store, and in any way process the personal data of Croatian citizens and citizens from the European Economic Area are obliged to comply with the General Data Protection Regulation.  
It is high time for companies, public authorities, and all data controllers/processors to realize that the deadline for adjustment has long passed and that after 6 years from the start of full application of the GDPR, ignorance and lack of knowledge can no longer serve as justification and excuse for violating the fundamental rights of Croatian and European citizens—the right to personal data protection.  
Although 6 years have passed since the start of full application of the General Data Protection Regulation, the state of compliance in organizations in Croatia is still not at a satisfactory level.  
In recent years, the European Union has been working on adapting its EU legislation to respond more effectively to technological development and ongoing changes in the digital age.  
REGULATION (EU) 2022/2065 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL of October 19, 2022, on a single market for digital services and amending Directive 2000/31/EC (Digital Services Act) applies from February 17, 2024. This Regulation establishes the responsibilities of digital service providers acting as intermediaries between consumers and what consumers purchase through platforms—goods, services, and content. In this way, it aims to better protect users of digital services and their fundamental rights on the internet, establish a strong legal framework for transparency and accountability of online platforms, and ensure a unified legal framework at the level of the European Union (EU).  
REGULATION (EU) 2022/1925 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL of September 14, 2022, on fair markets with the possibility of unlimited market competition in the digital sector and amending Directives (EU) 2019/1937 and (EU) 2020/1828 (Digital Markets Act) applies from May 2023 and imposes obligations and prohibitions on digital platforms identified as gatekeepers, aiming to ensure fair and competitive digital markets in the EU.  
REGULATION (EU) 2022/868 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL of May 30, 2022, on European data governance and amending Regulation (EU) 2018/1724 (Data Governance Act) applies from September 2023. The aim of this Regulation is to facilitate voluntary sharing of personal and non-personal data between data holders and data users across the EU, including the reuse of public sector data, providing data intermediary services, and sharing data for altruistic purposes.  
THE REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL on harmonized rules for fair access to data and their use (Data Act) obliges manufacturers and service providers to enable their users, whether companies or individuals, to access and reuse data generated by using their products or services. It also allows users to share this data with third parties—for example, car owners may in the future decide to share certain vehicle data with a mechanic or their insurance company. This Regulation is intended to give individuals and companies greater control over their data through strengthened rights to portability, easy copying, or transfer of data from different services, where data is generated through smart objects, machines, and devices.  
In 2024, the European Parliament adopted the Artificial Intelligence Act, which represents the first comprehensive legal framework for artificial intelligence in the world. The aim of the new rules is to promote trustworthy artificial intelligence in Europe and beyond by ensuring that AI systems respect fundamental rights, safety, and ethical principles while mitigating the risks of very powerful and influential AI models.
