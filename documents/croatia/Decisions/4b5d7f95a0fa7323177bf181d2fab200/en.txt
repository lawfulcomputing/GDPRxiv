In order to raise awareness about internet safety, every year, on the second day of the second week of February, we celebrate Safer Internet Day. This day reminds us of the importance of responsible use of digital technologies and the protection of personal data. The internet is full of opportunities for learning, play, creativity, and communication, but with all these advantages come numerous risks, especially for children. Given that children are spending more and more time online, it is important for them to understand the dangers that threaten them in the digital world and to learn how to protect themselves.  
Unfortunately, many of us are not always aware of all the dangers lurking on the internet, which can have serious consequences. Some of the most common threats are:  
1. Cyberbullying – children and young people are often exposed to online bullying, whether it involves threats, spreading false information, or inappropriate comments. This form of violence can leave long-term emotional consequences.  
2. Identity theft – children who unknowingly share personal information, such as their name, address, phone number, or pictures, can become targets for cybercriminals, who use that information for illegal purposes.  
3. Inappropriate or dangerous content – children may encounter content on the internet that is not suitable for their age, such as violence, pornography, or dangerous challenges.  
4. Fake profiles and scams – children may be drawn into conversations with unknown individuals who present themselves as their peers but have bad intentions, often trying to manipulate or steal personal information.  
How to protect children from online dangers?  
The first step in protection from dangers is recognizing the threats. Talk to children about all the risks on the internet and set clear rules about what is acceptable and what is not. Explain to them how to recognize false information, inappropriate comments, and messages, and how to react in those situations. Teach them not to share their personal information lightly and not to communicate with unknown individuals. Advise them to always ask an adult if they are unsure how to act in a particular situation, whether it involves a suspicious message, person, or content. Also, encourage them to use privacy settings on social media, apps, and online games to control who has access to their data.
