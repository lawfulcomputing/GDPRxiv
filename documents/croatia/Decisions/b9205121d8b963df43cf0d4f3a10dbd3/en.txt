The Regulation comes into force on October 1, 2024, and imposes more extensive obligations on employers regarding the keeping of records of employees, such as, among other things:  

1. A "Written Overview of Data" is introduced to facilitate easy and direct insight into employee data, as an integral part of the employee records; the form is not predefined.  
2. A provision is introduced that the employee has the right to access their data from the employee records.  
3. Any change in data must be reported by the employee to the employer no later than within 8 days.  
4. Authorized persons under the Labor Law and other laws must be provided with records and access to the written overview of basic employee data and all evidence and documents, and a record must be kept of the date and purpose of obtaining the written document and which authorized person it concerns.  
5. A specific list of documentation regarding employees that the employer must have for proof is introduced.  
6. The maximum scope of documentation must be kept for 6 years from the end of the year in which the employment relationship ceased, or until the final conclusion of a dispute, if the employer is aware that a labor dispute has been initiated regarding the realization of rights from the employment relationship or in connection with the employment relationship.  
7. Employers must keep records of employees (and their working hours) who have been temporarily assigned to them by related companies in accordance with the Labor Law, as well as of employees assigned to them as users by a temporary employment agency.  
8. In addition to records of employees, the employer is obliged to keep a special record of data on individuals who perform work for the employer based on other contracts or special regulations, especially regarding individuals undergoing professional training, students, regular pupils, children and minors working for the employer, and individuals serving a community service sentence.  
9. Regarding records of working hours, according to the new Regulation, in addition to names and surnames, dates, total daily working hours, fieldwork hours, standby hours, and absence time, the employer must also record the start and end of work, work interruptions, and special data on working hours that affect the realization of certain rights from the employment relationship or in connection with the employment relationship (night work hours, overtime work, shift work, split shifts, work on holidays or non-working days established by law, etc.).
