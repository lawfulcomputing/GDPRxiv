In the context of the increasingly present artificial intelligence, a key question is how to ensure that technology serves humanity, and not the other way around. With this goal in mind, the Personal Data Protection Agency organized a free workshop on the impact assessment of high-risk AI systems on fundamental rights, as prescribed by Article 27 of the AI Regulation, on June 13, 2025.

"With the entry into force of the AI Regulation, the General Data Protection Regulation does not lose its importance – on the contrary, the Regulation further emphasizes the crucial role of personal data protection. These regulations do not limit innovation; rather, they encourage it, creating safe and reliable foundations for the development of artificial intelligence that brings maximum benefits to society while simultaneously reducing risks," said the Director of the Agency and Vice President of the European Data Protection Board, Zdravko Vukić, in his opening speech, emphasizing that the Agency has been appointed as one of the competent authorities for overseeing and enforcing obligations related to the protection of fundamental rights in the use of high-risk AI systems. "In the context of the use of artificial intelligence and the processing of personal data, we emphasize that the Agency will not hesitate to use its new powers when necessary to protect individuals' rights."

During the workshop, Anamarija Mladinić, Head of the Sector for EU, International Cooperation, and Legal Affairs, delivered a lecture on the topic "AI Regulation: Risk-Based Approach and Relationship with GDPR," which introduced participants to the regulatory framework and obligations arising from it. One of the obligations is Article 27 of the AI Regulation, which stipulates that before using high-risk artificial intelligence systems, the potential impact on human rights and freedoms must be assessed. The aim is to protect citizens from discrimination, unfair automated decision-making, and privacy infringements.

Due to the challenges this obligation presents, particularly for public authorities, the Agency provided concrete support to obligations from both the public and private sectors through the workshop, demonstrating how impact assessment looks in practice. The workshop was led by Professor Alessandro Mantelero from the Politecnico di Torino, who developed the methodology for conducting Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA) in collaboration with partners and the Catalan data protection supervisory authority. Professor Mantelero also presented specific examples from practice where the FRIA model was used, highlighting the importance of impact assessment in the early stages of AI system development to identify risks and ensure the protection of human rights.

The methodology for Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA) represents a practical tool for identifying risks and determining measures that organizations need to take to protect human rights. The use of this methodology is recommended for all organizations developing or implementing high-risk AI systems, whether in the public or private sector, as it helps ensure the safety and ethics of technology from the early stages of the project cycle, while respecting the fundamental rights of each individual.

Additionally, participants had the opportunity to hear presentations of case studies prepared by Stefan Martinić (Law Office Stefan Martinić), Dr. Natalija Parlov Una (Apicura CERT), Ivan Ivanković (TrustPath), and Marko Đuričić (Visage Technologies), facilitating the exchange of experiences and showcasing different approaches to the application of the FRIA model.

At the very end of the workshop, through group work, all participants had the opportunity to apply the methodology under the expert guidance of mentors, which will undoubtedly contribute to their understanding and successful application of FRIA in practice.
