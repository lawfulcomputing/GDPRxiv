Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council of June 13, 2024, on establishing harmonized rules on artificial intelligence and amending Regulations (EC) No. 300/2008, (EU) No. 167/2013, (EU) No. 168/2013, (EU) 2018/858, (EU) 2018/1139, and (EU) 2019/2144, as well as Directives 2014/90/EU, (EU) 2016/797, and (EU) 2020/1828, known as the Artificial Intelligence Act, has introduced new challenges in the field of artificial intelligence development, as well as in the area of personal data protection.

The Artificial Intelligence Regulation entered into force on August 1, 2024, and its introductory and final provisions foresee a gradual application of certain articles. In accordance with Article 113 of the Artificial Intelligence Regulation, Chapters I and II began to apply on February 2, 2025.

The Artificial Intelligence Regulation establishes harmonized rules for the placing on the market, putting into use, and utilization of artificial intelligence in accordance with the new legislative framework and requires market oversight in terms of Regulation (EU) 2019/10202.

As stated in Article 1, paragraph 1 of the Artificial Intelligence Regulation, this Regulation aims to improve the functioning of the internal market and support innovation while promoting the application of human-centric and trustworthy artificial intelligence and ensuring health, safety, and a high level of protection of fundamental rights contained in the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union (hereinafter referred to as the "Charter"), particularly regarding fundamental rights to privacy and personal data protection (Articles 7 and 8 of the Charter).

Article 3, paragraph 1 of the Artificial Intelligence Regulation defines an artificial intelligence system as “a machine system designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy and which, after deployment, can demonstrate adaptability and which, for explicit or implicit goals, infers from the input values it receives how to generate output values such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that may affect physical or virtual environments.”

This definition encompasses seven main elements: (1) a machine-based system; (2) designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy; (3) that can demonstrate adaptability after implementation; (4) and which, for explicit or implicit goals; (5) infers, based on the inputs it receives, how to generate outputs (6) such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions (7) that may affect physical or virtual environments.

The definition of an artificial intelligence system adopts a lifecycle-based perspective that encompasses two main phases: the pre-implementation or ‘building’ phase of the system and the post-implementation or ‘use’ phase of the system. The seven elements listed in this definition do not need to be continuously present throughout both phases of that lifecycle. Instead, the definition recognizes that certain elements may appear in one phase but may not exist in both phases. This approach to defining an artificial intelligence system reflects the complexity and diversity of these systems, ensuring that the definition aligns with the objectives of the Artificial Intelligence Regulation by adapting to a wide range of AI systems.

The Artificial Intelligence Regulation and the General Data Protection Regulation should be regarded as complementary instruments, that is, instruments that mutually complement each other. It is important to emphasize that EU data protection law fully applies to the processing of personal data involved in the lifecycle of artificial intelligence systems, as explicitly stated in Article 2, paragraph 7 of the Artificial Intelligence Regulation (see also recitals 9 and 10).

The General Data Protection Regulation and the Artificial Intelligence Regulation are closely related legal norms, with compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation being a fundamental prerequisite for the development of ethical, lawful, and human-centric artificial intelligence, which is in accordance with fundamental rights, democratic principles, and the rule of law.

These regulations do not pose an obstacle to innovation but rather provide a regulatory framework that enables the development of artificial intelligence in a manner that maximizes social benefit while minimizing risks.

Since May 25, 2018, the General Data Protection Regulation has been fully applicable to the processing of personal data that is entirely automated and to non-automated processing of personal data that forms part of a filing system or is intended to be part of a filing system. However, in practice, it has been observed that a large number of organizations are unaware of this obligation and its implications for the development and use of artificial intelligence. We emphasize that the General Data Protection Regulation fully applies to the automated processing of personal data, regardless of whether the personal data is stored or not.

The Personal Data Protection Agency, as an independent supervisory authority overseeing the implementation of the General Data Protection Regulation, particularly emphasizes that any processing of personal data, including the processing of personal data in artificial intelligence systems, at all stages of development, testing, and use of artificial intelligence must comply with the General Data Protection Regulation. Organizations that develop or apply artificial intelligence systems that process personal data are obliged to ensure full compliance of such processing with the relevant provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation.

Given the legal nature and far-reaching effects of this regulation on the protection of personal data and the privacy of individuals, we draw the attention of stakeholders developing or enabling the use of artificial intelligence systems to their obligation to comply with the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation at all stages of the lifecycle of such systems when personal data is processed.

We particularly emphasize the importance of compliance with Articles 5, 6, 9, and 32 of the General Data Protection Regulation, where it is crucial to ensure that for each processing of personal data in artificial intelligence systems, an appropriate legal basis is identified and that appropriate technical and organizational measures for protection are established.

Chapter I covers Articles...
from 1 to 4, which regulate:

the subject of the Regulation on Artificial Intelligence, or its purpose,
the scope of application, or the subjects that are obliged to meet the requirements of the Regulation,
definitions[3],
literacy in the field of artificial intelligence.

In addition to the obligation to determine the applicability of the Regulation on Artificial Intelligence to business activities, it is necessary to take measures to ensure awareness and education regarding regulatory requirements in the field of artificial intelligence. 
When implementing education and awareness-raising activities, it is essential to consider the technical expertise, prior experience, educational level, and degree of competence of the participants, as well as the specific context of the application of artificial intelligence systems. 
Chapter II.[4] defines prohibited practices in the field of artificial intelligence, which include, among others:

Social scoring
Biometric categorization
Use of manipulative and deceptive techniques
Exploitation of vulnerabilities of certain individuals or groups
Assessment of the commission of a criminal offense
Creation/expansion of a database for facial recognition
Emotion recognition in the workplace and in education

It also establishes strict conditions under which systems for remote biometric identification in real-time may be used in public places. 
Considering the gradual commencement of application, attention must be paid to the following dates:

The Regulation on Artificial Intelligence applies from August 2026.
The Artificial Intelligence Office at the European Commission must submit the Code of Practice for general-purpose artificial intelligence models by May 2025.
The commencement of application of the following provisions occurs from August 2025:

Chapter III, Section 4 titled "Bodies that carry out registration and registered bodies" (Articles 28 to 39)
Chapter V "General-purpose artificial intelligence models" (Articles 51 to 56)
Chapter VII "Governance" (Articles 64 to 70)
Article 78 "Confidentiality"
Chapter XII "Sanctions" (Articles 99 to 100)[5]

The commencement of application of Article 6, paragraph 1 "Rules on the classification of AI systems as high-risk" and the corresponding obligations from the Regulation applies from August 2, 2027.
