The report “AI Privacy Risks & Mitigations – Large Language Models (LLMs)” presents a comprehensive risk management methodology for large language model (LLM) systems, along with a range of practical measures to mitigate the most common privacy risks in these systems.  
Additionally, the report includes examples of applying the risk management framework in real scenarios:

1. First example: virtual assistant (chatbot) for customer inquiries,

2. Second example: LLM system for monitoring and supporting the progress of students,

3. Third example: AI assistant for travel planning and scheduling.

Large language models (LLMs) represent a significant advancement in the field of artificial intelligence. These are machine learning models designed to process and generate human-like language, trained on extensive datasets. Their applications are diverse: from generating and summarizing text, to assisting in programming, to sentiment analysis and other functions.  
The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) initiated this project under the Support Pool of Experts program, at the request of the Personal Data Protection Agency of the Republic of Croatia (AZOP).  
The report “AI Privacy Risks & Mitigations – LLMs” provides a comprehensive methodology for the systematic identification, assessment, and mitigation of privacy and data protection risks.  
The report helps data protection supervisory authorities gain a comprehensive understanding of the functioning of large language models and the associated risks.  
Isabel Barberá, the author of the report and an external expert within the European Data Protection Board program, will present a comprehensive risk management methodology for large language model (LLM) systems during the webinar, which includes a range of practical measures to mitigate the most common risks and will answer participants' questions.  
Objective of the webinar:  
To support development engineers and users of LLM systems in the systematic identification, assessment, and mitigation of privacy and data protection risks, fully in accordance with: Article 25 of the General Data Protection Regulation (Privacy by design and by default) and Article 32 (Security of processing). The methodology to be presented is not a substitute for a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) under Article 35 of the General Data Protection Regulation, but rather a complementary tool, specifically focused on risks associated with large language models.  
Webinar participants will have the opportunity to deepen their understanding of the architecture of LLM systems and data flows, as well as gain knowledge about the criteria for identifying and assessing risks and specific measures for their mitigation.
