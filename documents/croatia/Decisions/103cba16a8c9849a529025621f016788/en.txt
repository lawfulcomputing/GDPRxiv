Brussels, January 20 – The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) has adopted the Report on the Implementation of the Right of Access for Data Subjects. The report summarizes the outcomes of a series of coordinated national measures carried out in 2024 based on the Coordinated Enforcement Framework (CEF). It outlines the issues identified for certain data controllers, along with a series of recommendations to assist them in implementing the right of access. A central element of this initiative is to determine how familiar data controllers are with the EDPB Guidelines 01/2022 on Data Subject Rights – Right of Access and whether they have applied these guidelines in practice.

Zdravko Vukíc, Vice-President of the European Data Protection Board, stated: “The coordinated enforcement action is a valuable initiative that helps strengthen cooperation among data protection authorities: by addressing selected topics in a harmonized manner, greater efficiency and consistency are achieved. The way data controllers implement the right of access is at the heart of data protection and is one of the most frequently exercised rights by data subjects.”

The Personal Data Protection Agency also participated in the EDPB's coordinated action. Based on insights gained from the conducted research, the Agency will carry out a campaign in 2025 to raise awareness and knowledge among data controllers on how to facilitate data subjects in exercising their key rights. This right is fundamental to transparency and enables individuals to obtain information about how their personal data is processed, who processes it, and for what purposes.

The Agency also notes that data subjects are often unaware of this right, meaning they have the right to request from any organization that processes their personal data all information under Article 15(1) of the General Data Protection Regulation, as well as a copy of their personal data. 

The Agency urges all citizens to inform themselves about their rights: https://azop.hr/prava-ispitanika/, https://azop.hr/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Gradani-upoznajte-svoja-prava-8.pdf, because without citizens who know their rights and actively work to exercise and protect them, the regulation of fundamental rights alone will not ensure a free, safe, and advanced society at times when technology accelerates and drives social changes in entirely new directions.

The Agency particularly calls on all data controllers to inform themselves about their obligations: https://arc-rec-project.eu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pravo-ispitanika-na-pristup-osobnim-podacima.pdf; https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-04/edpb_guidelines_202201_data_subject_rights_access_v2_hr.pdf.

It is important to highlight that according to Article 15, a data subject has the right to obtain from the data controller confirmation as to whether personal data concerning them is being processed, and if so, access to the personal data and the following information:
(a) the purposes of the processing;
(b) the categories of personal data concerned;
(c) the recipients or categories of recipients to whom the personal data have been or will be disclosed, particularly recipients in third countries or international organizations;
(d) where possible, the envisaged period for which the personal data will be stored or, if not possible, the criteria used to determine that period;
(e) the existence of the right to request from the data controller rectification or erasure of personal data or restriction of processing of personal data concerning the data subject, or the right to object to such processing;
(f) the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority;
(g) if the personal data are not collected from the data subject, any available information as to their source;
(h) the existence of automated decision-making, including profiling under Article 22(1) and (4), and at least in those cases, meaningful information about the logic involved, as well as the significance and the envisaged consequences of such processing for the data subject.

If a data subject requests a copy of their personal data from the data controller, the data controller is obliged to provide the data subject with a copy of their personal data free of charge. For any additional copies requested by the data subject, the data controller may charge a reasonable fee based on administrative costs. If the data subject submits a request electronically and unless the data subject requests otherwise, the information shall be provided in a commonly used electronic format.

During 2024, 30 data protection authorities across Europe launched coordinated investigations into the compliance of data controllers with the right of access by initiating official investigations, assessing the justification for official investigations, and/or implementing fact-finding procedures. A total of 1185 data controllers (small and medium-sized enterprises, large enterprises active in various industries and sectors, and public authorities) participated in the coordinated enforcement action.

Areas for Improvement and Main Challenges
The results indicate that data controllers need more awareness of the Guidelines 01/2022 on the right of access, both at the national level and at the EU level, as the guidelines assist data controllers in implementing the right of access, explain how to facilitate the exercise of that right, and outline exceptions and limitations to the right of access.

As a result of the CEF's actions for 2024, seven challenges have been identified. One of them is the lack of documented internal procedures for handling access requests. Additionally, inconsistent and excessive interpretations of the limitations of the right of access have been observed, such as an over-reliance on certain exceptions to automatically deny access requests. Other examples of barriers that individuals may encounter when exercising their right of access include formal requests or demands to provide personal data to an excessive degree (for example, copies of identity cards). For each identified challenge, the report provides a list of non-binding recommendations that data controllers and data protection authorities should consider.

**Discussion on Access Rights**

The discussion regarding the right of access has shifted from "average" to "high." An important factor identified as influencing the level of compliance was the number of access requests received by data controllers, as well as the size of the organization. Specifically, larger data controllers or those receiving more requests are more likely to achieve a higher level of compliance than smaller organizations with fewer resources.

**Positive Outcomes Observed Across Europe**

Positive results have been observed throughout Europe. This includes the implementation of best practices by data controllers, such as user-friendly online forms that enable individuals to easily submit access requests, as well as self-service systems that allow individuals to independently download their personal data with just a few clicks and at any time.

**Context and Next Steps**

The CEF is a key measure of the European Data Protection Board within its strategy for the period 2024-2027, aimed at simplifying implementation and cooperation among data protection authorities. 

The results of these national measures are consolidated and analyzed together to gain better insight into this topic and enable targeted monitoring at both the national and EU levels. 

The European Data Protection Board published a report in 2023 on its first coordinated action regarding the use of cloud services in the public sector. 

In 2024, the EDPB also published a report on the outcome of the second coordinated action concerning the appointment and position of data protection officers. 

The EDPB's coordinated enforcement action for 2025 will focus on the implementation of the right to erasure.
