The Personal Data Protection Agency has imposed
administrative monetary fines on data controllers totaling
nine in number, amounting to 51,000 euros for violations
of the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation
and the Law on the Implementation of the General Data
Protection Regulation.

**Administrative monetary fines for violations of the
provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation**
Two administrative monetary fines were imposed on data
controllers in the gambling and betting sector in the
amounts of 15,000 and 20,000 euros for unlawful processing
of personal data through cookies. The data controllers
collected and processed personal data of respondents via
cookies without allowing respondents to provide or withdraw
consent for the processing of personal data through cookies
in an informed and voluntary manner (i.e., cookies – small
files that the Internet browser stores on a computer,
mobile device, or any other device that the respondent used
to visit the websites, thereby remembering and tracking
their further actions on the websites, which processing
also relates to aspects of personal data), thereby violating
Article 6, paragraph 1, point a) and, in connection with
that, Article 7 of the General Data Protection Regulation.
In situations where the processing of personal data is
based on consent and has multiple purposes, the text of
the consent (in this case, the cookie banner) must be
presented in a way that can be clearly distinguished from
other purposes, in a comprehensible and easily accessible
form using clear and simple language. Since in this case
the data controllers did not separate the so-called cookie
banner and did not allow respondents to clearly give
consent for different purposes (marketing, analytics/statistics), it is clear that the consent did not meet the
legal requirements and therefore was not valid as a legal
basis.
Upon reviewing the Privacy Policies of both data
controllers, it was determined that the document in
question does not contain information about the legal
basis, groups/types of cookies, the function/purpose of
each cookie, the retention period of cookies, i.e., the
data controllers did not adequately inform respondents
about the processing of personal data, thereby violating
Article 13, paragraphs 1 and 2 of the General Data
Protection Regulation. Thus, regarding the processing
through cookies, the data controllers did not inform
respondents in accordance with the principle of
transparency, and in this way, respondents (visitors to
the websites) were deprived of information about the
processing of their data.
Additionally, the data controller who was fined 20,000
euros processed personal data of respondents at the very
moment the webpage was loaded, while they had not yet
given their consent for the collection of certain cookies,
which was unfair, as respondents were not even aware that
their personal data was being collected at the moment of
accessing the webpage. This resulted in unfair processing
of personal data of respondents, which is contrary to the
principle of lawful, fair, and transparent processing of
personal data as stated in Article 5, paragraph 1 of the
General Data Protection Regulation.

**Administrative monetary fines for violations of the Law
on the Implementation of the General Data Protection
Regulation**
Seven administrative monetary fines totaling 16,000
euros were imposed on data controllers for failing to
mark the object under video surveillance, i.e., the
marking is not visible upon entering the recording
perimeter and/or the marking does not contain all
relevant information. Individual fines ranging from 500
euros to 4,000 euros were imposed on hotels, catering
establishments, and stores.
Namely, according to Article 27, paragraph 1 of the Law
on the Implementation of the General Data Protection
Regulation, the data controller is obliged to mark that
the object or individual room within it and the external
surface of the object is under video surveillance, and the
marking must be visible no later than upon entering the
recording perimeter.
Paragraph 2 of the aforementioned article stipulates that
the notification must contain all relevant information in
accordance with the provision of Article 13 of the General
Data Protection Regulation, and especially a simple and
easily understandable image along with text providing
respondents with the following information:
- that the area is under video surveillance
- information about the data controller
- contact details through which the respondent can
exercise their rights
