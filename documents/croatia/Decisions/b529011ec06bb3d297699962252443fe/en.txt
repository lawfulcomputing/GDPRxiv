The principles of personal data processing are at the very core of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Article 5 of the General Data Protection Regulation stipulates seven key principles related to the processing of personal data, which data controllers and processors must adhere to when processing personal data:

1. Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency;  
2. Purpose limitation;  
3. Data minimization;  
4. Accuracy;  
5. Storage limitation;  
6. Integrity and confidentiality;  
7. Accountability.  

The aforementioned principles of personal data processing are the starting point for compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation. They represent fundamental comprehensive principles aimed at ensuring compliance with personal data protection regulations and protecting the rights of individuals (data subjects). Understanding the principles of personal data processing is crucial for understanding the obligations under the General Data Protection Regulation.

Before any processing of personal data, companies (data controllers) must ask themselves whether such processing of personal data is lawful, fair, transparent, necessary for achieving a specific purpose, whether the personal data held by the company is accurate, and whether it is retained only as long as necessary to fulfill a specific purpose, and whether appropriate measures have been taken to ensure a level of security for personal data that is appropriate to the risk.

The main outcome of this project is the web tool Olivia, which contains 15 GDPR courses, theoretical and practical modules that can help you understand the obligations under the GDPR and in drafting basic documents that will allow you to demonstrate compliance with the GDPR. Olivia is also an excellent tool for raising awareness about personal data protection within the organization, as it includes knowledge tests through which employees can test their knowledge, and if they achieve at least 80% correct answers, Olivia generates a certificate of successful completion of the module. We remind you that one of the most important organizational measures for the protection of personal data is regular employee training and raising awareness about personal data protection.
