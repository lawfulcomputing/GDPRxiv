**January 16, 2025** – At the plenary session of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) held in Zagreb, chaired by the Vice-President of the European Data Protection Board and Director of the Personal Data Protection Agency, Zdravko Vukić, the European Data Protection Board adopted Guidelines on Pseudonymization, as well as a statement on the interaction between competition law and data protection.

Vice-President of the European Data Protection Board Zdravko Vukić stated: “With the development of business models, the need for personal data protection is becoming increasingly important. The European Data Protection Board promotes compliance among separate, yet interrelated areas of regulation to ensure the best possible protection for individuals. To this end, we will continue to collaborate with competition authorities to strengthen the ability of data protection authorities to consider the economic context and the ability of competition authorities to include data protection issues in their assessments and decisions.”

The General Data Protection Regulation introduces the concept of “pseudonymization”* and refers to it as a protective measure that may be appropriate and effective for fulfilling obligations regarding data protection. The EDPB, in its guidelines, clarifies the definition and applicability of pseudonymization and pseudonymized data, as well as the advantages of pseudonymization.

The guidelines contain two important legal clarifications:
1. Pseudonymized data that could be attributed to an individual using additional information remains information related to a natural person whose identity can be determined and is therefore still personal data. Namely, if the data controller or someone else can link the data to an individual, they remain personal data.
2. Pseudonymization can reduce risks and facilitate the use of legitimate interests as a legal basis (Article 6(1)(f) of the General Data Protection Regulation), provided that all other requirements of the General Data Protection Regulation are met. Likewise, pseudonymization can help ensure compliance with the original purpose (Article 6(4) of the General Data Protection Regulation).

The guidelines also explain how pseudonymization can assist organizations in fulfilling their obligations related to the implementation of data protection principles (Article 5 of the General Data Protection Regulation), technical and integrated data protection (Article 25 of the General Data Protection Regulation), and security (Article 32 of the General Data Protection Regulation).

Finally, the guidelines analyze technical measures and protective measures in the application of pseudonymization to ensure confidentiality and prevent unauthorized identification of individuals.

The guidelines will be subject to public consultation until February 28, 2025.

During the plenary session, the European Data Protection Board also adopted a position paper on the interaction between data protection law and competition law.

In the ruling of the EU Court in Meta against the Bundeskartellamt dated July 4, 2023, it is clearly stated that data protection authorities and competition authorities must cooperate, in some cases, to achieve effective and coordinated enforcement of data protection and competition law.

Although these are separate legal areas aimed at achieving different objectives in different frameworks, in some cases, they may apply to the same subjects. Therefore, it is important to assess situations where the laws may overlap.

In the document in question, the European Data Protection Board explains the interaction between data protection and competition law. It proposes steps for incorporating market and competition factors into data protection practices and for considering data protection rules in competition assessments. It also provides recommendations for improving cooperation among regulatory bodies; for example, competent authorities should consider establishing a single point of contact to manage coordination with other regulatory bodies.

*“Pseudonymization” is defined in Article 4(5) of the General Data Protection Regulation as “the processing of personal data in such a manner that the personal data can no longer be attributed to a specific data subject without the use of additional information, provided that such additional information is kept separately and is subject to technical and organizational measures to ensure that the personal data cannot be attributed to an identified or identifiable natural person.”
