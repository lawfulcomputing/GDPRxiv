In order to raise public awareness about the fundamental human right – the right to personal data protection and privacy, the Council of Europe, with the support of the European Commission, declared January 28 as the European Data Protection Day in 2006, which is also known worldwide as Privacy Day.  
This year's European Data Protection Day is dedicated by the Agency to data protection officers – key individuals in the personal data protection system. Aware of the importance of the role of data protection officers, as well as the various uncertainties they regularly face, the Agency is organizing a GDPR conference on January 27 titled “Data Protection Officer – The Job of the Future,” which will gather experts and data protection officers from the public, financial, and IT sectors who will share their experiences regarding their roles, challenges, and solutions in implementing the General Data Protection Regulation.  
In the introductory part of the conference, speeches will be delivered by high-ranking state officials, Mr. Wojciech Wiewiórowski, European Data Protection Supervisor, and Ms. Anna Hänninen from the Finnish data protection supervisory authority, which is the coordinator of the initiative of the European Data Protection Board focused on the appointment and role of data protection officers.  
Additionally, the conference will be an excellent opportunity to familiarize participants with the Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on the European Health Data Space and the new digital regulations – the Digital Markets Act (DMA), the Digital Services Act (DSA), the Data Governance Act (DGA), and the Artificial Intelligence Act.  
Furthermore, one of the topics of the panel discussion will be the new directive of the Council of the European Union on the security of network and information systems (NIS 2), which came into effect on January 16, 2023. Along with the NIS 2 Directive, the CER Directive – Directive on Critical Entities Resilience was adopted. In addition to these two directives, DORA – the Digital Operational Resilience Act for the financial sector was also adopted. The new NIS 2 will also apply to financial institutions (a key sector – banking), with DORA being lex specialis. The NIS 2 Directive aims to ensure the resilience of critical entities regarding cybersecurity, while DORA aims to strengthen the security of financial entities.  
These regulations imply significant investments in cybersecurity and human resources, as well as a systematic approach to compliance, which will undoubtedly pose a challenge for organizations. How are these directives related to the General Data Protection Regulation and how will they affect the work of data protection officers in hospitals, banks, energy, and telecommunications sectors, and in state bodies that process personal data of all citizens of the Republic of Croatia?  
Find answers to these and other current questions at the conference on January 27, 2023, at the Westin in Zagreb.  
The aim of marking the European Data Protection Day is to raise awareness of the importance of data protection among all stakeholders and to improve the area of personal data protection in the Republic of Croatia. This is of exceptional significance for the Republic of Croatia in the context of preserving European democratic values, developing a unified digital market, the free flow of data between EU countries and third countries, economic growth, and cybersecurity.
