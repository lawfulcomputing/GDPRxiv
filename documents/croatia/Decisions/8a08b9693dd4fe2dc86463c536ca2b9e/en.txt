October 28, 2024. The project partners of the ARC II project (Awareness Raising Campaign for Small and Medium-sized Enterprises) held a hybrid final meeting in Florence, Italy. The ARC II project, co-financed under the European Union's Citizens, Equality, Rights and Values (CERV) Programme, is the result of a two-year collaboration. The project partners include:

1. Personal Data Protection Agency (project coordinator)
2. Italian Data Protection Authority (Garante per la protezione dei dati personali)
3. Faculty of Organization and Informatics, University of Zagreb
4. Vrije Universiteit Brussel
5. University of Florence

The main goal of the ARC II project was to support small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in their efforts to comply with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). In his opening speech, Zdravko Vukić, Director of the Personal Data Protection Agency, Vice-President of the European Data Protection Board, and member of the Management Board of the ARC II project, emphasized: “Why have we dedicated so much time and resources to SMEs? First in the ARCI project we conducted with the Irish Data Protection Authority, and then in the ARC II project, we have been investing a lot of effort and time for over 4 years to help SMEs understand and comply with their obligations arising from the GDPR. And this is not without reason. SMEs make up more than 99% of all businesses in the EU. Small and medium-sized enterprises play a key role in the European and Croatian economy. Micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises are the backbone of the economy as they constitute the majority of businesses in Europe. Even after more than six years of full application of the General Data Protection Regulation, a large number of them consider the General Data Protection Regulation to be a significant administrative and financial burden. Thanks to the activities we have carried out, small and medium-sized enterprises have not only had the opportunity to educate themselves for free on how to align their business processes with legal data protection regulations and achieve a higher level of compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation, but they have also realized significant financial savings.”

The aim of the final meeting was to present the main result of the ARC II project – the web tool Olivia, review the project results, determine the actions needed for the successful completion of the project, and exchange experiences and insights on the implementation of the GDPR in SMEs with stakeholders. The event was divided into two parts: the first in English and the second in Italian, dedicated to presenting Olivia to Italian SMEs. The meeting attracted a diverse group of participants, including representatives from the European Commission, the European Data Protection Board (EDPB), data protection authorities, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), and legal professionals. The main goal was to present the web tool Olivia, developed within the framework of the ARC II project. Olivia will be permanently and freely available to all interested stakeholders: https://olivia-gdpr-arc.eu/hr

Marianna Colonna from the European Data Protection Board presented a practical tool specifically designed for SMEs (the EDPB Guide for SMEs). This resource, available in 17 languages, can be found at https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme-data-protection-guide/home_en. Pavlina Peneva from the European Commission presented the EU program "Citizens, Equality, Rights and Values (CERV)." The call for project proposals CERV-2021-DATA is focused on two main priorities:

1. Facilitating SMEs in implementing obligations under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).
2. Raising awareness among the wider public about the General Data Protection Regulation.

Thanks to funding under the "Citizens, Equality, Rights and Values" program, data protection authorities across the European Union will have the opportunity to carry out activities aimed at SMEs and the wider public. The goal of these initiatives is to increase awareness and knowledge of the principles and practices of data protection. This collaborative approach, which combines the resources of EU institutions, national bodies, and academic partners, demonstrates coordinated efforts to strengthen data protection practices across Europe, with a particular focus on the needs of SMEs and increasing understanding of rights and responsibilities in the field of data protection among the wider public.

Pavlina Peneva also highlighted some recommendations from the second report of the European Commission on the General Data Protection Regulation:

- Further support for businesses' compliance efforts, especially SMEs.
- Clear and enforceable guidelines from data protection authorities.
- Data protection authorities should actively collaborate with organizations, especially SMEs.
- Adoption of the application of codes of conduct and certifications under the General Data Protection Regulation.

In accordance with the risk-based approach of the General Data Protection Regulation, compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation for SMEs conducting low-risk processing does not represent a significant burden. For example, SMEs engaged in low-risk processing can maintain a simplified record of processing activities based on templates developed by data protection authorities. Furthermore, such records should be considered a useful tool that enables entrepreneurs to analyze their processing activities. Olivia is an innovative platform specifically designed to facilitate compliance with the GDPR for small and medium-sized enterprises in Croatia and Italy. Olivia is currently available in Croatian, Italian, and English, tailored to the needs of Croatian and Italian SMEs. It consists of:
Theoretical part: small and medium-sized enterprises can familiarize themselves with the basics of the General Data Protection Regulation, data protection impact assessment, legal bases, principles of data protection, technical and organizational measures, privacy policies, and much more. Users can watch webinars, take tests, and upon achieving 80% or more points, receive a certificate of successful completion of the course.  
Practical part: SMEs can create key documents to demonstrate compliance, including privacy policies, records of processing activities, assessments of legitimate interests, data protection impact assessments, video surveillance policies, personal data protection policies, and information security.

Olivia has been developed in open source and allows all data protection authorities to adapt it to their national legislation and language. New languages and modules can be easily integrated, meaning that Olivia can be useful for SMEs and data controllers across the EU.
