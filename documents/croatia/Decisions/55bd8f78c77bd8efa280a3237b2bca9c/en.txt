Brussels, June 5 – During the last plenary session, the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) adopted the final version of the Guidelines on Article 48 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) regarding the transfer of data to authorities in third countries, following a public consultation. Additionally, the Board presented two new projects under the Support Pool of Experts (SPE) program, which include educational materials on artificial intelligence and data protection: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/support-pool-experts-projects/law-compliance-ai-security-data_en; https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/support-pool-experts-projects/fundamentals-secure-ai-systems-personal_en. The Board also discussed the European Commission's request for a joint opinion from the EDPB and the European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS) on the proposal to simplify the record-keeping obligation under the GDPR.

**Transfer of Data to Authorities in Third Countries**  
Following the public consultation, the EDPB adopted the final version of the guidelines on the transfer of personal data to authorities in third countries. In the guidelines, the EDPB clarifies Article 48 of the GDPR and provides instructions on how organizations can assess under what conditions they can lawfully respond to requests from authorities in third countries (i.e., authorities outside the European Economic Area). The EDPB explains that judgments and decisions from authorities in third countries cannot be automatically recognized or enforced in Europe. As a general rule, an international agreement can serve both as a legal basis and as a basis for data transfer. In cases where such an agreement does not exist or does not provide an adequate legal basis and safeguards, other legal bases or instruments for transfer may exceptionally be considered in specific cases. The amendments contained in the updated guidelines do not change their fundamental orientation but provide additional clarifications on issues highlighted during the public consultation. For example, the new guidelines elaborate on the situation where the recipient of the request is a data processor and further clarify the situation where a parent company from a third country receives a request from a local authority and then requests personal data from its subsidiary in Europe.

**Enhancing Knowledge and Skills in Artificial Intelligence and Data Protection**  
During the June plenary session, the EDPB presented two new projects under the Support Pool of Experts (SPE) program: Law & Compliance in AI Security and Data Protection and Fundamentals of Secure AI Systems with Personal Data. Both projects, initiated at the request of the Greek Data Protection Authority (HDPA), provide educational materials on artificial intelligence and data protection. The report Law & Compliance in AI Security & Data Protection is intended for Data Protection Officers (DPOs) and legal professionals in the field of personal data protection and privacy. The second report, Fundamentals of Secure AI Systems with Personal Data, is aimed at technical profile experts, such as cybersecurity specialists, software developers, and users of high-risk AI systems. The main goal of these projects is to address the expressed lack of expertise in the field of artificial intelligence and data protection, which is considered a key barrier to developing solutions that comply with the principles of personal data protection and privacy. The educational materials will assist in training professionals to create a more favorable environment for the implementation of data protection legislation. Given the rapid development of artificial intelligence, the EDPB also decided to launch a new innovative initiative: a one-year pilot project in the form of an open community document version that can be modified. The EDPB will collaborate with the authors of the reports to transfer the content to its Git repository, allowing external collaborators (with an open account and under the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike license) to propose changes and leave comments.

**Simplification of the Record-Keeping Obligation under the GDPR**  
The Board discussed the European Commission's request for a joint opinion from the EDPB and the EDPS on the proposal to simplify the record-keeping obligation for micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises and organizations with fewer than 750 employees. The proposal includes a targeted amendment to Article 30(5) of the GDPR. The EDPB and EDPS will deliver their joint opinion within eight weeks.
