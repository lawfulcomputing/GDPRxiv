Director of the Agency and Vice-President of the European Data Protection Board Zdravko Vukić participated in the IV International Congress of Lawyers, which took place from March 25 to March 29, 2025, in Bled. This year's focus of the conference was the protection of personal data and artificial intelligence, on which Zdravko Vukić spoke as Vice-President of the EDPB. In his opening speech, he emphasized that the processing of personal data in AI systems—especially in large language models—is at the center of the work of the European Data Protection Board. “With the entry into force of the Artificial Intelligence Regulation, the GDPR does not lose its importance—on the contrary, the Regulation further emphasizes the key role of personal data protection. The Artificial Intelligence Regulation clearly states that the right to privacy and the security of personal data must be guaranteed to individuals throughout the entire lifecycle of artificial intelligence systems. Compliance with the GDPR is a fundamental prerequisite for the development of artificial intelligence that is ethical, lawful, and human-centered, respecting human rights, democracy, and the rule of law. These regulations do not limit innovation; rather, they encourage it, creating safe and reliable foundations for the development of artificial intelligence that brings maximum benefits to society while simultaneously reducing risks,” said Vice-President Vukić.

On the second day, a panel discussion titled “Protection of Personal Data and Artificial Intelligence in the Region” was held, which once again brought together leaders of data protection supervisory authorities from the region. Alongside Director and Vice-President Zdravko Vukić, participants included Jelena Virant Burnik, Commissioner for Information of the Republic of Slovenia, Milan Marinović, Commissioner for Information of Public Interest and Protection of Personal Data of the Republic of Serbia, and Dragoljub Reljić, Director of the Agency for Personal Data Protection in Bosnia and Herzegovina.

On this occasion, Director Vukić spoke, among other things, about the challenges in regulating artificial intelligence and emphasized that the greatest challenge for supervisory authorities and organizations will be the lack of human and financial resources. “Supervisory authorities across the EU are chronically lacking IT experts who earn significantly more in the private sector, and supervisory authorities cannot compete in terms of salaries. For the regulation of artificial intelligence to be effective, supervisory authorities will need to receive significant financial resources to be able to employ experts in the field of artificial intelligence,” emphasized Director Vukić, adding that the Agency is ready to take on the supervisory role in accordance with Article 70 of the Artificial Intelligence Act. “The Agency has extensive experience in assessing the impact on human rights, implementing supervisory activities and educational initiatives, and imposing penalties.”
