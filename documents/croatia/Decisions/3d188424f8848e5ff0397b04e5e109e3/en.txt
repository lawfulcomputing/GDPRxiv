For months, you have wanted sneakers that are never on sale, and you have just come across an advertisement on social media for an online store offering those very sneakers at half price as part of a "Black Friday" sale. You happily open the ad on social media, which redirects you to what appears to be a legitimate online store of a well-known brand, where the desired sneakers are displayed. You select your size, add them to your cart, and then enter your personal delivery information and credit card details. You happily complete the purchase, and a message appears on your screen saying, "Your order has been successfully received." However, you soon notice that a larger sum of money has been withdrawn from your account by an unknown recipient. You think about where you have left your credit card information and revisit the online store through which you purchased the sneakers, only to realize that the site is fake. Although you thought this could never happen to you, you have become a victim of online fraud.

These days, we are exposed to advertisements from various merchants offering significant discounts and sales ahead of Black Friday, among which are fake online stores imitating well-known fashion brands. CARNET's National CERT has established that there are over 6000 fake online stores aimed at stealing personal data and money from citizens of the Republic of Croatia!

Although Black Friday and Cyber Monday present an opportunity for many citizens to shop at lower prices, for fraudsters, it is an ideal opportunity for scams through fake online stores and/or fraudulent messages. 

To avoid becoming a victim of online fraud when shopping online, follow these tips:

1. Beware of "too good to be true offers" on unverified online shops. Be cautious if the prices of products are extremely low and research the online store before ordering.
2. Shop only through verified online stores. Before purchasing, check the online store via the link https://iffy.cert.hr/ from the CERT iffy service developed by CARNET's National CERT or through Scamadviser at the link https://www.scamadviser.com/
3. Check the link of the online store in the address bar, specifically if the address starts with https:// which indicates secure communication between you and the merchant.
4. Verify that the website lists the name and headquarters, phone number, and email address of the merchant. Every online store must have these details listed.
5. Pay attention to grammar and spelling; fake websites are often full of errors.
6. If you are paying by card, only use secure payment service providers when making a purchase.
7. Pay only when you are connected to a secure connection – avoid free or open WiFi.
8. Do not share personal information that is not necessary for the purchase, such as copies of personal documents.

In addition to fake online stores, fraudsters often use delivery services, or present themselves as such, to obtain money and personal data from citizens. Below are several examples of fake messages in which attackers aim to prompt you to send money and provide your personal information:

- An email/SMS informing you that a package is waiting for delivery, and in order for it to be delivered, you need to pay a delivery fee of a few cents via a link. Clicking on the link will open a fake delivery service page, which will usually display a message stating that delivery is not possible and that payment is required; otherwise, the shipment will be returned to the sender.
- An email/SMS informing you that your package will soon be returned to the sender because the address is incorrect or missing, and you are asked to click on a link. Clicking on the link will open a fake delivery service page, which will state that you need to pay a postage fee within a certain timeframe; otherwise, the shipment will be returned to the sender.
- An email/SMS informing you that your package cannot be delivered until you pay customs duties and/or VAT, and you are asked to click on a link. Clicking on the link will open a fake delivery service page, which will state that you need to pay a fee in order to receive your shipment.

In all the mentioned examples, the goal is generally the same – to enter credit card details so that fraudsters can withdraw money from your account. If you are expecting a package and receive a message of similar content, pay attention to the following:

- Check the sender, i.e., the domain. Delivery services do not send emails from domains like hotmail, gmail, etc.
- If you received a link in the message, check if the link matches the official domain of the delivery service.
- Pay attention to grammar and spelling in the received message; such messages are often full of errors.
- Is an urgent response requested from you (e.g., confirm your address within 12 hours...)? Delete such messages immediately.
- Pay attention to how the sender addresses you; for example, a Croatian delivery service will not address you in English and will likely not wish you a happy life.

Protecting personal data primarily starts with ourselves, so remember:
Be careful where you leave and enter your personal information and credit card details; do not send copies of ID cards/credit cards, card PINs, CVC codes from cards (the last three digits on the back of the card), or codes generated by mobile banking. 
Also, keep in mind that only the IBAN/account number and the name and surname of the account holder are needed for a payment to an account, not the details from your card (card number, expiration date, and CVC code)!
