Brussels, October 9 – The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) adopted at its latest plenary session an Opinion on certain obligations arising from the relationship between controllers, processors, and sub-processors, Guidelines on legitimate interest, a statement on establishing additional procedural rules for the implementation of the General Data Protection Regulation, and the EDPB Work Programme for the period 2024-2025. Additionally, the request from the Agency for Information and Privacy of Kosovo to become an observer in the activities of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) was also adopted.

Full support for granting observer status to the supervisory authority of Kosovo was provided by the Director of the Agency for Personal Data Protection and Vice-Chair of the European Data Protection Board, Zdravko Vukić. He emphasized that the Agency for Information and Privacy of Kosovo meets all prescribed criteria, that the Agency operates independently, and that it is in the interest of the Board to have the supervisory authority of Kosovo as an observer, noting that Kosovo is a potential candidate for EU membership which has undertaken binding international obligations to fully align its data protection rules with those in the EU.

"One of the priorities of the Agency for Personal Data Protection is to improve data protection standards in the Western Balkans in line with EU standards. We have made significant efforts in North Macedonia and are willing to do the same in Kosovo. Aligning personal data protection standards in the Western Balkans with EU standards is important for the entire EU and EDPB members. I believe that granting observer status to Kosovo will significantly contribute to this goal and further improve data protection practices in the region," said EDPB Vice-Chair Zdravko Vukić, highlighting that the Agency for Personal Data Protection has successfully collaborated with the Agency for Information and Privacy so far.

The European Data Protection Board adopted Guidelines on the processing of personal data based on legitimate interest, in which the Agency for Personal Data Protection also participated in the drafting team. 

These Guidelines analyze the criteria established in Article 6(1)(f) of the General Data Protection Regulation that controllers must meet in order to lawfully process personal data based on legitimate interest. The recent ruling of the Court of Justice of the European Union on this matter (C-621/22, October 4, 2024) is also taken into account.

In order to rely on legitimate interest, the controller must meet three cumulative conditions:
1. the realization of a legitimate interest of the controller or a third party;
2. the necessity of processing personal data for the purpose of realizing the legitimate interest;
3. the interests or fundamental freedoms and rights of individuals do not override the legitimate interests of the controller or a third party (balancing exercise).

First and foremost, only lawful, clearly and precisely formulated, actual, and present interests can be considered legitimate. For example, such legitimate interests could exist in a situation where an individual is a client or in the service of the controller. Secondly, if there are reasonable, equally effective, but less intrusive alternatives to achieve the desired interests, the processing cannot be considered necessary. The necessity of processing should also be examined in accordance with the principle of data minimization. Thirdly, the controller must ensure that its legitimate interest does not prevail over the interests of the individual, fundamental rights, and freedoms. In this balancing exercise, the controller must take into account the interests of individuals, the impact of the processing, and their reasonable expectations, as well as the existence of additional protective measures that could limit the impact on the individual. Furthermore, these Guidelines explain how this assessment should be conducted in practice, including in a range of specific contexts such as fraud prevention, direct marketing, and information security. The document also clarifies the relationship between this legal basis and a range of rights of data subjects in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation.

The Guidelines will be subject to public consultation until November 20, 2024.

The European Data Protection Board also adopted an Opinion on certain obligations arising from the relationship between controllers, processors, and sub-processors, based on a request from the Danish Data Protection Authority sent to the Board in accordance with Article 64(2) of the General Data Protection Regulation. The Opinion relates to the interpretation of certain duties of controllers relying on processors and sub-processors, as well as the text of the contract between the controller and the processor. The Opinion explains that controllers should always have easily accessible information about the identity (i.e., name, address, contact person) of all processors, sub-processors, etc., in order to best fulfill their obligations under Article 28 of the General Data Protection Regulation. Additionally, the obligation of the controller to verify whether there are "sufficient guarantees" from the sub-processor should apply regardless of the risk to the rights and freedoms of data subjects, although the extent of such verification may vary, particularly based on the risks associated with the processing. The Opinion also states that, while the initial processor should ensure that it proposes sub-processors with sufficient guarantees, the final decision and responsibility for engaging a specific sub-processor still rests with the controller.

The European Data Protection Board considers that under the General Data Protection Regulation, the controller is not obliged to systematically seek to verify through sub-processing contracts whether data protection obligations have been passed down the processing chain. The controller should assess whether it is necessary to request a copy of such contracts or to review them in order to demonstrate compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation.

Furthermore, the Board adopted a Statement following the amendments made by the European Parliament and the Council to the Commission's Proposal for a Regulation establishing additional procedural rules regarding the implementation of the GDPR.

The Statement generally welcomes the amendments introduced by the European Parliament and the Council and recommends further consideration of specific elements to achieve the objectives of simplifying cooperation among bodies and improving the implementation of the General Data Protection Regulation with the new regulation.

The Statement outlines practical recommendations that can be used in the context of the upcoming trilogues. Specifically, the EDPB reiterates the need for a legal basis and a harmonized procedure for amicable resolutions and provides recommendations to ensure that consensus on the summary of key issues is reached in the most effective manner. The Board also welcomes the inclusion of additional deadlines and reminds that they must be realistic, encouraging co-legislators to remove provisions related to relevant and reasoned objections and "justifications" in the dispute resolution process.

Although the Statement welcomes the goal of achieving greater transparency, the introduction of a common case file, as proposed by the European Parliament, would require complex changes to the document management and communication systems used at both the European and national levels. Technical solutions for its implementation should be carefully assessed, and further clarification on the methods for granting access to those solutions should be provided.

The European Data Protection Board welcomes the Council's amendment allowing the lead data protection authority to be exempted from the so-called enhanced cooperation in simple cases, but emphasizes the need for further clarification on the scope of that exemption.

The Chair of the European Data Protection Board, Anu Talus, stated: "The draft regulation could significantly simplify the implementation of the General Data Protection Regulation by increasing the efficiency of case handling. Greater harmonization at the EU level is needed to maximize the efficiency of cooperation mechanisms and consistency from the General Data Protection Regulation."

Finally, the Board adopted its work program for the period 2024-2025. This is the first of two work programs that will implement the EDPB's strategy for the period 2024-2027, adopted in April 2024. It is based on the priorities established in the European Data Protection Board's strategy and takes into account the needs identified as most important for stakeholders.
