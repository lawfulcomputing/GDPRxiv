On Monday, March 3, 2025, a conference was held in Zagreb as part of the project "Paperless Commercial Courts in the Republic of Croatia" aimed at exchanging best practices from international and Croatian perspectives regarding paperless working processes and digitalization in commercial courts, as well as European agreements on the use of artificial intelligence. The project is co-financed by the European Union through the Technical Support Instrument and is implemented by the Council of Europe in cooperation with the European Commission.

The attendees were addressed by the Minister of Justice, Administration, and Digital Transformation, Damir Habijan, who emphasized that an independent, efficient, and modern judiciary is essential for addressing the everyday needs of contemporary society. 

"Thanks to such projects, it is possible to achieve our vision of future justice, which is faster, more transparent, and more efficient resolution of court cases. We are aware that technology is developing at an incredible pace today, and therefore our institutions must keep up with these changes to be ready to respond to the challenges of modern society," stated Minister Habijan, adding that increasing accessibility and inclusiveness of court proceedings are the main advantages of this project. 

"The improvement of judges' work through the reduction of administrative burdens and a focus on the quality of judgments is of particular importance for citizens facing geographical and logistical barriers," concluded Minister Habijan.

The project supports the digitalization of the workflow of Croatian commercial courts to increase their overall efficiency, optimize resources, and reduce the length of proceedings and backlogs, all in accordance with the standards of the Council of Europe, the European Convention on Human Rights, and the case law of the European Court of Human Rights. 

The pilot project is currently being implemented in four commercial courts in Dubrovnik, Split, Varaždin, and Zagreb, after which the methodology will be applied to all other commercial courts in Croatia. 

The conference provided an opportunity to exchange best practices from international and Croatian perspectives regarding digitalization and the use of artificial intelligence in the context of justice. 

Anamarija Mladinić, Head of the EU, International Cooperation, and Legal Affairs Sector of the Data Protection Agency and Vice-Chair of the Committee of Convention 108 of the Council of Europe, delivered a presentation on the Framework Convention of the Council of Europe on Artificial Intelligence. 

"The Framework Convention of the Council of Europe on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy, and the Rule of Law is the first international legally binding instrument in the field of artificial intelligence, and it will be open for signature on September 5, 2024. 

It is technologically neutral and aimed at protecting and promoting human rights, democracy, and the rule of law, and it does not explicitly regulate the economic and market aspects of artificial intelligence systems, as is the case with the Artificial Intelligence Regulation. 

Each party to the Convention is obliged to implement measures aimed at ensuring that artificial intelligence systems are not used to undermine the integrity, independence, and effectiveness of democratic institutions and processes, including the principle of separation of powers, respect for the independence of the judiciary, and access to justice. 

Artificial intelligence technologies can pose a significant threat to democracy and human rights and can serve as a powerful tool for fragmenting the public sphere, undermining civic participation, and reducing trust in democratic processes. They can also enable malicious actors to spread misinformation that threatens the integrity of information and promote the distribution of deepfake content, which can contribute to the creation and strengthening of prejudices against individuals and certain social groups. 

Artificial intelligence can significantly enhance the judicial system by enabling the summarization of legal documents, analysis of case law, real-time oral translation, and providing support in the decision-making process. However, it is important to highlight the risks that come with it. Artificial intelligence algorithms are trained on data that often reflect racial, gender, or socioeconomic biases present in society. In the context of the application of artificial intelligence in the judiciary, if appropriate safeguards are not taken, these systems may make decisions that adversely affect certain groups in society, resulting in discriminatory outcomes. The use of artificial intelligence in this context is considered high-risk and should be transparent, appropriately traceable, and explainable, and subject to human oversight. Therefore, it is crucial to ensure human oversight and to keep in mind that the final decision must always be made by a human, not an algorithm," emphasized Anamarija Mladinić.

The project was launched in August 2024 and is based on a three-dimensional approach to analyzing the legislative, informational, and business aspects relevant to commercial courts in the Republic of Croatia, which are pioneers of innovation in this area, thus enabling the transition to fully paperless working processes.
