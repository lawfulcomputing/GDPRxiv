Meta Introduces Changes: Data of European Users Soon to Be Used for Artificial Intelligence Development

So far, Meta has not used data from European users of Facebook and Instagram to train its artificial intelligence systems. However, this is about to change. Starting from the end of May 2025, the company plans to begin processing data from all adult users in Europe from Facebook and Instagram to develop its own artificial intelligence applications. This data will be used to enhance AI-based services, such as the Meta-AI chatbot on WhatsApp or language models like Llama.

Meta has announced that it intends to use all publicly available information from user profiles, including historical data. This pertains to public posts, comments, photos, and descriptions accompanying photos—essentially, all content that the user has not restricted visibility to through privacy settings. The company emphasizes that only content posted on Facebook and Instagram by adult users will be used for the purpose of training artificial intelligence.

On one hand, Meta uses user data to develop and improve AI technology within its products. However, this data is also used outside of Meta platforms—for example, to train the Llama language model.

Meta bases the processing of personal data for the purpose of training artificial intelligence on legitimate interest. Users have the right to object to such processing in accordance with Article 21 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). For this purpose, Meta provides specific forms through which users can object to the use of their content for the development of AI models.

The form includes a text field where users can, if they wish, enter reasons for their objection, but providing reasons is not mandatory. Every person who submits an objection will receive a confirmation—either as a pop-up message in the app or via email—making the objection valid. An objection can be submitted at any time.

However, Meta plans to start training AI at the end of May 2025. Objections submitted after that date will only apply to future processing and cannot revoke previously conducted training based on prior data. All publicly available posts, comments, photos, and descriptions that have not been covered by an objection by that time will be used for training.

If you have multiple Facebook or Instagram accounts, you need to submit a separate objection for each of them. However, if your accounts are linked through the Accounts Center, one objection is sufficient and will apply to all linked profiles.

It is important to emphasize that if you have submitted an objection, it only pertains to content that you personally have posted. It does not include photos or posts that depict you but were posted by other individuals. Whether such content will be used for AI training depends on whether those users have also submitted an objection. In such cases, you can ask them to do so or request the removal of the disputed content.

Meta has already trained its AI models using data from various sources—including publicly available information on the internet and licensed data purchased from other companies.

If you wish to submit a general objection to the use of your data for AI training, you can do so via the form at the following link: https://www.facebook.com/help/contact/510058597920541. This form requires additional information and supporting documentation.

Note: The use of AI chatbots on WhatsApp, Facebook, and Instagram is not related to your objection regarding the use of data for AI training—chatbots remain available regardless of your choice.

AI Training Project in Europe – Delay and New Approach

Meta announced last year that it plans to use all public posts and photos of European users to train its AI systems. However, the project was temporarily halted after the Irish Data Protection Commission (IDPC), as the competent supervisory authority in the EU, expressed concerns regarding the legal basis and transparency of such processing. The new approach now allows users to more easily submit objections to the use of their personal data for AI training purposes.

Do you want to protect your data? Act in time

Users of Facebook and Instagram from Europe now need to decide whether they want their personal data—including posts, photos, and comments—to be used for training AI models. This decision encompasses not only future content but also all previously published information.

If you do not mind that your publicly available content is included in the development of AI, you do not need to take any action.

However, if you do not want your data to be used for these purposes, it is important to submit an objection in a timely manner. Meta will notify all adult users in Europe about this change and provide them with instructions on available options. An objection can be submitted directly through the apps or via the following links:

Facebook: https://www.facebook.com/help/contact/712876720715583

Instagram: https://help.instagram.com/contact/767264225370182

For the objection to have full effect, it must be submitted no later than the end of May 2025. Although it is still possible to submit an objection after that deadline, data that has already been used cannot be withdrawn from the AI system. Once data is integrated into artificial intelligence models, its impact can no longer be removed, according to the current level of technological development.
