Agency for Personal Data Protection  
January 28, 2025  

For the 19th time, the Agency for Personal Data Protection marked the European Data Protection Day by organizing the conference "What Does Artificial Intelligence Bring Us?", focusing on the advantages and risks associated with the development and use of artificial intelligence.  
"The General Data Protection Regulation and the Artificial Intelligence Regulation are inextricably linked – compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation is a fundamental prerequisite for the development of artificial intelligence that is ethical, lawful, and anthropocentric, while respecting human rights, democracy, and the rule of law. These regulations do not limit innovation; rather, they encourage it, creating safe and reliable foundations for the development of artificial intelligence that brings maximum benefits to society while simultaneously reducing risks," said Zdravko Vukić, the Director of the Agency for Personal Data Protection, who addressed the audience for the first time in his capacity as Vice-President of the European Data Protection Board. He emphasized that the Agency for Personal Data Protection, along with ensuring adequate financial and human resources, is ready to take on the role of supervisory body for monitoring the implementation of the Artificial Intelligence Regulation.  
The envoy of the Prime Minister of the Republic of Croatia and Minister of Justice, Administration, and Digital Transformation, Damir Habijan, emphasized that the issue of artificial intelligence is not just a technological question, but something that affects society as a whole in today's world. He stated that the advantages, as well as the risks of artificial intelligence, are numerous and that good regulation is the foundation for the development of artificial intelligence that simultaneously respects fundamental rights and is competitive in the global market. "We need an open dialogue, interdisciplinary cooperation, and education, which the Agency for Personal Data Protection continuously works on, and I commend this, as well as the Agency's quick responses in cases where the rights of our citizens are violated."  
Regarding the negative effects on the fundamental rights and freedoms of individuals, among others, the envoy of the President of the Croatian Parliament and representative, Maksimilijan Šimrak, spoke: "It is undisputed that artificial intelligence has significantly changed our lives, education, healthcare, and even the way we do business; however, it can also have its negative sides that need to be addressed, establishing a balance between innovation and the protection of privacy and our personal data."  
All speakers emphasized that for the development of artificial intelligence that respects fundamental human rights and the freedoms of citizens, cooperation among all key stakeholders is necessary, from European institutions, national governments, the private sector, the academic community to citizens. "Our common mission must be that artificial intelligence brings maximum benefit to society," emphasized Andrea Čović Vidović, Deputy Head of the European Commission Representation in the Republic of Croatia.  
"What Does Artificial Intelligence Bring Us" was also discussed in a panel of the same name. Under the moderation of Anamarija Mladinić, Head of the Sector for EU, International Cooperation, and Legal Affairs at the Agency for Personal Data Protection, the panelists – Associate Professor Dr. Robert Kopal, Senior Research Associate, President of the Management Board, Effectus College; Dr. Karlo Knežević, Head of the AI Team, Sofascore; Stefan Martinić, LL.M. Harvard Law School, Law Firm Martinić; Tomislav Vazdar, Board Member and Expert in AI and Cybersecurity, CroAI; and Marko Đuričić, Legal Expert and Data Protection Officer, Visage Technologies – emphasized that artificial intelligence systems in many cases involve the processing of vast amounts of personal data and that it is crucial that in all phases of development, testing, and use of artificial intelligence, it must comply with the General Data Protection Regulation. It is certain that the use of artificial intelligence tools will become as commonplace as the use of internet search engines, but it is of utmost importance that artificial intelligence systems are designed and used in a responsible and transparent manner, based on respect for fundamental rights, principles of data protection, human control, and democratic values.  
In this mission, the establishment of a space for safe testing of new AI solutions – a sandbox – will also help. Namely, according to Article 57, paragraph 1 of the Artificial Intelligence Regulation, member states ensure that their competent authorities establish at least one regulatory isolated environment (sandbox) for artificial intelligence at the national level to facilitate the development and testing of innovative AI systems under strict regulatory supervision before placing these systems on the market or in use, which must be operational by August 2, 2026.  
At the conference, a free web tool "Olivia" was also presented, which the Agency for Personal Data Protection developed in collaboration with partners within the European ARC II project. Anamarija Mladinić, Head of the Sector for EU, International Cooperation, and Legal Affairs at the Agency for Personal Data Protection and Vice-President of the Committee of Convention 108 of the Council of Europe, presented to the participants the possibilities offered by "Olivia," which contains a range of learning modules, knowledge tests, as well as templates for internal acts with which entrepreneurs can demonstrate their compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation.
