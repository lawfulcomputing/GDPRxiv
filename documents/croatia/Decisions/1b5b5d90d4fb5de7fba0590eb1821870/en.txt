At the meeting of the European Data Protection Board held on April 9, 2025, attended by the Director of the Agency for Personal Data Protection and Vice-President of the European Data Protection Board, Zdravko Vukić, along with his associates, Marko Trošelj, Head of the Legal Advisory, Cooperation and Projects Department, was elected as the coordinator of the Compliance, e-Government and Health working group at the European Data Protection Board.  
The Head of the International Cooperation, EU and Legal Affairs Sector, Anamarija Mladinić, presented a comprehensive methodology for risk management in large language models, developed by Isabel Barbera, an external collaborator of the European Data Protection Board. The document titled “Risks in Large Language Models and Measures for Their Mitigation” is available at the following link: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/support-pool-experts-projects/ai-privacy-risks-mitigations-large_en.  
The risk management methodology described in this document is designed to assist those who develop large language models and those who use them in systematically identifying, assessing, and mitigating risks to personal data protection and privacy, thereby supporting the responsible development and application of large language models.  
These guidelines also support the requirements of Article 25 of the General Data Protection Regulation (privacy by design and by default) and Article 32 – Security of Processing, offering technical and organizational measures that help ensure an appropriate level of security and data protection.  
However, these guidelines are not intended as a substitute for the data protection impact assessment (DPIA) mandated by Article 35 of the GDPR. Instead, they complement the data protection impact assessment process by addressing privacy risks specific to large language models (LLMs), thereby enhancing the comprehensiveness and effectiveness of such assessments.  
The document is structured to guide readers through key technological concepts, the risk management process, main risks and mitigation measures, and practical examples. Its aim is to support organizations in the responsible implementation of systems based on large language models (LLMs), while simultaneously identifying and mitigating risks to the privacy and data protection of individuals.  
Below is an overview of the structure of the document and the topics covered in each chapter:

**Introduction**  

This chapter explains how large language models function and what their common areas of application are.  

**Data Flow and Associated Privacy Risks in LLM Systems**  

This chapter explores how privacy risks arise in different models of providing LLM services, emphasizing the importance of understanding the data flow throughout the entire artificial intelligence lifecycle. The chapter also identifies risks and mitigation measures and considers roles and responsibilities under the Artificial Intelligence Act and the General Data Protection Regulation.  

**Data Protection and Privacy Risk Assessment: Risk Identification**  

This chapter provides criteria for identifying risks and examples of risks specific to LLM systems. Developers and users of large language models can use this chapter as a starting point for identifying risks in their own systems.  

**Data Protection and Privacy Risk Assessment: Risk Assessment and Evaluation**  

This chapter offers guidance for analyzing, classifying, and assessing privacy risks, along with criteria for evaluating the likelihood and severity of risks. It explains how to conduct a final risk assessment for effective prioritization of mitigation measures.  

**Risk Management for Data Protection and Privacy**  

The chapter describes strategies for treating risks and offers practical mitigation measures for common privacy risks in LLM systems. It also discusses the acceptance of residual risks and the iterative nature of risk management in AI systems.  

**Assessment of Residual Risks**  

Assessing residual risks after mitigation measures have been applied is crucial to ensure that risks are within acceptable limits and that no additional measures are needed. This chapter explains how to assess residual risks to determine whether additional measures are required or if the system is ready for deployment.  

**Review and Monitoring**  

The chapter addresses the importance of reviewing risk management activities and maintaining a risk register. It also emphasizes the importance of continuous monitoring to detect new risks, assess real-world impacts, and improve mitigation strategies.  

**Examples of LLM Risk Assessment**  

Three detailed practical examples illustrate the application of the risk management framework in real scenarios. These examples demonstrate how risks can be identified, assessed, and mitigated in various contexts.  

**Tools, Methodologies, Metrics, and Guidelines**  

The concluding chapter brings together tools, assessment metrics, benchmarks, methodologies, and standards that can assist developers and users in managing risks and evaluating the effectiveness of LLM systems.  
The Agency will conduct a series of educational activities in the upcoming period aimed at supporting organizations in risk assessment and determining appropriate protective measures in systems based on large language models.  
All these activities are part of the Agency for Personal Data Protection's long-term efforts aimed at raising awareness and knowledge about personal data protection among all stakeholders and strengthening the fundamental rights of citizens of the European Union – the right to personal data protection and the right to privacy.
