Brussels – during the plenary session in December, attended by the Director of the Agency and the Vice-Chair of the European Data Protection Board Zdravko Vukić, the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) published guidelines on Article 48 of the General Data Protection Regulation regarding the transfer of data to authorities in third countries, approved a new European Data Protection Seal, and adopted a statement on the second report of the European Commission on the application of the General Data Protection Regulation.

**The European Data Protection Board assists organizations in assessing requests from authorities in third countries for data transfers**  
Organizations receive requests from public authorities in other countries for the exchange of personal data, which may, for example, assist in gathering evidence in a criminal case, verifying financial transactions, or approving new medicines. When a European organization receives a request for data transfer from an authority in a "third country," it must comply with the General Data Protection Regulation.  
The EDPB focused on Article 48 of the General Data Protection Regulation in the guidelines and explains how organizations can best assess under what conditions they can legally respond to such requests. In this way, the guidelines help organizations decide whether they can lawfully transfer personal data to authorities in third countries when requested.  
Judgments or decisions from authorities in third countries cannot be automatically recognized or enforced in Europe. If an organization responds to a request for personal data from a third country authority, that data flow constitutes a transfer and the General Data Protection Regulation applies. An international agreement may also provide a legal basis and grounds for the transfer. In the absence of an international agreement or if the agreement does not provide an appropriate legal basis or safeguards, other legal bases or reasons for transfer may be considered in exceptional circumstances and on a case-by-case basis.  
The guidelines are subject to public consultation until January 27, 2025.

**Approval of the European Data Protection Seal**  
The Board adopted an opinion approving the certification criteria of Brand Compliance, which relate to processing activities carried out by data controllers or processors. The Board had already issued an opinion in September 2023 approving the national certification criteria of Brand Compliance, thereby officially recognizing them as certification criteria in the Netherlands for data processing procedures carried out by organizations. With this new opinion, these criteria are approved as applicable across Europe and as the European Data Protection Seal.  
Certification based on the General Data Protection Regulation helps organizations demonstrate their compliance with data protection regulations and increases transparency. This transparency helps citizens trust the product, service, process, or system for which organizations process their personal data.

**Adoption of the Second Report of the European Commission on the Application of the General Data Protection Regulation**  
In its statement, the EDPB welcomes the reports from the European Commission and the European Union Agency for Fundamental Rights. The EDPB emphasizes the importance of legal certainty and the alignment of digital legislation with the GDPR and reminds of some of its ongoing initiatives to clarify the interaction of GDPR enforcement concerning the Artificial Intelligence Act, the EU Data Strategy, and the Digital Services Package.  
Furthermore, the EDPB announces that it will provide additional resources for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and other groups.  
Finally, the Board highlights the genuine need for additional financial and human resources to assist data protection authorities and the EDPB in dealing with increasingly complex challenges and additional jurisdictions.
