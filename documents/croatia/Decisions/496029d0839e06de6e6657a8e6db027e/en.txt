The Agency for the Protection of Personal Data, on the occasion of the European Data Protection Day, organizes a conference titled "What Does Artificial Intelligence Bring Us?" on January 28, 2025. Registrations are not possible; all places are filled!

The central theme of this year's conference is the risks and benefits associated with the development and use of artificial intelligence, and the conference will bring together representatives from the academic community, public authorities, and data protection experts from various sectors. 

Following the opening speeches by the Director of the Agency and the Vice-President of the European Data Protection Board, Zdravko Vukić, Andrea Čović Vidović, Deputy Head of the European Commission Representation in Croatia, Maksimilijan Šimrak, envoy of the President of the Croatian Parliament and Member of the Croatian Parliament, and Damir Habijan, envoy of the Prime Minister of the Republic of Croatia and Minister of Justice, Administration, and Digital Transformation, a panel discussion titled "What Does Artificial Intelligence Bring?" will take place. The panelists – Associate Professor Dr. Robert Kopal, Senior Research Associate, President of the Management Board, Effectus College; Dr. Karlo Knežević, Head of the AI Team, Sofascore; Stefan Martinić, LL.M. Harvard Law School, Law Office Martinić; Lorena Barić, Executive Director, CroAI Association; and Marko Đuričić, Legal Expert and Data Protection Officer, Visage Technologies – will discuss the advantages and challenges brought by the use and development of artificial intelligence. The panel will be moderated by Anamarija Mladinić, Head of the Sector for EU, International Cooperation, and Legal Affairs at the Agency for the Protection of Personal Data.

This multidisciplinary discussion will allow participants to exchange experiences, knowledge, and perspectives on how artificial intelligence can enhance various aspects of society, as well as the risks it entails. The focus will be on human rights, data security, innovations, and the impact of artificial intelligence on jobs, which will encourage constructive debate and collaborative problem-solving.

Indeed, artificial intelligence is becoming increasingly present in our daily lives, bringing significant challenges related to data protection. The right to the protection of personal data and privacy is inextricably linked to many other fundamental rights, such as the right to integrity and human dignity, the right to equality, the right to freedom of expression, the right to freedom of movement and security, the right to education, and the right to a fair trial. Failure to respect the right to the protection of personal data can lead to violations of many other human rights, and even fatal outcomes for individuals.

The processing of personal data in artificial intelligence systems that is not in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation can lead, among other things, to discrimination, biased decision-making to the detriment of individuals, and identity theft. Therefore, it is important to ensure transparency and accountability in how data is used to protect individuals and preserve fundamental human rights in the digital age.

In marking this year's European Data Protection Day, we also take the opportunity to present "Olivia," a virtual teacher and assistant for GDPR compliance, which allows entrepreneurs to learn about their basic obligations, test their knowledge, and create basic documents to demonstrate compliance with data protection regulations.

Let us recall that in order to raise public awareness about the fundamental human right – the right to the protection of personal data and privacy, the Council of Europe, with the support of the European Commission, declared January 28 as European Data Protection Day in 2006, which is also known worldwide as Privacy Day.
