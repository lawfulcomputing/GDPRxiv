European Commission adopted on July 10 a Decision on the adequacy of the data protection framework between the EU and the US. The adequacy decision concludes that the United States provides an adequate level of protection – compared to that of the EU – for personal data transferred from the EU to US companies participating in the data protection framework between the EU and the US. 

The decision concludes that the United States ensures an adequate level of protection – comparable to that of the European Union – for personal data transferred from the EU to US companies under the new framework. Based on the new Adequacy Decision, personal data can be securely transferred from the EU to US companies participating in the Framework, without the need for additional data protection measures. 

The EU-US data protection framework introduces new binding safeguards to address all the issues raised by the Court of Justice of the European Union, including limiting access by US intelligence agencies to EU data to what is necessary and proportionate, and establishing a Data Protection Review Court (DPRC), which individuals from the EU will have access to. The new framework introduces significant improvements compared to the mechanism that existed under the Privacy Shield system. For example, if the DPRC finds that data has been collected in violation of the new safeguards, it will be able to order the deletion of the data. The new safeguards regarding government access to data will supplement the obligations that US companies importing data from the EU will have to comply with. 

The adequacy decision followed the signing by the US of an Executive Order on "Enhancing Safeguards for United States Signals Intelligence Activities," which introduced new binding safeguards to address the issues highlighted by the Court of Justice of the European Union in its ruling in the Schrems II case from July 2020. Specifically, the new obligations were aimed at ensuring that US intelligence agencies can access data only to the extent that it is necessary and proportionate, and that an independent and impartial legal protection mechanism is established for processing and addressing complaints from Europeans regarding the collection of their data for national security purposes.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is an adequacy decision?  
An adequacy decision is one of the tools provided by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) for the transfer of personal data from the EU to third countries that, according to the Commission's assessment, provide a level of personal data protection comparable to that in the European Union. As a result of adequacy decisions, personal data can be freely and securely transferred from the European Economic Area (EEA), which includes 27 EU member states as well as Norway, Iceland, and Liechtenstein, to a third country, without additional conditions or approvals. In other words, transfers to a third country can be processed in the same way as data transfers within the EU. The adequacy decision on the EU-US data protection framework covers data transfers from any public or private entity in the EEA to US companies participating in the data protection framework between the EU and the US.

2. What are the criteria for assessing adequacy?  
Adequacy does not require that the data protection system of a third country be identical to that of the EU, but is based on the standard of "essential equivalence." It includes a comprehensive assessment of the country's data protection framework, both the protections applied to personal data and the available oversight and legal protection mechanisms. European data protection authorities have compiled a list of elements that must be considered in this assessment: https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/items/614108/en.

3. What is the data protection framework between the EU and the US?  
The Commission, in its adequacy decision, carefully assessed the requirements arising from the EU-US data protection framework, as well as the limitations and safeguards that apply when US public authorities access personal data transferred to the US, particularly for law enforcement and national security purposes. Based on this, the adequacy decision concludes that the United States provides an adequate level of protection for personal data transferred from the EU to companies participating in the EU-US data protection framework. By adopting the adequacy decision, European entities can transfer personal data to participating companies in the United States without introducing additional data protection measures. The framework grants individuals from the EU whose data would be transferred to participating companies in the US several new rights (e.g., obtaining access to their data or obtaining rectification or deletion of inaccurate or unlawfully processed data). Additionally, it offers various means of legal protection in case of mishandling of their data, including access to a free independent dispute resolution mechanism and an arbitration panel. 

US companies can confirm their participation in the EU-US data protection framework by committing to meet a detailed set of obligations regarding privacy. This could, for example, include privacy principles such as purpose limitation, data minimization, and data retention, as well as specific obligations regarding data security and sharing data with third parties. The framework will be managed by the US Department of Commerce, which will process certification requests and monitor whether participating companies continue to meet certification requirements. The Federal Trade Commission will enforce compliance of US companies with their obligations under the EU-US data protection framework.

4. What are the limitations and safeguards regarding US intelligence agencies' access to data?  
A key element of the legal framework in the US on which the adequacy decision is based relates to the Executive Order on "Strengthening Safeguards in the Context of United States Signals Intelligence Activities," which President Biden signed on October 7 and which is accompanied by...
**Letters issued by the Attorney General.** These instruments were adopted to address the issues highlighted by the Court in its judgment in the Schrems II case.  
For Europeans whose personal data is transferred to the U.S., the Executive Order provides for:

1. Binding protective measures that limit access to data by U.S. intelligence agencies to what is necessary and proportionate for the protection of national security;  
2. Enhanced oversight of the activities of U.S. intelligence agencies to ensure compliance with the limitations on surveillance activities; and  
3. The establishment of an independent and impartial legal protection mechanism, which includes a new court for the review of data protection to investigate and resolve complaints regarding access by U.S. national security bodies to their data.

**5. What is the new legal protection mechanism in the area of national security and how can individuals utilize it?**  
The U.S. government has established a new two-tier legal protection mechanism, with independent and binding authorities, for processing and resolving complaints from all individuals whose data has been transferred from the EEA to companies in the U.S. regarding the collection and use of their data by U.S. intelligence agencies.  
To have a complaint accepted, individuals do not need to prove that their data has actually been collected by U.S. intelligence agencies. Individuals can submit a complaint to their national data protection authority, which will ensure that the complaint is properly forwarded and that the individual is provided with all additional information related to the procedure, including information about the outcome of the procedure. This ensures that individuals can contact the competent authority in their country in their own language. The European Data Protection Board will forward complaints to the U.S.  
First, complaints will be investigated by the so-called "Civil Liberties Protection Officer" of the U.S. intelligence community. This person is responsible for ensuring compliance of U.S. intelligence agencies with privacy and fundamental rights.  
Second, individuals have the option to appeal the decision of the Civil Liberties Protection Officer to the newly established Data Protection Review Court (DPRC). The Court consists of members who are not part of the U.S. government and who are appointed based on specific qualifications, can only be removed for reasons such as a criminal conviction or being deemed mentally or physically unfit to perform their duties, and cannot receive instructions from the government. The DPRC has the authority to investigate complaints from individuals in the EU, among other things, to obtain relevant information from intelligence agencies, and can issue binding corrective decisions. For example, if the DPRC finds that data was collected in violation of the protective measures provided for in the Executive Order, it can order the deletion of the data.  
In any case, the Court will select a special attorney with relevant experience to support the Court, who will ensure that the interests of the complainant are represented and that the Court is well-informed about the factual and legal aspects of the case. This will ensure representation of both sides and introduce important guarantees regarding fair trial and due process.  
After the Civil Liberties Protection Officer or the DPRC completes the investigation, the complainant will be informed whether a violation of U.S. law was found or whether a violation was identified and rectified. At a later stage, the complainant will also be informed when information about the proceedings before the DPRC, such as the reasoned decision of the Court, is no longer subject to confidentiality requirements.  

**6. When will the decision take effect?**  
The adequacy decision came into effect on July 10.  
There is no time limit, but the Commission will continuously monitor relevant developments in the United States and regularly review the adequacy decision.  
The first review will be conducted within one year of the adequacy decision coming into effect to check whether all relevant elements of the U.S. legal framework are functioning effectively in practice. After that, depending on the outcome of that first review, the Commission will, in consultation with EU member states and data protection authorities, decide on the frequency of future reviews, which will be conducted at least every four years.  
Adequacy decisions may be adjusted or even withdrawn in the event of developments that affect the level of protection in a third country.  

**7. What is the impact of the decision on the ability to use other data transfer tools to the United States?**  
All protective measures that the U.S. government has established in the area of national security (including the legal protection mechanism) apply to all data transfers in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation to companies in the U.S., regardless of the transfer mechanisms used. These protective measures therefore facilitate the use of other tools, such as standard contractual clauses and binding corporate rules.
