Brussels, December 18, 2024 – The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) has adopted an opinion on the use of personal data for the development and use of artificial intelligence models.  
The opinion is available at the following link: Opinion 28/2024 on certain data protection aspects related to the processing of personal data in the context of AI models | European Data Protection Board  
In this opinion, the EDPB considers:  
1. when and how artificial intelligence models can be considered anonymous,  
2. whether and how legitimate interests can be used as a legal basis for the development or use of artificial intelligence models,  
3. what happens if an artificial intelligence model is developed using unlawfully processed personal data. It also considers the use of first-party and third-party data.  
The opinion was requested by the Irish Data Protection Authority in order to achieve regulatory alignment at the level of the European Economic Area. To gather information for this opinion, which pertains to rapidly changing technologies with significant societal impact, the European Data Protection Board organized a stakeholder event and exchanged information with the EU Office for Artificial Intelligence.  
The Chair of the European Data Protection Board, Talus, stated: “Artificial intelligence technologies can bring numerous opportunities and benefits to various industries and areas of life. We must ensure that these innovations are implemented ethically, safely, and in a way that benefits everyone. The European Data Protection Board aims to support responsible innovation in the field of artificial intelligence by ensuring the protection of personal data and with full respect for the General Data Protection Regulation.”  
Regarding anonymity, the opinion states that data protection authorities should individually assess whether an artificial intelligence model is anonymous.  
For a model to be considered anonymous, it should be highly unlikely that:  
(1) individuals whose data were used to create the model can be identified directly or indirectly, and  
(2) such personal data can be extracted from the model through queries. The opinion provides an exhaustive list of methods for demonstrating anonymity.  
When it comes to legitimate interest, the opinion outlines general considerations that data protection authorities should take into account when assessing whether legitimate interest is an appropriate legal basis for processing personal data for the development and introduction of artificial intelligence models.  
A three-step test helps evaluate the use of legitimate interest as a legal basis. The European Data Protection Board provides examples of a conversational agent that assists users and the use of artificial intelligence to enhance cybersecurity. These services can be beneficial for individuals and may rely on legitimate interest as a legal basis, but only if it is demonstrated that the processing is necessary and if a balance of rights is maintained.  
The opinion also includes a set of criteria to assist data protection authorities in assessing whether individuals can reasonably expect certain uses of their personal data.  
These criteria include: whether the personal data was publicly available, the nature of the relationship between the individual and the data controller, the nature of the service, the context in which the personal data was collected, the source from which the data was collected, possible further uses of the model, and whether individuals are indeed aware that their personal data is available online.  
If the balancing test shows that processing should not take place due to a negative impact on individuals, mitigation measures can limit that negative impact. The opinion includes an exhaustive list of examples of such mitigation measures, which can be of a technical nature, facilitate individuals' exercise of their rights, or increase transparency.  
Finally, if an artificial intelligence model is developed with unlawfully processed personal data, this could affect the legality of its application.  
Given the scope of the request from the Irish Data Protection Authority, the wide variety of artificial intelligence models, and their rapid development, the opinion aims to provide guidance on various elements that can be used to conduct case-by-case analysis.  
The European Data Protection Board is currently developing guidelines that address more specific issues, such as web scraping.
