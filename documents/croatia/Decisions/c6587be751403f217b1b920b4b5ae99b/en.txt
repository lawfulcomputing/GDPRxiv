Regarding the 20th anniversary of the establishment of the Agency for the Protection of Personal Data and the Protection of Personal Data in the Republic of Croatia, a conference titled “Risks and Compliance with GDPR in the Age of Artificial Intelligence” was held on Friday, May 24, 2024, at the Esplanade Hotel.  
The protection of personal data in Croatia is a constitutional category since the adoption of the first Constitution of the Republic of Croatia in 1990, and every citizen of the Republic of Croatia is guaranteed the protection of personal data as a fundamental human right. However, the first law regulating the area of personal data protection in the Republic of Croatia was adopted in 2003, when the Agency for the Protection of Personal Data was also established, and it began its operations in 2004. Over the course of twenty years of operation, the Agency achieved its best results to date last year, which includes the highest number of supervisory activities, as well as activities aimed at raising awareness about the importance of the right to data protection as a fundamental right.  
“The year behind us is particularly significant due to the imposition of the highest number of administrative monetary fines, as well as the imposition of the highest amounts of administrative monetary fines for violations of the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation. A total of 28 administrative monetary fines were imposed in the amount of 8.27 million euros. This places us among the top ten in the EU in terms of the total amount of administrative monetary fines imposed in 2023. Considering that in 2020 the Agency was at the very bottom of this ranking, our determination and commitment to the effective implementation of the General Data Protection Regulation, and thus to the protection of human rights and freedoms, is more than evident,” emphasized Director Zdravko Vukić.  
The envoy of the Prime Minister of the Republic of Croatia, Minister of Justice, Administration, and Digital Transformation Damir Habijan congratulated the Agency on 20 years of work and the results it has achieved in recent years. “Taking into account the accelerated development of technology and digitalization, as well as the development of artificial intelligence, there are certainly many challenges ahead for the Agency for the Protection of Personal Data, as well as for the legislative bodies that will soon have to change certain strategic documents and the normative legislative framework. I am confident that the Agency will continue to achieve excellent results.”  
The Agency for the Protection of Personal Data, as our national body in executing its jurisdiction, has demonstrated efficiency in the effective implementation of the General Data Protection Regulation. It is important to continue working on educating and informing citizens in light of the increasingly demanding development of the digital society and artificial intelligence, which is finding more and more space in everyday activities and business processes,” said the envoy of the President of the Croatian Parliament, representative Nataša Tramišak.  
“The unpredictable era of the fourth industrial revolution reminds us, clearer than ever before, that we have a global responsibility to protect our fundamental rights. Despite some shortcomings, there are no other global actors besides Europe that would regulate the digital world more in accordance with human needs,” emphasized Member of the European Parliament Karlo Ressler.  
Limor Shmerling Magazanik, head of the OECD accession process related to data governance, privacy, and digital security, highlighted the contribution of the Agency for the Protection of Personal Data in the field of privacy and human rights, which ultimately resulted in a positive opinion from the OECD on the actions taken by the Republic of Croatia as a candidate for OECD membership.  
Video messages were also delivered by Anu Talus, Chair of the European Data Protection Board, and Wojciech Wiewiórowski, European Data Protection Supervisor, who emphasized that since Zdravko Vukić was appointed as director in 2020, the Agency has achieved significant accomplishments and strong progress.  
“I am pleased to see that the journey of the Agency for the Protection of Personal Data over the past two decades has been extraordinary and is an example of adaptation in the new digital age, and your achievements have not only contributed to the protection of personal data in Croatia but have also influenced the broader European data protection environment,” said Wiewiórowski.  
“A recent example is the ARC II project, which helps small and medium-sized enterprises on their path to compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation, and the innovative online tool Olivia, a virtual assistant for compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation, is the result of the dedication of the director and his team in raising awareness about the General Data Protection Regulation and helping organizations become compliant,” said Talus, highlighting that the Agency also enjoys an international reputation.  
Thematic presentations titled “The Right to Data Protection: A Cornerstone of the Rule of Law in the Digital Age” were delivered by Matthias Schmidl, Commissioner of the Austrian Data Protection Authority, Guido Scorza, Garante Privacy – the Italian supervisory authority, Krenare Sogojeva Dermaku, Commissioner of the Agency for Information and Privacy Protection, Republic of Kosovo, Ventsislav Karadjov, President of the Bulgarian Data Protection Authority, and Mirosław Wróblewski, President of the Polish Data Protection Authority, who, among other things, thanked the Agency for mutual cooperation and congratulated it on its successes to date.  
The successful implementation of the European project ARC II was the subject of the panel discussion “Results of the EU Project ARC II: Supporting Croatian and Italian Entrepreneurs in Compliance with the Legal Framework for Data Protection.” Within the project, which the Agency implements as a coordinator together with partners, the focus is on micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises for which numerous training sessions for entrepreneurs have been held. However, the main goal of the project is the development of the digital tool “Olivia” – a tool designed to assist in the creation of internal acts through which entrepreneurs can demonstrate their compliance and reliability, and it offers a range of learning modules to help entrepreneurs improve their knowledge in the field of personal data protection. Ija Parlov Una, ACCREDI CERT; Marija Bošković Batarelo, Parser Compliance; Vlatka Vuković, Compliance Buro; Nikolina Staničić, Ericsson Nikola Tesla shared their experiences during the implementation of the ARC II project. In a part of the conference titled “Convention 108+ of the Council of Europe as a Global Privacy Standard,” the Secretary General of the Council of Europe, Marija Pejčinović Burić, addressed the audience via video message, announcing that last week the Council of Europe adopted the first international legally binding treaty aimed at ensuring respect for human rights, the rule of law, and democratic legal standards in the use of artificial intelligence systems. The treaty, which is open to non-European countries, establishes a legal framework that encompasses the entire lifecycle of artificial intelligence systems and addresses the risks they may pose while simultaneously promoting responsible innovation. “The global agreement is designed to ensure that artificial intelligence supports common standards in democracy, human rights, and the rule of law, rather than undermining them, and recognizes the inherent connection between the protection of personal data and the regulation of its use for the development, training, and management of artificial intelligence systems,” said Pejčinović Burić, adding, “The constant success of the Agency is proof of the country's commitment to the protection of personal data, and we can see this important commitment at the international level as well. Croatia was among the first countries to join the new protocol designed to ensure that the Council of Europe Convention on Data Protection remains relevant for the future.”

Elsa Mein, Chair of the Convention 108 Committee of the Council of Europe, also spoke about Convention 108+, congratulating the Agency for Personal Data Protection on its work and highlighting her appreciation for the effort the Agency has put into the Advisory Committee of Convention 108 of the Council of Europe.

The conference concluded with a panel discussion titled “Interaction between the Artificial Intelligence Act and GDPR: How to Develop Human-Centric Artificial Intelligence,” featuring panelists Isabelle Vereecken, European Data Protection Board; Limor Shmerling Magazanik, OECD; Matthias Schmidl, Austrian Data Protection Authority; Brendan Van Alsenoy, EDPS; and Andries Küter, representative of the Federal Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (BfDI), moderated by Martin de Moor from the Belgian Data Protection Authority. The panelists discussed potential new roles and tasks for data protection authorities concerning the implementation of the Artificial Intelligence Act. Participants also had the opportunity to deepen their understanding of the legal and ethical aspects related to the development and use of artificial intelligence and the protection of fundamental rights and freedoms.

Additionally, the conference marked a date that signifies an absolute turning point for the Agency – May 25, 2018, the beginning of the full application of the General Data Protection Regulation. This granted the Agency new corrective powers, including the ability to impose high administrative fines, of which 58 have been issued to date, totaling €9.1 million.
