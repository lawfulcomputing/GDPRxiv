November 5, 2024 – at the plenary session of the EDPB, which was held for the first time from Zagreb, under the presidency of the Vice-President of the European Data Protection Board and the Director of the Personal Data Protection Agency, Zdravko Vukić, the first report* of the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (DPF – EU-US Data Privacy Framework) was adopted, as well as the Statement on Recommendations of the High-Level Group (HLG)** regarding access to data for effective law enforcement.

The EDPB welcomes the efforts of the U.S. authorities and the European Commission to implement the DPF and acknowledges several events that have occurred since the adoption of the adequacy decision in July 2023. 

Regarding the commercial aspects, i.e., the application and enforcement of requirements applicable to self-certified companies under this framework, the EDPB notes that the U.S. Department of Commerce has taken all relevant steps to implement the certification process. This includes the development of a new website, updating procedures, collaborating with companies, and conducting awareness-raising activities.

Furthermore, a legal protection mechanism for individuals from the EU has been implemented, and comprehensive guidelines for handling complaints have been published on both sides of the Atlantic. However, the small number of complaints received so far under the DPF emphasizes the importance of initiating oversight activities by U.S. authorities concerning the compliance of DPF-certified companies with the principles of the DPF.

The EDPB encourages the development of guidelines by the relevant U.S. authorities, clarifying the requirements that DPF-certified companies should adhere to when transferring personal data received from the EU. Guidelines from U.S. authorities regarding human resources data would also be welcome. The EDPB expresses its readiness to provide feedback on these guidelines.

Regarding access by U.S. public authorities to personal data transferred from the EU to certified organizations, the EDPB focused on the effective implementation of the safeguards introduced by Executive Order 14086 into the U.S. legal framework, such as the principles of necessity and proportionality and the new legal protection mechanism. The Board believes that the elements of the legal protection mechanism have been established; at the same time, it renews its call for the European Commission to monitor the practical functioning of the various safeguards, e.g., the implementation of the principles of necessity and proportionality. The EDPB also recommends that the Commission monitor future developments concerning the U.S. Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act, particularly regarding the expanded scope of Section 702 after it was reauthorized by the U.S. Congress earlier this year.

The Vice-President of the EDPB, Zdravko Vukić, stated: “We are pleased that progress has been made since the adoption of the adequacy decision, thanks to fruitful cooperation between U.S. authorities, the European Commission, and the EDPB. At the same time, there is still room for improvement, and we should continue to work together to maintain a high level of data protection and protect the rights and freedoms of individuals in the EU.”

Finally, the Board recommends that the EU-U.S. Adequacy Decision should be reviewed within three years or less.

The Statement on Recommendations of the HLG regarding access to personal data for effective law enforcement by public authorities emphasizes that the fundamental rights of individuals must be protected. While the EDPB supports the goal of effective law enforcement, it highlights that some of the HLG's recommendations could seriously undermine fundamental rights, particularly the respect for privacy and family life.

Although the EDPB positively notes that the recommendations could lead to the establishment of a level playing field for data retention, it believes that a broad and general obligation for all service providers to retain data in electronic form would create a significant interference with individuals' rights. Therefore, the EDPB questions whether this would meet the requirements of necessity and proportionality under the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the EU and the case law of the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU).

In its statement, the EDPB also emphasizes that recommendations regarding encryption should not hinder its use or weaken the effectiveness of the protection it provides. For example, introducing a client-side process that allows remote access to data before it is encrypted and sent over a communication channel or after it is decrypted at the recipient’s end would, in practice, weaken encryption. Preserving the protection and effectiveness of encryption is important to avoid negative impacts on the respect for privacy and confidentiality and to ensure the protection of freedom of expression and economic growth that depend on reliable technologies.

* In accordance with Article 3 of the EU-U.S. Adequacy Decision, the European Commission is required to review the adequacy decision one year after its adoption. A review meeting was held in Washington on July 18 and 19, 2024, attended by five representatives of the EDPB.

** The HLG was initiated by the European Commission in June 2023 and is co-chaired by the Commission and a rotating presidency of the Council. It was launched with the aim of exploring challenges for law enforcement experts regarding access to data and proposing solutions and recommendations.

In June 2024, the HLG published 42 recommendations for further development of EU policies and legislation, structured as "capacity-building measures," "collaboration with industry and standardization," and "legislative measures." The recommendations specifically address encryption, collaboration with industry as well as between public authorities, and the need for harmonized data retention rules.
