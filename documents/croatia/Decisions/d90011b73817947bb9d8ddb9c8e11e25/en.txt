Before the introduction of high-risk artificial intelligence systems referred to in Article 6, paragraph 2 of the Artificial Intelligence Regulation, with the exception of high-risk AI systems intended for use in the area specified in point 2 of Annex III, entities introducing the system, which are public authorities or private entities providing public services, as well as entities introducing high-risk AI systems from point 5, subparagraphs (b) and (c) of Annex III, shall conduct an impact assessment regarding the effects that the use of such a system may have on fundamental rights. For this purpose, the entities introducing the system shall conduct an assessment that includes:
(a) a description of the processes of the entity introducing the system in which the high-risk AI system will be used in accordance with its intended purpose;
(b) a description of the period and frequency of intended use of each high-risk AI system;
(c) categories of natural persons and groups likely to be affected by the use of the system in the specific context;
(d) specific risks of harm likely to affect the categories of natural persons or groups identified in accordance with point (c) of this paragraph, taking into account the information provided by the supplier in accordance with Article 13;
(e) a description of the implementation of human oversight measures in accordance with the usage instructions;
(f) measures to be taken in the event of the materialization of those risks, including arrangements for internal management and mechanisms for submitting complaints.

In order to facilitate the implementation of the impact assessment on human rights for artificial intelligence operators, the Personal Data Protection Agency recommends the use of the methodology developed by the Catalonia Data Protection Authority (ADPCAT). The translation of the methodology into Croatian is available at the link: https://azop.hr/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/FRIA-Clanak21-AI-Act.pdf

The Artificial Intelligence Regulation establishes "ensuring a high level of protection of health, safety, and fundamental rights laid down in the Charter" as one of the main objectives of this Regulation (Article 1 of the Artificial Intelligence Regulation). In accordance with the risk-based approach adopted by the EU legislator, the impact assessment of artificial intelligence on fundamental rights is included in all risk management procedures established by the Regulation.

From compliance assessment to specific impact assessment on fundamental rights in accordance with Article 27 of the Artificial Intelligence Act, including a specific provision for general-purpose artificial intelligence models with systemic risk, the impact on fundamental rights must be taken into account when designing, developing, and deploying AI systems and models.

In this context, the provisions on how to conduct the impact assessment on human rights from Article 27 of the Artificial Intelligence Regulation, as well as the assessment on human rights from the Framework Convention of the Council of Europe, do not provide guidance to those who must carry out that assessment.

The methodology for conducting the impact assessment on fundamental rights is a crucial tool for identifying risks and the organizational and technical measures to be applied, with a transversal approach that must be integrated into multidisciplinary teams responsible for the application of artificial intelligence in the public and private sectors.

For this purpose, as already noted in the application of the General Data Protection Regulation, it is absolutely essential that data protection officers are involved from the very beginning in the strategies and digital transformation projects that will be implemented in each organization. In this way, these risks can be analyzed from the outset, and necessary organizational and technical measures can be taken.

In cases where it is necessary to conduct both an impact assessment on fundamental rights and an impact assessment on data protection, the Agency recommends that these two analyses be combined to complement each other.

The impact assessment on fundamental rights (FRIA) is mandatory at the stage of introducing a high-risk AI system, while the impact assessment on data protection should be conducted at the very beginning, that is, before the development of the artificial intelligence system begins.

The obligation to implement FRIA may also apply if there is no high-risk processing of personal data within the high-risk AI system. The impact assessment on data protection should also be conducted if it is not a high-risk AI system, but the processing of personal data within the AI system is considered high-risk.

Example: A chatbot will generally be considered a medium-risk artificial intelligence system. However, if the chatbot is used in a sensitive context, there may be processing activities that would be classified as high-risk, even if the system itself would not be high-risk. Therefore, it is possible that an impact assessment on fundamental rights is not required, but an impact assessment on data protection is necessary.
