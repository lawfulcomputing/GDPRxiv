Brussels, February 11 – At the plenary session of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB), which was attended by the Vice-President of the EDPB, Director Zdravko Vukić with his associates, the EDPB adopted a statement on age verification of users and decided to establish an AI enforcement working group. In addition, the Board also adopted recommendations regarding the World Anti-Doping Code of the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA). A representative of the Agency presented to the members of the European Data Protection Board the innovative web tool Olivia, developed in collaboration with partners within the ARC II project to support entrepreneurs in complying with the General Data Protection Regulation: https://olivia-gdpr-arc.eu/hr.

In the statement on age verification, the European Data Protection Board outlines ten principles for the compliant processing of personal data when determining the age or age range of an individual. The aim of the statement is to ensure a consistent European approach to user age verification, protect minors, and simultaneously respect data protection principles.

The President of the European Data Protection Board, Anu Talus, stated: “Ensuring user age verification is crucial to ensure that children do not access content that is not appropriate for their age. At the same time, the method of age verification must be as minimally intrusive as possible, and the personal data of children must be protected. The principles proposed by the European Data Protection Board will help the industry assess an individual's age in a manner that is consistent with data protection principles while simultaneously safeguarding the welfare of children.”

The EDPB is also collaborating with the European Commission on age verification in the context of the working group for the Digital Services Act.

During the plenary session, the Board also decided to expand the scope of the ChatGPT working group, which will now become the AI enforcement working group. Furthermore, EDPB members emphasized the need for coordination of actions among data protection authorities regarding urgent sensitive issues and will establish a rapid response team for this purpose.

The President of the European Data Protection Board, Anu Talus, stated: “The General Data Protection Regulation is a legal framework that promotes responsible innovation. The General Data Protection Regulation is designed to maintain high standards of data protection while fully harnessing the potential of innovations, such as artificial intelligence, for the benefit of our economy. The EDPB's working group on AI enforcement and the future rapid response team will play a key role in ensuring that balance, coordinating the actions of data protection authorities, and supporting these authorities in navigating complex situations related to artificial intelligence while respecting robust data protection principles.”

At the plenary session, the European Data Protection Board also adopted recommendations regarding the World Anti-Doping Code of the World Anti-Doping Agency. When processing personal data for anti-doping purposes, it is crucial to respect and protect the personal data of athletes. This will often involve the processing of sensitive personal data, such as health data obtained from biological samples.

The recommendations of the European Data Protection Board relate to key data protection principles, such as the need for an appropriate legal basis for processing personal data and purpose limitation. The recommendations also address the fact that individuals must be fully informed about the processing of their personal data and that they can effectively exercise their rights.
