European Data Protection Board (EDPB) has initiated an enforcement action under the Coordinated Enforcement Framework (CEF) for the year 2025. Following the coordinated action in 2024 focused on the right of the data subject to access, this year's action is directed at the right to erasure or the "right to be forgotten" (Article 17 of the GDPR).  
The Board selected this topic during the plenary session in October 2024 as it is one of the most frequently exercised rights under the GDPR, and it is also a right for which supervisory authorities often receive complaints from individuals.  
Throughout 2025, 32 data protection supervisory authorities across Europe will participate in this initiative, including the Personal Data Protection Agency.  
Supervisory authorities will soon contact a number of data controllers from various sectors across Europe, either by initiating new official investigations or conducting compliance checks.  
Supervisory authorities will examine how data controllers handle requests for data erasure that they receive, and specifically how they apply the conditions and exceptions for exercising that right.  
The results of national actions will be consolidated and analyzed to gain deeper insights into the topic, enabling targeted further activities at both the national and EU levels.  
The Coordinated Enforcement Framework (CEF) is a key initiative of the EDPB as part of its strategy for the period 2024-2027, aimed at improving enforcement and cooperation among data protection supervisory authorities.  
Over the past three years, three previous CEF actions have been carried out on different topics:  

1. the use of cloud-based services in the public sector,  
2. the appointment and position of data protection officers, and  
3. the implementation of the right of access to data by data controllers.
