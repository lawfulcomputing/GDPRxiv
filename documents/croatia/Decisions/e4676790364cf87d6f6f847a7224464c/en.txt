**Agency for the Protection of Personal Data for the Upcoming Local Elections** (elections for members of representative bodies of units of local and regional self-government and elections for municipal mayors, city mayors, and county prefects and their deputies) to be held on May 18, 2025, issues recommendations and obligations that political parties, candidates, and other participants must adhere to during the candidacy process and electoral promotion, in order to ensure that the processing of personal data of respondents or voters is in accordance with the legislative framework on data protection. 

In principle, any processing of personal data within the scope of the General Data Protection Regulation must respect the processing principles set out in Article 5, must be based on one of the legal grounds for lawful processing from Article 6, and, in the case that special categories of personal data (such as data on political opinions) are processed, must be conducted only with adequate respect for Article 9 of the General Data Protection Regulation.

**CANDIDACY – COLLECTION OF SIGNATURES**  
For the validity of party candidacies and candidacies proposed by a group of voters for the election of municipal mayors, city mayors, and county prefects and their deputies, it is necessary to collect the prescribed number of signatures from voters. According to Article 10 of the Law on Local Elections, voter signatures are collected on a prescribed form whose content and format are determined by the State Electoral Commission, and the following personal data are entered:

1. Name, surname, residence of the voter, number of a valid ID card and place of issuance, and signature of the voter.
2. Name, surname, nationality, residence, date of birth, personal identification number (OIB), and gender of the proposed candidates.

**PUBLICATION OF PERSONAL DATA OF DONORS ON THE DIP PAGES**  
Regarding the publication of reports on donations, in accordance with the Law on Financing Political Activities, Electoral Promotion, and Referendums, political parties and persons authorized to represent independent lists or candidates of groups of voters are obliged to submit reports on donations to the State Electoral Commission, which is in turn obliged to publish the said reports on its website. The report contains the personal name or title and address, as well as the personal identification number of the donor, the date of the donation payment, the amount donated, and the type of each individual donation. Data on the address of individual donors are not published.

**ELECTORAL PROMOTION**  
During the electoral campaign, candidates contact citizens and advertise themselves through various channels to promote themselves and their programs. For the purposes of advertising and sending personalized messages to voters, they can generally rely on the consent of the respondents or on legitimate interest. For example, if a person is a member of a political party that has proposed a certain candidate and/or is a donor to that party, they can expect personalized messages, meaning that the political party or candidate has a legitimate interest in sending such messages. 

However, if a political party/candidate addresses a voter who has not given consent for the processing of personal data for these purposes or if the voter is neither a member nor a supporter of that party/candidate or has not provided their personal data, such processing of personal data would not be in accordance with data protection regulations. 

Furthermore, we emphasize that every marketing message (SMS, email) should also contain a notice that the recipient can object to further receipt of such messages at any time (for example, the sent message must include a link to unsubscribe from such messages). If the respondent or voter objects to receiving messages, the political party/candidate must no longer send them. 

In light of the above, the Agency recommends that all political parties and candidates address citizens/voters as much as possible in ways that do not necessarily involve the processing of their personal data (for example, through public media, public forums, distributing non-personalized leaflets, brochures, and other materials in which they present and explain their program). For sending personalized brochures, letters, leaflets, and other materials (including sending SMS or emails) addressed to a specific citizen/voter with the inclusion of their personal data, it is necessary to ensure the existence of a lawful legal basis. 

Candidates and political parties must inform citizens in a transparent, clear, and concise manner about the processing of their personal data, as prescribed by Articles 13 and 14 of the General Data Protection Regulation (for what purpose personal data will be used; what is the legal basis for data processing; how long the data will be stored; with whom the data will be shared, etc.). For example, if a candidate has their own website, they must provide all relevant information about the processing of citizens' or voters' personal data through the privacy policy. 

After the expiration of the prescribed storage periods, all those processing personal data for electoral purposes are obliged to adequately destroy/delete them. 

In light of the above, we appeal to political parties and candidates to process the personal data of citizens or party members, supporters, in accordance with the legislative framework governing the protection of personal data, as in the case of violations of the provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation, the Agency has the authority to impose corrective measures (e.g., issue an official warning, caution, restrict or prohibit processing, etc.), the strictest of which is an administrative monetary fine.

**RIGHTS OF CITIZENS/VOTERS**  
During the electoral campaign, citizens often complain about receiving messages (either via SMS or email) inviting them to vote for a particular candidate. 

We emphasize that at any time, citizens have the right to object to the processing of their personal data, or they can object to receiving such messages, regardless of whether they have given consent or whether the political party/candidate has a legitimate interest. This right should be explicitly communicated to the voter and presented clearly and separately from all other information. If a political party/candidate continues to send marketing messages even after an objection has been made, citizens can contact the Agency for the Protection of Personal Data. 

We also emphasize that...
It is stated that if citizens receive a message from a political party/candidate, and they are not a member or supporter of that party, nor have they shared their email or mobile number with it, they have the right to request access to their personal data (including information about the source of their personal data) from the data controller (e.g., the political party from which they received the message). The data controller must respond to the request without unnecessary delay, and no later than within one month.

We encourage citizens to inform themselves about their rights guaranteed by the General Data Protection Regulation and to inquire with the data controller about the processing of their personal data. More information about rights and how to exercise them can be found at the following link: https://azop.hr/prava-ispitanika/

If respondents believe that their personal data is being processed contrary to the aforementioned conditions, in accordance with Article 34 of the Act on the Implementation of the General Data Protection Regulation, they may submit a request for the determination of a violation of the right to personal data protection to the Agency for Personal Data Protection at https://azop.hr/zahtjev-za-utvrdivanje-povrede-prava/.
