During its deliberation session on September 15, 2023, the National Commission for Data Protection (hereinafter: the "CNPD" or the "National Commission") sitting in plenary formation decided to open an investigation into the company "A" based on article 38 of the law of August 1, 2018 organizing the National Commission for Data Protection and the general regime on data protection (hereinafter: the "law of August 1, 2018") and to appoint Mr. Alain Herrmann as the head of the investigation. 
2. 
The said decision specified that the investigation conducted by the National Commission aimed to verify the application and compliance with regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (hereinafter: the "GDPR"), the law of August 1, 2018, and more specifically the compliance with article 30 of the GDPR concerning the register of processing activities and legal texts providing specific provisions regarding the protection of personal data.1

5.

6.

8. At the end of its investigation, the head of the investigation notified the controlled party on February 6, 2024, of a "finding of absence of grievances," specifying that the checks carried out in the context of the investigation led him not to retain grievances related to the subject of the investigation against the controlled party (hereinafter referred to as the "finding of absence of grievances").

9. By email dated February 28, 2024, the head of the investigation transmitted the investigation file to the National Commission sitting in a restricted formation (hereinafter referred to as the "Restricted Formation") along with a proposal for closure (hereinafter referred to as the "proposal for closure").

10. The Restricted Formation examined the case during the deliberation session on March 20, 2024.

11. The decision of the Restricted Formation on the outcome of the investigation is based on:
- the maintenance by the controlled party of a register of processing activities pursuant to Article 30.1, 3, and 4 of the GDPR; and
- the legal and regulatory provisions taken into account by the head of the investigation in his communication of absence of grievances.

12. Article 30 of the GDPR states that:
"1. Each controller and, where applicable, the representative of the controller shall maintain a record of processing activities under its responsibility. This record shall contain all of the following information:
a) the name and contact details of the controller and, where applicable, the joint controller, the representative of the controller, and the data protection officer;
b) the purposes of the processing;
c) a description of the categories of data subjects and of the categories of personal data;
d) the categories of recipients to whom the personal data have been or will be disclosed, including recipients in third countries or international organizations;

13. The Restricted Formation first notes that during the on-site visit on September 20, 2023, the controlled party stated that it had established a register of processing activities (hereinafter referred to as the "processing register"), but that it was not able to provide it to the agents of the CNPD "given that the Controlled Party is in a transition period due to […]. The register of processing activities is presented in the form of several documents extracted from different tools (particularly the HR part) […], which will no longer be the case in the future and is in the process of being integrated into a new tool." However, the processing register was shown to the agents of the CNPD during their on-site visit. 

14. All documents requested by the agents of the CNPD during the on-site visit, including the processing register, were communicated to them by the controlled party on September 21 and 22, 2023.

15. By email dated October 26, 2023, the controlled party specified that the agents of the CNPD noted, during the on-site visit, the existence of the register, notably through photographs taken.

The Restricted Formation further notes that the head of the investigation states in the absence of grievances report that "the Controlled presented to the agents of the CNPD, on the day of the on-site visit, a register of processing activities. The register was communicated to the agents in electronic format the day after the on-site visit and contains all the categories of information required under Article 30.1 of the GDPR." She therefore notes that it appears from the investigation file that the agents of the CNPD checked the existence of the register of processing activities of the controlled entity and verified that all the information required by Article 30.1 of the GDPR was included. 

17. She finally notes that the head of the investigation specified in the closure proposal that he believed the investigation had not revealed any facts constituting a violation of the GDPR or the law of August 1, 2018. 

18. Consequently, after analyzing the file, the Restricted Formation finds that the register of processing activities of the controlled entity indeed contains all the information required by Article 30.1 of the GDPR. She therefore agrees with the proposal of the head of the investigation and is of the opinion that the investigation can be closed in accordance with Article 10.2.a) of the internal regulations of the CNPD. 

In light of the developments above, the National Commission sitting in a restricted formation, after deliberation, decides: 
- to close the investigation opened during the session of September 15, 2023, of the National Commission for Data Protection regarding the company "A."
