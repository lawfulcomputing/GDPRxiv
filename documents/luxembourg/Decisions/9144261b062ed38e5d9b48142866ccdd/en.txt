I. Facts and Procedure 
1. 
On March 10, 2020, the National Commission for Data Protection (hereinafter: the “CNPD” or the “National Commission”) received a complaint from Ms. X (hereinafter: the “complainant” or “the data subject”) regarding a surveillance measure via a video surveillance system, which allegedly led to her immediate dismissal. In this context, the complainant reported to the CNPD potential violations of the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (hereinafter: the “GDPR”). 
2. 
In the course of the investigation of the complaint, the Municipal Administration of […] specified that a video surveillance system had been installed outside all municipal buildings in 2013 “in order to ensure a certain level of security for both employees and users of the facilities”1. Upon request2, the Municipal Administration of […] confirmed that it had reviewed the video surveillance footage “in order to accurately qualify the serious faults”3.   
3. 
During its deliberation session on July 7, 2023, the National Commission sitting in plenary decided to open an investigation with the Municipal Administration of […], located at […], L-[…] (hereinafter: the “controlled entity”), based on Article 38 of the law of August 1, 2018, on the organization of the National Commission for Data Protection and the general regime on data protection (hereinafter: the “law of August 1, 2018”) and to appoint Mr. Alain Herrmann as the head of the investigation. 
4. 
The aforementioned decision specified that the investigation conducted by the National Commission aimed to verify, in the context of complaint no. […], the application and compliance by the Municipal Administration of […] with the provisions of the GDPR, the law of August 1, 2018, and legal texts providing specific provisions regarding the protection of personal data, and more particularly those relating to the purpose of the contested processing (video surveillance)4.

II. In law 
II.1. On the grounds for the decision 
A. On the breach related to the principle of purpose limitation 
1. On the principles 
15. 
Article 5.1.b) of the GDPR states that personal data must be "collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes, and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes; […] (purpose limitation); […]". 
16. 
In the context of video surveillance, the European Data Protection Board (hereinafter: the "EDPB") has specified that before using a video device "the purposes of the processing must be specified in detail [Article 5, paragraph 1, point b)]. Video surveillance can have many objectives, such as the protection of property and other assets, […]. These surveillance objectives must be documented in writing (Article 5, paragraph 2) and specified for each surveillance camera used." 
17. 
More generally, the EDPB has clarified in its guidelines 4/2019 relating to Article 25 (Data protection by design and by default) that "in the case of further processing, the data controller must first ensure that the purposes of this further processing are compatible with the purposes

Article 6.4 of the GDPR states that “when processing for a purpose other than that for which the data was collected is not based on the consent of the data subject or on Union law or the law of a Member State which constitutes a necessary and proportionate measure in a democratic society to safeguard the objectives referred to in Article 23, paragraph 1, the controller, in order to determine whether the processing for another purpose is compatible with the purpose for which the personal data was initially collected, takes into account, among other things:

b) the context in which the personal data was collected, in particular regarding the relationship between the data subjects and the controller;

d) the possible consequences of the intended further processing for the data subjects;  
e) the existence of appropriate safeguards, which may include encryption or pseudonymization.”

II.2. On the administrative fine and corrective measures 
1. On the principles 
30. 
In accordance with Article 12 of the law of August 1, 2018, the National Commission 
has the powers provided for in Article 58.2 of the GDPR: 
“a) warn a controller or a processor that the intended processing operations are likely to violate the provisions of this regulation;  
b) reprimand a controller or a processor when the processing operations have resulted in a violation of the provisions of this regulation;  
c) order the controller or the processor to comply with requests made by the data subject in order to exercise their rights under this regulation; 
d) order the controller or the processor to bring the processing operations into compliance with the provisions of this regulation, where appropriate, specifically and within a determined timeframe; 
e) order the controller to communicate to the data subject a personal data breach;

34. In the statement of objections, the head of the investigation proposed to the Restricted Formation to adopt the following corrective measures "within a period of 3 months from the notification to the Controlled of the decision made by the Restricted Formation:
