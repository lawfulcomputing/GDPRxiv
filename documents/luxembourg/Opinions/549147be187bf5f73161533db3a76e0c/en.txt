1. 
In accordance with Article 57.1.e) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation) (hereinafter referred to as the "GDPR"), to which Article 7 of the law of August 1, 2018 on the organization of the National Commission for Data Protection and the general regime on data protection refers, the National Commission for Data Protection (hereinafter referred to as the "National Commission" or the "CNPD") "advises, in accordance with the law of the Member State, the national parliament, the government, and other institutions and bodies regarding legislative and administrative measures relating to the protection of the rights and freedoms of natural persons with regard to processing."

Furthermore, Article 36.4 of the GDPR states that "[m]ember states consult the supervisory authority in the context of the preparation of a legislative proposal to be adopted by a national parliament, or of a regulatory measure based on such a legislative measure, which relates to processing."

The European Data Protection Board (hereinafter referred to as the "EDPB") further specifies that for any limitation on the rights of data subjects adopted at the level of Member States, the data protection supervisory authority must be consulted before the adoption of such a measure by the national parliament.

2. 
By letter dated October 20, 2023, the Ministry of Health invited the National Commission to express its opinion on Bill No. 8331 amending the amended law of August 1, 2018 on the mandatory declaration of certain diseases within the framework of public health protection (hereinafter referred to as the "draft bill"). Furthermore, the said Ministry referred the CNPD to the EDPB regarding the draft Grand-Ducal regulation specifying the modalities and conditions for the establishment of the electronic vaccination record.

3. **Although the National Commission does not wish to challenge the usefulness of establishing an electronic vaccination record (hereinafter referred to as the "digital record") intended to replace the physical record, known as the "yellow card," for the benefit of the patient as well as for improving the efficiency and transparency of national vaccination campaigns, it emphasizes that sufficient guarantees regarding the respect for the fundamental principles of the right to the protection of personal data must be implemented.**

4. **In general, the CNPD welcomes in this context that the draft law and the draft Grand-Ducal regulation intend to provide a legal basis for the processing of data carried out in the context of the establishment of the digital record and its subsequent use. It notes that the digital record aims, among other things, to improve patient care by enabling the healthcare provider to deliver quality healthcare, on one hand, and allowing the patient to make an informed decision regarding vaccination, on the other hand, while granting the State the ability to manage vaccine stocks and monitor and evaluate the vaccination coverage of recommended vaccines. Its implementation could therefore correspond to a public interest mission in accordance with Article 6.1.e) of the GDPR.**

**Article 6.3 of the GDPR provides in this context a specific constraint related to the lawfulness of data processing necessary for the fulfillment of a public interest mission or related to the exercise of public authority vested in the data controller. In this case, the basis and notably the purposes of data processing must be specifically provided either by Union law or by the law of the Member State to which the data controller is subject, and this basis must meet "a public interest objective and be proportionate to the legitimate objective pursued."**

**I. Regarding the Compliance with GDPR Requirements**  
1. In order to ensure compliance with the requirements of the GDPR, particularly concerning the exercise of the rights of the data subject, the guidelines of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) state that "it is appropriate to allocate responsibilities for compliance, as is evident from the use of the term 'respective' in Article 26, paragraph 1. This does not exclude that Union law or the law of a Member State may already establish certain responsibilities for each joint controller. If this is the case, the agreement between the joint controllers should also cover any additional responsibilities necessary to ensure compliance with the GDPR requirements that are not covered by legal provisions."  

2. Paragraphs (3) and (4) of Article 2 of the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation specify, in this context, the modalities for exercising the rights of data subjects. The CNPD wishes to draw the attention of the authors of the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation to the fact that "[i]t is only a matter of defining, in their internal relations, which party is obliged to respond to the requests of the data subjects." Indeed, regardless of the terms of the agreement between joint controllers, the data subject may contact each of the joint controllers in accordance with Article 26.3 of the GDPR.

Log files may also serve the purpose of ensuring the right of access of the concerned individual to personal data relating to them, and more particularly to data concerning the recipients of the data in accordance with Article 15.1.e) of the GDPR. IV.  
Regarding the right to object  
30.  
It follows from the future Article 4bis (7) of the amended law of August 1, 2018, on the mandatory declaration of certain diseases that neither patients nor healthcare providers can oppose the recording of their data in the digital health record, on the one hand, and that they are informed of this limitation prior to the first recording of their data, on the other hand. Article 1 (2) of the draft Grand-Ducal regulation states in this context that it is the responsibility of the healthcare provider to provide the patient, before the creation of their digital health record, "the information regarding the processing of personal data and in particular regarding the limitation of the right to object." The commentary on Article 2 of the draft law contains justifications for the necessity of limiting the right to object of the concerned individuals "in order to achieve the public health purposes for which the electronic vaccination record is established and in particular to determine the vaccination coverage rate as well as to ensure traceability and manage the stocks of vaccines distributed by the State." It is also specified that it is "in the interest of public health to limit the individual right of persons to oppose the recording of their personal data" and that such "a measure is necessary and proportionate because without it the benefits of the [digital] record cannot be realized."  
31.  
The CNPD wishes to emphasize that, in principle, every concerned individual has the right to object "at any time, for reasons relating to their particular situation, to the processing of personal data concerning them based on Article 6, paragraph 1, point e) or f), including profiling based on these provisions," in accordance with Article 21 of the GDPR. In this case, "the data controller shall no longer process the personal data unless it demonstrates that there are legitimate and compelling grounds for the processing which override the interests, rights, and freedoms of the concerned individual, or for the establishment, exercise, or defense of legal claims."  
32.  
Limitations on the scope of this right may be provided for by European Union law or the law of a Member State through legislative measures based on Article 23 of the GDPR. Clarifications on these two points should therefore be integrated into the body of the text of the draft law.

37. Finally, the EDPS is of the opinion that "a general exclusion of the rights of data subjects with respect to all or certain data processing operations or with respect to certain data controllers would not respect the essence of the fundamental right to the protection of personal data as enshrined in the Charter." The French counterpart of the CNPD, the National Commission for Information Technology and Civil Liberties (CNIL), has also stated that "the limitations provided by law must therefore be proportionate and must not generally undermine the rights guaranteed by the GDPR. In this regard, the following should be prohibited: the exclusion, for the same processing, of all the rights provided by the GDPR as well as the general exclusion of the right to object for all processing carried out by a public authority."

38. The National Commission can only align itself with these findings. Therefore, it is essential that all other rights conferred to data subjects by Articles 12 to 20 and 22 of the GDPR are fully respected by the various data controllers involved, namely by the eHealth Agency, the health authority, as well as the various healthcare providers. Furthermore, the limitation of the right to object cannot solely target the processing of personal data carried out through the digital health record and in no case other processing carried out by the said data controllers.

Thus adopted in Belvaux on February 23, 2024.
