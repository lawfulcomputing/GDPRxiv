Bill No. 8330 on the organization of the Cadastre and Topography Administration aims to "record the current legislative reality," and to repeal and replace the aforementioned law of July 25, 2002. Indeed, according to the explanatory memorandum, "[s]ince the entry into force of the law of July 25, 2002, reorganizing the Cadastre and Topography Administration (...), the Cadastre and Topography Administration (...) has undergone profound changes leading towards its modernization."
In its opinion of February 27, 2024, the State Council suggested splitting the aforementioned bill "into two distinct bills, so as to provide for a first bill that addresses Article 18 amending the aforementioned law of March 19, 1988, and a second bill that addresses Articles 1 to 17, 19, and 20 relating to the Administration."
The Finance Committee, during its meeting on March 6, 2024, decided to follow the recommendation of the State Council and to split the aforementioned bill into two distinct bills:
Bill 8330A amending the modified law of March 19, 1988, on land publicity in the context of co-ownership, which only includes Article 18 of the initial Bill 8330; and
Bill 8330B on the organization of the Cadastre and Topography Administration, which includes the remaining articles (hereinafter referred to as the "bill").
In parallel, a draft Grand-Ducal regulation concerning the consultation and dissemination of documentation related to official measurement, cadastral documentation, topographic documentation, the national register of localities and streets, and the national coordinate reference systems managed by the Cadastre and Topography Administration; and establishing the fee schedule to be collected for the benefit of the State for office and field work carried out by the Cadastre and Topography Administration (hereinafter referred to as the "draft Grand-Ducal regulation"), has been developed in the context of the bill.

B. Regarding Article 3  
10.  
Article 3.2 of the draft law enshrines the principle of “land publicity.” Such provisions are new and do not appear in the amended law of July 25, 2002, concerning the reorganization of the administration of the cadastre and topography, which is intended to be repealed by the draft text. It is appropriate to commend the authors of the draft law for incorporating such a principle into the law.  
11. It is evident from the explanatory memorandum that “[t]he principle of ‘land publicity’ allows the public to be informed about the legal status of a property and includes, on the one hand, information related to the land cadastre and, on the other hand, mortgage publicity. This principle is included in the present draft law to explain the objective of consulting and disseminating data from the land register.”  
12.  
The authors of the draft law further explain that such an objective “lies in the application of the principle of land publicity through the ‘land publicity information system.’ This system, the functionalities of which are not detailed, would be managed by the State’s Information Technology Center and would integrate ‘data provided by notaries, the Administration of Registration, Domains and VAT, and the Administration of the Cadastre and Topography.’ The CNPD is therefore unable to assess whether such a system would comply with data protection.”  
13. It is also specified that the ACT would transmit to interested third parties “files related to the legal status of properties, in the form of an extract from the land register through the land publicity information system. The Grand-Ducal regulation determines the extent of the information transmitted depending on the quality of the requester” and that this transmission “is made within the limits of the information available to the administration and in compliance with the principles arising from the rules” regarding data protection. The CNPD will return below to the provisions of the draft Grand-Ducal regulation aimed at framing the principle of data communication to interested third parties.

18. The CNPD wishes to remind that Article 1.4 of the law of November 29, 2021, on open data and the reuse of public sector information, as well as Article 1.4 of Directive (EU) 2019/2024 of June 20, 2019, concerning open data and the reuse of public sector information, specify that their provisions are without prejudice to the rules relating to data protection. Recital 52 of the said directive states that "this directive does not affect the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data guaranteed by Union law and the law of the Member States, in particular Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and Directive 2002/58/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council, but also including any complementary national law provisions. This means, among other things, that the reuse of personal data is lawful only if the principle of purpose limitation set out in Article 5(1)(b) and Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 is respected."

19. Therefore, the public availability of the data referred to in the draft texts by the ACT, under the principle of "property publicity," does not imply that the reuse of such data by third parties is automatically lawful and legitimate within the meaning of Articles 5 and 6 of the GDPR solely because these data are considered "public." Indeed, without questioning the principle of "property publicity" (even if the principle is not defined in the law), particular vigilance should be exercised regarding the legality of the processing carried out by real estate professionals for commercial prospecting purposes. Such processing would only be lawful if Articles 5 and 6 of the GDPR are respected.

B. On Article 4
20. The authors of the Grand-Ducal Regulation should be commended for specifying the recipients.

21. Article 4.2 of the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation provides that the issuance of extracts from the land register may be made "to any identified interested party within the limits of ten requests per calendar month."
22. In this regard, the authors of the draft Grand-Ducal regulation specify that these new provisions aim to limit "this request to ten parcels or ten condominium lots per request and within a limit of ten requests per civil month, as inspired by French legislation in this matter, more specifically decree no. 2012-59 of January 18, 2012, relating to the provision to the public of certain cadastral information. This limitation aims to preserve the privacy of individuals while ensuring the principle of land publicity. The identification of applicants, necessary for verifying the admissibility of requests, is done through an identity document."

23. It is appropriate to commend the introduction of such provisions that could help limit abuses regarding the communication of data.

24. Furthermore, it is particularly important to draw the attention of the authors of the draft Grand-Ducal regulation to the interpretation of Article 15.1(e) of the GDPR, which grants the data subject the right to obtain from the data controller information on the recipients or categories of recipients to whom the personal data have been disclosed, following the judgment of the Court of Justice of the European Union (hereinafter "CJEU") of January 12, 2023. In this judgment, the CJEU considered that Article 15.1(e) of the GDPR "must be interpreted as meaning that: the right of access of the data subject to personal data concerning them, provided for by this provision, implies, when such data have been or will be communicated to recipients, the obligation for the data controller to provide that person with the identity of those recipients, unless it is impossible to identify those recipients or unless the data controller demonstrates that the access requests from the data subject are manifestly unfounded or excessive, within the meaning of Article 12(5) of Regulation 2016/679, in which case the data controller may only indicate to that person the categories of recipients concerned" (emphasis in bold).

25. However, in the absence of a strong authentication system for individuals making a request for an extract from the land register, the ACT will not be able to comply with the request of the CNPD.

If it emerges from the commentary on the articles that "the identification of applicants, necessary for verifying the admissibility of requests, is done using an identity document," the National Commission wonders whether such a solution would be transferable to requests made electronically, as well as to requests from legal entities.  
26. Thus, it is regrettable that the concrete modalities of such a mechanism are not specified in the draft text. Regarding the communication of data by the ACT electronically, the CNPD assumes that it will rely on the current procedure that allows for the communication of extracts of cadastral documentation on the geoportail.lu website via a user account. If the National Commission understands that the identification of natural persons would be done using an identity card, the modalities of such control in the context of electronic communication are not specified. Furthermore, what about the means of identification for legal entities?  
In any case, the National Commission deems it essential to establish a strong authentication system for individuals initiating the request for an extract of the cadastral documentation.

C. Concerning Article 6  
28. Article 6 aims to provide "online access rights for a part or the entirety of the land register" to the entities listed in said article. These provisions replicate the provisions of Article 9 of the Grand-Ducal Regulation of August 10, 2018, which is intended to be repealed by the text under review.  
29. To the extent that direct access to the land register is provided, it should be recalled that the data controller must respect the principle of integrity and confidentiality referred to in Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR. Furthermore, Article 32 of the GDPR states that "the data controller and the processor implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk." Such measures must be implemented to avoid, in particular, unauthorized access to data, data breaches, or unwanted modifications.  
30. It is important to emphasize that it is strongly recommended to define an access management policy, in order to identify from the outset the person or service within the concerned entity, and to which specific data this person or service would have access.  
31. Moreover, it is necessary to provide for an access logging system. In this regard, the CNPD recommends that log data be retained for a period of five years from their recording, after which they are deleted, unless they are subject to a control procedure.  
32. Furthermore, the National Commission emphasizes the importance of proactively conducting internal controls. To this end, it is necessary, in accordance with Article 32.1.d) of the GDPR, to implement a procedure "aimed at testing, analyzing, and regularly evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing."  
33. Finally, the CNPD wonders what measures would be put in place by the ACD to ensure compliance with the provisions of Article 4.2.1° of the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation, which states that "any identified interested party" may obtain an extract from the land register "within the limit of ten requests per calendar month."
