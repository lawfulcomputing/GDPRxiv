Notice from the National Commission for Data Protection
regarding the draft Grand-Ducal regulation on the energy data platform
Deliberation No. 21/AV9/2024 of April 26, 2024

In accordance with Article 57. 1.e) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation) (hereinafter referred to as the "GDPR"), which is referenced in Article 7 of the law of August 1, 2018, on the organization of the National Commission for Data Protection and the general regime on data protection, the National Commission for Data Protection (hereinafter referred to as the "National Commission" or the "CNPD") "advises, in accordance with the law of the Member State, the national parliament, the government, and other institutions and bodies regarding legislative and administrative measures relating to the protection of the rights and freedoms of natural persons with regard to processing."

Furthermore, Article 36. 4 of the GDPR states that "Member States consult the supervisory authority in the context of the preparation of a legislative proposal to be adopted by a national parliament, or a regulatory measure based on such a legislative measure, which relates to processing."

By letter dated October 3, 2023, Mr. Minister of Energy and Spatial Planning invited the National Commission to express its opinion on the draft Grand-Ducal regulation regarding the energy data IT platform, approved by the Government Council in its meeting of September 29, 2023 (hereinafter referred to as the "draft Grand-Ducal regulation").

The draft Grand-Ducal regulation aims to specify certain implementing measures relating to the energy data IT platform provided for in Article 27ter of the amended law of August 1, 2007, on the organization of the electricity market. The provisions of said article had already been commented on by the CNPD in its opinion of July 1, 2022, regarding draft law No. 78761.

1. 
On the various actors of the platform

5. 
The provisions of Article 3 of the draft Grand-Ducal regulation aim to clarify the responsibilities of the various actors of the platform from the perspective of the GDPR. Article 3. 1 of the draft Grand-Ducal regulation states that "in accordance with Article 27ter, paragraph 1, subparagraph 1, of the amended law of August 1, 2007, on the organization of the electricity market, the operator of the platform acts as the data controller within the meaning of the [GDPR] for all processing of energy data carried out on or by the platform. As for the energy data referred to in Article 2, paragraphs 1, point 2°, 2, point 2°, and 4, point 2°, electricity and natural gas companies are third parties within the meaning of the [GDPR] responsible for collecting this data from the data subjects and for importing it into the platform. The operator of the platform is responsible for the processing of the data imported through the interfaces referred to in Article 2, paragraphs 1, point 1°, letter b), and 4, point 1°, letter a). As for the processing of personal data referred to in Article 2, paragraph 2, point 1°, letter a), point /'/'., the electricity and natural gas companies referred to therein are considered as processors of the operator of the platform within the meaning of the GDPR."

6. 
It is commendable to acknowledge the efforts of the authors of the draft Grand-Ducal regulation for such clarifications, whereas the National Commission had, in its opinion of July 1, 2022, regarding draft law No. 7876, raised that "neither the legal provisions provided by the draft law nor the explanations provided by the authors of the draft law allow for a clear determination of the respective roles of the transmission system operator and the electricity or gas companies from a data protection perspective."

Furthermore, Article 3.2 of the text under review states that “the data controller shall carry out a data protection impact assessment in accordance with Article 35 of the GDPR.” The National Commission questions whether such provisions are not superfluous since the data controller must, in any case, comply with the obligations imposed on them under the GDPR. **2) On the Role of the Different Actors of the Platform**

9. The authors of the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation clarify in their commentary on the articles that while the transmission system operator is responsible for the processing “of all operations carried out on and by the platform, it is, in accordance with paragraph 2 of the aforementioned provision, not responsible for the collection of energy data from the data subjects and consequently not responsible for the quality of this data”4.

10. As noted by the authors of the draft text under review, the transmission system operator would be the recipient of the data collected by electricity and natural gas companies. The transmission system operator would then process the received data to carry out data processing via the IT platform. It is therefore necessary to distinguish between:
- The processing carried out by electricity and natural gas companies when importing data onto the platform. In this context, electricity and natural gas companies would be classified as data controllers; and
- The processing carried out, with the received data, by the transmission system operator on the IT platform, which would be classified as the data controller for such processing.

11. Consequently, in the context of their respective data processing activities, both the transmission system operator and the electricity and natural gas companies are required to comply with the principles and obligations imposed on them under the GDPR. As data controllers, they must in particular respect the principles set out in Article 5 of the GDPR, including the principle of accuracy. Under this principle, personal data must be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date.

12. The CNPD therefore wishes to remind its observations made in its opinion regarding draft law No. 7876, in which it noted that “the provisions under review should not exempt the data controller(s) from the obligations imposed on them under the GDPR. Thus, if the transmission system operator were to be the data controller, the provisions of Article 27ter.1 of the draft law should not exclude its responsibility regarding the ‘truthfulness and accuracy of the data’ that would be used via the platform”5. Furthermore, it also recalled that “the determination of the data controller is crucial as it is on the latter that the compliance with all the principles set out in Article 5 of the GDPR rests. Article 24 of the GDPR further states that ‘the data controller shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and to be able to demonstrate that processing is performed in accordance with this regulation’”6. Therefore, the aforementioned provisions cannot exempt the transmission system operator from its obligations as a data controller in the context of the processing carried out on the platform referred to in the text under review.

Moreover, it is important to draw the attention of the authors of the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation to Article 19 of the GDPR, under which “the data controller shall notify each recipient to whom the personal data have been communicated of any rectification or erasure of personal data or of any restriction of processing carried out in accordance with Article 16, Article 17(1), and Article 18, unless such communication proves impossible or involves disproportionate effort.” Thus, during the processing related to the importation of data onto the platform, the electricity and natural gas companies, as data controllers, should in particular notify the transmission system operator, as the recipient, of “any rectification or erasure of personal data.”

**1.** Electricity and natural gas companies are third parties within the meaning of the [GDPR] who are responsible for collecting this data from the data subjects and for its importation into the platform.  
**14.** Regarding these provisions, the authors of the text under review specify that electricity and natural gas companies collect the data referred to in the aforementioned provisions and "act as data controllers at the time of collection from the data subjects and import it into the platform using interfaces established by the platform controller. They thus act as 'third parties' within the meaning of the aforementioned [GDPR], and are responsible for their own processing."  
Under these provisions, the National Commission understands that these entities would therefore be considered as data controllers concerning the importation of data onto the platform, as mentioned in point 10 above.  
**15.**  
However, the authors of the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation further indicate that electricity and natural gas companies act as 'third parties' within the meaning of the GDPR while being "responsible for their own processing." It should be recalled that Article 4 of the GDPR defines a third party as "a natural or legal person, a public authority, a service, or another body other than the data subject, the data controller, the processor, and the persons who, under the direct authority of the data controller or the processor, are authorized to process personal data."  
However, the use of the term "third party" does not seem to be the appropriate qualification, as it emerges from the provisions under review and the commentary on the articles that electricity and natural gas companies should be considered as responsible for the processing referred to in Articles 2.1.2°, 2.2.2°, and 2.4.2°. The use of the term "third party," combined with the notion of data controller in the same provision, is likely to create confusion in the reader's mind regarding the legal qualification of said entities.  
It is suggested to replace the aforementioned current provisions of the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation with the following provisions: "[w]ith regard to the energy data referred to in Article 2, paragraphs 1, point 2°, 2, point 2°, and 4, point 2°, electricity and natural gas companies are third parties within the meaning of the [GDPR] to be qualified as data controllers regarding their responsibility for collecting this data from the data subjects and for its importation into the platform."

**II.**  
**On Security Measures**  
**16.** In accordance with Article 5.1.f) of the GDPR, personal data must be "processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures (integrity and confidentiality)."  
**17.**  
Article 32 of the GDPR further states that "the data controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk." Such measures must be implemented to prevent unauthorized access to data, data leaks, or unwanted modifications.  
**18.**  
It should be emphasized that it is strongly recommended to define an access management policy, in order to identify from the outset the person or service within the concerned entity, and to which specific data this person or service would have access.  
**19.**  
Furthermore, it is necessary to provide for an access logging system. In this regard, the CNPD recommends that log data be retained for a period of five years from their recording, after which they are deleted, unless they are subject to a control procedure.  
**20.**  
It should also be noted the importance of proactively conducting internal controls. To this end, in accordance with Article 32.1.d) of the GDPR, a procedure should be implemented "to regularly test, analyze, and evaluate the effectiveness of technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing."  
**21.** Moreover, as noted by the Council of State in its opinion regarding draft law No. 8089, which notably provides for the creation of a secure platform for storing administrative acts, "the GDPR imposes on the data controller the obligation to implement and document all 'appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security."

Regarding the verification of the user's identity "remotely through digital verification," the authors of the draft Grand-Ducal regulation specify that "it is done, for example, through access to a banking application or the myguichet.lu platform." It is regrettable that the practical modalities of such access are not more detailed in the commentary on the articles, so that the CNPD cannot truly analyze what potential questions may arise in terms of data protection. 

Furthermore, in the absence of clarifications regarding the reuse of the term "digital," the National Commission wishes to draw the attention of the authors of the draft Grand-Ducal regulation to the consequences of the possible use of facial recognition. If this were to be the case, it would be necessary to qualify such data processing as biometric data processing. The provisions of Article 9 of the GDPR should therefore be respected. It recalls that such processing is generally prohibited unless one of the conditions of Article 9.2 of the GDPR is met and that "the general principles and other rules of the [GDPR], particularly concerning the conditions for the lawfulness of processing" must be respected. 

It should also be noted that the Luxembourg legislator has not provided specific national provisions regarding biometric data based on Article 9.4 of the GDPR.

28. Furthermore, concerning the access management policy, Article 4.2.1° of the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation states that “[e]ach user of the platform has access to this log in order to trace accesses and processing related to the data concerning them.” It should be specified that access to this log, which pertains to the traceability of accesses to data processing carried out via the platform, should primarily be possible by the data controller of the platform in order to detect and, as far as possible, prevent any compromise of the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of the data processed via the platform. Access to this log thus allows the data controller to fulfill its security obligation under Articles 24 and 32 of the GDPR.

However, the National Commission wonders what the reasons would be for such access by users of the platform who are defined as “any person with individualized access to the platform under Article 27ter, paragraphs 5 to 7, of the amended law of August 1, 2007, on the organization of the electricity market.” 

It is regrettable that the authors of the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation did not specify in the commentary on the articles the justifications for such access for each of the users of the platform, namely electricity and natural gas companies, network users, and connection takers, as well as the minister, the regulator, the Government Commissioner for Energy, or the National Institute of Statistics and Economic Studies. Clarifications on this matter should be provided.

Furthermore, if the intention of the authors of the text under review is to provide that this access would allow network users and connection takers to detect any unlawful access to their data, then such intention should be reflected in the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation. If this were to be the case, the CNPD could welcome such provisions, which would allow the aforementioned concerned parties to protect themselves against potential unlawful processing of their personal data.

3) Regarding Article 4.3  
29. Finally, Article 4.3 of the draft Grand-Ducal Regulation states that electricity and natural gas companies "shall implement appropriate technical and organizational security measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risks associated with their activities." It should be recalled that the obligations referred to in Articles 24 and 32 of the GDPR are incumbent upon them as data controllers for their own data processing.

30. Similarly, when they act as processors for the transmission system operator, they must also ensure compliance with technical and organizational security measures in accordance with Articles 32.1 and 28.3.C) of the GDPR. In this regard, it should be specified that "[e]very processing of personal data carried out by a processor shall be governed by a contract or another legal act established in writing (...). However, the processing agreement should not simply reproduce the provisions of the GDPR; it should include more specific and concrete information on how the conditions will be met and on the level of security required for the processing of personal data covered by that agreement." 

It should also be emphasized that "the data controller must always stipulate certain elements in the subcontracting agreement, such as, with regard to the security requirement, the instruction to take all necessary measures in accordance with Article 32 of the GDPR. The agreement must also state that the processor must assist the data controller in ensuring compliance with Article 32, for example. In any case, the data controller remains responsible for implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that the processing is carried out in accordance with the regulation (Article 24) and to be able to demonstrate it."
