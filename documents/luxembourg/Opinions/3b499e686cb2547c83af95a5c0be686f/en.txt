In accordance with Article 57.1.e) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of April 27, 2016, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation) (hereinafter referred to as the "GDPR"), to which Article 7 of the law of August 1, 2018, on the organization of the National Commission for Data Protection and the general regime on data protection refers, the National Commission for Data Protection (hereinafter referred to as the "National Commission" or the "CNPD") "advises, in accordance with the law of the Member State, the national parliament, the government, and other institutions and bodies regarding legislative and administrative measures relating to the protection of the rights and freedoms of natural persons with regard to processing."

By letter dated June 4, 2024, the Minister of Digitalization invited the National Commission to express its opinion on the parliamentary amendments.

1. Preliminary Remarks  
It should be recalled that Article 6.1.e) of the GDPR, read in conjunction with Article 6.3 of the GDPR, provides a specific constraint related to the lawfulness of data processing necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller.  
In this case, the basis, that is to say, the public interest mission in question, and in particular the purposes of the data processing must derive either from Union law or from the law of the Member State to which the data controller is subject, and this basis must meet "a public interest objective and is proportionate to the legitimate objective pursued."  
In a recent ruling, the Court of Justice, for example, stated that the "determination of the purposes and means of processing and, where appropriate, the designation of this controller by national law can be not only explicit but also implicit. In this latter case, it is nevertheless required that this determination arises with sufficient certainty from the role, mission, and powers conferred on the person or entity concerned."1.  
In domestic law, it should be noted that Article 31 of the revised Constitution, applicable since July 1, 2023, states that "[e]very person has the right to informational self-determination and to the...

On the creation of a digital identifier
11. It should be noted that the ability for acts falling within the scope of the bill under review, which may bear an electronic signature or seal, requires the creation of a digital identifier.
12. Although the CNPD understands that the digital identifier would be linked to an act and not directly to an administrator, it wishes to draw the attention of the authors of the parliamentary amendments to the developments expressed by its French counterpart (the National Commission for Computing and Liberties) in its deliberation No. 2008-578 of December 18, 2008, concerning an opinion on a draft order relating to the creation of an online service called "mon.service-public.fr," according to which "the commission (...) took note that the numbers allowing the connection of the portal 'mon.service-public.fr' to the partner sites chosen by users would be random and non-significant alphanumeric character strings, allowing only the link between the online services, without the MSP technical system being able to know the sectoral identifiers of users. It notes that 'mon.service-public.fr' does not lead to a grouping of sectoral identifiers around a common identifier, and that the project does not aim to create a national administrative identifier. However, it considers that this question, fundamental in light of the major principles of the amended law of January 6, 1978, should be guaranteed by texts and not merely by technical measures, and that the principle of voluntary identity federation should be clarified. It thus requests that the draft order creating the portal 'mon.service-public.fr' expressly states that the system, while creating a link under the control of the user between different sectoral identifiers, should not lead to the creation of a 'unique administrative identifier' for users."

IX.  
On the Duration of Retention  
47.  
Pursuant to the principle of data retention limitation, personal data must not be retained longer than necessary for the purposes for which they are collected and processed. Beyond this, the data must be deleted or anonymized.  
However, while data should generally be deleted once the purposes of processing have been achieved, this rule does not imply that data must be systematically deleted as soon as the objectives of processing have been met. Indeed, personal data follow a life cycle during which the data may have several successive uses. This means that for the same data, different durations may apply.  
48. The amended draft law aims to establish a retention period of 30 years concerning the following personal data:  
- the encrypted content of acts signed or sealed electronically and provided with a digital identifier; and  
- the identity of the agent of the administrative authority or the entity exercising an administrative activity that requested the creation of a digital identifier.  
49. However, in light of the previous developments, the National Commission wonders whether the authors of the parliamentary amendments have taken into account the life cycle of the collected data? Indeed, it appears from the text under review that the collected data are processed to affix an electronic signature or seal and allow the parties to access it, and then, in a second phase, are retained to ensure the authenticity of the document signed or sealed electronically, which may allow the citizens to submit such a document as evidence. Furthermore, the CNPD also questions whether distinct purposes for which the data are processed have been considered in determining the retention period.  
This analysis should be conducted to best respect the principle of retention limitation. Moreover, distinctions regarding the establishment of the retention period of data based on their life cycle or purposes should also be taken into account.

Regarding the retention of the data under review for evidentiary purposes, it should be noted that it emerges from the commentary on the articles of the draft grand-ducal regulation that the platform is "not intended to replace the electronic document management solutions currently used by administrative authorities and other entities or any potential long-term storage solutions for these acts."

53. 
The authors of the parliamentary amendments specify that "the thirty-year period corresponds to the general statute of limitations. Given that the secure platform is intended to retain the original version of an electronically signed administrative act, thus allowing for the verification of the authenticity of the act, this retention period aims to ensure that citizens can provide the necessary evidence to assert their rights under the same conditions as if they had an original in paper format. In other words, the provision aims to ensure equal treatment of all citizens regardless of the format in which an act is transmitted to them."

54. 
The National Commission understands that the duration set by the draft law under review is aligned with the 30-year statute of limitations, in order to allow citizens to provide, if necessary, proof of the signature of the electronically signed or sealed act. However, it is worth questioning whether shorter statute of limitations periods would be applicable given the great diversity of acts that are likely to be signed or sealed electronically.

55. 
It is also important to draw the attention of the authors of the parliamentary amendments to the fact that a different solution seems to have been adopted in France. Indeed, in France, legal provisions provide for the tele-service "Service-Public.fr" that "the online storage space is under the control and responsibility of its holder who can close it at any time."
