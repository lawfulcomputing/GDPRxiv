In accordance with Article 57.1.e) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of April 27, 2016 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation) (hereinafter referred to as the "GDPR"), to which Article 7 of the law of August 1, 2018 organizing the National Commission for Data Protection and the general regime on data protection refers, the National Commission for Data Protection (hereinafter referred to as the "National Commission" or "CNPD") "advises, in accordance with the law of the Member State, the national parliament, the government, and other institutions and bodies regarding legislative and administrative measures relating to the protection of the rights and freedoms of natural persons with regard to processing."

5. The bill is part of a broader reform of youth law, consisting of three bills1 and a draft grand-ducal regulation2. The bill under review aims, in particular, to establish a juvenile criminal law and seeks, according to the explanatory memorandum, to enshrine, "while drawing its sources from Luxembourg's criminal procedure, [...] at the legislative level the fundamental principles regarding specific procedural guarantees for minors as provided by the Convention on the Rights of the Child adopted by the United Nations General Assembly on November 20, 1989" (hereinafter referred to as the "Convention"), ratified in Luxembourg by a law of December 20, 1993. Incidentally, the bill intends to transpose the aspects related to the new juvenile criminal regime provided by Directive EU/2016/800 of the European Parliament and of the Council of May 11, 2016, concerning the establishment of procedural guarantees for children who are suspects or accused persons in criminal proceedings (hereinafter referred to as the "Directive EU/2016/800") and Directive 2013/48/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of October 22, 2013, concerning the right of access to a lawyer in criminal proceedings and in proceedings related to the European arrest warrant (hereinafter referred to as the "Directive 2013/48/EU").

6. This opinion will limit its observations to issues related to respect for privacy and the protection of personal data raised by the bill.

7. It should be noted that the judicial control authority, established by Article 40 of the law of August 1, 2018, on the protection of individuals with regard to the processing of personal data in criminal matters as well as in matters of national security, is the competent authority to oversee the processing operations of personal data carried out by the judicial courts, including the public prosecutor's office, and by the administrative courts in the exercise of their judicial functions. Therefore, these data processing operations do not fall under the jurisdiction of the CNPD, pursuant to Article 5 of the law of August 1, 2018, organizing the National Commission for Data Protection and the general regime on data protection and Article 39 of the law of August 1, 2018, in criminal matters. Outside of this exception, the National Commission remains the competent authority to monitor and verify compliance with the provisions of the aforementioned law as well as the GDPR. While it understands that certain provisions of the bill under review relate to processing falling under the jurisdiction of the judicial control authority, the CNPD does not intend to make an arbitrary distinction in this opinion based on the data controller processing the data in question and intends to comment on all the provisions.

IV. 
La protection des données personnelles
13. L'article 5 du projet de loi stipule que « les données à caractère personnel des mineurs
victimes et témoins doivent être traitées de manière à garantir leur sécurité et leur confidentialité ».
La CNPD souligne l'importance de cette disposition et rappelle que le traitement des données
personnelles doit respecter les principes de minimisation et de limitation de la conservation, afin de
protéger les droits des mineurs.

V. 
Les droits des victimes
14. La CNPD note que le projet de loi reconnaît explicitement les droits des mineurs victimes et
témoins, notamment le droit à l'information, le droit à la protection et le droit à l'assistance
juridique. Elle souligne que ces droits doivent être garantis tout au long de la procédure pénale,
afin de garantir une justice équitable et respectueuse des droits des enfants.

14. As indicated, the minor may be accompanied by a "trusted person" of their choice in accordance with Articles 4 and 5 of the draft law. Article 5 of the draft law defines the "missions of the trusted person," namely to "provide the minor with appropriate moral support [...]" and to "accompany the minor to any hearing or statement [...]." It is not specified whether the said trusted person can refuse this designation by the minor. The authors of the draft law indicate in the comments on the articles that the "concept of trusted person is directly inspired by the concept of 'support person' provided for in Articles 15 and following of the [model law on justice in cases involving child victims and witnesses of criminal acts]," developed by the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) in cooperation with the United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF) and the International Bureau for Children's Rights (hereinafter the "UN Model Law"). However, unlike Articles 15 to 17 of the UN Model Law, the latter remains largely silent on the procedure to be followed for designating the trusted person and does not clearly define the competencies and functions of the said person. Thus, in the said model law, by "support person" "we mean a specially trained person to assist a child throughout the justice process in order to prevent the risk of coercion, revictimization, or secondary victimization," and Article 15 of said instrument recommends that

2. Examination of the Capacity to Testify

17. Article 7, paragraph 1, of the draft law provides for the possibility for the competent judicial authority to order an assessment regarding the capacity to testify of the minor "when there is doubt about the ability of the minor victim or witness to understand the proceedings or the questions posed to them or to understand the importance of telling the truth." The provision does not specify what type of examination or on what criteria the competent judicial authority should base its decision to order such an assessment. The commentary on the articles refers to the UN model law, but the provision under review does not incorporate either the procedural guarantees or the substantive guarantees that it advocates. In this regard, the CNPD suggests amending Article 7 of the draft law, particularly by specifying that "a psychological or psychiatric examination is not ordered to assess the child's competence unless it is established that there are compelling reasons to do so" and that "the questions are posed in a manner appropriate to the child, taking into account their age and maturity, and do not concern the issues at stake but only aim to determine whether the child is capable of understanding simple questions and responding truthfully."

18. Paragraph 2 of the said article then provides that "When the examination of the capacity to testify concludes that the minor victim or witness requires care, the competent judicial authority shall forward a copy of the file to the youth judge, who proposes measures of assistance, support, and protection or a medical or psychological examination for the minor, their parents, or their legal representatives, in accordance with the law of jj/mm/yyyy concerning assistance, support, and protection for minors, young adults, and families."

19. The CNPD wonders whether it would be appropriate to adapt this paragraph and provide for the referral to the ONE, in line with the logic of draft law No. 7994, instead of the referral to the youth judge. 3. The recording of a minor's hearing

21. Article 17 of the draft law amends Article 48-1, paragraph 3, of the Code of Criminal Procedure by providing, according to the authors of the draft law, a "broader scope regarding the mandatory recording of hearings of minor victims or witnesses of certain offenses" and excluding the possibility "for the public prosecutor to refuse to proceed with a recording." Similarly, Article 158-1 of the Code of Criminal Procedure is amended to limit the number of hearings of the minor victim or minor witness. These amendments all aim to avoid the secondary victimization of the minor.

4. The taking of an oath and false statements by a child witness

22. Under Article 16 of the draft law, Article 225 of the Penal Code would provide that "[t]he provisions [...] relating to false statements [Articles 215 to 224 of the Penal Code] do not apply" to children under the age of fifteen, instead of sixteen years as currently applicable. The authors of the draft law indicate that the modification aligns the age limit with Article 156 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, which provides that "[c]hildren under the age of fifteen are heard without taking an oath." The CNPD notes that Article 22, paragraph 2, of the UN model law recommends that "[a] child witness cannot be prosecuted for perjury," without distinction of age among the minors concerned, and therefore requests the authors to clarify the reasons for this divergence. If necessary, it would be appropriate to adapt Article 156 of the Code of Criminal Procedure to align it with Article 22, paragraph 1, of the UN model law, which provides that:

"1. The president of the court or the judge may decide that the child witness will not be required to testify under oath, for example, if the child is not capable of understanding the consequences of an oath. In such cases, the president of the court or the judge may invite the child to promise to tell the truth. In either case, the court hears the testimony of the child."

1. The election of domicile with a trusted person  
23. According to Article 8, paragraph 1, points 1° and 5°, of the draft law, judicial authorities may, in cases of "serious danger to the minor due to their testimony or hearing," "order an election of domicile with a trusted person" and "order that no mention be made in any procedural document in criminal matters, nor in any piece of the procedure, of the domicile or residence of the minor or the civil status of the minor."  
24. Regarding point 1°, the CNPD questions whether it should be understood that this refers to the same trusted person mentioned in Article 5 of the draft law. It should be noted that, even though Article 4, paragraph 2, of the draft law states that "[as far as possible, the minor is assisted by the same trusted person...]," this person "may choose at any time another trusted person." Furthermore, the CNPD wonders whether the trusted person can refuse this election of domicile at their private residence and the disclosure of said address to the accused, particularly considering the serious danger identified to justify this decision. Thus, the CNPD questions whether it would not be appropriate to provide for an election of domicile with the minor's counsel, as this would be a priori a professional address.  
25. Concerning point 5°, care should be taken to ensure that this election of domicile is reflected in all documents sent to the person from whom the "serious danger" emanates so that the measure is not rendered meaningless by the inadvertent transmission of the minor's actual address. Thus, as raised by the judicial authorities in their joint opinion of September 19, 2022, "the omission of the minor's address from the file only makes sense if it is applied from the very beginning of the procedure, thus from the first police report that mentions the minor as a victim or witness. The address should be omitted in this report but communicated to the judicial authorities in a sealed envelope."

**Procedural Acts Involving a Minor Witness or Minor Victim**

1. Procedural acts involving a minor witness or a minor victim do not systematically indicate their identity, address, and other identifying information, and such information is only made accessible to the other parties in the proceedings to the extent strictly necessary. The National Commission refers to Article 1 of the UN Model Law on the Best Interests of the Child, which states that “[...] while the rights of the accused and convicted must be preserved, every child, especially if they are a victim or witness, has the right to have their best interests considered as a priority.”

2. The National Commission notes with interest the provisions introduced to limit the number of hearings of minor victims and witnesses by using audio or audiovisual recordings through various amendments to the Code of Criminal Procedure introduced by Article 17 of the draft law. It recalls that adequate technical and organizational means must be implemented to ensure the security and confidentiality of these recordings.

**3. Conditions for the Hearing of the Minor Victim or Minor Witness**

3. The CNPD welcomes that Article 190 of the Code of Criminal Procedure is supplemented by a paragraph providing that “[i]nsofar as possible, waiting rooms used by minor victims or witnesses should not be visible or accessible to the accused. A courtroom may be specially designated for this purpose by the president of the court,” thus echoing Article 24 of the UN Model Law dedicated to this issue. By ensuring to limit, during hearings, the contact between minor victims and witnesses and the accused, the risks of secondary victimization can thus be reduced.

4. It notes that Article 19, paragraph 1, of Directive 2012/29/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of October 25, 2012, establishing minimum standards on the rights, support, and protection of victims of crime seems to go further, at least for minor victims, requiring that “[m]ember States establish conditions to avoid any contact between the victim and the members of their family, where applicable, and the offender in the premises where the criminal proceedings take place, unless the criminal proceedings impose such contact.”

**V. Right to Information**

5. Articles 11 and 14 of the draft law enshrine the right of the minor victim or minor witness to obtain information about their procedural rights, including the right to be accompanied, the possibility of benefiting from protective measures, and the possibility of appointing a guardian ad litem.

6. The National Commission welcomes that the draft law provides for the communication of this information also to the legal representatives of the minor and to their trusted person, and this as the proceedings progress. Furthermore, the CNPD notes that the Attorney General informs the minor ex officio when the convicted person is released or in the event of an escape.
