The draft contains multiple articles establishing grounds for the processing of personal data.1 The draft also includes relevant grounds for lower regulations concerning data processing.2  

For determining heat consumption, heat meters, heat cost allocators3, or a consumption-independent cost allocation system is used. The draft provides for the possibility that a consumer may request not to have the measuring device read remotely.4 The explanatory note refers to the advice on smart meters from the Authority.  

Advice  
The draft contains a basis for a general administrative regulation, in which rules can be established regarding, among other things, the frequency with which measurement data from remotely readable heat meters and heat cost allocators are collected.5 These consumption data are personal data if they relate to the consumption of a natural person. This will certainly be the case for the consumption data of a small consumer. The frequency to be chosen is not expected to change often and is an important element in the degree of intrusion that the regulation entails.6 A higher frequency allows for a more accurate picture of the household's life to be constructed. It is conceivable that it can be inferred from this whether and during which periods the house is occupied or whether the residents are on vacation. After all, the intrusion on the protection of personal data is greater as the frequency increases. The explanatory note does not yet provide an indication of the intended frequency.  

The Authority advises to include in the legislative text with what frequency the consumption data of small consumers can be read at most from a remotely readable heat meter or heat cost allocator and to substantiate this frequency in the explanatory note.  

Publication  
The Authority intends to make this advice public on the website www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl as soon as the text of the (amended) draft is made public. The Authority would like to know the expected time of publication as soon as this is known.
