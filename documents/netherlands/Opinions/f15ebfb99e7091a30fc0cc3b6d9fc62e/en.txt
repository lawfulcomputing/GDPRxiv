Dear Ms. Van Gennip,

In a letter dated February 16, 2024, the Authority for Personal Data (AP) was consulted regarding the draft proposal for the amendment of the Act on the Structure of the Implementation Organization for Work and Income in relation to promoting proactive services by the UWV, the SVB, and municipalities (Act on Proactive Services SZW) (hereinafter: the draft) based on the provisions of Article 36, paragraph 4, of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

The AP has objections because it is not established, without further substantiation, that the provisions of the draft regarding proactive services meet the criteria of point 4, legal certainty, of the framework in the annex. These provisions may need to be set aside due to conflict with higher law without further substantiation.

Additionally, the AP has several remarks regarding the clarification of the use of other methods to achieve the goal, the proposed ministerial regulations, the joint registration of opt-outs, and the retention period.

Main Points

- The Employee Insurance Agency (UWV), the Social Insurance Bank (SVB), and municipalities will exchange personal data in the context of proactive services. These authorities are also authorized to further process data for this purpose. The draft, or lower regulations, must specify which (categories of) personal data will be exchanged and further processed. The necessity of these data processing activities must be substantiated in the explanatory memorandum. Alternatively, the explanatory memorandum must substantiate why this exhaustive enumeration is impossible or undesirable.

Scope of the Draft

The draft amends the Act on the Structure of the Implementation Organization for Work and Income (Act SUWI) to combat the non-use of social provisions. For this purpose, the UWV, the SVB, and municipalities obtain the authority to investigate potential beneficiaries of a benefit or provision. And to inform, advise, and assist this group in making an application ('proactive services'). The proactive services relate to legislation and provisions designated by ministerial regulation.

The UWV, the SVB, and municipalities will exchange personal data for proactive services. The draft aligns with existing grounds for data processing. Additionally, the draft stipulates that these authorities are authorized to further process data in the administrations under their responsibility to the extent necessary for proactive services. Rules regarding data provision by other authorities and individuals will also be established by ministerial regulation. And rules may be established by ministerial regulation regarding temporary mutual data exchanges between the UWV, the SVB, and municipalities to investigate whether that data is necessary for proactive services.

Legislative Assessment  

Concretizing Data Processing  
Legislation regarding interference with the right to protection of personal data must, according to established case law, always be clear and precise, and its application must be predictable for those to whom it applies (legal certainty).12  

The intended data disclosures between the UWV, the SVB, and municipalities, and the further processing, are based on already existing grounds. Regarding the data exchanges, the draft explicitly refers to Articles 54, 62, and 73 of the SUWI Act. The wording in these articles, such as "everyone provides all data and information necessary for the execution of tasks to be carried out by the relevant authority under or by virtue of this Act or any other law," is hardly determinative and thus does not meet the principle of legal certainty (point 4 of the framework in the annex).  

The AP concludes that the draft, or in lower regulations, must specify which (categories of) personal data the UWV, the SVB, and municipalities exchange and further process. Additionally, the necessity of these data processing activities must be substantiated in the explanatory memorandum. Alternatively, the explanatory memorandum must justify why this exhaustive enumeration is impossible or undesirable.

Ministerial Regulations  
Various components of the draft will be elaborated in ministerial regulations. According to the explanation accompanying the draft, this choice was made to retain the necessary flexibility. Given the current number of initiatives surrounding proactive service delivery, the expectation is that a relatively large number of forms of proactive service delivery will be designated and elaborated, and that periodic changes will need to take place. The relatively short turnaround time for amending a ministerial regulation provides a guarantee that developments in society and implementation can be responded to with sufficient speed and flexibility, according to the explanation.17  

The Guidelines for Legislation stipulate that, in the case of delegation, a general administrative measure is preferred. Delegation to a minister should be limited to provisions of an administrative nature, elaboration of the details of a regulation, provisions that often require amendment, and provisions that are expected to need to be established with great urgency.18  

Regarding flexibility, it follows that provisions that are more structural in nature should be established at a higher level than a ministerial regulation. This includes, for example, designating laws and regulations and provisions for which proactive service delivery will 'always' take place, and rules regarding data provision by 'fixed' other authorities and individuals.  

The AP concludes that it must be assessed whether components of the draft for which elaboration in ministerial regulations is proposed can be established at a higher level.  

Joint Registration of Opt-Outs  
The explanation accompanying the draft is not clear whether the joint registration of opt-outs is an obligation or a power. On the one hand, the explanation states that the UWV, the SVB, and municipalities are responsible for joint registration.19 On the other hand, the explanation states that maintaining a joint register is among the possibilities.20 The draft does not contain any provisions regarding this. From the perspective of legal certainty, the AP considers an obligation to be the most appropriate.

From the explanation, it appears that the UWV, the SVB, and the municipalities are joint controllers for the register. Their respective responsibilities for compliance with the obligations under the GDPR, particularly regarding the exercise of the rights of the data subject and their obligation to provide information, must then be established by national provisions or mutual arrangements.  

The AP concludes that the obligation (or possibly the authority) for joint registration of opt-outs must be included in the legislative text. Furthermore, the respective responsibilities of the UWV, the SVB, and municipalities regarding this register must be documented.  

Retention Period  
The proposed legislation stipulates that retention periods will be established by ministerial regulation concerning data disclosures to the UWV, the SVB, and municipalities by other entities and individuals. And regarding the temporary mutual data disclosures between the UWV, the SVB, and municipalities to investigate whether that data is necessary for proactive service delivery.  
No retention period is established or will be established for other data processing for proactive service delivery.  

The GDPR states that personal data may not be retained longer than necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed. A general norm such as this means that it must be specified for specific data processing activities. The AP considers it, from the perspective of legal certainty, generally desirable for the legislator to determine the retention period when making assessments in individual cases is not appropriate and a logical retention period does not unmistakably arise from the context.  

There is no evidence that it is necessary to retain personal data longer for proactive service delivery after that service has been provided. Therefore, the personal data must be deleted after that service delivery. If the UWV, the SVB, or municipalities process the data based on other legal tasks, different retention periods may apply.

Assessment Framework AP  

The AP examines whether the draft regarding the processing of personal data is in accordance with the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union (EU), the GDPR (Regulation 2016/679), or the Police and Justice Directive (Directive 2016/680), the general legal principles of the EU, and other relevant law, whereby it must be sufficiently demonstrated that the principle of proportionality is met, by:  
1. the draft being suitable for achieving the pursued objective of general interest or the protection of the rights and freedoms of others (suitability);  
2. the objective cannot reasonably be achieved as effectively by other means that are less intrusive to the fundamental rights of the data subjects (subsidiarity);  
3. the interference is not disproportionate to that objective, which particularly implies a weighing of the importance of the objective and the severity of the interference (proportionality);  
4. the draft is sufficiently clear and precise regarding the scope and is predictable in application (legal certainty);  
5. the draft sufficiently indicates under what circumstances and conditions personal data may be processed, thereby ensuring that the interference is limited to what is strictly necessary (substantive and procedural safeguards); and  
6. the draft is binding under national law (bindingness under national law).  

The AP pays particular attention to the rights of data subjects and the compliance of the draft with Article 6, paragraphs 1 and 3, and Article 9 of the GDPR (Regulation 2016/679), or Articles 8 and 10 of the Police and Justice Directive (Directive 2016/680), and with the principles established in Article 5 of the GDPR or Article 4 of the Directive. This implies, among other things, that:  
(a) processing must be lawful, fair, and transparent with respect to the data subject;  
(b) personal data may only be collected for specified, explicitly defined, and legitimate purposes; and  
(c) processing must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purposes.
