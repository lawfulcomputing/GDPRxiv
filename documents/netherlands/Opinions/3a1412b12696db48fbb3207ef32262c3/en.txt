The AP has examined whether the draft regarding the processing of personal data complies with the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union (EU), the general legal principles of the EU, the GDPR, and other relevant law, whereby it must be sufficiently demonstrated that, among other things, the principle of proportionality is met, by:

The AP notes that it is unclear on which exception decentralized political parties should base the disclosure of special personal data. The AP has objections regarding this point and advises not to continue the procedure unless the objection is resolved.  

Scope of the draft  

The draft aims to provide a single law on political parties. In addition to the existing regulations regarding the financing of political parties – which will be incorporated and tightened in the draft – the draft establishes rules regarding the transparency obligations of political parties, particularly concerning political advertisements and microtargeting.1 The draft stipulates that a national political party must submit political advertisements to the authority for deposit in a central register.2 The advertisement must be accompanied by a transparency statement. This statement must include, among other things, the political party that commissioned the publication or distribution of the advertisement, and the amount paid to the publisher for storing and publishing or distributing the political advertisement.  

Furthermore, the draft imposes stricter requirements on political parties during elections, establishes an authority on political parties (hereinafter: the authority), and sets rules regarding the prohibition of political parties if a party poses a real and serious threat to one or more fundamental principles of the democratic rule of law due to its objectives or activities.  

The advice of the AP is limited to the parts that concern the processing of personal data. This includes the transparency rules regarding donations (Chapters 5, 6, and 8 of the draft) and the transparency rules regarding political advertisements and microtargeting (Chapter 7 of the draft).

Article 9, paragraph 1, of the GDPR states that the processing of personal data revealing political opinions is prohibited. When a natural person makes a contribution to a political party, they typically do so because their political opinions correspond with those of the relevant political party. The AP is therefore of the opinion that the processing of data regarding this contribution generally reveals the political opinions of the donor. These data are thus to be classified as special personal data within the meaning of Article 9 of the GDPR.11 The explanation accompanying the draft indicates that this position is endorsed.12 The processing prohibition does not apply if an exception as mentioned in Article 9, paragraph 2, of the GDPR is met.  

In the context of the disclosure of donations, data revealing political opinions are processed by the (national or decentralized) political party and by the authority. An exception to the processing prohibition for the processing of personal data by the authority due to a compelling general interest is elaborated in the draft and in the explanation.13 However, it is unclear whether the mandatory provision of donor data by national political parties to the authority also falls under that exception. For the (electronic) disclosure of data by decentralized political parties, it has not been explained at all whether that processing can be based on an exception.  

The AP advises clarifying which exception of Article 9 of the GDPR the provision of data by national political parties to the authority and the (electronic) disclosure by decentralized political parties is based on.

The proposal for a regulation on transparency and targeted political advertising (hereinafter: the draft regulation) aims to ensure that providers of political advertising services must collect and record information about, among other things, the political advertising message, the amounts they have charged, and the identity of the client. The draft regulation does not require providers of political advertising services to deposit a political advertisement in a central register.  

The draft must be suitable to achieve the pursued objective of public interest, and the aim of the draft must not reasonably be able to be achieved as effectively in another way that less infringes on the fundamental rights of the parties involved (subsidiarity).  

The AP doubts whether the register of political advertisements meets the requirements of effectiveness and subsidiarity. The AP considers imposing obligations on providers of political advertising services – as intended by the EU legislator – to be more effective and less intrusive. This is because it is typically the providers of political advertising services, rather than the political parties, who are responsible for the entire process of microtargeting. Moreover, the risk of (unintended) interference in the democratic process is smaller if the obligations are imposed on providers of political advertising services.  

The AP advises to clarify in the explanation whether it has been considered to impose the obligation to provide transparency declarations on providers of political advertising services and why this option was not chosen.

The AP supervises compliance with the GDPR and the processing of personal data.19 This includes oversight of the lawful processing of personal data20 (elements 1 and 2 above) and the information obligations that rest on a data controller21 (element 3 above). Thus, the AP also supervises parties that use political microtargeting techniques.  

It is unclear where the line is drawn between the oversight of the authority and the oversight of the AP, and whether it is anticipated that the authority and the AP will cooperate. For example, if the authority receives signals about unlawful microtargeting or if the authority indicates that a transparency statement has not been provided to the authority in error.  

Additionally, the AP notes that the explanation indicates that the authority will not oversee the substantive correctness of the transparency statements. Without the possibility of substantive enforcement of the transparency obligations, the AP considers it unlikely that the risks of microtargeting can actually be mitigated. A political party could then, without consequences, include incorrect information, as long as the statement is complete and provided in the correct format.  

The AP advises to clarify:  
- how the oversight of the authority and the AP relate to each other, and  
- whether and by whom oversight will be maintained on the correctness of the transparency statements.  

Definition of political advertisement  

A political advertisement is defined in the draft as “a message, [that] is evidently intended to influence the turnout or voting preference of voters in an election that a publisher stores and publishes or distributes on behalf of a political party.”22  

Under this definition, only “electoral” advertisements fall, meaning advertisements that explicitly refer to an election or an electoral candidate.23 So-called “issue” advertisements, meaning advertisements about a specific subject that is of political importance or may be24, do not fall under this definition. Political parties could thereby evade the obligation to provide advertisements and transparency statements to the authority by not referring to an election or an electoral candidate.

Workload  
Given the existing tasks of the AP, it is likely that the draft will necessitate additional effort by the AP, as the AP will receive more questions and complaints about political advertisements and microtargeting due to the central register of political advertisements. This additional effort has not yet been accounted for in the funding of the AP. This will need to be appropriately addressed by the Secretary of State for Justice and Security prior to the entry into force, in consultation with the Minister for Legal Protection as the responsible minister for the budget from which the AP is funded. This is for the benefit of the budget from which the AP is funded.
