Advice on draft decision amending the decision on the processing of personal data in general digital infrastructure  
in relation to the authority declaration service and authority module  
Dear Ms. Van Huffelen,  
In a letter dated March 31, 2022, the Authority for Personal Data (AP) was consulted pursuant to Article 36, fourth paragraph, of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) regarding the draft decision amending the Decision on General Digital Infrastructure concerning the establishment of rules regarding the authority declaration service and authority module.1 An amended draft was sent by email on May 17, 2022 (hereinafter: draft).  
The AP has the following comments on the draft and advises that these be taken into account.  
Scope of the draft  
According to Article X, first paragraph, of the Law on Electronic Communication Tax Authority, the minister is responsible for the establishment, availability, maintenance, operation, security, and reliability of facilities for electronic communication and information provision, as well as for facilities for electronic authentication and electronic registration of authorizations.  
This basis is used to regulate the processing of personal data in two new facilities, namely the authority declaration service and the authority module.  
These facilities enable legal representatives, such as administrators, curators, and persons with parental authority, to conduct business digitally on behalf of another person who is not permitted to do so themselves. To establish that there is an authority to act on behalf of another, personal data must be processed. This is further elaborated in the draft.  
An adjustment is also made as a result of a recent court ruling by the Central Appeals Tribunal regarding the sending of government messages via the Message Box of MijnOverheid.  
Advice  
Message notification Message Box  
On September 9, 2021, the Central Appeals Tribunal (CRvB) ruled in an objection procedure regarding the meaning of email notifications sent for messages in the Message Box of MijnOverheid. It was determined that administrative bodies must ensure that the email notification is sent in accordance with the notification preferences specified by the addressee. In this case, the decision was only sent to the Message Box of MijnOverheid. The question of whether an email notification was sent plays an important role. It is currently not the case that a citizen who opts for digital communication automatically receives an email notification. The citizen must check a box for this. In such a situation, the administrative body must verify whether the citizen indeed wanted to receive it. According to the explanation, in order to continue to facilitate the use of the Message Box by administrative bodies under these conditions, it is desirable that administrative bodies can check whether a recipient has set up to receive a notification when a message is placed in the Message Box and whether a sent notification has been received. Until the moment this decision comes into effect, there was no provision for providing administrative bodies with this data from MijnOverheid regarding the notification settings and whether or not a notification was successfully received. This amendment decision provides a basis for those provisions, according to the explanation.2 The AP has two comments on this:  
a. Necessity of the proposed regulation for the provision of notification data  
The GDPR requires that any processing of personal data is 'necessary' for a specific and legitimate purpose.3 However, the explanation of the draft refers to 'desirable', which is not yet a necessity. In this regard, the AP points to Article 2.10 of the Law on Modernization of Electronic Administrative Communication.4 It states that the administrative body must send a notification within 48 hours that a message has been placed in the Message Box, unless the addressee has indicated that they do not wish to receive this. Health Data  
The draft provides for the processing of health data. The AP has two comments on this:  
a. Necessity of health data for the intended representative  
The purpose of the authorization declaration service is to issue a declaration to public service providers regarding the authority of a natural person to act on behalf of another. This declaration is prepared based on information from the available government source. With the authorization declaration, the service provider can then decide to grant access to its services to a person logging in on behalf of another.  

Article 3a of the draft specifies which personal data is processed by the minister for the establishment, availability, maintenance, operation, security, and reliability of the authorization declaration service. Regarding the intended representative for whom an authorization declaration is requested, this includes "other data concerning the type and time, characteristics of the use, including health data where possible in encrypted form."

The AP is unclear about which health data of the intended representative will be involved and why it is necessary to process health data of the intended representative. Health data are special personal data for which, according to Article 9 of the GDPR, a processing prohibition generally applies, unless one of the exceptions is met, each with different conditions.  

The AP advises, particularly in light of the special protection of health data under the GDPR, to remove the processing of health data or to specify in the explanatory memorandum which health data of the intended representative will generally be involved and to substantiate why the processing of such data is necessary. The AP also advises to clarify which exception in the sense of Article 9 GDPR applies, as well as to explain how the conditions set for that exception have been met.  

b. Informing the child about the review of the medical file of the representative  
According to the explanation, the name of the service and the name of the public service provider that the intended representative wishes to use are processed to determine whether the intended representative, after the establishment of parental authority, can be granted access on behalf of the intended represented: "This is, for example, important in the case of access to medical files, for which children from the age of 6 have the same rights as adults."  

The concept thus implies that a representative, after the establishment of parental authority, can digitally view the medical file of the child (under 16 years). Given the additional protection of children and health data in the GDPR, the AP considers it appropriate to ensure that the child is informed about the fact that the representative has accessed the child's medical file, for example, by, if possible, a logging message. The AP advises to address this.  

Legal basis for processing  
The delegation basis for data processing is Article X of the Electronic Communication Act of the Tax Authority. Thus, the processing is, according to the explanation, lawful based on the legal basis mentioned in Article 6, paragraph 1, under e, of the GDPR (processing necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest), Article 6, paragraph 1, under c GDPR (processing necessary to comply with a legal obligation) in conjunction with Article 6, paragraph 3, under b (the intended legal basis must be established by national law applicable to the data controller).  

It is notable that the explanatory memorandum does not specify which legal basis is precisely applicable (fulfilling a legal task or legal obligation). This is significant as there are substantial differences in conditions and consequences for the different legal bases. The basis of a legal obligation implies that the data subject cannot object. The fact that this concerns the minister processing data for the implementation of the draft decision indicates, in the opinion of the AP, the execution of a legal task.  

The AP advises, in light of the above, to further elaborate on the legal basis of performing a legal task in the explanatory memorandum.
