Dear Ms. Schouten, 

In a letter sent by email on June 23, 2023, the Authority for Personal Data (AP) was urgently consulted regarding the proposal for a one-time compensation for energy costs to students as intended in the preliminary draft bill to amend the Participation Act concerning the one-time categorical provision of an energy allowance to low-income households in 2023 (hereinafter: the draft). A separate email on June 23, 2023, supplemented paragraph 5 of the draft. 

A request was made to provide advice within a few days. In light of this, a limited analysis of the bill sufficed for the provision of advice. 

The AP has a comment on the draft and advises to take this into account.

Scope of the draft 

In 2022, municipalities1 provided a one-time energy allowance to low-income households2 through categorical special assistance.3 Students were excluded from this allowance. The cabinet has decided to again grant an energy allowance to low-income individuals in 2023, in the same manner as in 2022. 

Students with a basic grant living away from home and an additional grant will also receive a one-time compensation for energy costs in 2023. For this, data is needed that is available in the systems of the Education Executive Agency. To this end, the draft stipulates that the Minister of Social Affairs and Employment (SZW) may process the personal data of students that the Minister of Education, Culture and Science has made available for the implementation of the Student Finance Act 2000, at least the following personal data, excluding special categories of personal data: 
a. contact details;  
b. identifying information including the citizen service number or the education number4; 
c. bank account number; 
d. whether an additional grant has been awarded to a student;  
e. whether a basic grant living away from home has been awarded to a student.5 

Advice 

Legal certainty  
The draft specifies which personal data the Minister of SZW may process for the purpose of granting a one-time compensation for energy costs to students. 

Legislation regarding interference with the right to protection of personal data must, according to established case law, always be clear and precise, and its application must be predictable for those to whom it applies.6 The wording 'at least' does not imply a limitation and therefore does not provide legal certainty. 

The AP advises to legally limit the personal data of students processed in this context, possibly with the help of a sufficiently specific delegation basis for this purpose, or to substantiate in the explanation why a limitative enumeration is impossible or undesirable.
