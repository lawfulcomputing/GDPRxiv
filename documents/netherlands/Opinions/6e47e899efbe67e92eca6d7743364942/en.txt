The AP notes that the necessity of requesting certain data on the Bibob questionnaire is not established (points 1, 2, and 3). Furthermore, there is a lack of a legal basis for requesting the citizen service number (bsn) of third parties1 (point 6). The AP has objections to the draft on these points and advises against continuing the procedure unless the objections are resolved.  

Scope of the draft  

Article 7a of the Act on the Promotion of Integrity Assessments by Public Administration (Bibob Act) establishes a legal basis for requesting and providing the data and documents from the data subject that are necessary for a Bibob investigation in all areas where the government is authorized to apply the Bibob Act. This concerns both the areas covered by administrative law, such as permits and subsidies, and the areas covered by private law, such as government contracts and real estate transactions.  

Article 7a, paragraph 5, of the Bibob Act states that one or more forms for the data request from the data subject are established by ministerial regulation. This regulation further elaborates on this provision.  

The amendment of the Bibob Act as of August 1, 20202 necessitated an update of the Bibob forms regulation. In this context, Member of Parliament Van der Graaf (Christian Union) submitted a motion in which she asked the government "to consider how the Bibob questionnaires can be made clearer, more concrete, and more effective."3 This task has led to a significant change, particularly in the structure of the questionnaires.  

Advice  

General  
Article 7a, paragraph 2, of the Bibob Act reads as follows:  

The data subject provides the administrative body or the legal entity with a public task with the data and documents to enable them to conduct their own investigation. This data and documents include at least:  

a. the name, address, and place of residence or establishment of the data subject;  
b. the name, address, and place of residence of the person representing the data subject;  
c. the citizen service number, as referred to in Article 1, under b, of the Act on General Provisions on Citizen Service Numbers, of the person referred to in items a and b;  
d. the registration number with the Chamber of Commerce;  
e. the legal form of the data subject;  
f. the trade name or trade names used or previously used by the data subject;

However, the Bibob questionnaire also requests information that is not included in Article 7a, second paragraph, parts a through h, of the Bibob Act. The basis for this is the phrase "in any case" in Article 7a, second paragraph, of the Bibob Act. The explanatory notes do not address the necessity of requesting this information.  

It must be assumed that the legislator at that time carefully weighed the necessity of requesting the information that is included in Article 7a, second paragraph, parts a through h, of the Bibob Act.  

This does not apply to the other information in the form. The necessity of requesting this information is therefore not automatically established.  

The AP advises that the necessity of requesting this information should be justified.  

Citizen Service Number  
The Bibob questionnaire also asks for the citizen service number (bsn) of ultimate managers, the bsn of ultimate persons with authority, the bsn of asset providers, and the bsn of landlords/lessors/those who allow a property or area to be used.  

It is evidently intended that the concerned party - who fills out the form - requests the bsn from these third parties. However, according to Article 46 of the Implementation Act of the General Data Protection Regulation (UAVG), this cannot be based on a ministerial regulation. This can only be done by formal law. But this request is not regulated in the Bibob Act. Therefore, the intended use is not permitted under Article 46 UAVG.  

According to Article 5, first paragraph, part a, of the GDPR, the request by the administrative body for the bsn of these third parties via the questionnaire is in violation of the GDPR.
