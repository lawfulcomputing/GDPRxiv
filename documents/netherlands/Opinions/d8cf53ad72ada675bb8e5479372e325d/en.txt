Advice on the draft for the Decree on the reliability and suitability of supervisors and directors of collective management organizations  
Dear Mr. Dekker,  
In a letter dated June 9, 2021, the Dutch Data Protection Authority (AP) was consulted based on the provisions of Article 36, fourth paragraph, of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) regarding the draft decree containing rules concerning the assessment by the Supervisory Board of the reliability and suitability of a person who is or will be entrusted with the tasks of the body exercising the supervisory function or managing the daily operations in a collective management organization (Decree on the reliability and suitability of supervisors and directors of collective management organizations) (hereinafter: the draft).  
The AP has several comments on the draft and advises that these be taken into account.  
Scope of the draft  
The draft concerns the authority of the Supervisory Board as referred to in Article 2 of the Act on supervision and dispute resolution for collective management organizations for authors' and neighboring rights (Supervision Act) to assess the reliability and suitability of the person who leads or supervises a large collective management organization (cbo).

The draft specifies which data will be provided and on what basis the reliability and suitability of the (candidate) manager will be assessed, and it includes legal safeguards. Nevertheless, the AP makes several remarks.  

Necessity  
The draft lists in  
4 Appendix 1 the categories of personal data for which it aims to grant the Supervisory Board the authority to process them; convictions related to any of the mentioned criminal offenses, criminal orders, settlements, dismissals, acquittals, or discontinuations of prosecution, or other facts or circumstances as evident from reports or official records; personal or business financial antecedents; supervisory antecedents; personal and business tax administrative antecedents; other antecedents. The list resembles Appendix A of the Decree on Prudential Rules Wft.  

The processing of personal data must be necessary for the fulfillment of the public interest task assigned to the Supervisory Board. Furthermore, the principle of minimal data processing applies, whereby the processing of personal data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purposes for which they are processed.  

The AP notes that Appendix 1 contains a particularly broad enumeration of data. It is not immediately clear why all these data are necessary for assessing the reliability and suitability of (candidate) managers of large CBOs. From the necessity requirement, it is essential to verify whether the data is needed for each category of personal data and for each time frame.  

Moreover, some of the categories of personal data are already considered by the Justis Service when determining whether to provide a certificate of conduct. It is unlikely that a "second" screening by the Supervisory Board on points that are investigated with a greater degree of protection of personal data by the Justis Service is necessary to establish the suitability and reliability of the (candidate) manager.  

Furthermore, it is questionable whether non-verified or "soft" data, for which there is no conviction or other authoritative determination of the antecedent, are reliable enough to be taken into account during a screening, or at least what weight should be assigned to such data (see under the heading 'Assessment Framework').  

Part of this data (particularly police data in reports or official records) cannot be processed in the manner intended by the draft because they cannot be provided by the individual concerned.

The AP advises that data already being processed for the certificate of conduct should not be included in Appendix 1 of the draft or that the necessity for supplementing the VOG should be justified.  
The AP advises that for each category of data and for each time period, the necessity of providing this data to the Supervisory Board for the investigation should be substantiated, taking into account that 'soft' data is less reliable.  

Processing of criminal personal data by another party  
The questionnaire regarding antecedents and the requirement to submit a certificate of conduct can both lead to the processing of personal data concerning criminal convictions and offenses or related safety measures (hereinafter referred to as criminal personal data). The draft stipulates that the assessment can be outsourced to another party. According to the explanation, this other party is a processor in the sense of the GDPR.  

The AP notes that outsourcing to a processor does not require a legal basis. The processing is then merely carried out factually by the other party, entirely under the responsibility and control of the Board. The consequence is that the contracting third party cannot use its own research methods insofar as essential means for processing must be determined by this party. In that case, the contractor acts as the data controller.  

The AP notes that outsourcing to a processor has such limitations that the question arises whether this can indeed provide the expected added value.  
Criminal personal data in the sense of Article 10 GDPR may be processed (1) under the control of official authority or (2) based on legal provisions that provide appropriate safeguards for the rights and freedoms of the data subjects. In this case, with processing by the Board—whether or not with the assistance of a processor—there is government oversight in the sense that the processing is carried out by a public authority that is the data controller in the sense of Article 4, point 7, GDPR.  

If the contractor is the data controller, there is no subsequent processing that takes place under the supervision of the government. Therefore, if the assignment also involves expertise regarding the choice of means, only holders of a license based on Article 33, paragraph 4, of the UAVG can qualify as contractors.  

The AP advises considering the nature of the intended outsourcing and depending on that:  
- either to delete the phrase 'or have it carried out by a third party to be determined by the Supervisory Board' in Article 5 of the draft and to elaborate in the explanation on the inherent limitations of using a processor; or  
- to clarify in the explanation that only contractors with a license are eligible for outsourcing.

Pursuant to Article 5, paragraph 1, subparagraph a and Article 6 of the GDPR, any processing of personal data must be based on a legal ground. The data was provided by the (candidate) manager to the cbo or to the Supervisory Board. The explanation mentions the consent of Article 6, paragraph 1, subparagraph a, of the GDPR as a possible legal basis for this provision.  

Consent in the sense of the GDPR is only legally valid if it has been given freely. There is no question of this when the data subject has no real choice. This situation arises when the data controller exercises a legal authority. The Supervisory Board can invoke the legal ground mentioned in Article 6, paragraph 1, subparagraph e, of the GDPR: the processing of personal data in the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller.  

The Data Protection Authority advises to adjust the explanation on this point.  

Assessment Framework  
The draft contains an assessment framework that the Supervisory Board must use when determining the reliability and suitability of a (candidate) manager. This assessment framework does not address the nature and relevance of the provided data. It is important to also consider the weight and age of the information and the antecedent. When information is provided that has not been verified in an authoritative manner or that reflects a personal opinion, the nature of the information must also be taken into account.
