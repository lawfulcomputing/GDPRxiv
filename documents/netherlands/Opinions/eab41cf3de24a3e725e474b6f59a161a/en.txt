Subject 
Law abolishing subscription fee Wmo 2015 
Dear Mr. Van Ooijen,

In your letter dated December 21, 2023, the Authority for Personal Data (AP) was consulted pursuant to the provisions of Article 36, fourth paragraph, of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), regarding the draft amendment of the Social Support Act 2015 to enable municipalities to request a personal contribution from clients for more services, which is dependent on their financial capacity (hereinafter: the draft).

On January 24, 2024, the AP received a new version of the draft.1 

You have indicated that the advice can be limited to Article I, part G, and Article I, parts H through P.2 

An assessment of the personal data aspects of the draft gives the AP reason to make some comments.

Scope of the draft

Currently, for a large number of services under the Social Support Act 2015 (Wmo 2015), the so-called subscription fee applies as a personal contribution. This is a fixed contribution of €20.60 per month (2024) that is the same for every Wmo client, regardless of income, assets, or the number of hours of required support.

Before 2019, there was an income and asset-dependent personal contribution (hereinafter: ivb) for the relevant Wmo services. The government intends to restore this situation as of January 1, 2026, which amounts to abolishing the subscription fee and reintroducing the ivb for tailored services under social support, with the exception of the contribution for sheltered housing and care.

The CAK will implement the ivb. To determine the amount of the contribution for a service, it is necessary for the CAK to conduct an assessment of the client's income and assets and to establish which household a client belongs to. The necessary data3 for identifying the person and their income and asset information will be provided to the CAK by various agencies.4 

The draft assigns the CAK the legal task of determining and collecting the ivb5 and the authority to process the personal data required for this.6 In the case of the shelter for the homeless, the ivb is implemented by the municipality7 or the Wmo provider that provides this shelter.8 

Advice

Health data 
Article 5.1.3, first paragraph, provides the CAK and a provider as referred to in Article 2.1.5, fourth paragraph, a basis for processing personal data of the client and their spouse, including health data.

Health data are special personal data as referred to in Article 9 GDPR. Special personal data may not be processed in principle unless one of the exceptions in Article 9, second paragraph, GDPR applies. In the explanatory memorandum of a draft that allows for the processing of special personal data, it must not only be stated that processing such data is necessary, but also which exception is being utilized. Furthermore, it must be indicated which (type of) data is involved.

The explanatory memorandum does not indicate which exception from Article 9, second paragraph, GDPR is considered applicable here. The term “health data” is not concretely defined in the text of the draft or in the explanatory memorandum.10

The AP advises to explicitly mention which health data is involved in the legislative text and to indicate in the explanation which exception ground of Article 9, second paragraph, GDPR is applicable.  

Technical aspects  
The references in Article 5.1.3, first paragraph, are partly inaccurate or incorrect.  
In the enumeration of parties from whom data is obtained – by referring to Article 5.2.1 (municipality), 5.2.2 (provider of a customized provision), and 5.2.3 (Tax Authority) – BRP, UWV, and SVB are missing. These latter data providers are mentioned in the explanatory memorandum.11 It is possible that references to the legal provisions regulating these disclosures are also missing in Article 5.1.3, first paragraph.  
The reference in Article 5.1.3, first paragraph, to Article 2.1.4 seems incorrect, as Article 2.1.4 pertains to general provisions, for which the personal contribution is determined by the municipality12 (and thus not by CAK or the "provider").  
The reference to Article 5.2.1 should specifically refer to Article 5.2.1, second paragraph under b. The reference to Article 5.2.2 should specifically refer to Article 5.2.2, under b.  

The AP advises to amend the wording of Article 5.1.3, first paragraph, Wmo 2015, so that the aforementioned objections are addressed.  

Workload AP  

It is likely that the draft will require the AP to make limited additional efforts. This effort has not yet been factored into the financing of the AP. This will need to be addressed in consultation with the Minister for Legal Protection - as the responsible party for the budget from which the AP is financed - prior to the entry into force. For that reason, a copy of this advice is sent to the Minister for Legal Protection.  

Disclosure  

This advice will be published on the AP's website within two weeks.
