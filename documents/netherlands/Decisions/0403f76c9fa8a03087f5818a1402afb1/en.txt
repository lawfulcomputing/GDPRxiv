Dear Ms. Kaag,

The Data Protection Authority (hereinafter: AP) has decided to impose administrative fines totaling €3,700,000 on the Minister of Finance (hereinafter: the Minister). The AP has concluded that the Minister, as the data controller for the processing activities of the Tax and Customs Administration, has acted in violation of the principles of legality, purpose limitation, accuracy, and storage limitation by processing personal data in the Fraud Detection Provision (hereinafter: FSV) from November 4, 2013, to February 27, 2020.1

In addition to violating the four aforementioned principles, the AP concludes that the Tax and Customs Administration has not taken sufficient appropriate technical and organizational measures regarding access security, logging, and control of logging to ensure an adequate level of security for the personal data in FSV.2 Finally, the AP has concluded that the Tax and Customs Administration did not properly and timely involve the Data Protection Officer (hereinafter: DPO) in the execution of the Data Protection Impact Assessment (hereinafter: DPIA) of FSV.3

The AP will further explain the decision below. Chapter 1 contains an introduction, and Chapter 2 includes the findings. Chapter 3 elaborates on the (amount of the) administrative fines, and Chapter 4 contains the ruling and the remedies clause.

1. Introduction  
1.1 AP Investigation

The AP has conducted an investigation into the FSV application that the Tax and Customs Administration used until February 27, 2020. FSV was an application in which signals were recorded regarding established fraud and signals that could indicate an increased risk of fraud with taxes and benefits. The AP has concluded in this investigation that the manner in which the Tax and Customs Administration utilized FSV has led to multiple and serious violations of the General Data Protection Regulation (hereinafter: GDPR) and the Personal Data Protection Act (hereinafter: Wbp), the law that was applicable until the entry into force of the GDPR. The AP has included these findings in a report (hereinafter: the investigation report) and made this report public on October 29, 2021.4

1.2 Procedural History

For a description of the investigation procedure, the AP refers to Chapter 1 of the investigation report.

In a letter dated November 12, 2021, the AP informed the Minister of its intention to impose an administrative sanction and provided the Minister with the opportunity to present a viewpoint.

In a letter dated January 14, 2022, a written viewpoint was submitted on behalf of the Minister by the Deputy Director-General of the Tax and Customs Administration, in which the aforementioned violations were acknowledged and further measures taken and to be taken were discussed.5

2. Findings  
The AP bases this decision on the investigation report and the findings contained therein. These facts are supported by evidence. The Minister has not disputed the facts laid down in the investigation report and has furthermore acknowledged the violations based on those facts.

In paragraph 2.1, the AP briefly discusses the identified violations. For a complete overview of all relevant factual behaviors and findings – insofar as they are not mentioned here – the AP refers to Chapters 3 and 4 of the investigation report. The AP then presents the Minister's viewpoint in paragraph 2.2.

2.1 Summary of findings  

The Tax Authority primarily included individuals in FSV who had committed fraud and individuals for whom there was a suspicion that they may have committed tax or benefit fraud. FSV was used within the Tax Authority for the assessment of tax returns and applications for benefits and was used for recording information requests from other authorities. FSV was also consulted for the preparation of risk models and in determining whether a fine should be imposed in the context of the collection of tax or benefit debts.  

From November 4, 2013, to February 27, 2020, the Tax Authority registered, modified, consulted, used, combined, and disseminated signals of suspected and established fraud and information requests in FSV concerning at least 244,273 individuals and 30,000 entrepreneurs. In doing so, the Tax Authority processed personal data (including data on health, nationality, and criminal records) within the meaning of Article 4, introductory phrase and under 1, 2, and 15, of the GDPR, Article 10 of the GDPR, and Article 1, introductory phrase and under a and b, of the Wbp and Article 16 of the Wbp.  

The AP establishes that the Minister is the data controller for the processing of personal data in FSV by the Tax Authority, as referred to in Article 4, introductory phrase and under 7, of the GDPR and Article 1, introductory phrase and under d, of the Wbp. Where the Tax Authority is mentioned in this decision, the AP equates this with the Minister.  

The AP subsequently establishes that the Tax Authority has acted in violation of the principles of legality, purpose limitation, accuracy, and storage limitation by processing personal data in FSV from November 4, 2013, to February 27, 2020. The AP elaborates on these violations below.  

Personal data must be processed in accordance with the principle of legality, as referred to in Article 5, first paragraph, introductory phrase and under a, of the GDPR and Article 6 of the Wbp. This means that there must be a legal basis for the processing of personal data as stated in Article 6, first paragraph, of the GDPR and Article 8 of the Wbp. The AP concludes that there was no legal basis for the processing of personal data in FSV. The Tax Authority could not successfully invoke the ‘legal obligation’ as a basis for those processes, as there was no obligation to process signals of (possible) fraud and information requests as counter-information.  

The Tax Authority's reliance on the basis of ‘necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority’ also fails. Although the system of the General Act on National Taxes, the General Act on Income-Dependent Schemes, Title 5.2 of the General Administrative Law Act, and the substantive legislation grants the Tax Authority the authority to collect personal data for supervisory purposes (in specific cases), this legislation is insufficiently precise to serve as a basis for a separate, structural, extensive, and cross-segment collection of various, (too) detailed (special and criminal) personal data in FSV. Furthermore, the processing in FSV was not necessary.

Personal data must also be processed in accordance with the principle of storage limitation, as laid down in Article 5, first paragraph, introductory phrase and under e, of the GDPR and Article 10, first paragraph, of the Wbp. 2.2 Minister's Position  

The Minister endorses the facts and conclusions in the research report of the AP. FSV should not have been deployed in this manner, as the Minister has acknowledged. The conclusions of the AP align with earlier conclusions from the Tax Authority that led to the deactivation of FSV.  

The Minister states that the Tax Authority informs the affected citizens who were included in FSV about their registration and communicates the reason why they were registered in FSV. If the Tax Authority does not know the reason for a registration in FSV, it will also inform the individuals concerned of this. The Tax Authority points out to the individuals their right to access their information and informs them that they can contact the Tax Authority by phone and that they can find more information on the FSV webpage. There is a reporting point for FSV, intended for citizens who suspect that they have suffered unjust consequences from their registration in FSV. When there is more clarity regarding which organizations the Tax Authority has shared data from FSV with, the affected individuals will also be informed about this. Currently, approximately 200,000 affected individuals have received a letter from the Tax Authority regarding their registration in FSV. Of these, about 100,000 have learned the reason for their registration in FSV.  

The Tax Authority is conducting further research into the effects of a registration in FSV, into external data sharing from FSV, and into the queries used. In anticipation of the outcomes of these studies, a compensation scheme is being developed for the wrongly affected citizens.  

In response to the issues with FSV, the Tax Authority initiated the program 'Restoring, Improving, and Safeguarding' (hereinafter: HVB) in 2020. HVB includes actions that have been implemented since March 2020 to resolve the problems regarding how the Tax Authority has dealt with (personal) registrations, risk models, and the use of personal data such as nationality.  

The executive directorates-general (Tax Authority, Allowances, Customs) are collaborating with the Ministry of Finance (policy department) on action plans to improve privacy organization from both central and decentralized perspectives. The action plans, including that of the Tax Authority, will outline the ongoing and additional measures for the (structural) compliance with this legislation and regulations. Periodic reports on the follow-up of the action plans will be made to the management board of the Ministry of Finance.  

Additionally, the Tax Authority is working on a new process for handling signals with a temporary technical provision. The conclusions from the research report of the AP have been used in drafting the adjusted GEB of this process. The Tax Authority has submitted the adjusted GEB to the FG for advice. Subsequently, the GEB will be submitted for advice to the AP.  

Furthermore, the Minister states that work is being done on the legislative proposal for the Law on Safeguarding Data Processing by the Tax Authority, Allowances, and Customs. 3. Fines  
3.1 Introduction  

The AP has determined that the Minister, as the data controller for the processing in FSV by the Tax Authority, has acted in violation of the principles of legality, purpose specification, accuracy, and storage limitation. The AP has also found that the Minister did not ensure an appropriate level of security for the personal data in FSV and furthermore that the DPO was not adequately and timely involved in the implementation of the GEB of FSV.  

The AP exercises its authority to impose fines on the Minister due to the aforementioned violations. Given the seriousness of the violations and the extent to which they can be attributed to the Minister, the AP considers the imposition of fines appropriate. Since this case involves ongoing violations that have occurred under both the Wbp and the AVG (with the exception of the involvement of the DPO), the AP has assessed the substantive law as it stood at the time the actions took place. These provisions aim to safeguard the same legal interests. There is no (substantial) material change in the regulations on this point.  

The AP justifies the imposition of the fines as follows. The AP first briefly outlines the fine system. This is followed by the justification of the fine amounts for the violations of the fundamental principles of the AVG. Next, the violation of the obligation to ensure an appropriate level of security for the personal data in FSV is addressed, followed by the requirement for proper and timely involvement of the DPO in the implementation of the GEB. Finally, the AP assesses whether the application of the fine policy leads to a proportionate outcome.

3.3 Fine Amount Regarding the General Principles of the AVG and the Security of Personal Data Processing  

Legality is one of the fundamental principles of data protection. The processing of personal data is lawful if it takes place on the basis of a legal ground. In the case of an interference with the right to respect for the private life of the citizen, it is particularly important that the Tax Authority, as a government agency, must be able to base its actions on a sufficiently clear, precise, and predictable legal provision. In this respect, the Tax Authority has failed. By processing personal data in FSV without a legal ground, the core of the right to protection of personal data of many citizens has been violated.  

Furthermore, personal data may only be processed if the - explicit and justified - purpose of the processing cannot reasonably be achieved in another way. The data controller must also take all reasonable measures to ensure that inaccurate personal data are rectified or erased and that personal data are not retained longer than necessary. The AP has determined that inaccurate personal data have been included in FSV and that these data have also been retained longer than necessary.  

To prevent organizations processing personal data from infringing on the privacy of citizens, the AP considers it of great importance that organizations apply a risk-based level of security. In determining the risk for the data subject, the nature of the personal data and the scope of the processing are among the factors that are important: these factors determine the

3.3.3 Culpability and Serious Negligence  
Since this concerns violations, it is not required, according to established case law, to demonstrate intent for the imposition of an administrative fine. The AP may assume culpability if the perpetration is established. The Minister, as the data controller under the GDPR, must observe the aforementioned fundamental principles when processing personal data. The AP has determined that the Minister, as the data controller under the GDPR, has acted in violation of the principles of legality, purpose limitation, accuracy, and storage limitation in the processing of personal data in FSV. Furthermore, the Minister has not ensured an appropriate level of security for the personal data in FSV. The AP considers this culpable.  

Additionally, according to the AP, there is serious negligence on the part of the Minister and the Tax Authority. Citizens who are required to provide personal data to the Tax Authority must be able to assume that the Tax Authority - as a government agency - has taken the necessary measures to process personal data lawfully, accurately, and securely. The Tax Authority has failed to do this. For years, the Tax Authority has unlawfully processed personal data, including special personal data such as medical information, in FSV. Moreover, the Tax Authority's methods were discriminatory in nature in some cases, leading to stigmatization, increased scrutiny, and/or negative financial consequences. Even after its own internal conclusion in January 2019 that the data processing in FSV did not comply with the GDPR, the Tax Authority and the Minister failed to intervene promptly. Therefore, the AP concludes that the Tax Authority - under the responsibility of the Minister - has acted with serious culpable negligence.

3.4 Penalty Amount Regarding Involvement of the Data Protection Officer  

The DPO supports the data controller, among other things, in overseeing internal compliance with the GDPR. It is important that the data controller ensures that the DPO is adequately and timely involved in all matters related to the protection of personal data. The AP has determined that the Tax Authority did not properly and timely involve the DPO in the execution of the Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) of FSV.  

The Tax Authority conducted the DPIA from November 6, 2018, to January 21, 2019. More than a year after this period, the DPO was only asked to advise on the DPIA. Therefore, the Tax Authority did not seek advice from its DPO during the execution of the DPIA. As a result, the DPO could not adequately perform his tasks, and the Tax Authority could not timely advise on compliance with the GDPR. Had the DPO been consulted in a timely manner, he could have warned the Tax Authority earlier about the risks associated with the unlawful processing of personal data in FSV.  

As previously described, the Tax Authority processed a significant amount of (sensitive) personal data of hundreds of thousands of citizens in FSV. Particularly in large-scale processing of personal data that can lead to adverse consequences for a large number of data subjects, the Tax Authority must timely conduct a DPIA and seek advice from the DPO. The AP is of the opinion that there is a serious violation by the Tax Authority, for which the Minister as the data controller is responsible.  

Finally, the AP also concludes regarding this violation that the Tax Authority, under the responsibility of the Minister, has acted with serious culpable negligence. The fact that the Tax Authority sought advice from the DPO more than a year after the execution of the DPIA and only in response to questions from the media about FSV is considered by the AP to be highly negligent.  

Since the Tax Authority did not timely seek the advice of the DPO when executing the DPIA, there is a violation of Article 35, paragraph 2, of the GDPR. In light of this and the aforementioned circumstances, the AP sees reason to impose a fine on the Minister. The AP increases the base amount of €310,000 by €70,000 due to (1) the nature, severity, and duration of the violation, by €50,000 due to (2) the negligence, and by €20,000 due to (3) the categories of personal data. Thus, this fine amounts to a total of €450,000.  

3.5 Proportionality  

Finally, the AP assesses based on Articles 3:4 and 5:46 of the General Administrative Law Act (proportionality principle) whether the application of its policy for determining the amount of the fine, given the circumstances of the specific case, does not lead to an disproportionate outcome. The application of the proportionality principle can play a role in the accumulation of sanctions. Furthermore, under Article 83, paragraph 3, of the GDPR, the total monetary fine may not exceed that for the most serious violation if the data controller commits a violation of multiple provisions of the GDPR concerning the same or related processing activities.  

In this case, the AP imposes an administrative fine for violation of Article 5, paragraph 1, under a (in conjunction with Article 6, paragraph 1), b, d, and e of the GDPR, Article 32, paragraph 1, of the GDPR, and Article 35, paragraph 2, of the GDPR. Although the violations committed infringe different interests and are therefore separately punishable, the AP sees in this case the connection between the absence of a legal basis and the storage limitation as a relevant factor to mitigate the fine for the violation of the storage limitation by €500,000.  

The AP establishes the total amount of the imposed fines at €3,700,000. In the context of Article 10 of the Fine Policy Rules, the AP establishes that the total fine does not exceed the statutory maximum fine (€20,000,000) for the most serious violation.  

The AP is of the opinion that (the amount of) the total fine is not disproportionate. The AP has taken into account, among other things, the severity of the violations and the extent to which these can be attributed to the Minister. Due to the nature and duration of the violations, the far-reaching consequences for the data subjects, and the previous relevant violations, the AP qualifies the respective violations of the GDPR as serious.  

3.6 Conclusion  

The AP establishes the total amount of the imposed fines at €3,700,000.

4. Ruling  

I.  
The AP imposes a fine on the Minister of Finance in the amount of € 1,000,000 (say: one million euros), because there was no legal basis for the processing of personal data in FSV. As a result, the Minister of Finance has violated Article 5, paragraph 1, introductory phrase and under a in conjunction with Article 6, paragraph 1, of the GDPR.  

II. The AP imposes a fine on the Minister of Finance in the amount of € 750,000 (say: seven hundred fifty thousand euros), because the personal data in FSV has been processed in violation of the principle of purpose limitation. As a result, the Minister of Finance has violated Article 5, paragraph 1, introductory phrase and under b of the GDPR.  

III. The AP imposes a fine on the Minister of Finance in the amount of € 750,000 (say: seven hundred fifty thousand euros), because the personal data in FSV has been processed in violation of the principle of accuracy. As a result, the Minister of Finance has violated Article 5, paragraph 1, introductory phrase and under d of the GDPR.  

IV. The AP imposes a fine on the Minister of Finance in the amount of € 250,000 (say: two hundred fifty thousand euros), because the personal data in FSV has been processed in violation of the principle of storage limitation. As a result, the Minister of Finance has violated Article 5, paragraph 1, introductory phrase and under e of the GDPR.  

V. The AP imposes a fine on the Minister of Finance in the amount of € 500,000 (say: five hundred thousand euros), because an insufficiently appropriate level of security has been ensured for the personal data in FSV. As a result, the Minister of Finance has violated Article 32, paragraph 1, of the GDPR.  

VI. The AP imposes a fine on the Minister of Finance in the amount of € 450,000 (say: four hundred fifty thousand euros), because the advice of the DPO was not sought during the execution of the DPIA. As a result, the Minister of Finance has violated Article 35, paragraph 2, of the GDPR.
