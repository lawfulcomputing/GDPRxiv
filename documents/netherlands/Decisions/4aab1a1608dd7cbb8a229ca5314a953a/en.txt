Subject 
Decision to impose an administrative fine for violating the GDPR 
Dear members of the management, 
The Authority for Personal Data (hereinafter: the AP) has decided to impose an administrative fine of € 4,750,000 (say: four million seven hundred fifty thousand euros) on Netflix International B.V. (hereinafter: Netflix) because Netflix has insufficiently informed its customers; firstly in its privacy statement and secondly in response to access requests regarding 1) purposes and legal bases for the processing of personal data 2) recipients of personal data; 3) retention periods; and 4) international transfers. This constitutes a violation of Article 5, first paragraph, under a, of the General Data Protection Regulation (hereinafter: the GDPR) in conjunction with Article 12, first paragraph, Article 13, first paragraph, under c, e, and f, and second paragraph, under a, and Article 15, introductory part and first paragraph, under a, c and d, and second paragraph, GDPR.  

This decision explains the administrative fine. To this end, it successively addresses the background, the established facts and the process, the violation, and the amount of the fine. Finally, the dictum follows.

1. Background of the investigation  
1. Netflix is a streaming service that provides digital entertainment such as series, movies, and games. Netflix customers can watch movies and series from Netflix via their TV, tablet, smartphone, and on their computer (via an internet browser). To use these services, Netflix customers must register and create an account for a fee. For this registration, they provide personal data to Netflix, such as their first name, last name, date of birth, email address, phone number, and bank account number. After registration, the customer can watch movies and series from Netflix. This viewing behavior is relevant for Netflix, as it enables Netflix to serve its customers with movies and series that they find interesting. Therefore, Netflix also processes data about its customers' viewing behavior. 

2. Netflix has established a privacy statement that provides insight to customers about how personal data is processed. Netflix has also set up a "help center" where customers can ask questions and make requests, for example, to gain access to their personal data. 

3. Such an access request was made by None Of Your Business (hereinafter: NOYB) on behalf of two individuals. Netflix's response to this was the reason for NOYB to file complaints on behalf of the two individuals against Netflix with the Austrian privacy authority (the Datenschutzbehörde). NOYB complains that Netflix insufficiently informs its customers who have made an access request about the processing of their personal data. The Datenschutzbehörde forwarded the complaints to the AP, after which the International Research Department of the AP initiated an investigation and prepared a report. The AP regrets that this investigation took a long time and apologizes for this to both the complainants and Netflix. 

2. Findings of the investigation report and process 
4. It is established that conclusions in the investigation report that do not form part of the assessment framework of this decision (naturally) fall outside the scope of this decision. This means that no judgment will be made in this decision regarding the accuracy of those conclusions.  

5. Regarding the privacy statement, the investigation report indicates that Netflix has not fulfilled its information obligation because Netflix provides insufficient information about:  

a. the purposes and legal basis for the processing of personal data; 
b. parties that receive personal data from data subjects; 
c. the retention period of personal data; and 
d. safeguards for the transfer of personal data to third countries.

5. Assessment  
5.1. Data Controller and Competence of the AP  

20. Netflix is the data controller (Article 4, introductory part and under 7, GDPR). Netflix has its main establishment in Amsterdam, while its privacy statement indicates that it is the data controller for all companies within the Netflix corporate group for the processing of personal data in the European Union (hereinafter: EU). Netflix offers its services in multiple EU member states and for these services, Netflix processes personal data of data subjects. This means that data subjects in more than one member state experience significant consequences from the processing of personal data by Netflix. This constitutes cross-border processing (Article 4, introductory part and under 23, sub b, GDPR).  

21. Given the location of Netflix and its role as data controller, the AP is competent to act as the lead supervisory authority (Article 56, paragraph 1, GDPR).  

5.2. Information Obligation and Right of Access  

22. In this case, the topics of 'information obligation' and 'the right of access' relate to the following four subcomponents, namely: 1) grounds and purposes of processing personal data, 2) recipients of personal data, 3) retention periods, and 4) international transfers. The remaining eight subcomponents, as contained in Articles 13 and 15 GDPR, are outside the scope of this case. This case is limited to the four subcomponents mentioned above. Below, the privacy statement of Netflix is summarized in generic terms, followed by an assessment per subcomponent for both the right of data subjects to receive information when personal data is collected from them (Article 13 GDPR) and the right of access (Article 15 GDPR).  

5.2.1. Assessment of Grounds and Purposes of Processing Personal Data  

23. The AP has established that the privacy statement of Netflix provides a list of personal data and the manner in which Netflix collects this personal data. This includes: data provided by data subjects themselves, data automatically collected by Netflix, data from other

5.2.2. Assessment of recipients of personal data  

25. The AP establishes that from Netflix's privacy statement it follows that they use service providers (who are active in the advertising market, among others) and that these service providers may process and disclose personal data of Netflix customers.  

26. The privacy statement of Netflix does not provide the names of these recipients, while data provided by Netflix to the AP shows that they do have this information regarding online advertising services.10  

27. Article 13 introductory phrase and first paragraph, under e, of the GDPR indicates that in relevant cases the data controller provides information to data subjects about who the recipients or categories of personal data are. The GDPR (recital 58) states that it is difficult for a data subject to understand by whom and for what purpose their personal data is collected when it comes to information such as that from online advertising services. In accordance with the principle of fairness, Netflix must provide information about the recipients that is most meaningful to the data subjects. In this regard, the AP cannot see why Netflix has not included the names of recipients, which are limited in number, in its privacy statement. The AP is of the opinion that Netflix should have done so and should have provided this information in the event of a request for access. Netflix has wrongfully failed to do this, thereby violating the GDPR (Article 13, first paragraph, under e, and Article 15, first paragraph, under c). The complaint from NOYB on this point is therefore well-founded.  

28. The AP establishes that Netflix, with the amendment of its privacy statement on July 7, 2022, added a link to a "help article" containing a list of recipients of personal data. With this, Netflix has ended the violation of the GDPR regarding the "recipients of personal data."  

5.2.3. Assessment of retention periods  

29. The privacy statement of Netflix indicates that the personal data of data subjects are retained as required or permitted by law and regulations. In this context, Netflix mentions several examples such as retaining personal data to comply with the choices of data subjects and for billing and administrative purposes. No specific retention periods are mentioned in the privacy statement.  

30. Upon request, Netflix submitted a table to the AP detailing the personal data that are retained and the specific retention periods applicable to them.11 The AP establishes that Netflix has failed to include these periods in its privacy statement. The AP has not found that Netflix would have had to take disproportionately many measures for this.

**5.2.4. Assessment of international transfer of personal data**  

33. The AP notes that from Netflix's privacy statement, it follows that when transferring personal data to countries outside the European Economic Area (EEA), it acts in accordance with the applicable privacy legislation of those countries. The AP further notes that information provided by Netflix upon request indicates that Netflix can process personal data in twelve countries outside the EU for the purpose of executing payments, customer service, and scaling and improving the availability of its services.  

34. In the opinion of the AP, Netflix has failed to include in its privacy statement what rights data subjects have when their personal data is processed outside the EEA. Netflix has also not specified in its privacy statement to which countries outside the EEA personal data of data subjects are transferred. Additionally, Netflix has failed to make a reference to any adequacy decisions and has not indicated whether (in the relevant case) there are suitable or appropriate safeguards and how data subjects can consult those safeguards.  

35. The AP concludes that the information that Netflix included in its privacy statement at least until July 31, 2020, does not meet the requirements of the GDPR (Article 13, paragraph 1, under f). The AP adds that Netflix, in response to access requests during the period from October 25, 2018, to November 19, 2019, failed to provide sufficient information regarding safeguards for transfers of personal data to third countries. 5.3. Principle of Care  

37. Netflix argues that the AP acted contrary to the principle of care by not providing Netflix the opportunity to respond to the draft investigation report.  

38. The AP states that the investigation report was sent to Netflix on March 24, 2022. Netflix was then given the opportunity to provide its views on that report. According to the AP, there is therefore no violation of the principle of care.  

5.4. Conclusion  

39. The AP concludes that Netflix has insufficiently informed its customers; firstly in its privacy statement and secondly in response to access requests regarding 1) the grounds and purposes of processing personal data; 2) recipients of personal data; 3) retention periods; and 4) international transfers. In doing so, Netflix has acted in violation of Article 5, first paragraph, under a, in conjunction with Article 12, first paragraph, GDPR and Article 13, first paragraph, introductory part and under c, e, and f, and second paragraph, introductory part and under a, GDPR during the period from May 25, 2018, to July 30, 2020. Netflix has also violated Article 5, first paragraph, under a, in conjunction with Article 12, first paragraph, GDPR and Article 15, first paragraph, introductory part and under a, c, and d, and second paragraph, GDPR during the period from October 25, 2018, to November 19, 2019. For these violations, the AP will impose a fine on Netflix. The amount of the fine will be determined subsequently.  

6. Administrative Fine  
6.1. Authority to Impose Fines  

40. The AP is authorized to impose an administrative fine based on Article 58, second paragraph, introductory part and under i, in conjunction with Article 83 GDPR and read in conjunction with Article 14, third paragraph, UAVG. Jurisprudence from the Court of Justice of the European Union (hereinafter: CJEU) indicates that the wording of Article 83, second paragraph, GDPR implies that breaches of the provisions of the GDPR committed by the data controller in a culpable manner – that is, intentional or negligent breaches – may lead to an administrative fine being imposed on the data controller under that article.12

6.2. Methodology for Determining the Amount of the Fine  

41. The EDPB approved the final text of the Guidelines 04/2022 for calculating administrative fines under the GDPR (hereinafter: the Guidelines) in its plenary meeting on May 24, 2023.13 The AP will apply these Guidelines to this case.14  

42. The Guidelines describe the following method for calculating administrative fines for breaches of the GDPR:  
1. identify which and how many actions and breaches are under assessment;  
2. determine the starting amount for the further calculation of the fine;  
3. assess whether there are mitigating or aggravating circumstances that necessitate an increase or decrease of the fine;  
4. check what maximum amounts apply for the violations and whether those maximum amounts are not exceeded as a result of increases applied in previous or subsequent steps;  
5. verify whether the calculated final amount of the fine meets the requirements of effectiveness, deterrence, and proportionality, and adjust the fine accordingly if necessary.  

43. These steps will be followed in succession.  

6.3. Calculation of the Fine Amount  

6.3.1. Step 1: Establishing Actions and Breaches  

44. First, it must be established whether there is one or more behaviors that need to be sanctioned. In section 5.4, it was determined that Netflix has insufficiently informed its customers in its privacy statement and in response to access requests regarding 1) the grounds and purposes of processing personal data; 2) recipients of personal data; 3) retention periods; and 4) international transfers. In light of this, Netflix has committed two distinct sanctionable behaviors. These behaviors consist of insufficiently informing customers; firstly in the privacy statement and secondly in response to access requests. This means that in this case, there are two culpable behaviors for which the AP can impose fines.  

6.3.2. Step 2: Determining the Starting Amount  

45. Next, the starting amount of the fine must be determined. This amount serves as the basis for further calculations in later steps, taking into account all relevant facts and circumstances. The starting amount is determined based on three elements:

Ad i) Categorization of the violations according to Article 83, paragraphs 4 to 6 GDPR  

46. As stated in the Guidelines on the calculation of administrative fines, almost all obligations of the data controller are categorized under the provisions of Article 83, paragraphs 4 to 6 GDPR. The GDPR distinguishes between two types of violations. On the one hand, violations that are sanctionable under Article 83, paragraph 4, GDPR, for which a maximum fine of €10 million applies (or in the case of a company, 2% of the annual turnover, if that is higher); on the other hand, violations that are sanctionable under Article 83, paragraphs 5 and 6 GDPR, for which a maximum fine of €20 million applies (or in the case of a company, 4% of the annual turnover, if that is higher). With this distinction, the legislator has provided an initial indication in abstracto of the seriousness of the violation: the more serious the violation, the higher the fine.  

47. In this case, for the violation of Article 5, paragraph 1, sub a, in conjunction with Article 12, paragraph 1, GDPR and Article 13, paragraph 1, introductory phrase and sub e and f, and paragraph 2, introductory phrase and sub a, GDPR, and the violation of Article 5, paragraph 1, sub a, in conjunction with Article 12, paragraph 1, GDPR and Article 15, paragraph 1, introductory phrase and sub c and d, and paragraph 2, GDPR, an administrative fine of up to €20 million (or in the case of a company, 4% of the global annual turnover, if that is higher) can be imposed. From this categorization, it follows that the violations of these provisions are considered serious by the legislator.  

Ad ii) Severity of the violation  

48. In determining the severity of the violation, account must be taken of the nature, seriousness, and duration of the infringement, as well as the intentional or negligent nature of the violation and the categories of personal data involved.  

49. The nature of the first violation in this case is the obligation of the data controller to provide the data subject with the information necessary to ensure fair and transparent processing towards data subjects, taking into account the specific circumstances and the context in which the personal data are processed. For the second violation, namely the restriction of the right of access, it is also necessary, as is the right to receive understandable and easily accessible information about the processing of personal data, to enable data subjects to exercise their other rights under the GDPR. Providing transparent information in the sense of Article 12, paragraph 1, GDPR is therefore of great importance. When the rights of data subjects are not respected, this affects the right that data subjects have to respect for their private life and the protection of their personal data.

50. In assessing the severity of the violations, the number of Netflix customers who may be affected by these violations is also taken into account. At the end of 2022, Netflix had approximately 231 million paying customers worldwide, a substantial portion of whom are in the European Union. This means that the processing of personal data has a broad scope and potentially affects a large number of data subjects. Although Netflix provides relevant information in its privacy statement regarding the collection of personal data from data subjects, the purposes of this processing, and the legal bases on which Netflix relies, as well as information about 'recipients of personal data', 'retention periods', and 'international transfers', the AP believes that the information provided is not specific enough. As a result, data subjects were hindered in exercising control over their personal data. Netflix has indicated, upon request, that it receives approximately one million inquiries annually through its 'help center' worldwide, the vast majority of which are technical questions, and that only a very small portion pertains to questions about (access to) personal data. The fact that not all customers, but only a relatively limited number of customers, had questions or complaints regarding the right to access somewhat mitigates the severity of the infringement, in the opinion of the AP.  

51. Regarding the duration of the violations, it has been established that they lasted from May 25, 2018, to November 19, 2019, and respectively until July 30, 2020. Although this is a significant period, the AP also takes into account the fact that the investigation took a long time. During this period, Netflix repeatedly expressed its willingness to adjust its privacy statement based on recommendations from the AP. After the investigation report was sent to Netflix, Netflix voluntarily adjusted its privacy statement at multiple points in time with the aim of specifying the information and thus addressing the shortcomings identified by the AP. The fact that the handling of this case by the AP took a long time (investigation and enforcement phase) and that the AP did not respond to Netflix's willingness to amend its privacy statement will be taken into account by the AP in determining the amount of the fine.  

52. Furthermore, in determining the severity of the infringement, it must be assessed whether the infringement was intentional or negligent. The severity of the infringement carries more weight when the data controller has knowingly committed a violation. When the infringement is the result of careless behavior, the severity of the infringement carries less weight. The AP also considers that Netflix wanted to maintain the uniformity of its Privacy Statement by not making the content of its privacy statement dependent on the device used to access Netflix's services. Taking these circumstances into account, the AP concludes that Netflix did not provide certain information completely or clearly. Ad iii) Revenue of the company  

55. A fair starting amount for the fine must be related to the size of the company. The size of the company is determined based on revenue. The starting amount for a small company is lower, and the starting amount increases as the revenue of the company is higher. If a company has revenue of more than €500 million, the amount of the fine is determined as a percentage of the company's annual revenue.16 This means that the size and revenue of the company have already been factored into the amount of the fine, so the starting amount does not need to be adjusted on that basis.  

56. For determining Netflix's revenue, it must be viewed in that context as a company in accordance with Articles 101 and 102 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Netflix is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Netflix Inc., based in Los Gatos, California (United States). According to the case law17 of the Court of Justice of the European Union, the revenue of the entire group must be used to establish the upper limit of the fine.  

57. For determining the revenue, data from the financial year preceding the imposition of a fine is used. In this case, that is the data for the financial year 2023.

6.3.3. Step 3: Assessing other relevant circumstances  

59. According to the Guidelines for calculating administrative fines, it must then be examined whether the circumstances of the case provide grounds for setting the fine higher or lower than the aforementioned starting amount. The circumstances to be considered are listed in Article 83, second paragraph, introductory phrase and under a to k, GDPR. The circumstances mentioned in that provision must each be considered only once. In the previous step – to the extent applicable – account has already been taken of the nature, severity, and duration of the violation (part a), the intentional or negligent nature of the infringement (part b), and the categories of personal data (part g). Therefore, only parts c to f and h to k remain.  

60. In this case, the AP takes into account that Netflix responded in a timely manner to the access requests by providing collected personal data as well as data regarding, among other things, viewing behavior, search history, IP addresses, payments, devices, and interaction with Netflix. Furthermore, Netflix also provided a copy of its privacy statement along with the aforementioned data. After receiving this data, NOYB filed complaints with the Datenschutzbehörde on behalf of the affected parties, while Netflix believed that it had satisfactorily handled the requests from the affected parties. Netflix also terminated the violation regarding the 'recipients of personal data' shortly after receiving the investigation report. Thus, Netflix did not await the decision-making of the AP but promptly supplemented the available information and made it available in multiple languages to the users of its service. The aforementioned circumstances, including the long duration of the handling of this case by the AP and the lack of response from the AP to Netflix's willingness to amend its privacy statement, are reasons for the AP to reduce the amount of the fine to be determined below from the perspective of proportionality (par. 97 Guidelines for calculating administrative fines).  

6.3.4. Step 4: Relevant maximum amounts  

61. Given Netflix's turnover, a maximum fine amount of €1.229 billion or 4% of the annual turnover will apply for the identified violations. It has been considered that in this case, the severity of the infringement should be classified at a low level. According to the Guidelines, for infringements with a low level of seriousness, the starting amount should be determined at a point between 0 and 10% of the maximum fine. This means that the range of the fine to be imposed is between 0% and 10% of €1.229 billion. This corresponds to an amount between €0 and €122.9 million. Taking into account all the relevant facts and circumstances mentioned above, the AP sets the starting amount at €2.5 million per violation. Since two violations have been identified, the starting amount will be €5 million. This amount is well below the maximum fine of 4% of Netflix's global annual turnover.

6.3.5. Step 5: Assessment of the requirements of effectiveness, proportionality, and deterrence  

62. Finally, it must be assessed whether the fine is effective, proportional, and deterrent. Article 49, third paragraph, of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the EU and Articles 3:4 and 5:46, second paragraph, General Administrative Law Act (Awb) indicate that the administrative fine, given the circumstances of the case, does not lead to an disproportionate outcome.  

63. As described in the Guidelines, the imposition of a fine can be considered effective if it achieves the purpose for which it was imposed. That purpose may lie in, on the one hand, punishing unlawful behavior and, on the other hand, promoting compliance with applicable regulations. Considering the nature, seriousness, and duration of the infringement, as well as the other factors from Article 83, second paragraph, GDPR as assessed above and taking into account the mitigating circumstance mentioned in paragraph 6.3.3, the AP is of the opinion that the imposition of an administrative fine under these circumstances achieves both goals and is therefore effective and deterrent. The amount of the administrative fine, which is also determined based on Netflix's turnover, is also considered effective and deterrent by the AP.  

64. The AP considers a fine of €4,750,000, given the seriousness of the violations and the size of the company, to be proportional. In the opinion of the AP, there is no evidence of special circumstances that would make the fine disproportionate.

65.
