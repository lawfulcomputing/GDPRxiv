The Administrative Court has also ruled in its decision regarding GZ Ra 2019/04/0071 on May 26, 2021, that it may only decide on an amendment of the application within the framework of § 13 para. 8 AVG if the essence of the matter is not changed and the boundary of the subject of the complaint is not exceeded (cf. also Köhler, § 28 VwGVG, in: Köhler/Brandtner/Schmelz [Eds.], VwGVG Commentary [2021] Rz. 41).

**D.2. General**  
According to § 1 para. 1 DSG, everyone has the right, particularly with regard to the respect for their private and family life, to the confidentiality of their personal data, as long as there is a legitimate interest in it. The existence of such an interest is excluded if data, due to their general availability or because they cannot be traced back to the affected individual, are not subject to a confidentiality claim.
According to § 1 para. 2 DSG, restrictions on the right to confidentiality, insofar as the use of personal data does not occur in the vital interest of the data subject or with their consent, are only permissible for the protection of overriding legitimate interests of another party, and in the case of interventions by a public authority, only on the basis of laws. Such laws may only provide for the use of data that is particularly worthy of protection by its nature for the safeguarding of important public interests and must simultaneously establish adequate guarantees for the protection of the confidentiality interests of the affected individuals.  
§ 24 para. 1 DSG provides that any affected person has the right to lodge a complaint with the data protection authority if they believe that the processing of their personal data violates the GDPR or § 1 or Article 2 of the 1st main part. The GDPR and, in particular, the principles enshrined therein must be taken into account when interpreting the right to confidentiality (see the decision of the DSB dated October 31, 2018, GZ DSB-D123.076/0003-DSB/2018).  
Under "processing" according to Art. 4 No. 2 GDPR, any operation or set of operations performed on personal data, whether or not by automated means, is understood to include the collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction. The subject of the proceedings involves the personal data of the first complainant (the correspondence for her clients, including with authorities and courts) being transmitted or disclosed to the second respondent.

Starting from the subject of the complaint, the question arises as to whether the first respondent and the second respondent have violated the first complainant's right to confidentiality according to § 1 DSG by the first respondent complying with the second respondent's request to block the first complainant's access to electronic legal communication (ERV) and furthermore redirecting all incoming correspondence to the ERV access of the second respondent, thereby disclosing it to them. The first complainant is a registered lawyer listed in the list of lawyers, to whom the Lawyers' Act (RAO) and the Disciplinary Statute for Lawyers and Trainee Lawyers (DSt) apply.  

As a legal basis for the data processing in question, both respondents (see the statements of the first respondent dated March 3, 2023, and of the second respondent dated February 20, 2023, and April 5, 2023) have presented the fulfillment of a legal obligation according to Art. 6 para. 1 lit. c GDPR. For this legality requirement, there must either be a legal basis within the framework of Union law or of the respective member state. The GDPR does not require a specific law for each processing operation itself, but rather a law can also serve as the basis for multiple processing operations. Recital 45 clarifies that under Art. 6 para. 1 lit. c GDPR, processing can be justified by both private and public sector controllers - specifically by the designated respondents.  
It should be noted that there are four disciplinary complaints against the first complainant (see point 2 of the findings and compare the statement of the first complainant dated February 7, 2023). According to § 19 para. 1a DSt, the Disciplinary Council can decide on a provisional prohibition of the practice of law against a lawyer as a precautionary measure (see § 19 para. 3 No. 1 lit. d DSt) if certain facts are reported by the committee, based on which there is suspicion of a disciplinary offense and urgent concern that further practice could lead to significant impairment of entrusted third-party assets, particularly in connection with the handling of client funds by the lawyer.  
The Disciplinary Council of the relevant Bar Association, where the accused (specifically the first complainant) is registered at the time when the chamber lawyer becomes aware of the suspicion of the disciplinary offense (§ 20 para. 1 DSt), is responsible for exercising disciplinary authority.  
In the proceedings, the committee of the competent and locally responsible H***n Bar Association issued a decision with GZ 2022/*45*, dated December 13, 2022, in which a provisional prohibition of the practice of law was imposed as a precautionary measure. In principle, the filing of an appeal is possible; however, according to § 57 para. 2 DSt, the enforcement of a precautionary measure decided by the Disciplinary Council according to § 19 DSt is not prevented by an appeal against it.

During the suspension of the right to practice law, membership in the Bar Association and registration in the list of lawyers remain in effect. **D.3.2. Redirecting Postal Correspondence Addressed to the First Appellant to the Second Appellee**

Based on the submissions of the first appellant, in addition to the blocking of the ERV access, the question arises whether the second appellee has violated the first appellant's right to confidentiality according to § 1 DSG by redirecting the postal correspondence to the post office L***burg, as the competent authority. 

The committee of lawyers is obliged to appoint a chamber commissioner when the authorization to practice law (§ 34 Abs. 1 and 2) is suspended. The chamber commissioner must inform the clients of the lawyer about his appointment and its legal consequences and, if necessary, advise on the transfer of mandates to other lawyers, ascertain the trust accounts of the lawyer and inform the involved parties about the possible management of the trust account by another trustee, ascertain and manage the lawyer's third-party funds, as well as ensure the proper safekeeping of the lawyer's files and the documents deposited with him. For this purpose, the affected lawyer must hand over the files and deposited documents to the chamber commissioner and provide access to the documents stored in his legal document archive.

The second appellee was appointed as chamber commissioner by decision dated December 13, 2022, in accordance with § 34a Abs. 2 RAO. The chamber commissioner has, among other tasks, the duty to inform the clients of the first appellant about his appointment and to clarify the legal consequences (cf. Vitek in Engelhart/Hoffmann/Lehner/Rohregger/Vitek, RAO11 § 34a Rz 9). The appointment of the chamber commissioner must be promptly and publicly announced on the Internet on the website of the Bar Association (cf. Vitek in Engelhart/Hoffmann/Lehner/Rohregger/Vitek, RAO11 § 34a Rz 11 (as of 1.11.2022, rdb.at)). This has been carried out by the H*** Bar Association (cf. Point 3 of the findings).

As can be seen from the statements of the first appellee dated March 3, 2023, and the second appellee dated February 20, 2023, the first appellant wanted to continue representing her clients despite being aware of the provisional prohibition of practicing law and was also unwilling to cooperate with the appointed chamber commissioner in the form of the second appellee. For this reason, the second appellee informed the first appellee and requested the immediate blocking of her ERV access and any submissions from her clients to be directed to him (cf. statement of the second appellee dated February 20, 2023). The ERV access was temporarily reactivated between December 23, 2022, and December 27, 2022, due to the pressure exerted by the first appellant. After a thorough legal examination of the situation by the first appellee, her access was blocked again.

Regarding the disclosure of correspondence that is exclusively related to professional activities (including communications from courts, notifications from authorities) within the electronic legal traffic (ERV) to the second appellee, it should be noted that he, as chamber commissioner, must inform the clients of the first appellant about his appointment and its legal consequences and, if necessary, advise on the transfer of mandates to other lawyers, ascertain the trust accounts of the lawyer and inform the involved parties about the possible management of the trust account by another trustee, ascertain and manage the lawyer's third-party funds, as well as ensure the proper safekeeping of the lawyer's files and the documents deposited with him (cf. § 34a and § 34b RAO).

A review of these provisions makes it clear that there is no violation of the right to confidentiality, as the actions taken by both appellees are based on a legal foundation. Consequently, no violation of the GDPR or DSG can be derived in this context. The Data Protection Authority notes that the first appellant herself triggered the actions of the second appellee by failing to comply with the legal obligation according to § 34a Abs. 2 last sentence RAO to hand over the files and deposited documents to the chamber commissioner - specifically the second appellee - and to provide him access to the documents stored in her legal document archive, and by refusing to cooperate with him.

For the sake of completeness, it should be noted that the presented facts must be viewed in a differentiated manner. The Data Protection Authority is a supervisory authority established under Art. 51 GDPR, which is responsible for monitoring the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and for the protection of natural persons in the processing of their personal data. Therefore, the authority is competent to examine the data processing in question regarding the disclosure to the second appellee or the blocking by the first appellee.

If the first appellant complains that a disciplinary procedure has been initiated against her, it should be pointed out that the decision to initiate such a procedure does not fall within the jurisdiction of the Data Protection Authority. This is the responsibility of the disciplinary council established for this purpose according to § 1 Abs. 2 in conjunction with § 5 DSt. It is also responsible for deciding whether there is sufficient reason to suspect that a procedure should be initiated against the accused person or whether it should not be pursued according to § 3 DSt because the lawyer's fault is so minor that his behavior has no or only insignificant consequences. The disciplinary statute for lawyers and trainee lawyers (DSt) specifically provides in § 46 ff. for the possibility of an appeal procedure to challenge findings of the disciplinary council. The Supreme Court (OGH) is responsible for deciding on the appeal.

It was therefore necessary to decide accordingly.
**Post Office**  
has requested that the mail addressed to the office address of the first complainant be forwarded to their address via a forwarding order. The first complainant is a registered lawyer on the list of attorneys, to whom the Lawyers' Act (RAO) and the Disciplinary Statute for Lawyers and Trainee Lawyers (DSt) apply.  
As a basis for the data processing in question, the second respondent (see the statements of the second respondent dated February 20, 2023, and April 5, 2023) cites the fulfillment of a legal obligation pursuant to Art. 6 para. 1 lit. c GDPR.  
As already stated in the legal assessment under point D.3.1., there were disciplinary complaints against the first complainant (see point 2 of the findings and compare the statement of the first complainant dated February 7, 2023). According to § 19 para. 1a DSt, the Disciplinary Council can decide on a provisional prohibition of the practice of law as a precautionary measure against a lawyer (see § 19 para. 3 no. 1 lit. d DSt).  
The second respondent was appointed as chamber commissioner by decision dated December 13, 2022, pursuant to § 34a para. 2 RAO. The chamber commissioner has, among other tasks, the duty to inform the clients of the first complainant about his appointment and to clarify the legal consequences (see Vitek in Engelhart/Hoffmann/Lehner/Rohregger/Vitek, RAO11 § 34a Rz 9). The appointment of the chamber commissioner must be announced on the internet on the website of the Bar Association immediately and made generally accessible (see Vitek in Engelhart/Hoffmann/Lehner/Rohregger/Vitek, RAO11 § 34a Rz 11 (as of November 1, 2022, rdb.at)). This has been done by the H*** Bar Association (see point 3 of the findings).  
As indicated in the statements of the second respondent dated February 20, 2023, and April 5, 2023, the first complainant wanted to continue representing her clients despite being aware of the provisional prohibition of her practice of law and was also not willing to cooperate with the appointed chamber commissioner in the form of the second respondent. This is evident not only from the statements but also from the behavior of the first complainant before the Data Protection Authority, as she still submitted the complaint in question on behalf of her clients despite being aware of the provisional prohibition of her practice of law. Furthermore, she attempted to circumvent both the ERV block and the forwarding order to the second respondent (see the assessment of evidence regarding point 3 of the findings).  
Due to the lack of willingness to cooperate from the first complainant, the second respondent informed the post office branch L***burg, as the responsible post office, about the situation with reference to the appointment decision and established a forwarding order (see point 5 of the findings). In light of the above circumstances, it becomes clear that the second respondent, as a lawfully appointed chamber commissioner, would not have been able to fulfill the tasks to which he is legally obligated without the establishment of a forwarding order (see §§ 34a, 34b RAO).  
The Data Protection Authority does not overlook that—as stated under point 3 of the findings—the private and professional postal address of the first complainant is identical, which she also confirmed in her statement dated April 25, 2023. As can be seen from the findings under point 5 of the present decision, the opening of the private mail, which was contested by the first complainant, did not occur, and therefore, there was no infringement of the first complainant's right to confidentiality (see also the decision dated March 4, 2020, GZ DSB-D124.256/0004-DSB/2019).  
In general, all facts on which an administrative decision is to be based, i.e., from which the relevant factual situation is composed according to the authority's view, require proof. Unless otherwise provided by law, full proof of a fact must be provided. This means that the authority must obtain certainty regarding the existence of the factual elements relevant to the decision (i.e., for example, regarding an actual event).  
The first complainant also did not comply with the request of the Data Protection Authority (see the request for a statement dated April 6, 2023) to provide evidence that her private mail was actually opened by the second respondent. The second respondent explained in his statement dated April 5, 2023, that he and his office staff separate private mail from professional mail. Regarding the private mail, the second respondent requested the first complainant to collect it, which she ignored. Subsequently, the second respondent personally delivered it to the office premises of the first complainant on January 16, 2023, after which it was returned to him by her.  
The complaint regarding a possible violation of the right to confidentiality concerning the private mail has therefore proven to be unfounded, especially since the first complainant has not succeeded in demonstrating a possible infringement. In the case of non-establishment, it must be assumed that the fact does not exist (see the ruling of the VwGH dated June 16, 1992, ZI. 92/08/0062).  
A review of these provisions makes it clear that there is also no violation of the right to confidentiality regarding the professional mail of the first complainant, as the actions taken by both respondents are based on a legal foundation (Art. 6 para. 1 lit. c GDPR). Therefore, no violation of the GDPR or DSG can be derived in this context. Regarding the private mail, it must be reiterated that no evidence of an actual opening has been provided by the first complainant, and thus there is a lack of objective evidence for a violation of the right to confidentiality.  

It was therefore decided accordingly.  
**Decision Point 3**  
The complaints filed by the first complainant on behalf of herself and her clients (second complainant) were submitted on January 18, 2023. Already on December 13, 2022, the exercise of the first complainant's practice of law was suspended by decision GZ 2022/*45*. This means that from that point on, the first complainant was no longer entitled to represent her clients (second complainant) lawfully as a lawyer. Consequently, the complaints submitted on behalf of her clients had to be dismissed accordingly.  
It was therefore decided accordingly.  

**Decision Point 4**  
From Art. 77 para. 1 GDPR and § 24 para. 1 and 5 DSG, a subjective right to initiate criminal proceedings against a certain responsible party cannot be derived. In this regard, the principle of ex officio applies according to § 25 para. 1 VStG (see Fister in Lewisch/Fister/Weilguni (eds), VStG Commentary2 [2017] § 25 Rz 1).  
The related request was therefore to be dismissed.
