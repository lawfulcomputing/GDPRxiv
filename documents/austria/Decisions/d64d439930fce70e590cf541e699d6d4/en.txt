2. The findings are made based on the following evaluation of evidence:

2.2. 3. Legally, this results in:
3.1. Regarding the material scope of application of the GDPR and the jurisdiction of the data protection authority
3.1.1. The material scope of application of the GDPR according to Art. 2 GDPR is undoubtedly fulfilled in the present case. The accused did not assert any claim that the GDPR would not apply. The so-called household exemption according to Art. 2 para. 2 lit. c GDPR is not fulfilled.
3.1.2. Art. 83 para. 5 lit. a GDPR stipulates that in the case of violations of the provisions of Art. 5, 6, 7, and 9 GDPR, fines of up to 20,000,000 euros or, in the case of a company, up to 4% of its total worldwide annual turnover of the preceding financial year may be imposed, whichever amount is higher. 3.2.1. Art. 4 No. 2 GDPR defines the term "processing" as any operation or set of operations performed on personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as the collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure or destruction;  
3.2.2. Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR defines the term "controller" as the natural or legal person, authority, agency, or other body that alone or jointly with others determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data.  
3.2.3. According to Art. 4 No. 1 GDPR, "personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("data subject"). A person is considered identified when the identity of the person can be directly inferred from the information itself. A person is identifiable if the information is not sufficient on its own to identify the person, but identification is possible when the information is combined with other information.  
3.2.4. According to Art. 4 No. 15 GDPR, "health data" means personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health services, from which information about their health status can be derived.  
3.2.5. According to Art. 9 para. 1 GDPR, special categories of personal data (formerly "sensitive data") are those data revealing racial and ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, or trade union membership, as well as genetic data, biometric data for the unique identification of a natural person, health data, or data concerning a person's sex life or sexual orientation.  

3.2.6. It is undisputed that the accused, as the controller, processed personal data of the affected individuals by publishing his response.  
3.2.7. There is also no doubt that the information regarding a person's vaginal infection is a health data within the meaning of Art. 4 No. 15 GDPR.  
3.2.8. The processing of health data is generally prohibited under Art. 9 para. 1 GDPR unless one of the exceptions listed exhaustively in para. 2 of the same article applies.  
3.2.9. Art. 9 para. 2 GDPR establishes exceptions to the processing prohibition of Art. 9 para. 1 GDPR and restricts the permissibility of data processing to specific conditions listed in para. 2 lit. a to j of the same article, which are narrower than those for the processing of "non-sensitive" data in Art. 6 para. 1 GDPR, particularly lacking the permissibility conditions of "processing in the legitimate interests of the controller or a third party" (Art. 6 para. 1 lit. f) and "processing for the performance of a contract" (Art. 6 para. 1 lit. b) (cf. Kastelitz/Hötzendorfer/Tschohl in Knyrim, DatKomm, Art. 9 GDPR, para. 1).  
3.2.10. In this case, the disclosure of the health data of the affected individuals could not be justified by any of the conditions listed in Art. 9 para. 2 GDPR. Processing based on the general justification condition of legitimate interests under Art. 6 para. 1 lit. f GDPR is excluded in the case of health data. The argument of the accused in his justification of June 2, 2023, that he aimed to create an accurate overall picture for potential readers, is therefore irrelevant.  
3.2.11. As an interim result, it should be noted that the accused has violated Art. 9 and Art. 5 para. 1 lit. a (principle of lawfulness).  
3.2.12. According to Art. 5 para. 1 lit. b GDPR, personal data must be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and may not be processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes ("purpose limitation").  
3.2.13. In the present case, there was neither a concrete, coherent, nor sufficiently close connection between the purpose of data collection and the further processing of the data, nor was it foreseeable for the affected individual in any way that the accused would publish data regarding her medical diagnosis in response to her W***net rating.  
3.2.14. The accused has therefore also violated the principle of purpose limitation according to Art. 5 para. 1 lit. b GDPR.  
3.2.15. According to Art. 5 para. 1 lit. c GDPR, personal data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed.
3.2.16. The purpose of publishing the diagnosis of the affected individual was, according to the accused, to make his replicating post comprehensible and to create a truthful image for readers.  
3.2.17. However, this purpose could not have been fulfilled solely by mentioning the medical diagnosis of the affected individual in the comment. The accused could have easily addressed the negative review without referencing the medical diagnosis.  
3.2.18. Therefore, the accused also violated the principle of data minimization according to Art. 5 para. 1 lit. c GDPR.  
3.4. Regarding the subjective aspect of the offense  
3.4.1. From a subjective perspective, it should be noted in the present case that due to the conscious publication of the medical diagnosis of the affected individual, it can be assumed that the accused intentionally carried out the processing in question. Therefore, there is culpability in the form of intent on the subjective side in the sense of Art. 83 para. 2 lit. b GDPR.  
4. Regarding the determination of penalties, the following should be noted:

4.1.  
According to Art. 83 para. 1 GDPR, the data protection authority must ensure that the imposition of fines for violations according to paragraphs 5 and 6 is effective, proportionate, and deterrent in each individual case. Furthermore, para. 2 of the same article stipulates that when deciding on the imposition of a fine and its amount, the following must be duly considered in each individual case:  
a) The nature, severity, and duration of the violation, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the processing in question, as well as the number of individuals affected by the processing and the extent of the damage suffered by them;  
b) The intentionality or negligence of the violation;  
c) Any measures taken by the controller or processor to mitigate the damage suffered by the affected individuals;  
d) The degree of responsibility of the controller or processor, taking into account the technical and organizational measures they have implemented in accordance with Articles 25 and 32;  
e) Any relevant previous violations by the controller or processor;  
f) The extent of cooperation with the supervisory authority to remedy the violation and mitigate its possible adverse effects;  
g) Categories of personal data affected by the violation;  
h) The manner in which the violation became known to the supervisory authority, in particular whether and to what extent the controller or processor reported the violation;  
i) […]  
j) […]  
k) Any other aggravating or mitigating circumstances in the specific case, such as financial benefits obtained directly or indirectly through the violation or losses avoided.  
4.2.  
The determination of penalties within a statutory penalty framework is a discretionary decision that must be made according to the criteria established by the legislator in § 19 VStG (see VwGH 05.09.2013, 2013/09/0106).  
4.3.  
According to § 19 para. 1 VStG, the basis for determining the penalty is the significance of the legally protected interest and the intensity of its impairment by the act. Furthermore, according to the purpose of the penalty, the relevant aggravating and mitigating factors, insofar as they do not already determine the penalty, must be weighed against each other. Special consideration must be given to the extent of culpability. Taking into account the nature of administrative criminal law, the provisions of §§ 32 to 35 of the Criminal Code shall apply mutatis mutandis. The income and asset situation and any maintenance obligations of the accused shall be taken into account when determining fines; however, this only to the extent that the directly applicable provisions of the GDPR do not displace the provisions of the VStG and to the extent that is ordered by Art. 83 para. 8 GDPR and Recital 148 regarding the procedural guarantees to be ensured.  
4.4.  
If a fine is imposed on a natural person, a substitute imprisonment must also be set according to § 16 para. 1 VStG for the case of non-collectibility. The substitute imprisonment must not exceed the maximum of the imprisonment threatened for the administrative offense and, if no imprisonment is threatened and nothing else is specified, two weeks.
4.5. According to the case law of the Administrative Court (VwGH), the authority must proceed with an assessment of the income and asset situation when the accused refuses to provide information about these circumstances during the administrative penalty proceedings (see e.g. VwGH 30.06.2004, 2001/09/0120; 22.04.1992, 92/03/0019; 21.06.1999, 98/17/0009). In this case, the accused must be held responsible for their lack of cooperation if the authority has disregarded circumstances in its assessment that could not have come to the authority's attention without their cooperation.

4.6. In the present case, the income and asset situation of the accused could not be established due to the lack of information provided by the accused and therefore could not be taken into account. The Data Protection Authority was therefore required to make an official estimate as a basis for determining the penalty.

4.7. According to the general income report of the Court of Auditors, specialist doctors in self-employed work earned an average of EUR 153,399 in 2022 (see https://www.statistik.at/fileadmin/publications/Einkommensbericht-2022.pdf, p. 183).

4.8. Based on this, a monthly gross income of the accused in the amount of € 12,800 is assumed in the present case.

4.9. Regarding the present facts, the following aggravating factors were considered in the determination of the penalty:
- Nature and severity of the violation: The processing in question by the accused constitutes a serious infringement of the data protection rights of the affected individual. The affected individual could trust that their health data, in the form of a medical diagnosis, would not subsequently be published on the internet by the accused. As a result, the nature and intensity of the infringement of the fundamental right are classified as high.
- Intentionality: The violation was committed intentionally by the accused (see point 3.5.).

4.10. Mitigating factors considered in the determination of the penalty include:
- Previous convictions: There were no relevant previous convictions against the accused with the Data Protection Authority due to violations of the GDPR or the DSG to date.
- Cooperation in the administrative penalty proceedings: The accused responded in a timely manner to the request for justification in the administrative penalty proceedings. The accused admitted to the Data Protection Authority that they had published the response containing the health data of the affected individual. This contributed to clarifying the facts of the case.

4.11. In determining the penalty, considerations of special prevention and general prevention may also be taken into account according to the consistent case law of the VwGH (see VwGH 15.5.1990, 89/02/0093; VwGH 22.4.1997, 96/04/0253; VwGH 29.1.1991, 89/04/0061). Supervisory authorities must also ensure, according to Art. 83 para. 1 GDPR, that fines are effective, proportionate, and deterrent in each individual case.

4.12. The imposition is necessary in the sense of general prevention to raise awareness among responsible parties (e.g., other responsible parties in the healthcare sector) regarding such unlawful data processing.

4.13. For the amount of the imposed fine, there are also special preventive reasons in the present case. The Data Protection Authority was given the impression that the accused could commit such violations in the future as well. In their justification, they stated that they had been forced to present the background to create an accurate picture. Furthermore, from additional responses of the accused to W***net reviews regarding medical visits that took place with them, it emerges that the accused continuously mentions details of the examinations they conducted in response to negative reviews. Corresponding screenshots can be found in the case file of the Data Protection Authority. Future publications of medical diagnoses by the accused do not seem unlikely at this time.

4.14. The specifically imposed penalty therefore appears to be appropriate in terms of the established income situation of the accused, considering the realized value of the offense measured against the available penalty range of Art. 83 para. 5 GDPR of up to € 20,000,000, and its imposition is necessary to deter the accused and third parties from committing the same or similar punishable acts.
