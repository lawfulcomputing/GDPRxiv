D.2. On the Fundamental Right to Confidentiality (Decision Point 1)  
The fundamental right to confidentiality enshrined in § 1 para. 1 DSG states that everyone, particularly with regard to the respect for their private and family life, has a right to the confidentiality of their personal data, as long as there is a legitimate interest in doing so. This includes the protection of the affected person from the investigation of their data and the transfer of data collected about them. However, the fundamental right to confidentiality is not absolute and may be restricted by certain permissible interventions. According to § 1 para. 2 DSG, restrictions on the right to confidentiality, insofar as the use of personal data does not occur in the vital interest of the affected person or with their consent, are only permissible to protect overriding legitimate interests of another party, whereby interventions by a state authority may only occur based on laws that are necessary for the reasons stated in Art. 8 para. 2 ECHR. Restrictions of § 1 para. 1 DSG arise from para. 2 leg. cit., but not from Art. 6 para. 1 GDPR. However, the GDPR and, in particular, the principles enshrined therein must be used for the interpretation of the right to confidentiality (cf. the decision of the Data Protection Authority of July 4, 2019, GZ DSB-D123.652/0001-DSB/2019 with references). According to Art. 4 no. 1 GDPR, "personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. In the case of image recordings, the depicted persons must at least be recognizable or determinable (cf. Hödl in Knyrim [ed.], DatKomm Art. 4 GDPR para. 16). The same applies to audio recordings regarding the recorded individuals.

**D.4. Official Performance Order Regarding the Restriction of the Image and Sound Recording Area of the Camera (Decision Point 2)**

This decision point concerns an official action by the Data Protection Authority in accordance with Article 58(2)(d) of the GDPR, which grants the Data Protection Authority remedial powers allowing it to instruct a controller to modify or conduct processing operations in a specific manner.

Since the image and sound recording area of the camera operated by the respondent captures areas that extend beyond the immediate entrance area of their own apartment, the respondent was instructed to restrict the image and sound recording area accordingly, so that neither the entrance area of the complainants' apartment nor any other areas extending beyond the immediate entrance area of the respondent's apartment (e.g., lift area, entire stairwell) are recorded.

For the interpretation of the "immediate entrance area of the respondent's apartment," reference should be made to the case law of the Data Protection Authority, which states that the permissible limit for capturing areas outside one's own property boundary is a maximum of 50 cm (see the decision of the Data Protection Authority dated December 5, 2017, GZ: DSB-D216.405/0006-DSB/2017) [Note by the editor: This is strictly speaking a recommendation according to the former § 30(6) of the Data Protection Act 2000, which would now be issued in decision form.].

The manner in which the second respondent implements the performance order is left to their discretion. Options may include, for example, a "physical relocation" of the camera or the system-side establishment of so-called "private zones."

The set deadline of three weeks appears to be reasonable to comply with the performance order.
