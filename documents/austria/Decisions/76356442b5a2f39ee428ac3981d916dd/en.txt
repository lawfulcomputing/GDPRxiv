D. From a legal perspective, it follows:  
1. On the right to confidentiality:  
According to § 1 para. 1 DSG, everyone has the right to the confidentiality of their personal data, provided there is a legitimate interest in doing so. The existence of such an interest is excluded if data is not accessible to a confidentiality claim due to its general availability or its lack of traceability to the affected individual.  
The legal definitions and principles of the GDPR are to be used for the interpretation of the right to confidentiality (cf. § 4 para. 1 DSG and the decision of the DSB dated October 31, 2018, GZ DSB-D123.076/0003-DSB/2018). According to Art. 4 No. 1 GDPR, personal data is any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. According to Art. 4 No. 2 GDPR, processing is any operation performed on personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as the collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, deletion, or destruction.  
2. On the distribution of roles:  
The determination of the data protection roles is crucial for the complaint procedure according to § 24 DSG and Art. 77 para. 1 GDPR, as it determines who is responsible for compliance with the respective data protection regulations, how the affected person can exercise their rights, and ultimately against whom the data protection complaint must be directed (respondent).  
According to Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR, the natural or legal person, authority, institution, or other body that determines the purposes and means of processing personal data is the controller. The essential criterion is the decision-making component. The role of the controller thus arises primarily from the fact that a specific entity has decided to process personal data for its own purposes. The "purpose" describes an expected outcome, while the "means" define how the expected outcome is to be achieved (cf. the EDSA Guidelines of September 2, 2020, 07/2020 on the concept of the controller and processor, Rz 15 ff).  
The respondent decided on the purpose of the data processing in question, namely the monitoring and subsequently the protection of Mr. J***, of whom he is the legal representative. He also decided on the means, namely the installation of video cameras. The respondent is therefore the controller according to Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR.  
3. On the camera in the garden:  
The camera in the garden captures, as established, only the exterior area of the ward's apartment. The camera does not capture the entrance area, which is why the comings and goings of the complainant are not recorded. The caregiving service itself is performed inside the apartment, which means that it cannot be assumed that the complainant was captured by this camera while performing her duties as a home helper. The complainant has also never claimed to have been recorded by this camera in the garden. Therefore, the scope of § 1 para. 1 DSG regarding the camera in the garden is not applicable, as the information does not relate to the complainant and thus cannot be classified as personal data. It was therefore decided accordingly, and the complaint on this point was dismissed.  
4. On the cameras in the living and reception rooms:  
In the matter at hand, the respondent records both video and audio with the cameras, thus capturing the appearance, all actions, and the spoken words of the complainant. Therefore, the scope of § 1 para. 1 DSG is applicable, as the information relates to the complainant and thus qualifies as personal data according to Art. 4 No. 1 GDPR. The respondent's action, namely the processing of image and sound, is to be regarded as processing according to Art. 4 No. 2 GDPR.  
Restrictions on the right to confidentiality are permissible according to § 1 para. 2 DSG if personal data is used in the vital interest of the affected person, if the affected person has given their consent (or in GDPR terminology: permission), if there is a qualified legal basis for the use, or if the use is justified by overriding legitimate interests of a third party.
**The data processing in question does not lie in the vital interest of the complainant, and there is neither consent nor a qualified legal basis. At this point, it should be noted for the sake of completeness that the fundamental right to confidentiality under § 1 para. 2 DSG also provides for "legitimate interests of third parties" as a legal basis for processing. However, unlike under Art. 6 para. 1 lit. f GDPR, it must be considered that § 1 para. 2 DSG requires not an equality but a predominance of the interests of third parties for the permissibility of processing. The scheme for the balancing of interests is, however, also transferable to the case of § 1 para. 2 DSG. Accordingly, processing on the legal basis of "legitimate interests" is permissible under three cumulative conditions (cf. Art. 29 Data Protection Working Party, Opinion 06/2014 on the concept of legitimate interest of the data controller according to Article 7 of Directive 95/46/EC, WP 217, 844/14/EN, p. 43 f, according to which implemented protective measures to prevent or mitigate inappropriate consequences for the data subject must be duly considered as a factor in the context of the balancing of interests):  
i. The pursuit of a legitimate interest by the controller or the third party or parties to whom the data is disclosed,  
ii. The necessity of processing the personal data to achieve the legitimate interest, and  
iii. No predominance of the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject over the perceived legitimate interest (cf. the judgment of the CJEU of July 4, 2023, C-252/21 para. 106).  
The respondent explains that the installation of the cameras serves to protect Mr. J***. He is the legal representative of Mr. J*** and explains in his submission the risk of self-harm posed by the represented person, particularly the risk of falling. The protection of vital interests of another natural person is to be considered a legitimate interest, especially in light of Art. 6 para. 1 lit. d GDPR.  
However, the data processing is designed to be (permanently) ongoing. The principle of data minimization under Art. 5 para. 1 lit. c GDPR is directly linked to purpose limitation and establishes three cumulative elements of the factual basis: appropriateness, relevance, and limitation to what is necessary. Recital 39 additionally states that personal data may only be processed if the purpose of processing cannot be reasonably achieved by other means. This principle is intended to ensure that the processing of personal data is reduced to an unavoidable minimum (Jahnel, Commentary on the General Data Protection Regulation Art. 5 GDPR, as of 12.1.2020, rdb.at). In an overall consideration, the principle of data minimization requires a minimization of the affected persons as well as a limitation of the data stored for each affected person.  
However, the recording of the entire living space, continuously, was not necessary to pursue the legitimate interest. During the presence of the nursing staff, these present individuals are able to monitor and ensure the health status and general well-being of Mr. J***. The respondent could have monitored the health and psychological condition of Mr. J*** in another way - namely, for a limited time. An examination of the predominance of the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject over the perceived legitimate interest is unnecessary. Even if the complainant - as he states - had ordered the covering of the cameras, there is still a lack of necessity, and a violation of the principle of data minimization exists, as the complainant is recorded when uncovering and covering, and the sound continues to be recorded.  
It was therefore decided accordingly, and the complaint regarding the cameras in the living room and the anteroom was upheld.  
5. Regarding the performance order:  
The Data Protection Authority has the authority to issue remedial measures under Art. 58 para. 2 lit. d GDPR, allowing it to instruct a controller to change or carry out processing operations in a specific manner and within a specific timeframe. Based on the aforementioned provision, the respondent was therefore instructed ex officio to change the operation of the camera in question.  
As established, the complainant violates the right to confidentiality by operating the cameras in the living room and the anteroom. The respondent argues that the image and sound processing is justified to protect the legitimate interests of third parties, namely the health status and general well-being of his ward. This cannot be followed.  
In the present case, there is certainly the possibility to track the presence and absence of the caregivers. The continuous monitoring of the apartment is not necessary to achieve the purpose and therefore contradicts the principle of data minimization according to Art. 5 para. 1 lit. c GDPR.  
The manner in which the first respondent implements the performance order in point 3 is left to him. The Data Protection Authority has issued a temporal restriction that no recordings shall be made by Camera 1, Camera 2, Camera 3, and Camera 4 during the time of the nursing service, or that the cameras shall be taken out of operation.**
set. Outside of the care service - and thus outside the presence of other affected individuals - it is the responsibility of the complainant to take preventive measures to protect their care recipient.  
Merely covering the cameras is not an adequate measure to safeguard the data protection rights of the care staff, as they are still continuously recorded through audio processing and are captured through image processing until the cameras are covered and after they are uncovered. Furthermore, such a measure places the burden of safeguarding their rights on the caregivers as affected individuals, while the complainant, as the responsible party for ensuring compliance with the GDPR, must take appropriate action.  
The respondent is required to provide the data protection authority with evidence of the restriction of processing in accordance with Art. 58 para. 1 lit. a GDPR. A period of two weeks seems reasonable to implement the changes, which do not involve significant technical effort, and to submit the corresponding documentation to the data protection authority.
