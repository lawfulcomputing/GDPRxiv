D. Rechtslage 
13. 
Die rechtlichen Grundlagen für die Verarbeitung von Bilddaten sind in der Datenschutz-Grundverordnung (DSGVO) sowie im österreichischen Datenschutzgesetz (DSG) geregelt. Gemäß Art. 6 Abs. 1 lit. f DSGVO ist die Verarbeitung personenbezogener Daten rechtmäßig, wenn sie zur Wahrung der berechtigten Interessen des Verantwortlichen oder eines Dritten erforderlich ist, sofern nicht die Interessen oder Grundrechte und Grundfreiheiten der betroffenen Person überwiegen. 

14. 
Im vorliegenden Fall ist zu prüfen, ob die Interessen der U***Ges.m.b.H. an der Aufklärung des Verdachts der Veruntreuung die Interessen der Beschwerdeführerin an der Wahrung ihrer Privatsphäre überwiegen. 

E. Entscheidung 
15. 
Die Datenschutzbehörde kommt zu dem Ergebnis, dass die Videoüberwachung im Gasthaus „T***stuben“ am 2. und 3. November 2019 nicht rechtmäßig war. Die Verarbeitung der Bilddaten war nicht erforderlich, um den Verdacht der Veruntreuung zu überprüfen, da die U***Ges.m.b.H. auch andere, weniger eingreifende Maßnahmen hätte ergreifen können, um den Verdacht zu klären. 

16. 
Die Beschwerde wird daher als begründet erachtet, und die U***Ges.m.b.H. wird angewiesen, die unrechtmäßig erhobenen Bilddaten zu löschen und die Beschwerdeführerin über die Löschung zu informieren.

D. From a legal perspective, it follows:  
D.1. Summary:  
13.  
The - timely submitted and thus permissible - complaint has proven to be unfounded because the respondent is not responsible for the data processing being complained about.  
D.2. Objection of expiration (preclusion) of the right to complain:  
14.  
The respondent has also argued against the complaint (statement from August 2, 2021, see above, para. 6) that the right to complain had already expired due to the passage of time at the time the complaint was submitted, and therefore the complaint is inadmissible in a corresponding manner.  
15.  
According to the procedural provision of § 24 para. 4 DSG, the claim for the handling of a complaint expires if the complainant does not submit it within one year after becoming aware of the event causing the complaint, but at the latest within three years after the event allegedly took place. Late complaints are to be rejected.  
16.  
There is no finding that the complainant was aware of the fact of being subject to image processing before June 10, 2020 (presentation of evidence by U***Ges.m.b.H. as a private participant in the main hearing before the District Court G***stadt, see above para. 11). But even if one were to assume that the complainant had learned something about it at the time of her dismissal on November 4, 2019, the complaint received by the Data Protection Authority on October 15, 2020, would still have been timely filed under the application of the one-year period according to § 24 para. 4 first case DSG. The objection of preclusion of the right to complain has thus proven to be unfounded.  
17.  
D.3. Subject of the complaint procedure, attribution of data processing:  
18.  
Both parties in this procedure have been represented by a lawyer from the outset. Therefore, there was no reason for the Data Protection Authority to inform the parties, especially the complainant, about the legal situation or to provide them with the necessary instructions for carrying out their procedural actions in accordance with § 13a AVG.  
19.  
The data protection complaint procedure according to Art. 77 GDPR and § 24 DSG aims to establish a violation of subjective data protection rights of an affected person upon their request (complaint) (Art. 57 para. 1 lit. f GDPR) and, where possible, to eliminate the legal violation by issuing a corresponding instruction to the responsible party or processor (Art. 58 para. 2 lit. c GDPR). The imposition of an administrative fine (penalty according to Art. 83 GDPR) is not one of these procedural goals, as it must be pursued ex officio in the administrative penalty procedure according to § 25 para. 1 VStG, and there is no subjective right (private prosecution right) of an affected person to impose such a penalty (consistent case law of the Data Protection Authority, see, for example, the decision of July 26, 2019, GZ: DSB-D123.921/0005-DSB/2019, RIS).  
20.  
According to § 24 para. 2 no. 2 DSG, the respondent must be named by the complainant. The Data Protection Authority does not have the authority to change such an explicit designation and to replace the party with which the complainant wishes to engage in the procedure with another one not designated by them (cf. the ruling of the Federal Administrative Court of January 19, 2017, Zl. W214 2117066-1, RIS, with references). It was also reasonable for a party represented by a lawyer to name the respondent here, as the attribution of data processing is a legal question, and the identity and role of the persons involved on the opposing side (respondent, U***Ges.m.b.H.) were known to the complainant.  
21.  
From the findings of fact, it is evident that the complainant was employed in the subsidiary "Gasthaus T***stuben" of U***Ges.m.b.H. This limited liability company was therefore the business carrier or the company according to Art. 4 no. 18 GDPR, whose decision and for whose purposes (investigation of crimes against the property of the company, gathering of evidence for recovery claims) image data of the complainant were processed.  
22.  
U***Ges.m.b.H. was therefore the responsible party for the processing of the complainant's image data according to Art. 4 no. 7 GDPR. The respondent, in turn, was merely the will-forming and executing organ of U***Ges.m.b.H. as the managing director, whereby
in the nature of a limited liability company lies in the fact that it, as a legal entity, relies on individuals as its organs for decision-making.  
23. 
However, this does not change the fact that companies or business entities are attributed by the Union legislators with the ability to make decisions in their own name and under their own responsibility (arg: "the natural or legal person [...], who [...] decides on the purposes and means of processing personal data," Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR, definition of the term "controller"). 
24. 
This interpretation of the GDPR is further emphasized by the Union law penal provisions of Art. 83 GDPR in conjunction with § 30 DSG, which represent one of the few cases in Austrian administrative penal law where penalties (fines) can be imposed on a legal entity, while its organs (and other responsible parties according to § 9 VStG) are only subsidiarily liable under administrative penal law according to § 30 para. 3 DSG.  
D.4. Conclusions:  
25. 
The decision to process image data of the complainant on November 2 and 3, 2019, which interfered with their right to confidentiality—whether justified or unjustified can remain open at this point—is therefore attributable to U***Ges.m.b.H. The respondent is not responsible for any corresponding interference.  
26. 
The complaint was therefore to be dismissed as unfounded in accordance with point 1 of the ruling.  
27. 
The complainant's request to impose a penalty against the respondent was, as already stated above in para. 19, to be rejected as inadmissible in accordance with point 2 of the ruling.
