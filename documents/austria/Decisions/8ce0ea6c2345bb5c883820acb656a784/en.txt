D E C I S I O N  
R U L I N G  
The Data Protection Authority decides in the context of the data protection review pursuant to Art. 58 para. 1 lit. b GDPR against B*** Parkraumbewirtschaftung Ges.m.b.H. (FN *9*3*1v) as follows:  
1. The data processing by B*** Parkraumbewirtschaftung Ges.m.b.H. through video cameras in parking and storage areas within the territory of the Republic of Austria is prohibited with immediate effect and under threat of further enforcement.  
2. The suspensive effect of any timely filed and admissible complaint pursuant to Art. 130 para. 1 no. 1 B-VG is excluded.  
Legal bases: Art. 4 no. 2 and no. 7, Art. 5, Art. 6, Art. 51 para. 1, Art. 57 para. 1 lit. a and lit. h as well as Art. 58 para. 1 lit. b and para. 2 lit. f of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter: GDPR), OJ No. L 119 of May 4, 2016, p. 1; §§ 9, 45 and 46 of the General Administrative Procedure Act 1991 (hereinafter: AVG), Federal Law Gazette No. 51/1991 as amended; § 13 para. 1 and para. 2 of the Administrative Court Procedure Act (hereinafter: VwGVG), Federal Law Gazette I No. 33/2013 as amended.

B. Subject of Review  
The subject of the ex officio review procedure is the question of whether the video surveillance of parking lots operated by B*** Parkraumbewirtschaftung Ges.m.b.H. and, consequently, any video surveillance of public traffic areas on the territory of the Republic of Austria is lawful, i.e., whether it complies with the principles of data processing according to Art. 5 GDPR and whether at least one legality condition according to Art. 6 para. 1 GDPR is fulfilled.  
If unlawful data processing and thus a violation of the GDPR is to be assumed, the further question arises as to which remedial powers according to Art. 58 para. 2 of the GDPR may be considered to eliminate the identified violations.

D. Legally, this leads to:  
D.1. On the lack of cooperation and the factual findings  
It should be noted that a party to the proceedings is generally obliged to contribute to the determination of the facts through a substantiated presentation (cf. VwGH 24.01.2013, 2010/07/0218). Ultimately, it is in the hands of the party itself to effectively participate in the proceedings and to submit its presentation comprehensively and in a timely manner to avoid any potential legal disadvantages.  
Where the authority cannot ascertain facts (including the taking of evidence) without the participation of the party, because they are only known to that party, it is up to the party to provide its knowledge (cf. Hengstschläger/Leeb, Administrative Procedure Law6, Rz. 302 referring to VwGH 19.12.2001, 2000/20/0318; 26.4.2016, Ra 2016/03/0038; 27.2.2018, Ro 2016/05/0009).  
The obligation of a party to cooperate extends only to factual questions and not to the legal question to be resolved or to the domestic legal provisions to be applied by the authority (cf. e.g., VwSlg. 12.684 A/1988). The same applies to the application of directly applicable Union law, as this is part of the national legal framework.  
As can be seen from the findings, B*** Parkraumbewirtschaftung Ges.m.b.H. was repeatedly unsuccessfully requested to participate in the present proceedings and to submit statements. Even the initiation of an administrative penalty proceeding and the imposition of a fine for violation of the duty to cooperate under Article 31 GDPR did not elicit any response from the respondent.  
Likewise, the sole authorized representative of B*** Parkraumbewirtschaftung Ges.m.b.H., Mr. Peter J***, did not appear for an oral hearing before the Data Protection Authority despite duly served summonses and the threatened coercive measures therein.  
The improper participation of a party in the evidence procedure is subject to free assessment of evidence according to § 45 para. 2 and § 46 AVG (cf. the case law cited in Walter/Thienel, Administrative Procedure Laws I 2, p. 678, E 218 f. to § 45 AVG; cf. also VwGH 04.09.2013, 2011/08/0201).  
According to the consistent case law of the Administrative Court, it is within the authority's discretion in the context of free assessment of evidence to draw potentially negative conclusions for the party from the lack of cooperation of a party in the investigative procedure (cf. e.g., VwGH 15.05.1986, Zl. 86/03/0044, or VwGH 11.06.1991, Zl. 90/07/0166).  
Since the relevant facts could be sufficiently established in the present case based on official inquiries and the reports received by the Data Protection Authority, and only legal questions remained to be resolved, it was deemed appropriate, expedient, simple, and cost-saving to refrain from further measures to compel B*** Parkraumbewirtschaftung Ges.m.b.H. to cooperate, especially since it was given sufficient opportunities to participate in the present proceedings.  
D.2. On the admissibility of using evidence from other administrative proceedings
According to § 46 AVG, any evidence that is suitable for determining the relevant facts of the case and is appropriate given the circumstances of the individual case is admissible. According to § 55 para. 1 AVG, the authority may also replace or supplement evidence collection with other investigations. In this regard, it is permissible for evidence and results from another administrative (investigative) procedure to be utilized in the administrative procedure based on free evaluation of evidence and while ensuring the right to be heard (see, for example, the ruling of the Administrative Court of Austria dated October 24, 1986, Zl. 84/17/0218, RS 4 and 5). Since the requests for access to files concerning the ho. GZs: D560.1123, D560.1124, and D560.1128 of B*** Parkraumbewirtschaftung Ges.m.b.H. referenced in the evaluation of evidence are known—especially since they were submitted by the party itself in those proceedings—there are no "secret" pieces of evidence that could not be utilized in the present procedure.

D.3. Regarding the liquidation or winding up of B*** Parkraumbewirtschaftung Ges.m.b.H.  
The Data Protection Authority does not overlook that the dissolution of B*** Parkraumbewirtschaftung Ges.m.b.H. was decided by a resolution of the general assembly on February 14, 2025. Liquidation aims to settle the affairs of a company with the goal of orderly economic winding up and removal from the commercial register. The purpose of a company in the liquidation stage is thus to convert the company’s assets into cash, satisfy creditors, and distribute the remaining assets (see OGH 31.08.2006, 6 Ob 15/05g).  
The onset of liquidation generally does not change the identity of the company or its legal capacity, as it only ceases with its (full) termination and not merely with the onset of liquidation (see RIS-Justiz RS0061815; see also, for example, BVwG 26.11.2020, W145 2216498-2). The use of an additional designation indicating liquidation (as in this case "in liquidation") does not lead to a change in the company name (see RIS-Justiz RS0121129).  
Thus, capital companies capable of being parties lose their capacity to be parties only with their full termination. This, according to prevailing case law, requires the absence of assets and the removal of the company from the commercial register (see RIS-Justiz RS0050186). This applies to both civil and administrative legal proceedings.  
Applied to the present case, it should therefore be noted as an interim result that B*** Parkraumbewirtschaftung Ges.m.b.H. was legally capable and capable of being a party in the sense of § 9 AVG at the time of the issuance of this decision.  
However, even if one were to dispute this in the present case, it should be pointed out that the case law of the European Court of Justice states that when processing personal data, it does not matter whether the responsible parties in the sense of Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR have legal personality and their own legal capacity or not (see ECJ 27.02.2025, C-638/23 [Office of the Tyrolean State Government], para. 34).

D.4. Regarding accountability and the burden of proof  
Art. 5 para. 1 GDPR establishes the principles for the processing of personal data, which represent directly applicable binding rules. According to the consistent case law of the European Court of Justice, any processing of personal data must comply with the principles set out in Art. 5 leg. cit. as well as at least one of the conditions listed in Art. 6 leg. cit. regarding the legality of processing (see regarding the comparable legal situation under Directive 95/46/EC already ECJ December 16, 2008, C‑524/06 [Huber], para. 48).  
The importance of adhering to the processing principles enshrined in Art. 5 para. 1 GDPR is underscored not least by the fact that a violation of those principles is sanctioned with a higher penalty framework according to Art. 83 para. 5 lit. a GDPR.  
According to Art. 5 para. 2 GDPR, the responsible party is responsible for compliance with para. 1 leg. cit. and must be able to demonstrate compliance. This "accountability" is a central element of the GDPR, which emphasizes the increased self-responsibility of the responsible party.  
The European Court of Justice derives from the accountability under Art. 5 para. 2 GDPR that the responsible party must not only be able to demonstrate the legality of data processing but also bears the burden of proof that the personal data processed by them is collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and processed lawfully, in good faith, and in a manner that is understandable to the affected individuals (see ECJ July 4, 2023, C‑252/21 [Federal Cartel Office], para. 95).

D.5. On the merits (point of decision 1)  
It should be noted at the outset that video surveillance—if it processes personal image data—constitutes "processing" in the sense of Art. 4 No. 2 GDPR, which falls within the material scope of the GDPR (see generally, for example, BVwG 09.01.2025, W211 2283857-1; see regarding the qualification of vehicle registration plates as generally available personal data, for example, BVwG 23.09.2022, W137 2252921-1).  
The data processing operations to be examined in this case are attributable to B*** Parkraumbewirtschaftung Ges.m.b.H., as it can be assumed that they process image data from the video surveillance of their parking spaces for their own purposes—thus making decisions about the purposes and means of data processing—and are therefore to be regarded as the responsible party in the sense of Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR.
As can be seen from the established course of proceedings, B*** Parking Management Ges.m.b.H. was repeatedly and unsuccessfully requested to participate in the present proceedings and to demonstrate the legality of the data processing it conducts through video surveillance of parking spaces on the territory of the Republic of Austria. 

B*** Parking Management Ges.m.b.H. ultimately failed to demonstrate that the data processing it operates through video surveillance, in particular, complies with the principles of transparency, data minimization, storage limitation, as well as integrity and confidentiality, nor that the data processing it conducts is lawful within the meaning of Art. 6 para. 1 GDPR. Rather, during the course of the investigation, the impression arose that B*** Parking Management Ges.m.b.H. does not wish to participate in the present proceedings or to evade the proceedings.

As a result, B*** Parking Management Ges.m.b.H. – disregarding its accountability and burden of proof according to Art. 5 para. 2 GDPR – could not demonstrate that the data processing it operates in the form of video surveillance of parking spaces on the territory of the Republic of Austria meets the processing principles and is lawful (cf. regarding the video surveillance of properties CJEU 11 December 2019, C‑708/18 [TK], para. 36 with references). Consequently, there is a violation of the GDPR.

The Data Protection Authority does not overlook that, according to the case law of the Administrative Court, it has no legal basis for an independent determination regarding the potential unlawfulness of a data protection-relevant processing operation in a procedure initiated ex officio (cf. VwGH 14.12.2021, Ro 2020/04/0032).

However, the Data Protection Authority has all remedial powers available under Art. 58 para. 2 GDPR in an ex officio initiated review procedure. According to the case law of the European Court of Justice, situations in which the GDPR is violated should be brought back into compliance with Union law through the intervention of the national supervisory authority (cf. CJEU 14 March 2024, C‑46/23 [Nemzeti Adatvédelmi és Információszabadság Hatóság], para. 40).

According to Art. 58 para. 2 lit. f GDPR, the Data Protection Authority can impose a temporary or definitive restriction on processing, including a prohibition. The chosen measure must be suitable, necessary, and proportionate concerning ensuring compliance with the GDPR, taking into account the circumstances of the individual case (cf. Recital 129 GDPR).

Since, in the present case, B*** Parking Management Ges.m.b.H. has not participated in the proceedings at all and given that, based on the documented complaints and media reports, it can be assumed that a large number of affected individuals are impacted by the processing activities of B*** Parking Management Ges.m.b.H., the Data Protection Authority deemed it necessary to issue an immediate prohibition on data processing, as the choice of milder means does not appear appropriate.

B*** Parking Management Ges.m.b.H. will further have to demonstrate to the Data Protection Authority in an appropriate manner whether and how it has complied with the performance order.

D.6. Regarding the exclusion of suspensive effect (Decision Point 2)

According to § 13 para. 1 VwGVG, a timely and admissible complaint according to Art. 130 para. 1 Z 1 B-VG has a suspensive effect.

According to § 13 para. 2 VwGVG, the authority can exclude the suspensive effect by decision if, after weighing the affected public interests and the interests of other parties, the immediate enforcement of the contested decision or the exercise of the rights granted by the contested decision is urgently required due to a risk of delay. Such a statement should preferably be included in the decision on the main matter.

This results in the immediate enforceability of performance decisions.

As previously stated, the video surveillance operated by B*** Parking Management Ges.m.b.H. of parking spaces on the territory of the Republic of Austria violates fundamental principles of the GDPR, with a large number of affected individuals being assumed.

The interest of B*** Parking Management Ges.m.b.H. in monitoring the parking spaces it manages through video cameras is therefore assessed as lesser in this case than the public interest – namely in the form of the interest of a large number of affected individuals – not to be subjected to unlawful video surveillance. Furthermore, the criterion of "risk of delay" is also met, as it can be assumed that B*** Parking Management Ges.m.b.H. continues to operate unlawful video surveillance, and consequently, if action is delayed, there is an ongoing and concrete risk that affected individuals will be unlawfully monitored by video cameras.

In other words, if the suspensive effect is not excluded, serious disadvantages for the public good or for third parties would arise, which would significantly outweigh the disadvantages that would be incurred by others if the suspensive effect of the complaint is not excluded (cf. VwGH 5.9.2018, Ra 2017/03/0105).

For this reason, a decision was made accordingly.
