D E C I S I O N  
R U L I N G  
The Data Protection Authority decides on the data protection complaint of Johannes A*** (complainant) dated August 31, 2022, against N*** Verkehrsbetriebe AG (respondent) regarding the violation of the right to erasure as follows:  
-  
The complaint is dismissed as unfounded.  
Legal bases: Art. 4 No. 7, Art. 6, Art. 17 para. 1 lit. d and para. 3 lit. b, Art. 51 para. 1, Art. 57 para. 1 lit. f, and Art. 77 para. 1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter: GDPR), OJ No. L 119 of May 4, 2016, p. 1; §§ 18 para. 1 and 24 para. 1 and para. 5 of the Data Protection Act (DSG), Federal Law Gazette I No. 165/1999 as amended; § 132 para. 1 of the Federal Fiscal Code (BAO), Federal Law Gazette No. 194/1961 as amended; § 190 and § 212 of the Commercial Code (UGB), dRGBl. p. 219/1897 as amended; § 18 of the Value Added Tax Act 1994 (UStG 1994), Federal Law Gazette No. 663/1994 as amended.

A. Submissions of the Parties and Procedural History  
1. In a complaint dated August 31, 2022, the complainant approached the Data Protection Authority and claimed that he had been violated in his right to erasure by the respondent. In this regard, the complainant stated that, according to a letter from the respondent dated late June 2022, he had been found without a ticket on May 22, 2022, in a public transport vehicle in H***. Since the complainant was not in H*** at the relevant time and he possessed a climate ticket, he assumed that it was a case of mistaken identity. He communicated this to the respondent on June 20, 2022. On June 21, 2022, the respondent requested him to provide a copy of his climate ticket and an official photo ID. After sending a scan of the climate ticket, the case was closed. With the submission, he also requested the deletion of the scan of the climate ticket after the case was concluded. Furthermore, on June 21, 2022, the complainant submitted a request for erasure pursuant to Art. 17 GDPR to the respondent regarding the data arising from this case. On June 30, 2022, the respondent informed him that it would not comply with the complainant's request and relied on § 212 para. 1 UGB. Since the additional fee demand was based on a misunderstanding, it was clarified after the submission of the climate ticket that the accounting process should not have continued.

After the respondent deleted the complainant's climate ticket, they would agree with this. For the sake of complete accounting, only the data related to the issuance of the invoice or the additional fee, as well as the cancellation, should be retained. However, based on § 190 Abs. 4 UGB, the complainant views the registration and retention obligations differently. The response letter indicated that all data processed in the context of the deletion request would be retained for three years. The respondent is subject to the bookkeeping obligation set forth in § 190 UGB. Essentially similar obligations also arise from § 18 UStG 1994 and § 132 BAO. The entry into the accounting system occurs with the automated transmission of data from "***IS" to "MG**a", as this triggers a claim of the company against the passenger (and also a VAT booking) in "MG**a". This will subsequently be set to either "paid" or "written off." The cancellation, the associated accounting write-off, and the proof thereof are also subject to the accounting retention period. The purpose of these regulations is to ensure a verifiable documentation of accounting transactions for any potential audits by control authorities (tax authorities, auditors) for the legally prescribed duration. This is intended to fulfill the obligations of a proper merchant on the one hand and the verifiability of the accuracy of tax-relevant transactions on the other. Deletion or anonymization of the complainant's data would result in a distortion of the entry in the books, and the respondent would be unable to fulfill its obligations. The purpose of the data application "***IS" is to create a revision-proof documentation of the control process as well as the input and validation of additional charges issued therein. The data contained therein is therefore part of the business transaction underlying the booking and is also subject to retention obligations. Furthermore, the processing of data in "***IS" also serves the function of being the basis for the exercise or defense of legal claims. In particular, the data contained therein—additional to the information in "MG**a"—such as the general description of the person or the names of the controlling employees, serves as evidence in court in the event of a dispute over a claim. No negligent behavior can be attributed to the data collection in question. The employees requested identification for reasons of clarity in identifying a passenger. However, if a controlled passenger does not carry or present an ID, the employees have no coercive means to establish the identity. Therefore, the processing of the data until the end of the retention obligation on December 31, 2029, is necessary and thus permissible.

D. From a legal perspective, it follows that:  
1. The respondent processes personal data of the complainant and is undoubtedly the controller within the meaning of Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR.  
2. According to Art. 17 para. 1 GDPR, every data subject has the right to request the deletion of their personal data from a controller. According to para. 3 lit. b of the same provision, the controller may refuse such a deletion request insofar as the processing of the data is necessary "for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject under Union or Member State law."  
3. The respondent relies on §§ 190 and 212 para. 1 UGB, § 18 UStG 1994, and § 132 BAO regarding the (further) storage of the complainant's personal data in the context of the cancellation of the additional fee.  
According to § 212 para. 1 UGB, the entrepreneur must keep his books, inventories, opening balances, annual financial statements along with the management reports, consolidated financial statements along with the consolidated management reports, received business letters, copies of sent business letters, and documents for bookings in the books he is required to maintain according to § 190 (booking documents) in an orderly manner for seven years; furthermore, as long as they are relevant for an ongoing judicial or administrative procedure in which the entrepreneur is a party. The enumeration of the documents to be retained in § 212 para. 1 UGB is merely demonstrative according to the materials (see Fraberger/Petritz in Hirschler, BilR § 212 Rz 10; ErlRV RLG 52).  
Tax law provides a parallel provision in § 132 BAO, whose provisions largely correspond to those of § 212 UGB (see U. Torggler in Straube/Ratka/Rauter, WK UGB II/RLG3 § 212 Rz 4). To the extent that business papers and other documents are relevant for tax assessment, these should also be retained for seven years (see also the recording obligation for invoices according to § 11 UStG 1994 regulated in § 18 UStG 1994).  
The norm is a necessary requirement to comply with the accounting obligation according to §§ 189 et seq. UGB (see Steiner in Straube/Ratka/Rauter, UGB II/RLG § 212). Business transactions and accounting processes should be presented in a traceable manner to enable the competent authorities – over a certain period – to make tax assessments and, in particular, audits. The cancellation of a claim and the related accounting write-off must also be recorded in the accounting to document the creation and processing of this business case in a traceable manner.  
4. Based on the findings made, the Data Protection Authority states that the personal data of the complainant was collected by the respondent without his involvement. The controlled person apparently acted with fraudulent intent when, during the control of his ticket, he did not provide his own personal data but that of the...
**1.** The complainant disclosed his name and date of birth, as well as a false address. Nevertheless, on May 22, 2022, employees of the respondent encountered a passenger without a valid ticket, issued an additional charge to this individual, and thus triggered a business case in the sense of the UGB (Austrian Commercial Code) and the BAO (Federal Fiscal Code).

**2.** As established, during this process, the control procedure, including the personal data of the complainant, was entered into the data application "Validation and Management of Ticket Controls," also referred to as "***IS," for the purpose of documenting the control procedure. The data relevant for debt collection and billing from the control procedure was transferred via an interface into the data application "Management and Processing of Additional Charges," also referred to as "MG**a," for the purpose of accounting retention. In both relevant data applications, the personal data of the complainant continues to be processed, as can be seen from the documentation submitted under point C.4.

**3.** Based on the legal obligation in the UGB and the BAO, the respondent must be supported in that the storage of the relevant personal data of the complainant in the data applications "***IS" and "MG**a" is necessary for fulfilling the legal tasks assigned to the respondent under the UGB and the BAO, in order to comply with the principles of proper accounting. It should be noted that the documentation of the control procedure in the system "***IS" is directly related to the business case in "MG**a" and is thus significant for tax-related traceability. 

**4.** Even though it is absolutely understandable for the data protection authority that it is very frustrating for the complainant that his personal data continues to be stored by the respondent without his involvement despite the cancellation of the claim, it appears necessary for the data protection authority, in terms of accounting verifiability by the tax authority, to maintain a personal reference to the complainant.

**5.** Against this background, the storage of the relevant personal data of the complainant in the data applications "***IS" and "MG**a" is necessary for fulfilling the legal tasks assigned to the respondent under the UGB and the BAO, in order to comply with the principles of proper accounting. 

**6.** Therefore, the respondent was obliged to retain the data relevant to the proceedings in accordance with § 132 Abs. 1 BAO and §§ 190, 212 UGB. In this case, there are also no indications that the complainant's data has been used by the respondent for purposes other than intended, which is why Art. 17 Abs. 1 lit. d GDPR is not applicable. 

**7.** As a result, the respondent rightly did not comply with the complainant's request for deletion dated June 21, 2022, as the processing is necessary under Art. 17 Abs. 3 lit. b GDPR. 

**8.** It was therefore to be decided accordingly.
