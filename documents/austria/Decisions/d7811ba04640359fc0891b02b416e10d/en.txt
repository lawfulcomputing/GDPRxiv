D. From a legal perspective, it follows:  
1. General information on the distribution of roles under data protection law  
According to Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR, the natural or legal person, authority, institution, or other entity that determines the purposes and means of processing personal data, either alone or jointly with others, is the controller for that processing. The essential criterion here is the decision-making component. The role of the controller thus primarily arises from the fact that a specific entity has decided to process personal data for its own purposes.  
The "purpose" describes an expected outcome, while the "means" define how the expected outcome is to be achieved (cf. Art. 29 Data Protection Working Party, Opinion 1/2010 on the concepts of "controller" and "processor," WP 169, 00264/10/DE, pp. 15 ff, still in relation to Art. 2 lit. d of the Data Protection Directive 95/46/EC, as well as regarding the new legal situation see the EDPB guidelines of September 2, 2020 "Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of controller and processor in the GDPR" available at https://edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/public-consultations-art-704/2020/guidelines-072020-concepts-controller-and-processor_en)  
According to Art. 4 No. 8 GDPR, the natural or legal person, authority, institution, or other entity that processes personal data on behalf of the controller is the processor. The essential criterion here is the binding nature of instructions. The role of the processor thus primarily arises from the fact that a specific entity has decided to process personal data on behalf of the controller (cf. Art. 29 Data Protection Working Party, WP 169, 00264/10/DE, pp. 30 ff).  
2. Regarding the complaint against the first respondent  

As can be seen from the established facts, the first respondent is required to ensure access to the land register database through IT applications based on an agreement with the Republic of Austria – represented by the second respondent. However, the first respondent does not have decision-making authority over which data/documents are included in the land register database; it is only responsible for ensuring that the content is forwarded unchanged and completely via IT applications, thereby enabling online access. It is also bound by the instructions of its principal.  
Therefore, the first respondent does not qualify as a controller under Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR, which is why the complaint against it had to be dismissed.  
3. Regarding the complaint against the second respondent  
According to § 1 para. 1 GUG, the Federal Minister of Justice is authorized, in agreement with the Federal Minister for Economic Affairs, to order the transition of the land register to automated data processing (§ 2 para. 1) according to the technical and personnel possibilities and taking into account the economic feasibility for certain courts by regulation.  
The second respondent is therefore to be qualified as (jointly) responsible for the operation of the land register database as well as the document database.  
However, the second respondent does not have decision-making authority over which data/documents are included in the land register database or the document database; this decision is made exclusively by the third respondent as the locally competent land register court (cf. § 75 GBG in conjunction with § 1 § 118 No. 4 JN). Also, any requests for restrictions on access to the documents included in the document collection and stored in the document database must be decided exclusively by the third respondent upon request of an affected person in non-contentious proceedings (cf. § 6b GUG).  
Therefore, the second respondent is not to be qualified as a controller under Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR regarding these specific processing activities (inclusion/restriction of data in the land register or document database), which is why the complaint against it had to be dismissed.  
4. Regarding the complaint against the third respondent  
4.1. Regarding the jurisdiction of the Data Protection Authority  
According to Art. 55 para. 3 GDPR, supervisory authorities are not responsible for overseeing processing carried out by courts in the course of their judicial activities.
Recital 20 of the GDPR states that this is intended to serve the independence of the judiciary in the exercise of its judicial tasks, including its decision-making. The judgment of the CJEU of March 24, 2022, regarding the interpretation of Article 55(3) GDPR indicates that the scope of the objective pursued by Article 55(3) GDPR, which is to safeguard the independence of the judiciary in the exercise of its judicial tasks, cannot be limited solely to ensuring judicial independence in the context of issuing a specific judicial decision (Judgment of March 24, 2022, C-245/20, Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, para. 33). Therefore, the term "judicial activity" should be understood broadly.

In the (preliminary) conclusions of the Advocate General dated October 6, 2021, it was stated that, according to Article 55(3) GDPR, two conditions must be met: first, the involvement of a certain type of institution ("courts") and second, a specific type of activity of these courts ("in the context of their judicial activity") (see Advocate General's conclusions of October 6, 2021, C-245/20, para. 72).

In this context, it should be noted that through the Land Register Amendment 2024, Federal Law Gazette I No. 91/2024, the GUG has been amended such that § 6b GUG now provides that the third respondent, as the competent land register court, must decide on applications from an affected person for the restriction of access to documents in the collection of documents in a non-contentious procedure. The applicant also has party status in such a procedure (see § 6b(2) GUG).

In this regard, the jurisprudence of the Data Protection Authority, according to which a land register court acts merely as a "register court" in land register matters and consequently there is a jurisdiction of the Data Protection Authority (see the decision of June 9, 2022, GZ 2021-0.643.804, RIS), cannot be upheld. 

Therefore, it concerns both the involvement of a court (first condition) and an activity with judicial character (second condition), which is why there is overall a judicial activity in the sense of Article 55(3) GDPR (see also the decision of January 22, 2019, GZ DSB-D123.848/0001-DSB/2019, RIS regarding maintenance disputes).

Except in the case of contrary statutory provisions, the authority must consider the factual and legal situation at the time of the decision (see e.g., VwGH August 24, 2023, Ra 2022/22/0093). 

The Data Protection Authority is therefore not competent to handle the complaint against the third respondent, which is why it had to be dismissed. 

It was therefore to be decided in accordance with the ruling.
