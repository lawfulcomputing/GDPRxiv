D E C I S I O N  
R U L I N G  
The Data Protection Authority decides on the data protection complaints of Rudolf A*** (first complainant) and Irene A*** (second complainant), both dated December 8, 2019 (received on December 9, 2019), against N* GmbH (respondent) due to alleged violation of the right to confidentiality as follows:  
1. The complaints are dismissed as unfounded.  
2. The requests of the first complainant and the second complainant for compensation for the damages caused by the legal violation are rejected.  
Legal bases: Art. 4, Art. 6 para. 1 lit. f, Art. 51 para. 1, Art. 57 para. 1 lit. f as well as Art. 77 para. 1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation, hereinafter: GDPR), OJ No. L 119 of May 4, 2016, p. 1; §§ 1 para. 1, 18 para. 1, 24 para. 1, para. 2 and para. 5 as well as 29 of the Data Protection Act (DSG), Federal Law Gazette I No. 165/1999 as amended; §§ 17 para. 3 and 39 para. 2 of the General Administrative Procedure Act 1991 (AVG), Federal Law Gazette No. 51/1991 as amended; § 151 of the Trade Regulation Act 1994 (GewO 1994), Federal Law Gazette No. 194/1994 as amended.

R E A S O N I N G  
A. Submissions of the parties and procedural history  
1. In initial submissions dated December 8, 2019 (received on December 9, 2019), the complainants alleged a violation of the right to confidentiality. The proceedings were subsequently joined for joint treatment in accordance with § 39 para. 2 AVG.  
2. Regarding the alleged legal violation, the complainants stated that it was entirely beyond their knowledge how the respondent obtained their personal data. There was a possibility that this data had been obtained from the former employment relationship of the first complainant or from the bank account of the second complainant. The submissions included copies of the respective information letters.  

3. By decision dated May 28, 2020, the Data Protection Authority requested the respondent to provide a statement.  
4. In a submission dated June 10, 2020, the respondent stated that it had the commercial authorization of an address publishing and direct marketing company. Based on this, it had obtained the personal data of the complainants. Consent was not required. The provided information indicated that the respondent had received the data from other address publishers. The data records of the complainants had been blocked due to the inquiry and were no longer shared.  
5. By decision dated November 10, 2020, the Data Protection Authority granted the complainants the right to be heard.  
6. No further statement was made by the complainants.  
7. By decision dated August 12, 2021, the Data Protection Authority requested the respondent to submit the statements of the data suppliers referred to in accordance with § 151 para. 4 and 5 GewO.  
8. In a submission dated August 20, 2021, the respondent submitted the requested documents, which were exempted from inspection based on a corresponding request according to § 17 para. 3 AVG and therefore were not transmitted to the complainants within the framework of the right to be heard.  
B. Subject of the complaint  
Based on the initial submission, the subject of the complaint arises from the question of whether the respondent violated the first complainant and the second complainant in their right to confidentiality according to § 1 para. 1 DSG by obtaining their address data, specifically salutation, name, date of birth, and address, from the customer and prospect databases of other address and direct marketing companies.

D. From a legal perspective, it follows:  
D.1. On the admissibility of "secret evidence"  
1.) According to § 45 para. 3 AVG, the parties must be given the opportunity to be informed and to respond to the results of the evidence collection (principle of granting parties a hearing, cf. VwGH March 27, 1990, 89/08/0250).  
2.) In exceptional cases, however, the admissibility of a decision justification based on evidence that is excluded from inspection may arise from the ruling of the Constitutional Court of October 10, 2019, E 1025/2018, according to which the interests of procedural parties in access to procedurally relevant information compete with the interests of procedural parties in the protection of confidential information and trade secrets.  
3.) Specifically, the Administrative Court stated (emphasis by the Data Protection Authority):  
"Neither the right guaranteed by Article 6 of the ECHR within the framework of the principle of equality of arms to access procedural files nor the right protected particularly by Article 8 of the ECHR to confidentiality of business and trade secrets can establish an absolutely protected legal position. Rather, in administrative proceedings or in administrative court proceedings, the right of access to decision-relevant information must be weighed against the rights of other procedural parties to protect their confidential information and trade secrets. The principle of protection of confidential information and trade secrets must be designed in such a way that it meets the requirements of a...
effective legal protection and the safeguarding of the procedural rights of the parties involved in the proceedings in accordance with the law, and that it is ensured that the right to a fair trial is respected overall.  
[…]  
The fact that individual documents are excluded from inspection under § 17 para. 3 AVG does not necessarily mean, in this context, that a violation of the right to be heard in the sense of § 45 para. 3 AVG occurs when the authority nevertheless refers to the corresponding documents. It is indeed a fundamental principle of any lawfully organized administrative procedure that there must be no secret evidence (as specifically addressed in VwGH 17.6.2004, 2003/03/0157; 25.9.2014, 2011/07/0006). However, in certain exceptional cases, it may be necessary to withhold certain information from the parties in order to protect the fundamental rights of a third party or other participants in the proceedings, or to safeguard important interests of the general public, as long as it is ensured that both the authority and the administrative court called upon in the appeal process have full access to all decision-relevant documents (see ECJ 14.2.2008, Case C-450/06, Varec SA; further Hanslik, aaO, 139 ff.). The information withheld from the parties to the proceedings must be limited to what is absolutely necessary, and all possibilities must be exhausted to restrict the decision-making basis so that withheld information does not need to be used for the decision-making process. The authority or the administrative court must provide a comprehensible justification for the balance between the claim of confidentiality and the right to access files, and thus the transparency of the decision-making basis, so that the parties to the proceedings can subject this to administrative court review or an appeal.  
In principle, effective legal protection (VfSlg 13.699/1994) and an effective remedy (Art. 13 ECHR) are thus guaranteed on the basis of § 17 AVG.  
4.) In the present case, the documents mentioned in the findings C.6. and C.7. ("Certificates of No Objection") and the (detailed) information contained therein were excluded from inspection of the files or presentation to the appellants based on a corresponding request from the respondent in accordance with § 17 para. 3 AVG. However, the consideration of this information by the Data Protection Authority was necessary insofar as the existence of a written "Certificate of No Objection" constitutes a significant legality requirement for processing in the sense of § 151 GewO (see Point D.2.).  

5.) It would not have been possible to transmit the evidence excluded from file inspection or from the right to be heard to the appellants without disclosing information covered by the confidentiality interest of the respondent regarding its business relationships with third parties (trade secrets).  
6.) At the same time, the interest of the appellants in knowing the entire content of the document is diminished in that the fact that the statements exist is known to the appellants, and thus the withholding of detailed information does not represent a significant limitation of the procedural transparency relevant to the appellants. Moreover, the legal assessment of the statements was to be carried out ex officio by the Data Protection Authority anyway.  
7.) A balancing of these conflicting interests in the present case therefore led to the conclusion that this evidence could not be made accessible to the appellants.  
D.2. Regarding the subject of the complaint and the legality of data processing (Decision Point 1)  
8.) The appellants claimed a violation of the right to confidentiality by asserting that the respondent unlawfully obtained their "most personal" data from a former employment relationship or bank account contract.  
9.) Based on this assertion, the Data Protection Authority assumed that the appellants primarily objected to the unlawful processing of the address data – salutation, name, date of birth, and address – and thus the subject of the examination was limited to this category of data. The authority's obligation to conduct an ex officio investigation of the truth does not extend so far as to require it to conduct investigations in every conceivable direction, but only to the extent that indications from the files (e.g., the party's assertion [VfSlg 13.227 A/1990]) provide cause for it (VwGH 4.4.2002, 2002/08/0021).  
10.) As can be seen from the findings, the respondent holds a valid business license to operate as an address publisher and direct marketing company. § 151 GewO imposes additional trade law provisions regarding the use of personal data for third-party marketing purposes, alongside the GDPR and the DSG, for those authorized to operate as address publishers and direct marketing companies. As "special data protection law" (see Risz in Ennöckl/N. Raschauer/Wessely, GewO § 151 Rn. 9 with references), the provision contains regulations under which conditions address publishers and direct marketing companies may collect and use personal data.
11.) The direct marketing entrepreneur must have access to data concerning the selected target groups in order to achieve their business purpose. This is also in the interest of the businesses that commission a direct marketing entrepreneur to carry out advertising campaigns. Therefore, the flow of data between the business and the direct marketing company must be enabled in both directions, with the limitation that the transmission of data must always be tied to the purpose of conducting advertising campaigns (cf. Gruber/Paliege-Barfuß, GewO7, § 151, Rn. 6).

12.) If, as in this case, there is no consent from the affected individuals (Art. 4 No. 11 GDPR) for the transmission of their data for third-party marketing purposes, address publishers and direct marketing companies may only obtain the data explicitly listed in § 151 para. 5 first sentence GewO from a customer and prospect database of a third party. Furthermore, it is a prerequisite that the holder of the database has provided a written declaration of no objection to the business, stating that the affected individuals have been adequately informed about the possibility of objecting to the transmission of their data for third-party marketing purposes, and that no objection has been made (declaration of no objection).

13.) The respondent, as a business, received the address data of the complainants from the customer and prospect database of "Marketing***XY AG" or "W***Direkt GmbH." Since, as established, the necessary declarations of no objection for lawful processing are available, the processing of the address data (salutation, name, date of birth, and address) for marketing purposes was deemed permissible even without explicit consent, and the complaints were ultimately found to be unfounded (cf. also the decision of April 18, 2019, GZ: DSB-D123.986/0003-DSB/2019).

14.) It was therefore decided accordingly.

D.3. Regarding the rejection of the claims for compensation for the damages incurred (Decision Point 2)

15.) The complainants also requested the award of appropriate compensation for the damages they allegedly incurred.

16.) According to § 29 para. 1 DSG, any affected person who has suffered material or immaterial damage due to a violation of the GDPR or the DSG has the right to compensation from the responsible party under Art. 82 GDPR.

17.) However, it should be noted that according to § 29 para. 2 DSG, for claims for damages in the first instance, the regional court responsible for the exercise of jurisdiction in civil matters is competent.

18.) Due to the lack of jurisdiction of the Data Protection Authority, it was decided accordingly.
