2. In a statement dated December 3, 2018 (supplemented upon request on January 10, 2019),  
the respondent, represented by B*** & C*** Attorneys-at-Law, submitted the following, as far as relevant to the proceedings:  
The respondent offers its services on the platform www.n***.at. As part of the free service, which ultimately represents a marketing activity for its own services,

In the course of the research, internet users have the opportunity to easily search for a legal entity registered in the commercial register to learn about any ownership relationships, as well as to determine with which other legal entities a natural person holds a representative function. However, a direct inquiry about the person is not possible.  
Since the exercise of this commercial activity would not be meaningful without the collection, storage, and transfer of data, it must also be assumed that the legislator has a legitimate interest that outweighs the interests of the affected parties in certain categories of cases. The legitimate interest of the respondent, within the framework of its business license, is to provide the published (commercial register) data, including the links with names and first names as well as functions as managing directors and shareholders of registered legal entities, to the users of the website as interesting and useful data.  
Since different companies also advertise on the platform, it is also in the interest of the respondent to generate as many users of the website as possible through interesting information provided there, in order to have as high a number of visitors on the website as possible, thereby generating advertising revenue. These advertising revenues constitute a part of the respondent's revenue.  
Data of the complainant can only be accessed by entering a legal entity that is registered in the commercial register.  
According to Section 9 para. 1 UGB, everyone is entitled to inspect the commercial register, and there are no restrictions on access. The commercial register also enjoys publicity in the sense of Section 15 UGB and is also a "list of persons with links" in the commercial register accessible to the public. It is generally known that such company data, thus facts that are subject to registration and publication obligations and are accessible in the commercial register, as well as data published in the Edict Data and the Wiener Zeitung in accordance with Section 10 para. 1 UGB, can be processed further by third parties, including the respondent. For example, the Bar Association also offers its members access to ****-Info as a service. This even provides extended functionality compared to the service of the respondent.

D. From a legal perspective, it follows that:
The fundamental right to data protection enshrined in § 1 DSG, according to which everyone, particularly with regard to the respect for their private and family life, has a right to the confidentiality of their personal data, as long as there is a legitimate interest in protection, includes the protection of the affected individual against the collection of their data and the disclosure of the data collected about them. However, the fundamental right to data protection is not absolute and may be restricted by certain permissible interventions.
According to § 1 para. 2 DSG, restrictions on the right to confidentiality, insofar as the use of personal data does not occur in the vital interest of the data subject or with their consent, are only permissible to protect overriding legitimate interests of another party. 

The data used by the respondent concerning the complainant originates from the commercial register. As the respondent correctly stated, the commercial register is public according to § 10 UGB and anyone is entitled to query the commercial register according to § 9 UGB. 

However, to the extent that the respondent argued in this context that the data in question is already "generally available" and thus implies that it would not be subject to a confidentiality claim, it must be countered that the general assumption of the absence of a violation of worthy confidentiality interests for data that is lawfully published is not in accordance with European legal requirements (see the judgment of the CJEU of December 16, 2008, C-73/07 Satakunnan Markkinapörssi and Satamedia, see also the decision of the BVwG of July 29, 2020, GZ W211 2221963-1, and of February 25, 2021, GZ W274 2236016-1, as well as the decision of the Data Protection Authority of April 23, 2019, GZ D123.626/0006-DSB/2018). 

If the respondent further claims that one can also find connections through a targeted person search in the commercial register and thus assumes that one might be dealing with a mere reproduction of "generally accessible data," they are mistaken, as the respondent recombines and links the commercial register data, thereby generating an informational added value, which means that in this context a new data use must be assumed, the permissibility of which must be examined according to the provisions of the DSG and the GDPR (see regarding the legal situation under the DSG 2000 Kotschy in Jahnel (ed.), Data Protection Law and E-Government Yearbook 2012, 27 [47], the decision of the BVwG of February 25, 2021, GZ W274 2238717-1, and the decision of the Data Protection Authority of January 15, 2019, GZ D123.527/0004-DSB/2018). 

As a justification, in the present case – as correctly stated by the respondent – data processing based on overriding legitimate interests in the sense of § 1 para. 2 DSG or Art. 6 para. 1 lit. f GDPR is conceivable. 

In this regard, an assessment of the legitimate interests of the complainant must be carried out and these must be weighed against the legitimate interests of the respondent (as well as possibly third parties). In the context of this balancing of interests, it must be taken into account that two cumulative conditions must be met for the respondent to rely on this permission provision: on the one hand, the processing must be necessary to protect the legitimate interests of the controller or a third party, and on the other hand, the fundamental rights and freedoms of the affected person (in this case: the complainant), which require the protection of personal data, must not outweigh (see the judgment of the CJEU of November 24, 2011, C-468/10 and C-469/10 [ASNEF and FECEMD] para. 38; see also the judgment of the CJEU of May 4, 2017, C‑13/16 [Rīgas satiksme] para. 28). 

In the context of the balancing of interests, it must be considered whether an affected person could reasonably foresee at the time of the collection of personal data and in light of the circumstances under which it occurs that processing for this purpose might take place. The weighting must be done from an objective perspective and not from the subjective perspective of individual affected persons; thus, individual feelings should not be taken into account (see Kastelitz/Hötzendorfer/Tschohl, op. cit. para. 51). 
[Note by the editor: referring to Kastelitz/Hötzendorfer/Tschohl in Knyrim, DatKomm Art 6 GDPR, para. 51] 

The respondent stated in this context that the complainant's data primarily serves their economic interest as well as the interest of participants in economic life to know which persons are involved in companies and what positions they hold in other companies. This allows a participant in economic life to better assess a potential contractual partner and, for example, recognize whether the person(s) behind the contractual partner as shareholders are still active in other companies or not. Since different companies also advertise on the platform, it is also in the interest of the respondent to generate as many users of the website as possible through interesting information provided there, in order to have as high a number of people on the website as possible, thereby generating advertising revenue. 

Considering that due to their general availability, a lower degree of protection for the complainant's data is to be assumed, and that this data ultimately only pertains to the professional sphere of the complainant, who decides for themselves whether to participate in economic life as a shareholder or managing director, and therefore only a low intensity of interference is to be assumed overall, the Data Protection Authority concludes that the legitimate interests of the respondent in processing the complainant's data within the framework of their web service outweigh the interests of the complainant. 

The complaint has proven to be unfounded and was therefore dismissed in accordance with § 24 para. 5 DSG.
