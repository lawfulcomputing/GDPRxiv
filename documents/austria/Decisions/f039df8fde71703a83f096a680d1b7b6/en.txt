D. From a legal perspective, it follows:  
D.1. Designation of the respondent  
Although the complainant referred to Mr. Charles Tr***, Legal Counsel of Wikimedia Foundation, as the respondent in his complaint, a reasonable interpretation of the complaint suggests that it was directed against Wikimedia Foundation Inc. (cf. the decision of the VwGH dated April 28, 2021, Ra 2019/04/0138). The respondent also assumed that the complaint was directed against it.  
D.2. Subject matter of the proceedings  
According to Art. 57 para. 1 lit. f GDPR, the data protection authority must investigate the subject matter of the complaint to an appropriate extent. The "subject matter of the complaint" is the administrative matter defined in scope by the party's submission at the time of application (here: at the time of filing the complaint), which is the "matter under negotiation" according to § 59 para. 1 AVG. It follows that the complainant is not free to expand the subject matter of the complaint with new submissions during the ongoing proceedings. Therefore, the subject matter of the proceedings is exclusively the deletion of the entry about the complainant on the platform of the respondent (cf. initiating submission dated February 14, 2019, and improvement dated February 21, 2019).
According to § 13 Abs. 8 second sentence AVG, the amendment of the application must not change the essence of the matter and must not affect the substantive and local jurisdiction. The Administrative Court has also decided in its ruling of May 26, 2021, Ra 2019/04/0071, that it may only decide on an amendment of the application within the framework of § 13 Abs. 8 AVG if the essence of the matter is not changed and the limits of the subject of the complaint are not exceeded (see also Köhler, § 28 VwGVG, in: Köhler/Brandtner/Schmelz [eds.], VwGVG Commentary [2021] Rz. 41).  
The articles linked by the applicant in the statement of January 13, 2021, regarding individual films in which he participated, are not the subject of the complaint in this proceeding. The subject of the complaint is clearly evident from the initiating submission.  
D.3. Regarding the applicability of the GDPR and the jurisdiction of the Data Protection Authority  
The respondent is a non-profit foundation based in the USA. The USA is to be considered a third country in the context of European law. The respondent operates an online encyclopedia, on which personal data (first and last name, date of birth, career, filmography) of the applicant are processed automatically. At the time of the issuance of the decision, the respondent, as an umbrella organization, has 38 associations (referred to by the respondent as branch associations), of which at least one is located in Austria (see point 1 of the findings).  
According to Art. 3 Abs. 2 lit. a GDPR, the GDPR applies to the processing of personal data of data subjects who are in the European Union by a controller not established in the Union, if the data processing is related to the offering of goods or services to a data subject in the Union, regardless of whether a payment is to be made by that data subject.  
The respondent itself offers Austrian website visitors information on a variety of topics through its online encyclopedia. The (Austrian) users access information about the applicant, which is presented in German, via the respondent's website. The term service is not defined in detail in the GDPR but is to be interpreted very broadly. It is evident that the respondent offers information services and has also targeted these at the Austrian market (“market location principle”). Furthermore, Art. 3 Abs. 2 lit. a second sentence GDPR explicitly states that the issue of whether the service is provided for a fee or free of charge is irrelevant (a search engine operator typically does not require a fee from the end consumer for providing information, see Ernst in Paal/Pauly, GDPR Art. 3 Rz 17).
According to Art. 3 para. 2 lit. a GDPR, data processing must be related to an offer of goods or services "in the Union." Although the wording does not refer to "directing" as was required in the earlier version of the Commission's proposal ("directed to") and an earlier draft proposal of the Parliament ("aimed at"), it is clear from Recital 23 that the offer must be aimed at affected persons located in the Union in accordance with the principle of market location (see Klar in Kommentar Kühling/Buchner 2017). 

The CJEU holds in the case of Pammer/Reederei Karl Schlüter GmbH & Co and Hotel Alpenhof/Heller (joined cases C-585/08 and C-144/09) that the individual components must always be considered as a whole and that, among other things, the endings of the top-level domain address, the use of a language, the location of the customer base, and the facilitation of access to the website are to be regarded as essential factors. The linguistic design of the information service can, according to Recital 23, be an indication of offering for the European (specifically the Austrian) market. 

The respondent has a total of 38 national associations, some of which are based in the European Union (including Austria, France, Germany, Belgium, and Sweden). The individual national websites either directly or through an intermediary link to the homepage of the respondent. Due to the structure and close interconnection of the websites of the national branches and those of the respondent, there is undoubtedly a facilitation of access. 

If the name of the complainant is entered on the website aimed at the Austrian market "http://wikipedia.at," which is also equipped with the country-specific top-level domain ending ".at," there is an automatic redirection to https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_A***. It is not denied by the data protection authority that the Austrian site is registered to the organization Wikimedia Österreich Gesellschaft zur Förderung freien Wissens, which is a branch in the form of an association of the respondent in Austria. However, this clearly demonstrates the close interconnection of the (branch) associations of the respondent with the respondent itself and the accompanying goal of facilitating access for Austrian users. Due to the automatic redirection (after entering a search term) from "http://wikipedia.at" to https://de.wikipedia.org, no further intermediary steps need to be taken by the website visitor. 

Additionally, the article about the complainant is only available in two languages (German and ***ish). Thus, the information service regarding the complainant is clearly aimed at the German-speaking area, and not merely at the US market. The film productions in which the complainant has participated are also marked with a reference to Austria.
It should be emphasized that the website of the complainant is not accessible in English, which is the official language of the country where the respondent is headquartered. Thus, information is specifically tailored to individual countries or regions.  
From these individual points, it is evident that the respondent clearly intends to attract Austrian users for its information service. Internet users are also deliberately redirected from the website with the ".at" domain ending (from a nationally established association of the respondent) to their own homepage.  
The material and territorial scope is present, and the GDPR is therefore applicable.  
D.4. Regarding the Responsibility Characteristic  
The determination of the distribution of data protection roles is crucial for the complaint procedure under § 24 DSG or Art. 77 para. 1 GDPR, as it determines who is responsible for compliance with the respective data protection regulations.  
According to Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR, the "controller" is the natural or legal person, authority, institution, or other body that alone or jointly with others determines the purposes and means of processing personal data.  
A key criterion is the decision-making component. The role of the controller thus primarily arises from the fact that a specific entity has decided to process personal data for its own purposes. The "purpose" describes an expected outcome, while the "means" define how the expected outcome is to be achieved (cf. Art. 29 Data Protection Working Party, Opinion 1/2010 on the terms "controller" and "processor," WP 169, still in relation to Art. 2 lit. d of the Data Protection Directive 95/46/EC).  
Crucial for the assignment of responsibility is therefore who decides on the essential aspects of the means of processing. For the attribution of the responsibility characteristic, it is not necessary for the controller to process data themselves, to be in possession of the processed data, or to have physical control. If they make the decision that data is to be processed, all persons and entities functionally attributable to them who carry out steps of data processing under their supervision or instruction (auxiliary organs) are included.  
In this context, the respondent states in its response dated April 28, 2021, that it is not the controller regarding the content.  
It must be countered that the respondent states in the same response that it is responsible for raising funds, controlling the servers, developing the software, and public relations to support all its projects – logically also for its website – and that it intervenes directly in cases where quick action is required, i.e., in cases of copyright infringements, child protection, blocking IP addresses, or when the safety of individuals is at risk, and also mediates in conflicts where the community (consisting of users and administrators) cannot find a solution on the platform itself. Administrators are elected by the community of users. They have – as explained under point 2 of the findings – normal (creating, correcting, supplementing, and deleting posts) and extended rights (right to delete and secure irrelevant posts, blocking users). The administrators and also the other contributors (remaining users) have no employment relationship or other contractual relationship with the respondent.  
Therefore, the respondent is to be qualified, especially in light of the administrators who also decide on the purposes and means, at least as a joint data protection controller under Art. 4 No. 7 GDPR.  
D.5. Applicability of the Media Privilege  
Regarding the assertion of the lack of jurisdiction of the Data Protection Authority in the respondent's response dated October 4, 2021, it should first be noted that if the conditions of § 9 para. 1 DSG are met, legal protection is only possible through the ordinary courts according to the Media Act, and there is no jurisdiction of the Data Protection Authority.  
The national regulation in § 9 DSG refers to Art. 85 GDPR, a fundamental provision with an opening clause (cf. Suda/Veigl in Gantschacher/Jelinek/Schmidl/Spanberger, Data Protection Act1 § 9 Rz. 1, still in relation to § 9 DSG as amended by Federal Law Gazette I No. 165/1999 as amended by Federal Law Gazette I No. 120/2017 [Data Protection Adjustment Act 2018]).  
Art. 85 GDPR regulates the processing and freedom of expression and information and stipulates in para. 1 that "the provisions of the DSG as well as the more specific chapters of the GDPR do not apply to the processing of personal data by media owners, publishers, media employees, and employees of a media company or media service within the meaning of the Media Act – MediaG, Federal Law Gazette No. 314/1981, for journalistic purposes of the media company or media service (...)."  
According to the consistent case law of the Data Protection Authority, two conditions must be cumulatively met for the application of the media privilege: First, there must be processing of personal data by media owners, publishers, media employees, and employees of a media company or media service within the meaning of the Media Act, and second, this processing must be for journalistic purposes of the media company or media service.
occur (see, among others, the decision of December 2, 2019, GZ: DSB-D124.352/0003-DSB/2019, as well as from December 18, 2019, GZ: DSB-D123.768/0004-DSB/2019). It can therefore be assumed that legal protection is only possible through the ordinary courts under the Media Act (MedienG) if both conditions of § 9 para. 1 DSG are met, and that there is no jurisdiction of the Data Protection Authority (see the decision of the DSB from August 13, 2018, GZ: DSB-D123.077/0003-DSB/2018). In all other cases, the Data Protection Authority is responsible for substantive treatment but must consider the right to freedom of expression under Art. 11 EU Charter of Fundamental Rights (EU-GRC) and Art. 10 European Convention on Human Rights (EMRK) in the context of the balancing process.

According to § 1 para. 1 no. 1 MedienG, a "medium" is understood to be any means of disseminating messages or presentations with intellectual content in words, writing, sound, or images to a larger audience through mass production or mass distribution. According to § 1 para. 1 no. 6 MedienG, a "media company" is a company that is responsible for the content design of the medium as well as its production and distribution (no. 1 leg. cit.) or its broadcasting or accessibility (no. 2 leg. cit.). According to § 1 para. 1 no. 11 MedienG, a "media employee" is an employee who contributes journalistically to the content design of a medium or messages of the media service in a media company or media service, provided that he or she exercises this journalistic activity continuously and not merely as an economically insignificant secondary occupation as an employee of the media company or media service or as a freelancer.

The Supreme Court (OGH) states in its consistent case law, among other things, in its decision 6 Ob 6/14x of December 15, 2014, that a media entrepreneur within the meaning of § 1 para. 1 no. 6 MedienG only becomes a media entrepreneur when he or she operates a company – with a minimum level of entrepreneurial structures – whose corporate purpose is the content design of the website, which is carried out by an editorial team and a number of employees or freelance media workers (see RIS-Justiz RS01298 and Noll in Berka/Höhne/Noll, Mediengesetz³ [2012] § 1 Rz 25, 48).

As stated in point 2 of the findings, the respondent does not have an editor-in-chief, an editorial board, an administrative office, or a similar institution. Likewise, the respondent does not review, control, or decide which contributions users publish on the website "www.wikipedia.org." This is done solely by the users of the website themselves. Only in the few cases of "Office Actions," where the "normal community editing process" cannot be applied due to the need for rapid action, does the respondent intervene. These are exceptional cases that concern child protection, copyright issues, or disputes that escalate to a level that the community of users cannot resolve on its own.

The latter is also the subject of the complaint. The issue could not be resolved within the "community," and for this reason, the complainant has submitted a complaint directly against the respondent. In advance, neither the content of the contribution was researched nor prepared by the respondent. This was all done by the complainant or other users.

From the above, it follows that the respondent does not carry out editorial activities. On the one hand, because the corresponding structures – as stated by the respondent itself in its statement of April 28, 2021 – do not exist (no editor-in-chief, editorial board, or similar), and on the other hand, also – apart from a few exceptional cases of Office Actions – the contributions are created, revised, and deleted by the users of the website themselves. Thus, fundamentally, neither the content is authored by the respondent nor is it controlled by them. The individual contributions of the websites are created, modified, or deleted by the users, as the respondent has detailed throughout the entire proceedings, who moreover do not have any contractual or employment relationship with the respondent. The community forms its own "ecosystem," into which the respondent fundamentally does not intervene – except in the aforementioned exceptional cases. Editorial processing or final approval of the individual published articles is fundamentally not carried out by the respondent or by persons attributable to them at any time.

Since the one who is understood as the media owner is the one who has the content and editorial ultimate responsibility for the content disseminated over the network (see OGH 4 Ob 226/05x of January 24, 2006), it must be stated that the respondent is not involved in the content design, which means that it lacks the essential qualification to be classified as a media owner. This qualification, which is not present in this case, is also required for liability under the Media Act. This also applies to the electronic domain (Litzka/Strebinger, MedienG5 § 1 Rz 17 mwN).

In summary, it follows from the investigation that the respondent only provides the financial, technical, and structural prerequisites for the users.
however, is not involved in the content design of the encyclopedia, which comprises over two million articles on various topics in the German language.  
Thus, the first requirement of § 9 para. 1 DSG ("the processing of personal data by media owners, publishers, media employees, and employees of a media company or media service within the meaning of the Media Act") is not met, and it can remain open whether the second requirement is present.  
As a result, § 9 para. 1 DSG does not apply. Consequently, there is jurisdiction for the data protection authority to handle the complaint (see also the decision of December 18, 2019, GZ: DSB-D123.768/0004-DSB/2019).  
D.6. Compliance with the Principles of Data Processing  
When processing personal data, all principles of data processing mentioned in Art. 5 GDPR must be adhered to. The complainant objects that the registered data does not correspond to factual accuracy or is not up to date (see Art. 5 para. 1 lit. d GDPR). The principle of accuracy obliges the controller to ensure that the data is, on the one hand, factually correct and, on the other hand, kept up to date as necessary. Regarding factual accuracy, only statements of fact can be applied, and no value judgments. The personal data provided by the complainant concerning his name, date of birth, marital status, artistic occupation (filmmaker, director, cameraman, and author), as well as his career path, are accurate and have not been disputed by him in the course of the investigation.  
Concerning the listed works, he stated in his submissions of January 13, 2021, and April 9, 2021, that the titles mentioned are incorrect and that the latest works have not been registered. It should be noted that the listed titles are accurate; however, the fictitious name of the main actor [Note by the editor: presumably referring to the main character] has been added. This is evidently intended to give the respective website user the option to click either on the film title or on the name of the fictitious film character. This redirects the user to two different entries: one for the film entry, which also lists the original film title (https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/J***(Film)), and the other for the entry about the fictitious film character (see https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/N***). Thus, no inaccuracy can be established in this context. This is part of the structural design of the website, aimed at providing the respective user with more information. As a result, there are also no data that can be classified as false, untrue, inaccurate, or misleading.  

Regarding the complainant's assertion about the incompleteness of his entry, it should be explained that this must be assessed in the overall context, taking into account all circumstances. The specific processing context must be considered. The legal obligation of the controller to complete the data exists only in those cases where the added information is actually relevant to the processing process to ensure the objective accuracy of the data.  
The purpose in question is to prepare general information about the complainant, which meets the internal relevance criteria, for the users of the respondent's website. However, this does not mean that all works in which the complainant has participated must be exhaustively listed; rather, a summary of the filmography is sufficient to adequately fulfill the processing purpose. Furthermore, there is no inaccurate portrayal of the complainant that would need to be corrected by adding further data (see Herbst in DS-GVO Kommentar Kühling Buchner 2017). Moreover, it is also noted in the entry itself that the filmography is only a selection (see point 4 of the findings).  
Thus, there has also been no violation of the processing purpose in the sense of Art. 5 para. 1 lit. d GDPR.  
D.7. Right to Deletion  
D.7.1. General  
In the proceedings, the complainant asserted that his right to deletion under Art. 17 GDPR has been violated.  
According to Art. 17 GDPR, a data subject has the right to request the immediate deletion of personal data concerning them from the controller, provided that one of the conditions mentioned in para. 1 is fulfilled.  
However, the right to deletion under Art. 17 para. 1 GDPR does not exist according to Art. 17 para. 3 lit. a GDPR to the extent that the processing is necessary for the exercise of the right to freedom of expression and information. The purpose of this exception is to prevent the freedom of expression and information, which forms an essential foundation of democracy, from being undermined by invoking data protection (see Koreng/Feldmann ZG 2012, 311). The exception is not limited solely to the field of journalism or "professional" media but also includes expressions of opinion in the non-professional sector, such as by (occasional) bloggers or on social media. There is no regulation that would grant absolute priority to freedom of expression and information over the fundamental right to data protection, and such a regulation would also not be compatible with the Charter of Fundamental Rights. The involved rights are therefore to be weighed against each other in their respective significance and according to the degree of their jeopardy.
to weigh. Such a weighing also provides for the opening clause of Art. 85 GDPR for national legislators. Therefore, in accordance with Art. 6 para. 1 lit. f GDPR, an assessment of the legitimate interests of the respondent (as an information service provider) and third parties (the public, which uses the information service) must be carried out, and these must be weighed against the interests and possible consequences for the complainant arising from the processing in question. 

In the weighing, the rights of the affected person must be taken into account, which are guaranteed by Art. 7 and 8 EU Charter of Fundamental Rights (EU-CFR) as well as Art. 8 European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR), namely the rights to respect for private and family life and to the protection of personal data, and on the side of the data controller or third parties, the fundamental right to freedom of expression and information according to Art. 11 para. 1 EU-CFR and Art. 10 para. 1 ECHR. 

The European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) and subsequently the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) have developed a number of relevant criteria for the purposes of weighing between the right to respect for private life and the right to freedom of expression in their case law (see, for example, the judgment of the ECtHR of February 7, 2012, 39954/08 [Axel Springer v. Germany] or the judgment of the CJEU of February 14, 2019, C-345/17 [Buivids]). Accordingly, the following criteria should be included in such a weighing: 

- The role of the affected person in public life and the public's interest in the information; 
- The factual accuracy of the published information; 
- The purpose and context of the publication; 
- The nature of the affected information, in particular its sensitivity to the private life of the affected person, and the likelihood or actual occurrence of material or immaterial harm due to the data processing; as well as 
- The time elapsed since the publication of the data. 

The weighting of these criteria must be carried out on a case-by-case basis and depends on the specific circumstances of the respective case. 

Therefore, a weighing of the legitimate interests of the respondent and third parties (the public, which consults the respondent's encyclopedia) on the one hand and the interests of the complainant on the other hand must be carried out. 

The interest of the respondent lies in operating an online encyclopedia that provides information services to the public. The interest of the public is to use this information service offered free of charge (see the decision of the Data Protection Authority of January 15, 2019, GZ: DSB-D123.527/0004-DSB/2018, which states that the right to freedom of expression and information enshrined in Art. 11 EU-CFR is established). 

In contrast, the interest of the complainant is that his data be deleted from the respondent's website. 

The Data Protection Authority notes in this context that the complainant is a person of public life who is known to a broad public. An indication of "publicity" is when information about a person's public role and activities can be found. In general, it can be said that a person of public life is a natural person about whom media reports are made due to their functions or obligations (see Art. 29 Data Protection Working Party, Guidelines for the Implementation of the Judgment of the Court of Justice of the European Union in Case C-131/12, WP 225, p. 16). The Federal Administrative Court has also stated that individuals who are reported on in the media due to their function/obligations are to be considered persons of public life (decision of May 20, 2021, GZ W214 2232956-1 with references). 

The complainant is a filmmaker, director, cameraman, and author (see point 1 of the findings). It is undisputed that he therefore meets the qualification for a person of public life. 

As already stated under D.6. of the legal assessment, these are also not data that can be classified as false, untrue, inaccurate, or misleading. 

All information published on the respondent's website about the complainant is available online on various other websites, including that of the complainant himself, for anyone to access. The Austrian Broadcasting Corporation also reports on the complainant (see point 7 of the findings). 

The intensity of the interference is to be classified as low due to the nature and disclosure of the information in question by the respondent about the complainant. 

D.7.2. Result 

In light of all these considerations, the Data Protection Authority concludes that the legitimate interests of the public according to Art. 11 EU-CFR in access to information outweigh the stated impairments of the legitimate interests of the complainant. 

The conditions of Art. 17 para. 3 lit. a GDPR are therefore fulfilled, which is why the respondent rightly did not comply with the complainant's request. 

Consequently, there is no violation of the right to deletion, which is why a decision in accordance with the law was to be made.
