I. Request  
1. The Budget and Finance Committee of the Assembly of the Republic submitted to the National Data Protection Commission (CNPD), for opinion, the Draft Law No. 109/XV/2nd (GOV) -- Approves the State Budget for 2024.  
2. The request made and this opinion fall within the attributions and competencies of the CNPD, as the national authority for the control of personal data processing, under the provisions of paragraph c) of paragraph 1 of Article 57 and paragraph 4 of Article 36 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016 (General Data Protection Regulation - GDPR), in conjunction with the provisions of Article 3, paragraph 2 of Article 4, and paragraph a) of paragraph 1 of Article 6, all of Law No. 58/2019, of August 8 (which aims to ensure the implementation, in the internal legal order, of the GDPR).  
3. The assessment by the CNPD is limited to the regime aspects related to the processing of personal data, that is, to the operations concerning information relating to identified or identifiable individuals - cf. paragraphs 1) and 2) of Article 4 of the GDPR - focusing on the provisions that foresee or imply the processing of personal data.  
II. Analysis  
4. Thus, considering the objective delimitation of this request, this opinion will particularly focus on the matters provided for in Articles 77, 79, 133, 134, 137, 166, 169, and 185, as they constitute provisions that directly embody and/or radiate matters of personal data.  
5. Primarily, this Commission cannot fail to emphasize that the matter of personal data shares the legal dimension of fundamental rights, thus assuming itself as part of that common core heritage of rights and freedoms on which the experience of a democratic society is based, with the political-legislative consequences that such dignity demands.  
6. Therefore, the protection of personal data is directly provided for in the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union1, and its processing must comply with the principles and foundational ideas widely recognized in European and national legislation that disseminate that identity, and that seek to confirm and promote the idea of human rights that is at its origin, as one of its manifestations.  
7. As a fundamental legal matter, the protection of personal data also enjoys the enshrinement of a set of specific principles, which emerge in dependence on the axiological core of that essential nucleus, in order to confirm their effectiveness.  
8. Thus, in this sense, the following principles emerge as conforming this matter2, in addition, of course, to those of lawfulness3, fairness4, and transparency5, and those of accuracy6, integrity and confidentiality7, as well as those of necessity, relevance, and adequacy to the specific purposes that justify the processing8 and, collectively, the minimization of the data to be processed, in at least two aspects: the quantity and type of data processed, and the extent of the processing to be carried out, focusing on the limitation of its retention.  
9. It is, therefore, in this light that all data processing must be considered, and likewise evaluated, which will also guide the observations that will be made in this document.  
10. Furthermore, it should be noted that the Draft Law under consideration is not supported by an impact study on the protection of personal data, which is, it should be recalled, mandatory under the combined provisions of Articles 18, paragraph 4, of Law 43/2004, 7 of Law 58/2019, and 35 of the GDPR.  
11. Such omission, it should be said, may compromise a more complete assessment regarding the probable risks arising from the processing of personal data to be carried out and, above all, may hinder the reasoned decision of the holders of political-legislative power on a matter in which understanding such risks is essential to conclude, on the legislative level, regarding the admissibility and conditions for the execution of new data processing.
