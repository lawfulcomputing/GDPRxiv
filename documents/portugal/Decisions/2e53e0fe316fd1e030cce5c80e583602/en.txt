DL 461/XXIV/2024, hereinafter referred to as Draft DL).  
2. The CNPD issues this Opinion within the scope of its powers and responsibilities as an Independent Administrative Authority with the authority to oversee the processing of personal data, conferred by paragraph c) of item 1 of article 57, paragraph b) of item 3 of article 58, and item 4 of article 36, all of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, of April 27, 2016, General Data Protection Regulation (hereinafter GDPR), in conjunction with the provisions of article 3, item 2 of article 4, and paragraph a) of item 1 of article 6, all of Law No. 58/2019, of August 8, which implements the GDPR in the internal legal order (hereinafter LERGPD).  
3. This opinion from the CNPD only concerns matters related to the legal protection of personal data and no longer to data concerning legal entities as such.  

II. Analysis  
i) Legal Protection of Personal Data  
4. The legal framework for the protection of personal data is essentially centered on the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic, through its article 35, the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union (hereinafter CDFUE), and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).  
5. The legal notion of personal data is established in article 4, 1) of the GDPR, considering that it refers to information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ("data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier, such as a name, identification number, location data, electronic identifiers, or one or more specific elements of the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person;  
6. In turn, according to article 8 of the CDFUE, the processing of personal data must be carried out with strict respect for the rights, freedoms, and guarantees of natural persons, in particular the right to the protection of personal data (principle of legality).  
7. Regarding the GDPR, it establishes in article 5, item 1 that personal data must be: i) Subject to lawful, fair, and transparent processing (lawfulness, fairness, and transparency); ii) Collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes, and not processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes (purpose limitation); iii) Adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purposes for which they are processed (data minimization); iv) Accurate and kept up to date, and all reasonable measures must be taken to ensure that inaccurate data is erased or rectified without delay (data accuracy); v) Kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects only for as long as necessary for the purposes for which the data are processed (storage limitation); vi) Processed in a manner that ensures their security, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures (integrity and confidentiality).  
8. Finally, article 5, item 2 of the GDPR establishes the requirement that the data controller must implement measures that allow them to demonstrate that the processing of personal data is carried out in accordance with the principles set out (accountability).  

ii) Legislative Purposes  
9. As previously mentioned, the Government decided to create a uniform and unique legal regime, aiming for objectives of simplification, efficiency, and equal treatment of mobility, seeking to clarify and simplify the respective regime.  
10. Furthermore, in order to prevent potential misuse by certain economic agents, a control mechanism is created to mitigate the possibility of charging fares above the prices practiced by air carriers.  
11. It was further added that the model meets the concerns raised by the European Commission regarding the functioning of the competitive market. Moreover, the model contained in this decree-law also ensures compliance with the defined principles.
