Under Deliberation/2024/137, dated March 25, 2024, the National Data Protection Commission (CNPD) applied an urgent corrective measure of limitation of processing, ordering the Worldcoin Foundation to refrain from collecting iris, eye, and facial data in the national territory for a maximum period of 90 (ninety) days.

31. Given that it is established that ZipCode GmbH is wholly owned by the Worldcoin Foundation, it is necessary to assess the concept of establishment.  
32. Now, the concept of establishment is not expressly defined in the GDPR; nevertheless, Recital 22, when addressing the term and from which a hermeneutic subsidy can be drawn, states that "Any processing of personal data carried out in the context of the activities of an establishment of a controller or a processor situated in the Union shall be done in accordance with this regulation, regardless of whether the processing itself is carried out in the Union. The establishment presupposes the effective and real exercise of an activity based on a stable facility. The legal form of such establishment, whether it is a branch or a subsidiary with legal personality, is not a determining factor in this context."  
33. That said, the understanding that has been addressed regarding the fulfillment of the concept of "establishment" has been filled as the exercise of a real and effective activity, even if minimal, through a stable facility, in the terms that will be better explained in the point below.  
34. As better established in the Judgment of October 1, 2015 — Case C-230/14 of the CJEU, referring to the conclusions of the Advocate General, it must be taken into account the existence of "a flexible conception of the concept of establishment, which rules out any formalistic approach according to which a company can only be considered established in the place where it is registered. Thus, to determine whether a company, responsible for data processing, has an establishment, in the sense of Directive 95/46, in a Member State different from the Member State or third country in which it is registered, both the degree of stability of the facility and the reality of the exercise of activities in that other Member State must be evaluated, taking into account the specific nature of the economic activities and services in question. This understanding is especially valid for companies that are dedicated to offering services exclusively on the Internet."  
35. It follows, arguing that "it must be considered, taking into account the objective pursued by this directive, which consists of ensuring effective and complete protection of the right to privacy and preventing legislation from being circumvented, that the presence of a single representative may, in certain circumstances, be sufficient to constitute a stable establishment if this representative acts with a sufficient degree of stability through the necessary means for the provision of the specific services in question in the Member State in question." and that, "to achieve the aforementioned objective, it must be considered that the concept of 'establishment', in the sense of Directive 95/46, encompasses any real and effective activity, even if minimal, carried out through a stable facility."
**stable.”**

**ii) On cross-border processing and the concept of establishment.**

37. Cross-border processing, under the GDPR, refers to the processing of personal data by a controller or a processor that occurs between different countries or regions. This may involve transfer, access, storage, or any other operation related to personal data that crosses geographical borders (cf. item 23 of Article 4 of the GDPR).

38. This article encompasses two types of processing operations, both revolving around the concept of “establishment”: “a) The processing of personal data that occurs in the context of the activities of establishments in more than one Member State of a controller or a processor in the Union, where the controller or the processor is established in more than one Member State; or b) The processing of personal data that occurs in the context of the activities of a single establishment of a controller or a processor, but which substantially affects, or is likely to substantially affect, data subjects in more than one Member State;”

39. As mentioned above, this concept is not expressly defined in the GDPR, and it has been understood that the concept of “establishment” should be filled as the exercise of a real and effective activity, even if minimal, through a stable installation.

40. This interpretation is particularly relevant for the purposes provided in sub-item b) of item 23 of Article 4 of the GDPR, as from its reading, it will follow that one is dealing with cross-border processing, with the effects provided therein, when the processing of personal data occurs in the context of the activities of a single establishment of a controller.

41. The processing of data must also be carried out in the context of activities of that establishment.

42. Here too, the CJEU considered that even if the European establishment in question does not play a role in the processing of data itself, that processing should be considered as carried out in the context of the activities of the establishment in question when there is an inseparable link between the activities of the establishment and the processing in question.

43. Finally, it is also required that the processing substantially affects, or is likely to substantially affect, data subjects in more than one Member State;

44. Now, it has been demonstrated that there is collection and processing of biometric data by Worldcoin in several States of the European Union, and since these processes are carried out in more than one Member State, this requirement will necessarily also be fulfilled, to the extent that the processing in question affects the data subjects similarly in more than one Member State where WorldCoin is operating.

**iii) On the Lead Supervisory Authority**

45. Having concluded that we are in the presence of cross-border processing, it is necessary to clarify the concept of lead supervisory authority.

46. The identification of a lead supervisory authority is only relevant when a controller or processor carries out cross-border processing of personal data, which is the case here. Simply put, a “lead supervisory authority” is the authority with the primary responsibility for dealing with a cross-border data processing activity and will coordinate any investigation involving the “interested” authorities.

47. Evaluating what is stated in item 16 of Article 4 of the GDPR, sub-item a) refers to a controller with establishments in several Member States, defining “Lead Establishment” as “the place where its central administration is located in the Union.”

**iv) On the Lead Establishment and the Sole Establishment of the Controller in the EU**

48. Given that we are dealing with a Controller that has only one establishment in the EU/EEA, it is necessary to understand, in conclusion, which regime to apply.

49. It is clarified in “Opinion 04/2024” of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) that this Opinion “only” addresses the concept of “lead establishment” in the sense of sub-item a) of item 16 of Article 4 of the GDPR, which presupposes the existence of several establishments (see in particular paragraph 37).

50. In other words, EDPB Opinion 4/2024 only focuses on the analysis regarding sub-item a) of item 16 of Article 4 of the GDPR, that is, it is argued that when in the presence of a Controller that has more than one establishment in the EU/EEA, it should be understood that when decisions regarding the purposes and means, as well as the power to implement those decisions are exercised outside the Union, there should not be a lead establishment under the terms of sub-item a) of item 16 of Article 4 of the GDPR, and the mechanism...
**de**  
The single window mechanism should not apply, but, the Opinion itself clarifies that this does not prejudice other cases in which the single window mechanism may be applied, as occurs when there is a single establishment of a data controller in the Union.  
51. This understanding has already been endorsed by the CJEU in the case Wirtschaftsakademi Schleswig-Holstein, as well as in those cases known as Google vs. Spain and Facebook, where the differences between having or not having an establishment within the Union were clarified, which was the situation with the group, at that time, Facebook (now Meta), which has its European headquarters in Ireland.  
52. Furthermore, the GDPR, in its Recital 124, states that “When the processing of personal data takes place in the context of the activities of an establishment of a data controller or a processor in the Union and the data controller or the processor is established in several Member States, or when the processing in the context of the activities of a single establishment of a data controller or a processor in the Union affects or is likely to affect substantially data subjects in various Member States, the supervisory authority of the main establishment or of the single establishment of the data controller or the processor shall act as the lead supervisory authority. (our emphasis)”, from which it is inferred that it is the GDPR itself that understands and clarifies that there is a lead supervisory authority whenever there is a main establishment (when there is more than one establishment in the Union) or when there is a single establishment of the data controller. As is the case at hand.  
53. Moreover, this seems to be the dominant position in doctrine, in the interpretation of paragraph 1 of Article 56 of the GDPR, when this provision states that: “(...) the supervisory authority of the main establishment or of the single establishment of the data controller or the processor is competent to act as the lead supervisory authority for cross-border processing carried out by the said data controller or processor in accordance with Article 60.”  
54. In conclusion, since we are not in the presence of a “main establishment” in the sense of subparagraph a) of paragraph 16 of Article 4 of the GDPR, but rather of a single establishment, and based on everything that has been said, it can only be concluded that, in these cases where there is a single establishment in the Union, the single window mechanism applies, and consequently, it is where the only establishment of the data controller in the Union is located that the lead supervisory authority is situated, even if data is collected and processed in more than one State of the Union.  
v) On the competence of BayLDA  
55. Given this reality, only invoked and made known to this Commission at a stage subsequent to that which determined and substantiated Deliberation/2024/137, and having concluded, from the analysis of the evidence collected in the meantime, that ZipCode GmbH is headquartered in Erlangen, Germany, and that it is the only establishment in the EU of the Worldcoin Foundation, and also that, in the absence of other establishments in the European Union, the single window mechanism is applicable, it is understood that from the conjunction of paragraph 1 of Article 51 and paragraph 1 of Article 56 of the GDPR, it results that the Bayerisches Landesamt für Datenschutz ("BayLDA") is the “lead supervisory authority.”  
56. Considering this conclusion, the assessment of the other claims is prejudiced. See Opinion 4/2024 of the EDPB, and the “Guidelines for identifying a controller or processor's lead supervisory authority,” available at https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-identifying-controller-or-processors-lead_pt

**III. DECISION**  
57. In light of the above, the CNPD resolves:  
a) To recognize that we are facing a competence incident and, consequently, to identify the Bayerisches Landesamt für Datenschutz ("BayLDA") as the “Lead Supervisory Authority” for the processing of personal and biometric data on behalf of the Worldcoin Foundation;  
b) To declare the CNPD as an Interested Supervisory Authority, in the sense of paragraph 22 of Article 4 of the GDPR;  
c) To decide, under the final part of paragraph 1 of Article 60 of the GDPR, to forward all relevant documents and information to the Lead Supervisory Authority.
