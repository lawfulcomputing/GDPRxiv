I. Request  
1. His Excellency the Secretary of State for National Defense requested the National Data Protection Commission (CNPD) to issue an Opinion on the compliance with the rules regarding the security of data processing in the request for authorization for the use of portable individual cameras by the Maritime Police.  
2. The CNPD issues this opinion within the scope of its powers and responsibilities, as an independent administrative authority with the authority to oversee the processing of personal data, conferred by paragraph c) of number 1 of article 57, paragraph b) of number 3 of article 58, and number 4 of article 36, all of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, of April 27, 2016 – General Data Protection Regulation (hereinafter GDPR), in conjunction with the provisions of article 3, number 2 of article 4, and paragraph a) of number 1 of article 6, all of Law No. 58/2019, of August 8, which implements the GDPR in the internal legal order.  

II. LEGAL FRAMEWORK  
3. This opinion specifically addresses the use of portable individual cameras (commonly known as bodycams) by the Maritime Police, following the acquisition of 112 "Coercive Kits," which include portable individual cameras, as provided for in articles 10 of Law No. 95/2021, of December 29, and article 3 of Decree-Law No. 2/2023, of January 2.  
4. The use of portable individual cameras by police forces currently finds specific support in the Portuguese legal framework, namely in Law No. 95/2021 and Decree-Law No. 2/2023, and the definitional list contained in article 3 of Law No. 59/2019, of August 8, is also applicable.  
5. In fact, although the use of such video surveillance systems may raise various problematic dimensions that are not always easily addressed regarding the protection of personal data, the foundational legal regime for their use and, consequently, the data processing operations to which they refer, would essentially be outlined by those diplomas. This Commission has, in previously issued opinions during the respective legislative projects, made a series of observations reflecting those dimensions, even fundamental rights - and in the contextual and material assessment of the legislative contents and intentions on this particular matter - to which reference is now made, which will constitute valuable hermeneutic subsidies for the interpretation of those diplomas from the perspective of personal data protection.  
6. Primarily, this Commission must emphasize that the matter of personal data shares the legal dimension of fundamental rights, thus being part of that common core of rights and freedoms upon which the existence of a democratic society is based, with the consequences that such dignity demands.  
7. Therefore, the protection of personal data is directly provided for in the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, and its processing must comply with the principles and foundational ideas widely recognized in European and national legislation that disseminate that identity, and which seek to confirm and promote the idea of human rights that underlies it, as one of its manifestations.  
8. As a fundamental legal matter, the protection of personal data also enjoys the enshrinement of a set of specific principles, which emerge in dependence on the axiological core of that essential nucleus, in the sense of confirming their effectiveness.  
9. Thus, in this regard, the principles that shape this matter emerge, in addition to those of legality, fairness, and transparency, and those of accuracy, integrity, and confidentiality, the principles of necessity, relevance, and adequacy to the specific purposes that justify the processing, and, together, the minimization of the data to be processed, in at least two aspects: the quantity and type of data processed, and the extent of the processing to be carried out, with an emphasis on limiting their retention.  
10. It is, therefore, in this greater light that all data processing must be considered, and likewise evaluated, which will also serve the observations that will be made below.  
11. For brevity's sake, any processing of personal data, given the nature described, may only occur when there is a lawful basis that authorizes it.

In other words, the data controller may only collect data for determined, explicit, and legitimate purposes, and once collected, may not use it for purposes that are not compatible with those.  
13. 15. **III. OF THE SPECIFIC USE OF INDIVIDUAL PORTABLE CAMERA SYSTEMS BY THE MARITIME POLICE.**  

34. The Maritime Police intends to implement a video surveillance system through the use of individual portable cameras, more specifically, with the equipment listed in Annex A and with the operational rules outlined in Annex B attached to the request for opinion.  

35. From the documents included in the request, it appears that the operational use rules for the bodycams (Annex B) generally correspond to the specific dictates of the legislator for their correct use, as provided in Decree-Law No. 2/2023, particularly regarding the mode of carrying, permission and/or obligation and prohibition of recording, the need for verbal announcement of use, and the duty of reporting and communication.  

36. As for the cameras acquired and intended for this use, it should be deemed that these must be pre-configured before being put into use by the operators, with no possibility for them to alter these configurations on the devices, in order to adapt them, at all times, to the legal regime that governs this use, as will be better explained below.  

37. Both Article 10, paragraph 7 of Law No. 95/2021 and Article 12 of Decree-Law No. 2/2023 emphasize that the police officer who executed the recording may not delete or alter it, and the integrity of the processed data must be permanently respected, with the collected data only being able to occur in strict compliance with the established rules that authorize them.  

38. It is also important to mention that the existence of audit logs is, in the case under analysis, essential, and that, in order for its integrity to be unquestionable, they must be digitally signed.  

39. From the information received, it is concluded that the cameras are of the Axon Body 3 model, from the manufacturer Axon Enterprise, Inc., a U.S. company based in the state of Arizona.  

40. Reviewing the technical characteristics of these devices, it is noted that they are capable of simultaneous recording of image and sound, without the need for handling by the operator, and have the following characteristics: recording in Full HD; battery that supports 14 hours of operation; 64GB of storage; AES-XTS 128-bit encryption; configurable pre-event buffer of up to 120 seconds.  

41. These cameras are also capable of interacting with other devices from the same manufacturer, namely Tasers, with the recording being able to be activated by the triggering of those devices.  

42. The review of the technical specifications of the camera on the manufacturer's website also leads to the conclusion that encryption on the cameras is active for the content of the disk where images and sound are recorded, a requirement that, it should be emphasized, must be met under any circumstances, not only in the use of image and sound collection but also in the process of transferring the captured and stored data, as provided in Articles 11, paragraph 5, and 16, paragraph 2, item k) of Decree-Law No. /2023 and Article 10, paragraph 7 of Law No. 95/2021.  

43. Before they can be used by the operators, the cameras must be registered.  

44. The registration may, depending on the characteristics of the devices, be done in two ways: through an Axon Device Manager (ADM) app installed on a smartphone, or through the View XL Standalone application installed on a Windows computer.  

45. In the case of registration being done through the ADM, the camera must be docked to a docking station (Axon Dock). These stations are connected to the network and must have access to the Axon Evidence server (described below). When docked to these docking stations, the cameras charge the battery, receive system updates, synchronize the internal clock, and download the...
films that are recorded on the internal disk for the Axon Evidence server, deleting them afterwards with confirmation that the transmission was successful.

46. To register the camera using the ADM, the application operator must have the necessary permissions configured on the Axon Evidence server.

48. After registration, the cameras must be assigned to a user, through configuration on the Axon Evidence server. This assignment consists only of associating the camera's registration with the user (through the name or ID of the police officer, for example) so that all films (video and audio) recorded identify the officer to whom the camera was assigned at the time of capturing the video and audio. The cameras can change operators simply by executing the reassign on the Axon Evidence server, and care should be taken to create procedures regarding this matter that allow for the secure registration of any changes made, and that allow for subjective and objective traceability of any operation concerning the use of the cameras.  
49. Furthermore, it is also clearly specified in Article 11 of the Decree-Law that “8 - The creation of different access profiles must be ensured to ensure that each police officer has strictly necessary access for the performance of their respective functions, and that “9 - The profiles to be created must be differentiated according to the functions performed, providing for each of these the respective scope and extent, differentiating permissions for viewing, processing, extraction, and conservation.”

50. Also regarding access to recordings, it is only permitted for duly accredited police officers for that purpose, through the system, at a fixed workstation located in a police facility, and, in support of what has been said, “4 - All operations of access to the recordings are recorded in the system, indicating the date and time, justification, and identification of the person responsible for the intervention.”. In this sense, “access” should be understood as any intervention performed in the system that has as its object, directly or indirectly, the aforementioned recordings, as well as the assignments of system manipulation profiles should be clearly defined to allow for a mechanism of checks and balances, and concentrated overlapping of operation/access/configuration/modification/deletion possibilities should not be permitted.  
51. However, from consulting the manual, it cannot be concluded whether an unassigned camera is disabled from recording. It may be understood that the camera can operate in recording mode without having been previously assigned to an officer, a situation that should be avoided, due to evident conflict with the principles referred to herein.  
52. The activation/deactivation of the various device settings is done on the Axon Evidence server and transmitted to the camera at the moment it is docked to a docking station. The configurations can also be sent to the cameras if they have Wi-Fi or LTE connectivity, which is not evident from the instructional information. As stated, once configured according to the legislative restrictions for their use, the cameras should not allow configurations different from those, and technical and security mechanisms should be created to prevent these risks, especially if they can be performed remotely and at any time, and potentially without records that allow for such control, as well as risks of intrusion.  
53. Among the settings that can be active/inactive, some stand out as having special relevance considering the permitted use of individual portable cameras in the context of the intervention of security forces.  
54. Without additional configuration, the cameras do not record sound from the pre-event period, that is, from the 30 seconds defined legislatively immediately prior to the moment of activation of the recording. As stated above, the recording buffer of the device model in question can be up to 120 seconds, and the cameras should be configured so that this is restricted to the 30 seconds provided for in number 2 of Article 7 of Decree-Law No. 2/2023.  
55. The cameras also have the capability to operate in stealth mode, where all light, sound, and vibration indicators are turned off. In the user manual of the Axon Body 3 camera, it is not possible to determine whether the stealth mode can be active during the period when the camera is recording.
**Consultation**

From the manual of older models, the description of stealth mode suggests that it may be active when the camera is recording.  
56. In any case, it should be noted that the legislative intent does not foresee or authorize such use, primarily due to the application of the principle of proportionality that governs it, which translates into the fundamental balance between the rights of data subjects and the public interest underlying the purposes that authorize this processing.  
57. In fact, it can be read in the explanatory memorandum of Decree-Law No. that “[...] the use of portable individual cameras must be subject to a comprehensive framework that delineates the situations in which the police officer may use this equipment, with respect for the rights, freedoms, and individual guarantees, preserving the personal dignity of those whose images are collected. For this purpose, it is essential that the activation of portable individual cameras is clearly perceptible to the different participants, which is why, under the terms of paragraph 4 of Article 10 of Law No. 95/2021, of December 29, the members of the security forces must proceed with a clear and perceptible warning of the start of recording. Through this Decree-Law, the manner in which portable cameras must be fixed to ensure their visibility to third parties is outlined, as well as the obligation for a warning or notice that must precede the start of recording.”  
58. As a rule, it must always be ensured that the recorded individuals are informed that recording is taking place, which should not be confused with their consent, which is what is excluded in paragraph 8 of Article 7 of Decree-Law No.  
59. Furthermore, the Legislator requires, as a rule, a verbal announcement that is clearly perceptible indicating that recording of images and sound is taking place, so, a fortiori, the cameras must also have a permanent indication that marks the moments when they are recording, in order to signal the operation, not only instantaneously but also throughout the entire duration of the recording, and in the different spaces and participants with which it may intersect, given the dynamic nature of a video and audio recording.  
60. Additionally, among the minimum requirements that the devices must meet, the characteristics of “c) Indicate the start and end of recording with a sound signal;” and “d) Activate a light signal when the recording mode is active;” are expressly stated – see Article 16, paragraph 2, subparagraphs c) and d) of Decree-Law No. 2/2023.  
61. In conclusion, it follows that stealth mode must also be definitively deactivated in all cameras before their use.  
62. The cameras will also allow the viewing of recorded films through the use of the Axon View app installed on a paired smartphone. Through the app, the operator can also include metadata (case ID, title, and category) in the recorded films, including the georeferenced location of the smartphone.  
63. The cameras may include, in the video recording, information regarding the location of the device, through the collection of data from the GNSS/GPS sensor.  
64. The need for geolocation is not mentioned as a minimum technical requirement for the cameras, being prescribed only in Article 11, paragraph 6, subparagraph f) of Decree-Law No. 2/2023, which states that the system must allow that the stored recordings include the unique and unalterable corresponding number and the date, time, and location of the recording, and must also allow for searching in the system through that field, which, in theory, will allow for the manual insertion of this information.  
65. Issues related to geolocation can involve complex assessments. In the present case, for example, it would be one thing if this functionality were only active at the time of justified recording, and another if it were active even when the camera is at rest, ultimately constituting a GPS device that tracks the entire path of the police officer while on duty, potentially allowing for control of their movements while in uniform or equipped, a situation that would require a very different judgment, at least in these terms.  
66. In fact, in Annex D attached to the request for an opinion, it is stated that “When the recording mode is activated, video and audio are recorded as well as the GPS coordinates, date, time, and user ID. This process will continue throughout the recording until the event is considered finished and the buffer mode is activated on the equipment.”  
67. From these statements, it seems to result that geolocation data is only recorded at the moment the recording is triggered. However, this Commission does not have sufficient information on this point to make a reasoned judgment on this relevant aspect, although it should always be stated that technical and security measures must be implemented that do not...
permit any use of this data that is different from those stated.

68. Furthermore, it is commendable that it has also been noted that regarding the collection of images of third parties not directly related to the police intervention event, the system will allow the placement of "masks" capable of concealing from the recording elements that are not essential for obtaining evidence for potential legally authorized sharing, namely with judicial authorities, while keeping the original unaltered in the system.

69. The cameras allow entering "mute" mode, in which the camera does not record audio when in recording mode. This function should also, by definition, be turned off, as the use of these systems presupposes the recording of both image and sound.

70. Regarding the use of paired smartphone devices, these should also not be permitted, and the function should be deactivated beforehand, as they could allow access to recorded videos and audios and other functionalities provided by the app, which are not compliant, in the first place, with the provisions set forth in Articles 11, 12, and 13 of Decree-Law No. 2/2023.

Article 11
Data Management and Storage System
1 - The data captured by the CPUI are managed, cataloged, and stored by a digital management and storage system, hereinafter referred to as the system.
2 - The components of processing, storage, and maintenance of the system are kept in a reserved location, with restricted access, equipped with a control mechanism and access logging.
3 - Without prejudice to other security measures, the system validates communication with the CPUI via the unique and unalterable identifier assigned by the manufacturer, transferring only the recordings from the CPUI that have been previously authorized by the highest commander of the force, and must be equipped with functionality that requires the association of the identification of the police officer to the transferred data.
4 - Access to and transfer of data captured by the CPUI are carried out exclusively through the system, in accordance with this article.

5 - In the process of transferring data captured by the CPUI, the system must ensure its immediate encryption, using a secure cipher algorithm, and also its security, integrity, and inviolability, ensuring that the integrity verification method is available, free of charge, at any stage of the evidence custody chain.
6 - In storing the recorded data, the system must comply with the following minimum technical requirements:
a) Not allow recordings to be deleted by users who are not duly accredited;
b) Delete the images recorded by the CPUI as soon as they are transferred to the storage system;

Security and Storage of Recordings

IV. Conclusion  
53. In the terms and with the grounds presented, the CNPD  
recommends:  
a) To determine the use of portable individual cameras for the purposes provided in  
Article 3 of Law No. 95/2021, as a concrete, necessary, and authorizing condition for the processing of the data in question, as better explained in point I. of this opinion;  
b) To ensure that the cameras are pre-configured  
before their use by the operators, complying with the rules provided in Decree-  
Law No. 2/2023, with no possibility of these configurations being altered on the devices or  
by the operators;  
c) To ensure that encryption on the cameras is  
active for the content of the disk where images and sound  
are recorded, not only during the collection of  
images and sound but also in the process of  
transferring the captured and stored data;  
d) To ensure the subjective and objective traceability  
of any operation regarding the use of the  
cameras, as well as in relation to any access to  
the data, understanding “access” as any  
intervention performed in the system that has, directly or indirectly,  
the aforementioned recordings as its object,  
as well as clearly defining the assignments  
of system manipulation profiles that allow for the exercise of a checks and  
balances mechanism, not allowing for the overlap.
