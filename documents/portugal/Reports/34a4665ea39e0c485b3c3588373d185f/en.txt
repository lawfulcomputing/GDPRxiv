1. MESSAGE FROM THE PRESIDENT

This Activity Report aims to comply with the Law on the Organization and Functioning of the National Data Protection Commission, approved by Law No. 43/2004, of August 18, as currently amended (Law on the Organization and Functioning of the CNPD), and is structured into 6 chapters: guiding and awareness-raising activities; procedural activities; extraprocedural activities; international activities; institutional cooperation activities; organization and functioning.

1. The National Data Protection Commission (CNPD) is the National Supervisory Authority for the purposes of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of April 27, 2016, also referred to as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Law No. 58/2019, of August 8, which implements the GDPR in the internal legal order (GDPR Implementation Law, or Law No. 58/2019), and the Law on the Organization and Functioning of the National Data Protection Commission, approved by Law No. 43/2004, of August 18, as currently amended (Law on the Organization and Functioning of the CNPD). In this capacity, it is responsible for controlling and supervising the application of the GDPR and compliance with these regulations, as well as other legal and regulatory provisions regarding the protection of personal data, in order to defend the rights, freedoms, and guarantees of individuals in the context of personal data processing (cf. paragraph 1 of article 51, and paragraph 1 of article 57/1 of the GDPR, article 3, and paragraph 2 of article 4, both of Law No. 58/2019, and paragraphs 2 and 3 of article 2 of the Law on the Organization and Functioning of the CNPD).

To pursue its mission, the CNPD is entrusted with a wide range of legal responsibilities, as provided for in articles 57 of the GDPR and 6 of Law No. 58/2019, and in various other standalone legislation, possessing advisory, authorization, regulatory, inspection, oversight, investigative, corrective, and sanctioning powers (cf. article 58 of the GDPR), also acting in the dissemination of information and issuance of Guidelines, and contributing to the strengthening of national and international cooperation, especially in cooperation with its European counterparts (mutual assistance and joint operations, cf. article 61 of the GDPR),

5/51

ensuring coherence through the procedure for the control of the coherent application of the GDPR in the European Union (cf. articles 64 and 65 of the GDPR) and the urgent procedure (cf. articles 66 and 67 of the GDPR).

The CNPD also has the responsibilities and legal competences provided for in the Law on Personal Data and Privacy in Electronic Communications, approved by Law No. 41/2004, of August 18, as currently amended (LPCE), in the communications sector, for the purposes of the provisions of EU law and national legislation, as established in the LOF of the CNPD.

The CNPD is an independent administrative entity, with legal personality under public law and authority powers, endowed with administrative and financial autonomy, functioning alongside the Assembly of the Republic (cf. paragraph 1 of article 51 of the GDPR, paragraph 1 of article 2 of the Law on the Organization and Functioning of the CNPD, and paragraph 1 of article 4 of Law No. 58/2019), and is not subject to governmental supervision or tutelage.

In pursuing its responsibilities and exercising the powers conferred upon it by law, the CNPD acts with total independence, is not subject to external influences, and refrains from taking any action incompatible with its functions (cf. paragraph 1 of article 52 of the GDPR, paragraph 4 of article 2 of the Law on the Organization and Functioning of the CNPD, and paragraph 3 of article 4 of Law No. 58/2019). It should be emphasized that the Members of the CNPD are not subject to external influences in the exercise of their functions, do not request or receive instructions from others, refrain from any action incompatible with their functions, and, during their term, may not engage in any activity, paid or unpaid, that is incompatible with them (cf. paragraphs 2 and 3 of article 52 of the GDPR, and article 5 of Law No. 58/2019).

2. GUIDING AND AWARENESS-RAISING ACTIVITY

For the year 2023, the CNPD planned to strengthen its awareness-raising action, selecting the development of guidelines and directives aimed at organizations, as they are responsible for the processing of personal data and subcontractors, which are, concurrently, the provision of information to data subjects.

The CNPD dedicated itself to the development of guidelines specifically focused on the processing of personal data that have had a greater impact on the rights of data subjects in Portugal. The criterion for selecting the topics to be addressed was based both on specific events for which the CNPD was called to intervene and on the recurrence of questions raised by citizens for the defense of their rights and by organizations.

It was in this context that the CNPD identified the need to issue guidelines for the area of security in the processing of personal data, thus resulting in Guideline/2023/1. It is publicly known that, in the year 2022, numerous cyberattacks occurred that had a direct impact on the personal data processed in information systems of public and private organizations, some of which were of significant size and complexity, affecting the core activities of the organizations.

In analyzing these violations, it was found that the main vectors of attack were the exploitation of vulnerabilities in infrastructures and the lack of training for users to detect phishing campaigns that subsequently allowed for the distribution of malware, with particular relevance to ransomware attacks. It was concluded, therefore, that the lack of awareness among those responsible for processing regarding the risks to the rights of data subjects that the lack of investment in security mechanisms entails is the primary cause of these attacks. In fact, it was observed that, in the vast majority of attacks witnessed, the consequences for the rights of data subjects could have been substantially reduced, if not entirely avoided.
13/51

The Guideline/2023/1 presents, therefore, a set of technical and organizational measures necessary to ensure that the processing of personal data has a level of security appropriate to the risk, including the ability to guarantee the confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of the processing systems and services, measures that, naturally, must be appropriate to the characteristics and sensitivity of each personal data processing and to the specificities of the particular organization.

Doubts regarding the exercise of the functions of Data Protection Officer (DPO) in public entities have also been identified as recurring, which, not having been regulated by the legislator, raise numerous questions for organizations and the DPOs themselves. First of all, the issue of the accumulation of functions with those of the person responsible for access to information (RAI) and also the matter related to the performance evaluation of the worker who is performing the function of DPO.

Regarding the first issue, the CNPD approved, on April 11, 2023, a Guidance in which it clarifies that since the functions of the DPO are of a consultative and supervisory nature regarding the processing of personal data carried out within the organization, they cannot be performed in accumulation if they imply decision-making on specific operations of personal data processing, under penalty of having to monitor oneself, in manifest conflict of interest. Now, because the functions of the RAI imply decision-making regarding access to administrative documents, which may correspond to documents containing personal data, they are subject to control and audit by the DPO. Thus, the CNPD concluded that the accumulation of DPO/RAI functions is incompatible.

Still in the context of the doubts presented to the CNPD, the availability of personal data within administrative procedures arises in situations where the applicant participated in the procedure and in situations where they are a third party in relation to the procedure.

14/51

Thus, on April 11, 2023, a resolution was approved that constitutes the guidance regarding the availability of personal data processed within administrative procedures, in which it clarifies the applicable legal regime and enumerates the data protection obligations that fall on the data controller.

On the same date, a resolution was approved in which the obligations that fall on the subcontractor regarding access to personal data were clarified, which was published as guidance on access to personal data held by a public entity in the capacity of a subcontractor.

A situation was also identified that raises questions of high complexity due to the lack of regulation of the status of workers performing that function: the performance evaluation of DPOs. Thus, the CNPD considered it appropriate to define guidelines on the performance evaluation of a worker who is a DPO, having adopted on April 18, 2023, a DRAFT GUIDELINE on the performance evaluation of the DPO. Although the guideline particularly aims at the performance evaluation of public administration workers, the underlying logic can still be extended to the evaluation of workers in private organizations.

To frame the problem, it is important to recall that the DPO cannot receive instructions regarding the exercise of their functions, nor can they be dismissed or penalized for that exercise and can only report to the highest leader of the organization in which they are integrated. The complexity of the issue increases when there is an accumulation of other labor functions with the functions of the data protection officer.

This project was placed in public consultation that ended on June 9, 2023, having received eight contributions, including that of an association representing DPOs.
15/51

Meanwhile, the revision of the Integrated System for Management and Performance Evaluation in Public Administration was announced, with the adoption of the final resolution awaiting legislative changes, expecting that the identified problems would be resolved, even if partially, through this means. Decree-Law No. 12/2024, of January 10, provided for the amendment of the evaluation regime, however, it was silent regarding the evaluation of the EPD.

The matter concerning the availability of personal data on the Internet is a recurring issue that generates a significant number of complaints from the affected data subjects.

For this reason, on April 18, 2023, two resolutions were approved:
- one consisting of the Guidance on the transmission of meetings of municipal bodies on the Internet, which explains the risks of exposure and improper reuse of images and statements made by citizens in this context and that, in the absence of a legal provision specifically foreseeing this data processing, nor a legal provision recognizing local authorities a specific function of media dissemination of their usual plenary activities, the prior and express consent of all persons covered by the filming and transmission appears as the only condition capable of legitimizing the aforementioned data processing;
- and another that gave rise to the Guidance on the publication on the Internet of the minutes of meetings of collegiate bodies, reconciling the requirements of administrative transparency and the protection of fundamental rights to respect for private life, non-discrimination, and the protection of personal data in light of the principle of proportionality. 3. PROCEDURAL ACTIVITY

Procedural activity continues to consume a large part of the CNPD's resources, with a significant weight on investigation processes. Here, the analysis of technological innovations regarding the processing of biometric data for the purpose of creating a World ID stands out.

Also, prior control processes deserve special mention, not for their quantity, but for the high complexity, both technical and legal, that they present. In fact, these processes are presented to the CNPD when the controller, after studying, within the scope of a comprehensive impact assessment of the risks to data protection that a processing activity presents and the adoption of measures that eliminate or at least mitigate those risks, concludes that the impact on the rights, freedoms, and guarantees of the data subjects is still high. In these cases, the intervention of the CNPD is required to analyze the situation and provide guidance on the measures to be adopted to mitigate the risks presented by the processing.

However, the year 2023 was particularly marked by the European Commission's evaluation of the national parts of the European information systems Schengen (SIS II) and Visas (VIS), for which in the previous year the CNPD had already conducted 19 inspection actions on the entities with competence in the area, which included the GNR and PSP, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Immigration and Borders Service, the Maritime Police, the SIRENE Office, and the Secretary-General of the Internal Security System.

The CNPD coordinated the entire evaluation visit to the various entities, having accompanied, at the suggestion of the European Commission, the team of evaluating experts regarding the data protection module. This evaluation focuses on verifying compliance with the SIS and VIS legal framework regarding data protection in Portugal.
21/51

The CNPD itself, as the national supervisory authority for these systems, was also subject to evaluation by the team of experts. Portugal is still awaiting the report of this evaluation, which is the responsibility of the European Commission.

All of this required the exclusive dedication, for considerable periods of time, of qualified workers from the CNPD.

The mechanism for ensuring consistency and cooperation among national data protection authorities of the Union for the handling of cross-border cases, as provided for in the GDPR, constitutes an aspect of the procedural activity of the CNPD that consumes significant resources. This is manifested both in the issuance of opinions when the CNPD acts as an interested supervisory authority, and in the investigation and decision-making on cases referred to us by counterpart authorities, in which the CNPD acts as the lead supervisory authority, within the context of the single-window mechanism.

It is also noteworthy that cross-border cooperation has another component, of a non-decisional nature, regarding requests for mutual assistance, informal consultations, and joint investigative operations, which entail a significant increase in workload. The uniform application of the GDPR necessitates this.

Opinions on Legislative Measure Proposals

In Opinion/2023/1, regarding the proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council establishing a common framework for market media services, referred to as the Media Freedom Regulation, the CNPD expressed its concerns regarding certain provisions. The fundamental rights involved in this project primarily pertained to freedom of expression and information, as well as to business freedom and the right to property, with privacy rights and data protection being only occasionally at stake. In this context, despite the existence of a provision prohibiting the installation of "spyware" on devices or machines used by media service providers, such installation is permitted "after assessment of the specific case, if justified by national security reasons or in the context of serious crimes."
From the CNPD's perspective, the admission of a legal provision, even if exceptional and case-specific, for the installation of spyware for discreet surveillance in the context of exercising press freedom and the right to information, with only a generic safeguard of proportionality, opens the door to legitimizing discreet surveillance of journalistic activity with a high risk of violation, not only of data protection and privacy but also of the essential content of fundamental rights to press freedom and freedom of expression, enshrined in Article 11 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights, as well as the guarantee of source confidentiality.

5. INTERNATIONAL ACTIVITY

The activity of the CNPD has a strong European and international component, which corresponds, in fact, to obligations imposed by different legal instruments of the European Union. Thus, the CNPD is part of various European bodies, the most important of which is the European Data Protection Committee (EDPC), a body of the Union, composed of all national data protection authorities from the European Economic Area, the European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS), and the European Commission (which has no voting rights), along with respective expert subgroups.

As mentioned above, the work currently being carried out within the Committee is particularly demanding, not only due to its guiding role for organizations regarding the interpretation and uniform application of the GDPR but also because of the mechanisms of cooperation and coherence, which require constant interactions between different national authorities, especially when data processing affects citizens located in more than one Member State. In 2023, the EDPC continued with intense activity, holding monthly meetings, with its work highlighted by the continuous issuance of guidelines, the preparation of various opinions under Article 64 of the GDPR, and the issuance of binding decisions within the framework of dispute resolution under Article 65 or the urgent procedure under Article 66 of the GDPR. This year, the CNPD actively participated in the work of the EDPC, not only in the monthly meetings of the EDPC but also in the various expert groups that the CNPD is part of and some task forces, such as the one related to the imposition of fines (Task Force Fining) and the new task force to address issues raised by generative artificial intelligence.

Also within the framework of the EDPC, but functioning autonomously, there is the Coordinated Supervision Committee (CSC), which is chaired by the representative of the CNPD, re-elected to the position in November 2022. The CNPD has been conducting intense work at this Committee, which includes all Data Protection Authorities of the Union and four States outside the EU but belonging to the Schengen Area (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, and Switzerland) as well as the European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS). The CSC currently ensures the coordinated supervision of three European information systems in the area of
42/51

police and judicial cooperation in criminal matters (Eurojust, Europol, and EPPO – European Public Prosecutor Office), the Schengen Information System (SIS), in the area of borders, asylum, and migration, and finally, the Internal Market Information System (IMI). The SIS has been subject to coordinated supervision by the CSC since March 7, 2023, with the implementation of the new system, which has enhanced functionalities and a new legal framework.

Coordinated supervision combines the national plan with the European plan, ensuring that the national components of European systems and the data transmissions from Member States to their respective central systems comply with the data protection rules specifically established in the respective legal instruments regulating the operation of these systems, in addition to the standards set forth in the general data protection regime provided, as applicable, in the GDPR or in Directive (EU) 2016/680 and the national laws that transpose them. Thus, both the EDPS for the central system and the national data protection authorities for their Member State, each within their competencies, ensure that European information systems are controlled in a global and coherent manner.

It is also anticipated that in the near future, despite the delays in the operational start-up that have been observed, two more large-scale European information systems will come under the coordinated supervision of the CSC. These are the Entry and Exit System (EES), in October 2024, and the European Travel Information and Authorization System (ETIAS), in the spring of 2025. In the coming years, it is also expected that the Visa Information System (VIS) and the EURODAC System for asylum seekers, as well as the Customs Information System (CIS), with the updating of their respective legal frameworks, as well as the new criminal records system for nationals of third countries (ECRIS-TCN) will transition to the jurisdiction of the CSC. At the same time, it ensured attendance at specialized training courses in the area of administrative procedure and process, as well as preparation for the new Digital Services Regulation, which contains matters that fall under the competence of the CNPD.

b) Budget

Regarding the CNPD's budget, in 2023, own revenues continue to have little weight, as decisions to impose fines of more significant amounts continue to generally be subject to judicial appeal, which naturally delays any potential revenue entry.

The initial budget was €2,976,534.00, with €107,344.00 from Own Revenues (OR) and the remainder from the Budget of the Assembly of the Republic (OAR).

As for the budget executed in 2023, total revenue reached €9,288,467.12, divided into €67,719.40 from OR, €2,490,000.00 from funds from the OAR, €65,711.89 from the balance of the OAR from 2021, and €6,665,035.83 relating to the balance from previous years.

The total expenditure was €8,940,900.34, net of reimbursements.
