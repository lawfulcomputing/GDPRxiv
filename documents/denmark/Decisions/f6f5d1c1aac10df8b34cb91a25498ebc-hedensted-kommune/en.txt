**Supervision of Notification of Breach of Personal Data Security**  
Date: 31-08-2023  
**Decision**  
Public Authorities  
No Criticism  
Supervision / Self-initiated Case  
Notification of Breach of Personal Data Security  
The Danish Data Protection Agency has conducted 16 planned inspections focusing on municipalities' and banks' handling of breaches of personal data security. The Agency found reason to express criticism in two cases.  
Journal Number: 2021-423-0241.  
Hedensted Municipality was among the public authorities that the Danish Data Protection Agency selected for supervision in the spring of 2021 under the General Data Protection Regulation [1] and the Data Protection Act [2].  
The Agency's supervision was a written inspection that focused, among other things, on whether Hedensted Municipality had implemented appropriate security measures in accordance with Article 32, paragraph 1 of the General Data Protection Regulation, aimed at reducing the number of breaches of personal data security involving unauthorized disclosure of personal data, including in relation to citizens with name and address protection.  
The supervision was announced in a letter dated June 21, 2021, to Hedensted Municipality, and the municipality was simultaneously requested to answer a series of questions and to submit an example of an instruction to the municipality's employees regarding the handling of personal data, including in connection with the transmission of information to, among others, citizens, authorities, etc.  
The Danish Data Protection Agency informed about the background for the supervision, noting that a review of the Agency's cases concerning notifications of breaches of personal data security revealed that Hedensted Municipality had reported significantly more breaches of personal data security per inhabitant than other municipalities in Denmark. The Agency noted in this context that the higher number of notifications does not necessarily indicate that the municipality complies with data protection regulations to a lesser extent than municipalities with significantly fewer notifications per inhabitant.  
In a letter dated August 9, 2021, Hedensted Municipality sent a statement in response to the Danish Data Protection Agency's questions. The municipality's response was also accompanied by examples of relevant procedures and guidelines.  
**Summary**  
The Danish Data Protection Agency initiated written inspections in the summer and autumn of 2021 regarding the handling of breaches of personal data security by eight larger municipalities and eight larger banks.  
The inspections were organized such that the municipalities and banks were divided into two groups – those that had reported the most and the fewest breaches in relation to the municipality's population and the number of employees in the bank.  
"One of the new things that came with GDPR over five years ago was that data controllers, as a general rule, must report all breaches of personal data security to the Danish Data Protection Agency within 72 hours, and they must also internally document all breaches of personal data security, regardless of whether they need to be reported to the Agency or not. Furthermore, there is now a heightened focus on what companies and authorities need to consider when customers' or citizens' information is mishandled by mistake," says Vibeke Dyssemark Thomsen, Chief Consultant at the Danish Data Protection Agency, and continues: "This can, for example, lead to the need to implement new or additional security measures to reduce the risk of further breaches. Therefore, we have now conducted a service review of the area, where we have looked at the procedures and taken some samples. The conclusion is broadly that the 16 selected municipalities and banks appear to have the right focus on complying with the rules."  

**Group with the Most Reported Breaches**  
For the group with the most reported breaches, the inspections focused, among other things, on whether the municipalities and banks had implemented appropriate security measures aimed at reducing the number of breaches of personal data security where unauthorized disclosure of personal data occurred in connection with the transmission of information to, among others, citizens, authorities, etc., including in relation to citizens with name and address protection and financial information.  
Compliance with the rules on appropriate processing security will normally entail that:  
The data controller must ensure that information about data subjects, including particularly confidential and sensitive personal data, does not become known to unauthorized persons. The data controller must, in this context, ensure that all employees in the organization are adequately familiar with any internal procedures for handling personal data, including in relation to the transmission of personal data to, among others, citizens, authorities, etc.  
Procedures, guidelines, workflows, technical security measures, etc., are continuously updated or introduced, including as a result of identified breaches of personal data security.  
The Danish Data Protection Agency found on the basis of the available information that all data controllers had implemented appropriate security measures. In its assessment, the Agency emphasized that:  
Procedures, etc., had been developed and activities had been carried out to educate employees in data protection, including to ensure that they are aware that there should be no accidental disclosure of personal data to an incorrect recipient.

**The Group with the Fewest Reported Breaches**  
For the group with the fewest reported breaches,  
the inspections focused on whether the municipalities  
and banks report and document breaches of  
personal data security in accordance with the  
requirements of the General Data Protection Regulation,  
including their processes for handling and recording  
breaches of personal data security.  

The Data Protection Authority stated in these cases  
that it is the Authority's view that compliance with  
the rules regarding the requirement that breaches of  
personal data security must, in principle, be reported  
to the Data Protection Authority within 72 hours,  
implies that:  
1. The data controller must establish appropriate  
procedures and guidelines to ensure that breaches  
are detected and handled correctly.  
2. In this regard, the data controller must ensure  
that all employees in the organization are familiar  
with internal procedures for handling breaches of  
personal data security to the necessary extent, and  
that relevant employees can identify, manage, and  
assess breaches of personal data security.  

The Data Protection Authority found that all data  
controllers, overall, have adopted appropriate  
procedures and guidelines, etc., and have carried  
out appropriate training activities that can support  
compliance with the requirements of the  
General Data Protection Regulation regarding the  
reporting of breaches of personal data security.  
Thus, they can ensure that breaches of personal data  
security are captured within the organization,  
so that these can be assessed to determine whether  
the breach should be reported to the Data Protection  
Authority, as well as to document the breaches,  
including, among other things, the reasons for not  
reporting them to the Data Protection Authority.  

However, the Data Protection Authority noted in  
relation to all inspections that the Authority did  
not have the opportunity, as part of the inspection,  
to concretely assess whether all relevant employees  
have completed the relevant training activities,  
and that the Authority is not familiar with the  
full content of the training material, including  
the content of, for example, ongoing awareness  
initiatives.  

Furthermore, the Data Protection Authority stated  
that the rule requiring the data controller to  
document all breaches of personal data security,  
including the actual circumstances of the breach,  
its effects, and the remedial measures taken,  
must ensure that the Data Protection Authority can  
verify that the provision regarding the reporting  
of breaches of personal data security is complied  
with. Therefore, data controllers should document  
their reasons for all significant decisions made  
as a result of the breach. This applies not least  
if the data controller, after assessing the breach,  
concludes that it does not need to be reported to  
the Data Protection Authority. Documentation  
should, in relation to this decision, include a  
detailed explanation of why the data controller  
believes that the breach is unlikely to result in  
a risk to the rights and freedoms of individuals.  

**Criticism in Two Cases**  
As mentioned, there has been pronounced criticism  
in two of the cases – specifically regarding  
two municipalities that did not document all  
breaches of personal data security.  
In one case, the Data Protection Authority expressed  
serious criticism of Roskilde Municipality for  
not documenting how many breaches of personal data  
security the municipality has identified from  
May 25, 2018, to September 2019, and that the  
municipality cannot document whether the recorded  
security incidents since September 2019 constitute  
actual breaches of personal data security.  
In the other case, the Data Protection Authority  
criticized Frederikshavn Municipality for not  
documenting how many breaches of personal data  
security the municipality identified in 2018.  

The Data Protection Authority notes that the list  
of breaches – in addition to serving as documentation  
to the Data Protection Authority – should also be  
used by the data controller to gain an overview of  
which breaches typically occur in the organization,  
and based on that, to consider whether, from a  
risk assessment perspective, there is a need to  
implement new or supplementary measures to avoid  
or further reduce the risk of additional breaches.  
It is also evident from the received consultation  
responses that several data controllers actively  
use the lists for this purpose.  

**1. Decision**  
After reviewing the submitted material, the  
Data Protection Authority finds on the basis  
presented that Hedensted Municipality has taken  
appropriate security measures in accordance with  
Article 32(1) of the General Data Protection  
Regulation, with a view to reducing the number  
of breaches of personal data security, where  
there is unauthorized disclosure of personal data,  
including in relation to citizens with name and  
address protection.  

Below follows a detailed review of the  
information that has emerged in connection with  
the inspection, and a justification for the  
Data Protection Authority's decision.  

**2. Justification for the Data Protection Authority's Decision**  
It appears from Article 32(1) of the General Data  
Protection Regulation that the data controller must  
implement appropriate technical and organizational  
measures to ensure a level of security that is  
appropriate to the risks associated with the  
data controller's processing of personal data.
The data controller is thus obliged to identify the risks that the data controller's processing poses to the data subjects and to ensure that appropriate security measures are implemented to protect the data subjects from these risks. It is the opinion of the Data Protection Authority that the requirement under Article 32 for appropriate security will normally imply that the data controller must ensure that information about data subjects, including particularly confidential and sensitive personal data, does not become known to unauthorized persons, and that the data controller, in this regard, must ensure that all employees in the organization are, to the necessary extent, familiar with any internal procedures for handling personal data, including in relation to the transmission of personal data to, among others, citizens, authorities, etc., and that procedures, guidelines, workflows, technical security measures, etc. are continuously updated or introduced, including as a result of identified breaches of personal data security.

Hedensted Municipality has, in its statement of August 9, 2021, submitted an example of an instruction to the municipality's employees regarding the transmission of personal data to, among others, citizens, authorities, etc. Hedensted Municipality has stated that considerations regarding the protection of name and address information are a natural part of the processing of personal data throughout the municipality. It is noted that it is implicitly understood in the concept of good data processing practices and behavior to ensure extra protection when name and address protection is desired. Should there be an accidental disclosure of information about individuals with name and address protection, it will be handled promptly and will always – if possible – trigger a telephone contact with the affected individuals. The municipality will also seek to identify the cause with a view to learning and thus avoid similar incidents. The municipality is therefore particularly aware that such disclosure may lead to a potentially dangerous situation for the affected individuals.

Furthermore, the municipality has stated that measures are continuously being implemented to avoid repetitions of breaches of personal data security, where accidental disclosures occur. Hedensted Municipality has indicated that specific incidents are discussed with the individual employee in order to determine further measures that can prevent recurrences. In the event of repetitions in a department, the municipality's data protection advisor is involved in a dialogue with the department to avoid future incidents.

It further appears from the case that Hedensted Municipality has reflected on past breaches of personal data security, where there has been accidental disclosure of personal data. Based on these reflections, the municipality has continuously implemented organizational measures, such as establishing a group that screens access requests before transmission. Additionally, the municipality has implemented technical measures, including the purchase of a scanning tool aimed at properly redacting documents for personal data. Hedensted Municipality has also stated that the municipality continuously implements technical and organizational measures aimed at reducing the number of breaches of personal data security, where accidental disclosures of personal data occur.

It is – notwithstanding that the Data Protection Authority has not had the opportunity to take a specific position on whether the municipality has been in dialogue with all relevant employees and departments, and that the authority is not aware of the full content of all training materials, etc. – based on the information available, the authority's assessment that Hedensted Municipality has taken appropriate security measures in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation's Article 32, paragraph 1, aimed at reducing the number of breaches of personal data security, where unauthorized disclosure of personal data occurs, including in relation to citizens with name and address protection.

In making this assessment, the Data Protection Authority has emphasized the information provided by the municipality, including that procedures have been developed for the transmission of personal data to external parties, that the municipality has reflected on and implemented both technical and organizational measures following past breaches of personal data security to avoid similar breaches, and that there is a strong focus on avoiding accidental disclosures of name and address protected information.

The Data Protection Authority can, upon a renewed review of the authority's cases regarding reports of breaches of personal data security, ascertain that since June 22, 2021, there has been a decrease in the number of reported breaches of personal data security from Hedensted Municipality. However, as there are still a number of breaches of personal data security being reported, where unauthorized disclosure of personal data has occurred, the Data Protection Authority recommends that the municipality continues to focus on implementing training and awareness activities, etc., as well as ensuring that procedures, guidelines, workflows, technical security measures, etc. are continuously updated or introduced, including as a result of identified breaches of personal data security.

Finally, the Data Protection Authority notes that the authority – typically if it receives new reports of breaches of personal data security – may resume the processing of previously reported breaches or include them in the assessment of any future breach or complaint cases.
