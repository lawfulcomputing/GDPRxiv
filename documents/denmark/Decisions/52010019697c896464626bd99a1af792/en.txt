The Danish Data Protection Agency has reported Odsherred Municipality to the police, as the agency assesses that the municipality has not complied with the requirements for an appropriate level of security under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Odsherred Municipality has been recommended for a fine of 100,000 DKK - 200,000 DKK for failing to establish sufficient and appropriate security measures.

The Data Protection Agency became aware of the case when Odsherred Municipality reported a breach of personal data security on September 1, 2022. It was revealed that a work computer containing sensitive personal data, information about CPR numbers, and information about children had been stolen during a burglary at an employee's home. The computer's hard drive was not encrypted.

During the investigation, the Data Protection Agency found that Odsherred Municipality had failed to ensure the encryption of approximately 1,200 laptops from at least May 18, 2018, until September 10, 2022, which the agency considers a serious violation of the regulation's requirements for processing security.

**Requirement for Appropriate Security**  

Data controllers are obliged to ensure that the information being processed does not come to the knowledge of unauthorized persons. It is relatively simple to access files stored on a laptop when the hard drive is not encrypted—e.g., by moving the hard drive to another computer. Furthermore, the agency assesses that stolen mobile devices are increasingly being examined for personal data such as credit card information and CPR numbers before being sold on. This is partly due to the growing underground market where such types of information can be resold, and partly due to the increased digitization of society in general, which expands the opportunities for the exploitation of personal data.

Encryption of personal data is a commonly recognized security measure and is specifically mentioned as an example of a technical measure in the General Data Protection Regulation. It is the Data Protection Agency's view that encrypting portable devices (including laptops) that process personal data must be considered not only relevant but also often both a necessary and required security measure. 

"As citizens, we cannot opt out of the municipality processing our information. Therefore, it is particularly important that we can be assured that it is well protected. Unfortunately, this has not been the case in this instance, where a basic measure such as encryption has been lacking—not only on the stolen computer but on all of the municipality's computers. We take this very seriously," says Ditte Yde Amsnæs, head of department at the Data Protection Agency.

The Data Protection Agency has previously recommended fines in similar cases regarding the lack of encryption of portable devices. Here you can read about Favrskov Municipality, Gladsaxe Municipality, Hørsholm Municipality, and the Civil Affairs Agency.

**Recommendation for Fine**  

The Data Protection Agency always conducts a specific assessment of the severity of the case under Article 83, paragraph 2 of the General Data Protection Regulation when determining which sanction is deemed appropriate by the agency. 

In assessing that a fine should be imposed, the Data Protection Agency has emphasized that Odsherred Municipality had not encrypted the hard drives of the municipality's 1,200 laptops prior to September 10, 2022. Furthermore, the agency has emphasized that ensuring encryption of portable devices, including computers, is a significant and basic security measure. Additionally, encryption must be regarded as a commonly used technology that has been best practice since 2007. The agency has further considered it significant that encryption has been a widespread, recognized, and general technical measure for many years, which should easily be addressed by the data controller.
