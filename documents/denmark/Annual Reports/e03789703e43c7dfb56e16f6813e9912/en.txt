5 2. Report  
2.1 Presentation of the Organization  
The Data Protection Authority is the central governmental authority that supervises any processing covered by the General Data Protection Regulation and the Data Protection Act as well as the Act on the Processing of Personal Data by Law Enforcement Authorities (the Enforcement Act), except for processing carried out for the courts. The Data Protection Authority exercises its functions in full independence but has a budgetary connection to the Ministry of Justice. The annual report is submitted for § 11.11.61. Data Protection Authority (operating grant). The Data Protection Authority consists of a council, the Data Council, and a secretariat. The Minister of Justice appoints the Data Council, which consists of a chairperson and seven other members. The Data Council primarily makes decisions in specific cases of a principled nature. The secretariat, which employed 78 staff as of December 31, 2023, manages the daily operations of the Data Protection Authority under the leadership of a director.  
The vision of the Data Protection Authority is to ensure responsible use of citizens' data in a digitalized society. At the same time, the mission of the Data Protection Authority is to be an open authority that works responsively, differentiated, and visibly, and that solves its tasks through practical guidance and information, accessible decisions, and targeted supervision, just as the Data Protection Authority influences national legislation and European cooperation. This is ensured, among other things, through the many telephone and written inquiries about the rules that the Data Protection Authority handles every day, as well as the ongoing meetings with, among others, interest and industry organizations, but also individual data controllers and processors, as needed. Furthermore, this is ensured through the Data Protection Authority's website, where the authority continuously publishes relevant practices and guidance in relation to the General Data Protection Regulation, etc. The Data Protection Authority also continuously participates with contributions, etc., on the rules and the authority's practices at conferences, seminars, and similar events. Furthermore, supervision of public authorities and private companies, etc., as well as the handling of complaints. The processing of complaints is also considered part of the Data Protection Authority's supervisory activities, as the authority here takes a specific position and makes decisions regarding data controllers' processing of information about the registered individuals. Furthermore, the Data Protection Authority processes applications for permission for certain processing of personal data, and the authority provides opinions on legislative proposals and drafts of regulations, etc., that are significant for the protection of privacy. The Data Protection Authority also participates in international work, including within the EU. In addition, the Data Protection Authority carries out tasks under certain special laws and supervises the Danish authorities' processing of information under, among other things, the Schengen cooperation. The Data Protection Authority also supervises the version of the Data Protection Act that was enacted by royal decree for Greenland on December 1, 2016. The Data Protection Authority submits an annual report on its activities to the Parliament. The annual reports can be found on the authority's website (www.datatilsynet.dk).

The Data Protection Authority has established the website www.whistleblower.dk, where one can read more about the National Whistleblower Scheme.

2.2 Management Report  
The Data Protection Authority's field of work is broad and varied, ranging from providing guidance and advice to handling complaints (which is also part of the Data Protection Authority's supervisory activities) and applications for permission to process personal data, as well as the Data Protection Authority also conducting various other types of supervisory activities with authorities and companies each year. The Data Protection Authority also actively participates in, among other things, the joint European cooperation on data protection. Particularly regarding the authority's guidance efforts, it targets very diverse actors: the Parliament, citizens, private organizations and companies, as well as state, regional, and municipal authorities. In this context, the Data Protection Authority works purposefully to ensure that everyone knows and complies with the rules for processing personal data, and that citizens are aware of and can exercise their rights. The year 2023 has generally been characterized by a high level of activity with many important guidance initiatives, larger case complexes, and tasks of an international nature. The Data Protection Authority has handled several larger and quite principled cases regarding the processing of personal data by both public authorities and private companies. Furthermore, the Data Protection Authority has actively participated in, among other things, the European Data Protection Board's handling of several larger cross-border cases in connection with the so-called One Stop Shop mechanism. The Board has, among other things, had to make final binding decisions in a number of larger cross-border cases due to the fact that the affected European supervisory authorities could not agree on the lead supervisory authority's draft decision in each of the cases. International Data Protection Day on January 28, 2023, was marked by the Data Protection Authority with the launch of a nationwide campaign aimed at citizens with a message that we all have secrets, and that data protection rules ensure that others treat our information as secrets. The campaign consisted of printed advertisements and a film about secrets. The campaign was a supplement to the extensive guidance that the authority has provided to companies and authorities since 2018 on how they can best comply with the rules. The citizen-directed campaign, which had a lighter and more personal tone, aimed to explain what data protection rules can do for citizens. In 2023, the Data Protection Authority published 22 national guidelines, website texts, etc. Among other things, the authority published a new guideline on direct marketing and three guidelines in the area of TV surveillance, aimed respectively at private companies, public authorities, and housing associations.
7. The Data Protection Agency has furthermore published a catalog of a wide range of technical and organizational measures that can be read independently of each other and which are intended to contribute to making it easier to find solutions for managing risks. Many of the measures contain concrete examples based on the Data Protection Agency's experiences from supervisory activities, reported breaches of personal data security, the European Data Protection Board's guidelines, and applicable ISO standards. In addition, the agency launched a new guidance universe on GDPR for small businesses in May 2023. The guidance universe has been developed with a focus on clarity and concrete examples, and it is written in an easily understandable manner. The Data Protection Agency also published a guidance document in October 2023 regarding public authorities' use of artificial intelligence (AI). The guidance includes a review of the requirements that authorities must pay particular attention to before they begin developing AI solutions. At the same time, the Data Protection Agency published a mapping of public authorities' use of AI, which provides the clearest insight to date into public authorities' use of AI. Among other things, the mapping shows that the use of AI is not yet widespread among public authorities. To the extent that AI solutions are used in the public sector, they often involve standard solutions or the same specially developed solution used by several authorities. In 2023, the agency also conducted and launched a project for faster guidance on relevant measures immediately upon reporting breaches of personal data security through the implementation of a new case processing procedure, where targeted guidance is sent to the reporter along with an acknowledgment of the report in connection with the assessment of a reported breach in 11 specifically listed areas. The goal is thus to provide quick and applicable assistance to the organizations facing the breaches. Furthermore, the Data Protection Agency in 2023 contributed to the publication of five common European guidelines, including the European Data Protection Board's guidelines for the issuance of fines. The agency has also played a very active role in connection with the statement on the EU Commission's draft adequacy decision regarding the USA, and the information released by the European Data Protection Board following the publication of the final decision in July 2023. At the Nordic meeting in Helsinki in the autumn of 2022, the Nordic data protection authorities decided, at the initiative of the Danish Data Protection Agency, to establish a working group to address children and online gaming. The Data Protection Agency has actively participated in the working group in 2023, together with the other Nordic supervisory authorities. In 2023, the Data Protection Agency has also continued to follow up on the strategy for a data- and risk-based approach that the agency developed in 2020 with the aim of ensuring that the agency conducts oversight where the risk of violations is greatest. In 2023, work has included improving data quality and data sources, conducting trials with more data-driven targeting for the risk-based supervisory effort, and continuing trials with a new supervisory model and with impact measurement. Additionally, an evaluation of the strategy, which expired in 2023, has been conducted.

8. The Data Protection Agency has in 2023 also developed a new updated strategy for a data- and risk-based approach, which includes 12 new initiatives that will further strengthen the agency's ability to target oversight to the areas where there is the greatest risk to citizens' data. The updated strategy continues to focus on strengthening the agency's data quality, data sources, data usage, and documentation. At the same time, the strategy includes a new strategic challenge regarding progress in supervisory cases. The Data Protection Agency's supervisory activities may lead the agency to assess that criminal law steps should be taken in a case. In this context, it is essential that not only the agency's employees but also employees at the police and prosecution service have a good understanding of the many factors that are important to be aware of from the beginning of such a case until its resolution in the courts. Therefore, in 2023, the Data Protection Agency has continued its collaboration with the National Police and the Director of Public Prosecutions, which was initiated in 2019, aimed at organizing the overall handling of criminal cases regarding violations of data protection rules across authorities. The previous government published back in December 2021 a new national cyber and information security strategy, which contains a wide range of initiatives. One of these initiatives has been the establishment of a data warehouse under the auspices of the Data Protection Agency, which makes knowledge from the many reports of breaches of personal data security available to the public. The Data Protection Agency spent a considerable amount of resources in 2022 to realize this initiative, and the data warehouse was launched in March 2023. The Data Protection Agency has also participated in the follow-up on the strategy in 2023. Finally, in 2023, the Data Protection Agency has continuously focused on the negotiation and adoption of new regulatory requirements in the digital area, particularly the AI regulation. At the same time, the Data Protection Agency has contributed to the national work that the EU's legislative initiatives have prompted.

After the General Data Protection Regulation began to apply from May 25, 2018, there is, as a general rule, an obligation for all data controllers to report breaches of personal data security to the Data Protection Authority. The report must be made without undue delay and, if possible, no later than 72 hours after the data controller becomes aware of the breach. Therefore, in 2018, the Data Protection Authority established a new case group concerning reports of breaches of personal data security. In 2023, 9,537 cases regarding reports of breaches of personal data security were established, compared to 8,816 cases in 2022, which is an increase of 8.2%. The Data Protection Authority expected that the number of cases concerning reports of breaches of personal data security would stabilize during 2021-2023, which is still not the case. Thus, in 2023, the authority received the highest number of reports to date. Before the regulation came into effect, it was estimated that the Data Protection Authority would receive about 4,000 reports of breaches of personal data security annually. It can now be stated that since 2020, the authority has received more than double the number of reports than expected before the General Data Protection Regulation began to apply. From 2022 to 2023, the number of international cases increased by 4.2% from 935 cases in 2022 to 974 cases in 2023. The case group "international cases" has increased since the General Data Protection Regulation began to apply from May 25, 2018, due in part to the regulation's rules regarding increased and more formalized cooperation between the European data protection authorities, aimed at harmonizing the interpretation of the rules in the EU, and due to a strengthened and increased focus on international cooperation from the Data Protection Authority's side. The Data Protection Authority actively participates in the European Data Protection Board and in all the expert working groups, etc., established under the board, as well as being active in other international forums, including the so-called Berlin Working Group. The number of cases concerning competence under other legislation has increased from 656 cases in 2022 to 708 cases in 2023, which is an increase of 7.9% on top of the very large increase of 44% that occurred in the case group from 2021-2022. In this case group, many cases particularly concern the law on law enforcement, access to archives, TV surveillance, and mass media information databases. This task also includes specific reports, written inquiries, and requests for rights, administration, telephone guidance, and collaboration with other actors.

The case group "complaints" had in 2023 an average processing time of only 85 days compared to 99 days in 2022, which means a decrease in the average processing time of 15%. This comes in addition to the fact that there was also a decrease in the average processing time for complaints in 2021-2022. In 2023, 50% of complaint cases were processed within 36 days compared to 34 days in 2022, and 80% of complaint cases were processed within only 88 days in 2022 compared to 118 days in 2022, thus indicating an overall improvement in the processing time for complaints. It is extremely satisfactory that it has been possible for several years in a row to reduce the average processing time for complaints, which can be a complex case type with many procedural steps. Additionally, the Data Protection Authority receives a large number of complaints each year, and the number of newly registered complaints in 2023 was at the same level as in 2022. The average processing time for "inquiries" was 38 days in 2023, a slight deterioration from 2022 with an average of 31 days, which means an increase in the average processing time of 24% compared to 2022 but at the same level as in 2021. 50% of inquiries were processed in 2023 within 30 days compared to 21 days in 2022, but also at the same level as in 2021. 80% of inquiries were processed in 2023 within 45 days compared to 51 days in 2022, which indicates an improvement in the processing time. 

There is an increase in the average processing time for cases concerning "opinions on legislative proposals, etc." from 10 days in 2022 to 15 days in 2023, which is a significant increase of 49%, but approximately the same level as in 2021 and an increase of only 5 days. In 2023, 50% of cases were processed within 3 days compared to only 1 day in 2022, which is a significant increase, but in terms of the number of days, it is approximately the same level, and 80% of cases were processed in 2023 within 21 days compared to 16 days in 2022.

However, the processing time must be viewed in light of the fact that the Data Protection Authority receives a large number of consultations on drafts of legislative proposals and regulations, etc., where there are no circumstances that may be significant for the protection of privacy in connection with the processing of personal data, and where the authority therefore should not have been consulted at all. Additionally, the number of consultations on legislative proposals has increased by one-third compared to last year, according to Table 3A. In 2023, the processing time for "permits, etc." was on average only 37 days compared to 78 days in 2022, which means a significant decrease in the average processing time of 53%, which comes in addition to a larger decrease in the average processing time from 2021-2022. In 2023, 50% of cases were processed within 26 days compared to 29 days in 2022, and 80% of cases were processed within 54 days in 2023 compared to 56 days in 2022. Overall, this indicates a significant improvement. 

Overall, the development in processing times at the Data Protection Authority can be described as very satisfactory.
