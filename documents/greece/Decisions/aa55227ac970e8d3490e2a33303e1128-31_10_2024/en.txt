The Hellenic Data Protection Authority convened, at the invitation of its President, in an extraordinary session at its headquarters on 06-06-2023, in order to examine the case referred to in the background of this document. Present via teleconference were the President of the Authority Konstantinos Menoudakos, the regular members Spyridon Vlachopoulos, as rapporteur, Grigorios Tsolias, Konstantinos Lamprinoudakis, Christos Kalloniatis, and Aikaterini Iliadou, and the alternate member Nikolaos Libos (in place of the regular member Charalampos Anthopoulos, who, although duly summoned, did not attend due to a conflict). In the session, without voting rights, were present, at the request of the President, Eleni Kapralou, legal expert, as assistant rapporteur, and Irini Papageorgopoulou, employee of the Administrative Affairs Department, as secretary.

The Authority took into account the following:

With the complaint No. G/IN/9156/27-07-2022 submitted to the Authority and the attached documents, A (hereinafter referred to as "the complainant"), who served in the National Intelligence Service (hereinafter "NIS" or "the complained party"), complains about the latter for the leakage of his personal data. According to the allegations, prior to the publication of Law 4873/2021 (Government Gazette A΄/248/16-12-2021), but also before the issuance of decision No. … by the Minister of Citizen Protection, regarding the transfer of the complainant, based on Article 74 of this Law, from his permanent organic position in the NIS to a personal position in the … branch of the Hellenic Police (…), the NIS transmitted via the Directorate of Administrative Support and Human Resources to the Hellenic Police (hereinafter ELAS) the document No. …, which included lists with the details of the personnel being transferred from the NIS, including the complainant. Specifically, regarding the complainant, these lists contained his full name, branch/category, as well as the subject of his studies, information which he claims was unlawfully leaked to ELAS. Furthermore, the complainant asserts that due to the non-publication of Law 4873/2021 in the Government Gazette (hereinafter "GG"), in which it was published on 16-12-2021, this Law had not come into effect at the time of the transmission of his personal data to ELAS, and the fact that this data was transmitted one day before its publication in the GG, namely on 15-12-2021, renders this transmission unlawful. Additionally, he claims that his personal data was unlawfully processed by employees of ELAS, who were not authorized for this processing during the period from 15-12-2021 to 07-01-2022, when the decision of the Chief of ELAS (…) regarding the placement of the complainant in … was issued. To support his above claims, he argues that at the time of the transmission, there existed the right of referral by the President of the Republic (Article 42 of the Constitution), claiming that the Law could ultimately not be published in the Government Gazette if the President of the Republic referred the said bill back to Parliament. Finally, he asserts that the processing in question was unlawful also because there was no prior opinion from the Authority regarding this transmission. CONSIDERED ACCORDING TO THE LAW

1. Whereas, according to the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter, GDPR) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), the Authority has the competence to supervise the implementation of the provisions of the GDPR, this Law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Specifically, from the provisions of Articles 57 paragraph 1 point (f) of the GDPR and 13 paragraph 1 point (g) of Law 4624/2019, it follows that the Authority has the competence to address the aforementioned complaint against the National Intelligence Service (EYP), as it concerns the sharing involving the transmission of data included in a filing system as defined in Article 4 points 2 and 6 of the GDPR, thus for processing subject to the regulatory scope of Articles 2 paragraph 1 of the GDPR and 2 of Law 4624/2019.

2. Whereas, specifically, according to Article 4 point 1 of the GDPR, "personal data means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, psychological, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person."

3. Whereas, furthermore, according to Article 4 point 2 of the GDPR, the processing of personal data is "any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure or destruction."

4. Whereas, in Article 4 point 7 of the GDPR, the data controller is defined as "the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data; where the purposes and means of such processing are determined by Union law or the law of a Member State, the controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be provided for by Union law or the law of a Member State."

5. Whereas, in Article 5 paragraph 1 of the GDPR, the principles that must govern processing are set out, and specifically: "1. Personal data shall be: a) processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject ('lawfulness, fairness, and transparency') (...)".

6. Whereas, furthermore, according to the principle of accountability introduced by the second paragraph of the same article, it is expressly stated that the data controller "shall be responsible for, and be able to demonstrate compliance with, paragraph 1 ('accountability')." This principle, which constitutes a cornerstone of the GDPR, entails the obligation of the data controller to be able to demonstrate compliance and for this purpose to verify and legally document a processing operation carried out in accordance with the legal bases provided by the GDPR and national data protection law.

7. Whereas, specifically, in order for a processing operation to be considered lawful (and in accordance with Article 5 paragraph 1 of the GDPR), one of the legal grounds for processing provided in Article 6 paragraph 1 (points a-f) of the GDPR must apply, namely "1. Processing shall be lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the following applies: a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes, b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract, c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the controller is subject, d) processing is necessary in order to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person, e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller, f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject."

necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or a third party, unless the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require the protection of personal data prevail, particularly if the data subject is a child. The provision (e) of the first paragraph does not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the exercise of their duties.

8. Since for personal data to be lawfully processed, that is, processed in accordance with the requirements of the GDPR, the conditions for the application and adherence to the principles of Article 5(1) of the GDPR must be cumulatively met, as also indicated by the ruling of the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) on 16-01-2019 in case C496/2017 Deutsche Post AG v. Hauptzollamt Köln. The existence of a legal basis (Article 6 GDPR) does not exempt the data controller from the obligation to adhere to the principles (Article 5(1) GDPR) regarding the legitimacy, necessity, and proportionality as well as the principle of data minimization. In the event that any of the principles set out in Article 5(1) GDPR are violated, the processing in question is deemed unlawful (contrary to the provisions of the GDPR) and the examination of the conditions for the application of the legal bases of Article 6 GDPR becomes unnecessary. Thus, the unlawful collection and processing of personal data in violation of the principles of Article 5 GDPR cannot be remedied by the existence of a legitimate purpose and legal basis. Furthermore, the CJEU in its ruling of 01-10-2015 in the context of case C-201/14 (Smaranda Bara) ruled that informing the data subject prior to the processing of their data is a prerequisite for lawful and legitimate processing of personal data.

Regarding the compatibility of the aforementioned provisions of Articles 24 and 26 of Law 4624/2019 with the provisions of the GDPR, which E.Y.P. invokes, the Authority, in its Opinion No. 1/2020, concluded that the GDPR does not authorize the national legislator to establish new "national legal bases," but only to specify the legal bases of Article 6 paragraph 1 letters c and e of the GDPR,
15

under the terms and guarantees provided by the provisions of paragraphs 2 and 4 of Article 6 GDPR, and that from the provision of Article 6 paragraph 4 GDPR regarding further processing for other purposes, albeit different, compatible with the purposes of the initial collection, it follows that the national legislator is not obliged to take implementation measures for the further processing since the GDPR itself establishes the criteria under a) to e) of the said paragraph 4. With this opinion, it was specifically accepted that, even if it is considered that the national legislator was authorized by the GDPR to establish "national legal bases" for the further processing of data under Article 6 paragraph 4 GDPR, these must only constitute a specification of the legal bases of Article 6 paragraph 1 subparagraphs c and e GDPR and must respect, among other things, the principles of legality of Article 5 paragraph 1 GDPR. With these considerations, the Authority opined that the provisions of the aforementioned Articles 24 and 26 of Law No. 4624/2019 do not meet any of the aforementioned substantive and procedural conditions and guarantees arising from the GDPR. In the same opinion, the Authority reserves the right to examine any specific issue related to the compatibility of the provisions of Law No. 4624/2019 with the GDPR that may arise in the context of exercising its powers and emphasizes that it will not apply provisions of Law No. 4624/2019 that are deemed to be in conflict with the GDPR or do not find a basis in "opening clauses - specifications."

Furthermore, according to Article 35 paragraph 1, subparagraph a of the Constitution, "No act of the President of the Republic is valid or executed without the countersignature of the competent Minister, who, by his mere signature, becomes responsible, and without its publication in the Government Gazette," while according to Article 42 paragraph 1, subparagraph a of the Constitution, "The President of the Republic issues and publishes the laws that have been voted by the Parliament within one month from their voting." From these provisions, the basic principle emerges, which is also based on other constitutional provisions, that for the completion of formal laws and presidential decrees, as well as other regulatory administrative acts, their publication in the Government Gazette is required as a constituent element of their validity. With its publication in the Government Gazette, the regulatory provision acquires legal status and can be applied, thus becoming accessible to citizens and creating a presumption of knowledge of it. 

Moreover, an "act" of the President of the Republic, according to the meaning of Article 35 paragraph 1 subparagraph a of the Constitution, is any written act that he performs in his capacity as a state organ and constitutes an exercise of his authority according to the Constitution and the Laws. Furthermore, a prerequisite—besides the countersignature of the competent minister or, as the case may be, competent ministers—for the acts of the President of the Republic to be valid, according to Article 35 paragraph 1 subparagraph a of the Constitution, is their publication in the Government Gazette. The publication constitutes an external formal element, the absence of which renders the act void, and the requirement for publication is reiterated, specifically concerning formal laws, by Article 42 paragraph 1 of the Constitution.

In this manner, the contentious processing was carried out in violation of the principles of legality, objectivity, and transparency (Article 5, paragraph 1, point a of the GDPR), resulting in the complainant's personal data being subjected to unlawful and unfair processing that occurred in a non-transparent manner concerning the data subject. 

Furthermore, regardless of the reservations mentioned in paragraph 13 by the Authority regarding the validity of the provision invoked by EYP, Article 26, paragraph 1 of Law 4624/2019, which concerns the transmission of personal data from one public authority to another when necessary for the performance of the duties of the transmitting authority or the third party to whom the data is transmitted, this provision does not apply in this case, for the reason that the contentious transmission of the complainant's personal data to the Hellenic Police (ELAS) did not constitute the execution of EYP's duties, given that this obligation had not been assigned to it by the legislator, at least at the time of the contentious transmission, in relation to the transfer of the complainant. Therefore, it is unnecessary to examine whether the conditions of Article 24, paragraph 1, point b (as provided in point a of paragraph 1 of Article 26 of Law 4624/2019 concerning processing carried out by public authorities) are met, as well as to examine the claim made by EYP in its response document number … that "a transmission of personal data took place from our Service to a public authority, the Ministry of Citizen Protection, under the terms of Article 26 of Law 4624/2019, namely within the framework of executing our service's duties, as well as the stipulated conditions of Article 24 of the aforementioned law, in view of the impending transfer of the complainant and the need to verify their details by the Hellenic Police, before the administrative act of their transfer is completed."

In light of the above, it is unnecessary to examine the complainant's claim that there was no relevant request to EYP for the said transmission from the authorities mentioned in Article 74 of Law 4873/2021 – among which EYP is not included – and that EYP acted voluntarily, arbitrarily deciding both on the transmission and on the personal data that were transmitted, without a relevant request from the Minister of Citizen Protection, given that the contentious provision at the time of the examined processing was invalid, according to the extensively analyzed points above (see paragraph 16) and, therefore, the contentious processing is inherently contrary to the principles of legality, objectivity, and transparency (Article 5, paragraph 1, point a of the GDPR). 

Furthermore, given that the contentious processing is contrary to Article 5, paragraph 1, point a of the GDPR, and that, according to what is stated in paragraph 8, the fulfillment of the conditions for the application and adherence to the principles of Article 5, paragraph 1 of the GDPR is cumulatively required for personal data to be lawfully processed, it is unnecessary to examine the fulfillment of the other principles of lawful processing of data based on Article 5, as well as the examination of the conditions for the application of the legal bases of Article 6 of the GDPR, and the related claims made by the involved parties, regardless of the fact that EYP, as the data controller, does not invoke any of the legally specified bases in Article 6 of the GDPR and is unable to prove the legality of the said processing. 

17. As mentioned above, the data subject was not informed about the transmission of their data, and the relevant claim by EYP that "the contentious provision had received significant publicity even before its enactment. Consequently, the complainant was already aware of their impending transfer to a service of the Hellenic Police (...)" is not substantial, as notification through the publicity invoked by EYP regarding this issue does not meet the requirements of Article 13 of the GDPR, according to which for the notification...
19. An action by the data controller is required, directed at the data subject, which did not occur in this case.  
18. Because, following the above, it is established that the National Intelligence Service (EYP) as the data controller carried out the processing of the complainant's personal data in violation of a) the principles of lawfulness, fairness, and transparency of Article 5(1)(a) of the GDPR, as it based its actions on a non-existent law, and b) Article 13 of the GDPR because it did not inform the complainant about the specific processing as the data subject.  
19. Because the violation of the fundamental principles for processing, as detailed above, incurs the imposition of administrative sanctions under Article 83(5)(a) of the GDPR, while the violation of the right of data subjects provided for in Article 13 of the GDPR incurs the imposition of the relevant sanctions under Article 83(5)(b) of the GDPR. According to the GDPR (Recital 148), in order to strengthen the enforcement of the rules of this Regulation, sanctions, including administrative fines, must be imposed for each violation of this Regulation, in addition to or instead of the appropriate measures imposed by the supervisory authority in accordance with this Regulation.  
20. Because the Authority, based on the above, considers that the imposition of a corrective measure is not sufficient to restore compliance with the provisions of the GDPR that have been violated and that, based on the circumstances established, an additional effective, proportionate, and deterrent administrative monetary fine must be imposed under Article 83 of the GDPR, both to restore compliance and to sanction the unlawful behavior.  
21. Because furthermore, the Authority took into account the criteria for assessing the fine set out in Article 83(2) of the GDPR, paragraph 5 of the same article applicable in this case, Article 39(1) and (2) of Law 4624/2019 concerning the imposition of administrative sanctions on public sector entities, and the Guidelines 4/2022 of the European Data Protection Board for calculating administrative fines for the purposes of Regulation 2016/679, which were approved on 24/5/2023, as well as the factual data of the case under examination and in particular:  
i) The fact that the entity being complained about, in its capacity as data controller, violated the principle of lawfulness, fairness, and transparency provided for in Article 5(1)(a), that is, it violated a fundamental principle of the GDPR for the protection of personal data.  
ii) The fact that adherence to the principles provided for in Article 5(1)(a) of the GDPR is of paramount importance, primarily the principle of lawfulness, so that if this is lacking, the processing becomes unlawful from the outset, even if the other processing principles have been adhered to, especially in this case where no documentation was provided regarding whether any of the legal bases defined in Article 6 of the GDPR apply. On the contrary, the EYP justified the processing in question merely by citing the acceleration of the procedure for service-related reasons.  
iii) The fact that the entity being complained about, in the absence of a legislative basis for the processing in question, did not fulfill its obligation to prove the legality of this processing, while at the same time failing to demonstrate that it fulfilled its obligation to inform as provided for in Article 13 of the GDPR, as detailed above.  
iv) The fact that the aforementioned established violation is not proven to be attributable to the intent of the complained entity, but rather to its negligence, due to the alleged failure of the National Printing Office, while the acceleration of the transmission by the EYP was done for service-related reasons.  
v) The fact that the data controller did not delay in responding to the Authority's documents.  
vi) The fact that from the evidence presented to the Authority, based on which it established the aforementioned violations of the GDPR, it does not appear that the data controller caused material damage to the affected individual.  

22. Based on the above, the Authority unanimously decides that an administrative sanction, as mentioned in the operative part, must be imposed on the complained entity as the data controller, which is deemed proportionate to the severity of the violation.  
FOR THESE REASONS  
The Authority,  
Imposes on the complained National Intelligence Service (EYP) as the data controller the effective, proportionate, and deterrent administrative monetary fine appropriate to this specific case, according to its particular circumstances, totaling five thousand (5,000) euros, specifically four thousand (4,000) euros for the established violation of Article 5(1)(a) and one thousand (1,000) euros for the established violation of Article 13 of the GDPR, as detailed above, in accordance with Articles 58(2)(h) and 83(5)(a) and (b) of the GDPR.
