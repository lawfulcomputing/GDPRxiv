THOUGHT ACCORDING TO THE LAW  
1)  
Since the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation 2016/679, hereinafter GDPR) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137) indicate that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, Law 4624/2019, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Specifically, from the provisions of Articles 57 paragraph 1 point (f) of the GDPR and 13 paragraph 1 point (z) of Law 4624/2019, it follows that the Authority has the competence to address the complaint of A against the Municipality of Metamorfosi, as it arises from the aforementioned claims from both sides that the respondent proceeded to extract the name and address of the complainant from a filing system according to Article 4 point 6) GDPR, and communicated this by transmitting it to the complained party, thus involving non-automated processing of personal data, as defined in Article 4 point 2) GDPR, falling under the regulatory scope of Articles 2 paragraph 1 of the GDPR and 2 of Law 4624/2019.  
2)  
Since, with Article 5 paragraph 1 of the GDPR, the principles that must govern a processing are established. Specifically, paragraph 1 of the aforementioned article provides, among other things, that personal data shall be processed lawfully and fairly in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject (“lawfulness, fairness, and transparency”, point (a)), collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes (…) (“purpose limitation”, point (b)), are adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the purposes for which they are processed (“data minimization”, point (c)). Furthermore, according to Article 5 paragraph 2 GDPR: “The controller shall be responsible for, and be able to demonstrate compliance with, paragraph 1 (“accountability”).”), therefore the controller is obliged to demonstrate their compliance with the processing principles of Article 5 paragraph 1 GDPR at all times.  
3)  
Since, with Decision 73/2010, the Authority ruled that the citizen against whom a complaint has been submitted before a public authority has, as the data subject concerning them, the right of access, not only to the text of the complaint but also to any information related to the origin (source) of that data, such as the name and address of the person who submitted the complaint, since the concept of “origin” may also include third parties who provided the relevant information. In this regard, as the Authority ruled in the aforementioned decision, and given that the complainant is also a data subject whose personal data is contained in the complaint (the identity details of the natural person, author of the complaint, and their contact details), each public service, already at the stage of data collection, has the obligation to inform them, among other things, about the recipients of that data. Consequently, it is advisable, already at the time of submitting a complaint to a public service, for the latter to inform the complainant that both the text of their complaint and their name are data that may be disclosed to the complained party, especially if the latter requests them. Therefore, each complainant should initially justify the reasons that warrant the non-disclosure of their details to the complained party. The public service, correspondingly, may initially examine the legality of the reasons presented by the complainant and, additionally, in case it discloses the information, it will not be obliged to inform the complainant again about this disclosure (since it will have already informed them at the time of submitting the complaint about the disclosure of that information to the complained party).

4) Because, in the case under examination, from the entirety of the evidence in the case file and the submitted memoranda, it does not emerge that a right of access to the data of the application-complaint with reference number … was exercised by the complainant before the Municipality of Metamorfosi. Consequently, the notification of the name and address of the complainant to the owner of the property for which the complainant submitted the application-complaint with reference number … before the Municipality of Metamorfosi, took place ex officio by the Municipality in question.  
5) Because, consequently, it must be examined whether the aforementioned ex officio action of the Municipality, namely the notification of the name and address of the complainant to the owner of the property for which the complainant submitted the application-complaint with reference number …, took place in accordance with the processing principles enshrined in Article 5 paragraph 1 of the GDPR. Regarding the adherence to the principle of transparency of Article 5 paragraph 1 point a) of the GDPR, the Municipality in question claimed before the Authority that during the period of submission of the application-complaint with reference number … by the complainant, it was providing, through its organs and services, upon the instruction of the Data Protection Officer, oral information to citizens regarding the potential notification of complaints to the complained parties. However, this claim was not sufficiently substantiated before the Authority, in accordance with Article 5 paragraph 2 of the GDPR, in the sense that no relevant opinion, directive, or other document was submitted that substantiates the aforementioned claim and consequently, in this respect, it was not proven by the Municipality in question, in accordance with its obligation arising from the aforementioned principle of accountability, adherence to the principle of transparency, by providing appropriate information, in the sense of Article 5 paragraph 1 point a) of the GDPR and in accordance with Decision 73/2010 of the Authority.  
6) Because, in any case, even if it were to be considered that oral information was provided to the complainant, it must be examined whether the contested notification of the name and address to the owner of the property for which the complainant submitted the application-complaint with reference number … meets the other processing principles, as they are enshrined in Article 5 paragraph 1 of the GDPR, since, in order for personal data to be lawfully processed, the conditions for the application and adherence to these processing principles must be cumulatively met.  
7) Because from the evidence in the file, and what emerged from the hearing process and the submitted memoranda, and taking into account the nature of the reported violation (urban planning), the purpose for which the complainant's data was communicated to the owner of the property does not emerge, as it is not connected to the purpose of the document with reference number …, namely the warning to the owner of the property regarding the deadline for remedying the urban planning violation, the control of which indeed falls within the ex officio exercised competences of the Municipality. According to the principle of proportionality (Constitution, Article 25 paragraph 1 point c), processing is permitted if it is capable of achieving the intended purpose (principle of suitability/appropriateness), but only if it is absolutely necessary for that purpose, meaning that the least burdensome action for the subject is taken, and any further processing is unnecessary, even if it is beneficial (principle of necessity). Every processing of personal data that is not suitable and necessary for achieving the intended purpose or is carried out beyond that is not lawful.  
8) Because following the above, the Authority finds that in this case the notification of the complainant's data to the owner of the property constitutes unnecessary processing, contrary to the principles of appropriateness, necessity, and minimization of processing set forth in the aforementioned provisions of the GDPR, and constitutes a violation of Article 5 paragraph 1 point c) of the GDPR. Given that in order for personal data to be lawfully processed, that is, processed in accordance with the requirements of the GDPR, the conditions must be cumulatively met.
9  
Cumulatively, the conditions for the application and adherence to the principles of Article 5(1) of the GDPR9 must be met, and if a lack of compliance with the principles of transparency and data minimization as provided in Article 5(1)(a) and (c) of the GDPR is established, the processing is deemed unlawful, and the examination of the fulfillment of the other principles of lawful data processing under Article 5(1) of the GDPR becomes unnecessary.

In light of this, the Authority deems it appropriate to exercise its corrective powers in accordance with Article 58(2) of the GDPR and Article 39 of Law 4624/2019, as well as Recital 148 of the GDPR, which states: "In order to enhance the enforcement of the rules of this Regulation, penalties, including administrative fines, should be imposed for each infringement of this Regulation, in addition to or instead of appropriate measures imposed by the supervisory authority in accordance with this Regulation. In the case of a minor infringement or if the fine that may be imposed would constitute a disproportionate burden on a natural person, a reprimand may be imposed instead of a fine. However, due consideration should be given to the nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, the intentionality of the infringement, the actions taken to mitigate the damage, the degree of responsibility or any other relevant previous infringements, the manner in which the supervisory authority became aware of the infringement, compliance with measures against the data controller or processor, adherence to a code of conduct, and any other aggravating or mitigating factors. (…)” In assessing the data, the Authority takes into account the following elements for the case at hand10:
