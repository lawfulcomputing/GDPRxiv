CONSIDERED ACCORDING TO THE LAW

1. From the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679 – hereinafter, GDPR) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), it follows that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Specifically, from the provisions of Articles 57(1)(f) of the GDPR and 13(1)(g) of Law 4624/2019, it follows that the Authority has the competence to address the complaint of A against "METRO S.A." and to exercise, correspondingly, the powers conferred upon it by the provisions of Articles 58 of the GDPR and 15 of Law 4624/2019. 

2. With Article 5(1) of the General Regulation (EU) 2016/679 for the protection of natural persons against the processing of personal data (hereinafter GDPR), the principles that must govern a processing are established.

14

According to Article 5(1)(a), (d), and (f) GDPR: "1. Personal data shall be: a) processed lawfully and fairly in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject ('lawfulness, fairness, and transparency'), [...] f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures ('integrity and confidentiality')." According to Recital 39 of the GDPR, "Personal data should be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security and confidentiality of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized access to such personal data (...)." Furthermore, according to the accountability principle explicitly stated in the second paragraph of the same article, which constitutes a cornerstone of the GDPR, the data controller "is responsible for, and must be able to demonstrate compliance with, paragraph 1 ('accountability')." This principle entails the obligation of the data controller to be able to demonstrate compliance with the principles of Article 5(1).

3. According to the provision of Article 24(1) GDPR: "1. Taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is performed in accordance with this Regulation. These measures shall be reviewed and updated when necessary," while according to the provisions of paragraphs 1 and 2 of Article 32 GDPR regarding the security of processing, "1. Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an appropriate level of security."
15

security against risks, […] 2. In assessing the appropriate level of security, particular consideration is given to the risks arising from processing, particularly from accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or access to personal data that has been transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.”  
4. A personal data breach (Article 4, point 12 of the GDPR) is understood as “a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or access to personal data that has been transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.” According to the Guidelines 18/2018 of the Article 29 Working Party of Directive 95/46/EC (now the European Data Protection Board) on Personal Data Breach Notification (“Guidelines on Personal data breach notification under Regulation 2016/679” WP 250 rev. 1), one type of personal data breach is categorized based on the security principle of “confidentiality,” when unauthorized access to personal data is identified (“confidentiality breach”). A breach can potentially have various significant adverse consequences for individuals, which may lead to physical, material, or moral harm. The GDPR explains that this harm may include loss of control over their personal data, restriction of their rights, discrimination, identity theft or misuse, financial loss, unlawful reversal of pseudonymization, damage to reputation, and loss of confidentiality of personal data protected by professional secrecy, etc. (see also recitals 85 and 75). Data breach incidents must be reported to the Authority within 72 hours from the moment the data controller becomes aware of them, according to Article 33, paragraph 1 of the GDPR: “1. In the case of a personal data breach, the data controller shall notify without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach, the supervisory authority competent pursuant to Article 55, unless the personal data breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. When notification to the supervisory authority is not made within 72 hours, it shall be accompanied by reasons for the delay.” The notification must contain at least the minimum information referred to in paragraph 3 of Article 33 of the GDPR, while according to paragraph 5 of the same article “The data controller documents any personal data breach, comprising the facts relating to the personal data breach, its effects, and the remedial actions taken. This documentation enables the supervisory authority to verify compliance with the present article.”  
5. Furthermore, the breach must also be communicated to the data subject, as appropriate and in accordance with the provisions of Article 34, paragraphs 1 and 2 of the GDPR: “1. When a personal data breach is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller shall notify the personal data breach to the data subject without undue delay. 2. The notification to the data subject referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article shall describe in clear and plain language the nature of the personal data breach and shall contain at least the information and measures referred to in Article 33, paragraph 3, points (b), (c), and (d).” In assessing the risk, consideration should be given, according to recitals 75 and 76 of the GDPR, to the likelihood and severity of the impact on the rights and freedoms of the data subjects, and the risk should be evaluated based on an objective assessment. According to Guidelines 18/2018 of the WP 29, when assessing the risk to individuals as a result of a breach, the data controller should consider the specific circumstances of the breach, including the severity of the potential impact and the likelihood of its occurrence, taking into account a range of criteria: the type of breach, the nature, sensitivity, and volume of the data, the ease of identifying individuals, the severity of the consequences for individuals, the specific characteristics of the individual, the specific...
17

characteristics of the data controller and the number of affected individuals (wp 250 rev.01, pp. 28-31).  
6. The data controller is obliged to facilitate the exercise of the rights of the data subjects as provided in Articles 15 to 22 (Article 12 para. 2 GDPR), which include the right of access under Article 15 GDPR and the right to erasure under Article 17 GDPR, as well as to act in each case upon the request of the data subject based on Articles 15-22 GDPR, informing the data subject within one month from the submission of the request (Article 12 para. 3 GDPR), while if there are reasonable doubts regarding the identity of the individual submitting the request referred to in Articles 15 to 21, the data controller may request the provision of additional information necessary to confirm the identity of the data subject (Article 12 para. 6 GDPR).  
7. Furthermore, according to Article 29 GDPR, "The processor and any person acting under the authority of the data controller or the processor, who has access to personal data, shall process those data only on instructions from the data controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law."  

8. In this case, from the elements of the file and following what emerged during the hearing, the following is established:  
With the extrajudicial declaration dated ..., the complainant informed the complained company that its former employee had used personal data (the mobile phone number of a customer of the company) which had come to his knowledge in the course of performing his duties, for a purpose other than the execution of his work and at a time when, according to the confidentiality agreement he had signed, he was obliged in any case to have deleted it. At the same time, he requested a) to be informed by the complained company about the processing of his data and b) to delete his personal data file. The complained company, although it has a Policy for managing personal data breach incidents as a data controller, did not take the appropriate actions to investigate and assess the incident, mistakenly considering that it was not a breach incident, because "the employee lawfully had in his possession the tax documents," as it states, overlooking the fact that these documents had been delivered to the customer upon delivery of the order, and the retention of the mobile phone number on the employee's personal device and its use for other purposes after the termination of his cooperation with the company constitutes a breach of data confidentiality, thus constituting a breach incident within the meaning of Article 4(12) GDPR. The fact that under the relevant confidentiality agreement between them, the said employee has undertaken to remedy any damage to the company does not affect its obligation, as a data controller, on the one hand to notify the incident to the Authority in accordance with Article 33 para. 1 GDPR, and on the other hand to take appropriate actions to handle the incident as referred to in para. 3 of Article 33 GDPR, specifically: to investigate the nature of the incident and the circumstances under which it occurred, to identify the potential consequences for the data subjects, to consider the possibility of taking measures to mitigate those consequences, and to reassess the technical and organizational security measures it implements to prevent similar incidents in the future, in accordance with Articles 24 and 32 GDPR, since the existing measures were not proven to be sufficient.  
Furthermore, it is established that the complained company violated the complainant's right of access to her personal data according to Article 15 GDPR, as a data subject whose data was held by the company in relation to the user account with the name “B” on its online store eshop.mymarket.gr. Specifically, following the extrajudicial declaration dated ..., the company did not check whether it held personal data of the complainant (user account in its online store, username, registration email, order history, etc.) according to the references in points 3.1.2 and 3.1.3 of the submitted Policy for managing requests from data subjects, nor did it request further supplementary information to identify the complainant as a data subject, if it had.
19

reasonable doubts, in accordance with Article 12 paragraph 6 of the GDPR, but was limited to the mention that the two contested orders indicated in the shipping details the full name of the complainant's husband, G. The company did not prove the claim that this account was opened with the details "G", arguing that it no longer had access to it, as it had deleted it in satisfaction of the right to erasure, following the document G/EXE/696/17-03-2023 from the Authority for clarification, after it had determined that the account belonged to the complainant. However, the company did not explain why it satisfied only the right to erasure and not (previously) the right of access that was also exercised by the complainant. 

At the same time, it is established that the respondent did not satisfy the right to erasure of the complainant's personal data in accordance with Article 17 of the GDPR, but only after the above-mentioned document from the Authority for clarification, which is evidenced by the fact that the complainant continued to receive newsletters from the respondent company until the beginning of the year .... In this case, it also appears that the procedures of the respondent company for responding to requests from data subjects were violated, because, despite the submission of a request for erasure by the complainant, the company, as the data controller, did not follow the provisions set out in points 5.2.2 and 5.2.3 of the aforementioned Procedure in order to identify the complainant as the subject of the user account with the name “B” in its online store and with the email address ..., in order to remove her from the list of recipients of its newsletter.

9. Therefore, the Authority finds the following violations on the part of the respondent company, as the data controller:

Based on the above, the Authority considers that there is a case to exercise its corrective powers under Article 58 paragraph 2 of the GDPR in relation to the established violations and that, based on the circumstances identified, an effective, proportionate, and deterrent administrative fine should be imposed pursuant to the provision of Article 58 paragraph 2 point (h) of the GDPR, both for the restoration of compliance and for the punishment of the unlawful behavior. Furthermore, the Authority took into account the criteria for assessing the fine set out in Article 83 paragraph 2 of the GDPR, the paragraphs 4 point (a) and 5 point (b) of the same article that are applicable in this case, the Guidelines for the application and determination of administrative fines for the purposes of Regulation 2016/679 issued on 03-10-2017 by the Article 29 Working Party (WP 253), and the Guidelines 04/2022 of the European Data Protection Board for the calculation of administrative fines within the framework of the General Regulation, as well as the factual data of the examined case and particularly the criteria presented below, by violation. 
A. Regarding the first established violation mentioned above (see paragraph 9 a), the following specific circumstances are particularly taken into account: 
• that the violation of the relevant obligations of the data controller falls under the provision of paragraph 4 of Article 83 of the GDPR.

• that the violation resulted in causing distress and adversely affecting the health of the complainant, who was in her ... month of pregnancy  
• the fact that the company did not take any action to investigate the incident and mitigate its consequences, arguing that it does not recognize the complainant as a subject, since the order was made in her husband's name, which is unrelated in this case to the leak of her mobile phone number  
• the fact that although the company has taken appropriate organizational measures (confidentiality agreement with employees, Incident Management Policy), it appears that these are not implemented in practice. Specifically, it was found that the prescribed procedure for the return or destruction of data after the employee's departure was not followed in the case at hand, but the company relied solely on his verbal assurance that he had not retained any personal data of customers, while subsequently, despite the alleged breach of the contractual obligations of the former employee, the company did not take any legal action to enforce them nor did it even assess the incident as a breach. Therefore, the relevant organizational measures seem to have been taken to cover the company's related formal obligation, without being genuinely adhered to
