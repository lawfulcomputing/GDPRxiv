The Hellenic Data Protection Authority convened, at the invitation of its President, in a meeting via teleconference on 26-04-2024, following a postponement on 26-03-2024, in order to examine the case referred to in the background of this document. Present at the meeting were the President of the Authority, Konstantinos Menoudakos, the regular members Charalampos Anthopoulos, Spyridon Vlachopoulos, Aikaterini Iliadou, Christos Kalloniatis, Konstantinos Lamprinoudakis, and Grigorios Tsolias, as well as the alternate member Nikolaos Faldamis, as the rapporteur. The Authority took into account the following:

With the identical complaints numbered G/EIS/10434/26-09-2022 and G/EIS/10441/26-09-2022 from A and B, respectively, against the EKAB, which concern the same factual circumstances, a violation of the complainants' right to access their personal data is reported, specifically regarding recorded telephone conversations they had with the EKAB call center. In particular, according to the aforementioned complaints, following an incident of delayed arrival of an ambulance that they had called on 15/6/2022 on behalf of a relative, specifically the (minor) granddaughter of the first complainant and daughter of the second complainant, the complainants submitted a request on 24/6/2022 via the contact form on the EKAB website for access to the recorded calls they made from the number … on 15/6/2022 at …, and from the number … on the same day and at the times …, …, … p.m. Nevertheless, as the complainants assert, EKAB did not respond in any way to their above request.

In the context of investigating the above complaints, the Authority, with document G/EKSE/3073/01-12-2022, communicated the relevant documents to the complained EKAB and invited it to express its views on the allegations, clarifying in particular what procedure was followed by it after receiving the above access requests. At the same time, the Authority requested EKAB to provide its relevant Data Protection Policy, as well as any other document from which the management of requests from data subjects in accordance with the GDPR could be inferred.

In its response numbered G/EIS/12651/19-12-2022, the complained EKAB initially stated that it is a public legal entity established in 1985, under the Ministry of Health, with the purpose, according to Article 29 of Law 4633/2019, "to coordinate the provision of immediate assistance and emergency medical care to citizens in emergency situations and to transport these citizens to health service units, to coordinate hospital and pre-hospital care in emergency and crisis situations, and to monitor and coordinate the emergency response systems of the hospitals of the National Health System (ESY)." CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW

1. From the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the GDPR and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), it follows that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the General Regulation (EU) 2016/679 for the protection of natural persons against the processing of personal data (hereinafter GDPR), this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Therefore, the Authority's competence to address a specific case is linked to the scope of application of the GDPR, which is defined by Article 2 paragraph 1 of the GDPR: "This Regulation applies to the processing of personal data that is automated in whole or in part, as well as to the non-automated processing of such data which are included or are intended to be included in a filing system," while "personal data" is defined according to the definition in Article 4 paragraph 1 of the GDPR as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, psychological, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person." Therefore, a critical prerequisite for the application of the GDPR and the establishment of the Authority's competence in this case is the determination that the recording of telephone calls to the emergency number 166 constitutes processing of data that can be attributed directly or indirectly to
17

specific, identified, or identifiable natural person, and therefore constitute personal data of the callers and, in this case, the complainants.  
2. According to Recital 26 of the GDPR, “...In order to determine whether a natural person is identifiable, all means that are reasonably likely to be used should be taken into account, such as the separation of that person, either by the data controller or by a third party, for the direct or indirect verification of the identity of the natural person. To ascertain whether certain means are reasonably likely to be used for the verification of the identity of the natural person, all objective factors should be considered, such as the costs and time required for identification, taking into account the technology available at the time of processing and technological developments. The principles of data protection should therefore not apply to anonymous information, that is, information that does not relate to an identified or identifiable natural person or to personal data that has been made anonymous in such a way that the identity of the data subject cannot or can no longer be ascertained.” Furthermore, in Opinion 7/2004 of the Article 29 Working Party regarding the meaning of the term ‘personal data’ it is stated: “As for the form or medium in which this information is provided, the concept of personal data includes information that is available in any form, e.g., alphabetical, numerical, graphic, photographic, or acoustic. According to Article 15(3) of the GDPR, the data subject has the right to receive a copy of the personal data being processed, while according to paragraph 4 of the same article, the right to receive a copy must not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others. As determined by the Authority (see decisions 20/2023, 36/2021, 26/2021, 13/2021), the GDPR does not establish a specific procedural framework for exercising the right of access but provides that the data controller must facilitate the exercise of the rights of data subjects (Article 12(2) GDPR), providing means for their electronic submission, especially when data is processed by electronic means (Recital 59 GDPR). According to the aforementioned Guidelines on transparency under Regulation 2016/679 (WP260 rev.01), the different ways provided by the data controller for the exercise of the rights of data subjects must be appropriate to the context and nature of their relationship and reflect the different ways in which data subjects interact with it. Specifically, a data controller who uses an electronic form on its website and paper forms at its reception still accepts access requests submitted in other ways (such as by letter or email) and provides a specific contact point for this purpose (access to which is possible via email or phone) to assist data subjects in exercising their rights (see example on page 35 of the guidelines WP260 rev.01). Therefore, the exercise of rights under Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR does not need to be in a specific form or to be exercised ceremoniously, e.g., by invoking the provisions of the GDPR or by explicit reference to its exercise. Although standardized forms for exercising rights may facilitate the recognition of a right of access, its exercise is equally valid if the data subject submits it via letter, email, or orally. Consequently, it is not mandatory to use any standardized form, which data subjects are merely encouraged to use (see EDPB 19/2022). Therefore, a right of access is validly exercised by a data subject through an email addressed, among others, to the DPO of the data controller.

According to the above, a policy that generally and in advance excludes the exercise of the right of access by callers to the number 166 to the records of recorded calls is contrary to the provisions of Articles 5(1)(a), 12(2), and 15 of the GDPR and should be revised within the framework of the compliance of the data controller with the General Regulation. It is also noted that the technical and organizational measures applied by the data controller, in order to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is carried out in accordance with the GDPR, which include appropriate policies and procedures (Article 24(2) GDPR), must be reviewed and updated when deemed necessary, as explicitly required by Article 24(1) GDPR.

11. In this case, given the above-stated factual circumstances, the following are established:
A. Considering that, in this case, the applicants exercised their right of access to their recorded calls to the number 166 as data subjects, identifying in their requests the phone numbers from which they called, the date and time of each call, and that the National Emergency Center (ΕΚΑΒ), as the data controller, did not respond in any way to their requests for access to the records of their recorded phone calls, did not inform the applicants of the reasons for not acting on their request, did not ask for additional information for their identification (e.g., confirmation from the provider of the possession of the number or other details that could lead to the identification of each caller), but on the contrary ignored these requests as repetitive, abusive, and "incomprehensible," a violation of Article 15(3) GDPR in conjunction with Articles 11(2), 12(4), and 12(6) GDPR is established on the part of ΕΚΑΒ as the data controller. As evidenced by the case file, ΕΚΑΒ responded to the complainant only after the complaint was submitted and communicated by the Authority, but sent its response to an incorrect email address, while claiming that no lawful right of access was ever exercised by the complainants, as no "official, signed request" was submitted to ΕΚΑΒ, which, however, is not necessary for the exercise of the right of access, as previously stated. In this context, it is considered aggravating that ΕΚΑΒ, in its initial response to the Authority, stated that the complainant knew at the time of submitting the request (June 2022) that he could not receive copies of the calls, while, as emerged from the examination of the case, this information was added to the ΕΚΑΒ website later, specifically in September 2022. It is also noted that the complainant, B, regardless of his status as a data subject (caller to 166), has an independent right of access to the data concerning his minor patient daughter, as exercising parental responsibility on her behalf, which ΕΚΑΒ, as the data controller, should have considered. Based on the above, the Authority unanimously finds that there is a case to exercise its corrective powers under Article 58(2) of the GDPR regarding the established violations and specifically that, based on the circumstances identified, an order should be issued under the provision of Article 58(2)(c) of the GDPR to satisfy the access rights of the complainants and additionally to impose, under the provision of Article 58(2)(f) of the GDPR, an effective, proportionate, and deterrent administrative monetary fine under Article 83 of the GDPR, both for restoring compliance and for punishing the unlawful behavior. For the determination of the fine, the Authority took into account the criteria set out in Article 83(2) of the GDPR, paragraph 5(b) of the same article applicable to this case, as well as the factual data of the examined case.

B. Furthermore, within the framework of the Authority's ex officio competence (Article 57(1)(a) and (d) GDPR) exercised in light of the examined complaint,
36

It is established that, according to the general policy of the EKAB (National Emergency Aid Center) and invoking the general confidentiality of telecommunications, copies or transcripts of recorded telephone calls are not provided to citizens, but only to the competent prosecutor within the framework of a criminal investigation. Information regarding the aforementioned policy is provided through the EKAB website (https://www.ekab.gr/pistopiitika-veveosis/) as follows: "Copies of conversations or transcripts of conversations from 166 are not provided because they are covered by special confidentiality, in accordance with the telecommunications confidentiality legislation (Article 5, paragraphs 1, 2, 3 of Law 2690/1999). The invocation of the 'right of access' under Article 15 of the GDPR cannot be substantiated because EKAB does not certify the callers." However, as emerges from the preceding considerations, the practice of preemptively excluding the exercise of the right of access to a copy of the data (in the file of the recorded call) under Article 15, paragraph 3 of the GDPR is incorrect: The voice data of a natural person constitutes, in any case, personal data of that person, because even if the name of the caller is not collected, the data subject's voice may, in certain cases, be identifiable through supplementary information, such as providing a certificate from the telecommunications provider that they are the holder of the specific number, provided it is proven that the holder of the telephone number made the specific call. Therefore, EKAB, as the data controller, must primarily ensure the possibility of exercising the right of access under Article 15 of the GDPR to the recorded telephone calls to the number 166. Only in exceptional cases and under the conditions of Article 15, paragraph 4 of the GDPR may a specific request not be fulfilled. However, the potential existence of the aforementioned conditions should be assessed on a case-by-case basis (in concreto), within the framework of examining a specific access request. Based on the above, the Authority unanimously decides to issue an order to EKAB, in accordance with the provision of Article 58, paragraph 2, point d of the GDPR, to revise, within a period of one (1) month from the notification of this decision, the said policy regarding the satisfaction of access rights of callers to the records of recorded calls to the number 166, in the context of EKAB's compliance with the GDPR, in such a way that the exercise of the right of access under Article 15 of the GDPR is not generally preemptively obstructed for all interested data subjects, and to inform the Authority accordingly.

C. Finally, regarding the fulfillment of EKAB's transparency obligations concerning the aforementioned data processing, in its response No. G/EIS/1892/14-03-2023, EKAB argued that the transparency obligations under Article 13 of the GDPR are satisfied "with the appropriate updates available at various points on the website, and more specifically the details of the data controller https://www.ekab.gr/diikisi-tou-ekav/, the contact details of the DPO and the reference to the right to lodge a complaint https://www.ekab.gr/dpo/15, a brief and concise reference to the agency's policy, the data that are provided and those that are not provided, and the contact details of the Data Controller https://www.ekab.gr/pistopiitika-veveosis/. Also, many times when elderly individuals call the service protocol and do not have internet access, the corresponding information is provided by phone either from the Secretariat of the Emergency Medical Service or the Independent Office of the Data Protection Officer with which an internal connection (transfer) is made. Additionally, in the physical file of the service, there is a continuously updated draft file in book form, which any citizen can study at the Central Service office." Within this document, the information regarding the right of access is described as follows: "you have the ability to exercise ... a) the right to information and the right of access to all identifiable personal data that EKAB holds and processes regarding you, the nature of the processing ...". After the discussion of the case in the Plenary (6/2/2024), EKAB posted the aforementioned Policy on its website (www.ekab.gr/dpo), with the addition of the following information:

The phrase "Identifiable data does not include the caller's details at 166 of the EKAB since personal data of callers is not requested during the incident notification." From the above, it is established that the information provided to data subjects (callers to number 166 and patients) until February 2024 did not meet the transparency requirements of the GDPR, on the one hand, because it was not easily accessible to the subjects, as the necessary information was scattered across the EKAB website and not presented in a clear and comprehensible format, while the mentioned paper-based, "continuously updated" file located at the Central Service was not easily accessible to subjects who might be located anywhere in the territory; on the other hand, both the information mentioned on the website and the submitted "Personal Data Protection Policy" were vague and incomprehensible regarding the possibility (or not) of granting access to recorded telephone calls to the subjects, within the framework of exercising the right of access. Based on the above, the Authority unanimously finds that there is a case to exercise its corrective powers under Article 58(2) of the GDPR concerning the established violation of the obligation to inform data subjects under Article 13 of the GDPR, in accordance with the fundamental principle of transparency (Article 5(1)(a) of the GDPR) in any case until February 2024, when the above addition to its Policy was posted on the EKAB website, regardless of whether this addition is in accordance with Article 15 of the GDPR, in light of what is mentioned above in point A, and that it must, in application of the provision of Article 58(2)(h) of the GDPR, impose an effective, proportionate, and dissuasive administrative monetary fine under Article 83 of the GDPR, both for the restoration of compliance and for the punishment of the unlawful behavior. For the determination of the fine, the Authority took into account the criteria set out in Article 83(2) of the GDPR, paragraph 5(h) and (i) of the same article applicable to the present case, as well as the factual data of the examined case.
