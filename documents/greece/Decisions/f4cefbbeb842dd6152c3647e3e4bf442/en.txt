CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW  
1) Since from the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation 2016/679) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137) it follows that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, Law 4624/2019, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Moreover, an activity cannot be considered exclusively personal or domestic, in the sense of the provision of Article 2 para. 2 subpara. c) of the GDPR and thus exempt from the scope of this Regulation when it extends, even partially, into the public sphere and, therefore, exits the private sphere of the data processor, while the submission before an authority or body entails, at least, the "use" and "disclosure by transmission" of "personal data" within the meaning of Article 4 subpara. 1) and 2) of the GDPR.

2) Because according to the provisions of Article 6, paragraph 1, point (f) of the GDPR regarding the legality of processing: “1. Processing is lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the following applies: (...) (f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, particularly if the data subject is a child. (...)”. A case of overriding legitimate interest exists, in particular, if the processing concerns data necessary for the recognition, exercise, or defense of a right in legal proceedings or before a disciplinary body5. Furthermore, a prerequisite for the lawful application of the above provision is that the data for which access is requested or which is used in any way is absolutely necessary and suitable for the recognition and exercise of the right (principle of necessity), particularly in light of the specific dispute that is pending. The necessity exists when the intended purpose cannot be achieved by other less intrusive means. The data must also not be more than what is absolutely necessary for the defense of the right6.

9) Since from the entirety of the evidence in the file, and what emerged from the hearing process and the submissions of the parties, it appears that the respondent submitted the letter-report dated 20-5-2021 with relevant details to the authorities that, in his belief, were competent to address the behavior of three of the complainants who hold the status of cleric, with the aim of informing these authorities about their behavior and submitting a request for the investigation of the described factual incidents. However, from the entirety of the evidence in the case, in light of the provisions mentioned above in considerations 4-8, it appears that only two of the six recipients of the contentious letter, namely Metropolitan L and Metropolitan M, initially had the ability to exercise disciplinary control over the complainants who hold the status of cleric and

fall under their jurisdiction, namely of G the first and of A and B the second, respectively. In this regard, the processing operation in question, which consists of the communication of the data of the aforementioned complainants to the authorities competent in this matter, is deemed to be, in principle, necessary, appropriate, and essential for the purposes of the legitimate interests of the data subject, and therefore falls under the provisions of Article 6(1)(f) GDPR. 

10) However, as emerges from the entirety of the case's elements, beyond the aforementioned Metropolitans L and M, who had jurisdiction to oversee the aforementioned three clerics according to the respective jurisdiction of each, as stated in the previous consideration, the other recipients of the contested letter-report do not have the authority to exercise oversight over the behavior of these three clerics. Consequently, the communication of their data does not appear to be appropriate or necessary for the exercise of the right to report of the data subject. Moreover, the communication of the data of the complainants who do not hold the status of clerics, to all recipients of the contested letter-report, along with the relevant details, was not necessary or appropriate for the exercise of the right to report of the data subject and cannot be considered to have a legal basis for such processing under the provisions of Article 6(1)(f) GDPR. 

11) Following all the above, a violation is established on the part of the data controller, of the provisions of Article 6(1)(f) GDPR, as set forth in consideration 10 above, and therefore, according to the Authority's assessment, there is a case for exercising its corrective powers under Article 58(2) GDPR. Specifically, the Authority deems that there is a case for imposing, in accordance with the provision of Article 58(2)(h) GDPR, an effective, proportionate, and deterrent administrative monetary fine, in accordance with the provisions of Article 83(3) and (5) GDPR, taking into account recitals 148 GDPR, as well as

In evaluating the data, in order to select the appropriate and corrective measure, the Authority takes into account the following criteria under Article 83(2) GDPR:  
• "the nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement, taking into account the nature, scope, or purpose of the relevant processing, as well as the number of data subjects affected by the infringement and the degree of damage suffered." It is noted that this specific violation concerns six subjects; however, it did not take on a broader character, while the complainants did not invoke or substantiate before the Authority any material damage related to this violation (Article 83(2)(a) GDPR). 
• "the intent or negligence that caused the infringement." It is noted that this violation is due to the negligence of the complainant (Article 83(2)(b) GDPR).  
• "any actions taken by the data controller or the processor to mitigate the damage suffered by the data subjects." It is noted that the contested letter-report along with the relevant details was submitted in a strictly personal file to its recipients, while the security of this data, after its inclusion in the recipients' file, is not the responsibility of the data controller, but exclusively of the recipients, as independently responsible processors (Article 83(2)(c) GDPR).
