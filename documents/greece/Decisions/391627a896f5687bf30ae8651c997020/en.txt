CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW

1. Since, from the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter "GDPR") and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), it follows that the Authority has the competence to supervise the implementation of the provisions of the GDPR, this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Specifically, from the provisions of Articles 57(1)(f) of the GDPR and 13(1)(g) of Law 4624/2019, it follows that the Authority has the competence to address the complaint of A against the company NIKOS LAZARIDIS OVGEE S.A. and to exercise, correspondingly, the powers granted to it by the provisions of Articles 58 of the GDPR and 15 of Law 4624/2019.

2. Since, Article 5 of the GDPR establishes the principles of processing that govern the processing of personal data. Specifically, it is stated in paragraph 1 that personal data, among other things: “a) shall be processed lawfully and fairly in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject (‘lawfulness, fairness, transparency’), b) are collected for specified, legitimate purposes and shall not be further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes (…), c) are adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for the purposes for which they are processed (‘data minimization’) (…), e) shall be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures (‘integrity and confidentiality’)”. In order for personal data to be processed lawfully, that is, processed in accordance with the requirements of the GDPR, the conditions for the application and compliance with the principles of Article 5(1) of the GDPR must be cumulatively met. The data controller, in the context of its compliance with the principle of lawful or fair processing of personal data, must inform the data subject that their data will be processed in a lawful and transparent manner and must be able to demonstrate at any time its compliance with these principles. The processing of personal data in a transparent manner constitutes an expression of the principle of fair processing and is linked to the principle of accountability, providing data subjects with the right to exercise control over their data, holding data controllers accountable. The collection and processing of personal data should not take place secretly or be concealed from the data subject, nor should all necessary information be concealed, unless provided for by law, in accordance with the conditions of Article 8 of the ECHR, as interpreted by the decisions of the ECtHR and always in light of the principle of proportionality.

3. Since, according to the provisions of Article 5(2) of the GDPR, it follows that the data controller bears the responsibility and must be able to demonstrate its compliance with the processing principles established in paragraph 1 of Article 5. As the Authority has already ruled, with the GDPR a new compliance model has been adopted, the central point of which is
11

The principle of accountability is established, under which the data controller is obliged to design, implement, and generally take the necessary measures and policies to ensure that the processing of data is in compliance with the relevant legislative provisions. Furthermore, the data controller bears the additional duty to demonstrate at all times their compliance with the principles of Article 5(1) of the GDPR.

4.  
Since, according to Directive No. 115/2001 of the Authority regarding the processing of data in employment relationships, it is stated that the consent of employees cannot override the prohibition of exceeding the purpose and that in the case of employment relationships, the inherent inequality of the parties and the generally dependent relationship of employees raises doubts about the freedom of employee consent, which is a necessary element for the validity of the processing. Additionally, according to the Guidelines of the Article 29 Working Party, the power imbalance between employer and employee leads to the conclusion that in the majority of cases of personal data processing at work, the legal basis cannot and should not be that of consent. In fact, WP29 cites as an example of the incorrect application of the legal basis of consent the case of operating a system for monitoring employees in the workplace through cameras.

5.  
Since, the employer, in any case, applying the principles of the GDPR, must implement acceptable use policies for electronic means and communicate them to employees. These policies must describe the permissible use of the networks and equipment of the entity and the processing carried out in detail, as well as the possibility of legitimate access by the employer to the electronic means used by employees. According to the Guidelines WP9, the case law of the CJEU, and as has been determined by the Authority, the employer may legitimately process the personal data of employees based on their contractual relationship, after having previously informed them transparently in accordance with the provisions set out in Recital 58 of the GDPR, in application of Article 5(1) of the GDPR for all individual processing operations.

6.  
Since, when personal data concerning a data subject is collected from the data subject, the data controller, at the time of collecting the personal data, provides the data subject with all the information specified in Article 13(1) and (2) of the GDPR. According to the Guidelines of WP29 regarding transparency when informing data subjects, the information provided must be specific, definitive, and clear. It is specifically clarified in the aforementioned Guidelines (Recital 13) that: "The use of vague terms such as 'may', 'certain', 'often', and 'likely' should also be avoided. When data controllers choose to use vague wording, they must be able, in accordance with the principle of accountability, to demonstrate the reason why the use of such wording could not be avoided and why it does not undermine the legality of the processing" (§ 13).

iii.  
the company has drafted the document "Acceptable Use Policy," on the first page of which it states: "approval details," where the full name of the complainant, her signature, the date, and the time are recorded, and on page 3, Chapter 2, point 1 states that "I acknowledge that the use of the information and communication systems provided to me by 'NIKOS LAZARIDIS S.A.' may be monitored and/or recorded for legitimate purposes," and the complainant states that "the phones and the computer that she will use and other means of communication may be monitored and/or the communications recorded by the employer." The phrase "approval" incorrectly gives the impression that the complainant provides her consent for the implementation of this Policy, a practice that contradicts the provisions set forth in considerations 4 and 5 above, as the complainant should have confirmed, under conditions of transparency, that she was aware of the relevant document. The Authority finds that the inclusion of the phrase "approval" in this document constitutes a violation of the principle of legality under Article 5 paragraph 1 subparagraph a of the GDPR and that in every corresponding policy or information document, the complained company must correct this reference/phrase in compliance with the provisions of the GDPR. Furthermore, the phrase "may" mentioned in the Policy in question, considering the aforementioned Guidelines (consideration 6), creates legal uncertainty for the subject, as it does not ensure the required transparency for the individual acts of processing personal data of its employees. The Authority finds that this wording constitutes a violation of the principle of transparency under Article 5 paragraph 1 subparagraph a of the GDPR and that in every corresponding policy or information document, the complained company must eliminate linguistic qualifiers such as "may" and provide clear and accurate information to the subject. Finally, from the elements of the file, it appears that the company has not installed and does not use a system for monitoring and recording telephone communications.  
iv.  
Regarding the company's response to the complainant that "Your personal data is kept securely by the company due to your employment contract, having your consent from …," referring to the date of signing by the complainant of the relevant documents mentioned above in consideration 8 ii, the Authority finds that the company's response referencing the legal basis of consent is incorrect, as it creates the impression that the legal basis for processing personal data is consent, while it is the contractual relationship between the complainant and the complained company, as evidenced by the elements of the case file.
