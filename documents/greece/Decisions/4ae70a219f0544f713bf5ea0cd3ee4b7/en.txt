as well as an excerpt from the email he had sent to the other 14 members of the Primary Education School Committee of the Municipality of Patras, in view of its meeting on 05-10-2022. 5. Regarding the personal data of the complainant, which were made public for the above journalistic purposes, it is noted that the specific personal data constitute data directly related to the complainant's capacity as a member of the Board of Directors of the specific legal entity and therefore are public for the purposes of transparency in public administration. The aforementioned named statement of the complainant was included verbatim in the minutes kept for the meeting, which are also public, as well as in the decision that was issued and posted on the DIAVGEIA Program (ADA:...) for transparency purposes. Even the fact of the complainant's political beliefs is also intertwined with his specific capacity as a member of the Board of Directors of the legal entity, since in this position, he is appointed by a municipal party, which he represents within the School Committee, according to the legislation governing school committees. Finally, beyond all the above, it concerned data (and specifically the data regarding his political beliefs) that had evidently been made public by the data subject himself.

6. Given that the above posts of the complainant on his Facebook profile are public and freely accessible to all (while he could have chosen privacy settings), the user's desire for the publication of his data is presumed, and therefore all information posted by the data subject himself in public access on the internet does not constitute personal data and does not fall under the protective provisions of the law on personal data.

2. 16. Whereas additionally, it is established that the controversial post from ... containing information on the official website of the respondent Municipality regarding the approval decision of the School Committee for the social tutoring, which included the aforementioned details of the complainant, was made for the purpose of informing the citizens and enhancing the transparency of municipal action. However, it mentioned among other things "..." in the context of intense political confrontation and criticism, and therefore the data subject could not reasonably expect this specific post on the said website with this content and indeed with reference to his professional status, his workplace, and the isolation of the content of the email he had sent to facilitate the committee meeting, while the purpose of informing the citizens could have been achieved without the provision of these details.  
At the same time, it is noted that in a related post of the same approval decision on the DIAVGEIA website (with ID: ...) only the full name of the complainant, his status as a regular member of the committee, as well as his position in the context of the dialogical discussion that took place during the committee meeting, which he had already sent via email to facilitate the committee meeting, were published, and not his professional status or workplace. In light of the above, upon the combined interpretation of cases (c) and (d) of the first paragraph of Article 28 of Law 4624/2019, the publication of the simple, in this case, personal data of the complainant's professional status, the place of work, and the content of the email he had sent to the School Committee to facilitate its meeting was not necessary for achieving the purposes of maintaining the specific website according to the law, which are to inform the citizens and enhance the transparency of administrative action, and exceeds the necessary measure, as these purposes could have been achieved by milder means. This is because the reference of the publication in the context of informing the citizens about the social protection and solidarity work of the Municipality, as well as for reasons of transparency of municipal work, must be made in such a way that the intrusion into the private and family life of the data subject is minimized within the framework of harmonized application of the protection of private and family life on the one hand and the right to information on the other. In this case, the principle of minimization (Article 5, paragraph 1, point (c) of the GDPR, which according to Article 28, paragraph 2 of Law 4624/2019 applies in this case) or of prudence in the processing of personal data, which also applies to the processing of personal data for journalistic purposes to satisfy a legitimate interest in informing citizens, imposes the least burdensome processing for the subject, even if it is not as effective compared to an overly comprehensive processing. In this specific case, the said publication constitutes a form of interference with the rights that constitute the constitutionally protected private sphere of the individual (Articles 9, paragraph 1, subparagraph (b) and 9A of the Constitution), which is deemed unlawful because it is not necessary for the purpose of informing the public, and exceeds the limits set by the
17

Principle of Proportionality (see Article 28 paragraphs 1 and 2 of Law 4624/2019 and Article 5 paragraph 1 subparagraph c of the GDPR).  
17. Given the above, it appears that the purposes of valid and complete information to the citizens could be achieved through milder means, such as, for example, informing about the approval decision of the Municipal Council for the public tutoring and posting this decision on the Municipality's website (as it was also posted on the DIAVGEIA website), mentioning the minority and majority opinions.  
18. Furthermore, it is taken into account that after the document providing clarifications sent by the Authority to the reported Municipality, as the latter acknowledges in its memorandum with reference number G/EIS/9059/22-11-2024 to the Authority, the aforementioned link was removed from the official website of the Municipality, but in the meantime, it had been published on several other websites, including that of the Mayor's municipal faction "F".  
19. As a result of the above, it is established that the principle of data minimization (Article 28 of Law 4624/2019, Article 5 paragraph 1 subparagraph c of the GDPR) has been violated, as the data being processed is not adequate, relevant, and does not limit itself to what is necessary in relation to the intended purpose, while the data subject could not reasonably expect that for the specific purpose of informing the citizens, there would be processing involving the public posting of the entire email message on the official website of the Municipality, with the details of their position and workplace, especially in the context of intense political confrontation and criticism.  
20. The violation of the fundamental principles for processing, as detailed above, incurs the imposition of administrative sanctions under Article 83 paragraph 5 subparagraph a of the GDPR. According to the GDPR (Recital 148), in order to strengthen the enforcement of the rules of this Regulation, sanctions, including administrative fines, should be imposed for each violation of this Regulation, in addition to or instead of the appropriate measures imposed by the supervisory authority in accordance with this Regulation.

21. Based on the above, the Authority considers that the imposition of a corrective measure is not sufficient to restore compliance with the provisions of the GDPR that have been violated and that, based on the circumstances identified, an additional effective, proportional, and deterrent administrative monetary fine should be imposed under Article 83 of the GDPR, both to restore compliance and to sanction the unlawful behavior.  
22. Furthermore, the Authority took into account the criteria for assessing the fine as defined in Article 83 paragraph 2 of the GDPR, paragraph 5 of the same article applicable to the present case, and the Guidelines 4/2022 of the European Data Protection Board for calculating administrative fines for the purposes of Regulation 2016/679, which were approved on 24/5/2023, as well as the factual data of the examined case and in particular:  
i) The fact that the reported party, in its capacity as data controller, violated the principle of data minimization as provided in Article 5 paragraph 1 subparagraph c, i.e., violated a fundamental principle of the GDPR for the protection of personal data.
