CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW  
1. Since from the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679 – hereinafter, GDPR) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137) it follows that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Article 5 paragraph 1 of the GDPR sets out the principles that must govern a processing operation. According to the principle of accountability introduced by this article, it is stated in paragraph 2 thereof that the data controller "bears the responsibility and is in a position to demonstrate compliance with paragraph 1 ('accountability')." As the Authority has ruled,1 with the GDPR a new compliance model has been adopted, the central point of which is the principle of accountability, under which the data controller is obliged to design, implement, and generally take the necessary measures and policies to ensure that the processing of data is in accordance with the relevant legislative provisions. Furthermore, the data controller is burdened with the additional duty to demonstrate itself and at any time its compliance with the principles of Article 5 paragraph 1 of the GDPR.  
2. Since according to Article 15 paragraphs 1, 3, and 4 of the GDPR "1. The data subject has the right to obtain from the data controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning him or her is being processed and, if so, the right of access to personal data and the following information: a) the purposes of the processing, b) the relevant categories of personal data, c) the recipients or categories of recipients to whom the personal data have been or will be disclosed, in particular, recipients in third countries or international organizations, d) where possible, the period for which the personal data will be stored or, when that is not possible, the criteria used to determine that period, e) the existence of the right to request from the data controller the rectification or erasure of personal data or the restriction of processing of personal data concerning the data subject or the right to object to such processing, f) the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, g) when the personal data are not collected from the data subject, any available information as to their source, h) the existence of automated decision-making, including profiling, as provided for in Article 22 paragraphs 1 and 4 and, at least in those cases, significant information about the logic involved, as well as the significance and the envisaged consequences of such processing for the data subject. 2. […] 3. The data controller provides a copy of the

With these provisions, the right of access of the data subject to their personal data is established. In the context of this right, the data subject must, on the one hand, have access to personal data that has been collected and pertains to them, in order to be informed and to verify the accuracy and nature of the processing of their data and to validate the lawfulness of the processing, and on the other hand, be able to exercise this right easily and at reasonable intervals.2 The data controller must provide remote access to a secure system through which the data subject can gain direct access to the data concerning them.3  
Moreover, the Authority consistently accepts that the data subject has the right to know whether personal data concerning them is being processed, as well as to gain access to it, without the need to invoke a legitimate interest4 since this is inherent and forms the basis of the right of access, that is, the right of the data subject to be informed about information that concerns them and has been recorded in a file maintained by the data controller, so that the fundamental principle of law for the protection of personal data, which consists of the transparency of processing as a prerequisite for any further control by the data subject of its lawfulness, is realized.5 Similarly, there is no requirement to invoke the reasons for which the data subject wishes to exercise the right of access.6 Furthermore, the obligation to satisfy the right of access is universal, meaning it pertains to all information concerning the data subject and, additionally, does not depend on the invocation of the reasons for exercising the right.7 Consequently, the satisfaction of the right does not depend on a prior assessment by the data controller as to whether the exercise of the right is justified or not.8  
Furthermore, regarding the determination of the scope of the limits of the right of access of a data subject to their personal data, access is understood to extend to the personal data as such, without a general description of such data being sufficient nor a mere reference to the categories of personal data processed by the data controller. Therefore, data subjects are entitled to access all data that is being processed and that concerns them, unless specifically provided for by the GDPR limits or restrictions (see Articles 12(5), 15(4), 23, 89(2) and (3), 85(2) of the GDPR). Finally, it is noted that if the data subject does not explicitly request otherwise, the request to exercise the right of access is understood as general and includes all personal data concerning the respective data subject.9

Because based on the above, the Authority decides to impose on the respondent company as the data controller the administrative sanctions mentioned in the operative part, which consist not only of fines but also of other appropriate measures, as deemed appropriate according to the severity of the violations.  

FOR THESE REASONS  
The Authority  
a) Finds that the respondent company violated Article 15 paragraph 1 of the GDPR in conjunction with the provision of paragraph 3 of Article 12 of the GDPR as it proceeded to an improper and specifically delayed satisfaction of the right of access concerning the data related to the complainant, as mentioned in considerations 4 and 7, and imposes on it for this violation an administrative fine of one thousand (1,000.00€) euros, in accordance with Article 58 paragraph 2 subparagraph (h) of the GDPR.  
b) Finds that the respondent company violated the complainant's right of access (Article 15 of the GDPR) regarding the recorded conversations concerning him, as it sent them in the manner desired by the complainant and dictated by the relevant corporate policy late, specifically after the hearing before the Authority, in violation of Article 12 paragraphs 2 and 3 of the GDPR, as mentioned in considerations 5 and 7, and imposes on it for this violation an administrative fine of two thousand (2,000.00€) euros, in accordance with Article 58 paragraph 2 subparagraph (h) of the GDPR.  
c) Finds that the internal policy of the company regarding the manner of satisfying the right of access to recorded conversations violates the provision of Article 12 paragraph 2 of the GDPR as detailed in consideration 6 of this decision and therefore issues an order to the respondent company to amend this internal policy within thirty (30) days from the notification of this decision, so that the simplest possible satisfaction for the data subjects becomes the rule and not the exception, and to subsequently inform the Authority of its actions. In any case, the Authority reserves the right to re-examine the case, particularly regarding the overall practice followed by the respondent company, especially in the event of the Authority examining more related complaints.
