CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW  

1. Since, from the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter "GDPR") and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A'137), it follows that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data.  
2. Since Article 55(3) of the GDPR states that "[s]upervisory authorities are not competent to monitor processing operations carried out by courts in the exercise of their judicial functions." Furthermore, in Recital 20 of the GDPR, it is stated that "While this Regulation applies, among other things, to the activities of courts and other judicial authorities, Union or Member State law could specify the acts and procedures of processing in relation to the processing of personal data by courts and other judicial authorities. The competence of supervisory authorities should not cover the processing of data...

3. Considering that the Authority, taking into account that:  
i. the Court of Justice of the European Union4 interpreted the provision of Article 55, paragraph 3 and Recital 20 of the GDPR, and accepted, among other things, that: “(...) 34. Therefore, the reference in Article 55, paragraph 3, of Regulation 2016/679 to the processing operations carried out by courts 'in the context of their jurisdictional competence' has, within the framework of this regulation, the meaning that it is not limited to the processing of personal data carried out by courts in the context of specific cases, but that it relates, more generally, to all processing operations carried out by courts in the context of their jurisdictional activity, resulting in the exclusion from the competence of the supervisory authority of the processing operations whose oversight by this authority could, directly or indirectly, affect the independence of judicial officials and weigh on their decisions. 35. In this regard, the nature and purpose of the processing carried out by a court, although primarily related to the examination of the lawfulness of the processing, may nevertheless provide indications from which it can be inferred that the processing falls within the exercise, by that court, of its 'jurisdictional competence'.”5  
ii. in his submissions in the same case6, the Advocate General states that “In summary, I propose that the term 'acting in the context of their jurisdictional competence', as included in Article 55, paragraph 3, of the GDPR, be approached from an institutional perspective ('is it a court?'), which will then be corrected, possibly, by a functional assessment of the nature of the activity in question ('what specific type of activity is the court engaged in?'). In light of the purpose set out in Recital 20 of the GDPR, according to the latter assessment of activities, a broad interpretation of the concept of 'jurisdictional competence' should be applied, which extends beyond the mere decision-making by a court in a specific case. It should also cover all activities that may indirectly affect the independence of judicial officials. Therefore, it should generally be considered that courts act in the context of their 'jurisdictional competence', unless it is established, regarding a specific type of activity, that it has an administrative character only.”  
It concludes that it has the authority to examine the complaint in question, as the personal data whose processing is being investigated pertains to a judicial employee and falls within the scope of the administrative duties of the Administrative Court of Athens, and there is no risk of adverse effects on the jurisdictional work of the Court, since the activity to which the complaint relates does not appear to be connected in any way to the exercise of jurisdictional work; any decision by the Authority regarding the complaint does not concern acts or omissions of judicial officials within the meaning of Articles 87 et seq. of the Constitution, but rather issues of administrative organization and operation of the Service that assists the Court.

**iii.**  
With the document under protocol number ... that the complainant submitted to the Administrative Court of Appeal of Athens, he requested access to his service file, which did not exist in the Service's Archive, raising the questions a. where is the file, b. since when, c. for what reason, to which he did not receive a response from the data controller. From the responses that the Authority has received from the Administrative Court of Appeal, it appears that the data controller has not satisfied the complainant's right of access to his service file, which contains all documents and information that determine the personal, family, property, and service status of the employee, without, however, presenting valid reasons for the non-satisfaction of this request, such as any obstruction of some internal process of the Court. Furthermore, the fact that there is the possibility through the digital application "Human Resources Registry of the Greek Public" for the subject itself to search for the details of its file does not negate the obligation of the data controller to respond, even negatively, to the subject's request, while the lack of response from the data controller constitutes a separate violation of Articles 12 paragraph 3 and the corresponding right of the GDPR. Regarding the removal in the year ... of the computer that the complainant used in his office, a fact that deprived him of access, on the one hand, to files containing personal data concerning him that he had stored on it, and on the other hand, to the OSDDY-DD platform, where there are documents containing personal data of the complainant, the Administrative Court of Appeal, with its document number ... to the Authority, responded that the files that the complainant had in his personal file on his computer exist in "backup" and that in response to the question posed to the complainant whether he needed them, the latter answered negatively. However, as it appears from the file's data, the complainant from the removal of the computer on ... no longer had the ability to access documents containing personal data concerning him, his evaluation reports, as well as his "browsing history" on the Internet that had been stored on the hard drive of that computer. It is noted that in the Guidelines 1/2022 of the European Data Protection Board regarding the rights of data subjects – right of access, it is explained that the right of access consists of three components: a) confirmation of whether personal data is being processed, b) access to it, and c) provision of information regarding the processing. It is further clarified that the critical time for assessing the existence of the first aforementioned element (i.e., the processing of personal data) by the data controller is the time of exercising the right, and its satisfaction requires the existence of...

of personal data (availability) at this time.11 Following the above, the Authority finds that the Administrative Court of Appeals of Athens, as a public body responsible for processing, has not satisfied the complainant's right of access and has violated Articles 15(1) and (3) in conjunction with Article 12(3) of the GDPR. Therefore, the exercise of the Authority's corrective power under Article 58(2)(b) of the GDPR is appropriate, issuing a reprimand to it, as well as under Article 58(2)(c) of the GDPR by imposing on it, as an appropriate corrective measure, the obligation to satisfy the complainant's right of access both to his service file, providing copies of all documents containing personal data concerning him, and to the hard drive of the computer he used until the year ..., providing copies of documents referencing his name, his evaluation reports, and the complainant's internet browsing history, considering that the complainant has had a computer since May 2023 and access to the OSDD-DA has been restored.

14. Since the Administrative Court of Appeals of Athens, in its administrative capacity, is obliged to appoint a DPO for matters related to the protection of personal data of its employees, the details of which it must publicize and notify to the Authority. However, from the case file, it does not appear that the Administrative Court has appointed a DPO, as there is no mention of this issue in the memorandum submitted after the hearing, even though its representative was asked about it during the hearing and reserved the right to respond in the memorandum. Furthermore, following a search on 09-12-2024 in the Authority's electronic system, where the notifications of DPOs of legal entities/bodies are recorded, no relevant statement from the Administrative Court to the Authority was found. Therefore, the Authority finds that the Administrative Court of Appeals of Athens, as a public body responsible for processing, has violated Article 37(1)(a) and (7) of the GDPR and Article 6(1) and (5) of Law 4624/2019, and the exercise of the corrective power under Article 58(2)(d) of the GDPR is appropriate, imposing on it, as an appropriate corrective measure, the obligation to appoint a Data Protection Officer.12

15. Regarding the complainant's claim that due to the flooding in a part of the Administrative Court of Appeals, the court's computers were damaged and for this reason the data controller should have reported a personal data breach incident to the Authority, the case file does not indicate that, because the computer was removed from the complainant's office through specific actions of the Court, and he did not have access to documents containing personal data, a breach of the personal data processed by the Administrative Court of Appeals of Athens occurred. Moreover, it is not substantiated and does not appear that there was a breach of confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal data due to the flooding, and therefore, there was no obligation for the Administrative Court of Appeals of Athens to notify a personal data breach incident, and no corresponding violation of Article 33 of the GDPR is established.

Based on the above, the Authority unanimously decides that corrective measures, as mentioned in the operative part, should be imposed on the Administrative Court of Appeals of Athens, as the responsible processor, which are deemed appropriate according to the severity of the violations.

FOR THESE REASONS

The Authority
