DECISION 9/2024

The Hellenic Data Protection Authority convened, upon the invitation of its President, in an extraordinary session via teleconference on 28-07-2023. Present were Konstantinos Menoudakos, President of the Authority, and the regular members Spyros Vlachopoulos, Konstantinos Lamprinoudakis, Charalampos Anthopoulos, Christos Kalloniatis, as rapporteur, and Grigorios Tsolias. The regular member of the Authority Aikaterini Iliadou and her alternate Nikolaos Faldamis were absent, despite being duly summoned in writing, due to an impediment. In the session, without voting rights, were present, by order of the President, Georgios Rousopoulos, a specialized IT scientist, as assistant rapporteur, and Georgia Palaiologou, an employee of the Administrative Affairs Department, as secretary.  
The Authority took into account the following:  
The Authority has received numerous complaints from telephone subscribers related to receiving telephone calls for the purpose of promoting products and services of the company ELPEDISON ELECTRICITY PRODUCTION SINGLE MEMBER S.A. (hereinafter Elpedison). In these cases, the Authority forwarded each complaint to Elpedison, usually within a short period, so that the company could investigate the allegations and present its views. Subsequently, the Authority examined Elpedison's views to ensure that all elements of each case were complete, sending further documents for clarifications when deemed necessary. In cases where the complaint was not confirmed (e.g., when the complainant was not registered in the Registry of Article 11 paragraph 2 of Law 3471/2006 - hereinafter the Registry - or had not exercised an objection based on Article 21 of the GDPR), the Authority rejected the complaint, responding to the complainant. For cases where, after the aforementioned examination of the case, it was likely that the complaint was confirmed or appeared to be valid, even if this was not accepted by the company, the Authority grouped the relevant cases for joint examination.  
In this specific case, forty (40) complaint cases are being jointly examined, which are included in the Appendix of this document. After completing the files of the complaints and considering Elpedison's views on each of the complaints, they were categorized as follows:  
Category A (6 complaints): Cases for which Elpedison appears to accept that there was a violation, primarily concerning its activity as the data controller.  
Category B (3 complaints): Cases in which the respective complainant provides sufficient evidence and proof for the documentation of advertising calls made on behalf of Elpedison, but during the investigation of each complaint case and the completion of its file, Elpedison provided evidence from its collaborating companies, based on which it does not accept, in a documented manner, the conduct of calls to the specific complainants.  
Category C (29 complaints): Cases in which from the initial investigation of the complaints and Elpedison's views, it emerges that while Elpedison has - correctly - sent the details of the complainant's telephone number to its collaborating companies, which conduct telephone calls for its promotional purposes, so that this number would not be called, these companies, for various reasons, failed to prevent the call to the complainant. Therefore, in these cases, it appears that there is liability, beyond Elpedison, of the respective company conducting the telephone calls (hereinafter also call center). These complaints concern the following companies:  

a) BEFON TELECOMMUNICATIONS Single Member LLC (hereinafter Befon): three (3) complaints (with references G.A.1 to G.A.3 in the Appendix).  
b) CALL EXPERTS CALL CENTER SERVICES I.C.C. (hereinafter Call Experts): eleven (11) complaints (with references G.B.1 to G.B.11 in the Appendix).  
c) Teleraise I.C.C. (hereinafter Teleraise): one (1) complaint (with reference G.C.1 in the Appendix).  
d) ST & Co. E.E. (hereinafter Zitatel): eight (8) complaints (with references G.D.1 to G.D.7 and G.E.71 in the Appendix).  
e) PLEGMAT NET M.E.P.E. (hereinafter PLEGMAT): seven (7) complaints (with references G.E.1 to G.E.7 in the Appendix).  

Category D (2 complaints): Cases of complaints that were under investigation at the time of the companies' summons to a hearing at the Authority.

In these two cases, Elpedison's views had not been submitted by the day of the hearing at the Authority. The first concerns a complaint in which Call Experts may be involved as the data processor, while in the second, Zitatel.  
Regarding its general activity, Elpedison had noted prior to the hearing (see its document with protocol number Γ/ΕΙΣ/6019/13-04-2022) that it conducts telephone calls through its external partners to inform consumers about its products and services.  
In compliance with the applicable legislative framework and the decisions of the Authority, it has established specific procedures for conducting the aforementioned outbound calls, which are described in detail in the document with protocol number Γ/ΕΙΣ/8221/17-10-2018 submitted to the Authority. Elpedison specifically highlights the difficulties created by the implementation of Law 3471/2006, which it states have also been recognized by the Authority in various decisions. Call Experts submitted its memorandum No. G/EIS/10817/07-10-2022, in which it briefly states the following:

Call Experts strictly adheres to the applicable legislation, and in this case, the proper monitoring of registered phone numbers in the Registry, and continuously makes every possible effort to comply with its obligations, taking into account that it has taken organizational measures to ensure the guarantees required for its compliance with the legality conditions during the execution of promotional and other actions, possessing not only appropriate and documented procedures according to international standards (ISO), but also having provided written and detailed instructions to its staff for the strict adherence to the procedure for maintaining the Registry. The company collaborates with Elpedison as a "Data Processor" based on a contract - which is attached - for the purpose of expanding Elpedison's customer base through the promotion and sale of products via sales channels in the sales area, with the aim of entering into supply contracts with selected customers. For this purpose, Call Experts makes calls based on a "cold list" and/or "warm list" (as these lists are widely known in the relevant sector).

A "cold list" is defined as a list consisting exclusively of phone numbers that is created automatically based on the so-called "random number allocation spectrum." 

A "warm list" is defined as the database list, usually of the existing customer base of the data controller, which contains, in addition to the customer's subscriber phone number, other details of the latter, such as name, postal address, and email address. This list is transmitted by the data controller to the call center in order for the latter to make phone calls to the aforementioned customers - subscribers in the name and on behalf of the data controller for the promotion of the latter's products/services according to the explicit instructions and directives provided each time.

The company describes in detail the process it follows for calls based on a "cold list," according to which the final list (electronic file) of phone numbers to which calls are to be made is created after first excluding, through an automated process, the phone numbers to which promotional calls cannot be made according to the applicable legislation and includes:  
I) An electronic file with the phone numbers that are
19

registered in the Registry. This file is obtained by the Company electronically (usually during the first 10 days of each month for the previous month) from all major telecommunications providers as well as from the company Elpedison on a monthly basis.  
II) Electronic file with the phone numbers registered in the Objections Registry maintained by Call Experts. After the above process excludes the phone numbers, the electronic file with the remaining phone numbers from the selected numbering range is imported into the dialer, which then begins to call these numbers. The dialer transmits the calls made in this manner to the employees of Call Experts responsible for the promotional activity (sales). The employee first informs the called party of their name, from which company they are calling (Call Experts), as well as that they are calling on behalf of the company Elpedison. There is no concealment of the calling numbers. Personal data is collected if the called party expresses interest in the offer and the negotiation process for the conclusion of a contract is to begin. Call Experts also states that it is audited regarding the proper implementation of the "opt-out" registry and the other rights of the data subjects by Elpedison. CONSIDERED ACCORDING TO THE LAW

1. The issue of telephone calls for the purposes of direct promotion of products or services and for any kind of advertising purposes is regulated by Article 11 of Law 3471/2006, which introduces regulations regarding unsolicited communications (see paragraphs 1 and 2). Specifically, Article 11 paragraph 1 of Law 3471/2006 states that: “The use of automatic calling systems, particularly using facsimile devices (fax) or electronic mail, and generally the making of unsolicited communications by any means of electronic communication, without human intervention, for the purposes of direct commercial promotion of products or services and for any kind of advertising purposes, is permitted only if the subscriber has expressly consented in advance,” while paragraph 2 of the same article states that: “The making of unsolicited communications with human intervention (calls) for the above purposes is not permitted, provided that the subscriber has declared to the provider of the publicly available service that they do not wish to receive such calls in general. The provider is obliged to register these declarations free of charge in a special subscriber list, which is available to any interested party.” Consequently, telephone calls with human intervention, in light of the above purposes, are permitted unless the called party has declared that they do not wish to receive them (opt-out system). Advertisers, when conducting telephone promotional activities with human intervention, are obliged to obtain updated copies of the registries of Article 11 of Law 3471/2006 from all providers and to ensure that they have available the declarations of subscribers that have been made up to thirty days prior to the making of the telephone call (see also the Authority's Decisions numbered 62-67/2016).

2. Furthermore, the telephone number of a natural person constitutes personal data, as it can serve as an element of indirect identification of its holder (cf. Article 4 paragraph 1 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, hereinafter GDPR), allowing communication with them. According to Opinion 4/2007 of the Article 29 Working Party of the EU regarding the concept of personal data, especially in the operation of electronic services, elements of indirect identification can sufficiently distinguish an individual from others within a specific set, even if their name has not been verified.

3. The conduct of telephone calls for the purpose of promoting products and services is primarily regulated by Article 11 of Law 3471/2006. It is noted, however, that Article 3 paragraph 2 of this law clarifies that “Law 2472/1997, as in force, and the executive laws of Article 19 of the Constitution, as in force, apply to any issue related to the provision of electronic communications services that is not specifically regulated by this law.” Furthermore, after the implementation of the GDPR, any reference to Law 2472/1997, which was issued in compliance with Directive 95/46/EC,
29

is understood as a reference to the GDPR (see also Article 94(1) GDPR). Therefore, for any issue related to the provision of electronic communications services to subscribers or users who are natural persons and which is not specifically regulated in Law 3471/2006, the GDPR applies (see also Article 95 GDPR as well as Recital 173, and Opinion 5/2019 of the EDPS regarding the interaction between the Directive on privacy in electronic communications and the GDPR, particularly concerning the competence, duties, and powers of data protection authorities).

4. Article 4(7) of the GDPR defines the data controller as “the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data…”. The processor is defined in the following paragraph of the same article as “the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which processes personal data on behalf of the controller.”

5. Article 28 of the GDPR, which regulates matters concerning the processor, specifically states in paragraph 1 that when processing is to be carried out on behalf of a controller, the controller shall use only processors providing sufficient guarantees to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures in such a manner that processing will meet the requirements of the regulation and ensure the protection of the rights of the data subject. Paragraph 3 of the same article stipulates that processing by the processor shall be governed by a contract or other legal act under Union or Member State law, which binds the processor to the controller and sets out the subject matter and duration of the processing, the nature and purpose of the processing, the type of personal data and categories of data subjects, and the obligations and rights of the controller. This contract or other legal act specifically provides, among other things, that the processor processes personal data only on documented instructions from the controller and takes all necessary measures pursuant to Article 32 of the GDPR.

6. Article 29 of the GDPR states that “The processor and any person acting under the authority of the controller or of the processor, who has access to personal data, shall process those data only on instructions from the controller, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law.” This provision imposes an obligation on the processor to process data only on the instructions of the controller, unless a law provides otherwise. Therefore, a breach of this provision by processors constitutes a violation of the GDPR on their part.

7. Article 32 of the GDPR provides, among other things, that both the controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, taking into account the state of the art, the cost of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons. The controller and the processor shall take measures to ensure that any natural person acting under the authority of the controller or the processor who has access to personal data processes them only on instructions from the controller. From these provisions, it follows that the responsibility for maintaining appropriate security measures lies with both the controller and the processor, and thus, the responsibility for a breach of security measures should be shared and appropriately allocated.

8. In the Guidelines of the EDPS 07/2020 regarding the concepts of the controller and the processor in the GDPR, it is stated (Recital 127) that the level of instructions provided by the controller to the processor regarding...
31

The measures to be taken depend on the specific circumstances. In certain cases, the data controller may provide a clear and detailed description of the security measures that must be implemented. In other cases, the data controller may outline the minimum security objectives that must be achieved, while simultaneously requesting the processor to propose the implementation of specific security measures. In any case, the data controller must provide the processor with a description of the processing activities and the security objectives (based on the risk assessment of the data controller), as well as the approval of the measures proposed by the processor.

9. In paragraph 10 of Article 28, it is stated that "Subject to Articles 82, 83, and 84, if the processor determines the purposes and means of processing in violation of this regulation, the processor shall be considered a data controller for that specific processing." Therefore, in cases where a processor, even within the broader context of its cooperation with a data controller, engages in processing activities for which it does not have documented instructions (either specific or general) from the data controller, it must be considered as the data controller, as it determines the purpose and means of those activities.

10. From the file's elements, it appears that Elpedison, through a contract, assigns collaborating call center companies to conduct advertising calls to promote its own products and services. Through the contracts, printed instructions, and other directives provided to these companies, Elpedison sets a series of specifications that define the operational framework of each of its partners with the aim of meeting the requirements of the GDPR and Law 3471/2006. Elpedison fully determines the purpose of the processing, and thus its objective, while also defining the key characteristics for the means of processing. As explicitly stated in Elpedison's instructions to the collaborating companies (see page 8 of the instruction document), "The contact list with the details of individuals called by Elpedison's partners is either sent by Elpedison to the partner or generated based on a random numbering range." Consequently, Elpedison is the data controller for both calls to a "cold list" and to targeted lists ("warm list"). Specifically, the following activities of personal data processing and telephone calls for commercial promotion to subscribers, which largely include natural persons, are identified and examined:

i. Calls from companies collaborating with Elpedison to a random numbering range, as selected based on criteria from Elpedison ("cold list"). For these calls, the legal basis for processing, taking into account Article 11 paragraphs 1 and 2 of Law 3471/2006, may be Article 6 paragraph 1 (f) of the GDPR ("processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, especially if the data subject is a child"), to the extent that it is ensured that numbers that have been registered in the Registry are not called and that the principles of Article 5 of the GDPR and the other conditions set by the Regulation are met, such as in relation to the satisfaction of the principle of transparency (see in particular Article 14 of the GDPR) and the satisfaction of the rights of the called data subjects.

ii. Targeted calls to numbers that have been pre-selected based on various criteria, such as due to the consumer's desire, due to a "call back" note, etc. Specific cases of this category of calls include the following actions:  
a. Calls based on lists provided by Elpedison ("warm list") e.g.
33

customer lists for outreach.  
b. Calls that arise after a request from the called subscriber, such as when they request a callback.  
c. Calls to numbers after direct collection actions by the subscribers of their data to be called, such as for example through the website "fthinoreyma.gr".  
For these calls, the legal basis for processing, taking into account Article 11 paragraphs 1 and 2 of Law 3471/2006, may be:  
For case a, either the aforementioned Article 6 paragraph 1 (f) of the GDPR, such as for calls to existing customers of the data controller, provided that they have not expressed opposition (either generally through the Registry or specifically), or Article 6 paragraph 1 (a) of the GDPR (consent). Calls based on consent may also be made to subscribers who have registered in the Registry of Article 11 paragraph 2 of Law 3471/2006.  
For case b, the legal basis that may be more easily applicable in the case of a request from the called subscriber is their consent, while specifically in cases where the called subscriber has registered in the Registry, consent is the only legal basis for them to be called, even in the case of a callback.  
For case c, the first stage of the processing activity involves the collection of data directly from the data subject (the subscriber to be called) through their own actions; therefore, the most appropriate legal basis may again be consent, without excluding the legal basis of processing that is necessary to take measures at the request of the data subject prior to the conclusion of a contract (Article 6 paragraph 1 (b) of the GDPR).  
It goes without saying that in all cases, the other conditions provided for in the GDPR must be met, both in relation to the fundamental principles of Article 5 (especially the satisfaction of the principle of transparency and the requirements of Article 14 of the GDPR) and in relation to the requirements of lawful consent, as defined in Articles 4 paragraph 11 and 7 of the GDPR. It is particularly emphasized that in cases of consent, the data controller must be able to demonstrate that the data subject consented to the processing activity. Specifically, the data controller must obtain consent in such a way that it can be securely verified that the specific data subject has taken a positive action of declaration. Therefore, a note in an information system entered by an employee of the controller or processor cannot be considered valid consent, as there is insufficient evidence to prove that the specific subscriber, a natural person, requested the call. Furthermore, the lack of response from a subscriber to a call directed at them cannot be considered valid consent when the initial call was not made on the legal basis of consent. Finally, for the application of the legal basis of Article 6 paragraph 1 (b), the data controller must be certain that the request comes from the actual subscriber and not from a third party; therefore, they must implement measures to ensure that the specific subscriber (natural person) requested the call during the pre-contractual stage. These measures must be commensurate with the case of proving consent.

The data controller is responsible, based on Article 12 of the GDPR, for fulfilling the rights of the data subjects. The data processors may engage in such activities for the specific purpose only after receiving explicit instructions from the data controller. Regarding the register of objections, it is noted that Elpedison has included a specific term in the instruction document that binds the collaborating call center companies. In this (see section 8.1, page 10), it explicitly states that "Especially regarding the expression of objection/objection/desire not to retract by the data subject at the time of the call concerning calls similar to the one being made to the data subject, the staff of the partner must record this desire along with the identification and contact details of the person and inform Elpedison through a procedure specified (e.g., via email, via CRM) regarding this request of the person." Therefore, the maintenance of a special register of objections internally by each call center company, when it is not the data controller, does not meet the requirements of Article 21 of the GDPR. This is because a called natural person who has expressed an objection to receiving calls for the promotion of Elpedison may be called again by another call center company for the promotion of Elpedison. Finally, if a call center company maintains such an internal register of objections for calls it carries out for multiple clients or for calls it carries out for a client without explicit instructions from that client, then it is considered the data controller and is liable for unlawful processing of personal data.

It is noted that the processing of a subscriber's (natural person's) data that occurs after the acceptance stage of an offer, which is of course included in the contracts between Elpedison and the call center companies, is not the subject of examination in this case.

11. The data controller and the data processor undertake to implement appropriate measures to ensure an adequate level of security against risks. The responsibility for the failure to implement appropriate measures, in violation of Articles 28, 29, and 32 of the GDPR, should be appropriately allocated between the data processor and the data controller. Therefore, in cases where an error occurs in the implementation of security measures by the data processor, who has specified the general measures set by the data controller, the data processor bears a greater burden for the implementation of those security measures. However, if it is found that the data processor violates the obligations imposed on it by...

In relation to the contract, if the processor processes data beyond or in violation of the instructions of the data controller, then a violation of Article 29 of the GDPR arises from the processor.

12. Regarding the technical and organizational measures that the controller and the processor must take in order to ensure an appropriate level of security against risks, it is noted that these measures cannot always prevent every potential incident of data security breach. However, it is necessary, after any failure of these measures, to follow up with an assessment and evaluation of their effectiveness, as an essential element for maintaining the security of the processing. Consequently, a primary step is the documentation of the incident, so that it is recorded and the compliance of the data controller with the principles of Article 5 of the GDPR is evidenced, as required by the principle of accountability. Such failures must be recorded, and to the extent that they concern an incident of security breach (of the requirements of confidentiality, integrity, or availability), the provisions of Article 33 of the GDPR regarding the notification of the incident to the supervisory authority also apply. Moreover, if such an incident is detected by the processor, it is their responsibility to promptly inform the data controller about the incident, without even conducting a risk assessment of the incident.

13. The responsibility of Elpedison as the data controller in relation to the processors is, at the initial stage, to provide appropriate tools, guidelines, and instructions so that only lawful calls and only lawful activities of personal data processing are carried out. In the examined cases, it was found that within the framework of these obligations, Elpedison collects the Registers from the providers at regular intervals, consolidates them, and sends them to the collaborating call center companies that carry out the processing. At the same time, it maintains a special register of those who have objected to receiving phone calls (based on Article 21 of the GDPR), which is also sent to the collaborating companies. It is determined that this activity is well-designed, but it remains to be checked how it has been implemented in each of the cases of the examined complaints. This is because, at the second stage, Elpedison's responsibility also concerns the adequacy of the control and supervision of the processors as well as the actions taken by the company itself as soon as it becomes aware of the complaints, in order to check the effectiveness of its procedures and the operation of the collaborating companies and their application of the instructions provided to them, to identify areas where these procedures can be improved or instances where the collaborating companies violate their obligations, and in case a violation is found, to take the necessary measures to avoid similar incidents in the future.

17. Regarding Call Experts IKE:
A key finding of the Authority is the delay in integrating the Registry into its systems. It is noted that it cannot be accepted that this process requires an exceptionally long time. It is a repetitive process, which concerns only telephone numbers, although in a quite large number, meaning a single data point with a very specific format, which can be easily verified. It must be accepted that some additional time is required for the verification of the Registry records obtained from the providers and for the homogenization of the files, as the format in which the Registry files are received from each provider differs; however, the Authority believes that such processes can now be easily automated, as after more than a decade of operating the Registry, issues of homogenization and formats should be recognized and resolved promptly, primarily in an automated manner and with minimal human intervention. Delays beyond one working day are not reasonable, while when a weekend intervenes, the company has the opportunity to complete these processes even more easily. This action should be the company's top priority, as it relates to the satisfaction of citizens' rights and constitutes a legal obligation of the data controller, which has been assigned through contracts to the Call Center companies. Therefore, it may be considered that in the telephone calls in cases C.B.4 and C.B.5 (which occurred one working day after the receipt of the Registry file) there is no violation by Call Experts, while in the cases of the five (5) cases C.B.6 (11 days delay in implementing the Registry), C.B.8 (6 days), C.B.9 (3 days while a weekend intervenes), C.B.10 (4 days while a weekend intervenes), and C.B.11 (5 days while a weekend intervenes) a violation of Article 32 of the GDPR arises. Furthermore, to avoid errors, the automatic system for conducting and
41

The call routing system (dialer) must be configured to reject calls before they even begin. It is not acceptable to perform such a check after the call routing has started, as there is a possibility of calling a subscriber who has objected. Therefore, in the case of automated calls, numbers should be rejected before they are loaded into the dialer (cases G.B.1 and D.1). For cases of manually dialed calls, which would only be permitted in exceptional circumstances, appropriate measures should be in place to ensure that before the call is made, it is verified that the number is not listed in the Registry or the objections registry, or that there is specific consent (case G.B.2). Consequently, from these three (3) specific cases of complaints, a systemic problem arises in the applications of Call Experts and a violation of Article 32 of the GDPR by the data processor.

For the other two cases of complaints (G.B.3, G.B.7), Call Experts states that a human error (in the first of these) and a technical error (in the second) occurred. However, it does not provide any relevant documented evidence of the error, nor are any internal communications submitted that could be considered as the minimum means of indication for the error. In fact, even in the case of the license upgrade, it is not specified which licenses these were and how they affected the processing. Furthermore, it is found that in no case has such an error been reported to the Authority or that an internal process was initiated by the data processor to inform the data controller regarding the error, as required by the data processor under Article 33(2) of the GDPR. Therefore, the claims of Call Experts regarding the justification of the errors are dismissed as unfounded and unproven, and thus violations of Article 32 of the GDPR arise.

18. Regarding Zitatel, it should be noted that it did not submit a memorandum of opinions, despite the presence of its representatives during the hearing, based on what has been communicated to the Authority by Elpedison, the following is established:

Therefore, based on the reasoning developed above, the related claims of the company are dismissed as unfounded and unproven. Consequently, in the above cases, a violation of Article 32 of the GDPR is established.

In the case of complaint D.2, it arises, as Elpedison states, that there was negligent behavior by Zitatel due to the failure to provide information to the data controller. Therefore, since the investigation process of a complaint is a necessary stage for evaluating the security measures taken, a violation of Article 32 of the GDPR also arises for which Zitatel is responsible.

19. Regarding the operation of the website fthinoreyma.gr, the collection of personal data through it, and the subsequent conduct of telephone calls with "consent," it is established that concerning the two complaints under investigation (G.D.5-G.D.6), Zitatel was the data controller, as it was an action that was implemented for the promotion of Elpedison's products and services, but beyond the instructions of Elpedison. This specific activity was also carried out in violation of the principle of transparency and the principle of legality of Article 5(1)(a) of the GDPR, as at the time of the complaints, the information on the website indicated a different company as the data controller and there was no information regarding the recipients (e.g., Zitatel). Furthermore, the collection of telephone numbers through their declaration in a website form does not meet the requirements of valid consent. This is because the data controller is not in a position to prove that the specific data subject who is a subscriber or at least a user of the telephone number consented to the

The processing action, that is, it was this individual and not a third party who registered their phone number on the website. The Authority reminds that it has issued guideline 2/2010 regarding electronic consent within the framework of Article 11 of Law 3471/2006, which provides guidance on the minimum measures of proof of consent concerning the collection of phone numbers and other electronic communication data. Consequently, statements and other personal data obtained by Zitatel through the aforementioned website have not been collected lawfully and must additionally be deleted.

It is also noted that even after the new agreement between Elpedison and Zitatel, based on which the operation of the website fthinoreyma.gr will be conducted on behalf of Elpedison, it is not clear that the risk of subscriber data (e.g., phone number) being entered into the online form by third parties has been addressed. Therefore, the legality of the requests and other personal data obtained through the said website must be evaluated, even after the agreement with Elpedison, and in case the above risk has not been addressed, this data must also be deleted.

21. Regarding PLEGMAT:  
As mentioned above, in cases of "retraction" or calls made at the request of the called party, the legal basis for the processing of personal data for the implementation of the call appears to be consent. Therefore, the information to prove this must provide certainty that the specific natural person/subscriber performed the action considered as consent within the meaning of Article 4, point 11 of the GDPR. For this purpose, a simple note in the company's systems is not sufficient, and in any case, the Registry must be checked before making any retraction for which there is no consent. Consequently, in the four (4) complaint cases G.E.1, G.E.2, G.E.3, and G.E.7, it is found that PLEGMAT, due to the failure to take adequate measures to avoid unlawful calls, violated Article 32 of the GDPR.

44

In the complaint cases G.E.4-6, PLEGMAT exclusively cites a systemic error due to a technical problem in its interface with its provider and Med Nautilus. Although requested by the Authority, no relevant documented evidence of the error was provided, nor was it mentioned that a data breach incident management process was initiated with the timely notification of the data controller, as required by Article 33, paragraph 2 of the GDPR. In any case, such an incident could have been addressed immediately using the "local dialer" infrastructure, but based on PLEGMAT's reports, this specific infrastructure operated, at least for some hours and indeed on different days, without the updated Registries, in violation of the obligations of the processor arising from Article 32 of the GDPR.  
22. It is also noted that both Call Experts and PLEGMAT state that they maintain an internal registry of objections, based on which they do not call again those subscribers who complain during the call. It is emphasized that, as analyzed above, "Call Center" companies are not authorized to maintain their own objections file, and this processing is outside the scope of the contract with the data controller, in violation of Article 29 of the GDPR. Conversely, they are obliged, as soon as a called subscriber informs them that they wish to exercise their right to object, to record this statement and immediately transfer this information to the data controller, so that it can be included in the objections file that they maintain, as stipulated in the contract with Elpedison. In the event that the aforementioned two Call Center companies still maintain such a registry, they must immediately forward it to the data controller (and in cases involving multiple controllers, to each respective controller) and delete it. However, the Authority deems that this activity was carried out without intent and was, albeit inadequately, in the interest of the called subscribers; therefore, no further corrective measures are required beyond those mentioned above.

23. For all complaints of category C, it is crucial to examine the role of Elpedison as the data processor. As mentioned, the Authority accepts that Elpedison has appropriate policies and procedures regarding the conduct of telephone calls for the promotion of its products and services, which it has communicated to the companies collaborating with it. At the same time, as indicated in its memorandum, it has taken steps to control the organizational and technical measures followed by some of these companies, as evidenced by the detailed audit reports submitted to the Authority. However, it is found that the process of reviewing the complaints forwarded by the Authority to Elpedison is inadequate and does not include a thorough investigation of the causes that led to an unlawful call. Elpedison, like any data processor, should not rely solely on the assurances received from the respective data processors but should identify the source of each error as soon as it is detected and take appropriate corrective measures. In this way, calls that are conducted "manually" and with human error, without checking the Registry, could have been avoided. Additionally, the failure to maintain an internal registry of objections in each company could have possibly been identified. Finally, the delay of a company in integrating the Registry would certainly have been identified, which should have led to the immediate adoption of relevant measures. It is also noted that, especially when the number of promotional calls is large, as reported by all companies, Elpedison, as the data processor, must take proactive control measures regarding the measures and procedures implemented by the data processors, such as conducting audits on the collaborating companies. These audits should not be limited to organizational and technical issues but, where the number of calls is large, in the order of thousands per day, should include a review of the calls made over a satisfactory period of time. Indeed, given that Elpedison already has the monthly Registry and that the data processors are required to maintain records of outgoing calls, such a review, which consists of automated cross-referencing of files with numbers (from the Registry and the outgoing call records), considering the current level of technology, is simple to implement and can be repeated periodically on a sufficient sample of calls, within a period not exceeding one year. Therefore, it is determined that Elpedison has not taken the appropriate measures, based on Article 32 of the GDPR, for the control of the data processors.
