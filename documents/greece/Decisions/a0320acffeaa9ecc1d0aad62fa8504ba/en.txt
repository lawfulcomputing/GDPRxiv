Ο Πρόεδρος της Αρχής, ως μονοπρόσωπο όργανο κατά το άρθρο 17 παρ. 1 του ν. 4624/2019  
(ΦΕΚ Α΄ 137), στο πλαίσιο των αρμοδιοτήτων που προβλέπονται στα άρθρα 4 παρ. 3 και 10  
παρ. 4 του Κανονισμού Λειτουργίας της Αρχής (ΦΕΚ Β΄879/25.02.2022), συνεδρίασε μέσω  
τηλεδιάσκεψης την 26η-10-2023 προκειμένου να εξετάσει την υπόθεση που αναφέρεται  
κατωτέρω στο ιστορικό της παρούσας απόφασης. Παρούσα χωρίς δικαίωμα ψήφου ήταν η

Υποβλήθηκε ενώπιον της Αρχής Προστασίας Δεδομένων η υπ’ αριθμ. πρωτ.  
Γ/ΕΙΣ/12619/16-12-2022 καταγγελία του Α, με την οποία ο τελευταίος καταγγέλλει τη μη  
ανταπόκριση του καταγγελλόμενου φροντιστηρίου με την επωνυμία «ΓΡΙΒΑΣ ΧΑΡΑΛΑΜΠΟΣ  
– ΜΟΥΤΖΟΥΡΗ ΑΝΑΣΤΑΣΙΑ Ο.Ε.» διά του ομορρύθμου εταίρου του, Β, στο δικαίωμα  
πρόσβασης σε προσωπικά δεδομένα του ανήλικου τέκνου του που άσκησε με γραπτά  
ηλεκτρονικά μηνύματα (sms). Ειδικότερα, στις 03-05-2022 ο καταγγέλλων απέστειλε στον  
προαναφερόμενο ομόρρυθμο εταίρο, Β, κατόπιν σχετικής τηλεφωνικής επικοινωνίας που  
είχε μαζί του, ηλεκτρονικό μήνυμα (sms) ζητώντας τις αποδείξεις παροχής υπηρεσιών που

ανήλικο τέκνο του. Ενόψει δε του ότι δεν ικανοποιήθηκε, κατά τους ισχυρισμούς του, το ως  
άνω αίτημα, ο καταγγέλλων επανήλθε με τα από 09-12-2022 και 14-12-2022 ηλεκτρονικά

εκπαιδευτική πορεία του ανήλικου τέκνου του. Σύμφωνα δε με τα καταγγελλόμενα, και  
παρά το ότι ο Β διαβεβαίωσε με ηλεκτρονικό του μήνυμα τον καταγγέλλοντα περί του ότι  
έχει αποστείλει τα ζητηθέντα στοιχεία, ο τελευταίος δεν τα έχει παραλάβει.

CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW  

1. Since the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679 – hereinafter, GDPR) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A’ 137) indicate that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Article 5 par. 1 of the GDPR sets out the principles that must govern a processing operation. According to the principle of accountability introduced by this article, it is stated in paragraph 2 that the data controller "bears the responsibility and is in a position to demonstrate compliance with paragraph 1 ('accountability')." As the Authority has ruled,2 the GDPR adopted a new compliance model, the central point of which is the principle of accountability, under which the data controller is obliged to design, implement, and generally take the necessary measures and policies to ensure that the processing of data is in accordance with the relevant legislative provisions. Furthermore, the data controller is burdened with the further duty to demonstrate himself and at any time his compliance with the principles of Article 5 par. 1 GDPR.

2. Since according to Article 15 paragraphs 1, 3, and 4 of the GDPR "1. The data subject has the right to obtain from the data controller confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning them is being processed and, if that is the case, the right of access to the personal data and to the following information: a) the purposes of the processing, b) the relevant categories of personal data, c) the recipients or categories of recipients to whom the personal data have been or will be disclosed, in particular, recipients in third countries or international organizations, d) where possible, the period for which the personal data will be stored or, when that is not possible, the criteria used to determine that period, e) the existence of the right to request from the data controller the rectification or erasure of personal data or the restriction of processing of personal data concerning the data subject or the right to object to such processing, f) the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, g) when the personal data are not collected from the data subject, any available information as to their source, h) the existence of automated decision-making, including profiling, referred to in Article 22 paragraphs 1 and 4 and, at least in those cases, significant information about the logic involved, as well as the significance and the envisaged consequences of such processing for the data subject. 2. […] 3. The data controller shall provide a copy of the personal data undergoing processing. […] If the data subject submits the request by electronic means and unless the data subject requests otherwise, the information shall be provided in a commonly used electronic format. 4. The right to obtain a copy referred to in paragraph 3 shall not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others." These provisions establish the right of access of the data subject to their personal data. In the context of this right, the data subject must, on the one hand, have access to personal data that has been collected and concerns them, in order to be informed and to verify the accuracy and nature of the processing of their data and to verify the lawfulness of the processing, and on the other hand, be able to exercise this right easily and at reasonable intervals. The data controller must provide remote access to a secure system through which the data subject can gain direct access to the data concerning them. Furthermore, the Authority consistently accepts that the data subject has the right to know whether personal data concerning them is being processed, as well as to gain knowledge of it, without the need to invoke a legitimate interest since this is inherent and constitutes the basis of the right of access, that is, the right of the data subject to be informed of information concerning them that has been recorded in a file maintained by the data controller, so that the fundamental principle of law for the protection of personal data, which consists of the transparency of processing as a prerequisite for any further control by the data subject of its lawfulness, is realized. Similarly, there is no requirement to invoke the reasons for which the data subject wishes to exercise the right of access. Furthermore, the obligation to satisfy the right of access is universal, meaning it concerns all information relating to the data subject and, additionally, does not depend on the invocation of the reasons for exercising the right. Consequently, the satisfaction of the right does not depend on a prior assessment by the data controller as to whether the exercise of the right is justified or not.  
Moreover, by proper interpretation and application of paragraph 1 of Article 15 of the GDPR, the data controller is not exempted from its obligation to provide information.

3. Because according to Article 12 of the GDPR "1. The data controller takes appropriate measures to provide the data subject […] with any communication in the context of Articles 15 […] 2. The data controller facilitates the exercise of the rights of data subjects provided for in Articles 15 […] 3. 4. Because according to the provision of Article 31 of the GDPR it is provided that "The data controller and the processor and, where applicable, their representatives cooperate, upon request, with the supervisory authority in the performance of its tasks."  
5. Because Article 1510 of the Civil Code regarding parental care stipulates, among other things, that "The care for the minor child is a duty and a right of the parents (Parental Care), which they exercise jointly and equally. Parental care includes the custody of the person, the management of the property, and the representation of the child in any case or legal transaction or litigation concerning the person or property of the child…". Furthermore, Article 1513 of the Civil Code stipulates, among other things, the following: "In cases of divorce or annulment of marriage or dissolution or annulment of a cohabitation agreement or cessation of cohabitation of spouses or parties to a cohabitation agreement and provided that both parents live, they continue to exercise parental care jointly and equally. The parent with whom the child resides undertakes the actions provided for in the first paragraph of Article 1516, after prior notification of the other parent."  
6. Because according to established case law of the Authority, as mentioned above, the person exercising parental care for their minor child (Articles 128 and 1510 of the Civil Code) has as a general rule the legal right of access to the data concerning their minor child, unless otherwise provided by a court decision (such as a decision designating the other parent as the one exercising parental care or a decision prohibiting contact with the child). Specifically, in the case under consideration, the complainant, as the one exercising parental care for their minor child jointly and equally with their spouse, has the right of access to the data of the latter, namely the requested tax documents that have been issued as well as other information regarding the educational progress of their minor child, as derived from the combination of the provisions of Articles 1510 and 1513 of the Civil Code and Article 15 of the GDPR, without the need for further demonstration of a specific legal interest on their part. This is because the complainant, as the one exercising parental care for their minor child, is identified with the subject of the specific data. Therefore, the entity being reported, as the data controller, is obliged, within the framework of providing services concerning them, to satisfy through their legal representative the relevant access rights exercised by the father and the one exercising parental care for the minor child.  
7. Because in this case, the complainant exercised the right of access on behalf of their minor child before the reported data controller, in their capacity as the one exercising parental care, as stated above, which does not appear to have been revoked. Specifically, the complainant requested from the reported tutoring center and specifically from the general partner, and thus the legal representative of it, B, to gain access to tax documents concerning the provision of services to their minor child as well as information regarding

Moreover, the complainant exercised the contested right of access in a clear manner through written messages, including full details of both himself and his child as a student of the reported tutoring center. Furthermore, as previously stated in paragraph 2 of this document with reference to the case law of the Authority, the satisfaction of the right of access does not require the invocation of a legal interest, as the legal interest (even if moral) of the subject to be informed of information concerning them, which has been recorded in a file maintained by the data controller, is considered given, so that the fundamental principle of law for the protection of personal data, which consists of the transparency of processing as a prerequisite for any further control of its legality by the data subject, is realized. In any case, the exercise of the right of access is not required to take place in a specific form or ceremoniously, e.g., by invoking the provisions of the GDPR or by explicitly referring to the exercise of the right.  
However, the above requests, which were made in writing on 03-05-2022 as well as on 09-12-2022 and 14-12-2022 via SMS sent to B's mobile phone, as extensively mentioned above, do not appear from the file's evidence to have been satisfied by the data controller, as there is no response regarding them. Therefore, according to what is stated in paragraphs 2 and 3, the reported party was obliged to respond through its legal representative and indeed in a timely manner by sending the requested details of the minor child that it possessed and/or at least informing him of the fact that it does not retain any data in its file concerning the minor child, namely for the school year 2022-2023. However, from the actual circumstances and the evidence presented to the Authority, it was not proven that the reported party responded appropriately to the complainant.  
Given these facts, since it is not proven that the right of access was satisfied, and indeed within the deadlines provided by the GDPR, or the justified non-satisfaction thereof as previously indicated, a violation of Article 15 of the GDPR in conjunction with the provisions of Article 12 paragraphs 3 and 4 of the GDPR is established.  
8. Furthermore, from the above factual circumstances, it appears that the data controller showed absolutely no willingness to cooperate with the Authority by providing clarifications regarding the complaint concerning it. Specifically, it ignored and did not take care to respond clearly to the specific questions posed for the first time by the Authority in its letter no. Γ/ΕΞΕ/463/23-02-2023, while the reported party sent no response to the Authority's letter no. Γ/ΕΞΕ/1449/07-06-2023. Only about three (3) months after receiving the second aforementioned letter and after the Authority sent another reminder (see letter no. Γ/ΕΞΕ/2246/06-09-2023), it responded that it had sent the corresponding receipts without attaching any document that would prove the above claim and without providing any other response to the questions initially posed by the Authority for the crystallization of the factual circumstances of the case under examination. In the question posed by the Authority, in the context of exercising its investigative and auditing powers, regarding the name under which the relevant tax documents related to the provision of services to the complainant's minor child were issued and the data contained therein, the reported tutoring center provided no response. In this way, the reported party violated, as the data controller, the obligation arising from the aforementioned Article 31 of the GDPR, which is
