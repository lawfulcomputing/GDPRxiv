With complaint No. G/EIS/5213/09-08-2021 submitted to the Authority, A (hereinafter "the complainant"), who worked as a civil engineer at the anonymous company named "Port Authority X" (hereinafter "OLX SA" or "the complained party") under an indefinite-term employment contract, full-time since 04-10-2004, and who had been promoted to the position of Head of the Projects Department since 2007, complains against OLX SA for violation of the data subject's rights (right to object) and unlawful processing of his personal data. CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW

1. Since, according to the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter, GDPR) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, this Law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Specifically, from the provisions of Articles 57, paragraph 1, subparagraph f of the GDPR and 13, paragraph 1, subparagraph z of Law 4624/2019, it follows that the Authority has the competence to address the said complaint against OLX S.A., as it concerns the processing of the complainant's personal data, which are included in a filing system as defined in Article 4, subparagraphs 2 and 6 of the GDPR, thus for processing falling under the regulatory scope of Articles 2, paragraph 1 of the GDPR and 2 of Law 4624/2019.  
2. Since according to Article 2, paragraph 1 of the GDPR "This Regulation applies to the, in whole or in part, automated processing of personal data, as well as to the non-automated processing of such data which are included or are intended to be included in a filing system."  
3. Since specifically, according to Article 4, subparagraph 1 of the GDPR "personal data means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, psychological, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person."  
4. Since, furthermore, according to Article 4, subparagraph 2 of the GDPR, the processing of personal data is "any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration,

the retrieval, the search for information, the use, the disclosure by transmission, the dissemination or any other form of provision, the correlation or combination, the restriction, the deletion or the destruction."  
5. Whereas, in Article 4(7) of the GDPR, the data controller is defined as "the natural or legal person, the public authority, the agency or another body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data; where the purposes and means of such processing are determined by Union law or the law of a Member State, the data controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be provided for by Union law or the law of a Member State."  
6. Whereas, according to Article 5(1) of the GDPR "Personal data shall be: a) processed lawfully and fairly in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject ('lawfulness, fairness and transparency'); b) collected for specified, legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes; further processing for archiving purposes in the public interest or for scientific or historical research purposes or for statistical purposes shall not be considered incompatible with the initial purposes according to Article 89(1) ('purpose limitation'); c) adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed ('data minimization'); d) accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; every reasonable step must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate, with respect to the purposes for which they are processed, are erased or rectified without delay ('accuracy'); e) kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed; personal data may be stored for longer periods, provided that the personal data will be processed solely for archiving purposes in the public interest, for scientific or historical research purposes or for statistical purposes, in accordance with Article 89(1) and provided that appropriate technical and organizational measures are applied as required by this regulation to safeguard the rights and freedoms of the data subject ('storage limitation'); f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures ('integrity and confidentiality').  
7. Whereas additionally, according to Article 6(1) of the GDPR "Processing shall be lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the following applies: (…) c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject (…) e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller; f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, in particular if the data subject is a child. The provision f) of the first paragraph does not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks." Therefore, processing carried out by public authorities cannot have as its legal basis the satisfaction of their legitimate interests as data controllers.  
8. Whereas according to Article 21 of the GDPR "1. The data subject shall have the right to object, at any time and on grounds relating to his or her particular situation, to processing of personal data concerning him or her which is based on Article 6(1)(e) or (f), including profiling based on those provisions. The data controller shall no longer process the personal data unless the data controller demonstrates compelling legitimate grounds for the processing which override the interests, rights and freedoms of the data subject, or for the establishment, exercise or defense of legal claims."

9. Whereas, according to Recital (47) of the GDPR, “The legitimate interests of the data controller, including those of a controller to whom personal data may be disclosed or third parties, may provide the legal basis for processing, provided that such interests do not override the interests or the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject, taking into account the reasonable expectations of data subjects based on their relationship with the controller. Such legitimate interest could, for example, exist when there is a relevant and appropriate relationship between the data subject and the controller, such as when the data subject is a customer of the controller or is in the service of the controller. In any case, the existence of a legitimate interest would require careful assessment, inter alia, as to whether the data subject, at the time and in the context of the collection of personal data, can reasonably expect that processing for that purpose may take place. In particular, the interests and fundamental rights of the data subject could override the interests of the controller when personal data are processed in situations where the data subject does not reasonably expect further processing of their data. Given that it is for the legislator to provide by law the legal basis for the processing of personal data by public authorities, such legal basis should not apply to processing by public authorities in the performance of their tasks. Processing of personal data, to the extent that it is strictly necessary for the purposes of preventing fraud, also constitutes a legitimate interest of the relevant controller. Processing of personal data for the purposes of direct marketing may be considered to be carried out for the sake of legitimate interest.”  
10. Whereas, according to Recital (69) of the GDPR, “When personal data can be lawfully processed because the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller or for legitimate interests of the controller or a third party, each data subject should nevertheless have the right to object to the processing of any personal data concerning them on grounds relating to their particular situation. It should be for the controller to demonstrate that their compelling legitimate interests override the interests or the fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject.”  
11. Whereas further, according to Article 5 of Law 4624/2019, “Public authorities are permitted to process personal data when the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller.” However, the Authority, in its Opinion 1/2020, noted regarding this provision, among other things, that: “[…] Article 5 of the law reiterates the legal basis of Article 6(1)(e) of the GDPR, does not introduce a new or supplementary legal basis into national law, nor does it exclude the application of the legal bases of Article 6(1) of the GDPR. Interpreted in this sense, the provision of Article 5 of Law 4624/2019 does not conflict with the GDPR, but violates the rule of Union law, according to which repetition of GDPR provisions in national legislation is not permitted.” Consequently, processing, when carried out by public authorities, is lawful when it is necessary for the fulfillment of a legal obligation, in accordance with the aforementioned Article 6(c) of the GDPR.

Since the complainant's claim that OLCH S.A.'s response did not satisfy the right to object exercised on 15-07-2020 with his aforementioned extrajudicial statement, in which he declared his opposition to any processing of his personal data, and requested to be informed whether the respondent processed his personal data voluntarily without his consent, as well as for what purpose such processing took place, simultaneously calling for him to sign a compliance statement, certifying that he would not engage in future processing of his personal data, is to be rejected, as OLCH S.A. timely responded on 04-08-2021 to the objection exercised by the complainant on 15-07-2021. In this response, it mentioned all the security measures taken during the processing of the complainant's personal data, as well as that it does not engage in further processing of his personal data except where necessary for the fulfillment of its legal obligations and for the defense of its legitimate interests within the context of their dispute.  
Furthermore, the aforementioned processing acts are carried out within the framework of the Organization's duties as a public entity (N.P.D.D.), pursuant to Circulars with protocol numbers DIDAD/Φ.34/35/οικ.14888/24-05-2013 and DIDAD/Φ.34/42/οικ.33906/16-12-2013, and are mandated by the principle of legality, according to which the Administration must revoke any illegal act, thereby restoring the legal order. Moreover, from all the submitted evidentiary documents and the claims of both parties, the following emerged:  
1. OLCH S.A. did not unlawfully obtain a copy of the complainant's criminal record, but after submitting a relevant request before the Prosecutor's Office of X (Criminal Record Department), the content of which was assessed by the competent prosecutorial authority (see also the decision of the Single-Member Court of Appeal of X-special procedure for labor disputes, with number …), which states that OLCH S.A. “(...) requested on 17-07-2020 from the competent Authority a copy of the plaintiff's criminal record, which was communicated to it by virtue of document number …”, resulting in the Authority being incompetent regarding this matter. Furthermore, in a similar manner, the Administration of OLCH S.A. lawfully obtained a copy of decision number 48/2016 from the Five-Member Court of Appeal of X (see also the submitted decisions with number … from the Single-Member Court of First Instance of Θ, which explicitly states that “it requested and received a copy of the criminal record, (...) as well as received a copy of decision number … from the Five-Member Court of Appeals for Felonies of X”, and … from the Single-Member Court of Appeal of X, which states that “The defendant had a legitimate interest in the criminal status of the plaintiff, given that the criminal conviction, especially for a felony, constituted an obstacle to appointment, according to Article 6 of the General Personnel Regulation”) (Government Gazette T. PRA.DI.T. No. 117/27-07-2020).  
2. OLCH S.A. submitted in a precautionary measures trial the sworn statement of employee B with number …, who testified under oath about factual incidents of which she had knowledge during the performance of her duties. Specifically, regarding the first issue of whether this employee lawfully obtained knowledge of this information, it should be noted that she lawfully obtained knowledge of the complainant's personal data under the direction of the Administration of OLCH S.A. for the purpose of fulfilling OLCH S.A.'s obligations regarding the verification of its employees' documents, pursuant to the aforementioned Circulars. Furthermore, regarding the second issue of whether this employee lawfully testified under oath in the context of a civil trial about what she learned during the performance of her duties as a witness in her sworn statement, this has already been assessed by a Competent Court, which evaluated this evidentiary means and freely assessed its legality as well as the validity of its content, without the Authority being competent to judge its legality in this case, according to Article 55, paragraph 3 of the GDPR, which states that “Supervisory authorities are not competent to oversee processing operations carried out by courts in the exercise of their judicial authority.”

3. Furthermore, OLCH S.A. unlawfully disclosed the criminal record of the complainant to the law firm Chiotelis and Partners, as this firm was collaborating with OLCH S.A. (see submitted private agreements for the provision of legal services), and the lawyers of this firm had represented the accused Organization in trials, defending its legal interests against the complainant. They are also bound by the Code of Lawyers to maintain confidentiality, both towards their client and regarding any personal data of third parties that came to their knowledge, with the possibility of disciplinary sanctions in case of violation of this duty. Regarding the issuance of the opinion dated 23-10-2020 on the question posed by OLCH S.A. concerning the possibility of terminating the complainant's employment contract, it emerges from all submitted documents and the claims of both parties that the signing lawyer, Panagiotis Chiotelis, was aware of the complaint filed by OLCH S.A. on 25-07-2014, the indictment drawn up on it, and the criminal record of the complainant. He mentions in his opinion the acts for which criminal prosecution was initiated, the penalty imposed on the complainant, the offense for which he was definitively convicted, as well as the penalty imposed on him, rejecting as unfounded the claim of the accused Organization that no copies of either the criminal record or the conviction decision were provided to the law firm Chiotelis and Partners, but rather a request was made for the drafting of an opinion regarding the possibility of terminating the complainant's employment contract, with OLCH S.A. providing the minimum background of the case. If such a claim were true, the signing lawyer would have mentioned it in the relevant opinion. However, the signing lawyer refers (by way of example) on the first page of the opinion to the complaint "with…", and recounts and cites the relevant indictment ("was charged based on the indictment"). Nevertheless, this form of processing was lawful, as it aimed to fulfill the legal duties and protect the legal interests of OLCH S.A., within the framework of the need to establish and support its legal claims, a purpose that could not be achieved by other milder means.

22

Specifically, this opinion was requested and drafted in the context of exercising the lawful right of OLCH S.A. (Law 2112/1920, Article 5, paragraph 1: "The employer may terminate the contract without observing any notice period if a complaint has been filed against the employee for a criminal act committed in the course of his service or if he has been charged with an offense in general, which is at least a misdemeanor"). Moreover, as can be seen from the annex to the employment contract – confidentiality clause – the complainant had already been informed as of 19-10-2020 that OLCH S.A. would not transmit or disclose personal data to third parties, unless it concerned: a) natural or legal persons to whom it has entrusted, partially or entirely, the processing of personal data, in accordance with applicable legislation, b) transmission required for the execution of the contract, and c) transmission required for compliance with a legal obligation of the Company.  
4. Furthermore, regarding the disclosure to all members of its Board of Directors, to employees who attended without voting rights at the Board Meeting of OLCH S.A., as well as to the employee who acted as secretary at this meeting, it is established that this took place lawfully, in support of the legal claims of OLCH S.A. against the complainant, within the framework of exercising the lawful rights of the employer (Law 2112/1920, Article 5, paragraph 1). Regarding the President of the Employees' Union of OLCH S.A., who attended to comment on the matter at the Board Meeting during which the decision to terminate the complainant's contract was made, she was informed about these issues by the complainant himself (see the minutes of the 40th Board Meeting of OLCH S.A., in which she states that she was informed by her colleague) and essentially attended to support the view regarding potential liabilities of the Board in the event that an employee is dismissed and subsequently acquitted.  
5. Additionally, the claim of the complainant that the opinion of the law firm Chiotelis and Partners, which collaborates with OLCH, contains more personal data than required, exceeding the principle of proportionality, is also rejected. This is because the personal data that were processed and referred to in this opinion were

suitable, relevant, and limited to what was absolutely necessary for achieving the intended purpose, which was to provide a thorough legal opinion on whether there were grounds for the termination of the employment contract due to a strong breach of trust in the employee (supporting the legal claims of OLCH S.A.), while adhering to the principle of data minimization, and this purpose could not be achieved - as previously mentioned - by other milder means.  
6. Finally, the complainant's assertion regarding the public posting of the decision of the Board of Directors of OLCH S.A. concerning the termination of his employment contract, that it was unlawfully published in the information system "DIAVGEIA," is similarly rejected. Specifically, apart from being a relevant obligation of the Administration (Article 76 paragraph 3 of Law 4727/2020 and Article 6 paragraph 1 subparagraph c of the GDPR), the relevant document that was posted only contains data that is absolutely necessary for the intended purpose of transparency and control of state action, as well as for determining the content of the act, in compliance with the processing principles of Article 5 paragraph 1 of the GDPR, particularly the principle of minimization, without the posted act revealing the reason for the complainant's dismissal or disclosing data of special categories concerning him.  
17. Because after all the above, OLCH S.A., as the employer company, proceeded with the lawful processing of the complainant's personal data, and had a lawful claim for the dismissal of the complainant without compensation under Article 5 paragraph 3 of Law 3198/1955, given that criminal charges had been brought against him for offenses in general—even outside of service with at least a misdemeanor character—for the protection of its legal claims and fulfillment of its legal obligations and interests, within the framework of the dispute between them, against which the complainant's right to oppose the processing of his personal data and to protection against such processing does not prevail, according to what is stated in Consideration 16 of this document, taking into account the reasonable expectations of him as the data subject based on his employment relationship with the data controller, but also considering that at the given time, the complainant's continued presence in his position as Head of the Projects Department of OLCH S.A. did not meet the conditions of Law 2112/1920 (Article 5 paragraph 1 thereof, which states: "The employer may terminate the contract without observing any notice period, if a complaint has been filed against the employee for a criminal act committed in the exercise of his duties or if a charge has been brought against him for an offense in general, having at least a misdemeanor character"), he could reasonably expect that the complained processing of his personal data could take place.  
FOR THESE REASONS  
The Authority,
