CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW  

1. Since from the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679 – hereinafter, GDPR) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137) it follows that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Article 5 paragraph 1 of the GDPR sets out the principles that must govern a processing operation, and paragraph 2 of this article stipulates that the data controller "is responsible and is able to demonstrate compliance with paragraph 1 ('accountability')." As the Authority has ruled,1 with the GDPR a new compliance model has been adopted, the central point of which is the principle of accountability, under which the data controller is obliged to design, implement, and generally take the necessary measures and policies to ensure that the processing of data is in accordance with the relevant legislative provisions. Furthermore, the data controller is burdened with the further duty to demonstrate his compliance with the principles of Article 5 paragraph 1 of the GDPR.

5. Whereas according to the provision of Article 31 of the GDPR it is provided that “The data controller and the processor and, where applicable, their representatives cooperate, upon request, with the supervisory authority in the performance of its tasks.” This provision introduces an independent general obligation for every data controller to cooperate with the supervisory authority when a relevant request is submitted in the exercise of the duties assigned to it by the European legislator, while the violation of its fulfillment incurs independently the imposition of the administrative fine of Article 83 paragraph 4 point a of the GDPR. It is noted that this obligation, together with the principle of accountability of Article 5 paragraph 2 of the GDPR, strengthens the role of the supervisory authority in the exercise of its powers towards the realization of the purpose of effective implementation of personal data protection rules. 

6. Whereas in the case under examination, from the elements of the file it appears that the complainant exercised the right of access to data concerning him before the complained lawyer as the data controller duly and clearly on 27-11-2019 with a relevant SMS, in which he requested the return of his file, while in the email dated 27-04-2020 that he also sent to the complained party, he requested the provision of all the files he has handled, as his legal representative, and that concern him. The latter request was reiterated in the extrajudicial statements – protests – notices served to the aforementioned complained party on 30-09-2020 and 22-06-2021. Given that the content of the above requests submitted to the Authority does not leave any doubt or ambiguity regarding the requested documents, the claim of the complained party regarding the attribution to the relevant requests of content such that it would not be practically possible to respond appropriately to them is to be rejected as unfounded. In any case, it is noted that the right of access does not necessarily require a specific form or to be exercised in a ceremonial manner. 

7. However, the complained lawyer was obliged, in accordance with the provision of paragraph 4 of Article 12 of the GDPR, to inform the latter, without delay and no later than one month from the receipt of the request, of the reasons for which he did not act.
