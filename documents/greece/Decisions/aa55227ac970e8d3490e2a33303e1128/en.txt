CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW 

1. Because, according to the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter, GDPR) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, this Law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Specifically, from the provisions of Articles 57 paragraph 1 point (f) of the GDPR and 13 paragraph 1 point (z) of Law 4624/2019, it follows that the Authority has the competence to address the said complaint against the EYP, as it concerns the disclosure through the transmission of data included in a filing system within the meaning of Article 4 points 2 and 6 of the GDPR, thus for processing subject to the regulatory scope of Articles 2 paragraph 1 of the GDPR and 2 of Law 4624/2019. 
2. Because, specifically, according to Article 4 point 1 of the GDPR, "personal data means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person..."
7

Identifiable natural person ("data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one whose identity can be ascertained, directly or indirectly, particularly by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, psychological, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.

3. Furthermore, according to Article 4(2) of the GDPR, the processing of personal data is defined as "any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure or destruction."

4. According to Article 4(7) of the GDPR, the data controller is defined as "the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data; where the purposes and means of such processing are determined by Union law or the law of a Member State, the data controller or the specific criteria for its designation may be provided for by Union law or the law of a Member State."

5. In Article 5(1) of the GDPR, the principles that must govern processing are set forth, specifically: "1. Personal data shall be: a) processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject ('lawfulness, fairness, and transparency') (...)".

6. Furthermore, according to the principle of accountability introduced by the second paragraph of the same article, it is expressly stated that the data controller "shall be responsible for, and be able to demonstrate compliance with, paragraph 1 ('accountability')." This principle, which constitutes a cornerstone of the GDPR, entails the obligation of the data controller to be able to demonstrate compliance and for this purpose to verify and legally document any processing it carries out in accordance with the legal bases provided by the GDPR and national data protection law.

7. Specifically, in order for a processing operation to be considered lawful (and in accordance with Article 5(1) of the GDPR), one of the legal grounds for processing provided in Article 6(1) (letters a-f) of the GDPR must apply, namely: "1. Processing shall be lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the following applies: a) the data subject has given consent to the processing of his or her personal data for one or more specific purposes, b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract, c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation to which the data controller is subject, d) processing is necessary in order to protect the vital interests of the data subject or of another natural person, e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the data controller, f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the data controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject which require protection of personal data, especially if the data subject is a child. The provision in letter f) of the first paragraph shall not apply to processing carried out by public authorities in the performance of their tasks."

8. In order for personal data to be processed lawfully, that is, in accordance with the requirements of the GDPR, the conditions for the application and observance of the principles of Article 5(1) of the GDPR must be cumulatively met, as also indicated by the judgment of the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) of January 16, 2019, in case C496/2017 Deutsche Post AG v. Hauptzollamt Köln. The existence of a legal basis (Article 6 of the GDPR) does not...
9

releases the data controller from the obligation to adhere to the principles (Article 5(1) GDPR) regarding the lawful nature, necessity, and proportionality as well as the principle of data minimization. In the event that any of the principles set forth in Article 5(1) GDPR are violated, the processing in question is deemed unlawful (contrary to the provisions of the GDPR), and the examination of the conditions for the application of the legal bases of Article 6 GDPR is rendered unnecessary. Thus, the unlawful collection and processing of personal data in violation of the principles of Article 5 GDPR is not remedied by the existence of a legitimate purpose and legal basis. Furthermore, the CJEU in its decision of 01-10-2015 in the context of case C-201/14 (Smaranda Bara) ruled that the informing of the data subject prior to the processing of their data is a prerequisite for the lawful and legitimate processing of personal data. Specifically, the data controller, in the context of its adherence to the principle of lawful or fair processing of personal data, is obliged to inform the data subject that their data will be processed in a lawful and transparent manner and to be in a position at all times to demonstrate its compliance with these principles (principle of accountability according to Article 5(2) in conjunction with Articles 24(1) and 32 GDPR).

9. Furthermore, the recognition and selection of the appropriate legal basis from those provided in Article 6(1) GDPR is closely linked to the principle of lawful or fair processing as well as to the principle of purpose limitation, and the data controller is obliged not only to select the appropriate legal basis prior to the commencement of processing but also to inform the data subject in accordance with Article 13(1)(c) GDPR about its use, as the selection of each legal basis exerts legal influence on the exercise of the rights of the data subjects. Specifically, the selection of the legal basis for processing personal data must take place prior to the commencement of processing, and the data controller is obliged based on the principle of accountability (see Article 5(2) in conjunction with Articles 24 and 32 GDPR) to select the appropriate legal basis from those provided in Article 6(1) GDPR, as well as to be in a position to demonstrate adherence to the principles of Article 5(1) GDPR, including necessarily the documentation on which it based its conclusion regarding the relevant legal basis. Moreover, with the GDPR, a new compliance model was adopted, the central axis of which is the aforementioned principle of accountability, within the framework of which the data controller is obliged to design, implement, and generally take the necessary measures and policies so that the processing of data is in accordance with the relevant legislative provisions. Furthermore, the data controller bears the additional duty to demonstrate at any time its compliance with the principles of Article 5(1) GDPR. It is not coincidental that the GDPR incorporates accountability (already mentioned Article 5(2) GDPR) into the regulation of the principles (Article 5(1) GDPR) governing processing, endowing it with the function of a mechanism for their compliance, essentially reversing the "burden of proof" regarding the lawfulness of processing (and generally the adherence to the principles of Article 5(1) GDPR), transferring it to the data controller, so that they bear the burden of invoking and proving the lawfulness of the processing.

Regarding the compatibility of the aforementioned provisions of Articles 24 and 26 of Law 4624/2019 with the provisions of the GDPR, which E.Y.P. invokes, the Authority, in its Opinion No. 1/2020, concluded that the GDPR does not grant the national legislator the authority to establish new "national legal bases," but only to specify the legal bases of Article 6 paragraph 1 subparagraphs c and e of the GDPR, under the terms and guarantees provided by the provisions of paragraphs 2 and 4 of Article 6 of the GDPR, and that from the provision of Article 6 paragraph 4 of the GDPR regarding further processing for other purposes, different but compatible with the purposes of the initial collection, it follows that the national legislator is not obliged to take implementing measures for further processing since the GDPR itself establishes the criteria under a) to e) of that paragraph 4. With this opinion, it was specifically accepted that, even if it is considered that the national legislator had the authority from the GDPR to establish "national legal bases" for the further processing of data under Article 6 paragraph 4 of the GDPR, these should only constitute a specification of the legal bases of Article 6 paragraph 1 subparagraphs c and e of the GDPR and must respect, among other things, the principles of legality of Article 5 paragraph 1 of the GDPR. With these considerations, the Authority opined that the provisions of the aforementioned Articles 24 and 26 of the Law do not meet any of the aforementioned substantive and procedural conditions and guarantees arising from the GDPR. Moreover, an "act" of the President of the Republic, according to Article 35 paragraph 1 subparagraph a of the Constitution, is any written act performed by him in his capacity as a state authority and constitutes an exercise of his powers under the Constitution and the Laws. Furthermore, a prerequisite—besides the countersignature of the competent minister or, as the case may be, competent ministers—for the acts of the President of the Republic to be valid, according to Article 35 paragraph 1 subparagraph a of the Constitution, is their publication in the Government Gazette. The publication constitutes an external formal element, the absence of which renders the act void, and the requirement for publication is reiterated, specifically concerning formal laws, by Article 42 paragraph 1 of the Constitution.

Furthermore, given that the contentious processing in question is contrary to Article 5, paragraph 1, point a of the GDPR, and that, according to what is stated in paragraph 8, the cumulative fulfillment of the conditions for the application and adherence to the principles of Article 5, paragraph 1 of the GDPR is required for personal data to be lawfully processed, it is unnecessary to examine the fulfillment of the other principles of lawful processing of data based on Article 5, as well as the examination of the conditions for the application of the legal bases of Article 6 of the GDPR, and the related claims made by the involved parties, regardless of the fact that the EYP, as the data controller, does not invoke any of the legal bases mentioned restrictively in Article 6 of the GDPR and is unable to prove the legality of the said processing.

17. As mentioned above, the data subject was not informed about the transmission of her data, and the relevant claim by the EYP that "Moreover, the above-mentioned provision of law that mandated the transfer of EYP employees to another Public Authority was already known from the time of its submission to the Hellenic Parliament for voting, had received significant publicity, and was discussed long before its voting (...)" is not substantial, as notification through the publicity invoked by the EYP regarding this issue does not meet the requirements of Article 13 of the GDPR, according to which notification requires an action by the data controller directed at the data subject, which did not occur in this case.

18. Therefore, it is established that the EYP, as the data controller, carried out the contentious processing of the complainant's personal data in violation of a) the principles of legality, objectivity, and transparency of Article 5, paragraph 1, point a of the GDPR, as it based its actions on a non-existent law, and b) Article 13 of the GDPR because it did not inform the complainant about this specific processing, as the data subject.

19. Since the violation of the fundamental principles for processing, as detailed above, incurs the imposition of administrative sanctions under Article
19

According to Article 83(5)(a) of the GDPR, the violation of the right of data subjects provided for in Article 13 of the GDPR incurs the imposition of relevant sanctions under Article 83(5)(b) of the GDPR. Furthermore, according to the GDPR (Recital 148), in order to strengthen the enforcement of the rules of this Regulation, sanctions, including administrative fines, should be imposed for each violation of this Regulation, in addition to or instead of appropriate measures imposed by the supervisory authority in accordance with this Regulation.

20. Since the Authority, based on the above, deems that the imposition of a corrective measure is not sufficient to restore compliance with the provisions of the GDPR that have been violated, and that, based on the circumstances identified, an additional effective, proportionate, and deterrent administrative monetary fine must be imposed under Article 83 of the GDPR, both to restore compliance and to sanction the unlawful behavior.

21. Furthermore, the Authority took into account the criteria for determining the fine set out in Article 83(2) of the GDPR, paragraph 5 of the same article applicable to the present case, Article 39(1) and (2) of Law 4624/2019 concerning the imposition of administrative sanctions on public sector entities, and the Guidelines 4/2022 of the European Data Protection Board for calculating administrative fines for the purposes of Regulation 2016/679, which were approved on 24/5/2023, as well as the factual data of the examined case and in particular:
i) The fact that the entity being reported, in its capacity as data controller, violated the principle of lawfulness, fairness, and transparency provided for in Article 5(1)(a), that is, it violated a fundamental principle of the GDPR for the protection of personal data.
ii) The fact that adherence to the principles set out in Article 5(1)(a) of the GDPR is of paramount importance, primarily the principle of lawfulness, so that if this is absent, the processing becomes unlawful from the outset, even if the other processing principles have been adhered to, especially in this case where no documentation was provided to determine whether any of the legal bases set out in Article 6 of the GDPR apply. On the contrary, the reported entity justified the contentious processing merely by citing the acceleration of the procedure for operational reasons.
iii) The fact that the reported entity, in the absence of a legislative basis for the contentious processing, did not fulfill its obligation to prove the lawfulness of this processing, while at the same time failing to demonstrate that it complied with its obligation to inform as provided for in Article 13 of the GDPR, as specified above.
iv) The fact that the aforementioned violation is not proven to be attributable to the intent of the reported service, but rather to its negligence, due to the alleged failure of the National Printing Office, while the acceleration of the transmission by the entity was done for operational reasons.
v) The fact that the data controller did not delay in responding to the Authority's documents,
vi) The fact that from the evidence presented to the Authority, based on which it identified the aforementioned violations of the GDPR, it does not appear that the data controller caused material damage to the affected individual through the contentious processing.

22. Based on the above, the Authority unanimously decides that an administrative sanction, as mentioned in the operative part, must be imposed on the reported entity as the data controller, which is deemed proportionate to the severity of the violation.

FOR THESE REASONS

The Authority,

Imposes on the reported National Intelligence Service (EYP) as the data controller the effective, proportionate, and deterrent administrative monetary fine that is appropriate for this specific case, according to its particular circumstances, in the total amount of five thousand (5,000) euros, specifically four thousand (4,000) euros for the aforementioned established violation of Article 5(1)(a) and one thousand (1,000) euros for the aforementioned established violation of Article 13 of the GDPR, as specified above, in accordance with Articles 58(2)(h) and 83(5)(a) and (b) of the GDPR.
