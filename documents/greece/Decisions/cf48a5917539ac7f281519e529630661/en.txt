The Hellenic Data Protection Authority convened at its headquarters, Kifisias 1-3, Athens, on Tuesday, October 17, 2023, following an invitation from its President, to examine the case referred to below in the background of this decision. Present were the President of the Authority, Konstantinos Menoudakos, and the regular members of the Authority Spyridon Vlachopoulos as rapporteur, Konstantinos Lamprinoudakis, Charalampos Anthopoulos, Christos Kalloniatis, also as rapporteur, Aikaterini Iliadou, as well as Maria Psalla, an alternate member of the Authority, replacing the regular member Grigorios Tsolias, who, although duly summoned in writing, did not attend. Present without voting rights were Eleni Maragkou, Ioannis Lykotrafitis, Anastasia Tritaki, and Aikaterini Chatzidiakou, auditors of the Authority, as assistant rapporteurs, and Irini Papageorgopoulou, an employee of the administrative affairs department, as secretary.  

The Authority took into account the following:  
At the end of 2021, the Authority became aware of the decision of the Greek government regarding the development and installation of the "Centaurus" Program by the Ministry of Migration and Asylum (hereinafter referred to as YMA) aimed at monitoring the reception and accommodation structures for third-country nationals on the islands of the Aegean. Additionally, in December 2021, the Authority received a request for information (No. Protocol of the Authority G/EIS/8231/17-12-2021) from the Committee on Civil Liberties, Justice and Home Affairs of the European Parliament (LIBE Committee) regarding surveillance technologies at the borders, while a request for investigation and provision of an opinion regarding the procurement and installation of the "Hyperion" and "Centaurus" systems in the reception and accommodation structures for asylum seekers was submitted before the Authority in February 2022 (with No. Protocol of the Authority G/EIS/2515/18-02-2022) by civil society organizations (i. Greek Union for Human Rights, ii. HIAS Greece, iii. Homo Digitalis) in collaboration with A, Lecturer at Queen Mary University of London. The Authority also received a letter in July 2022 (with No. Protocol of the Authority G/EIS/9479/11-08-2022) regarding the aforementioned systems from the Representation of the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees in Greece.  

The "Centaurus" program is said to constitute a comprehensive digital management system for Electronic and Physical Security around and within the facilities of Closed Controlled Structures (KED) and Reception and Identification Centers (RIC) for third-country nationals on the islands of Lesbos, Chios, Samos, Leros, and Kos, utilizing cameras and motion analysis algorithms (Artificial Intelligence Behavioral Analytics), the management of which will be carried out by YMA. The "Centaurus" program includes, among other things, the use of a CCTV system and unmanned aerial vehicles (drones), through which personal data processing will occur, at least of images.  

At the same time, the "Hyperion" Program is described as a comprehensive entry-exit control system at the facilities of the aforementioned structures aimed at controlling the entry and exit of both the hosted individuals and the certified members of certified non-governmental organizations (NGOs) by presenting the asylum seeker card or NGO member/employee card, and on the other hand, the employees in the structures through an RFID reader combined with a fingerprint (two-factor authentication), thus involving the processing of personal data, specifically biometric data.  

Based on the above, the Authority, with the No. Protocol G/EKE/563/02-03-2022 document, summoned YMA, as the data controller, to provide information regarding the said "Centaurus" and "Hyperion" programs. Specifically, the Authority requested YMA to provide clarifications, among other things, regarding: a) the legal basis (provision of law and/or law) for the intended processing, b) the other elements of this processing (such as data retention period, informing data subjects, etc.), and c) whether a study has been conducted...

θ) Regarding the adherence to the principle of proportionality for the automated processing being carried out, particularly through the video surveillance system, it is noted that the processing through the "Centaurus" video surveillance system is by definition automated processing to which the GDPR applies. The adoption of other preventive and protective measures that do not include video surveillance (e.g., static guarding, use of security doors, alarm systems, stronger lighting) is insufficient to achieve the purpose, as these were implemented in the previous period and were ineffective. For the monitoring of the facilities, digital (IP) fixed bullet-type cameras, as well as pan-tilt-zoom (PTZ) cameras, have been installed and are used, which support software for integrating image analysis algorithms (Video Content Analytics) and which constitute the "Centaurus" video surveillance system. The IP cameras have sensors that can capture images in standard resolution, as well as in high definition (HD), while supporting functions such as motion detection, connection to alarms, and patrol mode. Motion detection activates recording. The PTZ cameras have the capability of rotation and zooming in on the point of interest via remote control. It is irrelevant for the application of the provisions of Article 2 of Presidential Decree 75/2020 whether the surveillance systems use analog or digital technology, whether they have fixed, rotating, or mobile cameras, adapted to fixed bases or portable ones carried by vehicles of any kind (land, sea, or air, manned or unmanned) or by natural persons. The cameras capture images from an indeterminate and vast number of individuals. The number of individuals falling within the range of the cameras remains indefinite. These are critical facilities where residents are never alone but coexist with public officials from the Reception and Identification Service and the Asylum Service of the Ministry of Migration and Asylum, employees of various specialties in Non-Governmental Organizations (lawyers, interpreters, social workers, psychologists, doctors, health professionals), staff from the European Asylum Support Office (EASO), and individuals hired by the local Municipal Authority through public benefit work programs of the OAED, and therefore cannot claim a violation of their privacy. The aforementioned cameras do not record sound, while primarily monitoring the common areas and the surrounding environment of the Reception and Identification Centers (KED) and their perimeter. The recorded data consists of visual data. The data and control of the system are directed to a suitably configured area in the Signal Reception Center, where the Control Room of the facility is located, within which the corresponding equipment for data collection and recording, monitoring of the cameras, and the workstation of the operator-guard is installed. The network allows the transmission of images captured by each camera in real-time to the equipment of the control center. The video data – images are encoded with a compression algorithm and stored on storage media. "There are cameras with the ability to pan and focus which are generally permitted, and their movement is restricted to the maximum possible extent or when this is not physically possible, the camera operators are suitably trained and qualified. The ability to zoom in/focus is exceptionally permitted when there is an urgent need for it, such as during incidents and altercations."  
In the context of this specific processing of personal data, the following applicable Information Security measures have been taken: i) User computers have malware protection software (antivirus). ii) Mechanisms for controlling physical access to the facilities are implemented. iii) Measures for the physical protection of critical equipment used for processing are implemented (e.g., structured cabling, etc.). iv) Measures for protection against natural disasters are implemented (e.g., fire safety system, etc.). v) User access codes for the software supporting the surveillance system are required. vi) Security policies at the software level are implemented to prevent unauthorized access. vii) Maintenance of the information systems used in the context of the processing is carried out.

21) Since regarding the description, clarification, and definition of the role of the Ministry of Citizen Protection as the Data Controller and all those executing the processing, with the Authority's reference number G/EIS/2625/07-04-2023 memorandum, the Ministry of Citizen Protection corrected and clarified that it is the data controller for the processing carried out by the "Hyperion" and "Centaurus" systems and provided a list of processors for the aforementioned systems. Regarding the inadequate fulfillment of the terms of Article 28(3) of the GDPR concerning the contract between the data controller and the processor, it is noted that, as explicitly stated in the Guidelines 7/2020 of the European Data Protection Board regarding the concepts of the data controller and the processor, "since the Regulation establishes a clear obligation to conclude a written contract, unless no other relevant legal act applies, the absence of a contract constitutes a violation of the GDPR. Both the data controller and the processor are responsible for ensuring the conclusion of a contract or other legal act governing the processing." Therefore, the failure to fulfill the terms of Article 28(3) of the GDPR constitutes a violation for the data controller. According to the initial claims of ΥΜΑ, the contracts of ΥΜΑ with the other processors were not submitted or were not submitted in their entirety, as they are claimed by ΥΜΑ to be "confidential contracts," while in the memorandum dated 31-03-2023, ΥΜΑ states that a data protection agreement has been drafted between ΥΜΑ and the processors as an annex to the contracts with the contractors, and their review and signature are pending. In this regard, it is recalled that according to Article 58(1)(e) of the GDPR, the national supervisory authority has investigative powers, specifically the power "to obtain from the data controller and the processor access to all personal data and all information necessary for the performance of its tasks," and according to Article 15(1) of Law 4624/2019 regarding its investigative and corrective powers, "in conducting investigations and audits, the Authority has the power to obtain from the data controller and the processor access to all personal data that is subject to processing and all information necessary for the purposes of the relevant audit and the performance of its tasks, without being able to invoke any kind of confidentiality." In this specific case, the Authority called upon ΥΜΑ to submit relevant contracts only to ascertain their conclusion between the data controller and the individual processors and in particular to verify the required annex to the contract with the terms for the protection of personal data. However, the necessary elements for this review were not submitted before the Authority, but only one of ΥΜΑ's contracts with the processors, specifically in draft form, and the contract of ΥΜΑ with the Anonymous Company "Electronic Governance of Social Security S.A." (Η.Δ.Ι.Κ.Α. S.A.), which includes as an Annex a Contract for the Protection of Personal Data. From these incomplete elements, it is not possible for the Authority to draw reliable conclusions regarding the compliance with the conditions set by Article 28 of the GDPR for the processors, as it does not appear that data protection contracts have been concluded between ΥΜΑ as the data controller and the mentioned processors. Consequently, ΥΜΑ, as the data controller, did not sufficiently substantiate before the Authority the compliance with the terms of the aforementioned provisions of Article 28 of the GDPR, and therefore, a violation of this article arises.  
22) Given that regarding the maintenance of activity records, with the protocol number Γ/ΕΙΣ/2625/07-04-2023, ΥΜΑ pointed out that the preparation of the activity record has been entrusted to the Data Protection Officer (ΥΠΔ) and had not been completed at that time. It is noted that at that time the examined systems were already fully operational, while, according to the requirements of Article 25(1) and (2) of the GDPR regarding the requirement for protection by design and by default, the activity record should have been prepared at least before the commencement of the operation of these systems. Furthermore, according to Article 30(1) of the GDPR, the data controller has, in principle, the obligation to maintain an activity record and may, where appropriate, have it maintained by a representative. Therefore, the invocation of the late assignment of this specific obligation to the ΥΠΔ is futile. Consequently, ΥΜΑ, as the data controller, who bears the responsibility for maintaining this record, did not substantiate before the Authority its compliance with Article 30 of the GDPR.  
23) Given that regarding the conduct of an impact assessment, including the examination of compliance with the principle of proportionality, both with protocol number Γ/ΕΙΣ/10875/10-10-2023 and with protocol number Γ/ΕΙΣ/2625/07-04-2023, ΥΜΑ clarified that the conduct of the Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) on the existing technological systems took place without the entirety of their functionality and the scope of their parameterized functions being known due to a) the confidentiality of the content of the contracts and b) due to their evolving implementation,

Overall, the impact assessment conducted for each of the technological systems appears, based on the documents received by the Authority so far, to be, on the one hand, fragmented and limited in scope, covering specific individual processing operations of the systems and not their entirety, and on the other hand, not fully up-to-date. Furthermore, the claims of the ΥΜΑ regarding the confidential nature of the contracts and their evolving implementation are irrelevant to the relevant provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), particularly given that conducting an impact assessment is an obligation of the data controller, and invoking the confidentiality of contracts against the data controller itself or its representative or the appointed Data Protection Officer (DPO) is not permissible. Therefore, in violation of Article 35 paragraphs 1 to 3 of the GDPR, the ΥΜΑ, as the data controller, has not conducted the assessment as it was obliged to, in accordance with the requirements of Article 25 GDPR paragraphs 1 and 2 regarding the requirement for data protection by design and, by definition, a comprehensive and systematic assessment of the impacts of the planned processing operations on personal data protection before the commencement of any processing, nor has it timely sought the opinion of the DPO.

26) Since, taking into account the above conclusions, which are based on an assessment of all the elements of the file and those that emerged from the hearing process and the submitted memoranda, it is concluded that ΥΜΑ, as the data controller, did not sufficiently document before the Authority its compliance with the principle of accountability (Article 5(2) GDPR), as it did not provide, as it was obliged to do, before the Authority complete, accurate, and clear information to sufficiently document the legality of the actions carried out within the framework of the aforementioned systems concerning the processing of personal data. On the contrary, from the examination of the submitted elements and memoranda, ambiguities and contradictory claims arise, as well as a failure to comply with a series of provisions of the GDPR and Law 4624/2019 as mentioned in the previous considerations. The failure to comply constitutes an admission by ΥΜΑ in its memorandum dated 10-10-2022, in which a reference is made to the newly established nature of the Ministry; however, this claim is irrelevant as it does not justify the violation of the aforementioned fundamental rules of personal data protection during the operation of the above systems "Centaurus" and "Hyperion." Furthermore, the failure to comply raises doubts about the completeness of the design concerning the provision of the ability to exercise the rights of data subjects and the proper application of the relevant provisions of the GDPR, although this issue was not specifically examined by the Authority and, therefore, is not taken into account in forming its judgment. Furthermore, the Authority finds that the data controller, during the examination of the case and despite its repeated calls for the provision of specific clarifications and analysis of the issues under examination, showed difficulty in cooperating with the Authority regarding the provision of information and specifically with the submission of contracts with the processors and contracts with the data protection officer, as well as with the provision of clarifications for the processing carried out through the "Centaurus" system and particularly its automated functions, and the interconnection of the "Centaurus" and "Hyperion" systems with other public systems. Following these, the Authority deems that the failure to submit to the critical elements for the examination of the case under review constitutes a violation of the obligation of cooperation of ΥΜΑ, as the data controller, with the supervisory authority under the provisions of Article 31 of the GDPR, which fall within the accountability obligations of the data controller before the supervisory Authority.
