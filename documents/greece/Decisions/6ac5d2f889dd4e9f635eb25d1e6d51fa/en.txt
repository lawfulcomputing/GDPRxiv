The President of the Authority, as a single-member body pursuant to Article 17 paragraph 1 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), within the framework of the powers provided in Articles 4 paragraph 3 and 10 paragraph 4 of the Authority's Operating Regulation (Government Gazette B' 879/25.02.2022), convened via teleconference on January 25, 2024, in order to examine the case referred to below in the background of this decision. Present without voting rights were Charis Symeonidou, legal auditor – lawyer, and Irini Papageorgopoulou, employee of the administrative affairs department, as secretary.

The Authority took into account the following:

1.  
With the complaint No. G/EIS/12590/15-12-2022, A (hereinafter referred to as the complainant) is directed against the company named "GROUP MEDICAL DIAGNOSIS – PRIVATE MULTISPECIALTY CLINIC – MEDICAL S.A." (hereinafter referred to as the respondent), alleging a violation of the confidentiality of her personal data. Specifically, according to the complaint, on … an employee of the respondent's Diagnostic Center in [location] X disclosed the results of the complainant's examinations to her father over the phone, without her having given her consent for this and specifically without having stated her father's phone number and details as a person authorized to receive the results of her examinations on her behalf. The complainant argues that in this way her medical confidentiality and personal health data were violated and that her elderly father was distressed; she also states that she subsequently informed the respondent about the above incident through her communication with B, the director of the Diagnostic Center in [location] X, who admitted the incident and apologized.

2.  
In the context of investigating the above complaint, the Authority, with document No. G/EXE/655/13-03-2023, communicated the relevant documents to the respondent and invited it to express its views on the allegations, specifically clarifying the following: a) what is the procedure followed by the respondent for providing examination results via telephone and what security measures it has taken to ensure the confidentiality and protection of medical confidentiality, in accordance with Articles 24 and 32 of the GDPR, submitting its relevant Policy; b) whether this Policy was adhered to in the case of the alleged factual incidents, specifically stating what procedure was followed by the respondent for informing the complainant regarding the results of her examinations; c) whether the respondent, after being informed by the complainant about the incident, handled it as a personal data breach incident and what actions it took in this regard. In its response No. G/EIS/2283/28-03-2023, the respondent company stated the following:

From the form, it appears that the method of delivery of the results chosen by the complainant was via email, and as the respondent points out, in such cases, the method of encrypting the documents sent is followed.

According to the respondent, the person who called on behalf of the diagnostic center did not disclose any health information of the complainant to her father but informed him that it was a personal matter and asked him to inform her that they wished to contact the diagnostic center.

The respondent, orally through its representatives during the hearing, stated that it does not keep the contact details of the patients who visit it and that at each visit this information is updated orally, as declared by the patients at the reception. As it stated, in this case, medical reasons necessitated immediate communication with the examined individual, in order to obtain approval for the further examination needed; for this reason, the method of communication chosen was a call to the mobile phone rather than the landline, so that the called party, if unable to answer, could be informed of the call and call the diagnostic center back. According to the respondent company, the complaint in question has been submitted as a means of enrichment by the complainant, who has sought "an out-of-court settlement" through the sending of an extrajudicial statement, vaguely claiming moral damage to her father from the aforementioned telephone communication, while, as the respondent argues, no similar incident has ever been recorded. In response to a question regarding the organizational security measures it implements, the respondent emphasized that through its information system, where access levels for staff (role-based access) are maintained, the reception staff do not have access to the examination results of the patients, and it noted that as part of the ongoing updating of these measures, in the future, the declaration of the contact details of the patients will be done through electronic registration directly by them using a tablet with a stylus.

CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW

1. According to Article 2, paragraph 1 of the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679 – hereinafter, GDPR) "This regulation applies to the, in whole or in part, automated processing of personal data, as well as to the non-automated processing of such data which are included or are intended to be included in a filing system." As processing is understood, according to Article 4, point 2 of the GDPR, "any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure or destruction." Therefore, the collection, recording, and storage of information, such as the phone number and the results of medical examinations of the data subject, in the information system of the data controller, constitute data processing falling within the material scope of the GDPR. Furthermore, from the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the GDPR and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), it follows that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. According to the above, the Authority has the competence, under the provisions of Articles 57, paragraph 1, point (f) of the GDPR and 13, paragraph 1, point (z) of Law 4624/2019, to address the complaint of A against "MEDICAL DIAGNOSIS GROUP – PRIVATE POLYCLINIC – MEDICAL S.A." and to exercise, correspondingly, its powers provided for in the provisions of Articles 58 of the GDPR and 15 of Law 4624/2019.

2. With Article 5, paragraph 1 of the General Regulation (EU) 2016/679 for the protection of natural persons against the processing of personal data (hereinafter GDPR), the principles that must govern processing are established. According to Article 5, paragraph 1 (a), (d), and (f) of the GDPR "1. Personal data: (a) shall be processed lawfully and fairly in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject ('lawfulness, fairness, and transparency'), [...], (d) are accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; every reasonable step must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate, with respect to the purposes of the processing, are erased or rectified without delay ('accuracy'), [...] (f) shall be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures ('integrity and confidentiality')." Moreover, according to Recital 39 of the GDPR "Personal data should be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security and confidentiality of personal data, including protection against unauthorized processing."

“access to this personal data (…)”. Furthermore, according to the principle of accountability explicitly introduced by the paragraph of the above Article 5, which constitutes a cornerstone of the GDPR, the data controller “bears the responsibility and is able to demonstrate compliance with paragraph 1 (‘accountability’)”. This principle entails the obligation of the data controller to be able to demonstrate compliance with the principles of Article 5 paragraph 1.  
3. Every processing operation must be lawful and must be based on at least one of the legal bases of Article 6 GDPR. Furthermore, according to Article 9 paragraph 1 GDPR “1. The processing of […] data concerning health […] is prohibited”, while paragraph 2 of the same article enumerates exhaustively the exceptions to the above prohibition on processing special categories of data as per paragraph 1. In the provision of Article 4 point 15 of the GDPR, data concerning health is defined as: “personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, and which reveal information about the health status of the individual.”  
4. According to Article 24 paragraphs 1 and 2 GDPR “1. Taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is performed in accordance with this Regulation. Such measures shall be reviewed and updated when necessary. 2. Where justified in relation to the processing activities, the measures referred to in paragraph 1 shall include the implementation of appropriate policies for the protection of data by the data controller”, while according to the provisions of paragraphs 1 and 2 of Article 32 GDPR regarding the security of processing, “1. Taking into account the latest developments, the cost of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller and the processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an appropriate level of security against the risks, […] 2. In assessing the appropriate level of security, particular consideration shall be given to the risks arising from the processing, in particular from accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or access to personal data that has been transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.”  
5. According to Article 4 point 12 GDPR, a personal data breach is defined as “a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or access to personal data that has been transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.” According to the Guidelines 18/2018 of the Article 29 Working Party of Directive 95/46/EC (now European Data Protection Board) on Personal Data Breach Notification (“Guidelines on Personal data breach notification under Regulation 2016/679” WP 250 rev. 1), one type of personal data breach is categorized based on the security principle of “confidentiality”, when unauthorized access to personal data is identified (“confidentiality breach”).  
6. Because in this case, from the examination of all the elements of the file and the evaluation of the claims of the complainant and the reported diagnostic center that were developed during the hearing and with the corresponding submissions, there was no certainty regarding the content of the telephone conversation between the employees of the secretariat of the reported diagnostic center and the father of the complainant on …, it is therefore not established that the reported leak of personal data of the complainant, namely the results of the medical examinations, has occurred, given that,
12

According to the classified access of the employees of the reported company to its Information System, the secretarial staff does not have access to the health data of the subjects. Furthermore, it appears that, at the time of the reported incidents, the collection of patients' contact details by the reported company was done in accordance with their verbal statement during the visit, while, upon consideration of the case data, it is not proven that the number ending in -..., which was called by the Diagnostic Center, was obtained, as a contact detail with the complainant, in a manner different from that of its employees. Moreover, as determined in the context of a spontaneous examination of the technical and organizational security measures and related policies maintained by the reported company as the data controller, following a review and within the framework of the ongoing updating of its procedures, henceforth the registration of customers' contact details will be done with a signed entry on a tablet by themselves using a stylus. Therefore, the alleged violation of the principle of confidentiality of the complainant's data is not established, while it appears that the reported company acts in compliance with the provisions of Articles 32 and 24 paragraphs 1 and 2 of the GDPR.  
Following the above, it is determined that there is no case for exercising the corrective powers of the Authority under Article 58 paragraph 2 of the GDPR.
