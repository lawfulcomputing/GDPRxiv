CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW  

1.  
Whereas, from the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter "GDPR") and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), it follows that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Specifically, from the provisions of Articles 57 paragraph 1 point (f) of the GDPR and 13 paragraph 1 point (g) of Law 4624/2019, it follows that the Authority has the competence to address the complaints under examination and to exercise, correspondingly, the powers conferred upon it by the provisions of Articles 58 of the GDPR and 15 of Law 4624/2019.  
2.  
Whereas, according to Article 4 point (7) of the GDPR, the data controller is defined as "the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data," and with point (8) of the GDPR, the processor is defined as "the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which processes personal data on behalf of the data controller."  
3.  
Whereas, Article 5 of the GDPR establishes the principles of processing that govern the processing of personal data and in paragraph 1 states that personal data, among other things: "a) shall be processed lawfully and fairly in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject ('lawfulness, fairness, transparency'), (…) d) shall be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; all reasonable steps must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate are rectified or deleted without delay."

the immediate deletion or correction of personal data that is inaccurate, in relation to the purposes of processing ("accuracy"). According to paragraph 2, "the data controller is responsible and is able to demonstrate compliance with paragraph 1 ("accountability")."  
4.  
Whereas, according to Article 16 of the GDPR, "The data subject has the right to request from the data controller the rectification of inaccurate personal data concerning them without undue delay. Taking into account the purposes of processing, the data subject has the right to request the completion of incomplete personal data, including by means of a supplementary statement."  
5.  
Whereas, according to the provision of Article 17 paragraph 1 of the GDPR, "The data subject has the right to request from the data controller the deletion of personal data concerning them without undue delay and the data controller is obliged to delete personal data without undue delay, if one of the following reasons applies: b) the data subject withdraws consent on which the processing is based according to Article 6 paragraph 1 point a) or Article 9 paragraph 2 point a) and there is no other legal basis for the processing, [...], c) the data subject objects to the processing according to Article 21 paragraph 1 and there are no compelling legitimate grounds for the processing or the data subject objects to the processing according to Article 21 paragraph 2, d) the personal data have been processed unlawfully."  
6.  
Whereas, according to the provision of Article 18 paragraph 1 of the GDPR, "1. The data subject has the right to obtain from the data controller the restriction of processing when one of the following applies: a) the accuracy of the personal data is contested by the data subject, for a period enabling the data controller to verify the accuracy of the personal data, b) the processing is unlawful and the data subject opposes the deletion of the personal data and requests, instead, the restriction of their use, c) the data controller no longer needs the personal data for the purposes of processing, but such data are required by the data subject for the establishment, exercise or defense of legal claims, d) the data subject has objected to the processing according to Article 21 paragraph 1, pending verification of whether the legitimate grounds of the data controller override those of the data subject."  
7.  
Whereas, according to the provision of Article 12 paragraph 2 of the GDPR: "The data controller facilitates the exercise of the rights of data subjects provided for in Articles 15 to 22. In the cases provided for in Article 11 paragraph 2, the data controller shall not refuse to act on a request from the data subject to exercise their rights under Articles 15 to 22, unless the data controller demonstrates that it is unable to verify the identity of the data subject" and paragraph 3 "The data controller provides the data subject with information on the action taken in response to a request under Articles 15 to 22 without undue delay and in any event within one month of receipt of the request. This period may be extended by two further months where necessary, taking into account the complexity of the request and the number of requests. The data controller shall inform the data subject of any such extension within one month of receipt of the request, as well as the reasons for the delay. If the data subject submits the request by electronic means, the information shall be provided, where possible, by electronic means, unless the data subject requests otherwise."  
8.  
Whereas, according to Law 3758/2009 "Companies for the information of debtors regarding overdue claims and other provisions," as amended and in force, it is initially provided that creditors have the right to provide debtor information companies (DEO) with information regarding overdue claims against them, without their consent, for the legally prescribed purposes: a. to inform debtors about the

Regarding the obligation to inform, it is noted that according to the 3/2020 decision of the Plenary of the Supreme Court (similarly see the decision 958/2022 of the Supreme Court), it was accepted (contrary to the 98/2017 decision of the Authority) that in the case of the provision of data to a data processor, falling under one of the categories of recipients (e.g., EEO) for which there has been notification, there is no obligation for the data controller to provide new specific notification to the data subject, that is, at the time of the provision of personal data to the specific EEO1. Furthermore, with the aforementioned law, the operational framework of debt collection companies and the specific obligations that this service entails for both the aforementioned companies and the creditors are regulated, and the control mechanism of the said debt collection service is established. In this context, the general supervision of the debt collection service, whether conducted by debt collection companies or by the creditor themselves, falls under the jurisdiction of the Ministry of Development/General Directorate for Consumer Protection/General Secretariat for Commerce and Consumer Protection, according to article 10 par. 1 of Law 3758/2009, as amended and in force, while the Authority has special jurisdiction for only specific violations of Law 3758/2009, according to article 10 par. 1 subparagraph c of Law 3758/20092.

Therefore, the Authority finds that the rights to rectification and deletion were not duly and timely satisfied except after the relevant complaints and the intervention of the Authority, and a violation of Articles 16 and 17 paragraph 1 subparagraphs b, c, and d of the GDPR arises in conjunction with Article 12 paragraphs 2 and 3 of the GDPR.  
iv. Regarding the disturbances that the complainant has received from DEI's associates, it should be noted that from the table submitted by the company, it appears that the last date for the withdrawal of the assignments of the cases concerning the debts of the disputed services was … specifically from the law firms "Sioufas and Associates," "Frangou," and from the company "Palladino," which date coincides with the one mentioned by the complainant in his supplementary complaint, that he received a call on 04-01-2022 from the office "Sioufas and Associates." Additionally, in the description of the calls submitted to the Authority with the associates of the complained company, the statements of the complainant regarding the dispute of the association of his details with the services are recorded, so that DEI can be informed, as it has been informed in this case regarding the requests of the complainant, according to the correspondence between him and DEI. Therefore, the responsibility for the disturbances towards the complainant lies with DEI, which acts as the data controller, and not with the collaborating collection companies and law firms, which acted following the relevant assignment of the case. Therefore, the data controller, having clear knowledge of the complainant's dispute regarding the association of his VAT number with the disputed services, from which the debts arose, should have ceased further processing of the complainant's data, namely the transmission/disclosure/disposal to others until the issue was resolved (see Article 18 GDPR). In light of the above, the Authority finds that DEI, as the data controller, bears the responsibility for the processing of the complainant's data concerning the disturbances he received from November 2020 until January 2021, and it is established that DEI did not prove, based on the principle of accountability (Article 5 paragraph 2 GDPR) of the data controller who is obliged to demonstrate its compliance with the principles.

B. Regarding the Municipality of Athens:  
i. pursuant to Article 43 of Law 3979/2011 paragraph 1b “the municipal fees for cleanliness and lighting [...], the tax on electrified spaces [...] and the property tax [...] are borne by the person liable for payment of the electricity consumption bill and are collected by the Public Power Corporation (PPC) or the alternative electricity supplier, in installments equal to the number of annual bills. [...] The collections made by the PPC or the respective alternative supplier are paid to the entitled municipality, based on a relevant clearing statement within the second month following the end of the month to which the bills belong. [...] If the liable person does not pay the aforementioned collected amounts, the electricity supplier proceeds to disconnect the electricity supply and does not reconnect it until the owed amount is paid. If the liable person does not request the reconnection of the electricity within three months from the disconnection, the electricity supplier notifies the relevant municipality of the details of the debts, so that it can proceed with their collection.” From the above provisions of the law, it follows that the Municipality acts towards the subject and processes the personal data concerning it for the purpose of serving its own purposes, and the collection through the PPC is carried out only to facilitate the collection system of municipal fees and the property tax. The PPC, pursuant to the above law, transmitted the data to the Municipality of Athens for it to proceed with the collection of the debts concerning it. Therefore, the Authority finds that the Municipality of Athens becomes the data controller of the personal data of those liable for payment of the fees and taxes concerning the Municipality and collected with the electricity bill, as in the case under examination.
