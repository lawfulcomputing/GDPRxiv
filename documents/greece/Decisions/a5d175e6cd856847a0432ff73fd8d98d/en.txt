With complaint No. G/EIS/10340/22-09-2022, A alleges that there was an unlawful disclosure of her health data to third parties. Specifically, she states that on 23-03-2022, she sent to the already reported Inter-Municipal Water Supply and Sewerage Company X (hereinafter DEYA X), where she serves as a civil engineering employee, a Certificate of Positive Diagnostic Testing for Coronavirus COVID-19, recording the result of the molecular test she underwent on 22-03-2022, while on 24-03-2022, she was informed by the competent Service of Civil Protection that she was required to remain in isolation for five (5) days following the diagnosis. However, on the date of the expiration of the five-day isolation, namely on 28-03-2022, she claims that her attending physician recommended a five-day leave due to her fatigue following the subsidence of the coronavirus symptoms. CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW  
1. Whereas, from the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation 2016/679) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), it follows that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Specifically, from the provisions of Articles 57 para. 1 (e) of the GDPR and 13 para. 1 (g) of Law 4624/2019, it follows that the Authority has the competence to address the complaint of A, as the personal data of the complainant, including her health data, as extensively mentioned above, were included in the filing system maintained by the said Service for the complainant as its employee, and therefore the extraction from the aforementioned filing system and further disclosure of this data to another Service, in this case, the Independent Directorate of Civil Protection of Region P, constitutes processing falling within the scope of Articles 2 para. 1 of the GDPR1 and 2 of Law 4624/2019. Furthermore, the sending by email of the complainant's health data by the General Director of the complained party to the members of its Board of Directors, as well as to the Secretariat of the said Board, the Director of Administrative - Financial Affairs, and the Head of the Administrative Department of DEYA X constitutes automated processing which, likewise as mentioned above, falls within the regulatory scope of the GDPR and Law 4624/2019.  
2. Whereas, according to Article 4 (2) of the GDPR, processing is defined as "any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure or destruction," while according to the provisions of Article 4 (7) of the GDPR, the data controller is defined as "the natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data (...)." Furthermore, according to Opinion 1/2010 regarding the concepts of "data controller" and "data processor" of the Article 29 Working Party for the determination of the data controller, three main elements are taken into account according to the aforementioned definition: a) the personal aspect ("the natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body"), b) the possibility of joint control ("which, alone or jointly with others"), and c) the essential characteristics for distinguishing the data controller from other actors ("determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data"). It is noted that the concept of the data controller plays a crucial role in the application of the rules for the protection of personal data, the proof of compliance with them (accountability principle, Article 5 para. 2 GDPR), and the attribution of responsibilities in case of violations.  
Furthermore, it is explained in the aforementioned Opinion 1/2010 regarding the first element – as stated in the immediately preceding paragraph: "In the strategic perspective of the allocation of responsibilities, and in order to provide individuals to whom the data relates with a more stable and reliable entity for the exercise of their rights arising from the directive, it is preferable to consider the company or entity as the data controller rather than a specific individual within the company or entity. The company or entity will ultimately be held responsible for the processing of the data and for the obligations arising from data protection legislation, unless there is clear evidence indicating that a natural person is responsible. Generally, it should be presumed that the company or public authority is responsible for the processing activities that take place within the scope of their activities and risks. Occasionally, companies and public authorities appoint a specific individual responsible for carrying out processing tasks. However, even in cases where a specific natural person is appointed to ensure compliance with data protection principles or to process personal data,
13  
In character, the aforementioned natural person will not be the data controller, but will act on behalf of the legal entity (company or public authority), which will remain responsible in the event of a breach of the principles in its capacity as the data controller (…) However, from a strategic perspective regarding the distribution of responsibilities, it is preferable to consider the company or authority as the data controller rather than a specific individual within the company or authority. The company or authority will ultimately be held responsible for the processing of data and the obligations arising from data protection legislation, unless there is clear evidence indicating that a natural person is responsible, for example, when a natural person working in a company or public authority uses data for their own purposes, outside the activities of the company." Furthermore, it is clarified in the aforementioned Opinion 1/2010 of the Article 29 Working Party: "(…) It does not matter whether the decision to process personal data is 'lawful' in the sense that the entity that made the relevant decision was authorized to do so or that the data controller was formally appointed in accordance with a specific procedure. The issue of the legality of the processing of personal data will be relevant at a different stage and will be assessed in light of other articles (specifically, Articles 6-8) of the directive. In other words, it is important to ensure that, even in cases where the processing of data is unlawful, a data controller can be easily identified and held accountable for the processing (…)”2.  
In light of the above, it appears that the reported DEYA X, through its General Director, who, even if it is accepted that acted voluntarily, in any case, it does not emerge nor is proven from the entirety of the evidence in the file that she acted for her own purposes outside the scope and potential control of the activities of the reported legal entity3, determined the purpose and means of processing the health data of the complainant and therefore is considered a data controller. Specifically, with the contested processing of the data concerning the complainant, particularly with the disclosure of the complainant's health data not only within the sphere of the data controller but also to the Independent Directorate of Civil Protection of Region Y, the General Director acted with the aim of ensuring the smooth operation of the services of the reported entity4 as the data controller.  
3. Article 5(1) of the General Regulation (EU) 2016/679 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data (hereinafter GDPR) sets out the principles that must govern any processing. Specifically, paragraph 1 states that: "1. Personal data shall be: a) processed lawfully and fairly in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject ('lawfulness, fairness, and transparency'), b) collected for specified, legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes; further processing for archiving purposes in the public interest or for scientific or historical research purposes or for statistical purposes shall not be considered incompatible with the initial purposes in accordance with Article 89(1) ('purpose limitation'), c) adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed ('data minimization') …".  
4. It is also noted that the GDPR adopts a new compliance model, the central element of which is the aforementioned principle of accountability, under which the data controller is obliged to design, implement, and generally take the necessary measures and policies to ensure that the processing of data is in compliance with the relevant legal provisions. Furthermore, the data controller bears the additional duty to demonstrate on its own and at any time its compliance with the principles of Article 5(1) of the GDPR. It is not coincidental that the GDPR incorporates accountability (see Article 5(2) of the GDPR) into the regulation of the principles (Article 5(1) of the GDPR) governing processing, thereby providing it with the function of a mechanism for their compliance, essentially reversing the "burden of proof" regarding the lawfulness of processing (and generally the adherence to the principles of Article 5(1)

GDPR), transferring it to the data controller, so that it can be reasonably argued that he bears the burden of invoking and proving the legality of the processing.  
Because, as the Authority has ruled, taking into account the decisions of the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) and the Council of State, in order for personal data to be processed lawfully, that is, processed in accordance with the requirements of the GDPR, the conditions for the application and adherence to the principles of Article 5(1) of the GDPR must be cumulatively met. The existence of a legal basis (Articles 6 and 9 GDPR) does not exempt the data controller from the obligation to adhere to the principles (Article 5(1) GDPR) regarding the lawful nature, necessity, proportionality, and the principle of data minimization. In the event that any of the principles set out in Article 5(1) GDPR is violated, the processing in question is deemed unlawful (contrary to the provisions of the GDPR), and the examination of the conditions for the application of the legal bases of Articles 6 and 9 GDPR for the processing of, respectively, simple and special categories of personal data becomes redundant. Consequently, the unlawful collection and processing of personal data in violation of the principles of Article 5 GDPR is not remedied by the existence of a legitimate purpose and legal basis.  
5. In this case, as emerges from the case file (see the document with reference number … from the complainant), the General Director of the Water Supply and Sewerage Company (DEYA) X on 31-03-2022 communicated to the Personnel Department of DEYA X, the President and the Secretariat of the Board of Directors of the latter, as well as to the Director of the Administrative – Financial Department, an email message that included data concerning the complainant, particularly a detailed analysis of her presentation at the Board of Directors meeting of the complainant on 29-03-2022 regarding her illness from coronavirus. The purpose of this message, according to the complainant, was to inquire with the Head of the Administrative Department of DEYA X whether the complainant had submitted a Certificate of Negative Diagnostic Test for coronavirus posted on the gov.gr system during the relevant meeting of 29-03-2022.  

From the examination of the case file, it also appears that there was, on the part of the General Director of the complainant, the sending of a document with reference number … to all members of the Board of Directors of the latter, which communicated the document with reference number (of DEYA X) … that had been sent to the Independent Directorate of Civil Protection of Region Ψ. With this document, which included as attachments, among other things, the medical opinions of the complainant and the results of the diagnostic tests to which she had been subjected, the aforementioned Independent Directorate was requested to provide information on the measures that DEYA X must take in order to comply with the urgent measures to address the negative consequences of the emergence of the coronavirus and the need to limit its spread, as well as for the legitimization of the complainant's in-person participation in the relevant meeting of 29-03-2022.  

The two aforementioned processing actions, regardless of the legality of the initial collection of the medical opinion dated 28-03-2022, which the complainant herself submitted to DEYA X, took place within the sphere of the same data controller, to the extent that they were communicated to certain employees of DEYA X as well as to the members of its Board of Directors. However, in order to serve the purposes for which the above processing took place, it was not necessary to communicate all of the said data to all recipients, even within the sphere of the same Data Controller, namely DEYA X. Specifically, in the first case, the purpose of sending the message, within the sphere of DEYA X as the data controller, which consisted of the inquiry to the Head of the Administrative Department of the complainant regarding whether the complainant had submitted a Certificate of Negative Diagnostic Test for coronavirus on the date of her participation in the relevant Board meeting on 29-03-2022, could have been achieved by sending the relevant message only to the Head of the Administrative Department, without communicating it to the other recipients, especially considering that the meeting with the presence of the complainant had already taken place. Likewise, for the second aforementioned communication to all members of the Board of Directors...
17  
members of the Board of Directors of DEYA X, thus again within the sphere of the data controller, a document that addressed specific questions to another service including a series of documents that contained both simple personal data of the complainant as well as special categories of such data, it is clearly established that the purpose of informing the members of the Board of Directors, beyond the fact that it seems initially to lack meaning since they were already informed about the health status of the complainant from the date of the contentious meeting on 29-03-2022, in which the complainant was present in person, and any subsequent knowledge of this information would be difficult to justify for the sake of protecting the health of employees -contradicting the claims to the contrary made by DEYA X11-, could only be achieved by sending the document to the Independent Directorate of Civil Protection of Region Ψ without the other accompanying documents or simply referencing the questions posed for response to that Directorate. Therefore, it follows from the above that DEYA X, as the data controller, in violation of the principle of data minimization, disclosed the complainant's data to more recipients even within the sphere of the same data controller, without such processing being necessary, as previously indicated. Furthermore, from the elements of the file, there is neither evidence nor is there any internal policy or other internal document presented within which specific service recipients of employee data within the sphere of the complainant as the data controller are identified, from which classified access to information constituting special category data (health data) is also determined. The above finding regarding the non-existence of relevant internal policies is further reinforced by the fact that again within the sphere of the same Data Controller, namely DEYA X, the complainant as an employee of the latter became aware of service documents concerning her, of which she was not a recipient. Moreover, it is noted that the limitation of the contentious personal data as well as the recipients of such personal data of the complainant to those necessary for achieving the respective intended purpose cannot, by its nature, undermine the effective administration of DEYA X, especially considering that these events occurred after the presence of the complainant at the contentious meeting of the Board of Directors on 29-03-2022, while the present members of that Board appear from the elements of the file to have already been informed about the health status of the complainant, as mentioned above. Regarding the claim of DEYA X, which is first raised in its post-hearing memorandum12, that the informing of its Board members was necessary for reasons of readjusting service priorities due to the absence of the complainant as well as for ensuring the smooth operation of the service, this is rejected as irrelevant and therefore inadmissible to the extent that even an accurate supposed claim -which would be difficult to accept given that it concerned the absence of one employee- in any case, for the informing of the Board members in both of the above cases, the acts of processing the complainant's contentious data, as they occurred as detailed above, were not necessarily required by logical necessity. Finally, it is noted that the issue to be discussed at the contentious meeting of the Board of Directors of the complainant concerned a matter of the complainant's professional advancement13 and therefore the aforementioned health data that underwent the said processing acts were in no way critical for the members of the Board of Directors of DEYA X, to the extent that they were not related to their judgment in the context of issuing a decision on the matter to be discussed at the meeting. Therefore, a violation of the provision of Article 5(1)(c) of the GDPR is established for both of the above-described autonomous processing acts, rejecting all related claims of the complainant.
