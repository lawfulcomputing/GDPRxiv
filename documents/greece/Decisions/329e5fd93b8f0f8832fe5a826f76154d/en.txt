CONSIDERED IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LAW

1. From the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679 – hereinafter, GDPR) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), it follows that the Authority has the competence to supervise the application of the provisions of the GDPR, this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data. Specifically, from the provisions of Articles 57 paragraph 1 point (f) of the GDPR and 13 paragraph 1 point (z) of Law 4624/2019, it follows that the Authority has the competence to address the complaint of A against Alpha Bank and to exercise, correspondingly, the powers granted to it by the provisions of Articles 58 of the GDPR and 15 of Law 4624/2019. 
2. With Article 5 paragraph 1 of the General Regulation (EU) 2016/679 for the protection of individuals against the processing of personal data (hereinafter GDPR), the principles that must govern a processing are established. According to Article 5 paragraph 1 (a) and (f) of the GDPR, "1. Personal data shall be: a) processed lawfully and fairly in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject ('lawfulness, fairness, and transparency'), […] f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures ('integrity and confidentiality')," while as noted in the Preamble of the Regulation, "Personal data should be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate protection and confidentiality of personal data, including to prevent any unauthorized access.
11

"to this personal data and the equipment used for their processing or the use of this personal data and the said equipment" (Recital 39 in fine). Furthermore, according to the principle of accountability explicitly defined in the second paragraph of the same article and which constitutes a cornerstone of the GDPR, the data controller "bears the responsibility and is able to demonstrate compliance with paragraph 1 ("accountability")." This principle entails the obligation of the data controller to be able to demonstrate compliance with the principles of Article 5 paragraph 1.

3. According to the provision of Article 24 paragraph 1 GDPR: "1. Taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller implements appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is performed in accordance with this Regulation. These measures are reviewed and updated as necessary," while according to the provisions of paragraphs 1 and 2 of Article 32 GDPR regarding the security of processing, "1. Taking into account the latest developments, the cost of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks of varying likelihood and severity for the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller and the processor implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an appropriate level of security against the risks, […] 2. In assessing the appropriate level of security, particular consideration is given to the risks arising from the processing, in particular from accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure, or access to personal data that have been transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed."

4. According to Article 4 paragraph 12 GDPR, a personal data breach is defined as "a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to personal data that have been transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed." According to the Guidelines 18/2018 of the Article 29 Working Party of Directive 95/46/EC (now European Data Protection Board) on Personal Data Breach Notification (“Guidelines on Personal data breach notification under Regulation 2016/679” WP 250 rev. 1), one type of personal data breach is categorized based on the security principle of "confidentiality," when unauthorized access to personal data is detected (“confidentiality breach”). A breach may potentially have various significant adverse consequences for individuals, which can lead to physical, material, or moral harm. The GDPR explains that this harm may include loss of control over their personal data, restriction of their rights, discrimination, identity theft or misuse, financial loss, unlawful reversal of pseudonymization, damage to reputation, and loss of confidentiality of personal data protected by professional secrecy, etc. (see also recitals 85 and 75).

5. Data breach incidents must be reported to the Authority within 72 hours from the moment the data controller becomes aware of them, according to Article 33 paragraph 1 GDPR: "1. In the case of a personal data breach, the data controller shall notify without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach, the supervisory authority competent pursuant to Article 55, unless the personal data breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. When the notification to the supervisory authority is not made within 72 hours, it shall be accompanied by reasons for the delay." Furthermore, according to recital 85, as soon as the data controller becomes aware of a personal data breach, "it should notify the competent supervisory authority without undue delay, unless it can demonstrate, in accordance with the principle of accountability, that the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons."
13

"A personal data breach may not pose a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons." It is noted that according to the wording of Article 33 of the GDPR and in contrast to the wording of Article 34 of the GDPR, the obligation to notify the Authority does not require the existence of a "high risk" for the data subject but simply a "risk" to their rights and freedoms. According to the aforementioned Guidelines of the EDPB 29, "Article 33(1) makes it clear that, in the case of a breach that 'is not likely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons', notification to the supervisory authority is not required. An example may be the case where personal data is already publicly available and its disclosure does not pose a likely risk to the individual" (wp 250 rev.01, p.21). From the above, it follows that the GDPR establishes a "presumption" of the obligation to notify the Authority of breach incidents, with the sole exception being the absence of risk to the rights and freedoms of the affected data subjects, for which the data controller bears the burden of proof if they choose not to make such notification.

6. The notification must contain at least the minimum information referred to in paragraph 3 of Article 33 of the GDPR, while according to paragraph 5 of the same article, "The data controller documents every personal data breach, consisting of the facts relating to the personal data breach, its effects, and the remedial actions taken. This documentation allows the supervisory authority to verify compliance with the current article." Regarding the time of knowledge of the incident by the Data Controller, the aforementioned Guidelines 18/2018 of the EDPB (wp 250) state the following: "As detailed above, the GDPR requires, in the event of a breach, that the data controller notify the breach without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of it. This raises the question of when a data controller can be considered to have 'knowledge' of a breach. The EDPB considers that a data controller should be deemed to have 'knowledge' when that data controller has a reasonable degree of certainty that a security incident has occurred that has resulted in personal data being compromised. However, as mentioned earlier, the GDPR requires the data controller to implement all appropriate technical and organizational measures for the immediate detection of any breach and the prompt notification of the supervisory authority and data subjects. It also states that it should be established that the notification was made without undue delay, taking into account in particular the nature and severity of the personal data breach, as well as the consequences and adverse effects for the data subject. In this way, the data controller is obliged to ensure that they acquire 'knowledge' of any breaches in a timely manner so that they can take appropriate actions. The exact point in time when a data controller can be considered to have 'knowledge' of a specific breach will depend on the circumstances of that particular breach. In some cases, it will be relatively clear from the outset that a breach has occurred, while in others, it may take some time to ascertain whether personal data has been compromised. However, emphasis should be placed on timely action to address the situation."
15

"investigation of an incident, in order to determine whether personal data has been breached and, in such case, to take corrective measures and notify, if required." According to the above, Article 33 of the GDPR not only imposes the obligation to notify the supervisory authority of breach incidents but also the obligation to actively investigate any potential incident, provided that the data controller becomes aware of relevant indications. Otherwise, the data controller could easily circumvent its obligation to notify the supervisory authority of breach incidents by simply ignoring the indications of a potential incident and avoiding to ascertain and to "become aware" of it according to the above provision. Moreover, from paragraph 3 of Article 33 of the GDPR (content of the notification), it follows that the data controller has the obligation to promptly investigate the information mentioned therein, so as to be able to include it in the notification to the Authority (nature of the breach, categories and number of affected subjects, number of affected records, potential consequences of the breach, measures taken or proposed to address the breach, measures to mitigate potential adverse consequences), as well as to assess the risk to the rights and freedoms of the data subject,2 in order to decide whether notification is required to them under Article 34 of the GDPR, while paragraph 5 of Article 33 of the GDPR explicitly states the obligation to maintain documentation for all the above processes. Moreover, based on the principle of accountability (Article 5, paragraph 2 of the GDPR), the data subject who has notified the data controller of a potential data breach is not required to take further active action for the latter to ascertain the existence or non-existence of a breach incident. This obligation rests with the data controller, who bears the responsibility to demonstrate its compliance.

7. Furthermore, the breach must also be communicated to the data subject, as applicable and in accordance with the provisions of Article 34, paragraphs 1 and 2 of the GDPR: "1. When a personal data breach is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, the data controller shall notify the personal data breach to the data subject without undue delay. 2. The notification to the data subject referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article shall describe in clear and plain language the nature of the personal data breach and shall contain at least the information and measures referred to in Article 33, paragraph 3, points (b), (c), and (d)."

8. In the present case, given the above-mentioned factual circumstances arising from the file's evidence and following the hearing process, it is initially established that the provision of personal data by the reported Bank regarding the use of the complainant's credit card to his wife was done unlawfully, in violation of the principle of legality, objectivity, and transparency of processing (Article 5, paragraph 1(a) of the GDPR) and in violation of the principle of confidentiality of data (Article 5, paragraph 1(f) of the GDPR). This unauthorized processing (disclosure by transmission) constitutes a personal data breach incident, according to the definition in Article 4, number 12 of the GDPR, which is attributed to an employee's error at the Bank. Specifically, as revealed by the Bank's relevant investigation and the conduct of a disciplinary review, on …, during the visit of the complainant's wife to the Alpha Bank branch Ω Χ, with the purpose of being informed about the performance of joint investment products held with the complainant, the wife requested to be informed about the balance and transactions that had been conducted with the credit card that the complainant held exclusively in his name, assuring the employee that she had a relevant verbal authorization and...
17

Authorization from the complainant, as the data subject. The employee, misled regarding the existence of authorization, printed and provided the complainant's current personal credit card transactions to the complainant's wife, in violation of the relevant internal procedures and security measures of the Bank, which require written authorization (see memorandum of the respondent: "It is evident that this particular employee wrongly accepted the verbal authorization of the wife as sufficient, despite the internal instructions and procedures of the Bank"). Subsequently, although the respondent Bank was informed of this incident on … by the complainant, who even named one of the Bank's branches in the area of X as a possible source of the leak ("I suspect X"), and although it is evident that there was the possibility of checking the accesses that had occurred during the relevant period to the complainant's account information by its employees through its systems, the Bank did not take immediate active action to investigate the incident in order to obtain the necessary degree of certainty and to comply with its obligations under Article 33 of the GDPR, considering that the information it had at its disposal was not sufficient. Furthermore, although the complainant sent a related photograph of the account transaction copy that his wife had shown him to the Bank on …, the Bank's Service "inadvertently," as it claims, did not forward this information to the Internal Audit Department, resulting in it still being unable, in its opinion, to initiate the investigation. In fact, this information does not appear to have been forwarded to the Internal Audit Department even on …, when the latter, in a message to the Bank's Service, requested clarifications and further information regarding the case (see relevant document G/EIS/125/09-01-2023 from the respondent's opinions). Ultimately, the photograph of the printout of the complainant's credit card transactions was sent to the Internal Audit Department with significant delay on …, that is, 1.5 months after the Bank received the relevant report from the complainant to the Consumer Ombudsman (…), which ultimately led to all information being sent to the Internal Audit Department, which finally issued the report on …

Subsequently, the Incident Assessment Committee met on … (see the G/EIS/125/09-01-2023 response of the Bank), that is, 20 days after the above report. During the meeting, according to the same response, it was assessed that "based on the number and type of data concerning .. transactions via credit card that occurred over a period of approximately … months (… to …) and the fact that the unauthorized recipients of the above data are limited to one individual and indeed a relative of the complainant (spouse), there does not appear to be a risk to the freedoms and rights of the subject." However, this assessment by the Bank is evidently erroneous, given that it is certain that the incident had consequences on the rights and freedoms of the subject.

9. From the above-mentioned factual circumstances, a series of erroneous actions and omissions in the management of the incident of breach in question by the complained Bank emerge. Specifically, the Bank, as the data controller, despite having indications of a possible breach incident, initially did not investigate it, shifting the responsibility for identifying the source of the leak to the data subject, subsequently delayed significantly in handling it as a breach incident due to a lack of coordination among its competent Units, then underestimated its consequences for the data subject, and incorrectly assessed that it was not obliged to notify the Authority pursuant to Article 33 of the GDPR. The identified deficiencies and delays in the internal handling of the case do not appear to be due to inadequate Policies and Procedures of the Bank in accordance with Article 24(2) of the GDPR, as the actions that its bodies and services are obliged to follow without delay in the event of a potential breach incident are provided for in the documents that the Bank invoked and submitted (see Annexes 4-6 of the Bank's response letter No. Γ/ΕΙΣ/125/09-01-2023), but rather to the non-compliance with these procedures in the present case. Following the above, the Bank is found liable for the fact that it delayed for many months to investigate the incident in order to obtain a reasonable degree of certainty and to handle it as a breach incident, as well as for the fact that after confirming the incident, it did not proceed to notify the Authority nor did it take measures to mitigate its consequences.

10. Therefore, the Authority finds the following violations on the part of the complained Bank, as the data controller: 
a) unlawful processing of the complainant's personal data, due to the transmission of his credit card transactions to a third party, which occurred without a legal basis, in violation of the principles of lawfulness of processing and data confidentiality (Article 5(1)(a) and (f) of the GDPR) and constitutes a breach incident (Article 4(12) of the GDPR).
21

b) Incorrect handling of the incident and failure to notify the Authority in violation of Article 33 GDPR.  
11. Based on the above, the Authority deems it appropriate to exercise its corrective powers under Article 58(2) of the GDPR concerning the identified violations and that, based on the circumstances established, an effective, proportionate, and deterrent administrative fine should be imposed pursuant to Article 58(2)(h) of the GDPR, both to restore compliance and to punish the unlawful behavior. Furthermore, the Authority took into account the criteria for imposing fines set out in Article 83(2) of the GDPR, paragraph 5(a) of the same article applicable to the present case, the Guidelines for the application and determination of administrative fines for the purposes of Regulation 2016/679 issued on 03-10-2017 by the Article 29 Working Party (WP 253), and the Guidelines 04/2022 of the European Data Protection Board for calculating administrative fines under the General Regulation, as well as the factual data of the case under examination and particularly the criteria listed below, per violation.  
A. Regarding the first identified violation (see paragraph 10(a)), the following specific circumstances are particularly taken into account:  
a) that the violation of the legality of processing falls under the provision of paragraph 5 of Article 83 GDPR,  
b) that the incident appears to be isolated, as no sanction has been imposed by the Authority on the Bank for a similar violation in the past,  
c) that the violation directly affected one data subject, …,  
d) that the violation was due to human error by an employee attributed to negligence, while the Bank has established appropriate procedures that require written authorization as a condition for providing information to a third party,  
e) that the Bank did not take measures to mitigate the immediate effects of the incident for the data subject, such as contacting the data recipient and requesting them to return to the Bank or destroy the data that was unlawfully provided to them,  
f) that, however, the Bank ultimately conducted a disciplinary review of the employee who violated these procedures and imposed a disciplinary penalty.  
B. Regarding the second identified violation (see paragraph 10(b)), the following criteria are particularly taken into account:  
a) that the violation of the obligations of the data controller under Article 33 GDPR falls under the provision of paragraph 4 of Article 83 GDPR,  
b) the degree of responsibility of the complainant, who, although having taken appropriate organizational measures and established relevant policies for managing data breach incidents, was found not to have implemented them in practice in this case,  
c) that a delay of several months was identified in a process that should be conducted within 72 hours, indicating a possible attempt at concealment by the Bank, and in any case indifference towards compliance with its obligations under the GDPR,  
d) that in the context of the Authority's investigation of the complaint, the Bank shifted the burden of responsibility for investigating the incident onto the data subject, in a manner that contradicts the principle of accountability,  
e) that the Bank further disregarded the consequences of the incident on the data subject, of which it was aware and underestimated the risk to the subject to the point of characterizing it as "zero,"  
f) that based on the above, it appears that the overall incorrect and unlawful handling of the incident by the Bank constitutes a systemic problem that could potentially affect other customers.

B. Imposes on ALPHA BANK S.A., as the data controller, based on Article 58(2)(h) of the GDPR, an administrative fine of fifty thousand (50,000€) euros, for the identified violation of the obligation to handle and notify a data breach incident under Article 33 GDPR.
