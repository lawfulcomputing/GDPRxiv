Foreword  
Note  
It is with great satisfaction that I introduce the annual activity report of the Data Protection Authority for 2022. The report presents in detail the actions of the independent authority in all areas of its competence, as well as useful statistical data. At the same time, it reflects the work of the members and staff of the Authority who have exhausted the possibilities of maximizing the resources available to them for the fulfillment of the mission assigned to them by the State.  
In 2022, four years have passed since the implementation of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) began. This fundamental piece of legislation of the European Union aims to enhance the protection of personal data and, by extension, privacy in its broader form, which constitutes a fundamental value of European legal culture; it also aims to ensure the free movement of such data within the framework of the functioning of the internal market in the European Union, in light of technological developments and the rapidly increasing cross-border flow of personal data.  
It is well known that the GDPR has expanded the legal framework of the previous Directive 95/46/EC by providing for new measures and procedures, as well as by tightening the enforcement arsenal, based on which the possibility of imposing extremely high fines is granted. We observe, in fact, that national data protection authorities have also practically applied the stipulated high fines. It is characteristic that from the milestone date of May 25, 2018, until December 2022, the total amount of fines imposed by data protection authorities at the European level exceeds 2 billion euros. At the same time, there has been a gradual increase in the total amount of fines imposed by the Greek Data Protection Authority since the beginning of the GDPR's implementation. In the year 2022, which this annual report concerns, fines exceeding 30 million euros were imposed, compared to fines of 414 thousand euros that were imposed the previous year. However, it should be clarified that the Authority, acting in accordance with the general spirit of the legislation on the protection of personal data, does not aim at the imposition of sanctions, which is applied as a repressive measure with a deterrent purpose. The amount of fines imposed is not presented as a positive assessment of the Authority's work, but as an indication that towards the end of the first five years of the GDPR's implementation, significant violations of the rules that can no longer be justified continue to be identified. A general conclusion that has emerged from the first years of the Regulation's implementation is that compliance with its rules and, primarily, the incorporation of the spirit that governs data protection principles is a continuous exercise for organizations and businesses, but also that, in any case, the European Regulation has yielded results.  
The Data Protection Authority has responded to the maximum possible extent to the responsibilities and duties assigned to it by the European Regulation and national law, despite the longstanding problems arising from understaffing and lack of adequate resources combined with the continuously increasing flow of cases and other duties.  
I would like to mention, as an example, that, as evidenced by the data presented in the following pages, in 2022 the number of incoming cases of appeals/complaints, as well as the number of corresponding cases that were processed, increased by approximately 8% compared to 2021.  
In addition to examining complaints, the Authority consistently prioritizes the development of its proactive work, particularly its ex officio auditing activities, guidance actions, information – awareness-raising, as well as the analysis of new technologies and business models from the perspective of data protection, and finally, contributing to research efforts in the field of data protection and information security. Specifically, as indicative examples of exercising ex officio authority in 2022, the cases in which the Authority examined the processing of personal data that took place based on measures provided for by law to address the coronavirus and issues that arose in the context of modern distance education in primary and secondary education units following a decision from the previous year, in which the Authority had imposed a corrective measure and had issued recommendations to the Ministry of Education, are mentioned. Furthermore, following actions from previous years and after it was found that many websites do not provide visitors with the ability to accept or reject trackers through banners with the same ease, the Authority called in 2022...

The Authority continuously makes systematic efforts to raise awareness regarding the protection of personal data and to provide appropriate tools to facilitate compliance with the relevant protection rules. Given the limited financial resources, it utilizes funding opportunities provided by the European Commission through relevant programs for these actions. On another level of action, the Authority proceeded to sign Memoranda of Cooperation with the National Accreditation System (ESYD), as well as with the Competition Commission, with the aim of substantial cooperation between the parties when examining issues that fall within the interest or the responsibilities of both. The signing of Memoranda of Cooperation is part of initiatives that must continue in the coming years as it facilitates the work of the Authority and contributes to a more comprehensive handling of cases in areas where its responsibilities intersect with the work of other entities.  
Furthermore, the Authority responded to its ongoing European and international obligations and was actively present at the European Data Protection Board (EDPB), as well as in the working groups and committees operating under European data protection legislation. It should be noted that the EDPB has intensified its work. I mention, for example, that it issued nine Guidelines in 2022 on significant substantive and procedural issues, 32 opinions, as well as four joint opinions with the European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS) on proposals for Regulations from the European Parliament and the Council.  
National data protection authorities are firmly committed to the coherent implementation of the General Regulation using all means at their disposal. It is characteristic that during the EDPB Summit in Vienna at the end of April, they committed to further strengthen and expand their cooperation on strategically significant cases (e.g., cases involving a large number of subjects in the EEA, cases concerning a structural or recurring problem in the member states), so that the implementation of the Regulation becomes more effective. Beyond this, within the framework of the EDPB, national authorities undertake joint actions, such as the coordinated action regarding the use of cloud services by public authorities, which was initiated in 2022 and in which the Greek Authority also participates.  
In the coming years, both the EDPB and the national data protection supervisory authorities will need to devote significant effort to analyzing the impacts of the ever-increasing implementation of artificial intelligence systems in every area of activity and to formulating relevant recommendations and guidelines. Additionally, the impacts of the developing legal framework regarding the data strategy and the digital transformation strategy of the European Union should be analyzed, and effective cooperation mechanisms with other competent authorities should be established as appropriate.  
However, apart from the intensive efforts made by the Greek Authority to respond with its proactive and sanctioning actions to the high expectations of society and citizens, as it is obliged to do, it is crucial to emphasize once again that the competent state bodies must ensure that it has the necessary resources, in implementation of the relevant provisions of the Regulation. The rest is obviously our own responsibility and duty.

1.1. MISSION AND COMPETENCIES OF THE AUTHORITY  
The  
Data Protection Authority  
is a constitutionally established independent public Authority (Article 9A of the Constitution) founded by Law 2472/1997, which incorporated into Greek law the European Directive 95/46/EC on the protection of individuals with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data. It is served by its own Secretariat operating at the Directorate level and has its own budget.  
With Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council (General Data Protection Regulation - GDPR), which came into effect on 25/5/2018 in all EU countries, Directive 95/46/EC was repealed. From 29/8/2019, Law 4624/2019 ("Data Protection Authority, measures for the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, for the protection of individuals with regard to the processing of personal data and incorporation into national legislation of Directive (EU) 2016/680 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, and other provisions") is in effect. In the aforementioned law, Articles 9 to 20 refer to the Data Protection Authority (supervisory authority). Law 2472/1997 has been repealed except for certain provisions explicitly mentioned in Article 84 of Law 4624/2019. Law 4624/2019 also includes the incorporation into the Greek legal order of Directive (EU) 2016/680 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016, on the protection of individuals with regard to the processing of personal data by competent authorities for the purposes of preventing, investigating, detecting, or prosecuting criminal offenses or the execution of criminal sanctions and for the free movement of such data and the repeal of Framework Decision 2008/977/JHA of the Council.  
Furthermore, regarding the protection of personal data in the field of electronic communications, the Authority implements Law 3471/2006, which similarly incorporates the European Directive 2002/58/EC into national law.  
The Authority is responsible for overseeing the application of the provisions of the GDPR (Article 51 para. 1, rec. 123), Law 4624/2019, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data (Article 9 of Law 4624/2019).  
It contributes to the coherent application of the GDPR throughout the Union and for this purpose collaborates with the supervisory authorities of the EU member states and with the Commission (Article 51 para. 2, rec. 123 of the GDPR; Article 10 of Law 4624/2019). The Authority represents Greece in the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) and in other committees or bodies concerning data protection and collaborates with corresponding authorities of third countries and international organizations (Article 50 of the GDPR, Article 10 of Law 4624/2019).  
The Authority is competent to perform its duties (Articles 57 of the GDPR and 13 of Law 4624/2019) and to exercise the powers conferred upon it (Articles 58 of the GDPR and 15 of Law 4624/2019) within its territory (Article 55 para. 1, rec. 122, 129 of the GDPR; Article 9 of Law 4624/2019) with full independence (Article 52, rec. 117-118, 121 of the GDPR; Article 11 of Law 4624/2019).  
In particular, the Authority is, among other things, responsible for the following (Article 57 GDPR, Article 13 of Law 4624/2019):  
• To monitor and enforce the application of the GDPR, Law 4624/2019, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals against the processing of personal data.  
• To promote public awareness on issues of personal data protection and of those responsible for and executing the processing regarding their obligations. Special attention is given to activities aimed at children.  
• To provide opinions on any regulation intended to be included in a law or regulatory act concerning data processing.  
• To issue guidelines and make recommendations on any matter concerning data processing, subject to the duties of the EDPB.  
• To provide information upon request to data subjects regarding the exercise of their rights.  
• To handle complaints submitted for violations of the provisions of the GDPR.  
• To conduct investigations or audits regarding the application of data protection legislation.

Cooperation and Coherence  
In cases where cross-border data processing is conducted (Article 4, paragraph  
23 of the GDPR), the cooperation mechanism between the LSA and the concerned  
supervisory authorities generally applies, for which the primary responsibility for  
supervision lies with the LSA (Article 60, recitals 124-126 of the GDPR; WP244rev.01).  
The Authority is competent to act as the LSA for cross-border processing carried out  
by a data controller or processor who has its main or sole establishment in its  
territory, in accordance with the procedure provided for in Article 60 of the GDPR  
(Article 56, paragraph 1, recital 124 of the GDPR). In cases where one of the  
conditions mentioned in Article 4, paragraph 22 of the GDPR applies, the Authority  
acts as a concerned supervisory authority (Articles 60, 66, recitals 124-125, 130-131,  
143 of the GDPR). Additionally, it cooperates with the supervisory authorities of  
the EU member states by providing mutual assistance and conducting joint operations,  
on a bilateral or multilateral basis, to ensure the coherent application of the GDPR  
and the adoption of common control measures (Articles 61-62, recitals 133-134, 138  
of the GDPR).  
By way of derogation from the general rule, the Authority is competent to examine  
a submitted complaint or to address a potential violation of the GDPR in cases where  
cross-border processing has local impacts in its territory (cross-border processing  
with local impact), if the LSA decides not to take up the case after being informed  
by the Authority (Article 56, paragraphs 2-5, recital 127 of the GDPR). In such cases,  
the Authority takes up the case in accordance with Articles 61 and 62 of the GDPR  
(Article 56, paragraph 5 of the GDPR).  
Moreover, the Authority applies the consistency mechanism (recital 135 of the GDPR)  
when it intends to adopt any of the measures provided for in Article 64, paragraph 1  
of the GDPR (recital 136 of the GDPR) or upon request for an opinion to the EDPB  
regarding any issue of general application of the GDPR or an issue that produces  
effects in more than one member state (Article 64, paragraph 2 of the GDPR) or in  
the context of resolving disputes between supervisory authorities from the EDPB  
(Article 65, recital 136 of the GDPR) and the urgent procedure (Article 66, recital 137  
of the GDPR).

1.3. HUMAN RESOURCES  
The Authority, according to Article 20 paragraph 1 of its founding law 2472/1997 and Presidential Decree 207/1998 "Organization of the Secretariat of the Personal Data Protection Authority and establishment of organic positions," which remains in force under Article 18 paragraph 3 of Law 4624/2019, is served by a Secretariat that operates at the Directorate level and consists of four Departments: 1) the Auditors' Department, 2) the Communication Department, 3) the Administrative Affairs Department, and 4) the Financial Services Department, which was established according to the decision number G/EX/1435/21-2-2017 "Transfer and allocation of the responsibilities of Article 69Γ of Law 4270/2014 to the organic units of the Directorate of the Secretariat of the Authority. Establishment of a department of purely economic interest therein."  

1. Auditors' Department  
It has decisive responsibilities for fulfilling the mission of the Authority. Indicatively, this department is responsible for conducting administrative audits on any file, the preparation of regulatory acts for the regulation of specific, technical, and detailed issues, drafting guidance plans aimed at the uniform application of protective legislation for individuals, examining complaints, and preparing recommendations. Additionally, the Auditors' Department is involved in drafting the annual report of the Authority under the coordination of the Director of the Secretariat. Furthermore, the Auditors assist categories of data controllers in the preparation of codes of conduct and support their updating on personal data protection issues with presentations at conferences and seminars. They represent the Authority in European Union bodies, as well as in international working groups and conferences. They participate in the work of the European Data Protection Board and its expert subgroups. The responsibilities of the Department include the preparation of files, conducting studies, submitting recommendations, and generally assisting the Authority in the exercise of its responsibilities.  
The Department is staffed by legal and IT specialists, following the conversion of the organic positions of university education category into positions of specialized scientific personnel with an indefinite private law employment relationship, according to Law 4055/2012.  
The number of paid organic positions in the Auditors' Department in 2022 was 29, of which 17 were lawyers and 12 were IT specialists, including the Director of the Secretariat of the Authority.  
It is noted that one lawyer (EEP-IDACH) is on long-term unpaid leave as she works in a European organization in which Greece is also a participant, one lawyer (EEP-IDACH) has been seconded to the Ministry of Health for five years, one lawyer (EEP-IDACH) is on secondment to a European organization, and one IT specialist (EEP-IDACH) was absent on a nine-month leave for the upbringing of his minor child.  
Consequently, the number of personnel serving in the Auditors' Department in 2022 was 25 (14 lawyers and 11 IT specialists).  

2. Communication Department  
It has extremely significant responsibilities for achieving the mission of the Authority. In general, the main responsibility of the Department is to assist the Authority in shaping and implementing its communication policy as well as submitting relevant recommendations. Specifically, the Communication Department is tasked with organizing informational and scientific workshops, seminars, and conferences on issues related to the Authority's field of responsibilities, managing public relations with other public services and organizations, private individuals, and foreign services, including the relevant bodies of the European Union, issuing informational bulletins, brochures, and press releases, as well as creating radio and television messages for the Authority. Additionally, its responsibilities include creating and editing content for the Authority's website, monitoring and summarizing the media on personal data protection issues, as well as internal communication, organizing press conferences, and managing journalistic inquiries and requests for statements or interviews, ensuring the editing, publication, and dissemination of the Authority's annual activity report, as well as drafting the section on communication policy under the coordination of the Director of the Secretariat, and finally maintaining the Authority's library. The Communication Department also represents the Authority in the Communication Network of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB Communication Network).  
The Department is staffed by communication and translation specialists following the conversion of the organic positions of scientific personnel of university education category in the communication sector of the Authority into positions of specialized scientific personnel according to Article 18 paragraph 8.

1.4. FINANCIAL DATA  
According to the provisions of Article 19 of Law 4624/2019, the Personal Data Protection Authority, as an independent public authority, prepares its own budget, which is drafted under the responsibility of its President and submitted directly to the General Accounting Office of the State, in accordance with the procedure provided in the Public Accounting. The execution of the Authority's budget belongs exclusively to it, within the framework of its full autonomy, and the authorizer of the expenses is its President.  
For the fiscal year 2022, the Authority's budget was set at the amount of €2,523,000. The approved budget for 2023 amounts to €2,219,000.

1.5. OPERATIONAL PROBLEMS, GOALS, AND PROPOSALS  
Among the most serious operational issues that concerned the Authority in 2022 were the acute problem of housing, the pressing need to strengthen the human resources and resources of the Authority, the related exemption of the Authority from the ASEP procedures, the reorganization of its Secretariat, and the institutional strengthening of the Authority, mainly in its auditing work. The following sections describe these problems in more detail and present actions, goals, and proposals of the Authority for improving its operational framework.  
Since 2019, the Authority has foreseen that the issue of its housing was evolving into a particularly serious problem. The Authority communicated to the Real Estate Service and the Ministry of Finance the need to adjust the rent of office spaces to market prices, but without any response. In 2020, the Authority revised its building program and submitted a relevant housing request to the Athens Real Estate Service, and since then, until today, summer 2023, six announcements for bidding for property leasing have been published, which were unsuccessful, as well as five invitations for direct leasing without success. After the sixth invitation for direct leasing, two offers were submitted, which were rejected by the relevant Housing Committee (formed by the Real Estate Service). It is emphasized at this point that the limit on the rent amount per unit area that applies to the Public Sector is low, which is far from the prevailing rental prices in the real estate market in recent years, making the entire endeavor of finding housing for the Authority even more difficult. The problem would have been resolved if the Real Estate Service had the flexibility to respond to the conditions of the real estate market, even at a lower level than current prices. The current housing situation of the Authority's offices is as follows: In March 2023, the Authority was forced to vacate one of the two floors it occupied, as a result of a temporarily enforceable court decision regarding an eviction lawsuit by the owner of that floor, leading to the inability to house a large part of its services. There is an immediate risk for the office spaces of the floor to which the Authority has been restricted, as the owner of that floor has requested an increase in rent, which the Ministry of Finance does not accept based on the applicable legislation, and has already filed an eviction lawsuit in the Single-Member Court of First Instance of Athens, for which the hearing is set for 06.10.2023.  
The risk of complete inability to house the Authority is evident, and for this reason, it is necessary to seek measures to resolve the problem, possibly by taking the necessary legislative or other measures, such as providing the Authority with a suitable building from the broader Public Sector for its housing or allocating the necessary funds at a level that reflects the actual market conditions.  
As emphasized on every occasion, since its establishment, the Authority has been characterized by particularly inadequate staffing. It has never been possible to harmonize its staffing with the broad range of responsibilities arising from European and national legislation and the workload that these entail. The continuously increasing volume of incoming cases is a significant inhibiting factor in the Authority's efforts to fully respond to its mission. The negative impact of understaffing, combined with the Authority's low budget, is greater in the development of preventive work, such as systematic audits, and in the awareness-raising of data subjects, responsible parties, and processors. The current active staff of the Authority's Secretariat amounts to approximately 50, out of the 75 planned positions, distributed among specialized scientific personnel in legal and IT fields and communication, as well as administrative and financial staff. The staffing of the Authority significantly lags behind that of equivalent supervisory authorities in other member states. For example, in Hungary, the corresponding Authority has a staff of 128, while in the Netherlands, the staff is 270. The filling of 13 positions for Auditors (legal and IT) requested by the Authority and announced through ASEP in 2018 was completed, despite its urgent nature, after three years, and mainly constitutes a replacement of vacant positions due to departures during the economic crisis, and is included in the aforementioned 50 paid active positions. According to a related study accompanying the draft Organization for the reorganization of the Secretariat, the work that the Authority is called upon to perform requires a minimum total staff of 135 positions, which it assessed as necessary to meet its basic obligations. For this reason, the initial draft of its Organization provided for a corresponding number of positions, but this number was reduced to 91, following a relevant indication from the Ministry of the Interior. Therefore, it is necessary to fill these positions as soon as possible after the completion of the approval process of the Organization.  
At the same time, it becomes necessary to increase the financial resources allocated to the Authority for its operational expenses, beyond the funds intended for staff salaries.  
It is also noted that the GDPR, in paragraph 4 of Article 52, provides as an obligation for each member state to allocate to the national supervisory authority "…the necessary human, technical, and financial resources and the necessary facilities and infrastructures for the effective performance of its duties and the exercise of its powers, including those exercised within the framework of mutual assistance, cooperation, and participation in the Data Protection Council." The
The country's response to this obligation remains pending. Since its establishment and until the early years of the economic crisis, the Authority was exempt from the ASEP procedures for hiring its personnel, which aligned with its independence. Despite the fact that ASEP is also an independent authority, its involvement deprives the Authority of the ability to precisely determine the timing of the announcement for filling positions, the duration of the process, and the final selection of personnel, thus violating its independence. The following experience of the Authority regarding the mandatory involvement of ASEP in its personnel recruitment is particularly revealing. In 2018, immediately after the implementation of the GDPR and when the Authority had an urgent need to strengthen its human resources due to departures in the previous years of the economic crisis, the filling of 20 permanent positions was approved: 13 auditor positions, 2 communication positions, and 5 administrative-economic positions. ASEP agreed to announce only the 13 auditor positions, the filling of which was completed in 3 years, while the announcement of the remaining 7 positions was delayed by 4 years, and their filling is still pending, 5 years later. Despite the urgent need to strengthen the Authority with personnel, the provision from national law for hiring only through ASEP deprives the Authority of the ability to manage its human resource needs in a timely and effective manner, as it can be inferred from the aforementioned, ASEP's procedures are particularly rigid and time-consuming, suitable for large organizations with long-term planning and low sensitivity to sudden reductions in their personnel, but completely unsuitable for smaller authorities and other entities, such as the Authority, which must flexibly address sudden departures of even 1 or 2 of its staff members. For this reason, given the urgent need to strengthen the Authority with human resources, it is absolutely necessary to legislate full management of its personnel by the Authority itself, including the filling of approved vacant positions, as required by the GDPR. It is recalled that paragraph 5 of Article 52 of the GDPR states, "Each member state ensures that each supervisory authority selects and employs its own staff who are managed exclusively by the member or members...".

In addition to the above, it is noted that, despite the aforementioned provision of the GDPR and the continuous requests of the Authority for its exemption from ASEP procedures, legislation was promoted and passed to exempt other independent authorities and committees from ASEP procedures, such as EETT, the Competition Commission, and the Single Public Procurement Authority (see the relevant articles on hiring, Articles 56 and 57, and on secondments and transfers, Article 58 of Law 5043/2023 [Government Gazette Issue A’ 91/13.04.2023]). Following this recent legislative regulation, it is required that the Authority be included in the scope of Articles 56, 57, and 58 of Law 5043/2023. The inclusion of the Authority in the scope of the aforementioned articles constitutes a rational regulation and does not overturn the rule of filling public service positions through ASEP procedures due to its small size and therefore greater sensitivity to sudden reductions in its personnel, compared to larger committees or authorities (e.g., EETT and the Competition Commission) that are already covered by these regulations. Furthermore, the inclusion of the Authority in these regulations would also be a response from the State to the provisions of paragraph 5 of Article 52 of the GDPR mentioned above.

Regarding the reorganization of its Secretariat, the Authority submitted a draft presidential decree for the approval of a relevant Organization, according to the authorization of paragraph 3 of Article 18 of Law 4624/2019, yet the process has not yet been completed (as of early September 2023). The reorganization of the Authority's services became necessary due to the new data protection framework in the European Union and the country. Initially, the draft Organization was submitted to the Ministry of Justice on 22.11.2022, and subsequently, with significant delays, it was forwarded to the Ministry of the Interior and finally to the Ministry of Finance, taking into account all the comments made by the Authority, including the serious reduction of the projected necessary number of permanent positions from 135 to 91, following a relevant suggestion from the Ministry of the Interior. This process is being repeated for the third time after the elections of June 25, 2023, without having been completed by early September.

Regarding the institutional strengthening of the Authority, it is first proposed to establish a process for amicable settlement for the rapid handling of complaints in cases of minor significance for the rights of data subjects, and secondly, to give the Authority the ability to obtain necessary information for the audits it conducts from any other public authority that may possess it, in order to make its auditing powers more effective. Furthermore, amendments to Law 4624/2019 are proposed to eliminate contradictory provisions. Specifically, regarding the first two, the following are proposed:

1. The addition of a paragraph to Article 13 of Law 4624/2019 with the following content: "By decision of the Authority, amicable settlement procedures may be applied according to what is stipulated in the Regulation issued under Article 17 of this law, for the resolution of complaints concerning alleged violations, which are estimated to: (a) affect a limited number of data subjects or (b) have limited consequences for the rights and freedoms of data subjects." This process may...
**1.** It applies in cases of complaints which the Authority assesses as having limited consequences for the data subjects, while aiming to satisfy the data subjects and simultaneously ensure the compliance of the data controller with the legislation. If the process proves unsuccessful or is time-consuming, the Authority exercises the full range of its powers, while in any case retains the discretion to examine any issue concerning the processing of personal data ex officio. It is noted that, based on Article 58(6) of the GDPR, Member States have the option to provide by law that the supervisory authority has additional powers.

**2.** The replacement of Article 15(1), second paragraph of Law 4624/2019 with the following: "During the conduct of investigations and audits, the Authority has the power to obtain from the data controller, the processor, and any third natural or legal person, access to all personal data that is subject to processing and all information required for the purposes of the relevant audit and the execution of its duties, without any kind of confidentiality being able to be invoked against it." Additionally, the addition of a paragraph to Article 39 of Law 4624/2019 with the following content: "In the case of refusal to provide information, data, documents, and personal data, obstruction or hindrance of the Authority's investigations and audits in the case of Article 15(1), second paragraph, including the case of providing inaccurate, misleading, or incomplete information and personal data, an administrative fine of up to €1,000,000 shall be imposed on third natural or legal persons. For determining the amount of the fine, the criteria mentioned in paragraph 2 shall be taken into account, to the extent that they are relevant."

Another issue related to ensuring adequate staffing of the Authority is the provision of incentives for the retention of its personnel. As previously mentioned, particularly during the years of the economic crisis, a large percentage of specialized scientific personnel left, mainly in search of better working conditions. Moreover, as was found during the selection process of the 13 auditors mentioned above, most of the initially selected candidates did not accept due to excessively low remuneration for their qualifications. To retain the personnel of the Authority, particularly the specialized scientific personnel, which constitutes approximately 2/3 of the total, a significant improvement in their remuneration is required, in relation to their contribution to the Authority's work. Furthermore, with Article 59 of the recent Law 5043/2023, special provisions were established for the personnel of seven legally (and not constitutionally) provided independent authorities. Specifically, paragraphs 1 to 6 of this article establish a system of incentives and rewards, paragraph 7 provides for the establishment of committees and working groups to examine issues that require special management or immediate resolution, and sets a maximum percentage of personnel that may participate in these committees and a maximum limit for additional remuneration, while paragraph 8 sets the maximum allowable limit for overtime. It is considered reasonable or even necessary to include the personnel of the Authority in the above provisions in order to enhance the likelihood of their retention in the Authority and to eliminate any discrimination against them in this matter. Furthermore, it is proposed that the provisions of paragraph 1 of Article 25 of Law 4940/2022, which concerns the financial services of public entities, be applied accordingly to the personnel of the Authority.

For the smooth and effective operation of the Authority, in addition to strengthening the human resources to a satisfactory number and ensuring adequate financial resources, the creation of advanced computing and networking infrastructures is also necessary, including electronic services for use by the controllers, processors, and data subjects. In this direction, following the upgrade of the integrated information system (IIS) and the new online portal of the Authority, which were put into full productive operation at the beginning of 2021, in 2022 the implementation of the project for the expansion of the Authority's IIS began with new, more automated electronic services, such as management of data breach incidents, (self) assessment of security and data protection, and management of audits, as well as the creation of informative content to raise awareness among children on data protection issues. It is noted that this project has been included in the Operational Program "Public Sector Reform" of the NSRF and utilizes funding from the European Union. Furthermore, in 2022, the implementation of the project funded by a European Commission program, byDesign, was completed, with the main results being the creation of a supportive online application for use by controllers and processors (mainly small and medium-sized enterprises) to facilitate their compliance with the GDPR, and the creation of a training program and materials on data protection by design and by default aimed at professionals involved in the development, promotion, and use of information and communication products and services that are also used for the processing of personal data. Additionally, in September 2022, the implementation of another project of the Authority, also funded by a European Commission program, the project byDefault, began following a relevant proposal from the Authority and its successful evaluation by the European Commission. This project aims to create appropriate educational materials for primary and secondary education students on the subject of data protection.

In 2022, the number of incoming cases of appeals/complaints amounted to 1,250, an increase of approximately 8% compared to 2021 (from 1,160), while 876 cases of appeals/complaints were processed, also showing an increase of approximately 8% compared to the previous year (from 811). The number of data breach incidents reported to the Authority under the GDPR was 174, a decrease of approximately 4% compared to the previous year (from 181 in 2021), while 35 notifications of data breaches were submitted by electronic communications service providers, based on Law 3471/2006, showing a decrease of approximately 20% (from 44 in 2021), as well as 1 incident according to Article 63 of Law 4624/2019, meaning that the total incidents of breaches showed a decrease of approximately 7%. In 67 cases, the examination of the cases was completed with a decision issued by the Plenary or the Department. Additionally, in 2022, the Authority issued 5 opinions.  
It is noted that since 2018, the presented statistical data differ from those of previous years, reflecting changes in the Authority's responsibilities that primarily occurred with the implementation of the GDPR. Specifically, statistical data for inquiries from data controllers are no longer presented, as the Authority no longer has this duty, nor for notifications of processing records or international transfers and licenses, as there is no corresponding obligation for data controllers or authority jurisdiction, respectively. On the other hand, the cooperation of the Authority with counterpart supervisory authorities of member states for cross-border cases (one-stop-shop mechanism) is now provided for, as well as within the framework of the European Data Protection Board (consistency mechanism), the prior consultation with the Authority for high residual risk processing after conducting a data protection impact assessment, the examination and approval by the Authority of codes of conduct and certification criteria of relevant schemes, etc., with the related activities described in the following Chapter 3. Beyond these, the provision of information to data subjects, upon request, for the exercise of their rights is also provided, which is done either orally or with a written response.  
The total number of incoming documents to the Authority in 2022 amounted to 12,917, an increase of approximately 55% compared to 2021 (from 8,320). Compared to 2017 ‒ when the highest annual number of incoming documents (10,991) was recorded before 2022 ‒ the increase in incoming documents amounts to approximately 18%. In the years 2018 to 2021, the flow of incoming documents was restrained, initially due to the implementation of the GDPR and in 2020 and 2021 due to the pandemic. As expected, there is now a significant increase in the flow of incoming documents and, correspondingly, in cases for the Authority, an element that indicates the urgent need to increase its human resources and the resources available to it. Regarding the method of submission of incoming documents to the Authority, the very high percentage of electronic submissions in 2020 and 2021 was maintained in 2022, as it reached approximately 96% (from about 90% in 2020 and 96.8% in 2021), and specifically, submissions via email accounted for approximately 70% (from 64.82% in 2021) and through the online portal accounted for 26.2% (from 32% in 2021 and 5% in 2020). We observe a stabilization in the degree of use of email and the electronic services of the Authority offered through the online portal. Of course, the Authority seeks to maximize the use of its electronic services, with the greatest possible margin for the automation of document management tasks, and for this reason, it makes continuous efforts to utilize technological capabilities and generally improve its computing infrastructure.

3.1. COMPLAINTS  
In 2022, the Authority received 1,259 complaints and issued 67 decisions and 5 opinions. Specifically, 442 complaints were submitted regarding illegal processing of personal data, 298 complaints concerning unsolicited marketing calls for products and services, 19 Schengen appeals, 356 complaints regarding violations of the rights of data subjects, and 144 complaints about unsolicited electronic communications (SPAM/email-SMS).  
The largest number of incoming complaints concerned illegal processing of personal data, electronic communications, and specifically unsolicited marketing calls for products and services, unsolicited electronic communications (SPAM), followed by complaints regarding the private economy, video surveillance, public administration, labor relations, the financial sector, and health.  
Regarding the decisions issued, most pertained to electronic communications, public administration, and labor relations.  

3.1.1. NATIONAL DEFENSE  
In the context of the above thematic area, the Authority examined a series of issues presented to it, which pertain to the protection of personal data and involve the processing of personal data primarily by the Ministry of National Defense.  
Specifically, following a complaint that the issued type A military certificate unlawfully contained the information that the complainant had not fulfilled his military obligations and that he had been exempted from the obligation to serve, the Authority determined that it was sufficient to certify that the complainant had no military obligations, and called upon the Ministry of National Defense, as the data controller, to reissue the military status certificate type A for the complainant, stating only that he no longer has any military obligations (decision 2/2022).  
Additionally, following a complaint that the issued type A military certificate, in addition to certifying that the complainant has no military obligation, also included his military changes and the fact that he has completed alternative service, without these pieces of information being necessary for the specific purpose for which the certificate was requested, the Authority determined that it was sufficient to certify that the complainant has no military obligations, and called upon the Ministry of National Defense, as the data controller, to reissue the military status certificate type A for the complainant, stating only that he no longer has any military obligations (decision 3/2022).  

3.1.2. PUBLIC ADMINISTRATION AND SELF-GOVERNMENT  
The Authority examined a citizen's complaint regarding the sending of a mail folder to his residential address, the exterior of which contained personal data more than what was necessary for his identification as the recipient. The Authority determined that this specific incident was due to an oversight, as it was accepted that the standard practice of the complained Municipality is to send folders, the exterior of which only displays the name, surname, and address of the recipient. As a result, the Authority imposed an administrative sanction of reprimand on the data controller for violating Article 5(1)(b), (c), (f) of the GDPR (decision 14/2022).  
The Authority also determined that the complained transmission of letters to third parties, unauthorized entities [Workers and Employees Union of the Municipality and the Panhellenic Federation of Workers (P.O.E.) – OTA], during the evaluation stage of the complainant as an evaluated employee, which contained personal data of the complainant, was done in violation of the provision of Article 5(1)(a) of the GDPR and imposed an administrative fine on the respondent (decision 15/2022).  
Furthermore, the Authority examined a citizen's complaint regarding the loss of two medical certificates concerning the complainant (who belongs to a vulnerable group) which his wife had attached with his consent to her application to the complained Social Protection Center – where she works – in order to request her transfer to a service position that does not require daily contact with the public (back office), for reasons of protection.

Furthermore, the Authority examined a citizen's complaint regarding the partial satisfaction of the right to rectification. The complainant alleged that he submitted a request to the competent Civil Registry Office, concerning the correction of his mother's surname in his birth certificate, based on the attached birth certificate of his mother, and that he subsequently requested a copy of his birth certificate after the correction was made. The Civil Registry Office then sent the complainant a copy of his birth certificate, which, according to the complainant, shows that a handwritten correction of his mother's surname has occurred and a corresponding correction in the Citizens' Registry information system, without, however, mentioning the correction in the margin of the original entry of the act in the relevant year's civil registry volume and without mentioning it in the corresponding field "Corrections/Changes" in its transition to the Citizens' Registry information system.  
The Authority determined that the accuracy and completeness of personal data must be assessed based on the purpose for which the data was collected and is being processed, concluding that in this case, considering the purpose of processing (maintaining an accurate record of citizens' personal data for their identification and verification of their civil status, from which rights and obligations arise), it appears that the addition of the aforementioned additional information concerning the correction (e.g., when the correction took place, by whom, on what legal basis, etc.) is necessary in order to reflect the sequence of events—especially since the error was subsequently corrected. Therefore, the right to rectification exercised by the complainant was partially satisfied, as the respondent Municipality responded to it and corrected the erroneous personal data; however, it did not register this change and did not follow a specific archiving procedure for this correction, so that it would be clear to third parties, as well as to the administration itself, when this correction took place, by whom, on what legal basis, and for what reason. For these reasons, the Authority imposed the administrative sanction of reprimand on the respondent for the established violation of Article 5 paragraph 1 (d) of the GDPR (decision 29/2022).  
Additionally, the Authority, during the examination of an appeal, issued a warning and reprimand to a data processor for failure to implement organizational measures and for not previously informing the data subject. Specifically, the appealing physician reported the medical association, of which he was a member, for illegal processing and specifically for disclosing to a third party a document containing his personal data from a disciplinary file. The respondent claimed that in an effort to reach a settlement between the parties, he provided the disciplinary procedure file, including the document in question, to a representative of the complainant who had initiated the disciplinary procedure. In fact, the respondent claimed that he had anonymized the aforementioned documents. However, the respondent did not prove before the Authority, within the framework of the accountability principle, the truth of the above claims, as he did not provide relevant evidence. This inability was essentially due to the failure to take appropriate organizational measures and protection policies. Specifically, regarding the obligation to inform the data subject in advance, the respondent did not prove that he took the corresponding action (decision 31/2022).  
The Authority imposed the sanction of reprimand on a Municipality for failing to satisfy the right of access and instructed it to satisfy the complainant's right in question. It also issued a recommendation to the Municipality based on Article 19 paragraph 1 (c) of Law 2472/1997 for failing to inform the complainant about the processing of a decision by the Supreme Court in accordance with Article 7A paragraph 1 (a) of Law 2472/1997 (decision 42/2022).  
Moreover, the Authority imposed a monetary fine of 5,000 euros on a data processor in the context of electoral processes, for transmitting personal data to third parties, acting in this way as a data controller and without informing the data subjects about this transmission (decision 47/2022).  
The Authority examined a complaint from a citizen against the Mayor of his area. The citizen received a congratulatory message from the Mayor, to an email account he had created specifically for the Municipality's electronic services application. The complainant objected, requesting not to receive any further messages, but received another congratulatory message after a few months. After examining the complaint, the Authority found that the Mayor, using the complainant's contact details, violated the principle of data minimization.
**Purpose**, as such use was not expected by the data subject, violated the obligation to provide appropriate information under Article 14 of the GDPR and did not properly satisfy the exercised right, in violation of Article 12 paragraph 3 of the GDPR. For all violations, the Authority imposed a fine of 2,000 euros (decision 48/2022). 

Furthermore, the Authority found that the reported processing of sending the tax clearance certificate in an envelope with a window, from which the amount of the complainant's declared income was visible, was carried out in violation of the provisions of Articles 5 paragraph 1 point (f) and 32 of the GDPR, without taking appropriate technical and organizational measures, thus compromising the confidentiality of the data, and imposed a monetary fine of 8,000 euros on the Independent Authority for Public Revenue (AADE), as the data controller (decision 58/2022). 

Finally, the Authority determined that the mention of the imposition of a penalty and the relevant applicable provisions on the temporary identity certificate of a member of the Fire Brigade constitutes a violation of the provision of Article 5 paragraph 1 point (c) of the GDPR and imposed an administrative fine on the reported entity, while instructing the Fire Brigade and the Ministry of Citizen Protection to issue relevant guidelines (decision 59/2022).

**3.1.3. EDUCATION AND RESEARCH**

Following a complaint from educators regarding the legality of the processing of personal data carried out in the context of electronic voting for the election of elected representatives of educators in the Primary and Secondary Education Service Councils, the Authority concluded a) that this processing complies with the rules and conditions of personal data protection legislation, b) since the activity is systematic and concerns the introduction and organization of an electronic governance service, specifically electronic voting, the Ministry, as the data controller, was obliged to conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) prior to the introduction of this method, regardless of whether this is provided for in national law, as the GDPR applies directly, and c) the Electoral Committees cannot be considered Data Controllers either independently or jointly with the Ministry of Education, according to the provisions of Articles 4 point 7 and 26 of the GDPR. For these reasons, the Authority instructed the Ministry of Education and Religious Affairs to conduct a DPIA without delay and in any case before the next electronic voting for Service Councils, and to restore the proper application of the provisions of Articles 4 point 7 and 26 of the GDPR by informing the Authority accordingly (decision 44/22).

The Authority also examined a complaint from an unemployed person against the OAED (Public Employment Service) for the leakage of his personal data and against the vocational training centers KEC MASTER and KEC EXELIXI for the illegal processing of his data. The complainant, an unemployed person registered with the OAED, after expressing interest in participating in a subsidized employment and training program of the Ministry of Labor, received a phone call from the two reported KECs in order to receive the training services provided by them. From the examination of the complaint, no incident of data leakage by the OAED was found. Regarding KEC MASTER, it was found that during the initial phone communication, the complainant believed he was speaking with representatives of the OAED, being confused, which was due, at least in part, to the inadequate and unclear information he received from them. Therefore, the Authority issued a warning to KEC MASTER for the identified failure to comply with the principle of transparency. The reported KEC EXELIXI, while initially claiming that it found the complainant's details from an internet search, later stated that the complainant had filled out an expression of interest form on its website, through which he provided his consent, a fact that the complainant denies. It was found that the consent process invoked by KEC EXELIXI does not meet the requirements of Article 7 of the GDPR and is not valid, particularly due to inadequate information and the lack of confirmation procedures for the provided data, while additionally, the KEC conducted further searches for the complainant's details on the internet in the context of examining the complaint, without substantiating the legality of the above processing actions, nor the adherence to the principle of transparency towards the complainant. For this reason, a fine of 10,000 euros was imposed on KEC EXELIXI (decision 45/2022).

Furthermore, the Authority examined ex officio the compliance of the Ministry of Education and Religious Affairs with the recommendations of decision 50/2021 regarding the compatibility of modern distance education in primary and secondary education schools with the provisions of legislation on the processing of personal data. The Authority concluded that no new corrective measure is required and called on the Ministry to make necessary amendments to improve transparency. Specifically, the information provided to data subjects through the website must follow a multi-layered approach, while improvement is required regarding the use of "cookies." The Authority will examine the broader issue of the application of Chapter V of the GDPR to video conferencing services of companies that belong to a group controlled by an entity subject to U.S. law with other supervisory authorities through the cooperation and consistency procedures of the Regulation (decision 61/2022).

Additionally, the Authority examined a complaint concerning the collection of data from third sources about a candidate for a teaching position by the reported university, for the purpose of evaluating his candidacy. **3.1.4. HEALTH**

**Vaccination/Illness Certificates**

Complaints were submitted to the Authority regarding the collection of vaccination/illness certificates within the framework of the mandatory vaccination of the staff of OKANA, in accordance with the provisions of paragraphs 1 and 2 of Article 206 of Law 4820/2021. The Authority examined these complaints within the scope of its ex officio competence, pursuant to Articles 57 paragraph 1(a) and (h) of the GDPR and 13 paragraph 1(h) of Law 4624/2019, as on one hand, personal data of the complainants were not processed by OKANA, and on the other hand, in any case, processing (collection) of copies of vaccination/illness certificates of OKANA employees fell under the regulatory scope of Articles 2 paragraph 1 of the GDPR and 2 of Law 4624/2019. Specifically, the Authority, in decisions 7/2022 and 8/2022, found that vaccination/illness certificates related to COVID-19, as well as equivalent certificates or attestations provided for in the provisions of Articles 205 and 206 paragraph 5 of Law 4820/2021, constitute health-related data under Article 4(15) of the GDPR, and their processing is subject to the formalities regarding the processing of special categories of personal data under Articles 9 of the GDPR and 22 of Law 4624/2019. Furthermore, the Authority, in decisions 7/2022 and 8/2022, concluded that the complained processing of the collection of vaccination/illness certificates of employees by OKANA, both pursuant to the provision of Article 205 of Law 4820/2021 – that is, at a time before it was clarified whether OKANA staff were subject to mandatory vaccination – and pursuant to the provision of Article 206 of Law 4820/2021, as well as after the issuance of decision Δ1α/ΓΠ.οικ.52796 (Government Gazette B 3959/27.8.2021) and prior to the interoperability of the relevant information systems, was carried out in violation of the principle of Article 5 paragraph 1(a) of the GDPR, as the compliance check with the obligation to present the vaccination/illness certificate pursuant to Articles 205 and 206 paragraph 5 of Law 4820/2021 is specifically regulated by the provision of Article 33 of Law 4816/2021. Consequently, the Authority, in decisions 7/2022 and 8/2022, taking into account, among other things, the fact that OKANA securely destroyed the unlawfully collected vaccination/illness certificates, issued a reprimand, pursuant to Article 58 paragraph 2(b) of the GDPR, to OKANA, as the data controller, for the collection of vaccination/illness certificates of its employees in violation of the principles of Article 5 paragraph 1(a) and (c) of the GDPR.

**3.1.6.1. Complaints Regarding Disturbances in the Context of Debtor Notifications**

If the Authority received various complaints regarding disturbances in the context of notifying debtors, claiming that either the lender themselves (through their employees and/or through their lawyer) disturbed the complainant by phone regarding their overdue debts or that they were disturbed, on behalf of the lender, by a Debt Collection Company, without their own consent, approval, or authorization. The Authority examined and archived the complaints (see, for example, G/EXE/957, 1675, 1914, 2257, 2306, 2775, 2776, 2931, 2932, 3308/2022) which were:
• Clearly unfounded, as the lender has the right to process the debtor's data without the latter's consent. This processing is permitted as necessary for the execution of their contract with the lender, e.g., in the sense of the smooth continuation of the transactional relationship (through telephone reminders of the debt). It was also noted that according to Law 3758/2009, the lender has the right, under specific conditions, to provide Debt Collection Companies with information regarding overdue claims of their debtors and that the consent of the latter is not required for this processing. Regarding the obligation to inform, see decision 98/2017, with the note that this decision was issued based on a legal framework that is no longer in effect, specifically relying on an interpretation of Law 3758/2009 in conjunction with the already repealed Law 2472/1997, while the GDPR and Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137/29-08-2019) have already come into effect, the provisions of which are now applicable for the assessment of this issue. Furthermore, the Authority emphasized that according to the 3/2020 decision of the Plenary of the Supreme Court (contrary to decision 98/2017), it was accepted that in the case of data being provided to a processor, subject to one of the categories of recipients (e.g., Debt Collection Companies) for which notification has been made, there is no obligation for the data controller to provide a new specific notification to the data subject (i.e., at the time of providing the personal data to the specific Debt Collection Company),
• Inadmissible, as the required pre-procedure was not followed, since if the complainant wishes to exercise any of their rights as a data subject, they must first address the data controller (lender) in the appropriate manner, on whose behalf the calls are made. The Authority encourages complainants, if they have no relation to the sought debtor and the debt, to exercise their rights (especially correction/deletion/restriction of their data, e.g., by providing any relevant evidence for the possession of the called telephone number) to the data controller,
• Not falling within the scope of the legislation for the protection of personal data and/or the jurisdiction of the Authority, as the principles and framework within which debtor notifications must occur (e.g., to which phones, with what frequency, etc.) are defined in Law 3758/2009. The competent authority for the reported allegations (e.g., for continuous disturbances at inappropriate hours and/or on work phones and/or disturbances exhibiting offensive/threatening behavior) is the General Secretariat for Commerce and Consumer Protection, which has, under Law 3758/2009, general supervision over the activities of Debt Collection Companies and debtor notifications, whether conducted by Debt Collection Companies or by the lender themselves. Finally, the complaints regarding disturbances from a law firm were archived (see, for example, G/EXE/1084, 2608, 3303, 3304, 3307/2022, as for the assignment of a case to a law firm (for extrajudicial or judicial collection of a debt), no consent, approval, or authorization from the debtor is required (see Article 6 paragraph 1 subparagraphs b and f, Article 9 paragraph 2 subparagraph f of the GDPR), and the competent authority for the reported allegations (regarding repeated disturbances and/or offensive behavior by a lawyer) is the relevant Bar Association (see also decision 49/2011 of the Authority, Opinion 598/2012 of the Legal Council of State, from 14.11.2014 Press Release of the Athens Bar Association).

Responses to the Hellenic Parliament on Proposals for Regulations of the European Parliament and Council  
The Authority provided its views to the Hellenic Parliament on proposals for Regulations of the European Parliament and Council. Specifically:  
With the document No. G/EXE/2281/16-9-2022, the Authority transmitted its observations to the European Affairs Department of the Hellenic Parliament regarding the proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and the Council for the establishment of rules aimed at the prevention and combating of child sexual abuse and grooming. In particular, the Authority, after highlighting the Joint Opinion 4/2022 of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) and the European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS), for which the Authority actively participated as a member of the EDPB, even referring to specific paragraphs of it, stated the following:  
1. The said proposal for a Regulation raises serious concerns regarding the proportionality of the proposed intervention and the restrictions on the protection of fundamental rights to privacy, the confidentiality of electronic communications, and the protection of personal data. The proposed procedural guarantees cannot fully replace substantive guarantees. The proposed complex system of escalation, from risk assessment and mitigation measures to the issuance of a detection order, cannot replace the required clarity of substantive obligations.  
2. The proposal lacks clarity on key elements, such as the concepts of "significant risk." Furthermore, the entities tasked with implementing these safeguards, ranging from private entities to administrative and/or judicial authorities, enjoy broad discretion, which leads to legal uncertainty regarding how to balance the rights at stake in each individual case.  
3. Proportionality issues are also raised by the fact that the available technologies for detecting child sexual abuse material (whether already known material or new material), as well as the available technologies for detecting grooming, appear to be characterized by non-negligible margins of error regarding false detection – which margins of error increase particularly for cases of detecting new child sexual abuse material as well as for cases of detecting grooming.  
4. The need for national procedural guarantees to be taken into account by each member state in harmony with the relevant constitutional provisions and, in particular, the requirement for conducting the relevant processing under the guarantees of the judiciary according to Article 19 paragraph 1 subparagraph b of the Constitution, in conjunction with the requirements and guarantees of secondary Union law (Article 15 of Directive 2002/58/EC) should be considered.  
5. The impact on the general sentiment of all users of these services, who do not engage in illegal activities, that any such communication may be subject to surveillance/control without their knowledge should also be taken into account – which in turn may restrict other individual rights, such as freedom of speech (Article 14 paragraph 1 of the Constitution).  
6. It is deemed necessary for the said Regulation to clarify that no provision of it should be interpreted as a prohibition or weakening of encryption.  
7. The relationship between the duties of the coordinating authorities and those of the data protection authorities should be better regulated, and a more significant and specific role should be given to the DPAs within the framework of the proposed Regulation. Additionally, a system should be established for addressing and resolving disputes between the competent authorities and the data protection authorities regarding detection orders. In particular, data protection authorities should have the right to challenge a detection order before the courts of the member state of the competent judicial authority or the independent administrative authority that issued the detection order.  
8. Providers should be required to consult with data protection authorities through a prior consultation process, as mentioned in Article 36 of the GDPR, before implementing any measures for detecting child sexual abuse material or grooming, and not only when it concerns a targeted detection order regarding the solicitation of children.

3.5. IMPACT ASSESSMENT AND PRIOR CONSULTATION
The obligation to conduct an impact assessment regarding data protection (DPIA) is provided for in Article 35, paragraph 1 of the GDPR. The DPIA is carried out by the data controller when the processing operations are likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, particularly by using new technologies and taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing. Indicative types of processing operations that involve a high risk are listed in Article 35, paragraph 3 of the GDPR (see Recital 91 of the GDPR). The Authority issued a list (Government Gazette B' 1622/10.05.2019) of processing operations for which a DPIA is required, according to Article 35, paragraph 4 of the GDPR, which complements and specifies the relevant guidelines WP 248 rev. 01 of the European Data Protection Board.

Prior consultation with the Authority is provided for in Article 36, paragraph 1 of the GDPR, if the impact assessment regarding data protection (DPIA) (Article 35 of the GDPR) indicates that the processing operations involve a high risk that the data controller cannot sufficiently mitigate with appropriate measures (Recitals 84 and 94 of the GDPR). The data controller submits a request for prior consultation with the Authority if it has verified that the necessary formal completeness criteria of the DPIA study related to the consultation request are met, based on the relevant framework. The request for prior consultation must include, at a minimum, a detailed description of the remaining high risks and their potential impacts, as well as a detailed justification of the reasons why:
• it is not possible to foresee measures to reduce the high risks to an acceptable level, particularly regarding the available technology and the cost of implementing them (Recitals 84 and 94 of the GDPR), and
• the processing is necessary despite the remaining high risks. It must also include the provisions set out in Article 36, paragraph 3 of the GDPR and attach the DPIA.

In 2022, the Authority addressed the issue of DPIA in its decisions. Specifically, the Authority examined a complaint from an employee regarding the installation and operation of a video surveillance system in a production department of an agricultural cooperative, where high-risk machines operate during the evening and late-night hours. The Authority, in Decision 5/2022, found that the complained employer company failed to carry out, as required, the DPIA study provided for in Article 35, paragraph 1 of the GDPR, before installing and operating a video surveillance system in that department and concluded that the relevant study must be conducted, identifying issues that must be examined and analyzed in detail in this study, in addition to those provided for in Article 35 of the GDPR, taking into account that the employees of that department are already burdened by the night shift. In light of this, the Authority decided that an appropriate corrective measure, at this stage, is to issue a directive to the data controller under Article 58, paragraph 2, point (d) of the GDPR to bring the processing operations into compliance with the provisions of the GDPR, based on what is stated in the reasoning of Decision 5/2022, within two months from its notification to the complained company, reserving the right to re-examine the case in its entirety after the two-month period.

Furthermore, the Authority, in exercising its ex officio powers, examined the processing of personal data that took place under a total of seven Joint Ministerial Decisions (JMD) regarding the free distribution of self-diagnostic tests to eligible persons, the declaration of the results of the relevant tests in the special electronic application self-testing.gov.gr, and the further processing of data after the declaration of the results. 3.6. DATA TRANSFERS OUTSIDE THE EU  
Under the GDPR, the only instances in which a transfer authorization from the supervisory authority is required are as follows, according to Article 46, paragraph 3:  
1. Ad hoc contractual clauses between the importer and exporter of the data.  
2. Administrative arrangements between public authorities or bodies, which include enforceable and substantive rights of the data subjects (e.g., Memorandum of Understanding - MOUs between public authorities with corresponding responsibilities). The authorization of the supervisory authority is necessary because such administrative arrangements are legally non-binding.  
In the year 2022, the Authority did not grant any data transfer authorizations outside the EEA. However, the Authority participated in the work of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) and its subgroups, including the one dealing with international data transfers.

3.7. COOPERATION WITH COUNTERPART AUTHORITIES OF EU MEMBER STATES, MUTUAL ASSISTANCE, JOINT OPERATIONS  
The GDPR ensures the cooperation of national supervisory authorities in cross-border matters (Article 4, paragraph 23 of the GDPR), through the procedures established in the articles.
**Paragraphs 60 to 62 (cooperation within the framework of the "one-stop-shop", see Articles 55, 56 GDPR, mutual assistance, joint ventures). Cooperation in these matters is conducted by the national supervisory authorities, while the EDPB is only involved in case of disagreement between the national supervisory authorities (Article 65 GDPR) or urgency (Article 66 GDPR). The main objective of the consistency mechanism, as provided in Articles 63 to 67 of the GDPR, is to ensure the coherent and uniform application of the GDPR across all EU member states. To this end, the GDPR mandates the mandatory cooperation of national supervisory authorities and stipulates that before specific decisions are taken by national supervisory authorities (Article 64(1)), as well as in cases of general applicability or issues that produce effects in more than one member state (Article 64(2)), an opinion must be formulated by the EDPB. The coherent application of the GDPR is also ensured through the issuance of guidelines, recommendations, and best practices by the EDPB (Article 70(1)(e) GDPR). The Internal Market Information System (IMI) serves as an IT platform for the exchange of information between national supervisory authorities and the EDPB regarding cross-border issues. In 2022, the Authority received a total of 1,410 notifications-requests within the framework of cooperation among the data protection supervisory authorities of the member states, while it itself introduced 7 cooperation requests into the system. **3.8.2. MECHANISMS AND CRITERIA FOR CERTIFICATION**  
In 2022, the examination of the draft national certification scheme and its related criteria, which had been submitted to the Authority under Article 42(5) of the GDPR (see the Authority's Annual Report for 2021, section 3.7.2), continued. The owner of the certification scheme submitted to the Authority a newer version of the criteria, to which the Authority provided its comments in its document no. G/EXE/2777/2-11-2022. In this document, the Authority highlighted, among other things, the following general characteristics:  
1. The scope of the certification criteria must be absolutely clear, and presumptive conclusions about what is included and what is not should be avoided.  
2. The certification criteria must be, among other things, verifiable and validated. The method of checking and assessing/validating a certification criterion constitutes information distinct from the criterion itself, which falls under the certification scheme and essentially "complements" each criterion in order to determine its verifiability and validity. Each criterion, however, must be clearly articulated and distinguishable, regardless of the supplementary information associated with it. Furthermore, considering that the Authority can only approve the certification criteria and cannot take a position on information, such as the effectiveness of methods for checking compliance with the criteria, a clear distinction between the aforementioned concepts is necessary. This does not eliminate the need to record all information; however, the provided information must be appropriately structured so that it is clear what exactly the criteria are—those which the Authority will examine for approval or not, and subsequently, the European Data Protection Board will issue an opinion—and what the supplementary information is.  
3. It should be clarified whether certain criteria apply under conditions or not. This is because the introductory text of the application for the approval of the certification criteria states that "certification is granted provided that all the following criteria are met," thus implying a mandatory character for all criteria; however, from the study of these, it appears that some criteria could potentially not apply in certain cases.  
Subsequently, the Authority also made a series of more specific observations on these criteria of the scheme itself. A newer version of the certification criteria for this scheme is expected within 2023.

4.1. EUROPEAN DATA PROTECTION BOARD  
The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) began its operations on May 25, 2018, succeeding the Article 29 Working Party (WP29), in accordance with the relevant provisions of the GDPR. The EDPB was established as an independent European body, with the primary mission of promoting the consistent application of the GDPR and Directive 2016/680 on law enforcement in the European Union and fostering cooperation among national supervisory authorities. It consists of the chairs or representatives of the supervisory authorities of the member states of the European Union and the European Data Protection Supervisor. Only on GDPR matters, representatives of the supervisory authorities of the countries of the European Economic Area (EEA), Norway, Iceland, and Liechtenstein, can participate as members of the EDPB, but without voting rights or the right to be elected as chair or deputy chair. The European Commission may also participate in the EDPB's work, as well as the supervisory authority overseeing the aforementioned supervisory authorities of the three EEA countries, but without voting rights. The responsibilities and duties of the EDPB include, in particular, the following:  
• Providing general guidance for the clarification of legislation, including guiding principles, recommendations, and best practices;  
• Advising the European Commission on any matters related to the protection of personal data and new legislative proposals in the European Union;  
• Issuing consistency opinions and binding decisions in cases of disagreement between the lead and concerned supervisory authorities in cross-border cases; and  
• Promoting cooperation and effective exchange of information and best practices among national supervisory authorities.  
The EDPB publishes an annual report on its activities, which is disseminated to the European Parliament, the Council, and the Commission.  
For the more effective organization of its work, the EDPB utilizes sub-groups of expert specialists. In 2022, sub-groups of specialists operated on the following subjects:  
1. Border surveillance, passenger transport, police and judicial sector (former third pillar),  
2. Compliance, e-governance, and health,  
3. Cooperation among national data protection authorities,  
4. Law enforcement,  
5. Financial matters,  
6. Data transfers to third countries,  
7. Users of information technologies,  
8. Uniform interpretation of key concepts,  
9. Strategy,  
10. Social media, and  
11. Technological issues.  
Ad hoc sub-groups of specialists are also provided for the processing of current issues.

4.1.1. COORDINATED SUPERVISION COMMITTEE – CSC  
The Coordinated Supervision Committee was established within the framework of the European Data Protection Board in accordance with Article 37 of the Rules of Procedure of the aforementioned Board and Article 62 for coordinated supervision by the European Data Protection Supervisor and the national supervisory authorities of Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 for the protection of natural persons against the processing of personal data by the institutions and bodies of the Union and the free movement of such data or any Union act that establishes large-scale information systems, entities, offices, and bodies of the EU. The Committee began its work in December 2019 and includes representatives of the national supervisory authorities of the member states, the European Data Protection Supervisor, and representatives of the national supervisory authorities of states that are not EU members but are members of the Schengen area, where provided by Union law. Its mission is to ensure harmonized and coordinated supervision of the large-scale information systems of the EU as well as the entities, offices, and bodies of the EU that fall within its scope.  
The main thematic focus of the Committee includes issues of borders – asylum – migration, police and judicial cooperation, and the Digital Single Market. However, currently, its remit includes the supervision of the Internal Market Information System (IMI), the EU Agency for Criminal Justice Cooperation (Eurojust), the European Public Prosecutor’s Office (EPPO), and Europol in accordance with Article 44(2) of Regulation (EU) 2016/794 on Europol, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2022/991 and which came into force in 2022.  
For the effective performance of its duties, the Committee may exchange information, assist national supervisory authorities in conducting audits – inspections, examine difficulties in the interpretation and application of Union legal acts establishing large-scale information systems or bodies subject to coordinated supervision, conduct studies on issues arising during the exercise of independent oversight or the exercise of data subject rights, promote awareness of data subject rights, and develop harmonized proposals regarding problem-solving.  
The Committee prepares a two-year action plan as well as a report on its activities every two years, which it submits to the European Data Protection Board for submission to the European Parliament, Council, and Commission. The Committee meets at least twice a year in Brussels. The coordination of the Committee is managed by a Coordinator elected by its members for a two-year term. The first coordinator of the Committee was elected from the representative of the national supervisory authority of Portugal, who renewed her term for the next two years in elections held in November 2022. Greece participates in the Committee with its representative.

The purpose of the Customs Information System (CIS) is, on the one hand, the mutual assistance and cooperation of the competent administrative authorities of the Member States in combating the phenomenon of fraud within the framework of the customs union and the common agricultural policy, through the faster availability of information (formerly the 1st pillar of the EU), and on the other hand, to assist in the prevention, investigation, and prosecution of serious violations of national laws (formerly the 3rd pillar of the EU). The CIS allows a Member State to register data, requesting another Member State to proceed with one of the following proposed actions:  
• Observation and reporting,  
• Discreet monitoring,  
• Special audits, and  
• Operational analysis.  

These two different purposes of the system are served by two logically separate databases. Correspondingly, the regimes for the supervision of personal data protection for these two distinct purposes of the CIS are also different.  

Specifically, for the purposes related to the former 1st pillar, according to Article 37 of Regulation No. 515/1997, as amended by Regulation (EC) 766/2008, the national Data Protection Authorities are responsible for ensuring that the processing and use of the data contained in the CIS by the respective competent national authorities of each Member State do not violate the rights of data subjects. In particular, however, pursuant to Article 37(4) of the aforementioned Regulation, the Coordination Group for the Supervision of the Customs Information System (hereinafter "the Group") has been established. Specifically, it is provided that the European Data Protection Supervisor, who supervises the compliance of the CIS with Regulation (EC) 45/2001 based on paragraph 3(a) of the same article, convenes at least once a year a meeting with all the national data protection supervisory authorities responsible for the supervision of the CIS.  
In 2022, the Group met once. At that meeting, members were informed about the results of the first evaluation of Regulation 515/1997. Specifically, according to the relevant evaluation report, the existing legal framework does not require any revision; therefore, no legislative initiative will be undertaken to amend the regulation.  
At the same meeting, the Group continued the discussion on the assessment of training on personal data protection provided to employees of the competent national authorities in each Member State with access to the CIS. It is noted that the previous year, the Group had approved a draft questionnaire, which was sent to the competent authorities. The aim is to identify any problems, deficiencies, or needs for improvement in the training procedures for staff through the responses. Some preliminary findings were presented, which will be finalized once responses from all members are collected, likely within the next year.  
Finally, the Group's activity report for the years 2020-2021 was also adopted.  
For the purposes related to the former 3rd pillar, the Customs Joint Supervisory Authority (CJSA) has been established, in which the Authority participates and which met at the headquarters of the Council of the European Union in Brussels. The CJSA was established under Article 25 of Decision 917/2009/JHA of the Council of 30 November 2009 "on the use of information technology for customs purposes." It is responsible for supervising the operation of the CIS, examining any implementation or interpretation issues of this system, and assisting national auditing authorities in carrying out their supervisory duties, mainly by finding and adopting common solutions to the problems that arise.  
The CJSA did not meet in 2022.  
4.2.4. COORDINATION GROUP FOR THE SUPERVISION OF THE VIS INFORMATION SYSTEM  
The Authority participates as a regular member in the Coordination Group for the supervision of the VIS system, which meets at the offices of the European Data Protection Supervisor in Brussels at least twice a year. For the period 2020–2022 (June), Greece held the vice-presidency of the Group. The secretariat support is provided by the European Supervisor. The Group consists of one member – representative from each national data protection authority and the European Data Protection Supervisor. It was established under Article 43 of Regulation (EC) 767/2008 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 9 July 2008 on the Visa Information System (VIS) and the exchange of data between Member States for short-stay visas (VIS Regulation).  
The purpose of this Group is to ensure a common approach and handling of issues arising in the operation of the VIS database and the unified supervision of the system in the field of personal data protection during the implementation of both the Visa Code (Regulation (EC) 810/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 July 2009 establishing a Visa Code) and the aforementioned VIS Regulation.  
This central database was developed in accordance with Council Decision 2004/512/EC for the establishment of the Visa Information System (VIS), in order to improve the implementation of the common policy in the area of

During 2022, two hybrid meetings were held in the sense that both physical participation at the premises of the European Data Protection Supervisor and online participation were possible. A brief update was also provided by a representative of the European Commission regarding the revision of the VIS Regulation, which includes changes to the regulatory framework of VIS, such as the reduction of the age limit for children who can be fingerprinted from 12 years to 6, the inclusion of long-term visas in the regulatory scope of the system, and the storage of travel documents in the system for the purposes of returning third-country nationals. Moreover, a representative of the Commission was invited again this year to a meeting of the Group to present the latest developments regarding the issuance of digital visas expedited by the Commission. Specifically, the Commission has taken the initiative for the digitization of visa issuance, which will gradually replace the paper format aiming to save time and money for both travelers and consulates. The goal is for the entire process to be conducted online, with the creation of a central platform that will be used by all member states. The members of the Group had the opportunity to ask questions to the representative of the Commission regarding data storage, their quality, and the mandatory use of the platform for digital visas. In the same context, the opinion of the European Data Protection Supervisor is also moving, who, in the exercise of his duties, sent an official opinion to the Commission on the matter during this year.

The common audit plan is being developed based on a relevant audit plan for data security that had been previously adopted by the Coordinated Supervision Group for SIS II. Consequently, the subgroup tasked with preparing the audit plan presented an updated draft, having taken into account comments made by members of the Group. The aim is for the outcome of this action to provide national authorities and the European Supervisor with a user-friendly and practical questionnaire – list of items that need to be checked during audits of the national sections of the VIS. The adoption of the questionnaire was postponed to the next meeting of the Group in order to incorporate the new proposals that were made.  
Furthermore, during this year, the Group proceeded with the examination of the issue of early data deletion, as provided for in Article 25 of the VIS Regulation, which was also part of the action plan for the years 2019 – 2021. The purpose of this action is to investigate whether and how member states implement the relevant provision of the Regulation for early data deletion. Based on the results of the research, it will be possible in the next stage to identify and highlight best practices, as well as to adopt recommendations for the better implementation of the provision and to correct any deficiencies. This research is being conducted using a questionnaire, which was approved after discussion by the Group and completed by the member states. 4.2.5. COORDINATION GROUP FOR THE SUPERVISION OF EURODAC

The Authority participates with a regular member in the Coordination Group for the supervision of the EURODAC system, which meets at the offices of the European Data Protection Supervisor in Brussels at least twice a year. Since November 2019, Greece has held the presidency of the Group. The secretarial support is provided by the European Supervisor. The objective of this Group is to ensure a common approach and handling by the member states of the issues arising in the operation of the EURODAC database. This database, as is known, was created under the Dublin Treaty, with Regulation (EC) 2725/2000 concerning the establishment of "Eurodac" for the comparison of fingerprints for the effective application of the Dublin Convention, in order to collect fingerprints of asylum seekers and illegal migrants in the European Union, as well as to facilitate the identification of asylum seekers during the asylum examination process. Given that national data protection authorities are responsible for the use and generally the processing of the data sent and collected in the central database, and the European Supervisor for data protection oversees the processing in the central database and the transmission of data to the member states, they actively cooperate within the framework of their responsibilities, conducting regular audits both at the central database and at the competent services of the member states, thereby ensuring coordinated supervision of Eurodac (see Article 32 of Regulation (EU) 603/2013, concerning the establishment of "Eurodac" for the comparison of fingerprints for the effective application of Regulation (EU) No. 604/2013 establishing the criteria and mechanisms for determining the member state responsible for examining an application for international protection submitted to a member state by a third-country national or by a stateless person and regarding requests for comparison with Eurodac data submitted by law enforcement authorities of the member states and Europol for law enforcement purposes and for amending Regulation (EU) No. 1077/2011 establishing a European Agency for the Operational Management of Large-Scale IT Systems in the Area of Freedom, Security, and Justice).

During 2022, two hybrid meetings were held in the sense that both physical participation at the premises of the European Data Protection Supervisor and online participation were possible. Representatives from the European Agency for the Operational Management of Large-Scale IT Systems in the Area of Freedom, Security, and Justice (eu-LISA) and the European Commission – Directorate-General for Home Affairs (DG Home) attended the Group's meetings to inform the Group about the latest developments as is customary. In particular, a representative of the Commission is invited to each meeting to provide updates on the progress of the review of the "Eurodac" Regulation (Regulation (EU) 603/2013) based on the Commission's 2016 proposal, which had raised concerns within the Group, as this proposal expands the scope of the Regulation beyond asylum issues, aiming to utilize the system for identifying migrants on EU territory and for their return procedures. In the meetings of this year, the representatives of the EU did not attend but sent the Group brief informational notes, which were read. With the aforementioned notes, they reminded the Group that the review of the Regulation is now part of the broader Agreement on Asylum and Migration, consequently being accompanied by other legislative acts, such as the proposal for the new Dublin Regulation. During the French Presidency, consultations on the "Eurodac" Regulation Proposal were resumed in the Council. However, although a mandate was given to restart negotiations in June, which was adopted by the Council, they did not start immediately. The assignment of a mandate to the Parliament to restart negotiations remains pending, although the intention was for the Parliament's rapporteur to receive the relevant mandate by the end of the year. The intention of the French presidency and the Parliament is to proceed gradually and based on the broader legislative package for the Agreement on Asylum and Migration, resulting in the review of the "Eurodac" Regulation being dependent on negotiations regarding the other legislative acts.
**Legal Texts of Asylum and Migration**

Regarding the Agreement on Asylum and Migration, the Group was informed that negotiations have commenced with the support of the Commission in order for the legislative bodies to reach an agreement. During the French and Czech presidencies, an agreement was reached concerning the preliminary approach for the legislative texts on the screening of third-country nationals at the external borders (Screening Regulation) and the "Eurodac" Regulation. Additionally, an agreement was also reached on the Voluntary Solidarity Mechanism, which aims to support primarily Mediterranean states as first reception countries for migrants and to provide a specific, targeted response to the challenges arising from migratory waves. The implementation of this mechanism has been entrusted to the Commission, which will be assisted by member states and European agencies and organizations.

It was emphasized that during the Czech presidency, technical-level consultations continued in order to find the right balance between solidarity and responsibility based on a conceptual framework, which will be submitted for approval to the ministers during the December Justice and Home Affairs Council. Subsequently, work must continue to reflect this conceptual framework in the legislative bodies for the proposals of the Regulation on the Management of Asylum and Migration (AMMR) and the Regulation on the Asylum Procedure (APR) and to resume legislative negotiations during the Swedish presidency. It was also noted that, at an inter-institutional level, an agreement was reached in September between the European Parliament and the presidency of the Council regarding the mapping of the Pact's pathway, where representatives of the two institutions confirmed their commitment to make every possible effort to approve the legislative proposals before the end of the 2019-2024 legislative period.

On the part of EU-Lisa, the Data Protection Officer (DPO) of the Agency attended the Group's meetings to inform about the latest developments regarding the Eurodac system. She presented the level of overall performance and operation of the system while also providing some statistical data on its usage by member states, particularly regarding the volume of data in the system and the number of accesses from member states. Furthermore, the issue of false positive search results and the system's capacity concerning the enormous volume of data being input was discussed.

During this year, the Group continued the discussion on the issue of checks in the national part of Eurodac, which national supervisory authorities are obliged to conduct in national Eurodac systems at regular intervals, and the possibility of seeking a common harmonized way of reporting - presenting the checks, which would allow for comparison of results among member states. For this purpose, a small subgroup of volunteers had been created, which had already presented a draft questionnaire - checklist of audit points, which national authorities could use as the most suitable tool. Members exchanged views on the final form it would take and made relevant proposals for modification. Based on these proposals and discussions, the final form of the questionnaire was presented, and the Group ultimately proceeded to adopt this tool.

Additionally, the Group was engaged in the drafting of the new action plan for the period 2022-2024. Based on the results of the Group's actions from the previous two years, and taking into account new developments, it was proposed that the following topics be included in the new action plan: monitoring developments regarding the revision of the Eurodac Regulation (a topic that has remained in the respective action plans for the past few years), access of law enforcement authorities - including Europol - to the system, issues that need to be highlighted and studied regarding national-level checks, impacts from legislation on interoperability, the issue of false positive search results, data marking, monitoring of specific searches for entries, and finally, actions of the Group regarding coordinated supervision within the framework of the European Data Protection Board. The above proposals were approved by the Group and adopted in the Action Plan. Regarding the first mentioned action, which had concerned the Group since previous years in light of legislative developments, it had already been decided to draft a relevant opinion by representatives of the Greek authority and the European Supervisor. The opinion letter was drafted during 2022 and was discussed by the Group, which adopted it and ultimately sent it to the European Parliament. As for the action regarding access of law enforcement authorities and Europol to the system, it was decided to thoroughly examine their compliance with the provisions of the Regulation and to understand the statistics provided for accesses by law enforcement authorities at the national level (of the member states) as well as Europol. To this end, the Group agreed to draft a relevant questionnaire with the representatives of France and Portugal as rapporteurs.

Finally, at the meeting in June 2022, at the end of the two-year term of the Group's presidency, the representative of the Greek Authority was re-elected as president for the next two years.

4.4. SPRING SESSION  
The Spring Session of the data protection supervisory authorities is a closed session in the sense that participation rights are granted to certified authorities, namely the data protection supervisory authorities of the EU, as well as other countries, such as Switzerland and Norway, the European Supervisor, representatives of the EU, the Council of Europe, as well as authorities certified as permanent observers. Representatives of the EU, the Council of Europe, and authorities with permanent observer status do not have voting rights.

Croatia was initially designated as the host country and organizer of the Spring Session 2020, which, however, due to the pandemic, was postponed to May 2021 and subsequently postponed to 2022, when it took place over two days on May 19-20. Delegates from 40 countries participated in the Session. The first presentation session focused on the recent case law of the European Court of Human Rights, while the second session focused on Convention 108+ and issues of mutual assistance and global convergence. A discussion followed on the following topics:

• Recent developments and challenges regarding data transfers  
• Law enforcement cooperation: cross-border cases and Article 50 of the GDPR, mutual assistance between EU countries and third countries  
• Perspective, innovation, and monitoring of technology  
• Awareness-raising activities and implementation of projects funded by the EU in EU countries and outside the EU.  

Presentations were also made regarding the activities of the working subgroup on the future of the Session and the Working Group for sharing experiences from examining cases related to personal data protection (Case Handling Workshop).  

Finally, two resolutions were adopted, the first for the acceleration of the ratification of Convention 108+, aimed at protecting the privacy of individuals.

4.5. WORKING GROUP FOR THE EXCHANGE OF EXPERIENCES FROM THE HANDLING OF PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION REQUESTS (CASE HANDLING WORKSHOP)  
The Authority participates in the Working Group for the exchange of experiences from the examination of personal data protection issues (Case Handling Workshop) organized annually by member states and supervisory authorities in Europe (including non-EU countries such as Montenegro, Albania, Turkey, Serbia, etc.). The Group serves as an internal cooperation forum (workshop) among all EU Data Protection Authorities, aimed at exchanging experiences and discussing practical issues arising during the handling of complaints and requests related to personal data protection. Topics from previous workshops include the right to be forgotten from search engines, data processing by private investigators, video surveillance systems, credit scoring, and the legitimization of proceeds from illegal activities. The Greek Authority hosted the group's meeting in November 2006.  

This year's event, held on November 18 and 19, was organized and hosted by the Georgian Authority in the capital of the country, Tbilisi. More than 50 participants, two of whom were from the Greek Authority, from 26 data protection authorities, took part in the two-day event, during which topics were discussed, among others, regarding the extensive examination of audit evidence, the design and stages of the audit, the methodology and structure of the decision, as well as difficulties in the application of administrative sanctions. Also discussed were the shared responsibility of posters and social media in the display and sharing of data through the latter, the use of social networks in humanitarian actions, the handling of complaints from minor subjects, the exchange of data among authorities, cases of exemption due to domestic use of data, personal data in artificial intelligence, as well as aspects of data breach incidents.
