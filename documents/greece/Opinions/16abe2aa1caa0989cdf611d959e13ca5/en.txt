OPINION 2/2024

The Hellenic Data Protection Authority convened at the invitation of its President in a meeting at its headquarters on 6th February 2024, in order to issue an opinion on a draft law following a relevant request from the Ministry of Digital Governance. Present were the President of the Authority Konstantinos Menoudakos, as the rapporteur, and the regular members of the Authority Spyridon Vlachopoulos, Konstantinos Lamprinoudakis, Charalampos Anthopoulos, Christos Kalloniatis, Aikaterini Iliadou, and Grigoris Tsolias. Present without voting rights were Fotini Karvela, Kyriaki Karakasi, and Konstantinos Limniotis, expert scientists, as rapporteur assistants, and Irini Papageorgopoulou, an employee of the administrative affairs department of the Authority, as secretary.

The Authority took into account the following:  
The Ministry of Digital Governance, with its letter no. A/ΕΙΣ/824/2.2.24, sent a draft law (hereinafter: Draft Law) regarding the adoption of measures for the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 19 October 2022 on the single market for digital services and amending Directive 2000/31/EC (Digital Services Act, hereinafter: Act), requesting the issuance of an opinion. Specifically, representatives of the Authority participated in the Working Group established under the letter no. 20296ΕΞ2023/24-04-2023 of the Ministry of Digital Governance, which aimed to coordinate actions for the adaptation of national legislation to the provisions of the Act. The Act comes into effect on 17.02.20241, according to Article 93 thereof. According to Article 288 TFEU, the Act has direct applicability in all member states, which are obliged to take the necessary measures to adapt their national legislation.

The aforementioned Draft Law includes provisions that establish domestic law measures for the full and effective implementation of the Act2, the primary objective of which is to promote responsible and diligent behavior of providers of intermediary services3, particularly online platforms, such as social media and online marketplaces (online marketplaces, app stores), in order to ensure a safe online environment within which EU citizens can freely exercise their fundamental rights. Recognizing the increasingly significant role that online platforms have played in the economy and society in recent years, the Act imposes due diligence obligations on providers of intermediary services, depending on the nature of the services they provide and their size4.

With this approach, which becomes particularly evident in Chapter III of the Act, where the obligations imposed on providers of intermediary services are outlined in their entirety (Section 1), as well as according to the services they provide (Sections 2, 3, 4) and their scope (Section 5), a proportional and fair burden is achieved for all providers of intermediary digital services, and the identified problems are addressed only where they arise, without excessively burdening providers to whom these problems do not pertain. The provisions of Chapter IV regarding the implementation and enforcement of the Act also follow this spirit, which provides, among other things, for enhanced supervision of the corrective measures imposed in the event of a breach of obligations by very large online platforms or by very large online search engines, as well as the possibility of intervention by the Commission as specifically provided, particularly in Article 56 of the Act.

Moreover, citizens of the Union are exposed to increasingly significant risks online, from the dissemination of illegal content and activities to restrictions on freedom of expression and other harmful social consequences. The measures provided in this Act are expected to significantly improve this situation, as indicated in its own recitals, by providing a modern framework that effectively safeguards the rights and legitimate interests of all stakeholders, primarily the citizens of the Union. The Act introduces significant guarantees so that citizens can express themselves freely, while also enhancing user autonomy in the online environment, as well as the ability to exercise other fundamental rights, such as the right to effective remedy, the prohibition of discrimination, children's rights, and the protection of personal data and privacy online (subject to the general relevant Union legislation, as analyzed below).

Therefore, in light of the above, it follows that the Act harmonizes the rules for intermediary services in the internal market, aiming to ensure a safe, predictable, and reliable online environment, in which the dissemination of illegal content online and the social risks that may arise from the spread of misinformation or other content are addressed, and in which fundamental rights are effectively protected and innovation is facilitated.

In addition to the above, it is noted as of major importance the reservation in favor of the GDPR, Directive 2002/58/EC, and the general Union legislation for the protection of personal data, which is set forth in Article 2(4)(z) of the Act, under which the legal framework for the protection of personal data continues to interpretively permeate the issues arising from the application of the Act.

Additionally, provisions are made for the preparation of annual activity reports by the Coordinator for Digital Services and for the creation and filling of new staff positions within the Coordinator for Digital Services for the needs of implementing the Act. Finally, the coverage of expenses of the EETT for the implementation of the provisions of the Act is provided, while the possibility of imposing fees by the Coordinator on providers of intermediary services is also granted.  
In Chapter D, provisions are included with the necessary amendments to existing legislation, as well as the necessary authorization provisions for the implementation of the new law. In Chapter E, other regulations of the Ministry of Digital Governance are included, while in Chapter F, the commencement of the law's effectiveness is defined.

A. Introductory Remarks

1.  
According to the provisions of Articles 51 and 55 of the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (hereinafter, GDPR) and Article 9 of Law 4624/2019 (Government Gazette A' 137), the Authority has the competence to supervise the implementation of the provisions of the GDPR, this law, and other regulations concerning the protection of individuals from the processing of personal data.  
2.  
According to Article 57 par. 1 point c of the GDPR and Article 13 par. 1 of Law 4624/2019, the Authority provides an opinion on any regulation that is to be included in a law or regulatory act, which concerns the processing of personal data.  
3.  
According to Article 67 par. 2 of Law 4624/2019, when drafting bills or regulatory acts that concern the processing of personal data by competent authorities for the purposes of Article 43 of the same law or are related to it, the opinion of the Authority is requested in a timely manner.  
4.  
Respect for the privacy of individuals and the protection of personal data are guaranteed both in Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR) and in Convention 108/1981 of the Council of Europe for the protection of individuals from automated processing of personal data, as well as in Articles 7 and 8 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union.  
5.  
According to Article 9A of the Constitution, "Everyone has the right to protection from the collection, processing, and use, especially by electronic means, of their personal data, as provided by law. The protection of personal data is ensured by an independent authority, which is established and operates as provided by law."

B. Comments on the individual articles of the draft law

1.  
Article 5 Draft Law  
This provision defines the Digital Services Coordinator and the other competent authorities, which are entrusted with the supervision of the implementation and enforcement of the Act, according to Article 49 par. 1 thereof. Specifically, the Authority is designated as the competent authority for the supervision of providers of intermediary services and the enforcement of the regulations of Articles 26 par. 1 point d and 3 and 28 of the Act.  
Article 26 par. 1 point d of the Act imposes on providers of online platforms that display advertisements on their online interfaces the obligation to ensure that for each specific advertisement presented to each individual recipient, they can recognize in a clear, concise, and unambiguous manner and in real-time significant information that is directly and easily accessible from the advertisement regarding the main parameters used to determine the recipient to whom the advertisement is presented and, where applicable, regarding how to change these parameters. This obligation is very important, considering the leading role that online advertising plays in the online environment, such as in relation to online platforms, where the provision of the service is often compensated through advertising revenues. Online advertising can pose significant risks, such as advertisements that constitute illegal content themselves, or provide financial incentives for the dissemination of illegal content or biased advertisements, which have implications for the equal treatment and equal opportunities of citizens or generally targeted advertisements based on potentially "profiling" the recipient of the advertisement. Therefore, recipients of the service should have direct access to information regarding the main parameters used by the provider to decide that a specific advertisement will be presented to a specific recipient. This information must provide essential explanations regarding the logic used for this purpose, including when it is based on profiling. More specifically, these explanations should include information about the method used for presenting the advertisement and, where applicable, the key profiling criteria used, as well as the means available to the recipient to modify these criteria. The risks of profiling, especially through the technique...
10

"Microtargeting" is well-known and is particularly pronounced in the context of online advertising, where the presentation to recipients of advertising services based on targeting techniques that respond to their interests can have particularly serious negative consequences and can negatively affect entire social groups in certain cases or exacerbate social ills, contributing, for example, to misinformation campaigns or introducing discrimination against certain groups. 

Considering that online platforms are particularly sensitive environments for the aforementioned practices and present a higher social risk, the Union legislator has prohibited, under paragraph 3 of Article 26 of the Act, providers of online platforms from presenting advertisements to recipients of the service based on profile training, as defined in Article 4 point 4 of the GDPR, using special categories of sensitive personal data under Article 9 paragraph 1 of the GDPR. It is worth noting that the aforementioned obligations imposed on providers of online platforms apply without prejudice to other obligations that apply to them under Union law governing the protection of personal data.

Article 28 of the Act concerns the protection of minors online. Specifically, it provides that 1) Providers of online platforms accessible to minors take appropriate and proportionate measures to ensure a high level of privacy, security, and protection for minors in their service. 2) Providers of online platforms do not present advertisements on their interface based on profile training, as defined in Article 4 point 4 of the GDPR, using personal data of the recipient of the service when they know with reasonable certainty that the recipient of the service is a minor. 3) Compliance with the obligations set out in this article does not require providers of online platforms to process additional personal data in order to assess whether the recipient of the service is a minor.

The protection of minors is an important policy objective of the European Union. Digital services now continuously collect and exchange data about children, and the process of "datafication" is particularly widespread. In order to ensure the protection of minors online, the Act imposes the aforementioned obligations on providers of online platforms. The protection of minors is also a concern of the GDPR, which establishes specific protection regarding their personal data, recognizing that children may have less awareness of the relevant risks, consequences, and guarantees, and their rights concerning the processing of personal data. It emphasizes in Recital 38 that "this specific protection should apply particularly to the use of personal data for marketing purposes or for the creation of personality or user profiles and the collection of personal data concerning children when using services offered directly to a child."

The Authority welcomes the assignment of oversight of the above provisions of the Act to itself, considering that the purpose they serve is fully aligned with the Authority's mission to supervise the implementation of the GDPR.

With Article 18 of the Draft Law, an obligation is established for the Authority as the competent body to send to the EETT, as the Coordinator of Digital Services, by the end of February of each year, the information referred to in Article 55 of the Act and in paragraph 2 of this article of the Draft Law for the previous calendar year, and specifically the details of human and financial resources for the implementation of the proposed Draft Law and the Act, including, in cases where fees have been imposed, the fees that have been collected, the number of violations of the Act that were identified, the number and details of hearings, the number and details of sanction enforcement procedures and other measures as well as the decisions, regulations, and acts issued within the framework of the empowering provisions of Article 23 of this Draft Law. 9. Article 23 of the Draft Law
Article 23 of the Draft Law contains authorizations for the regulation of details and technical or procedural issues. Among the aforementioned empowering provisions, the one concerning the Authority is provided in paragraph 6, according to which, by a joint Act of the EETT, as the Coordinator of Digital Services, of the Authority, and of the National Council for Radio and Television (ESR) as the other competent authority, after the agreement of their Plenary Sessions, every issue concerning their cooperation is regulated bindingly and in detail. Such issues include, by way of example, the coordination of data and information collection as well as their exchange, the manner of use of national information systems, or the process of receiving and transmitting incoming complaints to each authority that pertain to issues falling within the scope of the Act. 

Additionally, with the provision of paragraph 7 of the aforementioned Article 23 of the Draft Law under consideration, the Minister of National Economy and Finance is authorized to issue a decision—following a decision of the President of the Authority and the submission of a relevant request by the Authority—by which the collection on behalf of the Authority of the fines and periodic monetary penalties that it will impose in the exercise of its duties within the framework of the Act and the proposed Draft Law will be assigned to the services of the Tax Administration, in cases of non-payment to the bank account of the Authority, as provided in Article 16 paragraph 7 of the Draft Law. In this way, the direct collection by the Authority of even overdue fines and periodic monetary penalties that it will impose in the exercise of its duties within the framework of the Act will be ensured.

CHAPTER A  
PURPOSE AND OBJECTIVE  

Article 1  
Purpose  

The purpose of this Law is to take measures for the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 of the European Parliament and of the Council of October 19, 2022, regarding the single market for digital services and amending Directive 2000/31/EC ("Digital Services Act," hereinafter referred to as the Act) into the Greek legal order.  

Article 2  
Objective  

The objective of this Law is to establish provisions that enable the implementation of the Act, and specifically:  
1. The definition of the Digital Services Coordinator and the competent authorities to which the power of supervision over providers of intermediary services and enforcement of the Act is granted, as well as the regulation of their specific responsibilities.  
2. The assurance that orders for action against illegal content and orders for the provision of information issued by the competent judicial or administrative authorities meet the requirements of the Act in their content.  
3. The establishment of a Digital Services Advisory Committee and the determination of its responsibilities.  
4. The determination and the process for imposing proportionate and effective sanctions that may be imposed on providers of intermediary services for violations of the Act and this Law.  
5. The regulation of legal protection issues and the right to appeal against the decisions of the Coordinator and other competent authorities.  
6. The creation of a Register of providers of intermediary services established in Greece and the determination of the process and manner of its maintenance.  

Article 3  
Definitions  

1. For the purposes of this Law, the following definition applies:  
Legal representative of a provider of intermediary services that does not have its main establishment in the European Union: the natural or legal person who has their main residence or is established in Greece and is designated in writing by a provider of intermediary services that does not have its main establishment in the European Union, as its legal representative in accordance with Article 13 of the Act.  
2. For other terms used in this Law, Article 3 of the Act applies.

Operation of the Digital Services Coordinator  
In exercising the responsibilities of the E.E.T.T. as the Digital Services Coordinator, in accordance with the Act and this law, Articles 6 to 11 of Law 4070/2012 (A’ 82) regarding its operation, the disqualifications for appointment, incompatibilities, obligations, and the disciplinary responsibility of its members shall apply.

Article 14
Advisory Committee for Digital Services
1. An Advisory Committee for Digital Services is established at the Ministry of Digital Governance. The Committee serves as an advisory body on matters of digital services, and its operation does not in any way affect the independence of the Digital Services Coordinator and the competent authorities of Article 5 of this Act in the exercise of their duties based on the Act and this law. 
In this context, the Committee:
a) is informed about developments in Union legislation on digital services and provides non-binding recommendations to the Ministry of Digital Governance, either spontaneously or upon request, regarding potential legislative initiatives that could contribute to the more effective implementation of the Act and this law, as well as the content thereof and generally legislative interventions on issues related to the field of digital services.
b) develops collaborations with other entities within the framework of its activities and for the more effective exercise of its responsibilities.
2. The Committee consists of nine (9) members, each appointed by the Hellenic Authority for Communication Security and Privacy (E.E.T.T.), the Hellenic Data Protection Authority (A.P.D.P.X.), the National Council for Radio and Television (E.S.R.), the Ministry of Digital Governance, the Ministry of Development, the Ministry of Justice, the General Secretariat for Communication and Information, the Cyber Crime Unit, and the National Cyber Security Authority. 
The Committee is chaired by the member appointed by the E.E.T.T. The members of the Committee may be freely revoked by the bodies that appoint them. Decisions of the Committee are taken by a majority of the present members.
3. The Committee meets regularly at least every three months, upon invitation from the President, and exceptionally, upon invitation from the President or at the request of at least four (4) members. Members are invited by the President in any suitable manner, and the invitation includes the agenda items. The Committee may also meet via videoconference.
4. The Committee may include, without voting rights, public officials and employees, all types of executives employed in the public sector, and private experts, provided they are invited by the Committee.

CHAPTER III  
MEASURES FOR THE IMPLEMENTATION OF THE ACT  
Article 16  
Sanctions  
1. In the event of a violation of the provisions of the Act and this document, the Coordinator of Digital Services, or the competent authority referred to in Article 5, according to their jurisdiction, imposes fines and periodic monetary penalties on intermediary service providers under their jurisdiction, in accordance with cases (c) and (d) of paragraph 2 of Article 51 of the Act. The fines and monetary penalties are effective, proportional, and deterrent.  
2. In making a decision regarding the imposition and amount of the fine, the following shall be duly considered and evaluated for each examined case:  
a) the nature, severity, and duration of the violation,  
b) the intent or negligence of the provider that caused the violation,  
c) any systematic or repeated non-compliance of the provider with the obligations it bears under the Act,  
d) the type, size, and nature of the provided intermediary service and the financial capacity of the provider,  
e) any activity of the provider in multiple Member States,  
f) the degree of cooperation of the provider with the competent authority to rectify the violation and limit its potential adverse effects.  
3. The amount of the fine shall not exceed 6% of the annual global turnover of the relevant intermediary service provider for the previous financial year.  
In particular, in the case of providing inaccurate, incomplete, or misleading information, failure to respond or correct inaccurate, incomplete, or misleading information, and non-submission to inspection, the fine shall not exceed 1% of the annual income or global turnover of the relevant intermediary service provider or the relevant person for the previous financial year.  
4. In the case of imposing a periodic monetary penalty, it shall not exceed 5% of the average daily global turnover or income of the relevant intermediary service provider for the previous financial year.  
5. The fines and periodic monetary penalties are imposed by a specifically reasoned decision issued by the Coordinator or the competent authority, after having previously provided the provider with the opportunity to express their views on the identified violations, orally or in writing, and in accordance with the provisions that define the sanctioning procedure for each competent authority. The decision to impose sanctions is communicated without delay to the concerned provider. Before this communication, the Coordinator or the competent authority does not disclose the decision or any part of it to any third party and in any manner.  
6. The fines and periodic monetary penalties imposed by the Coordinator of Digital Services under this article are collected in the name and on behalf of the Coordinator and are remitted to them.  
7. In cases of sanctions imposed by the Hellenic Data Protection Authority (A.P.D.P.X.) in the context of the implementation of the Act and this document, paragraphs 4 and 5 of Article 15 of Law 4624/2019 (A’ 137) apply mutatis mutandis. Beyond the sanctions provided for in the above provisions, the A.P.D.P.X. may also impose a periodic monetary penalty in accordance with Article 51 paragraph 2 subparagraph (d) of the Act. The fines and periodic monetary penalties imposed by the A.P.D.P.X. in the context of the implementation of the Act and this document are collected in the name and on behalf of the A.P.D.P.X. For the above collection, the A.P.D.P.X., by decision of its President, opens a simple bank account under the account group 260 - Cash Management at the Bank of Greece, where the amounts of the fines and periodic monetary penalties imposed in the exercise of its powers, as provided by the Act, will be directly deposited by those liable.

Article 17  
Right to Judicial Appeal  
1. Against the decisions of the Coordinator or the competent authorities of Article 5, which impose sanctions in accordance with Article 16, a substantive appeal may be filed before the Administrative Court of Appeal of Athens.  
2. Against other individual administrative acts issued by the Coordinator or the competent authorities of Article 5, as well as regulatory decisions issued under the provisions of this law, a request for annulment may be filed before the Council of State.  
3. The deadline for filing the request for annulment or the appeal, as applicable, and the filing itself do not suspend the execution of the contested decisions of the Coordinator or the competent authorities, unless, upon request of the applicant or the appellant, the court, by reasoned decision, suspends in whole or in part the execution of the act, in accordance with the applicable provisions.  
4. For the admissibility of the discussion of appeals filed against the decisions of the Coordinator or the competent authorities of Article 5, before the Administrative Court of Appeal, which impose fines, the deposit of an amount equal to fifteen percent (15%) of the imposed fine is required to be submitted to the Coordinator, and in any case not exceeding two hundred thousand (200,000) euros, to the authority that imposed the fine.  
5. Against the decisions of the Administrative Court of Appeal of Athens issued in accordance with paragraph 1 of this article, a request for cassation may be filed before the Council of State.

Article 21  
Fees  
1. The Coordinator may impose an annual supervisory fee on providers of intermediary services that (a) have their main establishment in Greece or (b) their legal representative resides or is established in Greece, provided that they do not have an establishment in the Union but offer services in the Union, including Greece. This fee covers exclusively the cost of fulfilling the duties assigned to the E.E.T.T. according to this law and the Act.  
2. The annual supervisory fee is imposed on providers of intermediary services in an objective, transparent, and proportional manner.  
3. The Coordinator may choose not to apply a supervisory fee to providers of intermediary services whose total global or national annual turnover at the national level from activities falling within the scope of the Act and this law does not exceed a certain minimum threshold or whose activities do not reach a certain minimum market share or are of very limited geographical scope or provide specific categories of intermediary services.  
4. For determining the amount of the supervisory fee, the E.E.T.T. may apply either a distribution method based on the total annual global turnover of the providers and/or the type of intermediary services they provide, or a flat-rate charge on them, or a combination of these.  
5. The supervisory fee provided for in this article, as well as any other amount collected by the E.E.T.T. for any reason within the general powers conferred upon it by this law and the Act, shall be paid directly as revenue in the name and on behalf of the E.E.T.T. while being deposited in its bank accounts and collected in accordance with the Code of Collection of Public Revenues (K.E.D.E., Law Decree 356/1974, A' 90).
