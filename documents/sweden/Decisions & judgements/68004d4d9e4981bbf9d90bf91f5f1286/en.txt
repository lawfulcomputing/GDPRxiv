Decision after supervision according to the  
Crime Data Act – The Police Authority's  
processing of personal data when  
using email  
Decision of the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection states that the Police Authority during the period from  
August 1 to August 27, 2018, processed personal data in violation of Chapter 3, Sections 2 and  
8 of the Crime Data Act (2018:1177). This was done by allowing personal data, which in the  
event of dissemination to unauthorized persons outside the authority could have a serious negative  
impact on personal integrity, to be sent internally via email without the authority having taken  
sufficient technical and organizational measures to ensure and demonstrate that the processing of  
personal data is lawful and to protect the personal data being processed from unauthorized or  
unlawful processing.  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection notes that there is a risk that the technical and  
organizational measures taken by the Police Authority are not sufficient to meet the requirement for  
an appropriate level of protection in Chapter 3, Section 8 of the Crime Data Act.  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection therefore recommends, with support from Chapter 5,  
Section 6, first paragraph of the Crime Data Act, that the Police Authority take further measures to  
increase the level of protection regarding the handling of data.  
Furthermore, the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection notes that there is a risk that the  
Police Authority does not fulfill the obligation to notify the registered individuals as required by  
Chapter 3, Section 10 of the Crime Data Act when a personal data incident has occurred, unless  
measures are taken to counteract the risk. The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection therefore  
recommends the Police Authority, with support from Chapter 5, Section 6, first paragraph of the  
Crime Data Act, to review its handling of when the authority is obliged to notify the affected  
registered individuals about a personal data incident.

Justification of the Decision  
The Police Authority's Obligation to Implement Technical and Organizational Measures to Protect Personal Data Sent via Email  

Applicable Provisions  
The Police Authority is, according to Chapter 3, Section 1 of the Crime Data Act and Chapter 1, Section 4 of the Act (2018:1693) on the Police's Processing within the Scope of the Crime Data Act, the data controller for all processing of personal data carried out under the authority's management or on behalf of the authority. As the data controller, the Police Authority is responsible for ensuring that the processing of personal data within the authority is compliant with legislation and adheres to the fundamental principles for the processing of personal data. As part of this responsibility, the Police Authority must, according to Chapter 3, Section 2 of the Crime Data Act, implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and demonstrate that the processing is carried out in accordance with the provisions of the Crime Data Act. This means, for example, that there must be clear routines and guidelines for employees' processing of personal data when using email. Chapter 3, Section 1 of the Crime Data Ordinance (2018:1202) states that the technical and organizational measures that the data controller must take should be reasonable considering the nature, scope, context, and purpose of the processing, as well as the specific risks associated with the processing.  

Furthermore, Chapter 3, Section 8 of the Crime Data Act prescribes an obligation to take appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect the personal data being processed against, among other things, unauthorized or unlawful processing. Such measures may include, for example, controls, training in data security, and information about the importance of adhering to applicable security routines. The measures must, according to Chapter 3, Section 11 of the Crime Data Ordinance, achieve a level of protection that is appropriate considering technical possibilities, costs of the measures, the nature, scope, context, and purpose of the processing, the specific risks associated with the processing, and how sensitive the personal data being processed is. Chapter 3, Section 3 of the Crime Data Act also establishes an obligation to ensure that protective measures are integrated into the processing when determining the means for processing and during the actual processing.  

IMY's Assessment  
From the incident report that occurred in September 2018, it is evident that employees of the Police Authority unintentionally disclosed personal data concerning, among others, individuals who were subjects of criminal suspicions or involved in criminal investigations, in connection with the processing of data in email. The report indicates that the incident took place between March 28, 2016, and August 9, 2018. Thus, the incident largely occurred before the Crime Data Act came into force on August 1, 2018.  

In the supervision, it has been revealed that the Police Authority, at the time of the first incident report, had guidelines that outlined how and when data could be sent via email. These guidelines have recently been replaced by new ones. According to both the older and the new guidelines, the assessment of how the information is classified based on its protection value is crucial for the question of how the data may be sent and handled in the email system. It should also be emphasized that personal data incidents are often caused by the human factor. Against this background, IMY assesses that the technical and organizational measures that the Police Authority had implemented at the entry into force of the Crime Data Act on August 1, 2018, and until the end of August 2018, were not sufficient to meet the requirements of Chapter 3, Sections 2 and 8 of the Crime Data Act.

IMY further notes that there are additional security measures that may be reasonable to consider. This could, for example, involve completely separating the email that goes internally within the authority from the email that goes externally or introducing technical controls that require the employee to actively confirm that they are aware that the message will be sent to an external recipient via an open network and confirm that it has been ensured that the information is such that it can be sent externally. Another option could be that the authority does not allow personal data that is deemed to have a high protection value to be sent internally via email. In this case, no technical obstacles or cost aspects have emerged that argue against the Police Authority's ability to implement additional security measures to strengthen protection.

The Police Authority's obligation to notify affected data subjects of a personal data incident  
Applicable provisions  
According to Chapter 3, Section 10, first paragraph of the Crime Data Act, the data controller shall inform the data subject without undue delay about an incident if it has resulted in or is likely to result in a specific risk of improper intrusion into the data subject's personal integrity. There are a number of exceptions to this notification obligation, which follow from the second paragraph of the section and Chapter 3, Section 11 of the Crime Data Act. The purpose of the notification is primarily to enable the data subject to take necessary measures to protect themselves.  

IMY's assessment  
This supervision has been initiated against the background that the Police Authority has made two reports of personal data incidents to IMY, and within the framework of the supervision, IMY has reviewed the considerations and assessments made by the Police Authority regarding the question of whether there is an obligation to notify the affected data subjects about the incidents or not. The reports have in both cases been made against the background of the provisions of the Crime Data Act, as the incidents mainly concerned personal data that the Police Authority processed within the framework of law enforcement activities.  

It is the responsibility of the Police Authority as the data controller to assess in each individual case whether there is an obligation to notify the data subjects about a personal data incident. In the assessment, consideration should be given to how serious the consequences may be for the data subject due to the incident and how likely it is that these consequences will occur, taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purpose of the processing. The so-called Article 29 Working Party, which has now been replaced by the European Data Protection Board, has developed guidelines on the notification of personal data incidents under the General Data Protection Regulation (WP250 rev0.1). The guidelines are, in applicable parts, also guiding for personal data processing under the Crime Data Act. The guidelines recommend that a data controller takes into account the following criteria in the risk assessment: the type of incident, the nature of the personal data, sensitivity and volume, how easy it is to identify individuals, the severity of the consequences for individuals, the specific characteristics of the individual, the

The calendar reportedly contained certain sensitive personal data, according to the Police Authority. Choice of Intervention  
Corrective Powers  
According to Chapter 5, Section 7 of the Crime Data Act, the corrective powers available to IMY in cases of violations of the aforementioned law are specified. These include, among other things, orders, prohibitions against processing, and the imposition of administrative fines.  
Above, IMY has established that the Police Authority, during the period from August 1 to August 27, 2018, violated Chapter 3, Sections 2 and 8 of the Crime Data Act. According to Chapter 6, Sections 1 and 2 of the Crime Data Act, IMY can impose an administrative fine for violations of these provisions. Chapter 6, Section 4 of the Crime Data Act specifies the factors that should be particularly considered when determining whether an administrative fine should be imposed and what should influence the amount of the fine. This provision states that IMY should particularly take into account whether the violation was intentional or due to negligence, the damage, danger, or infringement that the violation has caused, the nature, severity, and duration of the violation, what the data controller has done to limit the effects of the violation, and whether the data controller has previously been ordered to pay an administrative fine. The enumeration is not exhaustive but indicates the circumstances that are particularly important.  
IMY shall, depending on the circumstances of each individual case, choose a corrective measure that is effective, proportionate, and deterrent.  
IMY notes that the violation of the provisions of the Crime Data Act has lasted for almost a month and that it may have serious consequences for the registered individuals considering that it concerns personal data which, if disseminated, can pose significant risks to the intrusion of personal privacy. The identified deficiency has resulted in at least one unauthorized person gaining access to a number of personal data that were particularly sensitive in terms of privacy. The fact that the Police Authority became aware of the incident only when the individual voluntarily contacted the authority indicates that the authority has not had full control over how often emails have been incorrectly sent outside the authority instead of internally, which means it may have occurred on more occasions than what is reflected in the incidents reported to IMY. Against this background, it is difficult to assess how many registered individuals have been affected by their personal data being inadvertently sent to an email address outside the authority during the time of the current violation of the Crime Data Act. Preventive Powers  
If there is a risk that certain personal data processing may conflict with the law or other regulations, IMY shall, through the preventive powers that follow from Chapter 5, Section 6 of the Crime Data Act, attempt to persuade the data controller to take measures to counteract that risk. This can be done through advice, recommendations, or remarks. If it is not deemed sufficient, IMY also has the option to issue a warning.  
IMY has concluded above that there is a risk that the technical and organizational measures taken by the Police Authority are not sufficient to meet the requirement for an appropriate level of protection according to Chapter 3, Section 8 of the Crime Data Act and Chapter 3, Section 11 of the Crime Data Ordinance.  
Furthermore, IMY has concluded, as stated above, that there is a risk that the Police Authority does not fulfill the obligation to notify that follows from Chapter 3, Section 10 of the Crime Data Act when a personal data incident has occurred, unless measures are taken to counteract this.  
None of the risks are of such a nature that IMY finds it justified to issue a warning. However, IMY finds reason to recommend that the Police Authority take additional measures to raise the level of protection and prevent personal data, which may have a serious negative impact on personal integrity, from being disseminated via email to unauthorized persons outside the authority, and also to review its handling of when the authority is obliged to notify affected data subjects about a personal data incident.

How to Appeal  
If you wish to appeal the decision, you should write to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection. Indicate in the letter which decision you are appealing and the change you are requesting.
