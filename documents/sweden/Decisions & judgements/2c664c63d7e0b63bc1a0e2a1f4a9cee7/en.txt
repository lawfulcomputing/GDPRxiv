The Regional Board of Region Skåne  
Decision of the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) finds that the Regional Board of Region Skåne (the region), as the data controller, has processed personal data in violation of Article 32.1 of the Data Protection Regulation by storing unencrypted personal data about patients on a USB drive and losing control of the USB drive on November 1, 2020. The region has thus processed sensitive personal data without ensuring an appropriate level of security in relation to the risk of loss, unauthorized disclosure of, or unauthorized access to the personal data.  
IMY decides, based on Articles 58.2 and 83 of the Data Protection Regulation and Chapter 6, Section 2 of the Data Protection Act, that the region shall pay an administrative fine for the violation of Article 32.1 of the Data Protection Regulation in the amount of 200,000 (two hundred thousand) kronor.  

Account of the supervisory matter  
Starting point for the supervision  
IMY received a report from the region on November 18, 2020, regarding a personal data incident that occurred on November 1, 2020. The report indicates that a USB drive containing personal identification numbers and sensitive personal data about 1,934 registered individuals was forgotten by an employee in the pocket of clinical clothing that was placed in a laundry bag for transport to the regional laundry.  
IMY has since received two complaints regarding the personal data incident. Against this background, IMY decided to initiate supervision.  
IMY has examined whether the processing of personal data on the relevant USB drive meets the security requirements set out in Article 32 of the Data Protection Regulation.

From the first statement from the region, the following is mainly noted.  
The data controller is the legal entity Region Skåne, and the regional board is ultimately responsible for the organization’s processing of personal data.  
On the lost USB drive, there are two types of personal data; personal identification numbers and special categories of personal data related to Article 9.1 of the Data Protection Regulation in the form of health data concerning 1,934 registered individuals.  
Regarding the special categories of personal data listed in Article 9.1 of the Data Protection Regulation, there is health data. The health data pertains to a procedure-related quality register, SCAAR, concerning coronary artery disease. The data is health data of the type "biodata." The data is unencrypted, but most of the values are recorded as ones and zeros, representing "yes" or "no," and are therefore not in plain text. Employees in the region are allowed to use removable media in the form of USB drives for processing personal data provided that decided organizational and technical protective measures are taken. The region's governing documents prescribed that as technical protective measures for the storage and transport of storage media, the protection should correspond to the protection value that the information has based on the information classification that has been conducted, and that storage media containing valuable information should be protected against unauthorized access, misuse, or distortion during transport. During mandatory training of employees, the region informs that USB drives should be avoided, but that employees using USB drives for storing sensitive or valuable information should use encrypted USB drives. These can be ordered internally. The lost USB drive has not been recovered despite extensive efforts to locate it. The search for the USB drive has taken place over an extended period (November 2020 – March 2021). The investigation in the matter does not indicate that the data has been disseminated or that unauthorized individuals have gained access to the information since the USB drive was lost within the organization, but since it has not been found, it is reasonably safe to assume that a breach of availability has occurred.

From the second statement from the region, the following primarily emerges.  
The personal data on the lost USB drive originated from a national quality register, SCAAR. The request for extraction from the quality register is managed by Uppsala Clinical Research Center, UCR. The researcher employed by Region Skåne had applied for extraction and received a decision on the disclosure of register data from the quality register SCAAR for research purposes. UCR describes in the application that the transferred material shall be stored securely, in encrypted form so that unauthorized persons cannot access it. The responsible researcher, employed by the research principal Region Skåne, has accepted and committed to the security measures that the centrally responsible data controller has established for the disclosure of the relevant personal data.  
Region Skåne's use of storage media is followed up annually in accordance with the guidelines Annual Cycle for Region Skåne's Information Security Coordinator. A technical security measure that was implemented in 2021 following the incident in question is 7-zip. 7-zip is a compression program that can also be used to encrypt files. An additional technical security measure that has been taken is the development of a storage area, Secure External Storage, for the handling of sensitive personal data, confidential information, or other information deemed sensitive to privacy and worthy of protection, which is intended to be shared with parties outside Region Skåne. Organizational security measures have also been implemented due to the personal data incident. Region Skåne is conducting an ongoing review of the instruction Processing of Personal Data for Research and intends, among other things, to update the instruction with clarifying references to the existing technical security measures that have been decided.  
The administration Region Service has also introduced stricter routines for the units Laundry and Textiles and Customer Center regarding the handling of found items at the laundry. To further strengthen compliance with the decided technical and organizational security measures, in addition to the activities described above and in previous statements, the administration Skåne University Hospital has intensified the data protection legal support to the research organization.  
Region Skåne does acknowledge that the personal data incident constituted a breach of the General Data Protection Regulation and has therefore decided on compensation to the affected data subjects who have requested it, in accordance with the obligation in Article 82.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation. However, the breach of the General Data Protection Regulation in this regard does not imply that Region Skåne has failed in its accountability as a data controller or in the security measures taken, but rather the breach is solely due to the negligence of an individual employee.

Applicable rules, etc.  
Personal data and the responsibility of the data controller  
Article 4.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation defines the concept of personal data as any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.  
Data concerning health is defined in Article 4.15 of the General Data Protection Regulation as personal data relating to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, which provide information about that person's health status.

Health data constitutes so-called sensitive personal data.3 Data that can indirectly reveal sensitive personal data also constitutes processing of sensitive personal data according to Article 9.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).4 The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) has stated that the concept of health data according to the GDPR must be interpreted broadly in light of, among other things, the Court of Justice of the European Union's ruling in the Lindqvist case, and as it is evident from Recital 53 of the GDPR that health data deserves comprehensive protection.5  
The data controller is, according to Article 4.7 of the GDPR, a natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body that alone or jointly with others determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data. If the purposes and means of processing are determined by Union law or the national law of the Member States, the data controller or the specific criteria for how it is to be designated may be provided for in Union law or in the national law of the Member States. The data controller is responsible for and must be able to demonstrate that the fundamental principles in Article 5 of the GDPR are followed, as stated in Article 5.2 of the GDPR.  
According to Article 5.1 f of the GDPR, personal data must be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security of the personal data, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures. Furthermore, it is stated in Article 24.1 that the data controller is responsible for implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is carried out in accordance with the GDPR. The measures must be implemented taking into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing as well as the risks, varying in likelihood and severity, to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. The measures must be reviewed and updated as necessary.  
Recital 74 of the GDPR states that data controllers should be held responsible for all processing of personal data that they carry out or that is carried out on their behalf. Data controllers should particularly be obliged to take appropriate and effective measures and be able to demonstrate that the processing is compliant with the GDPR, including with regard to the effectiveness of the measures. The data controller should take into account the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing as well as the risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons within these measures.  
The EDPB has stated that, in principle, all processing of personal data by employees that occurs within the framework of an organization's activities can be considered to take place under the control of that organization. Employees who, for example, have access to personal data within an organization are generally not regarded as "data controllers" or "data processors," but rather as "persons acting under the authority of the data controller or data processor" in the sense intended in Article 29 of the GDPR. Requirement for security in the processing of personal data, etc.  
According to Article 32.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the data controller and the data processor must take appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security that is appropriate in relation to the risk of the processing. This must be done taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing as well as the risks, varying in likelihood and severity, to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. Appropriate security measures may include, among other things, pseudonymization and encryption of personal data.  
In assessing the appropriate level of security, special consideration must be given to the risks posed by the processing, particularly from accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration, or from unauthorized disclosure of or unauthorized access to personal data that has been transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed. This is stated in Article 32.2 of the GDPR.  
Recital 39 of the GDPR states, among other things, that personal data should be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security and confidentiality of the personal data and prevents unauthorized access to and unauthorized use of personal data and the equipment used for processing.  
Recital 75 of the GDPR outlines factors that should be considered when assessing the risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. Among other things, it mentions the loss of confidentiality regarding personal data that is subject to confidentiality obligations, as well as when the processing concerns data about health or sexual life.  
Recital 83 of the GDPR provides further guidance on the specific meaning of the GDPR's requirements for security in the processing of personal data, including that data controllers should assess the risks associated with the processing and take measures, such as encryption, to mitigate them. In assessing the data security risk, one should also consider the risks posed by personal data processing, such as destruction, loss, or alterations due to accidents or unauthorized actions or unauthorized disclosure of or unauthorized access to personal data that has been transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed, especially when this may result in physical, material, or immaterial harm.  
IMY's Assessment  
The investigation in the case shows that the region has lost a USB drive. The USB drive contains unencrypted personal data about health and personal identification numbers for 1,934 registered individuals. The USB drive has not been recovered.  
Data Controller  
The region has stated that they are the data controllers for the relevant processing but simultaneously claims that they have not failed in their accountability as the breach is due to an employee's negligence.

IMY states the following. Article 4.7 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) indicates, among other things, that a data controller is a natural or legal person, public authority, institution, or other body that alone or jointly with others determines the purposes and means of processing personal data. According to Article 5.2 of the GDPR, data controllers are responsible for and must be able to demonstrate compliance with the principles set out in Article 5.1 (the principle of accountability). Furthermore, it is stated in Article 24.1 that it is the data controller who must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and be able to demonstrate that processing is performed in accordance with the GDPR.  

IMY notes that the region has determined the purposes and means of processing personal data in the present case, that is, how and why the personal data will be processed. Thus, the region is the data controller for the processing of personal data.7 That the region is the data controller means that it is responsible for complying with the GDPR in the processing of personal data. This responsibility also includes the requirements for the security of the data. The processing that occurs within the operation is the responsibility of the data controller even if it involves mistakes or misjudgments made by, for example, an employee. It is only if the employee acts for purposes that are not covered by the employer's purposes that it may be a question of the employee or someone else being the data controller for the processing. In this case, both the storage of sensitive personal data on an unencrypted USB drive and the loss of the USB drive occurred within the scope of the employee's duties. The region is therefore responsible for ensuring that the processing of personal data has been carried out in accordance with the GDPR's security requirements.  

Processing has involved a high risk  
As the data controller, the region must take appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security that is appropriate in relation to the risks of the processing, see Article 32 of the GDPR. The personal data being processed must, for example, be protected against loss, unauthorized disclosure, or unauthorized access. What constitutes an appropriate level of security varies in relation to, among other things, the risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons posed by the processing, as well as the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing. In the assessment, it must, for example, be considered what type of personal data is being processed, such as whether it concerns health data.8  

Patients can be considered to have a high expectation that unauthorized persons will not be able to access the information that arises in contact with healthcare. This is because patients have the right to confidential communication with healthcare.9 Processing of personal data within healthcare generally involves a high risk to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.  

On the relevant USB drive, the region stores health data that are sensitive personal data. Processing of sensitive personal data can pose significant risks to personal privacy. Furthermore, the USB drive contains personal identification numbers, which are considered particularly sensitive personal data.10 The processing of the data on the USB drive is therefore of such a nature that the data require strong protection.

The region has not taken sufficient security measures  
IMY finds that the relevant processing involves the storage of personal data on a USB drive without these being protected by appropriate security measures such as encryption. Encryption means that the protected information is transformed from a readable format to a coded format using an encryption key. The information then becomes unreadable to anyone who does not have access to the encryption key. This means that the protected information cannot be read directly where it is stored without an encryption key.  
The investigation shows that it is directly and solely possible to read from the data on the lost USB drive that it concerns health data linked to patients' personal identification numbers, meaning it is possible to identify that the registered individuals have been subject to care and treatment. IMY further notes that even though some of the values are recorded as ones and zeros representing yes or no, this does not negate that it concerns health data that can be linked to an identifiable person.  
When personal data is processed on removable storage media, such as USB drives, there are significant risks that personal data may be unintentionally disseminated. The security measures to be taken based on the assessment according to Article 32 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) mean that the processing of sensitive personal data should not be lost, disclosed unlawfully, or spread unintentionally.  
The region is responsible as the data controller for ensuring that this personal data is processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security, which includes protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against loss, destruction, or damage due to accidents. The fact that the region has stored health data on an unencrypted USB drive, which they have also lost, means there is a significant risk that someone who does not have the right to access them may gain access to the data. This, in turn, means there is also a risk that the data may be further disseminated. The region has thus not ensured an appropriate level of security in relation to the risk of loss, unauthorized disclosure of, or unauthorized access to the personal data.  
Overall, IMY finds that the region has not taken appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security that is suitable in relation to the risks associated with the processing, as the region has used a removable medium for storing sensitive personal data without ensuring that unauthorized persons cannot access them and subsequently lost control of the USB drive. The region has therefore processed personal data in violation of Article 32.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation.  

Choice of intervention  
Applicable provisions  
According to Article 58.2 and Article 83.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation, IMY has the authority to impose administrative fines in accordance with Article 83. Depending on the circumstances of the individual case, administrative fines shall be imposed in addition to or instead of the other measures referred to in Article 58.2, such as orders and prohibitions. Furthermore, Article 83.2 specifies the factors to be considered when deciding whether administrative fines should be imposed and when determining the amount of the fine. If it concerns a minor infringement, IMY may, as stated in Recital 148, instead of imposing a fine, issue a reprimand according to Article 58.2 b. Consideration shall be given to aggravating and mitigating circumstances in the case, such as the nature, severity, and duration of the infringement as well as previous relevant infringements.

Member States may establish rules regarding whether and to what extent administrative fines can be imposed on public authorities. This is stated in Article 83.7 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Sweden has decided, in accordance with this, that the supervisory authority shall be able to impose fines on authorities. For violations of, among other things, Article 32, the fine shall amount to a maximum of 5,000,000 kronor. This is stated in Chapter 6, Section 2 of the Data Protection Act and Article 83.4 of the GDPR.  
Each supervisory authority shall ensure that the imposition of administrative fines in each individual case is effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. This is specified in Article 83.1 of the GDPR.  

Imposition of Fine  
IMY has assessed above that the region has violated Article 32.1 of the GDPR. Violations of that provision may, as stated above, lead to administrative fines. IMY finds, upon a comprehensive assessment of the circumstances described under the heading "Size of the Fine," that there are grounds to impose a fine on the board and that it is therefore not a minor violation such that a reprimand would be warranted instead.  

Size of the Fine  
For violations of, among other things, Article 32 of the GDPR, the administrative fine for public authorities may amount to a maximum of 5,000,000 kronor. This is stated in Chapter 6, Section 2 of the Data Protection Act and Article 83.4 of the GDPR. In assessing the seriousness of the violation, IMY considers, in accordance with Article 83.2 g of the GDPR, that the processing has involved sensitive personal data concerning health.  
Furthermore, IMY takes into account what has emerged regarding the nature, severity, and duration of the violation based on what is stated in Article 83.2 a of the GDPR.  
The violation occurred when the region stored unencrypted personal data about patients on a USB drive and lost control of the USB drive. This has meant that the region did not ensure an appropriate level of security in relation to the risk of loss, unauthorized disclosure of, or unauthorized access to the personal data. The processing has involved that a larger number of patients can be directly identified through personal identification numbers together with health information, which poses a high risk to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects. The region's failure to meet security requirements is serious because it concerns personal data of such a nature that the data requires strong protection based on the nature of the processing.  
It also concerns personal data that is protected by confidentiality. Given that the region has stated that it involves 1,934 registered individuals, it can also be noted that the violation affects a large number of registered individuals.  
IMY considers it an aggravating circumstance that the USB drive has not been recovered and it is unclear what dissemination the personal data has undergone.  
IMY determines, based on a comprehensive assessment, that the region shall pay an administrative fine of 200,000 (two hundred thousand) kronor.
