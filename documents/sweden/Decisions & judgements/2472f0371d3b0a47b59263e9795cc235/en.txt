G Inkasso AB  

Decision after supervision according to the Debt Collection Act  
– collection of questionable claims  
The decision of the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection  
G Inkasso AB (556564-1106) has violated  
-  
Section 4 of the Debt Collection Act (1974:182) by failing to take sufficient measures to verify the validity of the claims it is collecting on behalf of the client Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd, thereby causing debtors unnecessary harm and inconvenience, and also violated  
-  
Section 8 of the Debt Collection Act by taking debt collection actions regarding claims from this client when there were reasonable grounds to believe that the claims were not legally founded or otherwise appeared to be unfounded.  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection expects G Inkasso AB to take measures to further improve its routines in order to avoid participating in the collection of questionable claims.  
The matter is concluded.  
Account of the matter  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) has been made aware that G Inkasso AB has been collecting claims for the Hong Kong-registered company Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd.  
IMY has initiated supervision of G Inkasso AB to investigate whether the company has taken reasonable measures to ensure that the claims it has undertaken to collect have a legal basis.  
Background  
G Inkasso has IMY's permission to conduct debt collection activities.  
In 2021, IMY received information from, among others, the Swedish Consumer Agency regarding G Inkasso AB as a debt collection representative for Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd. The information from the Swedish Consumer Agency and the ruling issued by the Patent and Market Court regarding Svensk Fordonsgaranti's marketing mailings revealed the following.

Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd.  
In 2020, the Swedish Consumer Agency became aware of the Hong Kong-registered company Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd (hereinafter Svenskt Fordonsskydd). The company sends mailings to private individuals in Sweden offering factory warranties for vehicles and extensions of previous warranty periods, despite the company having no connection to the seller or vehicle manufacturer. In some cases, the mailings have been sent to individuals whose warranty is still valid.  
The mailings constitute offers that, according to the Swedish Consumer Agency, can be mistaken for an invoice and mislead the consumer into making a payment. Those who sign up for the service become bound for a certain period.  
Since 2020, the Swedish Consumer Agency has received over 800 complaints against Svenskt Fordonsskydd and has attempted to contact the company, without success.  
On June 4, 2021, the Swedish Consumer Agency decided to place Svenskt Fordonsskydd on its warning list. The press release stated the following.  
After a large number of complaints and grievances, the Swedish Consumer Agency has chosen to place the company Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd on the Warning List.  
Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd. is a company registered in Hong Kong that markets vehicle warranties through direct marketing via mail.  
Since 2020, the Swedish Consumer Agency has received over 800 complaints against Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd. primarily concerning misleading marketing. The Swedish Consumer Agency's information service Hallå konsument has also noted a large number of complaints against the company during this period. The complaints have mainly concerned the offers that the company has sent out, which many have perceived as invoices.  
Consumers perceive the marketing as misleading for three reasons:  
1. It states that it is an extension of their previous warranty despite the fact that they have no connection to the seller or manufacturer.  
2. For some consumers, the information is incorrect as their warranty is still valid.  
3. Some perceive the "offer" as an invoice as it does not clearly indicate that it is an offer.  
The Swedish Consumer Agency assesses that Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd. has caused and continues to cause consumers significant problems. The authority also finds that the problems pose an imminent risk of financial harm to consumers.  
The Consumer Ombudsman, KO, has previously taken legal action against another company – Svensk Fordons Garanti AB (Garanti för bilar i Skandinavien AB in bankruptcy) – which marketed vehicle warranties in a similar manner, but which should not be confused with Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd.  
Since Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd. is registered in Hong Kong and has been unreachable, legal actions have been complicated. The Swedish Consumer Agency assesses that it is urgent to reach out to consumers and inform them about the problems and that the best way to do this is to place the company on the warning list.

G Inkasso AB is responsible for the debt collection management of Svenskt Fordonsskydd. Invoice services are also included in the assignment. The Swedish Consumer Agency was in contact with G Inkasso from September to November 2020 to obtain information about the collaboration with Svenskt Fordonsskydd. The Swedish Consumer Agency also provided the following information in the letter to G Inkasso.  
The Swedish Consumer Agency has, in its supervisory work through complaints, noted the company Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd, which markets vehicle warranties, among other things, through direct marketing via letters to consumers. The letter is designed in such a way that it lacks advertising identification and does not clearly indicate that it is an offer and not an invoice. Furthermore, the company claims in the letter that it concerns extending the consumer's protection for an additional three years since the consumer's manufacturer warranty has expired.  
Consumers are therefore misled into paying for the service under the belief that it is both an invoice regarding an entered agreement and that it concerns a warranty from the manufacturer of the vehicle.  
According to information from the Enforcement Authority, 20 applications for payment orders concerning Svenskt Fordonsskydd have been submitted. Most have been disputed or withdrawn. No ruling has been issued.  
Swedish Vehicle Warranty  
Swedish Vehicle Warranty (after a name change later to Warranty for Cars in Scandinavia AB) sent out mailings to private individuals during the years 2017 and 2018, which resemble the mailings described above.  
In this case, the Swedish Consumer Agency received over 800 complaints from consumers. The Consumer Ombudsman considered the marketing to be misleading and improper and sued the company in the Patent and Market Court. The court shared the Consumer Ombudsman's view. In a ruling from the Patent and Market Court on April 12, 2019, in case no. PMT 13814-18, Warranty for Cars in Scandinavia AB was prohibited under penalty from marketing to consumers, among other things, to claim that the consumer can extend the warranty for an additional three years or extend their protection for an additional three years through a new vehicle warranty when the marketing, according to the court, was misleading. In the judgment, which also included certain other misleading claims in its marketing to consumers, the company was ordered to pay a market disruption fee of 1.5 million kronor.  
G Inkasso AB (then Authority Inkasso AB) was responsible for the company's debt collection management. According to information from the Enforcement Authority, 375 applications for payment orders were submitted, with Swedish Vehicle Warranty as the creditor. Of these, 79 were disputed, 237 were withdrawn, and 31 were dismissed.  
Against this background, IMY has initiated supervision against G Inkasso AB with the aim of reviewing the company's procedures to counteract invoice fraud and similar issues, as well as how the company has applied the procedures in connection with the collaboration with Svenskt Fordonsskydd.

Justification of the Decision  
Applicable Provisions  
Debt collection activities shall be conducted in accordance with good debt collection practices. This means that a debtor must not be caused unnecessary harm or inconvenience, nor subjected to improper pressure or other improper debt collection measures (Section 4 of the Debt Collection Act). What constitutes good debt collection practice is specified in Sections 5-11 of the Debt Collection Act, the general guidelines "Application of the Debt Collection Act," and the practices of the IMY in debt collection matters.  
A debt collection measure should not be taken if there are probable reasons to believe that a claim is not legally founded or if the claim otherwise appears to be unfounded (Section 8 of the Debt Collection Act).  
According to the preparatory works of the Debt Collection Act, the provision in Section 8 expresses the principle that debt collection companies should not participate in the collection of questionable claims. Debt collection companies should, as far as possible, try to limit the risk of unfounded claims. Although debt collection companies cannot be required to thoroughly examine the basis for every claim, if it becomes clear from submitted documents or during the handling of the case that a claim lacks a legal basis, the debt collection company should no longer deal with the matter. In such a situation, the company should therefore reject the debt collection assignment or, if it later turns out that the payment claim is unfounded, withdraw from it (Prop. 1974:42 pp. 78 and 111).  
In IMY's general guidelines, it is stated regarding the control of the basis for claims during an ongoing collaboration that a simplified review of claim documentation can occur with a large influx of cases concerning a certain type of claim, but that new clients should be checked more thoroughly (p. 19).  
Furthermore, the general guidelines state that if several debtors raise the same objection against a certain client's claim, for example, that a product or service has not been ordered, there are often reasons for the debt collection representative to question whether the claim has a legal basis. Under such circumstances, the representative should not accept/decline the assignment (p. 21).  
Moreover, it is indicated that debt collection companies should respect a debtor's wish to have a matter concerning a disputed claim finally resolved. In that situation, the debt collection company should not pursue the claim but instead obtain an enforcement title or dismiss the case. If the claim is disputed and no enforcement title exists, it is justified to pursue a claim only if the debtor evidently lacks the ability to pay (p. 40).  
Assessment by the Data Protection Authority  
IMY believes that the principle that debt collection companies should not participate in the collection of questionable claims entails an obligation for the debt collection company to take reasonable measures to ensure that the claims the company undertakes to collect have a legal basis.  
A debt collection company that is about to initiate a collaboration with a new client should therefore always familiarize itself with the prospective client's operations and routines. In addition to obtaining information from the client itself, the debt collection company should conduct its own checks to assess the nature of the client's operations. Such checks can, for example, reveal whether the client appears on the Consumer Agency's or Swedish Trade's warning list, or if it has been subject to actions from any

authority and what is written about it on the internet in general. The company should further, at this initial stage, conduct a general check of the basis for the prospective client's claims.  
In the event that the collection agency, during the initial check, suspects that the client's operations are not entirely serious but still considers initiating a collaboration, the collection agency should take additional control measures to comply with what IMY considers to be good collection practice. Depending on what has emerged during the initial check, it may, for example, be relevant to engage in a dialogue with the client about their stance on what has come to light or to contact an authority.  
If doubts about the client's seriousness remain thereafter, the collection agency, in order to adhere to good collection practice, should not initiate any collaboration with that client. If the collection agency instead believes that what emerged during the initial check is sufficiently investigated and that there is generally a legal basis for the client's claims, collaboration may commence. Even in that case, however, the collection agency should, to a greater extent than usual, verify the basis for the client's claims before collection actions are taken and be especially attentive to reactions from the recipients of the claims or other circumstances that warrant further checks to assess the validity of the claims.  
In this case, it has emerged that G Inkasso AB conducts initial checks of clients when starting a new collaboration. These routines have, as far as can be seen, also been applied when initiating collaboration with Svenskt Fordonsskydd. However, it has emerged in the case that there were circumstances regarding the collaboration with Svenskt Fordonsskydd that, in light of the above-mentioned requirements according to good collection practice, should have given G Inkasso AB reason to take further investigative measures to assess the client's operations and the validity of the claims beyond the usual checks. IMY particularly considers that the contacts G Inkasso AB had with the Consumer Agency from September to November 2020, the large number of complaints to the Consumer Agency, the contact with the Enforcement Authority in November 2020, and the Consumer Agency's decision on June 4, 2021, to place Svenskt Fordonsskydd on its warning list should have prompted further checks.  
It is evident from the case that G Inkasso AB has also taken certain investigative measures in light of the information that emerged from the contact with the authorities. In connection with the Consumer Agency contacting the company in the fall of 2020 and informing them that the agency believed that the letter sent by Svenskt Fordonsskydd violated the provisions of, among others, Sections 8 and 10 of the Marketing Act (2008:486) as it is misleading and improper, G Inkasso AB made its own assessment that the Marketing Act and the Distance Contracts Act were followed and that Svenskt Fordonsskydd's claims were legally grounded. G Inkasso AB has not further justified its legal analysis in this regard.  
G Inkasso AB has also conducted certain in-depth investigations during 2020 and 2021, both before and after IMY initiated the current supervision. In a statement to IMY, the company has attached documents obtained in connection with their request for clarification of Svenskt Fordonsskydd's routines on October 5, 2021. IMY can confirm that the documents included, among other things, examples of the so-called guarantee offers that Svenskt Fordonsskydd had sent out. In these letters, the following formulations were included, among others:

The design of the letters can be compared to the letters that were deemed to constitute misleading marketing according to the Patent and Market Court's judgment on April 12, 2019, in case no. PMT 13814-18. In the relevant case, the following was stated, among other things:  
The marketing conveys the impression of a connection to the vehicle manufacturer or general agent and the guarantees these actors offer when purchasing a new car, which is supported by the reports submitted to the Consumer Agency by the Consumer Ombudsman (KO). In several of the reports, the complainants expressed that they believed the marketing had a connection to the vehicle manufacturer or its general agent. However, it is undisputed in the case that the company lacks a connection to vehicle manufacturers or general agents and the guarantees issued by them.  
The impression of a connection to a guarantee commitment from the vehicle manufacturer or general agent is further reinforced by how the marketing as a whole is designed. Notably, the presence of the statements at the top right of the mailings, where it states "Application for extended warranty for your passenger car with registration number (...)" (court annex 1), "Offer to apply for a new guarantee for your passenger car with registration number (...)" (court annex 2), and "Offer to apply for a new guarantee" (court annex 3).  
G Inkasso AB should have been well aware of the aforementioned judgment as the company acted as a collection agency for Garanti för bilar i Skandinavien AB, the company whose marketing was subject to assessment in the case. The fact that G Inkasso AB had previously been a collection agency for a company with a similar business model, where the marketing was deemed misleading, should further, according to IMY, have strengthened G Inkasso AB's reasons to be particularly careful in its assessment of the claims documentation from Svenskt Fordonsskydd. IMY believes that G Inkasso AB, against this background, should have conducted a more in-depth review in connection with the initiation of cooperation with Svenskt Fordonsskydd or, at the latest, when the Consumer Agency contacted the company in September 2020. This investigation should further, in light of the obvious similarities of Svenskt Fordonsskydd's business model with the company that G Inkasso AB had previously represented and the outcome in the judgment from the Patent and Market Court, at least have included a review of the guarantee offers that the company sent.  
IMY believes that the letters sent by Svenskt Fordonsskydd have obvious similarities to the letters assessed in the Patent and Market Court's judgment and the wording that was deemed to violate the Marketing Act. According to what is stated above, G Inkasso AB should have reviewed these letters at the latest in connection with the Consumer Agency's contacts with the company during the autumn of 2020.  
The fact that G Inkasso AB in October 2020, without clear justification, assessed that Svenskt Fordonsskydd's marketing was not in violation of marketing legislation, despite the Consumer Agency's information and the judgment mentioned above, strongly indicates, according to IMY, that G Inkasso AB did not sufficiently act to avoid participating in the collection of dubious claims.

claims. It is the debt collection agency that is independently responsible for assessing, based on current and relevant information, whether a collaboration with a client should be entered into or continued. The absence of any injunctions, ongoing cases, or judgments registered with the Consumer Ombudsman or the Consumer Agency's website regarding Svenskt Fordonsskydd can further be explained by the fact that Svenskt Fordonsskydd is registered in Hong Kong and has not been reachable, which was also stated in the Consumer Agency's press release regarding the inclusion of Svenskt Fordonsskydd on the Consumer Agency's warning list.  
IMY believes that, upon a comprehensive assessment, the claims procedures applied by G Inkasso AB in collaboration with Svenskt Fordonsskydd have meant that G Inkasso AB has taken debt collection actions when there have been probable grounds for claims not being legally founded or where claims otherwise appeared to be unfounded.  
From G Inkasso AB's statement, it further appears that no debt collection cases have been referred to court for substantive examination since G Inkasso AB's agreement with the Enforcement Authority on November 26, 2020, remained in effect pending the conclusion of the Consumer Agency's investigation or final determination in court. IMY cannot see that the agreement referred to by G Inkasso AB, which meant that all ongoing cases would be withdrawn and that no upcoming debt collection cases would be forwarded to the Enforcement Authority, also meant that G Inkasso AB was prevented from seeking to have Svenskt Fordonsskydd's claims subjected to substantive examination in court by applying for summons in disputed cases. G Inkasso AB's failure to facilitate the final examination of the validity of the claims in court, when there were circumstances that gave reason to question the accuracy of the claims, has also resulted in harm to debtors as disputed claims have routinely been put on hold instead of being finally examined in court. G Inkasso AB has also not contacted IMY for guidance on what constitutes good debt collection practice.  
Upon a combined assessment, IMY believes that G Inkasso AB has violated Section 4 of the Debt Collection Act by not taking sufficient measures to verify the validity of the claims it is collecting for the client Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd, thereby causing debtors unnecessary harm and inconvenience, and against Section 8 of the Debt Collection Act by taking debt collection actions regarding claims from this client when there were probable grounds for the claims not being legally founded or otherwise appearing to be unfounded.  
G Inkasso AB has now terminated the agreement with Svenskt Fordonsskydd Ltd and stated that they have taken measures to improve their processes. IMY does not, among other things, believe that there are grounds at this time to take any further actions against the company other than to point out the above deficiencies. IMY assumes that G Inkasso AB will continue to take measures to further improve its routines in order to avoid participating in the collection of questionable claims.

How to appeal  
If you wish to appeal the decision, you should write to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection. Indicate in the letter which decision is being appealed and the change you are requesting. The appeal must be received by the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection no later than three weeks from the day you received notice of the decision. The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection will forward the appeal to the Administrative Court in Stockholm for examination, unless the authority itself changes the decision in the manner you have requested. The Chancellor of Justice may also appeal the decision to safeguard public interests. However, the time for appeal for the Chancellor of Justice is counted from the day the decision was made.  
You may email the appeal to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection if it does not contain any sensitive personal data or information that may be subject to confidentiality. The authority's contact details are provided on the first page of the decision.
