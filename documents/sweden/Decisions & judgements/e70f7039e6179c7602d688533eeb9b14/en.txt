Trygg-Hansa Försäkring branch  

Decision following supervision under the  
Data Protection Regulation – Trygg-Hansa Försäkring branch  
Decision of the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) finds that Trygg-Hansa Försäkring branch (organization number 516403-8662) has processed personal data in violation of Articles 5.1 f and 32.1 of the Data Protection Regulation1 by failing to implement appropriate technical measures during the period from October 2018 to February 2021, thereby allowing unauthorized access to sensitive personal data concerning its customers. The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection decides, based on Articles 58.2 and 83 of the Data Protection Regulation, that Trygg-Hansa Försäkring branch shall pay an administrative fine of 35,000,000 (thirty-five million) kronor for the violation of Articles 5.1 f and 32.1.  
Description of the supervisory matter  
Background  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) received a tip in December 2020 that Moderna Försäkringar, a branch of Tryg Forsikring A/S (Moderna Försäkringar), had enabled access for unauthorized persons to personal data concerning sensitive information about Moderna Försäkringar's customers. IMY initiated supervision against Moderna Försäkringar in March 2021 to examine whether Moderna Försäkringar had taken appropriate measures to ensure a level of security that was adequate in relation to the risks associated with the processing of personal data, in accordance with Articles 5.1 f and 32 of the Data Protection Regulation.  
As part of its review, IMY has examined the 16 documents referred to in the tip. These include several different types of insurance documents, such as claims reports, invoices, insurance policies, insurance decisions, response cards regarding insurance compensation, changes in coverage, and requests for additional information for insurance investigations. The documents contain a large number of categories of personal data, such as names, contact details, health information, personal identification numbers, financial information, insurance holdings, event sequences (for example, time, place, actions, and other information provided by the data subjects in free text fields), and information regarding ownership and property damage.  
In April 2022, Moderna Försäkringar merged with Trygg-Hansa. Moderna Försäkringar then changed its name to Trygg-Hansa branch (Trygg-Hansa) but continued to operate under the same organization number as before (516403-8662). IMY will consistently use the new name of the supervisory subject, Trygg-Hansa, in its account of the events that occurred.  
Account from the supervisory subject  
Trygg-Hansa has mainly stated the following.  
On November 30, 2020, Trygg-Hansa was contacted by a person by phone who informed about the deficiency. The recipient of the tip at Trygg-Hansa did not understand that it concerned a possible incident, and the deficiency was therefore not reported further within Trygg-Hansa's organization.  
The security deficiency occurred in the following manner:  
1. An existing or new potential customer contacted customer service by phone and requested a quote for insurance. After the phone call, the customer service representative sent an SMS or email to the customer.  
2. The SMS or email contained a unique web address to a quote page on Trygg-Hansa's website.  
3. On the quote page, there were clickable links with web addresses leading to documents with insurance information. The person who contacted Trygg-Hansa as mentioned above was able to open the documents by clicking on the links.  
4. These documents had web addresses that could at the time be modified by the person in their browser by replacing numbers with other numbers. In this way, the person could retrieve documents of other customers.  
There was access via the internet to information about approximately 650,000 customers during the period from October 2018 until IMY contacted the company at the end of February 2021. The information involved includes names, personal identification numbers, contact details (address, email address, phone number), insurance numbers, claim numbers, financial information, health information, insurance holdings, ownership information (such as pet ownership, vehicle information, property information), property damage (such as information about workshops, compensation notices), event sequences (for example, time, place, actions, and other information provided by data subjects in free text fields) and other free text fields. It cannot be ruled out that information about legal violations (such as in connection with claims reports) or information about membership in a trade union (such as when insurance has been taken out with a trade union) may also have been disclosed.  
Analysis of behavioral patterns in logs indicates that 202 customers are likely directly affected in such a way that information about them (documents) may have been shown to an unauthorized person. As far as Trygg-Hansa has been able to ascertain, after reviewing logs, only the tipster and IMY have accessed the documents. To prevent similar security deficiencies from occurring, Trygg-Hansa had taken measures prior to the incident by developing an IT security policy, conducting regular penetration tests, and logging on nodes, transactions, and customer management systems, as well as holding annual training in data protection and security and ongoing...

Justification of the decision  
Has Trygg-Hansa ensured an appropriate level of security for the personal data?  
Applicable provisions  
According to Article 5.1(f) of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller shall process personal data in a manner that ensures appropriate security, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organizational measures (integrity and confidentiality).  
According to Article 9.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation, it is, as a rule, prohibited to process special categories of personal data (so-called sensitive personal data), including data concerning health. Article 9.2 specifies certain exceptions to the prohibition.  
Article 32.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation states that the data controller shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a security level that is appropriate in relation to the risk of the processing. This shall, according to the same provision, be done taking into account the latest developments, the costs of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing as well as the risks, varying in likelihood and severity, to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. In assessing the appropriate level of security,

Assessment by the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection  

Trygg-Hansa is the data controller  
Trygg-Hansa has stated that it is the data controller for the personal data processing that the tip-off concerned, which is supported by the investigation in the case. The Authority assesses that Trygg-Hansa is the data controller for the processing covered by the supervision.  
The processing has involved significant privacy risks and required a high level of protection  
The data controller must ensure a level of security that is appropriate based on the risks of the processing. The assessment of the appropriate level of protection must take into account, among other things, the nature, scope, context, and purpose of the processing as well as the risks, of varying likelihood and severity, to the rights and freedoms of individuals. In the assessment, special consideration must be given to the risks posed by the processing, including unauthorized disclosure of or unauthorized access to the personal data.  
The Authority notes that the processing of personal data has involved a large number of registered individuals within Trygg-Hansa's core operations. According to Trygg-Hansa's own information, it has concerned data about approximately 650,000 customers.  
The Authority further notes that the processing has involved a large number of personal data about each registered individual, which has enabled mapping of individuals' personal circumstances. Choice of Intervention  
Legal Regulation  
If a violation of the Data Protection Regulation has occurred, IMY has a number of corrective powers available under Article 58.2 of the Data Protection Regulation.  
According to Article 58.2 of the Data Protection Regulation, IMY shall impose administrative fines in accordance with Article 83, in addition to or instead of other corrective measures referred to in Article 58.2, depending on the circumstances of each individual case.  
Each supervisory authority shall ensure that the imposition of administrative fines in each individual case is effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. This is stated in Article 83.1 of the Data Protection Regulation.  
Article 83.2 specifies the factors to be considered in determining whether an administrative fine should be imposed, as well as what should influence the amount of the fine. Relevant to the assessment of the seriousness of the violation are, among other things, its nature, severity, and duration.  
According to Article 83.4, in cases of violations of, among other things, Article 32, administrative fines of up to 10,000,000 EUR or, if it concerns a company, up to 2% of the total global annual turnover in the preceding financial year shall be imposed, depending on which value is higher.  
According to Article 83.5, in cases of violations of, among other things, Article 5, administrative fines of up to 20,000,000 EUR or, if it concerns a company, up to 4% of the total global annual turnover in the preceding financial year shall be imposed, depending on which value is higher.  
If it concerns a minor violation, IMY may, as stated in Recital 148, instead of imposing a fine, issue a reprimand in accordance with Article 58.2(b) of the Regulation.  

IMY's Assessment  
Administrative Fine to be Imposed  
IMY has assessed that Trygg-Hansa has processed personal data in violation of Article 32.1 and that the violation is of such a serious nature that it also constitutes a violation of the principle of integrity and confidentiality in Article 5.1(f).  
The violation has occurred because Trygg-Hansa processed personal data with an insufficient level of security, which has resulted in a risk that unauthorized persons could gain access to approximately 650,000 customers' data during the period from October 2018 to February 2021. The personal data has included sensitive personal data and personal identification numbers, and unauthorized access to this data poses a high risk to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects.

IMY does not consider that the violations in question are of a less serious nature. Therefore, Trygg-Hansa shall be imposed an administrative sanction fee for the violations. In determining the amount of the sanction fee, IMY shall take into account the circumstances specified in Article 83.2 and ensure that the administrative sanction fee is effective, proportionate, and deterrent.  
The parent company's annual turnover shall be the basis for the calculation.  
In determining the maximum amount for a sanction fee to be imposed on a company, the definition of the term "company" used by the Court of Justice of the European Union in the application of Articles 101 and 102 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (see Recital 150 in the General Data Protection Regulation) shall be applied. The court's case law indicates that this encompasses any entity engaged in economic activity, regardless of the entity's legal form and the manner of its financing, as well as whether the entity legally consists of several natural or legal persons.  
What constitutes a company shall therefore be based on the definitions from competition law.  
The rules for group liability in EU competition law revolve around the concept of an economic unit. A parent company and a subsidiary are considered part of the same economic unit when the parent company exercises decisive influence over the subsidiary. The decisive influence (i.e., control) can be achieved either through ownership or through agreements. Case law indicates that a one hundred percent or nearly one hundred percent ownership presumes that control is deemed to exist. However, this presumption can be rebutted if the company provides sufficient evidence to demonstrate that the subsidiary operates independently in the market. To rebut the presumption, the company must provide evidence concerning the organizational, economic, and legal connections between the subsidiary and its parent company that shows they do not constitute an economic unit despite the parent company holding 100 percent or nearly 100 percent of the shares.  
Trygg-Hansa constitutes a branch of the Danish company Tryg Forsikring A/S. Tryg Forsikring A/S, in turn, is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Tryg A/S ("Tryg"). According to the presumption described above, it is therefore Tryg's turnover that shall be the basis for calculating the maximum sanction fee amount. To deviate from the presumption, Trygg-Hansa must provide sufficient evidence that another turnover should be the basis for the calculation.  
Trygg-Hansa has argued that it is the part of Tryg's turnover corresponding to the turnover of Moderna Försäkringar that should be the basis for calculating the maximum sanction fee. Trygg-Hansa has estimated this turnover to be 2,406,294,859 Danish kroner. In the alternative, Trygg-Hansa believes that the maximum sanction fee should be based on the turnover of Moderna Försäkringar and Tryg, whereby the turnover of the companies acquired by Tryg after the period under review should be excluded from the calculation of the sanction fee. Trygg-Hansa has estimated this turnover to be 23,622,304,333 Danish kroner.  
IMY has understood Trygg-Hansa's position to mean that the maximum sanction fee should primarily be calculated based on the hypothetical turnover that the branch Moderna Försäkringar would have had in 2022 if Trygg-Hansa and Moderna Försäkringar had not merged in April 2022. Severity of the Violation  
IMY makes the following considerations regarding the severity of the violation. The existence of a possible unauthorized access to the data of approximately 650,000 customers implies that there has been a risk to a high number of individuals. The data has included sensitive personal information, such as health data, and other information of a privacy-sensitive nature, such as personal identification numbers and financial information. It cannot be ruled out that information about legal violations may have been disclosed. The processing of personal data has involved significant risks. Individual persons have been directly identifiable, which has meant that sensitive information could be linked to identified individuals. The data has been processed in a context where the registered individuals have legitimate expectations of a high level of confidentiality and robust protection against unauthorized access.  
The data has been collected to enable assessments and decisions regarding the registered individuals, which is a type of personal data processing that can entail higher risks and requires higher protection. Information about, for example, ownership, which could pose a risk of theft, could easily be linked to names and addresses in the event of unauthorized access. Due to the nature of the data, and since the documents contained multiple aggregated pieces of information, any unauthorized access has posed a high risk of reputational damage and loss of confidentiality. Trygg-Hansa's analysis of behavioral patterns in logs indicates that 202 customers are likely directly affected in such a way that information about them in documents may have actually been shown to unauthorized persons, and IMY notes that unauthorized access has occurred at least once, in connection with the tip-off about the deficiency provided to IMY.  
The violation has also persisted for a longer period, between October 2018 and the time when IMY contacted Trygg-Hansa and pointed out the deficiency in February 2021. Trygg-Hansa received information about the security deficiencies through an external tip in November 2020, which could have been used to address the deficiencies and thereby reduce privacy risks for individuals. However, Trygg-Hansa was unable to use the information to rectify the deficiencies. The violation has concerned Trygg-Hansa's core operations, where Trygg-Hansa can be assumed to have knowledge of the risks and requirements for the protection of personal data.  
According to the EDPB's guidelines, the supervisory authority should assess whether the violation is of low, medium, or high severity.  
IMY has determined that the violation is so serious that it also constitutes a violation of the fundamental principle of integrity and confidentiality according to Article 5.1 f of the GDPR.

The administrative fine must be effective, proportionate, and deterrent  
The administrative fine must be effective, proportionate, and deterrent. This means that the amount must be determined so that the administrative fine leads to correction, that it has a preventive effect, and that it is also proportionate in relation to both the current violations and the financial capacity of the supervisory subject.  
In the proportionality assessment, IMY takes into account that Trygg's annual turnover has significantly increased due to the acquisition of companies that were not included in the company's total turnover at the time of the violation.  
IMY also attaches great importance to the fact that the violation, as far as has been revealed in the case, occurred solely in the Swedish branch. To base the assessment solely on the group's turnover in this case, where the violation has affected a limited part of the business, would result in the administrative fine being set too high in relation to what has occurred. How to appeal  
If you wish to appeal the decision, you must write to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection. Indicate in your writing which decision you are appealing and the change you are requesting. The appeal must be received by the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection no later than three weeks from the day you received notice of the decision. If the appeal is submitted in a timely manner, the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection will forward it to the Administrative Court in Stockholm for examination.  
You can email the appeal to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection if it does not contain any sensitive personal data or information that may be subject to confidentiality. The authority's contact details are provided on the first page of the decision.
