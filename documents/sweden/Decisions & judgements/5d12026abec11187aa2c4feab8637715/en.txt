If Skadeförsäkring AB  

Decision following supervision under the  
Data Protection Regulation – If  
Skadeförsäkring  
Decision of the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten) finds that If Skadeförsäkring AB, on November 6, 2020, processed personal data in violation of Article 32.1 of the Data Protection Regulation. This occurred when If Skadeförsäkring AB sent sensitive personal data about the complainant in an email without using a sufficiently secure encryption solution. Thus, If Skadeförsäkring AB did not take appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a security level that is adequate in relation to the risks associated with the processing.  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection issues a reprimand to If Skadeförsäkring AB under Article 58.2 b of the Data Protection Regulation for the identified violation.  

Account of the supervisory case  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) has initiated supervision regarding If Skadeförsäkring AB (If or the company) following a complaint.  
The complainant has stated that personal data concerning health was transmitted via email without being protected by encryption all the way from the sender to the recipient, i.e., through a so-called end-to-end encryption. IMY has initiated supervision in response to the complaint to investigate whether If has ensured an appropriate security level in accordance with Article 32 of the Data Protection Regulation for the relevant processing.  

The handling has taken place through written correspondence. Given that it concerns cross-border processing, IMY has utilized the mechanisms for cooperation and consistency found in Chapter VII of the Data Protection Regulation. The relevant supervisory authorities have been the data protection authorities in Denmark, Finland, Norway, and Estonia.

Information from If  
If has primarily stated the following.  
Transmission of sensitive personal data via email on November 6, 2020  
If has stated that the company is the data controller for the personal data processing to which the complaint pertains. Furthermore, If has indicated that within the framework of its claims handling, it sent an email to the complainant regarding a personal injury reported by the complainant. The email was sent on November 6, 2020, to the email address provided by the complainant. It contained If's decision and an attached file containing the medical assessment that formed the basis for the decision. The medical assessment included background, sequence of events, diagnosis, assessment, grading of any disability, and date of birth (not personal identification number).  
The email was sent encrypted with so-called enforced Transport Layer Security (TLS) encryption. This meant that the message was transmitted encrypted from If's servers to the recipient's email server, which in this case was with Tele2 (the operator). If the receiving server could not accept a TLS-encrypted message, it was not sent. In this way, it was ensured that the message was always transmitted encrypted. The guidelines in effect at that time stipulated that when sensitive personal data was sent via email, the email should always be encrypted.  
The solution with enforced TLS encryption was implemented following a decision from the Danish Data Protection Agency, where If was criticized for using so-called opportunistic TLS for encrypting emails containing sensitive personal data.  
If has also referred to a decision from the Danish Data Protection Agency, where the data protection authority concluded, after a review at a law firm, that the use of enforced TLS 1.2 provides encryption with sufficient security for emails containing confidential and sensitive personal information during transmission. If has stated that it was this encryption solution that was also used when the email containing the medical assessment was sent to the complainant on November 6, 2020.  

New solution for handling emails  
If has indicated that the company, following the complaint, has increased security, among other things, by developing and launching a new communication solution for emails to the company's customers. Within the framework of this solution, If's customers can access emails via "My Pages" on the company's website. The solution works in such a way that a notification is sent to the customer via email or SMS with information that the customer has received a message from If that can be read on "My Pages." To log in to "My Pages," the customer needs to authenticate themselves with BankID.

**Motivering av beslutet**  
**Tillämpliga bestämmelser**  
Uppgifter om hälsa utgör s.k. känsliga personuppgifter. Det är förbjudet att behandla sådana särskilda kategorier av personuppgifter enligt artikel 9.1 i dataskyddsförordningen, såvida behandlingen inte omfattas av något av undantagen i artikel 9.2. Dessa uppgifter anses extra skyddsvärda eftersom behandlingen av dessa uppgifter kan innebära betydande risker för enskildas grundläggande rättigheter och friheter.  
Den personuppgiftsansvarige ska vidare, enligt artikel 32.1 i dataskyddsförordningen, vidta lämpliga tekniska och organisatoriska åtgärder för att säkerställa en lämplig säkerhetsnivå till skydd för de uppgifter som behandlas. Vid bedömningen av vilka tekniska och organisatoriska åtgärder som är lämpliga ska den personuppgiftsansvarige beakta den senaste utvecklingen, genomförandekostnaderna och behandlingens art, omfattning, sammanhang och ändamål samt riskerna för fysiska personers rättigheter och friheter.  
Enligt artikel 32.1 omfattar lämpliga skyddsåtgärder bland annat:  
• Pseudonymisering och kryptering av personuppgifter.  
• Förmågan att fortlöpande säkerställa konfidentialitet, integritet, tillgänglighet och motståndskraft hos behandlingssystemen och tjänsterna.  
• Förmågan att återställa tillgängligheten och tillgången till personuppgifter i rimlig tid vid en fysisk eller teknisk incident.  
• Ett förfarande för att regelbundet testa, undersöka och utvärdera effektiviteten hos de tekniska och organisatoriska åtgärder som ska säkerställa behandlingens säkerhet.  
Enligt artikel 32.2 i dataskyddsförordningen ska vid bedömningen av lämplig säkerhetsnivå särskild hänsyn tas till de risker som behandlingen medför, i synnerhet för oavsiktlig eller olaglig förstöring, förlust eller ändring eller till obehörigt röjande av eller obehörig åtkomst till de personuppgifter som överförts, lagrats eller på annat sätt behandlats.  

**IMY:s bedömning**  
E-postmeddelande sänt till den klagande den 6 november 2020  
Eftersom den som är personuppgiftsansvarig enligt artikel 32 i dataskyddsförordningen är ansvarig för säkerheten vid behandlingen, behöver den personuppgiftsansvarige bedöma de risker som är förknippade med den behandling av personuppgifter som ska ske och vidta lämpliga tekniska och organisatoriska åtgärder för att hantera de risker som identifieras. Vad som är lämpliga åtgärder ska inte uppfattas som att det är frågan om en godtycklig bedömning utan en bedömning som är adekvat utifrån behandlingens art, omfattning, sammanhang och ändamål samt riskerna för den enskildes fri- och rättigheter. I detta fall är det frågan om överföring av känsliga personuppgifter över öppet nät (internet). Att det rör sig om behandling av känsliga personuppgifter innebär att det ställs högre krav på de tekniska och organisatoriska åtgärder som den personuppgiftsansvarige ska vidta.

the email message passes along the way. A consequence of this is that anyone who  
has access to equipment through which unprotected email messages pass can take part in,  
disseminate, or distort them.  
By taking appropriate technical and organizational measures, personal data  
transmitted over an open network should not be readable by unauthorized parties. This can  
be achieved by encrypting the email message that contains personal data and/or by  
protecting the transmission of the email message through encryption. Mandatory TLS is an  
example of an encryption solution that can be used to protect an email message. In this  
particular case, the transmission of the email message occurred with mandatory TLS.  
IMY notes that the solution used by If to transmit the email message to the complainant  
only encrypted the email message during transport from If's email server to the email  
server provided by the complainant's operator. This meant that the encryption ceased  
before the message reached the final recipient and thus did not constitute so-called end-to-end  
encryption. In this way, there was a risk that unauthorized parties could access the email  
message in plaintext after the encrypted transmission had ceased.  
In light of the above, If cannot be considered to have protected the data in such a  
way that only the intended recipient could access it after the email message had been  
delivered to the operator's email server. At this point, the encryption ceased, and thus the  
data lacked sufficient protection against unauthorized disclosure or unauthorized access to  
the personal data. Since it involved sensitive personal data, this constituted a significant  
risk of privacy infringement against the complainant.  
If has referred to a ruling from the Danish Data Protection Agency, concerning a law  
firm, to demonstrate that the Data Protection Agency has assessed mandatory TLS as a  
sufficiently secure solution for transmitting sensitive personal data. IMY can note that the  
decision does not pertain to a specific processing but rather concerns planned supervision  
regarding the security of personal data processing, particularly in the use of encrypted  
email messages. The law firm has indicated various methods they use to ensure secure  
communication. The method used is assessed in each individual case, and one of the  
methods is to encrypt the transmission of email messages using mandatory TLS. The Data  
Protection Agency has assessed that the law firm's actions were in accordance with the  
General Data Protection Regulation. IMY's review in this case differs from the cited  
ruling as this review concerns whether a specific shipment has been covered by sufficient  
protection all the way from the sender to the recipient.  
In summary, IMY finds that If, at the relevant time, did not take appropriate  
technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security that was appropriate in  
relation to the risks of the processing, as If sent the email message containing sensitive  
personal data without ensuring that the chosen encryption solution protected the message  
all the way to the recipient. If thus processed personal data in violation of Article 32.1 of  
the General Data Protection Regulation.  
If's new solution for handling email messages  
If has stated that the company, in the time following the complaint, has developed and  
launched a new communication solution for email messages to the company's customers. It  
is noted that the personal data processing that occurs within the framework of this solution  
is not subject to the complaint and is therefore not part of IMY's review.

Choice of Intervention  
According to Article 58.2 and Article 83.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation, IMY has the authority to impose an administrative fine. Depending on the circumstances of the individual case, an administrative fine may be imposed in addition to or instead of the other measures referred to in Article 58.2, such as orders and prohibitions. Furthermore, Article 83.2 specifies the factors to be considered when deciding whether to impose an administrative fine and when determining the amount of the fine. If it concerns a minor infringement, IMY may, as stated in Recital 148, instead of imposing a fine, issue a reprimand in accordance with Article 58.2 b. Consideration should be given to aggravating and mitigating circumstances in the case, such as the nature, severity, and duration of the infringement, as well as previous relevant infringements.  
IMY has determined that If has processed personal data in violation of Article 32.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation. A violation of that provision may lead to an administrative fine. If's infringement occurred when the company sent an email containing sensitive personal data to the complainant on November 6, 2020, without using a sufficiently secure encryption solution that protected the message all the way from the sender to the intended recipient (so-called end-to-end encryption).  
IMY's supervision concerns an email that If sent without using adequate security measures – the subject of the complaint. If has been working to improve security, partly by changing from opportunistic to mandatory TLS following the Data Inspectorate's decision against If, and partly by taking security measures after the complainant pointed out security deficiencies, including developing and launching a new communication solution for emails to the company's customers.  
Overall, IMY therefore considers this to be a minor infringement, and thus If is given a reprimand under the support of Article 58.2 b of the General Data Protection Regulation.

How to Appeal  
If you wish to appeal the decision, you must write to IMY. Indicate in your letter which decision you are appealing and the amendment you are requesting. The appeal must be received by IMY no later than three weeks from the day you were informed of the decision. If the appeal is submitted in a timely manner, IMY will forward it to the Administrative Court in Stockholm for examination.  
You may email the appeal to IMY if it does not contain any privacy-sensitive personal data or information that may be subject to confidentiality. The authority's contact details are provided on the first page of the decision.
