Decision of the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection finds that the Discrimination Ombudsman has processed personal data in violation of Article 32.1 of the Data Protection Regulation by failing to take appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure an adequate level of security for personal data when using the web form for tips and complaints about discrimination during the period from September 1, 2020, to September 17, 2021.  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection decides, pursuant to Chapter 6, Section 2 of the Data Protection Act and Articles 58.2 and 83 of the Data Protection Regulation, that the Discrimination Ombudsman shall pay an administrative fine of 100,000 (one hundred thousand) Swedish Krona for the violation of Article 32.1 of the Data Protection Regulation.  

Account of the supervision case  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) decided on October 6, 2021, to initiate supervision against the Discrimination Ombudsman (DO) following DO's report on a personal data incident on September 20, 2021 (IMY's case PUI-2021-4142). The purpose of the supervision is to examine DO's processing of personal data in connection with the authority's collection and storage of personal data when individuals use DO's web form for tips or complaints about discrimination. The supervision has been conducted as an inspection at DO on October 20, 2021, followed by written proceedings.  
IMY's examination in the case has been limited to assessing whether DO has complied with the obligation to take appropriate security measures according to Article 32.1 of the Data Protection Regulation for personal data collected via the web form for tips and complaints about discrimination during the period from September 1, 2020, to September 17, 2021.  

Reasoning for the decision  
Applicable provisions, etc.  
According to Article 4.7 of the Data Protection Regulation, the data controller refers to a natural or legal person, public authority, institution, or other body that alone or jointly with others determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data. If the purposes and means of the processing are determined by Union law or the national law of the Member States, the data controller or the specific criteria for how they are to be designated may be provided for in Union law or in the national law of the Member States.  
Processing of personal data must comply with the fundamental principles in Article 5 of the Data Protection Regulation. Article 5.2 states the accountability principle, meaning that the data controller must be responsible for and be able to demonstrate compliance with the fundamental principles.

Special categories of personal data are listed in Article 9.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation, so-called sensitive personal data. These consist of personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, or membership in a trade union, as well as the processing of genetic data, biometric data for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person, data concerning health, or data concerning a natural person's sex life or sexual orientation.  
According to Article 32.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation, both the data controller and the data processor must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk of the processing. In assessing which technical and organizational measures are appropriate, the data controller and the data processor shall take into account the latest developments, the implementation costs, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing, as well as the risks to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.  
According to Article 32.1, appropriate safeguards, where appropriate, include:  
a) pseudonymization and encryption of personal data,  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services,  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data in a timely manner in the event of a physical or technical incident, and  
d) a procedure for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  
According to Article 32.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation, in assessing the appropriate level of security, special consideration shall be given to the risks posed by the processing, in particular the risk of accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration, or unauthorized disclosure of or access to personal data that has been transmitted, stored, or otherwise processed.  
Recital 75 of the General Data Protection Regulation states that in assessing the risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, various factors should be taken into account. Among other things, it mentions personal data subject to confidentiality, data concerning health or sex life, whether the processing involves personal data concerning vulnerable natural persons, particularly children, or whether the processing involves a large amount of personal data and concerns a large number of data subjects. Recital 76 states that the likelihood and severity of the risk to the rights and freedoms of the data subject should be determined based on the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing. The risk should be assessed based on an objective evaluation, through which it is determined whether the processing involves a risk or a high risk.

On September 22, 2021, DO published the news "DO shuts down the web form for tips and complaints," indicating that DO had become aware of a security issue in its web environment.

IMY's Assessment  

The processing has required a high level of security  
The investigation shows that the DO, based on the authority's mandate regarding discrimination, has used a web form to collect personal data related to discrimination in the form of tips and complaints. This has been done by allowing the data subject to describe what has happened in free text fields and, as a mandatory requirement, indicate the type of discrimination intended through predefined options. Among these options were discrimination related to ethnic origin, disability, gender identity and expression, religion or other beliefs, and sexual orientation. The data subject of a tip or complaint could provide their email address or name. Personal data about children could occur, for example, names of students. Other examples of personal data included names of employees at a specific workplace. Tips and complaints could concern events that others than the submitter of the web form had been subjected to. The information could be subject to statutory confidentiality according to Chapter 33, Section 1 of the Public Access to Information and Secrecy Act (OSL).  
The DO, as the data controller, is obliged to take appropriate security measures. In assessing the appropriate level of security, special consideration must be given to the risks that the processing entails, particularly from accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration, or to unauthorized disclosure of or unauthorized access to the personal data that has been transferred, stored, or otherwise processed, see Article 32.1 and 32.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Ensuring appropriate security means that the security level must be adapted to the risks associated with the specific processing.  
IMY notes that sensitive personal data according to Article 9.1 of the GDPR includes, among other things, personal data that reveals race or ethnic origin, religious or philosophical beliefs, health data, and data concerning a natural person's

The web form could be used for tips or complaints regarding the grounds for discrimination, which include gender identity or expression, ethnic origin, religion or other beliefs, disability, and sexual orientation. In summary, IMY assesses that the nature, scope, and context of the personal data processing have posed such risks that high demands must be placed on the security measures that need to be implemented according to Article 32.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation regarding the processing of personal data collected via the web form.  

DO has not taken sufficient measures to protect the data  
IMY finds that during the period from September 1, 2020, to September 17, 2021, there was a lack of a security setting that protected personal data on the summary page of the web form, with the exception of the fields for telephone number and email address. This led to personal data from the summary page of the web form being transferred to the data processor.  

According to IMY, the mere fact that DO inadvertently transferred the relevant data to the data processor means that the data was, in fact, not protected against unauthorized disclosure. DO has also stated in its report of the personal data incident that the security incident consisted of unauthorized disclosure.  

The investigation in the matter shows that DO had routines for, among other things, the registration of systems containing personal data and that DO decided to take a number of measures to protect personal data collected via the web form prior to its launch. Before the launch, DO noted, among other things, that the security setting in the analysis tool needed to be activated. However, it was not ensured that the setting functioned fully. The measures taken by DO were therefore not sufficient to protect the personal data from being disclosed to the data processor.

In a comprehensive assessment against the background of the above circumstances, IMY assesses that this constitutes a violation of Article 32.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation of low severity.  
IMY will also assess whether there are any aggravating or mitigating circumstances that may affect the size of the administrative fine.  
IMY notes that the DO took measures after the incident by ceasing the collection of personal data via the web form for tips and complaints and by contacting the data processor to investigate the incident. The DO also ensured that the processor ceased processing the personal data. Information about the incident was provided by the DO publishing a news item on the authority's website. IMY assesses that these measures cannot be considered to go beyond what is expected of the DO in this case. Therefore, the measures taken do not constitute a mitigating factor according to IMY.  
IMY assesses that, in addition to the circumstances considered above, there are no aggravating or mitigating circumstances that affect the administrative fine.  
In light of the seriousness of the violations, IMY determines the administrative fine to be 100,000 kronor for the violation. IMY assesses that this amount is effective, proportionate, and deterrent.

How to appeal  
If you wish to appeal the decision, you should write to IMY. Indicate in the letter which decision you are appealing and the change you are requesting. The appeal must be received by IMY within three weeks from the day you received the decision. However, if you are a party representing the public, the appeal must be submitted within three weeks from the day the decision was announced. If the appeal has been submitted in a timely manner, IMY will forward it to the Administrative Court in Stockholm for examination.  
You may email the appeal to IMY if it does not contain any sensitive personal data or information that may be subject to confidentiality. The authority's contact details are provided on the first page of the decision.
