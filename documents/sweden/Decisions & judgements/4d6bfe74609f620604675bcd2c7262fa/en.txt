Decision of the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) finds that Apohem AB (559094–8401) has processed personal data in violation of Article 32.1 of the Data Protection Regulation by failing to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a suitable level of security for personal data when using the analytics tool Meta-pixel during the period from April 15, 2021, to April 26, 2022.  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection decides, based on Articles 58.2 and 83 of the Data Protection Regulation, that Apohem AB shall pay an administrative fine of 8,000,000 Swedish kronor.  

Report on the supervisory case  
Background, etc.  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) received a notification of a personal data incident from Apohem AB (Apohem or the company) on May 14, 2022. The notification indicated that Apohem had transferred more personal data than intended to Meta Platforms Limited Ireland (Meta) for customers who had consented to marketing cookies in the company's cookie consent manager. The transfer had occurred through a Meta-pixel that was implemented on the company's website. The data that had been transferred to Meta included contact details and purchase information but no information regarding prescription medications. The company became aware of the transfer through an external source and subsequently chose to disable the pixel.  
IMY initiated supervision of Apohem on May 31, 2022, based on the information contained in the notification. The supervision has been limited to the question of whether Apohem has taken appropriate technical and organizational measures in accordance with Article 32 of the Data Protection Regulation.

Justification of the Decision  
IMY must initially determine whether the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is applicable and whether IMY is the competent supervisory authority. If so, IMY should assess whether Apohem is the data controller and whether the company has taken appropriate security measures according to Article 32 of the GDPR to protect the personal data processed through the Meta pixel, with the AAM function activated, during the period from April 15, 2021, to April 26, 2022.  

IMY's Competence  
Applicable Provisions  
According to Article 95 of the GDPR, the GDPR shall not impose any additional obligations on natural or legal persons processing personal data for areas already covered by obligations under the so-called ePrivacy Directive. Furthermore, it is stated that this does not prevent such storage or access that is necessary for the transmission of an electronic message via an electronic communications network or that is necessary for providing a service.

Data Controller  
Applicable Provisions  
The data controller is, according to Article 4.7 of the GDPR, the entity that alone or jointly with others determines the purposes and means of processing personal data. The fact that purposes and means can be determined by more than one actor means that multiple actors can be data controllers for the same processing.  
The data controller shall, according to Article 5.2 of the GDPR, be responsible for and be able to demonstrate compliance with the principles in Article 5.1 (the principle of accountability).  
The Court of Justice of the European Union has stated in the case Fashion-ID that a website operator using so-called plugins from social networks on their website may be jointly responsible with the social network for data processing. This applies to the collection and disclosure of website visitors' personal data that occurs through the social network's plugins. The court also stated that each party is only responsible for the parts of the processing chain for which it has actually determined the purposes and means.

Has Apohem ensured an appropriate level of security for the personal data?  
Applicable Provisions  
The requirement to take appropriate protective measures  

According to Article 32.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of security that is appropriate in relation to the risk of the processing. This shall, according to the same provision, be done taking into account the latest developments, the implementation costs, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing as well as the risks, varying in likelihood and severity, to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. According to Article 32.1, appropriate protective measures, where appropriate, include:  
a) pseudonymization and encryption of personal data,  
b) the ability to ensure the ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems and services,  
c) the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data in a timely manner in the event of a physical or technical incident, and  
d) a procedure for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of the technical and organizational measures to ensure the security of processing.  

In assessing the appropriate level of security, special consideration shall be given, according to Article 32.2, to the risks posed by the processing, in particular from accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration, or to unauthorized disclosure of or access to the personal data that has been transferred, stored, or otherwise processed.

Processing of Sensitive Personal Data  
Data concerning health and sexual life constitutes special categories of personal data, so-called sensitive personal data, which are afforded particularly strong protection under the GDPR. As a general rule, it is prohibited to process such personal data according to Article 9.1 of the GDPR, unless the processing falls under one of the exceptions in Article 9.2 of the Regulation.  
Data concerning health is defined in Article 4.15 of the GDPR as personal data relating to the physical or mental health of a natural person, which provides information about their health status.  
In Recital 35 of the GDPR, it is stated that personal data concerning health should include all data relating to a registered person's health condition that provides information about the registered person's past, present, or future physical or mental health condition.  
The Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) in the case of Lindqvist established that information about a person having injured their foot and being partially sick-listed constitutes personal data concerning health according to the Data Protection Directive (the directive was repealed by the GDPR). The CJEU stated in the case that, considering the purpose of the Data Protection Directive, the term "data concerning health" should be given a broad interpretation and is considered to encompass data relating to all aspects of a person's health, both physical and mental. The CJEU further noted in a later ruling, Vyriausioji tarnybinės etikos komisija, that the concept of sensitive personal data under Article 9.1 of the GDPR should be interpreted broadly and assessed that even personal data that indirectly, through intellectual inference or assessment, reveals a natural person's sexual orientation constitutes sensitive personal data under the relevant provision.

The investigation in the matter shows that the use of the Meta pixel and the activation of the AAM function has resulted in Apohem transferring data to Meta about customers who have initiated or completed purchases on the company's website. The initiated purchases have included situations where customers have manually filled in their personal information in the checkout form and proceeded in the purchasing process but did not complete the purchase. It has further emerged that Meta has implemented a so-called filtering mechanism aimed at attempting to detect and delete information that has been transferred to Meta in violation of Meta's policy. According to a statement from Meta dated February 16, 2024, the mechanism is designed to detect and delete potentially prohibited information, such as data concerning health and finance, in data that users of the pixel transfer to Meta before it is stored and used in Meta's advertising system. When such data is detected and deleted, the user receives a notification about it, but the filtering mechanism also functions even if such a message is not sent to the user.  
Apohem has contended that Meta's statement seems to be based on current conditions regarding how the filtering mechanism operates. However, IMY notes that there is nothing in the investigation that indicates that the filtering mechanism has functioned differently during the relevant time period in this case.  
IMY thus concludes that the investigation in the matter shows that the pixel did not contain a filtering mechanism that prevents the transfer of data to Meta. The filtering mechanism is designed to filter out potentially privacy-sensitive data only after it has been transferred to Meta and if their system has been able to identify that the transferred data contains such prohibited information. The absence of notifications about prohibited and deleted information cannot, in itself, be considered confirmation that potentially privacy-sensitive data has not been transferred to Meta.  
In summary, the existence of the filtering function has not prevented the established transfer of personal data to Meta.

If it concerns a minor violation, IMY may, as stated in Recital 148 of the Data Protection Regulation, instead of imposing a fine, issue a reprimand in accordance with Article 58.2 b.  
IMY's Assessment  

A fine shall be imposed  
IMY has assessed that Apohem has processed personal data in violation of Article 32.1 of the Data Protection Regulation.  
The violation occurred because Apohem processed personal data with an insufficient level of security, which resulted in sensitive personal data about a large number of data subjects being inadvertently transferred to Meta during the period from April 15, 2021, to April 26, 2022. Unauthorized access to such data poses a high risk to the freedoms and rights of the data subjects. IMY believes that this is not a minor violation that could warrant a reprimand instead of a fine.  
The Court of Justice of the European Union has clarified that it is required that the data controller has committed a violation intentionally or negligently for administrative fines to be imposed under the Data Protection Regulation. The Court has stated that data controllers can be imposed fines for actions if they cannot be considered to have been unaware that the action constituted a violation, regardless of whether they were aware that they were disregarding the provisions of the Data Protection Regulation.10  
According to the principle of accountability, which is expressed, among other things, in Article 5.2 of the Data Protection Regulation, those responsible for the processing of personal data must ensure and be able to demonstrate that the processing is in compliance with the Data Protection Regulation.  
IMY notes that Apohem is therefore responsible for ensuring that the personal data processed in its operations is processed in a manner that ensures an appropriate level of security. IMY has determined in its assessment that the company has not met the requirements set by the Data Protection Regulation in this regard. Apohem cannot be considered to have been unaware that its actions constituted a violation of the Regulation.11  
IMY thus assesses that the conditions for imposing an administrative fine on Apohem for the violation are met. In determining the amount of the fine, IMY shall consider the circumstances specified in Article 83.2 and ensure that the administrative fine is effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.

Two percent of the company's annual turnover for 2023 is 12,060,000 SEK. Therefore, the highest sanction fee that can be established in the case is 10,000,000 EUR.  

Severity of the Violation  
According to the EDPB's guidelines, the supervisory authority must assess whether the violation is of low, medium, or high severity.12  
In assessing the severity of the violation, IMY takes into account the following circumstances. The relevant security breaches have led to an incident that has affected a large number of data subjects, and that Meta has been able to access a significant amount of personal data that should not have been transferred to them for an extended period. The data has included sensitive personal data about directly identifiable customers, meaning data that requires a high level of protection. Furthermore, the violation occurred in a pharmacy operation where the data subjects must be considered to have had a legitimate expectation of high confidentiality and that their personal data would not be disclosed to unauthorized parties. The sale of over-the-counter and other health-related products must also be considered part of Apohem's core business, which makes the violation more serious than if that were not the case.13  
Moreover, IMY considers that Apohem had implemented a number of appropriate technical and organizational security measures at the time of the violation. The personal data has also been transferred in hashed, that is, unreadable, format to a single recipient, and thus it does not involve uncontrolled disclosure where the data has, for example, been shared with many unauthorized parties or been publicly available on the web.  
In a comprehensive assessment based on the above circumstances, IMY considers that this constitutes a violation of Article 32.1 of the General Data Protection Regulation of low severity.  
In its assessment of the size of the sanction fee, IMY shall also take into account such aggravating and mitigating factors as listed in Article 83.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation. After the violation, Apohem has, among other things, attempted to contact Meta regarding the transfer of personal data and has developed new governing documents and procedures to reduce the risk of similar incidents. Apohem also completely disabled the pixel when the company became aware of the transfer. However, IMY notes that these measures were taken only after the company was alerted to the relevant deficiencies by an external source. The measures taken cannot, according to IMY, be considered to go beyond what is expected of Apohem in the current case. They are therefore not of such a nature that they affect IMY's assessment of the size of the sanction fee in a mitigating direction. The same applies to the fact that Apohem submitted a report of the personal data incident to IMY, as this constitutes a circumstance that should be considered neutral in determining the sanction fee.14 IMY notes that no other circumstances have emerged that affect the assessment of the size of the sanction fee, either in an aggravating or mitigating direction.

The Sanction Fee Must Be Effective, Proportionate, and Deterrent  
The administrative sanction fee must be effective, proportionate, and deterrent. This means that the amount must be determined so that the administrative sanction fee leads to rectification, provides a preventive effect, and is also proportionate in relation to both the current violations and the supervisory subject's ability to pay.

How to Appeal  
If you wish to appeal the decision, you must write to IMY. Indicate in the letter which decision you are appealing and the change you are requesting. The appeal must be received by IMY no later than three weeks from the day you received the decision. However, if you are a party representing the public interest, the appeal must be received within three weeks from the day the decision was announced. If the appeal has been submitted in time, IMY will forward it to the Administrative Court in Stockholm for consideration.  
You may email the appeal to IMY if it does not contain any sensitive personal data or information that may be subject to confidentiality. The authority's contact details are provided on the first page of the decision.
