Decision after supervision according to the Data Protection Regulation – CDON AB  
Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection decision  
The Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) finds that CDON AB has processed personal data in violation of:  
•  
Articles 5.1(c) and 12.6 of the Data Protection Regulation by requesting additional information from the complainants in complaints 1–3 and 6-7 when they requested to have their personal data deleted, without the processing being necessary to confirm the identity of the complainants.  

•  
Article 12.2 of the Data Protection Regulation by using a burdensome verification method against the complainants in complaints 1–3 and 6-7. The company has thus not sufficiently facilitated the complainants in exercising their right to erasure according to Article 17 of the Data Protection Regulation.  
IMY issues a reprimand to CDON AB under Article 58.2(b) of the Data Protection Regulation for violation of Articles 5.1(c), 12.6, and 12.2 of the Data Protection Regulation.  

Account of the supervisory case  
Processing  
IMY has initiated supervision regarding CDON AB (CDON or the company) due to seven complaints. The complaints have been submitted to IMY, in its capacity as the responsible supervisory authority under Article 56 of the Data Protection Regulation. The transfer has occurred from the supervisory authority in the countries where the complainants have submitted their complaints (Finland and Denmark) in accordance with the Regulation's provisions on cooperation in cross-border processing.  
The processing at IMY has taken place through written correspondence. Given that the complaints concern cross-border processing, IMY has utilized the mechanisms for cooperation and consistency found in Chapter VII of the Data Protection Regulation. The relevant supervisory authorities have been the data protection authorities in Denmark, Norway, and Finland.

According to Article 11.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation, if the data controller, in the cases referred to in paragraph 1 of this article, can demonstrate that they are unable to identify the data subject, the data controller shall, where possible, inform the data subject of this. According to Article 12.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller shall facilitate the exercise of the data subject's rights in accordance with Articles 15–22. In the cases referred to in Article 11.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation, the data controller shall not refuse to comply with the data subject's request to exercise their rights under Articles 15–22, unless the data controller demonstrates that they are unable to identify the data subject.

**General Principles**  
It can be stated that the data controller, in order to identify a data subject, may request additional information that is necessary if the data controller has reasonable grounds to doubt the identity of the person submitting a request. The General Data Protection Regulation does not explicitly regulate which information may be requested or how the additional information should be collected. The data controller must conduct a proportionality assessment to determine what is appropriate in light of the regulation's requirements, including security, but also against the background of the requirement in Article 12.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation, according to which the data controller shall facilitate the exercise of the data subject's rights. Requiring identification information routinely without consideration of whether the information is necessary as described in Article 12.6 of the General Data Protection Regulation is, according to IMY, contrary to both this provision and the principle of data minimization in Article 5.1(c) of the General Data Protection Regulation.  
As follows from the wording of these provisions and as confirmed by the EDPB's guideline 01/2022 on the right of access, the data controller must conduct a proportionality assessment and be able to justify the verification method used. To avoid excessive data collection, a request for additional information must be proportional in relation to the type of data being processed and the harm that may arise. This is also confirmed by the guidelines.  

**Has there been a violation of the General Data Protection Regulation regarding the complaints raised in this case?**  
The question is whether the information that the company has required to meet the requests in the individual cases where the General Data Protection Regulation is applicable (i.e., complaints 1-3 and 6-7) has been necessary to identify the respective complainants and thus in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation. The information that the company has required in the individual complaints, in addition to name and email, has been date of birth, registered address, customer number, order number, and payment method for the most recent order, as well as, depending on the payment method, price and reference number for invoice payments, the last four digits of the card for card payments, and reference or invoice number for direct payments.  
The company has been given the opportunity to justify how each requested piece of information has been necessary to identify the complainants in the individual cases. The company has responded without further explaining the necessity of each requested piece of information, stating that name and email alone have not been sufficient to identify the complainants and verify that the correct person is making a request. According to IMY, the company's account does not provide sufficient support to establish that all of the other relevant information has been necessary to identify the data subjects in accordance with Article 12.6 of the General Data Protection Regulation and the principle of data minimization in Article 5.1(c) of the General Data Protection Regulation. It is CDON, as the data controller, that must be able to demonstrate that the processing is carried out in accordance with the regulation (Article 5.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation). IMY believes that CDON has not done this. IMY thus concludes that CDON AB has processed personal data in violation of Articles 5.1(c) and 12.6 of the General Data Protection Regulation.  
In this case, the complainants have had to provide relatively many personal data to be able to exercise their right to erasure, including order number and price for the most recent order as well as reference number for invoice purchases, along with additional information.

Is the company's current routine compliant with the General Data Protection Regulation?  
The investigation shows that the company has continuously reviewed its routines for handling erasure requests since 2018, when all relevant complaints in the matter were received. The general routines that have been reviewed are those that have been in effect from January 22, 2021, until the date of IMY's decision in the current case.  

To ensure the identity of the data subject requesting erasure, the data subject now needs to answer two questions (one question from category 1 and one question from category 2), such as date of birth and order number. In category 1, since January 22, 2021, data subjects do not need to provide their personal identification number but only the date of birth if the data subject chooses to supplement with that information. No new personal data is requested to confirm the identity of the data subject, but rather two different pieces of information to compare against data that the company already processes about the data subject to verify the data subject. That CDON verifies the identity of the data subject before erasing personal data is also a protection for the data subject, who should not have their personal data erased by mistake. The company also offers an alternative route for the data subject who cannot or does not want to answer the security questions, namely to contact customer service to find another way to verify the identity of the data subject. For a customer who has not made an order, there is thus the option to contact customer service instead.  

Against this background, IMY finds that CDON's existing routine is not disproportionate and thus not in violation of the General Data Protection Regulation, provided that the company only collects the information specified in the routine in situations where there is reason to doubt the identity of the data subject and that only the information necessary to identify the data subject is requested.  

Choice of intervention  
Article 58.2 and Article 83.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation state that IMY has the authority to impose administrative fines in accordance with Article 83. Depending on the circumstances of the individual case, administrative fines shall be imposed in addition to or instead of the other measures referred to in Article 58.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation, such as orders and prohibitions. Furthermore, Article 83.2 of the General Data Protection Regulation specifies which factors should be considered when deciding whether administrative fines should be imposed and when determining the amount of the fine. If it concerns a minor infringement, IMY may, as indicated in Recital 148, instead of imposing a fine, issue a reprimand in accordance with Article 58.2 b of the General Data Protection Regulation. Consideration shall be given to aggravating and mitigating circumstances in the case, such as the nature, severity, and duration of the infringement as well as previous relevant infringements.  

IMY notes the following relevant circumstances. The current supervision covers CDON AB's handling of five individual complainants' requests in the situation to which the complaints pertain.

How to appeal  
If you wish to appeal the decision, you should write to the Swedish Data Protection Authority. Indicate in the letter which decision you are appealing and the change you are requesting. The appeal must be received by the Swedish Data Protection Authority no later than three weeks from the day you received notice of the decision. If the appeal has been submitted in a timely manner, the Swedish Data Protection Authority will forward it to the Administrative Court in Stockholm for consideration.  
You can email the appeal to the Swedish Data Protection Authority if it does not contain any sensitive personal data or information that may be subject to confidentiality. The authority's contact details are provided on the first page of the decision.
